Grothendieck's unpublished mathematics · state of 17 November 2026

The Montpellier Manuscripts

The Université de Montpellier holds Grothendieck's mathematical papers: 178 folders (cotes), 16,074 scanned pages, put online in full. About 11,300 of those pages have never been edited or studied. We are transcribing all of it, checking every page twice against the scans, and working out the mathematics he left open. This page says what is in the archive, what has come back so far, what the research built on it has found, and what comes next. It is a snapshot of the project on that date; the work goes on, and the repositories hold its newest state.

Where it stands

A first transcription is a full diplomatic text of the page (his spelling, deletions, insertions and margin notes kept). The second pass is a line-by-line check of that text against the scan, which corrects about two readings a page. Two whole works, La Longue Marche and Les Dérivateurs, were finished earlier and count as done.

The six books, and how far each has got

The archive is filed in 22 sections, but its mathematics forms six programmes. Grothendieck left two tables of contents of his own that cover most of it: the plan of the EGA, chapters V to XII (the Paris years, 1957 to 1970), and the Esquisse d'un programme (1984), for the Montpellier years, 1973 to 1991. So the edition is organized as a front door (the annotated Esquisse), six books, and a workshop of early notes and documents. Each page will carry three layers: what the folder is and what became of it; his text, in French with the English beside it, linked to the scan; and a commentary, with a development and full proofs where he left the work open.

The books one by one

Book I

Elements, continued ("EGA V and after")

The chapters of the EGA after IV that he planned and drafted: projective morphisms; Hilbert and Hom schemes, descent and representable functors; group schemes and torsors; Picard schemes and Jacobians; residues and duality; intersections and Riemann–Roch; étale cohomology. It opens with his own plan of chapters V to XII, each marked written, drafted or unwritten.

What has come back
  • EGA V (cotes 24 and 25, the "Nouveau EGA V") is transcribed and its readers are out (14 Nov).
  • Most of the claims he makes in these drafts are now theorems in print: of the 1,387 unproved claims our notes record across the archive, about a thousand are SGA, EGA, FGA, Picard and crystal statements of this kind.
  • Eight reader prompts are built and wait only for their folders: the local Picard schemes and the letters to Murre (57, 29), ramification (29), Griffiths for SGA 7 (36), the SGA 3 VIB draft and tensor categories (10), the re-editions (37), and typed notes on groups (123, 32, 45, 46). Cotes 57 and 29 are about a third transcribed, with five packages each in the queue.
Next

The remaining packages of cote 57 and the others; then the map of his drafts against EGA I–IV, SGA and FGA, which becomes the table of contents.

Book II

Cohomology and motives

One cohomology behind all of them: de Rham, crystals, Barsotti–Tate groups, Gauss–Manin; algebraic cycles and the standard conjectures; motives and their Galois groups. He never wrote this up. It opens with his 1967 talks on the de Rham cohomology of algebraic varieties (cote 7).

What has come back
  • Cote 16: the ring of correspondences as an algebra over a universal Lefschetz ring, with an induction on hyperplane sections and two letters to Coates. It is the folder the rest of the archive's notes cite most (119 times).
  • Cote 47: a Fourier transform on the derived categories of abelian schemes, with its inversion theorem, written after 1968. In print the transform is Mukai's (1981), developed by Laumon (1996).
  • Cote 54: de Rham H¹ through the universal vector extension. Cote 44: a programme of seven theorems on intermediate Jacobians with a height formula, and his note "A prouver que cette dernière est positive !"
  • Cote 136, the 1976 IHÉS lecture on the divided-power de Rham complex (375 pages): by his own account, where the idea of schematizing homotopy types began. The notes raise the automorphisms of the PD de Rham functor and his uniqueness conjecture for integral PD de Rham theories.
  • A new cluster, cycles, motives and integral structures (cotes 11, 14, 44, 56, 131, 133, 136, 153, 155), has ten research items and no prompt yet.
Next

The crystals (cotes 4–9, 100) are transcribed and wait for their second pass. The motives (12–15, 18) are about two-thirds transcribed, cote 12 furthest behind. The cycles cluster gets its research prompt once its last pages are in; cote 136's first block (P081) went out today.

Book III

The Long March

The action of the Galois group of Q on the tower of fundamental groups of the moduli spaces of curves, and anabelian geometry. At its centre is La Longue Marche à travers la théorie de Galois (1981, cote 140, 1,584 pages), with the dated notes around it: Kummer theory (141), the paradigms of algebraic curves (143), the Teichmülleries of 1981–82 (144), Spring 1981 (145), the Teichmüller groupoids (146), the structure at infinity (147), the five-point diagram of 1983 (148), and the anabelian notes of 1983 (149).

What has come back
  • La Longue Marche is transcribed, checked twice and published in French and English, with readers (two French books final).
  • Cotes 141, 142, 143 and 149 are checked twice, and cote 147 up to scan 137 (135 of its 189 pages). Cotes 145 and 148 were checked twice on 17 Nov.
  • Cote 149, read in full, ends on the problem of rebuilding a variety along its strata (tori, a "maquette simpliciale"), the same problem as §§11–13 of cote 147, in the arithmetic setting.
  • Cote 148 draws a diagram of 542 objects and 1,620 arrows. The notes point to two strong leads: that the strata of dimension at most two determine the whole tower (147/060; the second pass of 17 Nov found that 148/063 poses a reconstruction programme as a question and is not a witness for it), and his "meilleur exposant" table (146/071–073).
Next

The companion: one born-digital book that sets each of our results at the passage where he asks the question. Its research is nearly all in (below). Its outline is the next piece of work.

Book IV

Maps and polyhedra

Maps on surfaces as finite sets with a group action (the cartographic group), regular polyhedra and polygons over any ring or finite field, the icosahedron. A map drawn on a surface is a curve over a number field, so this is the elementary face of Book III. It opens with his icosahedron course of 1977–78. Almost none of it has been developed by others.

What has come back
  • This is the least advanced book: most of its folders are still in the queue.
  • The notes found a cluster of their own: regular polyhedra over rings (Esquisse §4; cote 72), which waits for cote 79.
  • Three questions from the Longue Marche research land here: the icosahedral centralizer in the profinite mapping class groups, the resolvent maps at 7 and 11, and Lattès maps on dessins.
Next

Cote 79 (six packages queued) and 83 (five); then 76, 88, 70 and 75. Cote 138, the earliest folder on maps, is filed with Book III and has three packages queued.

Book V

Tame topology and the geometry of forms

A topology made for geometry: stratified spaces and their unfolding, tame spaces, pseudolines, and the 1986 "géométrie des formes". It opens with §5 of the Esquisse, his own statement of what is wrong with general topology. The structure at infinity of the moduli spaces (Book III) is a stratified structure, and these notes were written for it.

What has come back
  • Stratified spaces and conic neighbourhoods (150), the stratified topos of 1981 (151), the category of word types (152), and both folders on tame topology (120, 121) are transcribed, all but a few pages. So are the first four parts of the géométrie des formes (156-1, -2, -3, -5).
  • The strongest single item the notes found anywhere in the archive is here: his mobility stratification and "Conjecture 1", a coarsest partition into manifold leaves (121/026–028 and 034, with a relative form for proper maps on 036–037).
  • His axioms of tame topology can now be set against o-minimal structures (120, 121). The tame-topology cluster (cotes 39, 120, 121, 150–152, 154, 156) has ten research items and no prompt yet.
Next

The rest of the géométrie des formes (156-9 and the other parts) and cote 154, which is being transcribed now; then the research prompt for tame topology.

Book VI

Stacks and derivators

Homotopy types as algebraic objects: n-stacks, test categories, schematization, derivators. One line from the Tapis de Quillen (1968) through Pursuing Stacks (1983) to Les Dérivateurs (1990–91), his final answer to what a derived category of coefficients should be.

What has come back
  • Les Dérivateurs (2,135 pages) is transcribed, translated and out as readers. Our companion to it runs to 1,867 pages.
  • The Pursuing Stacks typescript (cotes 134-1 to 134-8) is collated against the 2021 typed edition, and its readers are out (14 Nov). Of the dossier around it, cotes 103–107 are transcribed; 108, 115, 118 and the notes around the derivators (158) are queued.
  • The research on Les Dérivateurs has answered his question of ms. 36ter: filtered ⊊ exact ⊊ total ⊊ quasi-filtered. It also found that a footnote of the printed edition calling Cor. 7.7.2 (XVI, ms. 118) "probablement faux" is wrong.
Next

Three research prompts are ready: localizers and excision, the Fraïssé line, and the smallest finite category of the type of CP² (FINMON_CP2). Then the companion's organization and the dossier.

The front door, the annotated Esquisse d'un programme (cote 119), has its readers out (14 Nov). The rest of that dossier waits for its reader. The workshop holds the early notes and documents: functional analysis in 1953 (cote 1), the thesis topics, the medal.

What the research built on the archive has found

Research runs (GPT-6 Astra, ChatGPT Pro mode, each ten to forty hours) take up the questions his pages raise. Every statement in their returns is the run's own claim until one of our readers has checked it. The status beside each result below says who has.

checkedClaude Opus 5.5 at effort max, 14 Nov
Drinfeld's group is larger than the Galois group, by an explicit sign. There is a continuous character Δ of GT̂₀ (Drinfeld's profinite group defined by the two- and three-cycle relations) with values ±1, zero on the Galois group of Q and non-zero on an explicit element. So GT̂₀ is strictly larger than the image of Galois. This answers a question La Longue Marche exists for. (LM7_PUSH, Longue Marche §§46–47.)
claimed, partly checkedno second reader yet
Does Δ vanish on the profinite Grothendieck–Teichmüller group (with the pentagon)? One run (EXIT_TOWER) claims it does, through the action of GT̂ on the whole tower of moduli spaces. The run that checked it (GT_TOWER_CHECK) repaired steps and reached the same answer, with GT̂ strictly inside the kernel of Δ, strictly inside GT̂₀. That would be a difference between GT̂ and GT̂₀ visible modulo 12. The checking run's last checkpoint was too large to save and is lost, so its final state is known only from its chat.
proofs followedtwo readers (Claude Fable 5.2 at efforts high and max)
Fermat curves and sections. Every Selmer section of the affine Fermat curve of odd exponent at least 3 is cuspidal. This is a case of his section conjecture, with Fermat's curves as the test. (LM6, Longue Marche §51.)
open; pushed tonightbuilt by two runs, unchecked
Ghosts of the section conjecture. His conjecture for the line minus three points over Q (cote 149, scan 56) is open. Two runs built "ghosts": data that look like a rational point at every prime and at any one level of the tower you choose, but come from no rational point. Each one built so far fails at some later level, and one family is killed by an elliptic curve (y² = x³ + 9). Whether a ghost can survive the whole tower at once is the question of a new prompt going out tonight.
claimedfirst check sound so far as it read
Points as sections over finite fields. The prime-to-p map from points to sections is injective in every case, so the exceptional sets of Saïdi and Tamagawa are empty. (ANABELIAN_SUCCESSOR, cote 149.)
claimedintake reading at statement level
Finite models of homotopy types. The smallest finite monoid whose classifying space is the complex projective plane has 23 elements. A finite category of that type needs between 17 and 22 arrows: every category with 16 or fewer is excluded, and the search checked, among others, 215 million tables for 17 arrows on four objects. The exact number is the subject of a new prompt. No sequence of finite categories represents the 2-sphere on finite tests. (FINMON, S2COUNT; Les Dérivateurs.)
running37 hours so far
Finite subgroups and involutions of GT̂. The longest run (LM4_SUCCESSOR) is on whether every involution of GT̂ is conjugate to complex conjugation, and on the centralizer of the icosahedral group. Both are still open at its 34th checkpoint (362 statements).

In all, the Longue Marche runs hold about 3,500 numbered statements (2,885 proved by their own account, 289 refuted, 137 open) and the Dérivateurs runs about 1,700. What is missing is the writing-up: the companions that set these results at his passages.

What the transcribers' notes contain

Each transcription comes with research notes on what the pages say. Read together, they are a map of the archive's open mathematics:

  • 1,387 of Grothendieck's own unproved claims and conjectures, each with cote and scan. 250 bear on one of our runs. Most of the rest are statements from his SGA, EGA and FGA drafts that are now in print.
  • 56 research items that no run has taken up yet, from three readings of every note. The strongest: the mobility stratification (121), the tower determined by its strata of dimension at most two (147/060), his "enveloppe profinie" of the category of hyperbolic surfaces (142), the Albanese equivalence (14), and the positivity he asks for in cote 44.
  • About 700 edition matters: places where a reading, a convention or an error of the page needs a decision. They are checked on the scans in the second pass.
  • The hubs: the folders other folders' notes cite most are 16 (119 times), 44 (58), 149 (47), 147 (42), 54 (33) and 47 (29). The companion of whatever they belong to needs them early.

How it is made

1
First transcription. Packages of about 45 scans go to GPT-6 Astra in ChatGPT Pro mode, two to two and a half hours each. Each package comes back with a diplomatic LaTeX text, a table of doubtful readings, an inventory and research notes. With every package goes a short orientation, the programme map and, credited, Michel Hua's readings of the same pages as a second witness. The run fixes its own reading before it opens them. Easy hands went over Codex web (GPT-6 Astra at reasoning effort max; 1,060 pages), and the clean typescripts over the Anthropic API.
2
Second pass. Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max checks every line against the scan: three images a page, about two corrections a page, roughly one in five of them serious. It works folder by folder, so that each finished check releases a reader. Places it cannot settle go to another witness.
3
Readers. One per finished work or folder: the French text in its diplomatic and reading forms, the English beside it, typeset and checked page by page for overflow. Out so far: La Longue Marche, Les Dérivateurs, Pursuing Stacks, the Esquisse and EGA V.
4
Research and companions. Questions from the pages become research prompts. Their returns are checked by Claude readers (Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 at effort max), and the results are set in the companions at the passage where he asks the question.
5
Everything public. Every prompt, return, review and check is on GitHub as it comes in, with curated datasets on Hugging Face.

The companions

A companion is the born-digital book of record for one work of his: his text, and every result of our runs set at the passage where he asks the question, each with its complete proof, a status line and a record of who checked it. Checked and unchecked results stand in separate parts. The rule for placing a result, from the war game of 14 Nov: a result lives where his own text asks its question most fully, and every other place gets a pointer of two or three lines.

Adjacent mathematics we took note of

Some mathematics came up as we worked that lies beside the programme rather than inside it. It is kept apart here, with no judgment of whether it is worth pursuing. None of it is refereed. Our working notes sort them by programme, and we keep a list of other people's recent results that bear on the work.

Geometric Langlands, read today against the papers
  • The 2024 proof comes in five papers. Before it gets to work, the first (Gaitsgory–Raskin, arXiv:2405.03599: the introduction's "Comparison of the different versions of GLC", and §5) proves that all forms of the conjecture are equivalent: de Rham and Betti, restricted and full, tempered and not.
    • The restricted de Rham and Betti forms are equivalent by Riemann–Hilbert. It applies because D-modules on BunG with nilpotent singular support have regular singularities, a non-trivial result of Arinkin et al. (arXiv:2010.01906, Cor. 16.5.6).
    • The series then works in the de Rham setting, where Kac–Moody localization is available, and gets the Betti case for free.
    • In 2025 Gaitsgory and Raskin obtained part of the ℓ-adic conjecture in characteristic p from characteristic zero (arXiv:2508.02237).
  • For Book II, this is one statement holding across realizations, the shape of "one cohomology behind all of them". But it applies Riemann–Hilbert; it is not a new comparison theorem. It says nothing about crystals, divided powers or integral structures, so no item of Book II rests on it. The companion cites it where it compares realizations.
  • Two neighbours of Book II on the same shelf:
    • Maulik, Shen and Yin (arXiv:2407.05177) prove the Corti–Hanamura motivic decomposition and the relative Lefschetz standard conjecture for the GLn Hitchin system. This sits beside cote 16's ring of correspondences and the cycles cluster.
    • Færgeman (arXiv:2405.18268) proves that rigid G-local systems with quasi-unipotent monodromy are motivic, generalizing Katz.
Outside both programmes, kept for its own sake
  • Periods of CM parts from the zeta function of Q(i) (Chowla–Selberg, Lerch).
  • Weight one and the cosmic Galois group (Connes–Marcolli), with a thread of our own on Dedekind zeta.
  • The discriminant −23, where η(τ)η(23τ) is the M24 eta product.
  • Connes–Consani: class field theory as covers of the adelic space.
  • Brumer–Stark and explicit class field theory for totally real fields (Dasgupta–Kakde), noted from memory and not yet read.
  • Moonshine at Landau–Ginzburg points (Căldăraru–He–Huang; Hong–Mertens–Ono–Zhang), Heegner points and Humbert surfaces.
  • Our own notes on Eisenstein planes and the Leech lattice.
Bridges: outside material used inside
  • Banerjee–Merel's Eisenstein cycles meet the archimedean side of LM8's cusp-divisor term.
  • Ramanujan's signatures 2, 3, 4 and 6 meet both the base of S6 and the Long March's tripod.
  • 31/4 is S6's Néron–Siegel value at the order-3 CM point, and it also generates the field of Δ's bit on the Galois side. The identities are exact and checked; the mechanism behind them is a hunch.
