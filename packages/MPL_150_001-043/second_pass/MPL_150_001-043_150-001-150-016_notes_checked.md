## 3. Own notes (Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max)

- **O1, scan 003 l.91-95 (own idea; confidence high).** The uniqueness he states is correct as a statement about preorders. If arbitrary unions of closed sets are closed (the case "φ loc. finie"), the topology is Alexandrov, and its preorder is recovered as the specialization preorder ($i\leq j$ iff $i\in\overline{\{j\}}$). So "associée à un ordre unique" must be read as "préordre": the next sentence (l.94-95) asks when this preorder is an order.
- **O2, scan 004 l.97-106, 129, 133 (terminology).** The term he defines is "partition stratifiante" (#22): loc. finie, with pieces loc. fermées and an open equivalence relation (l.103). It recurs as "partitions stratifiantes" (l.106), "partition stratifiante stricte" (l.129) and "Partitions stratifiantes ⟺ strat. strictes" (l.133). The first pass's "stratifiée" (struck at l.102, live at l.129, 133) should not enter a glossary.
- **O3, scan 005 l.53-62 (structure of the proof; confidence high).** With the box cancelled (#29), the argument runs:
  1. Take $J$ with $X_J$ closed, $J$ not closed, and minimal among such ($I$ finite "pour simplifier", #31).
  2. For every closed $K$ with $K\cap J$ not closed, $K\supset J$.
  3. So $J$ has a largest element $j$ (#32), and $J_0=J\setminus\{j\}$ is closed.
  4. Then $X_j^\bullet\subset X_{J_0}$ without $\overline j\subset J_0\cup\{j\}$.

  Step 2 needs $X^*_{K\cap J}=X^*_K\cap X^*_J$ closed. That holds because $X^*_K$ is closed for $K$ closed (φ continuous). Minimality then gives $K\cap J=J$. The cancelled route through $J'_1=\overline j\cap J$ should not be cited.
- **O4, scan 005 l.76, 107, and 007 l.34 (notation).** The boundary piece $X_j^\bullet=\overline{X_j^*}\setminus X_j^*$ and the dotted tube $\operatorname{Tub}^\bullet$ use the filled dot that marks $\dot T$ (bord) from 012 on (#35, #38). The first pass's $X_j^\circ$ would collide with $\mathring T$ (intérieur, 012 l.71). Edition and glossary should use the bullet throughout the cote.

By c), every point of $\bar T\setminus Y$ is $\lambda x$ with $x\in\dot T$. Then $0.(\lambda x)=(0\cdot\lambda)x=0.x\in Y$, and $0.y=y$ by a). "à qui celle-ci donne un sens": c) needs $0.\dot T\subset Y$ to define $f$. The label "b′)" is not on the page (#46).
