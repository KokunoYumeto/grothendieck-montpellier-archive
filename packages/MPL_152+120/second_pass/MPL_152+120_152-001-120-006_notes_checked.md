  Esquisse terminology is traced. Medium (the reading "carte" is medium).
- **N4, 120/003 l.45-49: claim checked.** "M1, M2, M4 impliquent que les [M_n] sont stables par intersection de
  deux": for A, B ∈ M_n, A × B ∈ M_{2n} by M4 (with the reindexing [1,2n] ≅ [1,n] ⊔ [1,n], a permutation, M1); the
  map u : [1,2n] → [1,n], i ↦ i and n+i ↦ i, is surjective with u^*(x) = (x, x), and M2 gives
  u^{*-1}(A × B) = A ∩ B ∈ M_n. So the claim holds with exactly the axioms of his insertion "(moyennant M1, M2, M4)"
  (#40). The page writes 𝔖_n for M_n three times (#39): editions print 𝔖_n with a note. High.
- **N5, 120/003 l.50: reading question with a mathematical answer.** "pour avoir M3, il suffit de le prouver pour u
  \unsure{surjectif\|injectif}, et m̄ seulement pour u^* l'op. standard R^{n+1} → R^n". Factor u = j ∘ s with s
  surjective, j injective; then u^{*-1}(B) = j^{*-1}(s^{*-1}(B)) and s^{*-1}(B) ∈ M by M2, so M3 is needed only for
  injective j, whose u^* is a coordinate projection; projections compose from standard ones, and the induction uses
  M1 (images of A under projections). So "injectif" is the word that makes the sentence true; if the ink is
  "surjectif" (the first letter has the shape of his s; Hua reads « surjectif »), it is a slip. High for the
  mathematics; the reading stays open.

- **N12, 152/004 l.55-61: possible gap in the recursion (bears on @902, P04, P12).** As written, one edge of an
  adjacent two-element orbit ε is contracted ("de l'une des \deleted{deux} arêtes ∈ ε") and σ′ is "déduite de σ". If
  the partner edge then becomes a fixed edge, nested pairs are rejected: for σ = (1 4)(2 3) on a four-edge segment,
  contracting 2 (or 3) leaves 1 and 4 separated by the fixed edge, so no step is possible, although the chords 1-4 and

  usual recursive test for non-crossing pairings ((1 4)(2 3) → (1 4) → ∅). Which rule he meant decides whether
  "admissible" coincides with "totalement non pénétrante"; the struck "deux" shows he hesitated at exactly this word.
  Medium (it depends on how σ′ treats the partner edge, which the page does not say).
