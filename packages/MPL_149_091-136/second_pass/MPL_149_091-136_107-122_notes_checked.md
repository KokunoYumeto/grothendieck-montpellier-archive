## Own notes (second pass, Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max)

1. **Scan 109 (and 107, 112, 113), reading question.** Every T of scan 109, lines 4–7 and (11), is written over an earlier Z (the Z's
   bars are visible under a heavy T stem). Grothendieck named the 1-connected base object Z on scan 107 ("un Y-schéma Z 1-connexe",
   "un Z 1-connexe s'envoyant dans X") and switched to T while writing scan 109; Z survives in scan 112 (diagram … ← X̃ ← Z and
   E^Z_{X_Ỹ} in the inserted (18)) and in the insertion "(Z non précisé)" of scan 113. Z and T therefore name the same kind of object;
   an edition should not treat E^Z and E^T as different constructions. Confidence high (ink).
2. **Scan 119, left margin, unproved claim.** "Ici I_{x'} joue le rôle d'un π^{L̄}_{Ũ_x/k(x)} ; en fait il est = π^{L̄}_{Ũ_x/S̃_x} !"
   With S̃_x the (non-strict) henselisation, π1(S̃_x) ≅ G_{k(x)} and π1(Ũ_x) is the decomposition group, so the kernel of
   E_{Ũ_x} → E_{S̃_x} is the inertia group: the "=" is right, and it is the relative group (10)–(11) of the punctured trait over the trait.
   It matches scan 120: "I_{x'} est le noyau de E_{Ũ_x} → E_{S̃_x}". The "π_{Ũ_x/k(x)}" is only an analogy, since Ũ_x is not a
   k(x)-scheme. Not in the first pass's notes. Confidence high.
3. **Scan 120, reading question.** The map E_{Ũ_x} → E_{S̃_x} in "le noyau de …" is drawn with a double shaft, the only such arrow on
   these pages. If deliberate it marks a surjection (true: D → G_{k(x)} is onto), matching the insertion "surjectif" in (30). Low
   confidence in the intent; transcribed as `\unsure{\Longrightarrow|\longrightarrow}`.
4. **Scan 117, NB margin, reading question.** "NB on n'intéresse|s'intéresse en termes 'absolus' que pour des schémas épais (quasi-proj. ?)
   réguliers": the letter before the apostrophe looks like n. Either way the sense is a restriction, without a base field, to regular
   schemes, parallel to scan 116's "tout modèle régulier"; "épais" remains unconfirmed. Low confidence.
5. **Scan 120, second margin, own idea.** A test of "Ça devrait être le cas pour U 'élémentaire' à facteurs pas de types (0,0) ni (0,1)":
   take U = C1 × C2 over C with both curves hyperbolic and v the divisorial valuation of the blow-up of (∞1, ∞2), a pair of cusps in
   C̄1 × C̄2. Its inertia generator maps to (γ1, γ2) in π1(U) = π1(C1) × π1(C2), with γi the cuspidal loops, which are nontrivial for
   hyperbolic curves; the image is nonzero and v has no centre on U, consistent with the restricted converse. A proof would need the
   general case of valuations centred on boundary strata of a good compactification. Confidence medium (computation sketched, not
   checked for all divisorial valuations).
6. **Scans 107–113 and 119, reading question.** The geometric-point glyph (25 places) cannot be read as ξ or ζ from the ink (montage
   `M08`): one long stroke with a small top hook, no double bowl; in "p^t géom ξ de K̃_x" (scan 119) it has a bottom loop. Its role is
   clear (a geometric point, paired with η on scan 108, (8)). On scan 112 the subscript of the point before "s'interprétant" is written
   like α, a cursive lowercase x; the other subscripts (X, Y, T) are too small to tell case. Kept as capitals.
