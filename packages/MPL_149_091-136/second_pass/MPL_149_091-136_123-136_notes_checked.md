## 2. Our own notes (second pass, Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max)

**N1 — scan 123, NB — own idea (reading of "pas stables par réunion finie").** If "modèle" means an affine model, his claim is true. Take $L=K(t)$: $\mathbb A^1_K$ and $\mathbf P^1_K\setminus\{0\}$ are affine models whose opens in $\mathfrak X$ together cover $\mathfrak X\simeq\mathbf P^1_K$. An affine model whose open is all of $\mathfrak X$ would be proper and affine, hence finite, which is impossible. If every separated model is allowed, the class is stable (123-O2). The NB therefore tells which convention he uses; this should be compared with the definition of "modèle" on scans 120–122. Confidence: high for the example.

**N2 — scan 124, "des $T_\infty^\alpha$ essentiellement, où $\alpha$ est la codim" — own idea.** In residue characteristic 0 all ramification is tame, so for a valuation ring $V$ of $L$ the inertia group of $\bar L$ over the henselization is $I_V\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma_V\otimes\mathbf Q/\Gamma_V,\ \mu_\infty)$. Hence $I_V\simeq T_\infty^r$ exactly when $\Gamma_V\simeq\mathbf Z^r$. For a point of dimension 0 of $\mathfrak X$ (residue field algebraic over $K$) the codimension is $n=\operatorname{trdeg}L/K$. So his shape $T^\alpha$ holds exactly for the Abhyankar valuations, where the rational rank is $n$ and $\Gamma_V\simeq\mathbf Z^n$. It fails for the arc valuations of 130-O1 ($\Gamma=\mathbf Z$, $I\simeq T$, codimension 2). It also fails for valuations with value group $\mathbf Z[1/2]$, where $I\simeq\prod_{p\ne2}\mathbf Z_p(1)$, which is not a power of $T$. Confidence: high for the formula (ramification theory of henselian fields with residue characteristic 0) and for the Abhyankar equivalence.

**N3 — scans 126–127, $\mathcal E_K^0$ and $\Gamma^0$ — reading question / own idea.** On 126 a germ normalizing $I_{V_1}\simeq T$ acts on it by the cyclotomic character of its image in $\mathcal E_K$. "Sa restriction à $\mathcal E_K^0$ centralise $I_{V_1}$" therefore needs $\mathcal E_K^0\subset\ker(\chi_{\rm cycl})$. The natural reading is $\mathcal E_K^0=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar K/K(\mu_\infty))$, and then $\Gamma^0$ on 127 is the same group for $\Gamma=\mathcal E_K^{\bar K}$. With this reading, $\operatorname{Centr}_\Gamma(\Gamma^0)=(1)$ would follow from two properties of fields $K$ of finite type over $\mathbf Q$, still to be checked: $G_{K(\mu_\infty)}$ has trivial centre, and $G_K$ has no nontrivial abelian closed normal subgroup. Indeed the centralizer meets $\Gamma^0$ in its centre and maps injectively into $\Gamma/\Gamma^0\subset\hat{\mathbf Z}^\times$. Confidence: medium (the meaning is inferred from use, not from a definition on these pages).

**N5 — scan 130, 2°) — own idea (criterion and counterexample to "même surjective").** Let $f_i$ ($i\in I(x)$) be local equations of the branches at $x$. Let $M=\bigoplus_{i\in I(x)}\mathbf Z e_i$ and $\varphi:M\to\Gamma_V$, $e_i\mapsto v(f_i)$. The geometric map $I_V\to I_x=T^{I(x)}$ is the Pontryagin dual of $M\otimes\mathbf Q/M\to\Gamma_V\otimes\mathbf Q/\Gamma_V$.
- Its image is open iff $\varphi$ is injective (the $v(f_i)$ are linearly independent over $\mathbf Q$) and $\varphi(M)$ has finite index in $M'=(\varphi(M)\otimes\mathbf Q)\cap\Gamma_V$. The index of the image is then $[M':\varphi(M)]$. So 2°) needs card $I(x)\le$ rational rank of $\Gamma_V$; 130-O3 is the case where $\varphi$ is not injective.
- If card $I(x)=\dim X$ and the weights are independent, $V$ is monomial in the $f_i$ and $\Gamma_V=\varphi(M)$, so the map is an isomorphism. This is the case where "même surjective" holds.
- "Même surjective" fails already for card $I(x)=1$ with residue field $\mathbf Q$. Take $X=\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\supset U=(\mathbf P^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\})^2$ and $L=\mathbf Q(x,z)\to\mathbf Q((s))$, $x\mapsto s^2$, $z\mapsto 2+s+s^2h(s)$, with $h$ transcendental over $\mathbf Q(s)$. Then $V=L\cap\mathbf Q[[s]]$ has $\Gamma_V=\mathbf Z$ (uniformizer $z-2$) and residue field $\mathbf Q$, with centre $(0,2)$, where only the branch $x=0$ passes. Since $x=(z-2)^2u$ with $u\equiv1$, the map $I_V=T\to I_x=T$ is multiplication by 2: the image is open of index 2, not all of $T$.

Confidence: high for the example; medium-high for the criterion (Kummer description of $I_x$ in characteristic 0 at a normal-crossing point).

**N6 — scan 131, "l'image de $\mathcal E_K^{\bar L}$ doit normaliser un sous-groupe $\Delta_x\simeq T^{I(x)}$" — own idea.** This conclusion needs only the factorization (35) and 1°), not 2°). The section of a valuation with residue field $K$ factors through $N_V\to N_x\to\mathcal E_U$, so its image lies in the image of $N_x$, which normalizes the image $\Delta_x$ of $I_x$. Confidence: medium-high.

**N7 — scan 128, "des $V$ distinctes définissent-elles des $\Delta_V$ distinctes ?" — own idea.** By N5, every monomial valuation at a crossing point $x$ with weights independent over $\mathbf Q$ has $I_V\to I_x$ an isomorphism, so $\Delta_V=\Delta_x$ for all of them.
