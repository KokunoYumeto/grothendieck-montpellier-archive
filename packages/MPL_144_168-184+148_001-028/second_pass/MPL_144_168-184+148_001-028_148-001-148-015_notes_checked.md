## C. Notes of my own (Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max), labelled

**C1. 148/002 l.59-60 and 003 l.3-21: the NB as a fibre product gives the splittings and their stabilizer.**
Kind: own idea / unpursued implication.

The NB reads SUT(X) ≃ T(X) ×_{𝔖_I} Aut((T_i)_{i∈I}). Then (*) (002 l.89-90) is the pullback, along T(X) → 𝔖_I, of
1 → 𝕌^I → Aut((T_i)) → 𝔖_I → 1.

A marking s ∈ ∏T_i identifies Aut((T_i)) with 𝕌^I ⋊ 𝔖_I. The subgroup 𝔖_I then gives the splitting
φ_s(γ) = (γ, (1, π(γ))) of (*), which is the splitting of 003 l.4-6.

For u ∈ 𝕌^I: int(u)∘φ_s(γ) = (γ, (u·π(γ)(u)^{-1}, π(γ))). This is φ_{u.s}, as on 003 l.21. It equals φ_s for every
γ exactly when u is fixed by π(T(X)), that is, when u is constant on the orbits of T(X) on I.

So the map s ↦ φ_s from ∏T_i to the class of splittings is equivariant. Its stabilizer is (𝕌^I)^{π(T(X))}, which
always contains the diagonal 𝕌. That is the "(pas fidèlement d'ailleurs)" of 003 l.37, made exact, and it settles the
stabilizer R18 asks for.

Confidence: high, given the reading of #3. It is a direct computation.

**C2. 148/002 l.45-54: "cocart" read as a pushout of extensions.** Kind: own idea / reading consequence.

The square has Z^I ↪ S_*T^!, Z^I → R^I, and ":" in the corner, with a diagonal to SRT^! (label λ, doubtful; perhaps an
isomorphism mark). In the category of groups, the pushout of R^I ← Z^I → S_*T^! is an amalgamated free product, which
cannot be what is meant.

The consistent reading is the pushout of the extension 1 → Z^I → S_*T^!(X) → T^!(X) → 1 along Z^I ⊂ R^I. That gives
R^I ∧_{Z^I} S_*T^!(X) = (R^I × S_*T^!)/Z^I, an extension of T^!(X) by R^I. The same contracted product appears on l.108-110
for the marked group.

R^I acts near the boundary and S_*T^! fixes it, so they commute. The map to SRT^! = R^I.S_*T^!(X) (l.39-42) is then onto.
Injectivity fails exactly where Z^I → S_*T^! or Z^I → R^I is not injective: the excluded types of R17.

Confidence: medium (it rests on an interpretation of "cocart").

**C3. 148/002 l.104-110: why the cancelled "!" matters.** Kind: reading consequence.

With the exponent cancelled (#7), the displays read S_*T(X,s) ↪ SRT(X) and S_*T(X,s) ∧_{Z^I} R^I ≃ SRT(X). This agrees
with the margin (l.62-65: S_*T(X) needs the extra structure s) and with 003 (S_1T(X,s), no "!").

in that case.

Confidence: medium on the reading (hatching over the "!"); high on the consequence, given the reading.

**C4. 148/007 l.10, l.36: the cells of X_S/±1.** Kind: own idea (count).

The doubled decomposition of C/Z[i] (4 vertices ₂X, 8 edges of length ½, 4 square faces centred at (1+i)/4 + ½Z[i])
has χ = 0. Multiplication by −1:
- fixes the 4 vertices;
- swaps the two edges of each horizontal and each vertical circle ([v, v+½] ↦ [v−½, v]);
- moves the face centred at c to the face centred at c + (1+i)/2, never the same face.

So the quotient has 4 vertices, 4 edges and 2 square faces: χ = 2, a sphere made of two squares glued along a square
equator with vertices e, η, η′, η″.

The struck "deux" (l.36) was the right number; the surviving "subdivisée en [faces] carrées, se raccordant suivant un
équateur ... subdivisé en quatre arêtes" agrees with it.

Confidence: high.

**C5. 148/013 l.6 → 015 l.4: edges, diagonals and 2-torsion.** Kind: own check of a claim.

In the one-vertex triangulation of C/Z[ζ] (two triangles, three edges, one vertex), the three edges are the lattice
segments in the directions 1, ζ, ζ̄. These are the three long diagonals of the hexagon S (vertices ±1, ±ζ, ±ζ̄, as
drawn on 009). Their midpoints 1/2, ζ/2, ζ̄/2 are the three nonzero points of ₂X_S. So "Les arêtes correspondent aux
trois diagonales de l'hexagone, ou encore aux trois pts d'ordre 2 distincts de e" holds as written.

The doubled decomposition has 4 vertices, 12 edges, 8 faces (χ = 0, with "12" over a struck digit on 015 l.6). Its
quotient by ±1 has 4 vertices, 6 edges, 4 faces (χ = 2), the tetrahedron of 015 l.38.

Confidence: high.
