## Edition matter

### #g673: 151/015, "et de là, plus généralement" (error of the page)

**Reading.** Confirmed on the scan. 015 l.20 reads "et de là, plus généralement, la forme équivalente \unsure{unique} de
Hyp 1, 2, 2' :", and the Hyp of l.23–27 requires the comparison to be epi for every finite diagram. The parenthesis on
l.29–33 claims it is enough to postulate it for diagrams in A and for the three shapes "$I=\bullet$, $\bullet\
\bullet$, [cospan]", which correspond to "Hyp 1, Hyp 2, Hyp \unsure{2|1}'". Only the third label changed (#41 of the
changes).

**Mathematics.** Checked and kept: this is an error of the page. The three epi tests, together with b′) and c′) for
all presheaves, do not give the epi condition for all finite diagrams. My own check of the counterexample:
- Take A = BC₂ (one object, End = C₂) and 𝒳 = Set, with φ(*) = a point. Then φ_! sends a C₂-set F to its orbit set F/C₂.
- The terminal comparison is pt → pt.
- (F×G)/C₂ → F/C₂ × G/C₂ is onto, since any pair of representatives gives a preimage.
- (G×_F H)/C₂ → G/C₂ ×_{F/C₂} H/C₂ is onto: if u(g) = v(h)·c, then (g, h·c) lies in G×_F H.
- The equalizer fails. Take the regular C₂-set R and the two equivariant maps id and translation by the generator; C₂
  is abelian, so translation is equivariant. Their equalizer is ∅, while both maps induce the identity of R/C₂ = pt, so
  the comparison is ∅ → pt, which is not epi.
- An equalizer is a pullback over Y×Y, but the pullback test c′) compares with a fibre product over φ_!(Y×Y), which
  differs from φ_!Y×φ_!Y. Here φ_!(R×R) has two points, so c′) holds (∅ → ∅) and gives no control of the equalizer.

The first pass's C06 is therefore correct, and the claim of l.29–33 is false as stated.

### C07: 151/015, final question about left exactness, own idea of the first pass. Keep: checked, correct
The question reads "Mais cette hypothèse peut-elle être vérifiée, sans que φ_! soit $=$ ex.\textsuperscript{t} à gauche
?" (015 l.35). The "=" is his "même", and the abbreviation has a raised t. Hua batch-01's "exact ? Quand ?" is not borne
out: the last word is "gauche".

The two-cone argument holds:
- For a finite D with limit M, the diagram D⁺ adds two vertices with value M and all cone legs, and no arrow between
  the two vertices.
- lim D⁺ = M, and lim L(D⁺) = L(M) ×_{lim L(D)} L(M).
- The Hyp for D⁺ makes the diagonal of c: L(M) → lim L(D) epi, hence invertible, so c is mono. The Hyp for D makes c
  epi, so c is iso. This includes D = ∅.

So for the full Hyp the answer to his question is no: it forces left exactness.

### N3: 151/017 l.20, reading question with a mathematical consequence
The margin defines δ_nK_n, where δ_n: [1,n] ↪ [1,n+1] is the inclusion and K_n: [1,n+1] → [1,n] a retraction. The
composite is idempotent, δ_nK_nδ_nK_n = δ_n(K_nδ_n)K_n = δ_nK_n, and its image is [1,n] ⊊ [1,n+1]. It is therefore not
a permutation.

Read as "ρ_{n+1}", the letter used on the same page for the circular permutation of 𝔖_{m+1} (l.82, l.93–97), the margin
would assert a falsehood. Read as "p_{n+1}", it names the projection that its own gloss describes: "projection dans
𝔼_{n+1}, rétraction sur 𝔼_n ⊂ 𝔼_{n+1}".

The ink form is a straight-stemmed p, like "p_n = δ_n^*" on l.33. The first pass and Hua batch-01 read ρ, so the doubt
mark stays. Confidence: medium on the letter; high that p_{n+1} is the only reading under which the margin is true.
