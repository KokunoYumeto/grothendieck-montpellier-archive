## The check

- **The six points in characteristic 5.** There 2² = −1, so i = ±2 and {0, ∞, ±1, ±i} = P¹(F₅).
- **Its stabilizer.** An element of PGL₂(k) is fixed by the images of 0, 1, ∞. One that maps P¹(F₅) to itself
  therefore lies in PGL₂(F₅) ≅ 𝔖₅, of order 120.
- **Five Klein four-groups share it.**
  - The 15 involutions of PGL₂(F₅) with fixed points in P¹(F₅) are the double transpositions of 𝔖₅.
  - The Klein four-groups whose three fixed-point pairs partition P¹(F₅) are the five normal Klein subgroups of the
    point stabilizers 𝔖₄ ⊂ 𝔖₅.
  - So five groups V, and five groups Γ, have the same set S: the maps a → c and b → c are five-to-one.
- **Computation.** `octa.py` (Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max) works over F_{p²} for p = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23,
  29.
  - For every p except 5: the stabilizer of S has order 24, and one Klein four-group has S as its six-point set.
  - For p = 5: the stabilizer has order 120, and five Klein four-groups do.
- **Why 5 is the only exception** (my argument):
  - An element fixing three points of P¹ is the identity, so Stab(S) acts faithfully on six points and its order
    divides 720.
  - For p ≥ 7, and in characteristic 0, that order is prime to p. Stab(S) is then cyclic, dihedral, A₄, 𝔖₄ or A₅, and
    the only one of these containing 𝔖₄ is 𝔖₄.
  - For p = 3 the computation gives 24. For p = 5 the stabilizer is 𝔖₅.
  - When Stab(S) = 𝔖₄, only its normal Klein subgroup has fixed points in S. The other Klein subgroups contain the
    rotations about edge midpoints.
