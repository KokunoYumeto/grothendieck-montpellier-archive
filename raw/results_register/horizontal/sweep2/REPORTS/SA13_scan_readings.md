## 1. Cote 34, scans 5-6: Picard group of a surface of degree ≥ 4

- **Transcription:** `results_register\horizontal\inputs\SA13\pro\MPL_33_147-181+34_002-017_transcription.tex`, lines 1107-1116.
- **What the notes said:**
  - Pro note M07 (entry `MPL_33_147-181+34_002-017#47`): no genericity condition occurs here, and a possibly omitted condition is a reading question.
  - Pro note M08 (`#48`): a counterexample to the statement as written. The Fermat quartic x₀⁴+x₁⁴+x₂⁴+x₃⁴ = 0 contains the line x₀ = ζx₁, x₂ = ζx₃ (ζ⁴ = −1). If Pic were generated by the hyperplane class H, then [L] = mH would give 1 = H·L = 4m.
  - Sweep 1 flagged a suspected misreading.
- **What the ink shows:**
  - Foot of 34/5: "S'inspirant d'arguments de Lefschetz, Deligne montrera plus tard que si X₀ est une surface de degré ≥ 4".
  - Top of 34/6: "dans P³_k, alors on a Z ≃ Pic(P³) ⥲ Pic(X₀), k étant un corps de car. quelconque."
  - There is no genericity word, no insertion between the lines, and nothing in the margin at this place.
- **Decision:** the transcription reads the ink correctly. The omission is his.
- **What it changes:**
  - As written the statement is false: M08's quartic contains a line, so its Picard group is not generated by H.
  - The sentence announces a later result of Deligne's. The true statement is about the generic surface of degree ≥ 4, in any characteristic. My identification of it is the theorem of SGA 7 Exposé XIX ("Le théorème de Noether"); I have not checked that against the exposé here.
  - Nothing else on these pages depends on the sentence.
  - Item SA13-22 is updated; the flag is closed as his slip.

## 2. Cote 39, scan 20: the coefficient α in the preparation proof

- **Transcription:** `results_register\horizontal\inputs\SA13\pro\MPL_36_053-085+39_transcription.tex`, line 1455 for scan 20 and line 1403 for scan 19's Ḟ = U̇Z^N.
- **What the notes said:**
  - Pro note N94 (`MPL_36_053-085+39#94`): α's coefficient is uncertain, while scan 19 has Ḟ = U̇Z^N; a scalar α need not account for the higher coefficients.
  - The first-pass record (`research_leads#49`) noted "a_N+1 (uncertain against N−1), surprising relative to a_N unit".
  - Sweep 1 flagged a suspected misreading, since the mathematics wants a_N.
- **What the ink shows:**
  - The line is "et comme Ḟ = ~~ȧ_{N+1}~~ α Z^N (α = a_{N±1} ∈ A₀*) ceci est manifestement bijectif, donc φ aussi ; cqfd".
  - The struck coefficient has a dot over the a and the subscript N followed by plus strokes.
  - In the parenthesis, the subscript is a dense overwrite (an N written over another character), then a short horizontal stroke and a hooked 1. At five-fold magnification "+1" and "−1" cannot be told apart.
  - There is no plain a_N in either place.
- **Decision:** the transcription's reading a_{N+1} | a_{N−1} is confirmed, and the + or − doubt remains. This is not a misreading; the index is his slip.
- **What it changes:**
  - The last step needs Ḟ = (unit)·Z^N in the associated graded ring.
  - Write F = Σ aᵢZ^i, with aᵢ topologically nilpotent for i < N and a_N a unit, as on scan 19. Then Ḟ = ȧ_N Z^N + ȧ_{N+1} Z^{N+1} + … = U̇ Z^N, with U̇ a unit.
  - Multiplying by a unit is bijective, so ((b_i), Q) ↦ Σ_{i<N} b_i Z^i + Q Ḟ is bijective, and the "cqfd" stands.
  - a_{N+1} and a_{N−1} are not units in general, so α must be read as the leading unit ȧ_N (in the polynomial case) or the unit series U̇. The slip does not touch the division and preparation theorem.
  - Item SA13-55 is updated.

## 3. Cote 36, scan 6: "6.3 est faux" or "63 est faux"

- **Transcription:** `results_register\horizontal\inputs\SA13\pro\MPL_36_002-052_transcription.tex`, line 216: "ie \unsure{6.3|63} est faux (déformer de façon générale E×F, E et F elliptiques, E ordinaire, F non ordinaire ; général ⇒ générique simple."
- **What the notes said:**
  - Pro note N20 (`MPL_36_002-052#20`) is headed "The manuscript calls 6.3 false" and quotes "6.3 est faux" as certain.
  - Sweep 1 flagged the note as overstating the page, because the transcription keeps the doubt.
- **What the ink shows:**

  - On the same sheet, statement numbers carry points: 4.7, 5.2.3.1, 5.7, 5.15, 5.11, 6.6.
  - Pages of the draft appear either as bare numbers (54 quater, 55 ter, 57/58) or with "pg" (pg 41 … pg 59).
- **Decision:** the note misreads the page. The transcription's doubt resolves to "63", which by the sheet's own conventions is probably page 63 of the draft; that is my inference.
- **What it changes:**
  - The refuted assertion is identified by its content, stated in the line before: "Je ne crois pas que la plus grande sous VA ordinaire puisse se relever (ni en cas de bonne réduction)."
  - The counterexample is unchanged. Deform E×F (E ordinary, F not) in a general way, so the generic fibre is simple and the ordinary abelian subvariety E of the special fibre does not lift.
  - What changes is the label ("63", not "6.3") and, as the further finding below shows, the author of the remark.
  - Item SA13-37 is corrected, and the note's fidelity is now "misreads".

## 4. Cote 36, scan 56: the sign in the identity of a quadratic map

- **Transcription:** `results_register\horizontal\inputs\SA13\pro\MPL_36_053-085+39_transcription.tex`, line 113.
- **What the notes said:**
  - Pro note N09 (`MPL_36_053-085+39#9`) proposed "a decisive elementary test" of the identity as written.
  - The first-pass record (`research_leads#4`) said the minus is visible and warned against silently replacing it by the parallelogram plus.
  - Sweep 2 flagged a suspected misreading.
- **What the ink shows:**
  - The line reads "… est une application quadratique i.e. φ(u+v) − φ(u−v) = 2(φ(u)+φ(v))."
  - Between the two φ terms is a plain short dash, the same as the dash in "u−v".
  - Both plus signs on the line, in "u+v" and "φ(u) + φ(v)", are drawn as small crosses.
  - There is no correction.
- **Decision:** the reading is confirmed. The minus is his slip.
- **What it changes:**
  - The identity of a quadratic map is the parallelogram law φ(u+v) + φ(u−v) = 2(φ(u)+φ(v)).
  - With the minus it fails: for φ(u) = u², u = 1, v = 0, the left side is 0 and the right side is 2.
  - The lemma's content is unaffected. That content is (n·id_A)*L ≡ n²L, and the quadratic map Hom(A,B) → Hom(NS(B), NS(A)) that the biextension argument uses. The display should be read as the parallelogram law.
  - Item SA13-39 is updated.

## Further finding: whose hand the "Remarques. SGA 7 IX" on 36/3-8 are in

- **The text.** 36/3 opens "Remarques. SGA 7 IX". The remarks go section by section and page by page through a draft of Exposé IX (pg 41 to pg 59 on 36/6). They address its author as "tu": "tu supposes tantôt A⁰ ℓ-divisible", "Que fais-tu de la partie discrète ?", "tu n'as plus besoin ici de car(K) ≠ ℓ".
- **The hand.** I stacked a sample of 36/6 ("pg 42 début dém. 4.7 : le cas n'est pas clair pour n = 2, et je n'ai pas confiance en dehors du cas strictement local") between a sample of the signed Deligne letter at 32/31 and Grothendieck's hand on 36/56.
  - The 36/6 hand is, to my eye, the same small regular cursive as Deligne's letter; compare "pas", "n'ai" and "en dehors du cas" with "dans un temps".
  - It is clearly not the fast hand of 36/56.
  - Marginal replies in another instrument ("Je le dis!", "C'est dit dans … 5.15") may be the author's answers. "CANULAR ?!" is in the margin beside "pg 59"; who wrote it is not decided.
- **What follows.** The folder is "SGA 7 (ma part)". The remarks on 36/3-8 are therefore very probably Deligne's comments on Grothendieck's draft of Exposé IX, not Grothendieck's self-flagged doubts.
  - The Pro notes on these pages (`MPL_36_002-052` N01-N29) call them "self-flagged", "the manuscript calls 6.3 false" and so on.
  - Sweep 1 called them his.
  - Both are corrected: items SA13-34, SA13-37 and SA13-38 now say "a reader's remarks (probably Deligne's)", and the 28 placement rows on 36/3-8 carry a note.
- **Limits.** This is an attribution by eye from three pages, not a paleographic certainty. A comparison with more pages of 36/3-8 and with other Deligne letters in the archive would settle it.
