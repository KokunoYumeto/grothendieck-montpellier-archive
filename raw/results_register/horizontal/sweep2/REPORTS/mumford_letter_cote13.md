## What he withdraws (decided on scans 14, 19 and 20)

Scan 14, read on the image: "I have become aware that I have been a bit rash with the conjectures I told you about Hodge
structures, as in the form I stated them they contradict Tate's conjecture, which implies the following: if X is a smooth,
connected simply connected scheme over C, and V a polarized complex analytic family of Hodge structures parametrized by
X^hol, then V is "algebraic" only if it is a constant family." Easy examples of non-constant V exist (X any homogeneous
space under an affine group over C), "therefore (one hopes!) not 'algebraic'".

The conjectures are the ones the P.S. (scans 16-18) states. Data: G reductive over Q, the circle U_R, a homomorphism
i_0: U_R -> G_R with i_0(-1) central and the centralizer K_R of i_0(sqrt(-1)) maximal compact; Q = G_R/C_R with
C_R = Centr(i_0); the "Siegel-space" gamma = G(R)^0/(G(R)^0 n C_R(R)), a connected component of Q(R), with a natural
complex structure. Every Q-representation V of G with a lattice V_0 gives a complex analytic family of Hodge structures on
gamma (globally polarizable). His conjectures:
1. the modular varieties gamma/Gamma (Gamma commensurable with G(Z)) "carry algebraic structure over Q (and morally, Z)";
2. the modular families of Hodge structures on them are "algebraic", i.e. come from relative motives.

Why they fail as stated (scans 18-19, read on the image): the gamma of the orthogonal and symplectic type are "universal"
(every modular family is induced from one on them), so if the modular family there were algebraic, every one would be.
"Taking the case G(R) compact, thus gamma/Gamma = gamma = complex projective homogeneous space X under G_C by Borel, we
would get a polarized motive over the analytic space gamma"; with the GAGA yoga it would be a motive over X, which
contradicts Tate's conjectures because X is simply connected. "So I really do not know what to believe!"

What he keeps (scan 20, read on the image): a necessary condition, "granting Tate's and Hodge's conjectures", for gamma/Gamma
and the families on it to be algebraic: for every g in G(R)^0 and every Gamma_0 of finite index in G(Z), the smallest
algebraic subgroup H of G with H_R containing g i_0(U_R) g^-1 and Gamma_0 is G itself. The next paragraph, cancelled by
hatching on the sheet, says he did not test this on the usual Siegel space and that if it failed there, Hodge's or Tate's
conjecture would be wrong. Margin: "If G_R is semi-simple without compact factors, is G(Z) always Zariski-dense in G?" He
asks Mumford whether, for the modular varieties of his Boulder talk, the image of gamma/Gamma in the Siegel modular variety
is algebraic or at least constructible.

## What agrees and what conflicts

- The Codex transcription and the Codex note H13-01#1 agree with the scans on every passage quoted above.
- The note's heading for the folder, "Steps the writer marks as unverified", undersells the letter: the letter withdraws a
  conjecture and replaces it by a necessary condition. Recorded in SA05 CORRECTIONS.
- (mine, not on the pages) The obstruction he meets is the one Griffiths' transversality explains (1968): the tautological
  family on such a gamma is a variation of Hodge structure only when gamma is hermitian symmetric of the right kind, and
  only then are the gamma/Gamma the algebraic varieties (Shimura varieties) he expects. His case "G(R) compact" is the
  extreme case where transversality fails. The condition on scan 20 is a Mumford-Tate-type genericity condition. His
  margin question is answered by Borel's density theorem (arithmetic subgroups are Zariski-dense when G has no Q-simple
  factor compact over R).
