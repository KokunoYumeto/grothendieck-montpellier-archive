## 1. What the block is

Dated "déc. 81 -- janv. 82" on its title sheet, the block holds three things:
1. **The deployment programme (002, 007-008, 010).** A stratified space is to be replaced by its *deployed strata* (manifolds with boundary), its *tubes of raccord* and their *junctions*, indexed by flags of the index poset, with boundary inclusions, fibrations and cartesian squares.
2. **Stratifications as maps to a poset (003-005).** A stratification is a map φ: X → I to a preordered set with its Alexandrov topology. He works out when φ is continuous, when it is open, when the poset is recovered, and when I carries the quotient topology. This gives his "stratifications fidèles", "partitions stratifiantes" and "stratifications strictes", and a criterion for the quotient topology.
3. **Conical neighbourhoods (012-016),** headed "Espaces stratifiés (étude par voisinages coniques)", circled I, sheets 1-3. This part contains:
   - the absolute cone as a mapping cylinder;
   - the collar ("partie bord");
   - strict conical neighbourhoods;
   - conical subspaces and compatibility.

009 is a typed accounting sheet of the Montpellier department (1979 deficit, research credits, D.E.A.), cancelled by one stroke; 001 is the IMAG cover.

### 003: specialization, Alexandrov spaces, pseudo-partitions (sheet "1")

**What he does.**
- The specialization preorder Ord(X) (x ≤ y iff x ∈ closure of {y}).
- The space Esp(I) of a preorder, with closed sets the down-sets and open sets the up-sets (the convention of @809).
- NB1: a map of preorders is continuous iff monotone.
- NB2: a topology comes from a preorder, necessarily its specialization, iff arbitrary unions of closed sets are closed.
- The functors Esp and Ord with Hom(Esp I, X) ≅ Hom(I, Ord X).
- The pseudo-partition φ: X → I, X_i^* = φ⁻¹(i), and when φ is continuous.
- When the preorder is determined by φ, and the definition of a faithful stratification.

**Re-derived:**
- NB1, "Plus gén.", the adjunction and the comparison maps (SA10-R150a-03). Ord∘Esp = id on preorders. The identity Esp(Ord X) → X is continuous because every open set is an up-set for specialization, and it is a homeomorphism iff X is Alexandrov. The ink writes the identities as id_𝓔 and id_𝓞, interchanged; this is his slip (SA10-K150a-03).
- NB2, and the NB of l.68: the subspace topology of an Alexandrov space is the Alexandrov topology of the induced preorder, since an up-set V of I′ is ↑V ∩ I′ (SA10-R150a-04).
- The continuity criterion and its sufficiency (SA10-R150a-05). φ is continuous iff the X^*_{ĩ} are open iff the X_i = ⋃_{j≤i} X_j^* are closed (the transcribed text has ⋃_{j≤i} X_j, true but trivial: SA10-K150a-08), provided I is finite or φ is locally finite. In the second case a down-set's preimage is locally a finite union of closed sets.
- The uniqueness analysis (SA10-R150a-06):
  - if X ≠ ∅, φ must be onto, since an index outside the image can be made isolated or a top element, both continuous;
  - for locally finite φ the quotient topology is Alexandrov, because locally only finitely many of the closed sets matter, so it comes from a unique preorder (l.91-92, read "un ordre unique de I" since 17 Nov; Michel Hua's project reads the same);
  - i ≤ j iff i lies in every closed J containing j;
  - the preorder is an order iff every {i} is locally closed iff the closure of {i} meets its smallest open neighbourhood only in {i} iff X^*_{ī} ∩ X^*_{ĩ} = X_i^*.

**Open on the page.** His margin (l.71-77) rewrites the question: which further conditions on the X_i^* give uniqueness? 004 answers it with openness and non-emptiness.

### 004: open stratifications, strict stratifications, the correspondence (sheets "3" and "4)")

**Left half.**
- "Condition plus forte": φ continuous, surjective and open.
- Openness ⟺ φ⁻¹(closure of B) = closure of φ⁻¹(B). Under local finiteness this reduces to singletons: closure of X_j^* = ⋃_{i≤j} X_i^* (SA10-R150a-07).
- The conditions:
  - 2°: I carries the final (quotient) topology;
  - 3°: closure of X_i^* = ⋃_{j≤i} X_j^*, i.e. φ open;
  - 1°: φ onto.
- NB: with 1° and 3° the preorder is recovered as inclusion of the closures (SA10-R150a-08).
- Such data are the same as a locally finite open equivalence relation. I is ordered iff distinct indices have distinct closures iff the strata are locally closed (SA10-R150a-08; the ink writes "les X_i", which are closed by hypothesis, and means X_i^*: SA10-K150a-04).
- "Stratification stricte": a locally finite increasing family of closed X_i with X_j nowhere dense in X_i for j < i and X_i ∩ X_j = ⋃_{k ≤ i, j} X_k.

**Right half.**
- The equivalent conditions for a locally finite partition (SA10-R150a-09):
  - a) every closure of X_i^* is saturated;
  - b) a preorder with closure of X_i^* = ⋃_{j≤i} X_j^*;
  - c) φ open onto the quotient.

  The step a) ⇒ c): the pieces missing an open U form a down-set, whose union is closed by local finiteness. The uniqueness of the preorder in b) (l.88) needs X_i^* ≠ ∅, which he struck from a) (SA10-K150a-05, a precision).
- He names such partitions "stratifiantes" (l.102).
- The correspondence (l.105-120), re-proved both ways (SA10-R150a-10). On one side, stratifying partitions with I ordered. On the other, locally finite families (X_i) with:
  - 1°, increasing closed sets covering X;
  - 2°, X_i ∩ X_j = ⋃_{k ≤ i, j} X_k;
  - 3°, X_i^* = X_i ∖ ⋃_{j<i} X_j ≠ ∅;
  - 4°, X_j nowhere dense in X_i for j < i.

  How the proof runs:
  - 4° follows from the density of X_i^* in its closure;
  - conversely, 4° makes X_i ∖ X_i^* a locally finite union of closed nowhere dense sets, hence nowhere dense, so X_i^* is dense in X_i;
  - the order is recovered: X_i ⊂ X_j forces X_i^* into some X_k with k ≤ i, j, and k < i is impossible.
- Margin NB: given 1°, 2° and 4°, 3° is equivalent to the weaker 3° bis (X_i ≠ ∅). A nonempty X_i cannot be a locally finite union of closed subsets nowhere dense in it (SA10-R150a-11).
- "Strat. stricte : fidèle et condition de densité : Partitions stratifiantes ⟺ strat. strictes" is the correspondence just proved.

**The moduli case (SA10-R150a-12).** The stratification of M̄_{g,n} by stable graph type is strict in this sense:
- 2° is EXIT_TOWER A.3;
- 4° holds because each closed stratum is irreducible and the lower ones are proper closed analytic subsets;
- 3° holds.

**Open on the page (l.64-68).** "Dans le cadre de la top. modérée, il faut voir comment pour toute partition par des parties loc. fermées modérées, il existe une plus petite stratification modérée [~~strictes~~] … la partition donnée." The relation to the given partition stays unread: the struck verb is majorent|engendre. Existence of some compatible stratification is the o-minimal stratification theorem (L. van den Dries, *Tame topology and o-minimal structures*, Cambridge Univ. Press 1998, Ch. 4; locator to be checked). The *least* one is the open part.

**Right half: the quotient criterion (I finite "pour simplifier").**
- The box from "Donc ∃ j∈J" to "J′ un" is crossed out (17 Nov).

### 012: the absolute conical neighbourhood (I, sheet "1)")

- **Definition.** A "voisinage conique (absolu)" of Y, or "cône sur Y", is a space T̄ ⊃ Y, "le tube" (reading of 17 Nov; Michel Hua's project agrees), with a closed Ṫ ("le bord du tube") and an action of the multiplicative monoid I = [0,1] such that:
  - a) λy = y on Y;
  - b) 0.T̄ ⊂ Y;
  - c) the square (2) of I×Ṫ ⊃ {0}×Ṫ → Y is cocartesian.
- So T̄ is the mapping cylinder C(f) of f: Ṫ → Y, f(x) = 0.x, called "cône de f".
- **Re-derived (SA10-R150a-23):**
  - the margin's homotopy: λ ↦ λ·t runs from id at λ = 1 to a retraction onto Y at λ = 0, fixing Y;
  - the NB: 0.Ṫ ⊂ Y and c) give b), since every point off Y is λx with x ∈ Ṫ and 0.(λx) = 0.x;
  - the "* Cela signifie" margin: the pushout topology;
  - "Ṫ est connu quand on connaît l'opération": Ṫ = T̄ ∖ ⋃_{λ<1} λT̄ in C(f);
  - (5): T̊ = T̄ ∖ Ṫ = ⋃_{λ<1} λT̄.
- The situation reads (Y ↪ T̄ ↩ Ṫ, I×T̄ − T̄); the arrows were corrected on 17 Nov.
- The cone keeps Ṫ and f. Those are the data the 1-truncated category 𝓕 of 151/35 loses (151/39-40, S²). Here S² = D² ∪_{S¹} C(S¹ → pt), the first-pass note R45.

### 015: strict conical neighbourhoods

- **Definitions.** (11) X^* = X ∖ T̊ = (X ∖ T̄) ∪ Ṫ, "c'est une partie fermée de X". The Proposition: for a given λ ∈ ]0,1[, the following are equivalent:
  - a) Ṫ is bord in X^*;
  - b) some homeomorphism of X carries (T̄, Ṫ) to (T̄_λ, Ṫ_λ);
  - b′) the same, the identity on T̄_λ′;
  - c) T̄ = λT̄′ for a conical neighbourhood T̄′, compatibly.

  "Strict" is defined by these. Corollaries: strict ⇒ Ṫ is the frontier of T̄; every T̄_λ (λ < 1) is strict. Margin: better to define strict neighbourhoods as germs of larger ones (1+ε)T̄.
- **Refuted in the example of 013 (SA10-R150a-27):**
  - T̊ is not open in X, so X^* is not closed (SA10-K150a-16).
  - Take μ = 0.6. T̄_μ satisfies c) with λ = μ (T̄′ = T̄).
  - It fails b): a homeomorphism of X fixes the branch point (1,0), which lies in T̄_μ but not in T̄_{μ²} (μ² < 1/2).
  - So c ⇒ b and Corollary 2 fail (SA10-K150a-17).
- **Repair.** With T̄ closed and T̊ open in X, c ⇒ b holds: reparametrize radially inside T̄ by a homeomorphism of [0,1] sending λ to λ², equal to the identity near 1, extend by the identity outside, and glue by the pasting lemma. Corollary 2 then follows.
- **Open.** a ⇒ b needs the exterior collar to be glued to the radial structure. The page gives no proof (first-pass note R15). His margin's germ definition is the natural home for the hypothesis "T̊ open".

### 016: conical subspaces and compatibility (sheet "3)")

- **Definition (12).** T̄′ ⊂ T̄ is a conical subspace if it is stable under the homotheties and is, for this action, a conical neighbourhood "de Y". The ink has Y. The description below it uses Y′ = T̄′ ∩ Y, so (12b) should read Y′ (@817; SA10-K150a-18).
- **The NB, checked (SA10-R150a-28).**
  - Ṫ′ = T̄′ ∩ Ṫ and T̄′_λ = T̄′ ∩ T̄_λ;
  - T̄′ is given by a closed Ṫ′ ⊂ Ṫ and a Y′ ⊂ Y with Y′ ⊃ 0.Ṫ′, as T̄′ = Im(I×Ṫ′) ∪ Y′. Its preimage in Y ⨿ I×Ṫ is closed when Y′ is closed, so the restricted quotient map gives T̄′ its cone topology.
  - The margin's special sub-cones, T̄′ ∩ Y = 0.Ṫ′, correspond to the closed Ṫ′ when f(Ṫ′) is closed, for instance Ṫ compact.
  - The margin word for them reads "strict" to me and to Michel Hua's project (17 Nov), not "pur". It then shares its name with the strict neighbourhoods of 015, a different notion.
- **Compatibility.** A conical structure is compatible with closed T̄_i if they are conical. T̄ is compatible with a closed X′ if X′ ∩ T̄ is conical, and strictly compatible if T̄ is strict and some collar of Ṫ in X^* is compatible with X′^*. This continues on 017.
