### 009 (④): the group presentation and the fibres E₂(α)
**The NB (l.14):** "δ₁(x) ne peut être fixé par ρ (donc par 𝔖₃) que si x = δ₀p₀(x) (condition néc. et suff.)". It
follows from the relations alone (R151a-10):
- If ρδ₁x = δ₁x, then p₁₂ρ²δ₁x = p₁₂δ₁x gives x = σx, and p₁₂ρδ₁x = p₁₂δ₁x gives δ₀p₀x = σx = x.
- Conversely, δ₁δ₀ is 𝔖₃-fixed.

**The box (l.15-21):** "δ₀p₀(x) = 1, σ(x).x = 1, p₁₂(z)p₂₃(z)p₃₁(z) = 1 pour z ∈ E₂ (une relation supplémentaire par
orbite sous 𝔖₃ qui ne rencontre pas δ₁(E₁))". This is the edge-path presentation of a one-vertex system. Michel Hua's
project reads it as normalization, inversion and Čech cocycle.

**Re-derived (R151a-11):**
- For z = δ₁(x) the relation reads σ(x)·δ₀p₀(x)·x = 1. That is the displayed line l.20, and a consequence of the first
  two.
- Along an 𝔖₃-orbit the relations are cyclic conjugates (ρ) or inverses (σᵢ, through p₁₂σ₃ = σp₁₂).
- So one relation per orbit off δ₁(E₁) is right.
- For E = π₀ of the Čech nerve of a Galois covering with group G, E₁ = G and E₂ = G², and the presentation is the
  multiplication table, so it gives G.
- The sheet makes no comparison with a fundamental group (first-pass note B02).

**The fibres (l.22-37):** "F × F × F" with σ₁, p₁₂, p₁₃; "F_α ⊂ F × F", pr₀; "(x,y) ×_{\unsure{x}} (x′,y′) ∼ (x, y, y′)";
and, from the second pass, the label of the overbrace:
\unsure{Π|π}₀(F_α ×_F F_α) = p₁₂⁻¹(α) ∩ p₁₃⁻¹(α) = E₂(α) ∋ δ₁(α) = δ₁σ(α), "stable par σ₁".
- So E_n is read as π₀ of the fibre powers of a covering, and **E₂(α) is defined** as the components of the fibre
  square of F_α over F.
- His argument: "Prenons α ≠ δ₁ [the base point], alors δ₂ ∉ p₁₂⁻¹(α), les pts fixes par σ₁ sont dans image δ₁(E₁),
  mais p₁₂(δ₁(x)) = p₁₃(δ₁(x)) = σx, donc δ₁(x) ∈ E₂(α) ⟺ σx = α i.e. x = σα, et δ₁σ(α) joue le rôle de
  δ_{\unsure{2|1}} = δ₁(α)".
- In the tuple model this holds: E₂(α) = {δ₁σ(α)} (R151a-12). The overbrace's "δ₁(α)" stands for δ₁(σα) = δ₁σ(α).

**Wrong in general (R151a-13, K151a-02).** "Les pts fixes par σ₁ sont dans image δ₁(E₁)" is a property of the tuple
model, and of Galois coverings, where E₂(α) is a single point. It fails for π₀ of the Čech nerve of a degree-3 covering
with monodromy 𝔖₃:
- E₁ has two points;
- the fibre E₂(α) over the off-diagonal α has two points, both fixed by σ₁;
- one of them, the class of triples with distinct entries, is not in δ₁(E₁) (computed).

So the count of fixed points that his argument needs, and the group presentation built on it, need the covering to be
Galois or the systems to be tuple-like.

### 012 (2): Hyp 2 and its general form
**Hyp 2.** For α, β, the U_γ → U_α × U_β indexed by the objects of A_{/α×β} (product in Â) form a covering family.
"[Idem pour une suite finie (non vide) α₀,…,α_n (n ∈ ℕ^{~~*~~}) …]".
- The struck asterisk and "(non vide)" make n ≥ 0: sequences of n+1 terms.
- n = 0 is Hyp 1′ and n = 1 is Hyp 2.

**"Mais Hyp 2 pour n = 1 implique-t-il Hyp 2 pour n ≥ 1 ?"** He answers by induction, and the induction is right
(R151a-16). Write the family U_{γ′} → U_γ × U_{α_{n+1}} → (U_{α₀} × ⋯ × U_{α_n}) × U_{α_{n+1}}, with v: γ′ → γ × α_{n+1} and
u: γ → α₀ × ⋯ × α_n. Then:
- the first arrows cover by Hyp 2 for (γ, α_{n+1});
- the second family covers because a covering family times an object covers in a topos;
- the composite of covering families covers, and every composite is an object of A_{/∏α_i}.

The labels φ(v) and φ(u) × id agree with "(v parcourant …, et u …)" (second pass).

### 013-014 (3-4): Hyp 2′ and the reformulation through φ_!
**Hyp 2′** is Hyp 2 in the slices: for α₁ → α₀ ← α₂, the U_γ over the commutative squares cover
U_{α₁} ×_{U_{α₀}} U_{α₂}. On 013 l.6 the target 𝒳_{/α₀} abbreviates 𝒳_{/U_{α₀}}.

**With φ_!: Â → 𝒳 the extension commuting with small lim→,** Hyp 1, 2 and 2′ "se reformulent joliment" as follows:
- a) φ_!(e_Â) → e_𝒳 est épi;
- b) φ_!(α×β) → φ_!α × φ_!β est épi;
- c) φ_!(α ×_{α₀} β) → φ_!α ×_{φ_!α₀} φ_!β est épi.

This is right (R151a-17):
- φ_!(e) is the colimit of U over A, and ⊔U_α → φ_!(e) is epi;
- φ_!(α×β) is the colimit over A_{/α×β}, whose image in U_α × U_β is that of ⊔U_γ.

**"Un cas particulièrement agréable …":** "c'est quand φ_! est exact à gauche, i.e. qu'il correspond à un morphisme de
topos f: 𝒳 → Â par la formule φ_! = f^*".
- This is Diaconescu's theorem, stated without a name. Michel Hua's project gives Diaconescu, "Change of base for
  toposes with generators", J. Pure Appl. Algebra 6 (1975), 191-218 (theorem number: locator to be checked).
- Then a)-c) are isomorphisms.

**"Je présume que l'inverse est vrai également. Oui -- et c'est essentiellement évident, en écrivant canoniquement …
F = lim→_{A/F} α":** if a)-c) are isomorphisms, φ_! is left exact (R151a-18).
- Colimits are universal in Â and in 𝒳, so φ_! preserves the terminal object, binary products and fibre products of
  arbitrary presheaves once it does so for representables.
- Terminal object and pullbacks give all finite limits.

### 014-015 (4-5): b′), c′) and the single axiom (Hyp)
**"On voit de la même façon que b) et c) impliquent b′) … c′) …"** for arbitrary F, G, H in Â. This is right
(R151a-19); 018, in the next block, carries his computation of φ_!(F × G).
- b′) follows by covering φ_!G and φ_!H with the U_α, U_β, and Hyp 2.
- c′) needs one more step:
  - The kernel pair of ⊔U → φ_!F is the equivalence relation generated by the arrows over F, and in a topos that is the
    union of finite zigzags.
  - A pair (s, t) joined by a zigzag is covered by spans γ → α, γ → β over F, by induction on the length.
  - Each backward step is shortened by c), applied to a cospan in A.

**"et de là, plus généralement, la forme équivalente unique de Hyp 1, 2, 2′ : (Hyp) Pour tout diagramme fini (F_i)
dans Â, la flèche canonique φ_!(lim← F_i) → lim← φ_!(F_i) est épi. (Il suffit d'ailleurs de le postuler quand les F_i
sont dans A, et dans les trois cas particuliers I = •, • •, ↘↙, correspondants aux axiomes Hyp 1, Hyp 2, Hyp
\unsure{2|1}′.)"**

- **False as stated** (R151a-20, K151a-04; this is edition matter #g673, confirmed on the scan on 17 Nov). Take A = BC₂
  and φ(*) = pt, so φ_! is the orbit set.
  - a), b′) and c′) hold.
  - For the two equivariant maps id and the translation of the regular C₂-set, the equalizer is ∅. The induced maps on
    the orbit set pt are equal, so the comparison is ∅ → pt.
  - The first pass found this example (its note C06).
  - Michel Hua's project takes the reduction to I = ∅, • • and the cospan as correct. It cites Shulman's covering-flat
    functors (M. Shulman, "Exact completions and small sheaves", Theory Appl. Categ. 27 (2012), no. 7, §4, Cor. 4.11)
    and says that Shulman's reduction is "le même découpage, à ceci près que Shulman prend des égaliseurs là où la page
    prend des produits fibrés". The difference is exactly what fails (K151a-11).
- **What is true** (R151a-21). Hyp 1, 2 and 2′ hold if and only if the comparison is epi for every finite diagram
  indexed by a finite forest: ∅, single points, discrete pairs, cospans, and trees with edges in any direction.
  - A limit over a tree is an iterated pullback.
  - Its comparison factors as a c′)-epi followed by a base change of an epi, so it is epi.
  - A cycle, the parallel pair first, is not covered.
  - With the parallel pair added (Shulman's local pre-equalizers; Mac Lane-Moerdijk, *Sheaves in Geometry and Logic*,
    1992, VII.8-VII.9, "filtering" functors; locators to be checked), (Hyp) holds for all finite diagrams.
- **The slip "I = •" (l.29):** a one-object diagram gives an identity comparison. Hyp 1 is a), the empty diagram (014
  l.5), so the first shape should be ∅ (K151a-05).
- **The closing question (l.35):** "Mais cette hypothèse peut-elle être vérifiée, sans que φ_! soit $=$
  ex.\textsuperscript{t} à gauche ?" The "=" is his "même"; the last word is "gauche", not Michel Hua's project's
  "Quand ?".
  - The answer is no (R151a-22).
  - For a finite D with limit M, add two vertices with value M and all cone legs. The (Hyp) comparison of the enlarged
    diagram is the diagonal of c: L(M) → lim L(D).

**What the gap means.** Hyp 1, 2 and 2′ are strictly weaker than (Hyp), and the weakness matters for a hypercovering
(R151a-24).
- The symmetric nerve of a group, E*_n = G^n/G (n points, diagonal action), satisfies the analogue of Hyp 1, 2 and 2′
