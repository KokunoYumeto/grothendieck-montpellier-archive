**The frame (056-057, read in 150_044-057).** He restarts with an ordered space Σ and three kinds of condition:
- ⓐ topological: the target map b = pr₂: Σ(1) → Σ(0) is proper and a local bordant immersion;
- ⓑ set-theoretic: Σ_{≤x} is finite and totally ordered;

The relative structure of Σ^<(1) over Σ through b (for each x, the finite chain Σ_{<x}) reconstructs every Σ^<(d) with

**The block answers four questions:**
1. What must be added to that relative structure to recover the order on Σ? A source map s = p₁ with a cartesian square

4. How does it extend to a topological category, and what is its π₀? §13 (067-071). There the index set comes back as

## 1. Page by page

### 058 (verso of 24): what the relative structure lacks

- **The converse question.** Σ carries a "stratification locale cylindrique ordonnée":
  - Σ′(1) is the space of codimension-1 strata (germs);
  - b: Σ′(1) → Σ sends a germ to its centre;
  - Σ′(2) is the space of pairs of germs B, B′ at x with {B} < {B′}.

  The margin draws the tower Σ^<(2) ⇉ Σ^<(1) → Σ with the two maps b′, s′ and then b.
  - These are the cofinal faces, which keep the last vertex. In flag terms, b′(x,y,z) = (y,z) and s′(x,y,z) = (x,z).
    The non-cofinal face p₂(x,y,z) = (x,y) is missing.
  - So the relative structure is the semi-simplicial space of flags with its last face map removed: the décalage of
    the nerve (L. Illusie, *Complexe cotangent et déformations II*, LNM 283, Springer 1972, ch. VI; locator to be
    checked). That is my reading; he does not use the word.
- **(36):** "il faut donc de plus se donner une application continue s = p₁: Σ^<(1) → Σ qui soit injective … sur chaque
  fibre b^{-1}(x) … (i.e. (s,b) inj.) et d'image ∌ x (i.e. Im(s,b) ∩ diag = ∅)". **Checked (R150e-01):** both "i.e."
  are equivalences. The first holds because b is constant on b^{-1}(x); the second says s(u) ≠ b(u).
- **The struck draft:**
  - "(37) Σ^<(2) →φ₂ Σ*(1) (↪ Σ×Σ)", with the NB "la donnée de φ₂|p₂ équivaut à celle des deux composés pr∘φ ; pr∘φ";
  - the margin "inutile de se donner p₂, il est déterminé (v p₁), cf pg suivante";
  - a struck (38) into Σ₀×Σ₀×Σ₀.

  **Checked (R150e-02):** a map into Σ*(1) ↪ Σ×Σ is determined by its two components. Existence is the condition that
  the pair lands in the image, which is what 059 imposes.

### 059 (sheet 25): the cartesian square

- **(37)-(38):** there exists p₂: Σ^<(2) → Σ^<(1) with b∘p₂ = s∘b′ and p₁∘p₂ = p₁∘s′. "Ce qui détermine p₂ de façon
  unique, puisque (p₁,b) … est mono."
  - **Re-proved (R150e-03):** (p₁,b)∘p₂ = (p₁∘s′, p₁∘b′), and (p₁,b) is injective.
  - This also decides the doubtful second component on 062 l.2: it must be p₁∘b′, the only type-correct reading.
- **"De plus, on doit exiger que le premier carré (38) soit cartésien, et que p₁ … est transversal p.r. à l'immersion
  bordante b (ce qui est l'unique condition de nature non purement algébrique…)."**
  - The cartesian square says Σ^<(2) = Σ^<(1) ×_{b,Σ,p₁} Σ^<(1): flags are composable pairs. That is the Segal
    condition at level 2.
  - On the role of transversality, see 064.
- **(39):** x ≺ y ⟺ ∃u ∈ Σ^<(1), x = p₁u, y = b u. (The number (39) is used again on 061.)

### 060: the order

- **Transitivity, written out:**
  1. take u, v with p₁u = x, bu = y = p₁v, bv = z;
  2. by the cartesian square, (∃!) d ∈ Σ^<(2) with b′d = v and p₂d = u;
  3. w = s′d has p₁w = p₁p₂d = x and bw = b s′d = b b′d = bv = z.
- **Re-proved (R150e-04).** The step b∘s′ = b∘b′ is a structural identity: both cofinal faces keep the last vertex, and
  it is built into the relative structure of 058. The page uses it without comment, and Michel Hua's project notes the
  same.
- **Uniqueness of d** follows from the cartesian square, so "∃!" is right whatever the ink of its dot (open reading,
  l.16).
- **Irreflexivity:** x ⊀ x because Im(p₁,b) misses the diagonal. Antisymmetry then follows, so ≺ is a strict order.
- **Consequences:** Σ^<(1) is the graph of ≺, and Σ^<(2) is the space of flags x ≺ y ≺ z.
- **The small drawing "w : x ⤳ z":** the composite arrow.

### 061 (sheet 26): the face maps and the Scholie

- **(39), the five maps:**
  - (x≺y) ↦ x by p₁ = s, and ↦ y by b;
  - (x≺y≺z) ↦ (x,y) by p₂, ↦ (x,z) by s′, ↦ (y,z) by b′.

  The last two rows carry his corrections (y→x, x→y). **Checked (R150e-05):** the corrected rows satisfy b s′ = b b′,
  p₁ p₂ = p₁ s′ and b p₂ = p₁ b′. With the uncorrected rows, s′ = (y,z) and b′ = (x,z), both equations of (38) would
  fail: p₁ s′ = y ≠ x = p₁ p₂, and p₁ b′ = x ≠ y = b p₂.
- **Scholie:**
  - 1°) a space Σ with a "(pré)cylindrique" stratification, with Σ₁ the space of codimension-1 strata, such that
    b: Σ^<(1) → Σ is a proper bordant immersion. The margin names it: "stratification cylindrique loc^t. tot^t
    ordonnée" (the "tot^t" restored on 17 Nov).
  - 2°) a closed relative total order Σ*(2) ⊂ Σ^<(1) ×_Σ Σ^<(1) on the codimension-1 strata through each point.
  - 3°) a continuous p₁ = s with: a) (s,b) injective and off the diagonal; b) (062) …
- **Checked (R150e-06):** the relative order on b^{-1}(z) is ≺ on Σ_{<z}. The pair ((x,z),(y,z)) lies in the
  relative order iff x ≺ y, in both directions, by the cartesian square and the injectivity of (p₁,b). So 2°) is the
  set-level form of 063 a).

### 062: condition b), the NB, and the restatement

- **b):** (p₁∘s′, p₁∘b′): Σ^<(2) → Σ×Σ factors through (p₁,b) as p₂, with the square "Cartésien" and p₁ "transversal
  à [l'imm]ersion-but b".
- **NB:** "Si Σ est séparé … et Σ^<(1) →b Σ propre, il en résulte que Σ^<(1) → Σ×Σ est propre donc un plongement
  fermé." **Checked (R150e-07):**
  - pr₂∘(s,b) = b is proper and pr₂ is separated, so (s,b) is proper;
  - an injective proper map is a closed embedding (N. Bourbaki, *Topologie générale*, ch. I, §10; locator to be
    checked).
- **Margin:** "cette condition de transversalité est peut-être conséquence des autres…" **Decided (R150e-10,
  K150e-09):**
  - Under 1°) of 063 (s fibrant, locally trivial) it holds in every category. Over a trivializing U the map is
    U × F → U, and the preimage of a collared face is that face times F, collared by the collar times F.
  - Without 1°) it is independent of the others. Take s₁₂(t, y) = (t, t·y) on [0,∞) × ℝ → [0,∞) × ℝ, over the face
    {t = 0}: the preimage of the face is a face, the cartesian square and the algebra hold, but no choice of collars
    makes the map a product. The face is crushed to a point, and the collar layers are mapped injectively.
  - This explains a choice on 070-071: there 1°) is assumed and the transversality conditions are no longer listed.
- **"De façon équivalente … un espace topologique séparé Σ muni d'une relation d'ordre x ≼ y satisfaisant les
  conditions suivantes".** A struck first draft follows. The live list is on 063.

### 063 (sheet 27): the ordered-space form, and the three cases

- **The conditions:**
  - a) every Σ_{<x} is totally ordered;
  - b) b is a proper immersion, "donc les Σ_{<x} = b^{-1}({x}) sont finis". **Checked (R150e-08):** compact and discrete
    give finite.
  - c) the germs at x of the images of the (Σ^<(1), z_α) form a transversal family of bordant subspaces;
  - d) the source is transversal to b.
- **Checked (R150e-09):** this list is equivalent to the Scholie, at the set level and topologically.
  - Σ^<(2) ⊂ Σ³ is closed (Σ^<(1) is closed by the NB). It is homeomorphic to Σ^<(1) ×_Σ Σ^<(1) through
    (x,y,z) ↦ ((x,y),(y,z)).
  - The relative order of 2°) is the image of Σ^<(2) under (s′, b′), which is closed.
- **The letter in d)** was \unsure{s|b}. On the native scan it is a bold letter with no bowl at the foot, so not b
  (R150e-35). "b transversal to b" would say nothing, and 059 l.36 and 062 l.15 state the condition for p₁ = s. So d)
  is about the source map, written s or p (K150e-05).
- **The three cases:**
  - 1°) equisingular: s fibrant (proper);
  - 2°) nonsingular: Σ is a manifold with boundary b(Σ*(1));
  - 3°) both together: s is a proper fibration whose fibres are manifolds with boundary, and the bundle of boundaries is
    the image of s′: (x≺y≺z) ↦ (x≺z).
- **The margin:** "c'est sans doute conséquence de la conjonction des deux conditions 1°), 2°)". **Refuted in Top,
  true in PL and smoothly (R150e-14, K150e-07).** This is the same answer the reading 150_044-057 gave to the
  051 margin (R150d-25, K150d-09). I use its example.
  - **The counterexample.** Let H be a homology 3-sphere with nontrivial π₁. The suspension ΣH is a compact
    non-manifold, and ΣH × ℝ ≅ S⁴ × ℝ by the double suspension theorem. Take:
    - Σ₀ = ℝ;
    - Σ₀₁ = ΣH × ℝ, a 5-manifold, with s the projection;
    - Σ₁ = Σ₀₁ × [0,1), with b the boundary inclusion.

    Every condition of the Scholie holds (Σ^<(2) = ∅), and so do 1°) (s is locally trivial and proper) and 2°). But
    the fibres ΣH are not manifolds, so 3°) fails.
  - **In PL, 3°) holds.** If F × U is a PL manifold, the link of (p, u) is the join of Lk(p, F) with a sphere. That join
    is a PL sphere or ball only if Lk(p, F) is one, so F is a PL manifold, and likewise with boundary (C. Rourke and B.
    Sanderson, *Introduction to piecewise-linear topology*, Springer 1972, ch. 2; locator to be checked). Smoothly, a
    locally trivial fibration of manifolds with corners has fibres that are manifolds with corners.
  - **The boundary part of 3°)** then needs the face decomposition ∂Σ_ij = s^{-1}(∂Σ_i) ∪ (vertical faces):
    - s^{-1}(∂Σ_i) = ⋃_{k<i} Σ_kij by the cartesian square;
    - the vertical faces are ⋃_{i<k<j} Σ_ikj = Im s′, by the 067 margin, which holds in PL (below).

  - the fibre of p over (x≺y) is the fibre of s over y;
  - so its boundary is the image of Σ^<(3) under the face that deletes the third vertex, as the margin says.

### 065 (sheet 28): §12, the Récapitulation

- **The data:**
  - a) spaces Σ_i (i ∈ I);
  - b) spaces Σ_ij (i < j);
  - c) maps s_ij: Σ_ij → Σ_i and b_ij: Σ_ij → Σ_j.
- **The margin:** the Scholie's structure plus a continuous strictly increasing φ: Σ → I, "telle que ∀x ∈ Σ, φ|Σ_{<x}
  soit injective (donc la relation d'ordre de Σ_{<x} soit induite par celle de I_{<i})".
  - **Checked (R150e-15):** a strictly increasing map from a chain is injective and an order embedding. So the
    injectivity is automatic once Σ_{<x} is totally ordered, as Scholie a) requires (K150e-16, a precision). The "i" of
    the margin is φ(x).
- **The conditions:**
  - a) b_ij is a closed embedding ("plongement", replacing "immersion"; "bordant" struck);
  - b) Σ_{i,k} ∩ Σ_{j,k} = ∅ if i, j are not comparable (NB i, j < k);
  - c) Σ_ijk := Σ_ik ∩ Σ_jk ⊂ Σ_k, and "dans Σ_jk", Σ_ijk = b_ij^{-1}(Σ_ij).
- **b) and @836.** This is the disjointness for noncomparable indices that the 045 margins lacked (N01, @836,
  confirmed in 150_044-057). The NB margin, "La condition b) équivaut à celle que dans l'ens ordonné Σ = ∐Σ_i, les
  ensembles Σ_{<x} sont tot^t ordonnés", is **re-proved (R150e-18)** for an ordered Σ:
  - ⇐: two lower elements over incomparable i, j would be comparable, hence so would i and j, since φ is strictly
    increasing;
  - ⇒: for y, y′ < x over i < j, c) puts the arrow y′ → x over Σ_ij. So some y″ ∈ Σ_i has y″ < y′ < x, and the
    injectivity of φ on Σ_{<x} gives y″ = y.
- **c) as written is not type-correct (R150e-34).** b_ij^{-1}(Σ_ij) is Σ_ij itself (b_ij is the identification of
  Σ_ij with a closed part of Σ_j, l.22), and Σ_ij does not lie in Σ_jk. The ink reads b_ij for two readers (17 Nov, and
  Hua batch-04 p. 64). Meant is s_jk^{-1}(Σ_ij), the cartesian square of (41) (K150e-01; first-pass R04; Michel Hua's
  project's modern reading has the same footnote). The relation is "=", read on 17 Nov.
- **c) is Mather's second control condition.** If Σ_ij is the level ρ_i = ε inside X_j and s_jk is the tubular
  retraction π_j, then ρ_i∘π_j = ρ_i gives s_jk^{-1}(Σ_ij) = Σ_ik ∩ Σ_jk. This is the condition the reading 150_072-084
  found behind the cartesian concatenation (3) of 040 (its R150f, "ρ_i ∘ r_j = ρ_i").
- **"On a donc, pour i<j<k, un diagramme commutatif hexagonal (41)."** **Refuted as a consequence, refined (R150e-16,
  K150e-02).**
  - The upper square (corner) and the lower right square (concatenation, c)) are cartesian by definition.
  - But the **left square**, s_ik = s_ij∘s_jk on Σ_ijk, follows from nothing in a)-c).
  - **Counterexample.** Take any deployment and replace s_02 by g∘s_02, with g a homeomorphism of Σ_0 that is not the
    identity. Every condition of 065-066 holds, since each concerns one map or the subsets of one space. But for
    x = s_01(u), y = b_01(u) = s_12(v), z = b_12(v), the arrow of Σ_02 over z starts at g(x) ≠ x. So ≺ is not
    transitive, and Σ_{<z} is not totally ordered.
  - **The missing axiom** is the commutativity of the left square of (41). It is the indexed form of the second
    equation of (38), p₁∘p₂ = p₁∘s′, and it is Mather's first control condition π_i∘π_j = π_i.
  - In the functor Σ on Drap(I) of 039-043 the left square commutes by functoriality: both composites are the map
    for (i) ⊂ (i,j,k). It was lost when the Récapitulation cut the data down to Σ_i and Σ_ij.
  - The literature for control data: J. Mather, "Notes on topological stability" (1970), Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 49
    (2012), 475-506, §7; C. G. Gibson, K. Wirthmüller, A. du Plessis and E. Looijenga, *Topological stability of
    smooth mappings*, LNM 552, Springer 1976, ch. II. Locators to be checked.
- **The arrows** marked ↪ are closed inclusions "(bordantes)", "cf plus bas", and those marked → will be fibrant and
  proper in the equisingular case.

### 067 (sheet 29): the cases, and §13
