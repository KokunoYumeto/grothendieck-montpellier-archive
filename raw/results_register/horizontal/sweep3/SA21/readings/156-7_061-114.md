## 2. Scans 62-69 (pp. 61-68): supports

**Scan 62.**
- Σ_𝓕 (≪-closed parts of 𝓕 stable under sups) and Σ_M (≪-closed parts of M) correspond. cosupp A = {G | G ∥ A} lies in Σ_𝓕 by At D₀, and supp A = cosupp cosupp A.
- His margin lists the equivalent forms of A ∥ B, and asks whether it is also equivalent to supp A ∩ supp B = ∅ ("ça l'implique en tous cas").
- The implication holds: A ⊂ cosupp B = ∁ supp B gives supp A ⊂ ∁ supp B (SA21-R7b-04).
- **The converse, for all A and B, holds exactly when Σ is Boolean.** The same is true of the converse of (x) on scan 64, A ∩ ∁B = ∅ ⇒ A ⊂ B, which is the same statement with B replaced by ∁B.
  - "Disjoint implies orthogonal" gives the orthomodular law (for a ≤ b put d = a ∨ (b ∧ a′); then b ∧ d′ = 0, so b ≤ d).
  - It then gives a = (a ∧ b) ∨ (a ∧ b′) for all a and b, since a ∧ (a ∧ b)′ is disjoint from b. So every pair commutes, and an orthomodular lattice in which all pairs commute is Boolean (G. Kalmbach, *Orthomodular lattices*, Academic Press 1983, ch. 1 §3; locator to be checked).
  - Re-derived as the check of his question (SA21-R7b-04).

**Scans 63-64.** The properties (i)-(x): a Galois closure, Σ°_𝓕 a complete lattice with the involutive anti-isomorphism ∁, A ∩ ∁A = ∅_Σ, and (x) A ⊂ B ⇒ A ∩ ∁B = ∅. Already checked (SA21-R7-19).
- His NB: the converse of (x) is "pas clair", and the union of two supports is not in general a support ("il est faux"). The intuitive reason he gives: a figure contained in the union of the supports of two figures need not be contained in either, "même dans le cas des multistrates, et même dans le cas des lieux (quand on n'est pas dans un atelier ensembliste tout au moins…)".

**Scan 65.**
- He doubts distributivity, "cela a tendance à être vrai". Settled since: false in general (156-7/84, SA21-R7-25; SA21-R7-21), and reader C's O₆ shows that even the orthomodular law fails.
- His margin: "Si A ⊂ 𝓕 est fermée pour ≤, on a cosupp_𝓕(A) ∩ L = cosupp_L(A ∩ L)". Codex has L with alternative M; what holds is the M form, cosupp_𝓕(A) ∩ M = cosupp_M(A ∩ M), because disjointness from a figure is tested on its strata, which a ≤-closed A contains. The L form needs At L2. Decided by content, not on the image (SA21-K7b-03; check SA21-R7b-05).
- "Cette fois, le plus petit élément de Σ°_M est bien la partie vide de M" (no X with X ∥ X).

**Scans 66-67.**
- Disj_Λ (disjointness tested on omb_Λ) gives cosupp A = cosupp omb_Λ(A), omb_Λ(A) = Λ ∩ A for ≪-closed A, and supp A = supp(omb_Λ A).
- The bijection Σ°_𝓕 ≅ Σ°_Λ, A ↦ A ∩ Λ, with inverse S ↦ {F | omb_Λ F ⊂ S}, respects inclusion and complements.
- I re-derived the inverse. It uses At D₀ as well as Disj_Λ: an X ∈ omb_Λ F with F ∈ supp S is disjoint from every Y ∈ Λ that is disjoint from S (SA21-R7b-06).
- With Λ = L this needs At L2: Σ_𝓕 ≅ Σ_M ≅ Σ_L. His margin: the notion of support then "ne dépend que de L et de la relation ∥_L".

**Scan 68.** When ∥_L is "≠", Σ_L = P(L), supp A = omb(A), and both doubts resolve. That this case is exactly the canonical Boolean case was the first reading's SA21-R7-20.

**Scan 69, struck in full** (raw .tex: "a long oblique cancellation"). A first draft of the realization by supports:
- P ⊂ L, "P comme 'points'", with (a) x ≠ y ⇒ x ∥ y on P, and (b) disjointness detected on P ("At P2", which implies At L2).
- Then Σ ≃ P(P), compatible with ∁, and "les perplexités s'évanouissent". His margin: "Un peu bref !".
- The statement holds (Disj_P with Λ = P, then SA21-R7-20; SA21-R7b-07). He rewrote it as the Proposition of scan 81.

## 3. Scans 70-73 (pp. 69-72, 25 June): the Stratos of an ordered set

**Scan 70.** "En dehors du cas quasi-ensembliste, je suspecte que la notion de support ... est cornée" (Codex; alternatives "cornue", "connerie"). His typical example is the stratos of an ordered set I:
- M₀ is the points, M₁ the segments [a, b] with a < b;
- ≤ is incidence and ≪ inclusion;
- X ≍ Y in three cases: (a) X = Y; (b) one is an end of the other; (c) ∂X ∪ ∂Y totally ordered "et X ∩ Y est vide, ou réduit à un point, d'où" the cases α (a ≤ b < c ≤ d) and β (a < b < c, sharing b).

**A precision** (SA21-K7b-04; SA21-R7b-08).
- Read literally, "réduit à un point" makes a point x interior to [a, b] compatible with it.
- Then x ≪ [a, b] without x ≤ [a, b], which breaks Prat 6.
- His "d'où" lists the intended cases, α and β, where the intersection is empty or a common end. With α/β the pre-atelier axioms hold.
- ≪° here: a point is an internal refinement of a segment iff it lies strictly inside. His margin defining ≪° is partly struck and was not decided at 200 dpi (open).
- "Tous les axiomes sont satisfaits, à l'exception de AtL1", which holds iff every ]a, b[ is non-empty. Checked, including At L2 for a partially ordered I: there, two incomparable points are neither compatible nor disjoint.

**Scan 71.**

**Scans 72-73.**
- 1-strata [a, b] with ]a, b[ = ∅ force supports relative to a chain Δ = {a₁ < … < a_n}: the Boolean algebra on the "points" {a_i}, [a_i, a_{i+1}].

## 7. Scans 102-114 (pp. 101-113, 26 June): the turn to |∘|

**Scan 102.** "J'ai envie de prendre ... comme relation primitive ... au lieu de ≍ ou de ∥, la relation X |∘| Y ⇔ X° ∥ Y° ... de façon à arriver à donner un sens raisonnable à X°, même dans des cas où ... elle serait vide."
