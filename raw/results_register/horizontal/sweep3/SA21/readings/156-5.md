## 1. Préadmissible and admissible families (scans 2-7)

- **156-5/2.** L a set, 𝓛 ⊂ P(L), |𝓛| = ⋃A, ∂A = ⋃{B ∈ 𝓛 | B ⊂ A}, A° = A ∖ ∂A. The inclusion must be strict (B ⊊ A), or else ∂A = A; the transcription has ⊂. Open reading SA21-K5-04: the ink may carry ⊊, or "B ≠ A".
  - Margin: "Voir variante 'indexée' formellement plus forte, voir GF VIII, pg 37", that is 156-8, scan 38.
- **Proposition 1.** The following are equivalent.
  - 1°) for x ∈ |𝓛|, 𝓛_x = {A | x ∈ A} has a smallest element A(x);
  - 2°) α) A ∩ B is the union of the members inside it, and β) every x lies in some A°;
  - 3°) γ) the A°, A ∈ 𝓛′ = {A | A° ≠ ∅}, partition |𝓛|; δ) every A is saturated; η) A° ⊂ B with A° ≠ ∅ ⇒ A ⊂ B.
  - Margin: δ, η may be replaced by δ′) A = ⋃_{B⊂A} B°.
  - **Proof (scan 3):** followed; it checks (SA21-R5-01).
  - **Computation (SA21-R5-01):** 1° ⇔ 2° ⇔ 3° ⇔ (γ + δ′) holds for all 65,536 families of subsets of a 4-point set.
- **Remarks (scan 4).**
  - 1°: β is automatic for artinian 𝓛. A decreasing sequence with non-empty intersection satisfies α but not β. Holds (SA21-R5-02).
  - 2°: "la condition η n'est pas surabondante", with a drawn example. His margin: "Exemple idiot — mais voir contre-exemple correct (avec I infini) GF VIII, pages 44, 45".
  - **His margin is right, and the drawn example cannot work** (SA21-R5-03, SA21-K5-05). For an artinian family, γ alone gives η. Take p ∈ A° ⊂ B, and E ⊂-minimal among the members with p ∈ E ⊂ B. Then p ∈ E°, so E = A by γ, and A ⊂ B.
  - The computation agrees: on ≤ 4 points no family satisfies γ and δ but not η. A counterexample needs an infinite family, which is what 156-8/45-46 is cited for.
- **Definition (scans 4-5).** Préadmissible: the conditions of Prop 1. Admissible: in addition A° ≠ ∅ for all A, so ∅ ∉ 𝓛; this is why 156-6/17 says 𝓕 ∋ ∅ is "jamais admissible".
- **Scans 5-6.** An admissible family is the same as an ordered set I, a part S ⊂ L and a surjection φ: S → I. With the order topology on I (closed sets = down-sets) and its inverse image on S, 𝓛 = {φ⁻¹(I_{≤i})}.
  - Closed, open and locally closed parts of S correspond to the closed, open and convex parts J of I.
  - For finite 𝓛, the A° are the atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by 𝓛. Its elements are the saturated sets: the "parties 𝓛-modérées ou 𝓛-constructibles".
  - Margin: "Les A ∈ 𝓛 sont les parties fermées irréductibles", of the preorder. Checked: x and y lie in the same atom iff 𝓛_x = 𝓛_y iff A(x) = A(y) (SA21-R5-04).
- **Scans 7-8.** Sub-families: Ψ ⊂ 𝓛 closed for inclusion. Then A°^(Ψ) = A°^(𝓛), and Ψ is admissible. Prop: a) Ψ closed in 𝓛 ⇔ b) Ψ admissible, |Ψ| saturated, R_Ψ induced by R_𝓛. His proof checks (SA21-R5-05).
  - Scholie: sub-families correspond to closed parts of 𝓛 or of |𝓛|.

## 2. Compatibility, the figures ensemblistes, C1-C3 (scans 8-12)

- **Proposition (8-10).** For admissible Φ and Ψ, the following are equivalent.
  - a) Φ ∪ Ψ admissible with Φ, Ψ closed in it; a′) both are sub-families of one admissible family;
  - b) Φ ∩ Ψ closed in both, and |Φ| ∩ |Ψ| = |Φ ∩ Ψ|;
  - c) |Φ| ∩ |Ψ| is closed for both, and the members inside it are the same;
  - and, from the "Remords à prop. page 7" (scan 12), d) stratum by stratum: A ∩ B closed in Φ_A and Ψ_B, with Φ_{A∩B} = Ψ_{A∩B}.
  - **Definition:** Φ and Ψ are compatible.
- **Corollaries.**
  - Cor 1: the two equivalence relations agree on the common support. "Mais attention, cette dernière condition ne suffit nullement": Φ and Φ° = {A°} have the same support and relation but are compatible only if equal.
  - Cor 2: for compatible Φ and Ψ, |Φ| ⊂ |Ψ| iff Φ ⊂ Ψ.
  - Cor 3 (margin, scan 11): closed parts of compatible families stay compatible, and their union is closed in Φ ∪ Ψ.
  - **Computation** (SA21-R5-06): a ⇔ b ⇔ c ⇔ d and Cor 2 hold for all 2,025 pairs of admissible families on 3 points. Cor 1's example checks (SA21-R5-07).
- **156-5/11-12.**
  - 𝓕, the set of admissible families, ordered by "sub-family": these are the **"figures ensemblistes" de L** (margin). This is Fig(L) of 156-6/3.
  - C1: bounded Sups exist (unions).
  - The irreducible elements are the families with a largest member, the **"multistrates ensemblistes"** (margin).
  - C2: every Φ is the Sup of its Φ_X.
  - C3: compatibility is tested on multistrates.
  - These are C1-C3 of the fourth system of 156-4 (156-4/54-62), verified in their model. That is the role of this part, and the dates fit.

## 3. Connectedness of the multistrates (scans 13-15): false

- **His claim.** The multistrates with a minimal element all lie in one component of (𝓜, ≤). So, at least for finite figures, "M est connexe", and "l'axiome C4 relatif à la décomposition de M en comp. connexes (que X, Y appartenant à des composantes différentes sont compatibles) est automatique" (scan 14).
- **His argument.** {A} and {{x}} (x ∉ A) are both below "le multistrate {A, {x}, A ∪ {x}}" (scan 13). Scan 15 adds the general construction F ⊠ G = {A ∪ B}, with "(A ∪ B)° = A° ∪ B°".
- **Refuted** (SA21-R5-08, correction SA21-K5-01).
  - {A, {x}, A ∪ {x}} is not admissible: its top member is the union of its two other members, so its open stratum is empty.
  - Likewise (A ∪ B)° = ∅ in F ⊠ G whenever A and B are both non-empty, and his own formula ∂(A ∪ B) = (∂A ∪ B) ∪ (A ∪ ∂B) says so.
  - **Computation:** for |L| = 2, (𝓜, ≤) has 5 elements in 3 components: {{a}} < {{a}, {a,b}}; {{b}} < {{b}, {a,b}}; and {{a,b}} alone. For |L| = 3 it has 28 elements in 5 components.
  - **C4 fails:** {{a}, {a,b}} and {{b}, {a,b}} lie in different components and are not compatible. There are 14 such pairs on 2 points and 324 on 3.
- **What this means.** The set-theoretic model Fig(L) does not satisfy 156-4's C4 (156-4/62-66, STUDY §2.5). The error is a topological intuition, the join of two strata, where the set-theoretic model has no new points. 156-6's At 1-At 14 contain no component axiom. Whether he dropped it for this reason is not on the pages I read (H5-3).

## 4. Supports, the Stone compactification, variants (scans 16-20)

- **156-5/16.** F and G are disjoint (for ≤) iff |F| ∩ |G| = ∅. The supports are the arbitrary parts of L, with set-theoretic complement. Checked (SA21-R5-09); this is the Σ = P(L) of STUDY §2.5 (SA21-C3-08).
- **156-5/17.** From (𝓜, ≤, compatibility) "on récupère P(L)", but not L itself: "mais L̂, le compactifié de Stone (L se récupère cependant comme l'ens des points isolés de L̂)". Hence the suggestion to redo the theory over a compact totally disconnected space X with clopen strata. With infinite figures, the support-cosupport pairs are pairs of regular open sets U = int(Ū). (H3, H6.)
- **156-5/18-20 (15 June).** Sub-orders of 𝓕 stable under sub-figures and Sup:
  - topological conditions on strata: closed, compact, connected, A° i-connected, |∂A| a sphere, density A = closure of A°;
  - "Si 𝓛 est un espace modéré" one keeps tame closed strata;
  - local finiteness (stable under finite Sups only), finite figures, figures of finite type;
  - an ordered set L realized by closed cells X_x in a triangulated space, where every such figure "admet le recouvrement donné de X comme une 'subdivision'".

## 5. Refinement, subdivision, intersection (scans 20-30)

- **Proposition (20-23).** F refines G (F ≪ G) iff any of the following holds.
  - a) every A ∈ F has a smallest B ⊇ A in G, and A ∩ B is F-closed;
  - b) every A° lies in some B°, and A ↦ B is increasing;
  - b′) every A° lies in some B°, and A° ⊂ B ⇒ A ⊂ B;
  - b″) (margin) |F| ⊂ |G| and the preorder of F is finer than that induced by G.
  - NB: F refines G iff every multistrate of F does.
  - ≪ is an order, and sub-figures refine.
  - **Computation** (SA21-R5-10): all four conditions agree on all 2,025 pairs on 3 points. ≪ is antisymmetric and transitive there, and ≤ ⇒ ≪.
- **Proposition (23-24).** F is a subdivision of G (F ≼ G) iff any of the following holds.
  - a) F is maximal among the refinements of G;
  - b) F ≪ G and |F| = |G|;
  - c) F ≪ G and every stratum of G is a union of strata of F;
  - d) (margin, without assuming F ≪ G) every stratum of G is a union of strata of F, and every G^A has a smallest element.
  - The proof of a) ⇒ b) uses the Corollary that compatible refinements of G have a union refining G.
  - **Computation** (SA21-R5-11): a ⇔ b ⇔ c ⇔ d holds.
  - This is the set-theoretic model of 156-4/42-43 and of 156-6/84-85 (Cor 1 under At 13).
- **156-5/25-26.** A refining pair is a square S ⊂ T over I → J. Proposition: F ≪ G iff F ≤ G′ ≼ G for some subdivision G′ of G, namely G′ = F ⨿ {{x}}.
  - The page indexes the singletons by "x ∈ |F| ∖ G", which is empty; read x ∈ |G| ∖ |F| (SA21-K5-02).
  - **Computation** (SA21-R5-12): with that reading the statement holds. So **in the set-theoretic model, refinement is "sub-figure of a subdivision"**: 156-4's definition (1.29) of ≪, which 156-6/21 doubted for tame simplices.
- **156-5/27.** "les relations d'ordre ≪ et ≼ se déterminent mutuellement, en termes de ≤. Peut-on de même exprimer ≤ en termes de ≪ et de ≼ ?" (margin "??").
  - NB: F ≤ G and F ≼ G ⇒ F = G. Computation, holds (SA21-R5-13). "Il faudra que ceci soit vrai dans contrée": via "si F ≤ G, F ≠ G, ∃H non vide disjointe de F, H ≪ G". **This is the axiom 156-6 states as At 13 and Cor 5** (156-6/73, 84).
  - Then 156-4's axioms in this model: C5 (trivial on supports), C6 (induced subdivision, scan 30), C7 (extension, scans 30-32), C8₀ "trivial".
  - **156-5 is where the fourth system of 156-4 is tested on its model** (DEPENDS).
- **156-5/28-29. The "figure intersection" F.G**, the Inf for ≪: the members A ∩ B with A° ∩ B° ≠ ∅.
  - "Attention, ce n'est pas en général la figure F ∩ G"; the two coincide for compatible F, G. |⋀F_i| = ⋂|F_i|.
  - "Cette propriété d'existence d'un Inf pour la relation ≪ s'apparente à une propriété de 'modération'."
  - **Computation** (SA21-R5-14): F.G is admissible and is the Inf for ≪, with support |F| ∩ |G|; it equals F ∩ G for compatible pairs. All of this holds.
- **156-5/30.** F′ ≪ F ⇒ F′.G ≪ F.G, and the same for ≼ and ≤ (with F′.G = {A ∈ F.G | A ⊂ |F′|}); hence "L'axiome C6 est valable".
  - Margins: "il faut vérifier encore l'unicité"; and a) P ≪ Q ≪ R with P ≼ R ⇒ P ≼ Q and Q ≼ R, b) the same for ≤. These are the "two of three" properties proved abstractly at 156-6/86.

## 6. Extension of subdivisions, its canonical form, ultratopologies (scans 30-49)

- **C7 (30-32).** For G ≤ F and G′ ≼ G, F′ = G′ ∪ (F ∖ G) is a figure, subdivides F and induces G′. His proof is followed.
  - **Computation** (SA21-R5-15): it holds in all 387 cases on 3 points.
- **The Corollary (32-34).** It claims to characterize this F′ by b) (φ: F′ ∖ G′ → F ∖ G bijective), c) and d). His margins: "Ultra faux"; "les conditions b) c) d) sont équivalentes, et impliquées par a) ... Mais ces trois cond. (b, c, d) sont loin d'impliquer a)"; "φ* bijectif n'implique pas néc. φ* iso !!!". His own correction stands, and I did not test it further.
- **156-5/35-37.** The data of F′ inducing G′ as factorizations S → I′ → I. The ordered set K = J′ ⨿ (I ∖ J), with α ≤ β iff h_J(α) ≤ β across the parts, makes the square cartesian, and K is a final object among such squares.
  - **This is the coarsest extension, the set-theoretic form of 156-6's At (fac) 14 bis** (156-6/88, with its cartesian square). It answers there his "Je présume ... unique" in this model (DEPENDS).
- **156-5/37-38 (18 June).** "le point de vue des ultr. topologies (i.e. top. préordonnées) est peut-être le plus pratique": a figure of L is a pair (S, τ) with τ an ultratopology, that is an Alexandrov topology of a preorder, on S ⊂ L.
  - Compatibility amounts to the amalgamated sum along a common closed subspace, "opération topologique bien familière !".
  - Sub-figures are closed parts with the induced topology.
- **Lemma (39-44).** Let (S, τ) be a space, T ⊂ S closed, and τ′₀ a topology on T finer than the induced one.
  - a) There is a coarsest topology τ′ finer than τ that induces τ′₀.
  - b), c) Its closed sets are B ∪ closure_τ(X ∩ U), with U = S ∖ T and B τ′₀-closed, containing the trace of that closure.
  - d) It is an ultratopology if τ and τ′₀ are.
  - **Re-derived** (SA21-R5-16). The sets A ∪ B (A τ-closed, B τ′₀-closed) are stable under arbitrary intersections in any topology: ⋂(A_i ∪ B_i) = (⋂A_i) ∪ (T ∩ ⋂(A_i ∪ B_i)). His own route through a union over all subsets J (scan 40) needs the Alexandrov property.
  - Cor (45): closures of parts of U are the same for τ and τ′. Cor (46): the closure formula.
- **The characterization by (i)-(iv) (42-43, 48-49).** His margins: "C'est faux, ça implique (i) (ii) (iii) (iv), mais ces conditions ne suffisent nullement, voir plus bas ! p. 48"; "C'est cette condition que j'avais d'abord oubliée, à la page 31 !" (the "Ultra faux" of scan 32); "mais c'est faux !!"; "C'est ultra faux on voit …". His counterexample: I′ with three elements, I with two, J with one, J′ with two, and A = {b} with closure I′ for τ but {a, b} for τ′.
  - His final characterization: "pour A ⊂ U = S ∖ T, son adhérence pour τ, et pour τ′, est la même".
  - The closure formula at scan 49 is transcribed with "∩". It is "∪", as on the margin of 156-6/44-45 and by finite additivity of closure (SA21-K5-03; open as ink).
- **156-5/47: homotopy.**
  - "Corollaire (Ultratopologies) Supposons φ* submersif à fibres 0-connexes — alors φ itou. Supposons φ* homotopiquement propre i.e. les φ*⁻¹(J_{≤x}) 'faiblement contractiles'. Alors kif-kif pour φ."
  - "Si φ* est homotopiquement propre à fibres faiblement contractibles (donc une équivalence d'homotopie faible universelle), itou pour φ."
  - His reduction: the case where I has a largest element x ∉ J, whose unique preimage x′ in I′ is then largest.
  - I did not re-derive these. They are statements of Quillen's fibre-lemma type for maps of finite T₀ spaces (H5-2): the first place in cote 156 where the homotopy of posets enters.
- **The part ends on scan 49** with the corrected characterization.
