It works the model case through, the ordered line, and from it the "tronçon ordonné", its tame parts and their Boolean algebra. 156-7/71 calls this part "CF III" and corrects its terms (below).

## 1. In order

### 1.1 The data of a network via cuttings (scans 2-6, 8 June)
**Scan 2.** "Je voudrais définir une autre axiomatique [de] la structure de réseaux sur un ens. 𝓛 (ens. des 'lieux')." The data:
- (a) S₀ ⊂ P₂(𝓛), the embedded 0-spheres, or tame ("modérées") pairs of lieux. "In terms of the axiomatics by segments", {a, b} ∈ S₀ means that one of a, b is a singular node, or that they lie on different lines, or that they are the two ends of a segment I.
- A finite T is tame if P₂(T) ⊂ S₀.

**Scan 3.** C_𝓛(T) = {t ∉ T | T ∪ {t} tame}, the "complémentaire modéré".
- His margin asks whether C(T) determines T, and answers "Oui". In both of his examples C(T) = 𝓛∖T, so it does there. In general his one-line argument assumes that t ∈ T′∖T is tame with respect to T, which needs saying. Not checked further.
- (b): for each finite tame T, an equivalence relation R_T on C(T); its classes are the connected components.

**Scan 4.** (c): for each class A, a frontier Ȧ ⊂ T. He foresees that (c) follows from (b), and (b) from the R_ε for ε ∈ S₀. So the essential data would be the tame relation on pairs and the cuts R_ε. "Axiomes provisoires".

**Scan 5.** Ex 1, the discrete network: S₀ = P₂(𝓛), C(T) = 𝓛∖T, R_T discrete, Ȧ = ∅. Axiom: a maximal finite tame part is all of 𝓛, which is then discrete.

**Scan 6.** "Hypothèses de finitude draconiennes, peut-être provisoires":
- Dec₁: finitely many components;
- Dec₂: for T ⊂ S, each component A of C(S) lies in a component B of C(T), with Ȧ ∩ T ⊂ Ḃ.

### 1.2 The ordered line and the tronçon (scans 7-11)

**Scan 7, Ex 2, the "ligne ordonnée".**
- An ordered set, directed both ways, without greatest or least element, and divisible.
- S₀ is the comparable pairs, and the finite tame parts are the finite chains.
- For t₁ < … < t_n the components are ]-∞, t₁[, ]t₁, t₂[, …, ]t_n, ∞[ (n + 1 of them), with their frontier points.

**Scan 8.** The structure depends only on the biorder, the order up to reversal. Tronçons of the line, their boundary, and "parties modérées": finite unions of disjoint tronçons in mutually tame position.

**Scans 9-10, Définition: "tronçon ordonné".** An ordered set satisfying:
- (a) every non-empty L_{>a} is downward directed;
- (a′) dually;
- (b) directed both ways;
- (c) divisible;
- (d) card > 1.

A segment has both extremities, an interval neither, a half-interval one. Opposites of tronçons are tronçons. His margin: "tronçon est 'ens biordre'". ∂L is the set of extremities. 𝒮₀(L) = Drap₂(L), the comparable pairs, and Mod_f(L) = Drap(L), the finite chains.

**The definition does not require a total order.** ℝ² with the product order satisfies (a)-(d), and so does the "doubled line" ℚ × {0, 1}, in which (q, i) < (r, j) iff q < r. He knows this: the formula of scan 20 contrasts [a, b] ∪ [b, c] with [a, c], and scan 21 characterizes the totally ordered tronçons.

### 1.3 The biorder from betweenness, and betweenness from the cuts (scans 11-17)

**Lemme (scans 12-13).** A biordered tronçon is known from the ternary relation R(a, b, c), "b strictly between a and c".
- First, "a, b disjoint" (distinct and comparable) iff some c lies strictly between them; this uses divisibility.
- Then a pair {a, b} with a chosen origin a gives L_{≥b}, L_{≤a}, ]a, b[.
- Then u ≤ v iff some x ≤ a and y ≥ b have x ≤ u ≤ v ≤ y, expressed through betweenness.
- This holds, for non-total tronçons too, using (b) and (c) (SA21-R3-01). The two orientations of the chosen pair give the order and its opposite, so the biorder is determined.

**Scan 14.** The lemma on x, y, u, v ("trois élémts", read four; Michel Hua's project notes the same): u, v between x and y, u between x and v, x < y ⇒ x ≤ u ≤ v ≤ y. It holds (SA21-R3-02). His NB: divisibility served only to obtain disjointness from betweenness.

**Scans 15-16.** From Drap₂(L) and the cuts of C(E) for E ∈ Drap₂, betweenness is recovered. For a < b < c, the components are:
- X_{a,b}, the component of C({a, b}) that contains c, = L_{>b};
- X_{b,c}, the one that contains a, = L_{<b};
- X_{c,a}, the one that contains b, = ]a, c[.

Only for the middle element b do its two pairs give disjoint components: "On gagne !". This holds, and uses divisibility (SA21-R3-03).

**Scan 17, remarks.**
- (1) The largest full subcategory 𝒯 of ordered sets that is directed both ways, of cardinality ≠ 1, divisible, and stable under L ↦ L_{>x} and L_{<x} consists exactly of the tronçons of scan 9. This holds (SA21-R3-04). Michel Hua's project verifies the same.
- (2) "Il faudrait expliciter quelle condition la relation R(a, b, c) doit satisfaire, pour définir une structure de tronçon biordonné", and likewise for (S₀, R_E). **Open on the page.** He returns to it on scan 41.

### 1.4 Closed tame parts (scans 18-22)

**Scan 18.**
- Closed sub-tronçons [a, b], L_{≤a}, L_{≥b}, L, with their relative frontier. His margin: "frontière relative ... et non du bord abs.".
- Wide closed sub-tronçons include points.
- Two of them in tame relative position (frontier points pairwise comparable) meet in the empty set or a wide tronçon, with frontier in the union of the frontiers. This holds: the frontier points then form a chain, and the intersection is [max, min] (SA21-R3-05).

**Th (scan 19).** X a finite union of pairwise disjoint, mutually tame, pseudo-closed tronçons T_i: the family is unique, and Ẋ = ⋃ Ṫ_i. X is called a "partie modérée fermée".
- His bracket identifies the T_i with the connected components of X.
- Uniqueness holds by divisibility: the T_i are the maximal pseudo-tronçons in X. Michel Hua's project proves the same.
- They are the topological components only for complete L. For L = ℚ, [0, 1] ∩ ℚ is not connected (SA21-R3-06).
- He says so himself on scan 30: "composantes (connexes !) ... mais pas pour la top canonique".

**Prop (scan 20).** Two closed tame parts in tame relative position have a closed tame intersection, with (X ∩ Y)˙ ⊂ Ẋ ∪ Ẏ, and a sup X ∪_mod Y, by "double récurrence". The Cor gives the same for finite families.
- His "Attention": an arbitrary A ⊂ L has in general no smallest closed tame part containing it, "déjà faux ds le cas standart L = [0, 1]", with A = {0, 1/2, 1/3, …}. This holds: any such part contains some [0, d] with d > 0 (SA21-R3-07).
- Ex: [a, b] ∪_mod [b, c] = [a, c] ≠ [a, b] ∪ [b, c] in general.
- L is totally ordered iff L_{≤b} ∪_mod L_{≥b} = L_{≤b} ∪ L_{≥b} for every b (scan 21).

**Th (scans 21-22).** For closed tame X ⊃ Y, the smallest closed tame Z containing C_mod(Y, X) is X ∖_mod Y. The relative frontier F of Y in X is finite and tame, and C_mod(Y, X) = C_mod(F, Z). Michel Hua's project notes that the end of this theorem is overwritten on the page.

### 1.5 9 June: the topology of a tronçon (scans 22-25)

**The canonical topology.** Its base is the ]a, b[, L_{<a} and L_{>b}.
- {a} need not be closed.
- For the lieux of a topological ficelle, b ∈ cl{a} iff b ⊃ a, so that space is T₀ ("présobre").
- His question: "je ne sais si c'est le cas pour tout tronçon, ni si un tronçon, avec sa top. canonique, est sobre".

**T₀ fails in general** (SA21-R3-08, a counterexample re-proved independently). In the doubled line ℚ × {0, 1}, the points (q, 0) and (q, 1) have the same strict upper and lower cones. So every basic open set contains both or neither. Michel Hua's project gives the same answer first, with "ℝ with a doubled origin". Sobriety for T₀ tronçons stays open.

**Totally ordered tronçons.** Only for these are points closed, the space Hausdorff, and [a, b], L_{≥a}, L_{≤a} closed.

**The two other topologies (scans 23-24).**
- The topology with closed base [a, b], L_{≥a}, L_{≤a} loses the openness of ]a, b[.
- Their supremum fails cl ]a, b[ = [a, b] already for ficelle lieux, his own example.

**Decision (scan 25).** "Finalement, on travaillera avec la top. définie d'abord", and the [a, b] are called "fermés" by abuse of language.

### 1.6 Pseudo-tronçons, the interstitial tronçon, Mod(L) (scans 26-34)

**Scans 26-27.**
- The absolute boundary ∂T.
- The tronçons T∖α (α ⊂ ∂T), open and half-open; pseudo-tronçons (a point or a tronçon).
- Tame relative position: ∂T ∪ ∂T′ is a chain.

**Scan 28.**
- Prop: T ∩ T′ is empty or a pseudo-tronçon ("Immédiat").
- The order T < T′ (all of T below all of T′).
- "Raccordement": Sup T = Inf T′, attained in exactly one of them.
- T ≪ T′: separated by a point x.

**Prop (scan 29).** For a = Sup T and b = Inf T′, these are equivalent:
- (a) T < T′ without raccordement;
- (b) some x separates them;
- (c) a pseudo-tronçon S with T < S < T′ that raccords with both.

S is unique, with ∂S = {a, b}: the "interstitial" pseudo-tronçon. Cor 1 gives the left and right interstitials.
- Re-proved for totally ordered L (SA21-R3-09).
- For non-total L the page's set description {x | T ⊂ L_{<x}, T′ ⊂ L_{>x}} is not the interstitial pseudo-tronçon. Michel Hua's project: "the two differ for a doubled point".

**Scans 30-32: Mod(L).**
- Mod(L) is the set of X_Φ = ⋃ T_i, for Φ a finite ≪-chain of pseudo-tronçons. The map Φ ↦ X_Φ is injective.
- Fr(X) is ⋃ ∂T_i minus the points of ∂L interior to X. The T_i are "les composantes (connexes!) de X (mais pas pour la top canonique)", noted Π₀(X).
- The tame complement X′ = C_mod(X) is the largest tame part disjoint from X and in tame position with it. It is given by the interstitial tronçons, so |Card Π₀(X) − Card Π₀(X′)| ≤ 1.
- This holds for n ≥ 1 (SA21-R3-10), once his first case is read "T₁ non minoré, T_n non majoré : n′ = n − 1" (the page has "non majoré" twice; SA21-K3-03). Michel Hua's project reads minoré/majoré strictly, and notes that the count fails for n = 0.

**The structure on 𝓜 = Mod(L) (scans 32-34).**
- (a) the order ⊂;
- (b) "{X, Y} modéré", with his margin "Cette relation est-elle déductible de la relation d'ordre -- p.ex. {X, Y} mod ssi X ∧ Y existe ???";
- (c) 𝓜_f, the closed tame parts.

Properties 1)-8):
- least and greatest elements;
- inclusion implies tameness;
- inf, sup and distributivity for mutually tame families;
- the tame complement X′ as an involutive anti-isomorphism, compatible with tameness and the De Morgan laws;
- a closure X̄;
- Fr X = X̄ ∧ X̄′;
- "X finite ⇔ X = Fr X ⇔ X° = ∅".

His margin: mutually tame families "engendrent une 'alg. de Boole'". Then the Boolean-ring notation X + Y, X·Y (scan 34).

**His tentative criterion fails** (SA21-R3-11, refuted, with a counterexample; correction SA21-K3-07).
- In ℝ² with the product order, take X = [(0, 1), (2, 2)] and Y = [(1, 0), (2, 2)].
- They are not in tame position: (0, 1) and (1, 0) are incomparable.
- Yet X ∧ Y exists in Mod(L): it is the box [(1, 1), (2, 2)] = X ∩ Y, whose frontier is a chain.
- So tameness is not "X ∧ Y exists". In a totally ordered L every pair is tame, and the question arises only for non-total tronçons.

**For totally ordered L** (SA21-R3-12, targeted check):
- Mod(L) is the Boolean algebra of finite unions of points and open intervals with ends in L ∪ {±∞}, and 1)-8) hold.
- These are exactly the subsets of L definable with parameters in the dense linear order (L, <): the one-dimensional sets of the basic o-minimal structure. "X finite ⇔ X° = ∅" is the o-minimal dichotomy on the line.
- Michel Hua's project makes the same link: "structures o-minimales, dont le premier axiome dit que les parties admissibles de la droite sont les réunions finies de points et d'intervalles". It cites the interval algebras of Mostowski-Tarski 1939 (locator to be checked).
- This is H2's tame geometry, on the line, three weeks before 156-4/70.

### 1.7 Relativization, connected components, the Boolean algebra of a subdivision (scans 35-41)

**Scans 35-37.**
- 𝓜_A = 𝓜_{≤A} satisfies 1)-7) again. "1_{M_A} = X" should read A; Michel Hua's project notes the same (SA21-K3-05).
- A structure is connected when its only clopen elements are 0 and 1. The clopens should generate a Boolean algebra whose spectrum gives the components.

**Scans 38-39, Ex.** The components of a tame A are its pseudo-tronçons T_i.
- (a) each T_i is clopen in A;
- (b) each is connected, since a tame part of a tronçon with empty tame frontier is ∅ or L.
- This holds in the combinatorial sense of scan 30 (SA21-R3-13).

**Scan 40.** For mutually tame X_α whose frontier points are t₁ < … < t_n, the generated Boolean algebra has the atoms [L_{<t₁}], {t₁}, ]t₁, t₂[, {t₂}, …, {t_n}, [L_{>t_n}]. The page's "]t₁, t₃[" should read ]t₂, t₃[; Michel Hua's project notes the same (SA21-K3-04). "Ainsi, cette alg. de Boole est connue quand on connaît S ... (ou la 'subdivision' du tronçon L)."

**Scan 41, his ending.** The characterization of an ordered interval as a network by Drap₂(L) and the cuts R_ε: "J'ai commencé à faire l'exercice, mais j'ai l'impression que ça n'en vaut pas la peine de l'expliciter jusqu'au bout. Ça doit avoir été fait depuis longtemps (Hilbert ?) dans le cas totalement ordonné (et divisible) (en termes tout au moins de la relation : s est entre a et b …)".
- He is right that it is classical for total orders. Betweenness axioms of the line go back to M. Pasch, *Vorlesungen über neuere Geometrie*, 1882, and D. Hilbert, *Grundlagen der Geometrie*, 1899, §3 (axioms of order). The axiomatization of linear betweenness is E. V. Huntington and J. R. Kline, "Sets of independent postulates for betweenness", Trans. AMS 18 (1917) 301-325 (all locators to be checked).
- Michel Hua's project also cites M. Altwegg 1950 for partial orders, and B. Courcelle 2020 (Theorem 7) for the order from betweenness and comparability (locators to be checked).
- The part his scans 15-16 add, betweenness from the cuts in a possibly non-total tronçon, is not covered there. Michel Hua's project says Courcelle "does not treat cut data".
