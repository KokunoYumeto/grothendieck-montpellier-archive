## 1. Scans 2-60, in order

### 1.1 The data and the "sine qua non" axioms (scans 2-7, 23 June)

**What he sets out to do (scan 2).** A recapitulation of the data and axioms of an "algèbre des figures", or "atelier". The axioms are "purely algebraic": they leave out the topological properties (density, connectedness, acyclicity), the geometric ones (regularity, balls and spheres) and the "fine combinatorial properties" around simplices. At 1-At 7 are "sine qua non"; the rest are "facultatifs".

**Why the figure is primary (scan 3).** The algebraic formalism is simplest with figures, of which lieux and multistrates are special cases. His margin gives a second reason: for an "application" of one "région" into another, the inverse image of a figure tends not to be a figure, but the inverse image of a multistrate is a multistrate.

**Notation (scans 3-4).**
- F̃ ⊂ M: the strata of F (the "déploiement").
- Mulomb(F), the "grande multiombre": F as a set-theoretic figure on M. Its support Omb(F) is the "grande ombre"; it does not determine F.
- mulomb(F), the "petite multiombre": F as a set-theoretic figure on the lieux L, with support omb(F). He notes he had been silent on this support in GF V̄I.

**The data (scan 4).** 𝓕 (figures); ≤ (sub-figure); ≪ (refinement). At 0: F ≤ G ⇒ F ≪ G.
- (a) F ∨ G: the sup for ≤, when it exists; it is then also a sup for ≪. His NB: a sup for ≪ (which always exists in Fig(L)) need not be one for ≤.
- (b) F ∩ G = Inf^≤: always exists. When F ∨ G exists it is also the Inf for ≪.
- (c) F·G = Inf^≪, the "intersection fine", when it exists.
- (d) ∅_𝓕, the least element for both orders.
- (e) M: the strictly irreducible figures, the multistrates. X ◁ F (X incident to F) means X ∈ F̃.
- (f) L ⊂ M: the lieux, the minimal elements of 𝓕∖{∅} for ≪.

**At 1-At 7 (scans 5-6).**
- At 1: 𝓕 ≠ ∅, and stable under bounded sups of any family.
- At 2: every figure is the sup of its strata. Equivalently: F ≤ G iff every stratum of F lies under a stratum of G.
- Scholie: At 1-At 2 say that F ↦ F̃ identifies 𝓕 with a family 𝓕̃ of down-closed subsets of (M, ≤), closed under closed parts and bounded unions. Compatibility F ≍ G :⇔ F ∨ G exists.
- With figures of finite type, (𝓕, ≤) is the same as (M, ≤, ≍): ≍ reflexive, symmetric, closed downward, and every bounded closed part of M finitely generated; 𝓕̃ is then the set of finitely generated closed parts made of pairwise compatible elements. This is the (M, ≤, R) of 156-4/62, with R = ≍.
- At 3: compatibility is tested on strata.
- At 4: F ≪ G iff every stratum of F refines a stratum of G. At 0 follows.
- At 6 (the 6 written over a 5): "axiome de la plus petite strate". For X ≪ F, the set F^X = {Y ∈ F̃ | X ≪ Y} has a least element. X ≪° Y (X an "internal refinement" of Y) means Y is that least element for F = Y.
- At 5: if X ≍ Y, then X ≪ Y ⇒ X ≤ Y. Equivalently, for sub-figures of one figure, ≪ is ≤.
- At 7: X ≪° Y ≪° Z ⇒ X ≪° Z.

**A slip in At 6 (scan 6, decided on the image at 250 dpi).** The ink reads "∀X, F ∈ 𝓕, X ◁ F (i.e. X ∈ F̃), soit F^X = {Y ∈ F̃ | X ≪ Y}". With X ∈ F̃ the axiom says nothing: X itself is the least element, by At 5. The hypothesis he needs is X ≪ F. That is how his own translation At_M 4 a) on scan 7 states it ("Si X ≪ Y"), and it is the hypothesis under which X ≪° Y is defined. Correction SA21-K7-01.

**The translation into (M, ≤, ≪, ≍) (scans 6-7).** At_M 0-At_M 6, among them:
- At_M 4 a): if X ≪ Y, the set Ỹ^X = {Z ∈ Ỹ | X ≪ Z} has a minimal element;
- At_M 4 b): X ≪° Y, X ≪° Y′ and Y ≍ Y′ give Y = Y′.

Check SA21-R7-01: a) and b) together give the least element of At 6. Let Z be minimal in Ỹ^X. Then Z̃^X = {Z}, so X ≪° Z. Two minimal elements are compatible, being strata of Y, so they are equal by b). For any W ∈ Ỹ^X, a minimal element of W̃^X is that same Z, so Z ≤ W.

Without the figures as data, 𝓕̃ is fixed only up to a choice of "certain infinite antifiltres" in the finitely generated part 𝓕̃_tf. The sub-atelier 𝓕* = {Mulomb(F)} ⊂ Fig(M) refers to "Scholie GF p. 28" and "p. 32" (156-6/29 and 33).

### 1.2 Disjunction and the criteria At CD (scans 8-11)

**Disjointness (scan 8).** F ∥ G :⇔ F ≍ G and F ∩ G = ∅_𝓕. This depends on ≤ alone and is tested on strata. In M: X ∥ Y iff X ≍ Y and {X, Y} has no lower bound in M.

His NB: "il résulte des axiomes précédents" that X ∥ Y leaves X and Y without a common refinement. Check SA21-R7-02 (holds):
- Suppose Z ≪ X and Z ≪ Y. Then Z ≪ X ∨ Y.
- At 6 gives a least stratum W of X ∨ Y with Z ≪ W.
- W ≤ X and W ≤ Y, against X ∥ Y.

The converse, "no common refinement ⇒ disjoint", is left to a later axiom. His margin: an atelier satisfying it is "spacieux"; "Contre-ex.: les ateliers simpliciaux".

**Propriété 1 (scan 9).** For F ≍ G, L = F ∩ G, F′ ≪ F and G′ ≪ G: F′ ≍ G′ ⇔ F′_L ≍ G′_L, where F′_L is the refinement of L induced by F′. He gives two sources:
- "cf GF V̄I p. 73" (156-6/74);
- "l'ancien At 12", with "Critère de compatibilité pour les raffinements de sous-figures".

His margin says this is false in the simplicial case, with a drawing, and is "un contre-exemple à l'At ∥, et a fortiori à At 11 (cf GF V̄I p. 67, 68)".

His margin NB proves a step: if X′ ≍ Y′, X′ ∥ L and Y′ ∥ L, then X′ ∩ Y′ = ∅. Check SA21-R7-03 (holds):
- A common Z ≤ X′, Y′ refines X and Y, so it refines L (scan 4 (b)).
- Z ≤ X′ ∥ L gives Z ∥ L.
- Z ∥ L and Z ≪ L contradict the NB of scan 8.

**Propriété 1′₀ and At CD (scan 10).** The weakened form, with ∥ in place of ≍: F′ ∥ G′ ⇔ F′_L ∥ G′_L.
- ⇒ always holds.
- ⇐ is equivalent to At CD 1′₀, the same statement for multistrates. His margin calls it "Critère de disjonction pour les raffinements de sous-figures".

Its corollaries:
- Cor 2: F′_L = ∅ ⇒ F′ is disjoint from every refinement of G.
- Cor 3 (At D₀): F ∥ G, F′ ≪ F, G′ ≪ G ⇒ F′ ∥ G′.
- Cor 4 (margin): F′_L = ∅ ⇔ F′ ∥ L. The "if" needs no extra axiom; the "only if" needs At CD.

"J'ai tendance à regarder At CD comme 'axiomes obligatoires' pour les ateliers."

**Propriété 2, the spacious ateliers (scans 10-11).** For F, G not assumed compatible, with L = F ∩ G: if every F′ ≪ F with F′_L = ∅ is disjoint from every G′ ≪ G with G′_L = ∅, then F ≍ G. This holds iff At CD 2 holds, the same statement for multistrates (margin: "Critère de compatibilité absolue des ateliers spacieux").

At CD 2 fails "le plus souvent" for the polyhedral ateliers. In those, two compatible multistrates with a common lower bound have an inf in M. For them he sets At CD 2′: the same criterion, plus the hypothesis that K = x ∩ y lies in M.

"N.B. Tous les ateliers que j'ai vus comme motivants satisfont At CD 1₀, et l'un des deux axiomes At CD 2 ou At CD 2′."

Margin "Attention": in M, ∥ is not determined by ≤ alone, because ≍ is not; it is a primitive datum. The point of At CD is to reduce ≍ in M, "essentielle pour la création des figures", to the disjunction ∥ in M.

### 1.3 Strict refinement, polyhedral and simplicial ateliers (scans 12-15)

**The "bec ennuyeux" (scans 12-13).** He had taken ≪ between simplices to be the set-theoretic refinement. So a Δ¹ lying inside a Δ² refined it, and he had to accept subdivisions of Δ² into two triangles "ce que personne ne regardera comme une subdivision simpliciale!" (drawings on scan 12).

**The fix (scan 13).**
- Strict refinement: in the definition of GF V p. 19-20 (156-5/20-21), add that for strata A of F and B of G, A ∩ B is empty or a sub-stratum of A.
- Likewise a strict, or "polyhedral", compatibility: F ∪ G must be a polyhedral set-theoretic figure.
- So the special axioms split into polyhedral and spacious ateliers.
- For set-theoretic polyhedral ateliers the embedding condition becomes mulomb(F) ≪^pol mulomb(G) ⇒ F ≪ G. Codex reads "mulens"; the definition on scan 4 and the axiom it replaces say mulomb. This is a reading to confirm on the scan.

**The reference "Axiome des Cat PL VI p.52" (scan 13 margin), decided.**
- The ink, at 400 and 600 dpi, has "propriété qui remplace Axiom[e] d[u|es] ... V̄I p.52". The VI carries the overbar he uses for his fascicules (GF V̄I on scans 4, 9 and 11). The two or three letters before V̄I, read "Cat PL" by Codex, are not legible to me: a c, an a or A with a point, then a word beginning with "pli".
- **The reference is decided by content.** 156-6, manuscript p. 52 (scan 53), defines the "quasi ensembliste" atelier by the axiom At ens 1: ∀F, G ∈ 𝓕, mulomb(F) ≪ mulomb(G) ⇒ F ≪ G. That is exactly the property the formula of 156-7/13 replaces by its polyhedral form.
- The margin of 156-6/53, seen on the image, points back: "Pour correctif voir CF V̄II p. 12". 156-7 p. 12 is scan 13.
- So "Cat PL VI p.52" is GF VI (cote 156-6), p. 52, axiom At ens 1. It is not a separate text on PL categories, and not cotes 120-121. This refutes the second half of H11 (correction SA21-K7-02; check SA21-R7-04).

**Polyhedral ordered sets (scan 14).** I is polyédral if two elements with a common lower bound have an inf.
- When the intervals are inductive (for instance finite), this is equivalent to an interpolation condition: for x′, y′ ≤ x, y there is a z with x′, y′ ≤ z ≤ x, y.
- That condition is self-dual. So, with finite intervals, two elements with a common upper bound have a sup. He writes "ont un plus grand élément"; it should be a least upper bound.
- His margin, in topological terms: the intersection of two strictly irreducible closed parts is again one, "chose des plus remarquables certes!".
- The Lemme: if the intervals are finite, I is polyhedral iff I^opp is.

Check SA21-R7-05 (independent re-proof, holds). If I is polyhedral, x ∧ y interpolates. Conversely, the common lower bounds of x and y form an upward-directed set. With finite intervals it has a maximal element, which is then the greatest. Dually for I^opp.

**Polyhedral figures and ateliers.** A figure is polyhedral when F̃ is: two non-disjoint strata meet in a stratum. At pol 1: X ≍ Y with a common lower bound ⇒ Inf^{M,≤}(X, Y) exists. His margin calls this "condition de la stricte intersection".

**Simplicial ordered sets, and an error (scans 14-15).**
- I is simplicial if it is polyhedral and every I_{≤x} is a combinatorial simplex, P*(J) with J finite.
- He then states that a finite ordered set S is a combinatorial simplex iff S is polyhedral, has a greatest element, and each non-minimal element has at least two predecessors. Induction gives that each element is the sup of the minimal elements below it, "et si J est l'ens de ces éléments minimaux, on trouve S ≃ P*(J)".
- The simplicial atelier is defined accordingly by At pol 1 together with At simp 1: ∂X = {Y < X} is empty or has at least two maximal elements.

**The characterization is false** (check SA21-R7-06, refuted and refined; correction SA21-K7-03).
- Take the face poset of a square: four vertices, four edges and the square.
- It is polyhedral: two edges with a common vertex meet in it, and other pairs have no common lower bound. It has a greatest element. Each edge has two vertices below it, the square four edges.
- Yet it is not P*(J).
- The same holds for the face poset of any convex polytope that is not a simplex. So At pol 1 + At simp 1 describe polytopal (lattice) cell structures, not simplicial ones.

**The correct statement** (my re-proof, SA21-R7-06): a finite S with a greatest element is P*(J) iff S ∪ {0̂} is a distributive lattice and At simp 1 holds.
- With a greatest element and the polyhedral condition, S ∪ {0̂} is a finite lattice.
- At simp 1 says that every non-minimal element covers at least two elements, that is, the join-irreducibles are exactly the atoms.
- By Birkhoff's representation theorem a finite distributive lattice is the lattice of down-sets of its join-irreducibles. When these form an antichain, the lattice is Boolean (G. Birkhoff, *Lattice theory*, AMS Colloquium Publications 25, 3rd ed. 1967, ch. III, §3, Theorem 3; locator to be checked).

His first claim, that every element is the sup of the minimal elements below it, is right: a finite lattice whose join-irreducibles are atoms is atomistic.

### 1.4 The complementary axioms (scans 15-24)

**At C (scan 15), the compatibility criterion for refinements of sub-figures.** For X ≍ Y, L = X ∩ Y, X′ ≪ X and Y′ ≪ Y: X′_L ≍ Y′_L ⇒ X′ ≍ Y′. Here X′_L = {X″ ∈ X̃′ | X″ ≪ L} = Inf^≪(X′, L). The Proposition is the same for figures.

**The cartesian squares (scan 16).** The diagram of the Omb's, and of the déploiements, of H = F ∪ G ⊃ F, G ⊃ L, with "ch. des trois carrés cartésien".

The Proposition says one need only test the strata x′ ≪ L, y′ ≪ L, "ce qui équivaut d'ailleurs à x′ ≪ G, y′ ≪ F". This holds (SA21-R7-07): a stratum x′ of F′ refines F, and if it refines G as well it refines F ∩ G = Inf^≪ (scan 4 (b)).

**At D (scans 16-17)** is the same with ∥ in place of ≍. Its corollary At D₀: F ∥ G, F′ ≪ F, G′ ≪ G ⇒ F′ ∥ G′.

**At pol 2 (scan 17).** Codex reads "At pol 1[?2]"; At pol 1 is already taken on scan 14, so it is 2. This is the compatibility criterion for polyhedral ateliers: At CD 2′, with "L = ∅ or L ∈ M".

The Prop that follows: given At pol, F ≍ G iff
- (i) two strata of F and G with a common lower bound meet in a stratum, and
- (ii) refinements x ≪ F and y ≪ G with x_L = y_L = ∅ are disjoint.

His margin: "On en voit il me semble des tas d'axiomes supplém."

**At spac (scan 18)** is At pol without the condition on L. The proposition for spacious ateliers drops (i). His margin: here the weakened At D′₁ suffices (X′_L = ∅ and Y′_L = ∅ ⇒ X′ ∥ Y′), and At D′ ⇒ At D₀. He draws a diagram of implications: At C ⇒ At D ⇒ At D₀.

**At fil (scan 18):** an increasing, locally finite family of refinements of F has a sup in (𝓕, ≤). His margin asks whether to require only F_i ≪ F_j. The corollaries give the gluing of refinements (156-4's C7) and the filtering version for ≪.

**At supp (scan 19), the axiom of supports of a refinement.** For G ≪ X: if some Y is disjoint from G but not from X, then there is such a Y with Y ≪ X.
- The Prop: the same for any figure F.
- His Question, "ce serait en somme ça, le 'bon axiome'": can such a Y be refined to a Y′ ≪ F? Through At L 1 this reduces to lieux: x ∥ Y ⇔ x ∉ omb(Y). He notes this "n'est guère vérifié que pour les ateliers ensemblistes".
- This gives At ens D: X ∥ Y iff Omb X ∩ Omb Y = ∅.
- Its corollaries (scan 20): F ∥ G ⇔ Omb F ∩ Omb G = ∅; x ∥ F ⇔ x ∉ omb F; for lieux, x ∥ y ⇔ x ≠ y.
- "Trivialement At ens D ⇒ At supp." Check SA21-R7-08 (holds): Y ∦ X gives a common refinement Z, and Z ∥ G because Omb Z ⊂ Omb Y.
- At supp holds in every atelier he has thought of, At ens D does not.

**The lieux (scans 20-21).**
- At L 1, "axiome de divisibilité": every X has a lieu in its open ombre, omb(X)° ≠ ∅. His comment: "Cet axiome d'anodine apparence implique pourtant ... que les 'espaces' où vivent nos ateliers sont 'infiniment divisibles', et par suite (quand ils ne sont discrets) sont infinis : c'est l'introduction de 'l'infini actuel' !"
- At L 2: X ∥ Y iff all their lieux are pairwise disjoint. His margin: "À ne pas confondre avec omb(X) ∩ omb(Y) = ∅ !"
- At L 3: if X ≍ Y and X ≠ Y, lieux of the open ombres of X and Y are disjoint. Equivalently, two lieux in different open strata of a figure are disjoint; given At D it suffices for Y ≤ X.
- At CL(spac): X ≍ Y as soon as the lieux of omb(X)∖omb(L) are disjoint from those of omb(Y)∖omb(L).

### 1.5 The Théorème-Scholie: a spacious atelier is (M, ≤, ≪, ∥ on the lieux) (scans 25-39)

**The question (scan 25).** At CL(spac) reduces ≍_M to ∥ on the lieux, "i.e. à celle d'une structure de graphe sur 𝓛". In the set-theoretic case x ∥ y ⇔ x ≠ y. Given (M, ≤, ≪), 𝓛 its ≪-minimal elements and a symmetric antireflexive ∥ on 𝓛, when does this come from a spacious atelier with L1-L3 and CL(spac)? He defines X ≍ Y by the condition of At CL.

**The lemmas (scans 26-34).** Conditions ML 0-ML 3 on (≤, ≪) alone (scans 26-27):
- ML 0: ≤ ⇒ ≪;
- ML 1: the induced refinement, first for lieux;
- ML 2: divisibility;
- ML 3: the minimal stratum for lieux.

Then:
- Lemma 1 (scan 26): (i) X ≤ Y, (ii) X ≪ Y, (iii) omb(X)° ⊂ omb(Y), (iv) omb(X)° ∩ omb(Y) ≠ ∅ are equivalent.
- He observes (scan 27) that nothing so far used the minimality of 𝓛, and that ML 1 must be generalized from lieux to any Z.
- Lemma 2 (scan 28): for x ≪° Z, Z ≪ X and X′ ≤ X with x ≪ X′: Z ≪ X′, and x ≪° X′ ⇔ Z ≪° X′. Hence At_M 4 a).
- Corollaries 1-4 (scans 29-30), Cor 4 being At_M 5.
- The definition of ≍ (scan 30) gives omb(X) ∩ omb(Y) = omb(L) by antireflexivity.
- Lemma 3: strata of one multistrate are compatible.
- Lemma 4: At_M 6.
- Lemma 5: ≍ is closed downward.
- Lemma 6: At_M 4 b).
- Lemma 7 (scan 33): on 𝓛, ≍ is "= or ∥", and ∥ is the atelier's disjunction.
- Lemma 8 (scan 34): At L 2, "C'est tautologique !".

**The Théorème-Scholie (scans 34-36).** For (M, ≤, ≪) with two orders, 𝓛 the ≪-minimal elements and ∥ symmetric antireflexive on 𝓛: the data come from a spacious atelier satisfying At 1-7, At L 1-3 and At CL(spac) iff the following hold.
- (ML 0) ≤ ⇒ ≪.
- (ML 1) Z ≪ X ≥ X′, and x ∈ 𝓛 with x ≪ Z and x ≪ X′, give Z′ ≤ Z with x ≪ Z′ ≪ X′. If Z ≤ X, one may take Z′ ≤ X′.
- (ML 2) divisibility.
- (ML 3) the minimal stratum.
- (ML 4) ∥ symmetric antireflexive.
- (ML 5) Y ≤ X, y ∈ omb(Y), x ∈ omb(X)∖omb(Y) ⇒ x ∥ y.

The atelier is then Φ, the down-closed sets of pairwise compatible elements. Every atelier with these data is a spacious sub-atelier of Φ that contains the X̃ (scans 36-37).

**Checks of the Théorème-Scholie.**
- SA21-R7-12, targeted proof check of sufficiency (holds): Lemmas 2-8. I re-derived Lemma 2, Cor 3 (iii)⇒(v), Cor 4, Lemmas 3-7 and At L 3. At 1-At 7 on Φ were checked directly. The antisymmetry of ≪ on Φ was checked for finitely generated figures (through the maximal strata), which is his own "petit flottement dû à la possibilité de figures infinies" (scan 37).
- SA21-R7-13, independent re-proof of necessity (holds): generalized ML 1 follows from At 6 + At 7 in any atelier. Take x ≪° Z₀ ≤ Z; the least stratum W₀ of X with Z₀ ≪° W₀ is the least stratum of X under x, hence W₀ ≤ X′. "If Z ≤ X then Z′ ≤ X′" follows by At 5.

**The faults in the lemmas.**
- **Lemma 1 (scan 26) lacks its hypothesis** (correction SA21-K7-04). As written, X and Y are arbitrary, and (ii) ⇒ (i) is false: a lieu interior to a 1-stratum Y refines Y without being a stratum of it. The proof uses ML 1, which needs X, Y below a common element. The ink, as transcribed, has struck "X′, Y′ … avec" and an unfinished "Supposons", so he began to write the hypothesis. His later restatement, Lemma A (scan 41), has it: "supposons ∃Z avec X, Y ≤ Z".
- **That ≤ was decided on the image (500 dpi).** It is a ≤ written heavily over a ≪. Codex reads "≪", and with ≪ the lemma is false. Correction SA21-K7-05; check SA21-R7-14.

### 2.2 Supports (scans 62-68)

**The definitions and the formal properties (scans 62-64).**
- Σ_𝓕: ≪-closed parts of 𝓕 stable under sups.
- Σ_M: ≪-closed parts of M.
- Σ_𝓕 and Σ_M correspond.

- supp A = cosupp cosupp A.

The formal Galois properties (i)-(x) are those of a symmetric relation and are correct (SA21-R7-19). The supports form a complete lattice with an involutive anti-isomorphism ∁, and A ∩ ∁A = ∅ because ∥ is antireflexive except at ∅_𝓕. In current terms it is a complete ortholattice.

**His doubts (scans 64-65).** He doubts the converse of (x) and the distributivity P ∧ ⋁Q_i = ⋁(P ∧ Q_i), "Pourtant, cela a tendance à être vrai ... dans les ateliers auxquels j'ai songé". This is the question left open at 156-4/69.

**Supports on a part Λ and on the lieux (scans 66-68).**
- Disj_Λ: disjointness is tested on omb_Λ. It gives Σ_𝓕 ≅ Σ_Λ.
- With Λ = L this requires At L 2, and then Σ_𝓕 ≅ Σ_M ≅ Σ_L, the closed sets of the graph (L, ∥_L).

**What I add (SA21-R7-20, independent re-proof): Σ_L = P(L), canonically, iff ∥_L is "≠".** If every part of L is closed, take A = L∖{x}. Some y ∈ A^⊥ has y ∦ x. Then y ≠ x is impossible (y ∈ A^⊥ ∩ A would give y ∥ y), so y = x and x is disjoint from every other lieu.
- This sharpens H3 for the 156-7 axioms. Given At L 2, the supports are Boolean in the canonical way exactly when distinct lieux are disjoint.

**Distributivity fails under the 156-7 axioms** (SA21-R7-21, refuted, with a counterexample).
- Take M = L = {a, b, c, d}, discrete (≤ = ≪ = equality), with ∥ the matching a–b, c–d. ML 0-ML 5 of the Théorème-Scholie hold; ML 5 is vacuous.
- So this is a spacious atelier with At 1-7, At L 1-3 and At CL(spac).
- Its supports are ∅, {a}, {b}, {c}, {d} and L, the lattice MO₂: a ∧ (c ∨ d) = a, but (a ∧ c) ∨ (a ∧ d) = ∅.
- At 156-4/62 the axiom C4 excluded this; 156-7 has no such axiom. He allows non-compatible lieux himself (scan 44).

### 2.3 25 June: where supports go wrong, and realization by supports (scans 70-84)

**"La notion de support ... est cornée" (scan 70).** He suspects the notion of support is flawed outside the quasi-set-theoretic case. It should be used only for mutually compatible strata, or strata that become compatible after subdivision.

**The Stratos of an ordered set I (scans 70-71).**

**Supports relative to a chain (scans 72-73).** He takes supports relative to a chain Δ = {a₁ < ... < a_n}: the Boolean algebra on the "points" {a_i} and [a_i, a_{i+1}]. For a figure in a chain, the supports form P(F̃), and a refinement F′ ≪ F gives φ*: P(F̃) → P(F̃′). Outside the quasi-constructible case he sees no canonical common P(P) for figures that are not compatible.

**Realization by supports (scans 74-81).**

  - |supp A| = |A| and |cosupp A| = P∖|A|;

**His counterexample to distributivity: the "ficelle à deux brins" (scans 82-84).**
- The lieux are pairs z = (x, x′) in I × I′; two of them are disjoint when comparable.
- For z₁, z₂ incomparable and z₃ between them in the twisted way (x₁ < x₃ < x₂, x′₂ < x′₃ < x′₁): A_{z₃} ∩ (A_{z₁} ∨ A_{z₂}) = A_{z₃}, while (A_{z₃} ∩ A_{z₁}) ∨ (A_{z₃} ∩ A_{z₂}) = ∅.
- "donc on n'a pas la propriété modulaire de distributivité de ∩ p.r. à ∨ !"
- Check SA21-R7-25 (holds). Every lieu disjoint from both z₁ and z₂ is comparable with both, hence with z₃ (a lieu between them in the product order would need x′₁ < x′₂). So z₃ lies in supp{z₁, z₂}.
- **This settles, in his own hand, the distributivity question of 156-4/69 (H3): it fails in general.**

His "Conclusion-Scholie": "Le formalisme des supports est bon, pourvu qu'on ne s'avise pas de comparer les supports des strates et des figures qui ne soient compatibles, ou qui ne puissent l'être moyennant subdivision."

**The formal Lemma behind it is false, and he says so (scans 97-99).**

- On scan 99, "Mais c'est faux!": a graph with a path A′–A–B–B′ in which A and B are closed, A ∥ B and A ∨ B = M, yet ∁A ≠ B.
- Check SA21-R7-27 (holds). On the path alone, the supports are ∅, {A}, {B}, {A′, B}, {A, B′} and M: the hexagon O₆, with {A}′ = {A′, B}.

- So the supports of a general disjunction graph form an ortholattice that need not be orthomodular, still less distributive.
- He "subodore" that the Théorème needs "hypothèses délicates", and suspects it holds under divisibility L1. **Open on the page; this is the strongest reduction.** The Théorème needs orthomodularity, or Booleanness, of the sublattice generated by the X°, X ∈ F̃, of one figure.

### 2.5 The turn to |∘|: the magasins (scans 102-114, 26 June)

**The new primitive (scan 102).** "J'ai envie de prendre ... comme relation primitive ... au lieu de ≍ ou de ∥, la relation X |∘| Y ⇔ X° ∥ Y°." **The final axioms (scans 110-114), his "p. 110-113".**
- A magasin is M with ≤, ≪ and |∘| ("intérieurement disjoints"):
