## A. Scans 2-47 (18-20 June)

### A1. The data: lieux, multistrates, figures (scans 2-7, 18 June)

- **156-6/2.** Title "Analysis situs (deuxième mouture)", sheet GF VI, dated 18 June 86. Before the contrée he describes "une 'contrée avec notion de multistrates'", the multistrates playing the role of a family of opens generating a topology, or of a generating family of a topos. Section I: "Algèbre de figures" ou "Atelier". Three sets 𝓛, 𝓜, 𝓕 (1.1) with canonical injections 𝓛 ↪ 𝓜 ↪ 𝓕 (1.2).
- **156-6/3-4.** (1.3) a) 𝓕 ↪ P(𝓜), F ↦ F̃ = Dépl(F), the déployement (the multistrates incident to F, its strata); b) 𝓕 → Fig(𝓜), F ↦ Fig_𝓜(F) = {X̃ | X ∈ F̃} (1.4), where Fig(𝓜) is the set of set-theoretic figures in 𝓜 (the objects of 156-5). 𝓜 will carry an order ≤ with X̃ = {Y | Y ≤ X} (1.5) and F̃ closed. The figure attached to X ∈ 𝓜 is X̃ = M_{≤X}, "nullement {X}".
- **156-6/5-6.** (1.6) a) F ↦ Omb_𝓜(F), "la grande ombre": the multistrates X with X ≪ F, which "raffinent" F. "Il n'est pas clair que cette application ... soit injective." b) F ↦ Multomb(F), the family of the Omb(X), X ∈ F̃ (1.7), with Omb(X) = {Y ∈ 𝓜 | Y ≪ X} (1.8). Through 𝓛 ↪ 𝓜 (inverse images, a partial map Fig(𝓜) ⇀ Fig(𝓛)): omb(F) = Omb(F) ∩ 𝓛, the (petite) ombre, and mulomb(F) (1.9); "la première n'a aucune raison d'être injective, la seconde ... a une tendance à l'être".
  - **Reading of (1.8), decided on the scan** (SA21-K6-01, check SA21-R6-01). The ink of the left side is "Mul" with an overwritten letter, then "mb(X)": he began "Mult..." and overwrote it towards "Omb". The line before says "alors que Omb(X) s'explicite par", and (1.8) is "analogue à (1.5)". So (1.8) defines **Omb(X) = {Y ∈ 𝓜 | Y ≪ X}**, not Multomb(X); the transcription's "Multomb[?MultOmb](X)" is faithful to the ink, the slip is his. STUDY §5 ("Multomb(X) = {Y ∈ 𝓜 | Y ≪ X} (1.8)") should read Omb(X). Michel Hua's project restores the same formula (its footnote on (1.7)-(1.8)). (1.7) is written "{X ∈ F̃ | Omb X}" in the ink: his notation for the family (Omb X)_{X∈F̃}.
- **156-6/7.** "l'ombre d'une figure est formée des lieux qui la raffinent — on dit aussi que ce sont les lieux 'de la figure' (à ne pas confondre avec les strates de la figure qui sont des lieux)". The multiombre is indexed by F̃, its inclusion order being incidence, "ou par ≪ au choix, ça revient au même" (proved at 156-6/23, Cor 1). "Alors qu'une figure ne se reconstitue pratiquement jamais à partir de son ombre, elle se reconstitue pourtant souvent (cas des 'Ateliers ensemblistes') à partir de sa multiombre."

### A2. The orders and the six relations (scans 8-14)

- **156-6/8-9.** Orders on 𝓕: ≤, ≪, ≼ (1.10); on 𝓜: ≤, ≪ (1.11); margin: on 𝓛 both are discrete; G ≤ F ⇒ G ≪ F. Compatible: {F, G} bounded above for ≤. Disjoint: compatible with inf ∅_𝓕. (1.13), "par les axiomes": F ∥ G ⇔ omb(F) ∩ omb(G) = ∅ and every lieu of one is compatible with (in the prose: disjoint from) every lieu of the other. In set-theoretic ateliers, two lieux are always compatible, and disjoint iff distinct.
  - **Display and prose agree** (check SA21-R6-05): two distinct compatible lieux are disjoint, since a lieu's only sub-figures are ∅ and itself (G ≤ x gives G ≪ x, and x is ≪-minimal).
  - **(1.13) does not follow from At 1-At 8** (check SA21-R6-06, correction SA21-K6-07). Model: 𝓜 = {a, b, c, d}, ≤ discrete, a ≪ c, b ≪ d, c ⋈ d, the other distinct pairs incompatible; figures ∅, {a}, {b}, {c}, {d}, {c, d}. It satisfies At 1-At 8 (lieux a, b; omb(c) = {a}, omb(d) = {b}). Then {c} ∥ {d}, but their lieux a and b are not compatible. The first clause does hold under At 5: if x ≪ F and x ≪ G with F ∥ G, the smallest stratum of F ∨ G refined by x lies in F̃ ∩ G̃ = ∅. The clause on lieux needs the "axiomes des lieux" of scans 60-65 (At 9, At 10); "par les axiomes" is a forward reference.
- **156-6/10.** "Moralement, pour récupérer l'atelier (à iso unique près) il suffit de connaître sur 𝓜 ces deux relations d'ordre, et la relation de compatibilité" (margin: "au moins dans le cas modéré"). Signs for ∥ (1.14) and ⋈ (1.15). ≼ is "la notion la plus spécifique dans l'Algèbre des figures".
- **156-6/11-12.** Base data on 𝓕: a) ≤, ≪; b) ≤, ≼; c) ≪ with the incidence (1.16). F ◁ G :⇔ F ∈ 𝓜 and F ≤ G (1.17). Diagram (1.18) of the six relations: ◁ ⇒ ≤; ≼ ⇒ ≪; ≤ ⇒ ≪ and ≤ ⇒ ⋈; ∥ ⇒ ⋈. His margin corrects "quatre relations d'ordre": ◁ is transitive but not reflexive (F ◁ F ⇔ F ∈ 𝓜), so take ⊴ = (◁ or =); checked (SA21-R6-07). F ∥ F ⇔ F = ∅ (1.19). Remarks: a) ≤ gives 𝓜 as the strictly irreducible elements (1.20), and ◁ ⇔ ≤ (1.21)-(1.23); b) ≤ determines ∥ (1.24 a, the definition); ≪ determines ∥ by (1.24 b): F ∥ G ⇔ Sup_≪(F, G) exists and Inf_≪(F, G) = ∅ (margin "Vérifier →").
  - **(1.24 b), his "Vérifier", decided** (check SA21-R6-08, correction SA21-K6-06). "⇒" holds under At 1-At 8: F ∨ G is Sup_≪ by At 4, and a non-empty common refinement would carry a lieu (At 8) in omb(F) ∩ omb(G) = ∅. "⇐" fails under At 1-At 8: 𝓜 = {a, b, c}, ≤ discrete, a ≪ c, b ≪ c, no two distinct elements compatible (At 6 forces a, c and b, c incompatible). Then Sup_≪(a, b) = c and Inf_≪(a, b) = ∅, but a and b are not compatible. This is his pseudo-disc of 156-4/45: two interior lieux of one disc that do not form a figure.
- **156-6/13.** c) ≪ determines ≼: F ≼ G ⇔ F ≪ G and the same figures are disjoint from both (1.25): subdivision is refinement with the same support, as in 156-4/70 (C5). d) 𝓛 = the ≪-minimal non-empty figures (1.26), "un lieu est une figure non vide dont le seul raffinement non vide est elle-même", the definition of 156-4/88. e) ≪ and ⋈ determine ≤: G ≤ F ⇐ (G ≪ F and G ⋈ F) (1.27), which is 156-4/39 (and 156-4/49, Cor 3); here it is recovered at 156-6/23 (Cor 2) from At 4 and At 6.
- **156-6/14.** Optimal data (1.28): (≤, ≪), (≤, ≼) or (≪, ⋈) (margin: "Non, ça ne suffira pas, d'après nouveau point de vue"). (1.29): in the first mouture (156-4) he took ≤, ≼ with G ≪ F ⇔ ∃F′, G ≤ F′ ≼ F, "le centre de gravité géométrique et intuitif" being subdivision; but it was laborious "de déduire les propriétés de transitivité et surtout de réflexivité pour ≪, cela amenait à mettre des axiomes en surnombre". So he now starts from (≤, ≪) (margin: from p. 20 on, ≼ will be determined by ≪ and ≤).
  - **"réflexivité" is in the ink** (scan 14 at 220 dpi; check SA21-R6-02, correction SA21-K6-02). Reflexivity of ≪ is immediate in the first mouture (G ≤ G ≼ G); what needed extra axioms there was antisymmetry: 156-4/75-76 ("il manque un axiome") and C8₀, "nécessaire et suffisant pour que ≪ soit un ordre" (STUDY §2.5). Read "antisymétrie".

**The first difference from 156-4.** 156-4's fourth system had (𝓕, ≤, ⪯) primitive and ≪ derived; 156-6 takes (𝓕, ≤, ≪) primitive, with F ≤ G ⇒ F ≪ G (1.30), and derives ≼ ((1.25) here, and At 13 with the definition of scans 84-86 later). The reason is on the page: antisymmetry of the derived ≪ cost the extra axiom C8₀.

### A3. In terms of 𝓜: préadmissible families and compatibility (scans 15-19)

- **156-6/15-16.** From (1.30): 𝓜 by (1.20), 𝓛 by (1.26), F̃ = {X ∈ 𝓜 | X ≤ F} (1.31), F ≤ G ⇔ F̃ ⊂ G̃ (1.32). "≪ n'a pas d'interprétation simple directe ... ainsi G ≪ F n'implique nullement Fig_𝓜(G) ≪ Fig_𝓜(F). Aussi l'application (**) et la notation Fig_𝓜(F) sont-ils finalement un peu bidon !" In terms of 𝓜 the data are 𝓕 ⊂ P(𝓜) and ≪ (1.33). First axiom: 𝓕 is a **pré**admissible family of support 𝓜 (margin "attention au 'pré' !"), so it induces a preorder ≤ on 𝓜 which must be an order.
- **156-6/17.** (i) each x lies in a smallest F_x ∈ 𝓕 (= M_{≤x}); (ii) x ↦ F_x injective. The F ∈ 𝓕 are then closed parts of 𝓜 containing the M_{≤x}; ∅ ∈ 𝓕, "ce qui implique que 𝓕 n'est jamais admissible !" (so 156-5's admissible families exclude ∅). He refers to GF IV (156-4), pages 53-54, 56, 58 (= scans 54-55, 57, 59) for C1-C3, which he renames At 1-At 3 (margin); the description: an ordered set 𝓜 with a set 𝓕 of closed parts containing the M_{≤x}, ∅, the closed parts of its members, and the criterion: if M_{≤x} ∪ M_{≤y} ∈ 𝓕 for all x ∈ F, y ∈ G, then F ∪ G ∈ 𝓕. This is the fourth system of 156-4 as STUDY §2.5 states it (checked there, SA21-C3-05, -06).
- **156-6/18.** "dans un premier temps, je m'attache aux aspects 'algébriques' ou 'triviaux' d'un formalisme des figures". The axioms must hold 1°) in every "atelier" Fig(L) of set-theoretic figures in a set L, "sans conditions de finitude d'aucune sorte", and 2°) for finite affine simplicial figures over ℝ or any ordered field. Finite type: F̃ a finite union of M_{≤x_i} (1.34).
- **156-6/19.** The essential datum is ⋈ on 𝓜, reflexive and symmetric (1.35), closed downward (1.36); "il y a corr. biuniv." between such relations and the families 𝓕 above all of whose members are of finite type.
  - **The bijection needs a finiteness hypothesis** (check SA21-R6-09, correction SA21-K6-08). It holds when every closed part of a finite-type closed set is again of finite type (for example every M_{≤x} finite, his Cont 3′ of 156-4). Otherwise no family 𝓕 can contain the closed parts of its members and consist of finite-type sets. Example: 𝓜 = {x_n : n ∈ ℕ} ∪ {ω}, with the x_n incomparable and below ω, and ⋈ total. M_{≤ω} would be a figure, and its closed part {x_n} is not of finite type. Under that hypothesis the bijection is 156-4/62 (SA21-C3-05).
  - **Two readings on the scan** (19 at 200 dpi). The second display is tagged like the first, "(1.3?)"; the text cites "(1.36)", which is how it should be read (an open reading of the tag, no mathematics hangs on it). The sentence "via 𝓜 (multistrates), muni de ≤ et d'une relation de **disjonction** ⋈" has "disjonction" in the ink, with the compatibility sign. The relation is reflexive (1.35), so it is compatibility: his slip of word (check SA21-R6-03, correction SA21-K6-03).

### A4. The grande ombre, the change of 19 June, and At 4-At 7 (scans 19-28)

- **156-6/20.** Omb(F) = {X ∈ 𝓜 | X ≪ F} (*), Multomb(F) = {Omb(X) | X ∈ F̃} (**). He wants Multomb(F) to be a set-theoretic figure in 𝓜, with support the ombre, faithfully indexed by F̃. Axioms first written C4 (compatible X, Y: X ≪ Y ⇒ X ≤ Y) and C5 (F^X = {Y | X ≪ Y ≤ F} has a ≤-least element), with the margin "Je vais changer plus bas le point de vue pris pour ≪ et les axiomes C4, C5"; margin Cor: X ≪ F ⇔ ∃Y ∈ F̃ with X ≪ Y.
- **156-6/21.** F ↦ Multomb(F) is injective, since X is the ≪-largest element of Omb(X) (checked: it needs only that ≪ is antisymmetric on 𝓜). (1.38) G ≤ F ⇔ Multomb(G) ≤ Multomb(F) (both directions hold, the second by injectivity of Omb on 𝓜).
- **19 June, the change of point of view** (156-6/21-22): he had read G ≪ F as "G est une sous-figure d'une subdivision de F". Already in the simplicial case that "n'a pas l'air naturel": for two tame simplices X, Y in ℝⁿ, "on dira que Y ≪ X si c'est le cas pour les figures ensemblistes correspondantes — ce qui n'implique sans doute pas l'existence d'une subdivision de X telle que Y en soit une multistrate", and that question "semblerait accessoire". Hence:
  - **At 4:** G ≪ F ⇔ for every X ∈ G̃ there is Y ∈ F̃ with X ≪ Y. So ≪ on 𝓕 comes from ≪ on 𝓜, and for figures of finite type the whole atelier is recovered from (𝓜, ≤, ≪, ⋈). Margin Cor: Omb(F) = |Multomb(F)|. **Re-derived** (SA21-R6-10): apply At 4 to the figure X̃ and F.
  - **At 5:** X ≪ F ⇒ F^X = {Y ∈ F̃ | X ≪ Y} has a ≤-least element. With At 6 this says that Multomb(F) is préadmissible.
  - **At 6:** X ⋈ Y and X ≪ Y ⇒ X ≤ Y.
  - In the ink of scan 22 the multiombre is written "{Ỹ | Y ∈ F̃ i.e. Y ◁ F}", with a tilde, and scan 23 writes "Ỹ°" in the same role. Both stand for Omb(Y) (his notation slip, SA21-K6-04, check SA21-R6-04); the argument "Y ∈ Ỹ°" holds for Omb(Y) too, as his margin at scan 20 says ("X ∈ Omb(X)°").
- **156-6/23.** Cor 1: on F̃, ≤ and ≪ coincide. Cor 2: for compatible F, G, F ≪ G ⇔ F ≤ G. **Re-derived** (SA21-R6-11): by At 4 each X ∈ F̃ refines some Y ∈ G̃, both lie below a common bound, so At 6 gives X ≤ Y. This is 156-4/39 (sub-figure ⇔ refines and compatible), now from At 4 and At 6. (1.39): X ↦ Omb(X) = M_{≪X} embeds (𝓜, ≪) in (P(𝓜), ⊂), and the Multomb(F) are the images of the F̃.
- **156-6/24-25.** Prop: F ≤ G ⇔ F̃ ⊂ G̃ ⇔ Multomb(F) ≤ Multomb(G) ⇔ Multomb(F) ⊂ Multomb(G). Cor 1: for compatible F, G, the multiombres are compatible, Multomb(F ∨ G) = Multomb(F) ∪ Multomb(G) and Multomb(F ∧ G) = Multomb(F) ∩ Multomb(G). Cor 2: disjoint figures have disjoint multiombres, via Multomb(∅) = ∅ (1.40). **Re-derived** (SA21-R6-12): (F ∨ G)~ = F̃ ∪ G̃ because multistrates are strictly irreducible, and closedness passes through the embedding (1.39) because ≪ = ≤ on each déployement. NB (25): the converse of Cor 1 fails, "même si F et G sont réduits aux lieux distincts x et y", but should hold for set-theoretic ateliers (margin "vérifier plus tard").
- **156-6/25-28. Prop: F ≪ G ⇔ Multomb(F) ≪ Multomb(G)** (set-theoretic refinement: |Ψ| ⊂ |Φ| and an increasing f making the square (1.42) of stratum maps φ commute). For "⇒" one needs: X ≪ G ≪ F, X′ = min G^X, X″ = min F^{X′} ⇒ X″ = min F^X. "Il semble qu'il faille le poser en axiome, sous forme en apparence affaiblie; mais en fait équivalente":
  - **At 7:** X ≪° Y ≪° Z ⇒ X ≪° Z, where X ≪° Y means X ≪ Y and X ≪ Y′ ≤ Y ⇒ Y′ = Y.
  - **Re-derived in both directions** (SA21-R6-13). At 7 ⇒ the corollary: X ≪° X′ and X′ ≪° X″ (by minimality), so X ≪° X″; min F^X ≤ X″ and X ≪° X″ force equality. The corollary ⇒ At 7: apply it to the figures Ỹ and Z̃. His "en fait équivalente" holds.
  - "⇐" of the Prop (he: "On n'a pas utilisé que f soit croissante") checks: f(X) = φ_F(X) for X ∈ G̃, which is At 4.
  - **The "Corollaire à la prop." (156-6/28) has a superfluous clause** (check SA21-R6-14, correction SA21-K6-05). ### A5. The atelier as a family of set-theoretic figures in 𝓜: At* and the Théorème-Scholie (scans 29-34)

- **156-6/29.** Scholie: via F ↦ Multomb(F), (𝓕, ≤, ≪) "s'interprète comme un 'sous-atelier' de Fig(𝓜)": an atelier is a set 𝓜 with 𝓕_𝓜 ⊂ Fig(𝓜) ⊂ P(P(𝓜)) (1.49) satisfying conditions that translate At 1-At 7. "la notion de (grande) multiombre reprend un plus grand intérêt".
- **156-6/30-32.** 𝓜* = all strata of all Φ ∈ 𝓕_𝓜. At*1₀: 𝓜* is a set-theoretic figure of support 𝓜 whose preorder (≪) is an order (the strata separate points); then x ↦ M_{≪x} = Omb(x) is a bijection 𝓜 → 𝓜* turning ≪ into ⊂, and "Les A ∈ 𝓜* sont les parties fermées irréductibles de 𝓜 admettant un pt générique": the language of an Alexandrov T₀ topology on (𝓜, ≪). At*2₀ (smallest figure Φ_A containing a stratum A; "C'est C1 pour 𝓕_𝓜"), At*3₀ (bounded unions, ∅ ∈ 𝓕_𝓜), At*4₀ (compatibility tested on strata). Margin: cf GF IV pages 54 (Cor. 2), 55 (Remarque), 58 (C3). Corollary (32): Φ ⊂ Ψ ⇒ Φ closed in Ψ; checked from the reformulated At*2₀.
- **156-6/33-34. Théorème-Scholie:** the groupoid of ateliers satisfying At 1-At 7 is equivalent to that of (𝓜, 𝓕_𝓜) satisfying At*1 (∅, bounded unions), At*2 (Φ_A = Ψ_A: "Toute strate porte canoniquement une structure de multistrate"), At*4 (Φ_A ∪ Ψ_B ∈ 𝓕_𝓜 for all A, B ⇒ Φ ∪ Ψ ∈ 𝓕_𝓜; margin: set-theoretic compatibility of Φ_A and Ψ_B is not enough), At*5 (𝓜* a figure of support 𝓜 separating points), At*3 (A ⊂ B ⇒ Ã ≪ B̃; margin: At*2 and At*3 merge into A ⊂ B ⇒ Φ_A ≪ Ψ_B). "J'espère que je n'ai pas oublié d'axiomes !"
  - **Targeted proof check** (SA21-R6-15): At 1-At 3 ↔ At*1, At*2, At*4 as in 156-4/55-62. At 5 is built into 𝓕_𝓜 ⊂ Fig(𝓜), and At 6 into the inclusion order of a set-theoretic figure. ### A6. Examples, set-theoretic ateliers, morphisms, sub-ateliers (scans 35-45, 20 June)

- **156-6/35.** 𝓕 → Fig(𝓛), F ↦ mulomb(F) is increasing for ≤ and ≪ and commutes with Sup and Inf for ≤, "Mais elle n'est plus néc. injective". The restriction Fig(𝓜) ⇀ Fig(𝓛) is partial: a trace can lose an open stratum; At 8 (scan 46) is what makes mulomb(F) indexed by all of F̃.
- **Example 1 A)** (35): triples (X, Σ, Φ), with X closed in a space E, Σ a piecewise-linear (or tame) structure, and Φ a set-theoretic figure of closed PL strata. ≤ and ≪ come from Φ′ ≤ Φ and Φ′ ≪ Φ, with X′ a PL subspace and Σ′ induced. "On vérifie les axiomes"; 𝓛 ≅ E, and F ↦ Φ is not injective (an arc in E carries several PL structures). I checked At 4 and At 6 for it (SA21-R6-16).
- **Example 1 B)** (35-36): affine simplices (A, σ) in E. ≤ is face, ≪ is refinement of the simplicial set-theoretic figures, and ⋈ means that A ∩ A′ is empty or a common face. Figures are finite (or locally finite) closed sets of pairwise compatible simplices. 𝓜 → Mulstrat(𝓛) is not injective. Downward closure (1.36) holds, since faces of a common face are common faces. The page writes "(X, σ) et (X′, σ′)" for (A, σ), (A′, σ′).
- **Example 2** (36): a pseudo-disc (B, ∂B, …) "avec pt marqué": the figure is not known from the "centre" a alone, one needs "une structure de reliement de a au bord" (156-4/32, H5). So "il est sans doute illusoire d'espérer" mulomb(F) ≤ mulomb(F′) ⇒ F ≤ F′ (and the same for ≪); "et en fait, le contraire est vrai dans les 2 exemples 1. A) et B)" (37).
- **156-6/37. Définition:** 𝓕 is **ensembliste** if a) 𝓕 → Fig(𝓛) is injective; b), c), d): mulomb reflects ≤, ≪, ⋈. Margin: a later axiom for every atelier will give "mulomb(F) élémentaire ⇔ F élémentaire" (it is At 8 with its Cor 1, scan 47).
