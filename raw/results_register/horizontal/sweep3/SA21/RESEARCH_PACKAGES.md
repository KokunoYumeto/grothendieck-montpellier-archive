## P1. The contrées of 156-4 as event structures (H1)

**The question:**
- Is the finitist contrée (M, ≤, R) of 156-4/62 a prime event structure, with figures as configurations?
- Under that dictionary, what are his subdivision, his refinement ⪯ and his supports Σ?
- Which of his axioms C1-C8 (156-4/53-89), Mag 1-4 (156-8/8) and At 1-At 5 (156-8/19-21) become known properties of event structures or of their domains of configurations, and which are new?

**Why it matters:**
- If the dictionary holds, his figure order is a known kind of domain, and the theory of domains answers his open questions on it.
- If it fails, the failure shows what is special to his notion of figure.

**What it rests on:** 156-4/53-89 (read in full; SA21-13); 156-8/3-21 (SA21-15); 156-5 (the set-theoretic sorite, SA21-14).

**Strongest known reduction:** the finite description of 156-4/62 is checked (SA21-C3-05). The correspondence of figures with families Φ ⊂ P(M) is checked (SA21-C3-06).

**Material:**
- the 156-4, 156-5 and 156-8 `.tex` with the scan PDFs;
- Nielsen-Plotkin-Winskel 1981 (TCS 13);
- Winskel's survey of event structures (locator to be checked).

## P2. The tame Hauptvermutung as an axiom: o-minimal contrées (H2, H7, H8)

**The question:**
- Is the o-minimal contrée a contrée in the sense of the fourth mouture? Its figures are the finite definable stratifications with the frontier condition, in a definably compact set.
- Does it satisfy C1-C8₀, and is it "modérée" (156-4/70)?
- Is the frontier problem of 156-4/17 true after definable refinement?
- How do the tame axioms of cotes 120-121 sit against C1-C8 and Mag 1-4?

**Why it matters:**
- It ties his 1986 axioms to the tame geometry he asked for in the Esquisse (§5-6) and in cotes 120-121.
- It ties them to the o-minimal theory that realizes it.
- Cote 156 has never been read against that theory.

**What it rests on:**
- 156-4/2-25 and 70 (read; SA21-13);
- 156-7/11-15, polyhedral and simplicial ateliers (skeleton);
- cotes 120-121 (SA20-12 to SA20-15, left to this agent and not read tonight);
- SA10-5, SA11-4.

**Strongest known reduction:**
- "Modérée" amounts to Shiota's theorem on definable triangulations: any two have a common subdivision up to definable homeomorphism.
- The frontier condition can be reached by refinement: a definable stratification refines to one with the frontier condition (van den Dries, *Tame topology and o-minimal structures*, LMS LN 248, 1998, ch. 4; locator to be checked).

**Material:**
- the 156-4 and 156-7 `.tex` with the scans;
- the 120-121 packages (MPL_152+120, MPL_121_001-048, MPL_121_049-073+153+155);
- van den Dries 1998, Shiota 1997, and the broad agent's report `sweep2/REPORTS/cote156_against_tame_topology.md`.

## P3. Is the lattice of supports Boolean, and when is Σ ≃ P(L)? (H3, H6)

**State after the readings:**
- Distributivity fails in general (156-7/84).
- Faithful ⇒ Σ_M is the complete Boolean algebra RO(M, ≪°).
- Under divisibility and Mag L2: faithful ⇔ Σ is Boolean with lieux as atoms.
- The axiom of supports is the orthomodular law, which fails on O₆.

The package now reduces to the non-faithful case: P8-1 and P9-1.

**The questions:**
- Under C1-C8 (156-4), is Σ distributive?
- Is it Boolean, or is there a counterexample?
- For a lieu x, is supp x an atom of Σ* (156-9/3)? And is Σ ≃ P(L) exactly the separation property he reduces it to?

**Why it matters:**
- Σ is his replacement for the lattice of open or closed sets.
- Its distributivity decides whether contrées are locales (pointless spaces).
- The class "Σ_M ≃ P(L)" of 156-8/3 is where points come back.

**What it rests on:** 156-4/66-69; 156-8/3 and 15-17; 156-9/3.

**Strongest known reduction:**
- In the set-theoretic case Σ is Boolean, with Stone space β(E_discrete) (SA21-C3-08).
- My test lattice MO₂ is excluded by C4 for discrete M.
- His reduction on 156-9/3 is to a separation property for pairs of lieux.

**Material:** the 156-4/66-69, 156-8 and 156-9/2-7 `.tex` with the scans; Johnstone, *Stone spaces* (1982), ch. II-III.

## P6. Maquettes and regular cell complexes: his "Sauf erreur" on 156-8/6 (H9, H10)

**State after the readings:** dimension ≤ 1 is Serre's graphs, and dimension 2 is refined as in H9. The package continues as P8-2.

**The questions:**
- Prove or refute his claim that the oriented maquettes of dimension ≤ 2 are the combinatorial cell complexes of dimension ≤ 2.
- Find the statement in every dimension.
- Place At simp 1 (156-7/15) and the flag description of 156-9/7 in it.

**Why it matters:**
- It is a precise claim of his, marked by himself as unchecked.
- It is the point where his forms meet finite T₀ spaces. Those are the programme questions P01-P16 of the notes (SA21-8).

**What it rests on:** 156-8/3-6; 156-7/14-15; 156-9/5-7.

**Strongest known reduction:**
- Finite T₀ spaces are posets, with the McCord weak equivalence (McCord 1966).
- A poset is the face poset of a regular CW complex when its lower intervals are face posets of spheres (Björner 1984; locator to be checked).
- In dimension ≤ 1, his "graphes" are the case where the intervals are trivially spheres.

**Material:** the 156-7/10-15, 156-8/2-8 and 156-9/2-7 `.tex` with the scans; McCord 1966; Björner 1984.
