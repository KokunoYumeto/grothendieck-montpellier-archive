## 1. SA20-2: the quotient topos of a foliation and the theorem "Th" (119/145–146)

**What he writes (145):**
- "Topos quotient d'un espace E [ou topos ?] par une relation d'équivalence locale R (ex: feuilletages de variétés)."
- The quotients include the "espaces" (X, G): X a space, G a discrete group acting on X, and "G est le groupe
  fondamental d'une 'feuille'".
- A bracketed model case: all leaves Fᵢ are homeomorphic, and a map E → F induces isomorphisms Fᵢ → F.
- Margins: "Mettre où" and "N. [définition|question] de l'holonomie".

**What he states (146), "Th":**
- **C** is the category of pairs (X, R): X a topological space; R a local equivalence relation on X, locally open, with
  locally connected fibres. Its arrows are the continuous maps compatible with the relations.
- **C′** is the category of systems (X, 𝒴, f): X a space; 𝒴 a "mult. top." (multiplicité topologique); f: Top(X) → 𝒴
  a morphism that is (locally) open, "à fibres locᵗ connexes et connexes". He adds: "c'est une 2-cat. où les Hom(ξ, η)
  sont des cat. discrètes".
- **The claim:** C ⇄ C′ is an equivalence.
- **Variants:** foliated manifolds of class C^n, n ∈ [0, +∞] ∪ {ω_ℝ} ∪ {ω_ℂ}.
- **His question:** "Quelles propriétés obtient-on avec des feuilletages ???"

**Reading decided on the image (146).** The third condition on f, unread in Hua's reading, is on the page: "à fibres locᵗ
connexes **et** connexes", with "et" underlined. The fibres of f are to be locally connected and connected. The Pro
transcription has it, and the image confirms it.

**What the page proves:** nothing; the theorem is announced.

**Working through.**
1. *The model case (145), re-derived.*
   - Let E = (F̃ × X)/G be a suspension foliation: F̃ simply connected with a free proper G-action, F = F̃/G, and the
     leaves the images of F̃ × {x}.
   - Sheaves on E that are locally constant along the leaves pull back to sheaves on F̃ × X that are locally constant
     along F̃. These come from X, with a G-equivariant structure. So the quotient topos is B_G(X), the topos of
     G-equivariant sheaves on X.
   - Its isotropy at the image of x is G_x. The leaf through x is F̃/G_x, whose fundamental group is G_x. So "G est le
     groupe fondamental d'une feuille" says that the isotropy of the quotient at a leaf is the fundamental group of
     that leaf.
   - In his bracketed case (each leaf maps isomorphically onto F) the action on X is trivial, and the quotient is
     X × B_{π₁(F)}.
   - This is the **monodromy** quotient. The holonomy quotient would have trivial isotropy at a leaf without holonomy
     (the one-leaf foliation of S¹, Pro note N06). So the page's "topos quotient" is the monodromy version, and the
     marginal "holonomie" is a question he raises, not the definition he uses.
2. *The 2-categorical claim, sketched.*
   - A 1-morphism of C′ is (u: X → X′, v: 𝒴 → 𝒴′, an isomorphism f′∘Top(u) ≅ v∘f). A 2-morphism is θ: v ⇒ v′
     compatible with these isomorphisms.
   - Suppose f is an open surjection of topoi (open, with nonempty connected fibres). Then f is an effective descent
     morphism (Joyal–Tierney 1984; Moerdijk; from memory), so whiskering with f is faithful on 2-cells. A 2-cell whose
     whiskering with f is the identity is then the identity, and Hom(ξ, η) is discrete.
   - This is a sketch, not a proof: the page does not fully specify the morphisms of C′.
3. *The equivalence.*
   - The functor C → C′, (X, R) ↦ (X, X/R, the quotient map), is the construction of SGA 4 IV, exercise 9.8.3 (1972).
   - The converse for étendues is Kock–Moerdijk, JPAA 82 (1992). The monodromy topos is in Kock–Moerdijk, Topology
     Appl. 72 (1996).
   - These attributions are Michel Hua's project's ("le foncteur C → C′ est dans SGA 4, exposé IV … exercice 9.8.3")
     and the Pro notes' (P1, P2). I have not opened these texts.
   - The round trip (X, R) ↦ (X, X/R, f) ↦ the relation read off f can enlarge R (Kock–Moerdijk 1992, Remark 6.5, per
     Pro note N77). So the equivalence needs a condition on R (an atlas or "local kernel" condition) or on the
     morphisms of C. Open.

**Status:**
- an announced theorem, plausible with 𝒴 an étendue, the conditions as read, and the monodromy quotient intended;
- open as an exact statement (package R20-119_144-161_276-281-3).

## 2. SA20-3: collars and cones in tame topology (119/148–149)

**Readings decided on the images (148):**
- **Line 2** reads "Voisinages tubulaires d'une partie ⟨modérée⟩ quelconque d'un espace modéré".
  - "quelconque" is **underlined and live**, with "modérée" inserted above it. Hua's reading has it struck through; the
    ink does not.
  - The subject is the neighbourhoods of an arbitrary tame part.
- **The strictness condition** reads "(Ċ est bordant dans **X∖(C∖Ċ)**)".
  - The ink has a B overwritten by X, then the set-minus sign, then (C∖Ċ). The transcription's "<-B> (C∖Ċ)" drops the
    X and the sign.
  - This matches the cone definition on 149 ("Ċ est bordant dans X∖(C∖Ċ)"), and it is the only reading that makes
    sense. It is a correction to the transcription.
- **The collar map** is written C ←φ∼ B × I, with Ċ = φ(B × {1}); the two agree (Pro N12). Hua's φ: C → B × I with φ⁻¹
  is his own normalization.

**What he defines:**
- B "bordant" in X. The word is undefined; he struck "comme 'bord'".
- A collar: C ⊂ X a neighbourhood of B with φ: B × I ⥲ C, φ(b, 0) = b, and Ċ = φ(B × {1}). It is strict if Ċ is
  bordant in X∖(C∖Ċ), and rigidified if φ is kept.
- Around a point a: a cone (C, Ċ) with (C, a) ≅ Cone(Ċ); strict and rigidified as for collars.

**What he conjectures:**
- **I, collars:**
  - a) a collar exists, hence a strict one;
  - b) the space of strict collars is contractible;
  - c) the space of rigidifications of a collar is contractible, "ou encore: l'espace des autom. d'un collier, induisant
    l'identité de B (et 'libres' sur Ċ) est contractile".
  - A struck version said "l'espace des colliers rigidifiés et stricts est fibré sur [les colliers stricts]".
  - His conclusion: the choice of a strict collar, of a strict rigidified collar, or of a rigidification of a collar,
    is "anodin".
- **II, cones:**
  - a) a cone exists;
  - b) the space of strict cones is contractible;
  - c) the automorphisms of Cone(Y) that are the identity on Y and fix a form a contractible space, "(Alexandroff ?)".
  - The same conclusion as for collars.

**Working through.**
1. *II c) is true (re-derived).*
   - For Y compact and h an automorphism of Cone(Y) fixing Y and the apex o, put H_t(h) = δ_t∘h∘δ_t⁻¹ on the subcone of
     radius t and the identity outside, with H_0(h) = id. Here δ_t is the radial scaling.
   - The pieces agree on radius t because h fixes Y. Continuity at t = 0 holds because the subcone shrinks to o.
   - The formula is definable in an o-minimal structure, so it stays tame, and it works in families.
   - This is J. W. Alexander's trick (1923). His "Alexandroff ?" is Alexander, as Michel Hua's project says ("le
     procédé est celui de J. W. Alexander (1923), non de P. S. Alexandroff"). The question mark is his own.
2. *I c) is false as written in general.*
   - For B a closed manifold, the automorphisms of B × I that fix B × {0} pointwise and preserve B × {1} ("libres" on
     Ċ) form the pseudo-isotopy (concordance) space C(B).
   - Its π₀ is nonzero for suitable closed manifolds of high dimension with nontrivial fundamental group (all from
     memory: Hatcher–Wagoner, Astérisque 6, 1973, for smooth and PL; the topological case through
     Burghelea–Lashof–Rothenberg, LNM 473, 1975).
   - A PL pseudo-isotopy is tame, and a tame family is a topological family. So a PL pseudo-isotopy with a nonzero
     topological invariant is not tamely isotopic to the identity through pseudo-isotopies, and for such B the tame
     automorphism space is not connected.
   - Michel Hua's project makes the same objection ("lu à la lettre … ne peut pas valoir en toute généralité",
     Hatcher–Wagoner). The Pro notes keep it conditional (N14).
   - The refined statement is true (re-derived; Pro N15): the group of fibrewise automorphisms h(b, u) = (b, h_b(u)),
     h_b increasing with fixed ends, is contractible by the straight-line homotopy (1 − t)h_b(u) + tu.
3. *I b) falls with c) (re-derived).*
   - His struck line says it: the space E of strict rigidified collars fibres over the space of strict collars. The
     fibre is the set of rigidifications of one collar, a torsor under the group of c). So b) and c) together make E
     contractible.
   - Conversely, suppose E is contractible, as the space of collar embeddings is in the smooth category (from memory).
     Then the space of strict collars is a classifying space of the group of c), and b) fails exactly when c) does.
   - What survives of his conclusion is the middle clause: the choice of a strict **rigidified** collar is anodyne (the
     smooth analogue; the tame case is open).
4. *I a).*
   - For topological manifolds and their boundaries this is Brown's collar theorem (1962; Pro P3).
   - For a tame "bordant" subspace, the word needs a definition (Pro N10). If "bordant" means locally collared, a) is a
     local-to-global collaring statement, open here in the tame category.
5. *II a) and b).*
   - a) is the local conic structure theorem of o-minimal geometry (from memory: Coste; van den Dries, Tame topology and
     o-minimal structures, 1998).
   - By 1 and the fibration argument of 3, b) is equivalent to the contractibility of the space of strict rigidified
     cones. Open.

**What is wrong:** I c) as literally stated, and with it I b).

**What is open:**
- I a) for tame "bordant" subspaces;
- II b);
- the contractibility of the space of rigidified collars in the tame category.

**The asymmetry:** the apex of a cone absorbs any automorphism (Alexander), while the free end of a collar does not.

## 3. SA20-4: the cascade of ∞-isotopic equivalences, tubular neighbourhoods, the equisingular case, canonical filtrations (119/149–153)

### 3.1 The six categories and the eleven functors (149–150)
- **The categories:**
  - PB: pairs (X, B), B bordant in X;
  - PBC: with a strict collar (C, Ċ);
  - PBCR: with a strict rigidified collar;
  - EP: pointed spaces (X, a);
  - EPC and EPCR: with a strict cone, and with a strict rigidified cone.
- **"On a une cascade d'équivalences de cat. ∞-isotopiques":** a diagram with PB on top and these arrows:
  - recoller B × I, PB → PBCR, labelled "B × {0}, nouvelle partie bordante B × {1}";
  - recoller Cône(B), PB → EPCR;
  - id_PB, down to PB;
  - the long arrow "contraction de B en un point", down to EP;
  - the dashed PBCR ⇢ EPCR and PBC ⇢ EPC;
  - the forgetful arrows, and PB → EP at the bottom.
- **Its 2-cells:** α (the oubli-collier side against id_PB) and β (the oubli-cône side against the contraction).
- **Reading decided (150, image):**
  - The margin reads "Système des **11** foncteurs" (retraced digits). The transcription's alternative "14" is out.
  - The first diagram has exactly eleven arrows: recoller B × I, recoller Cône(B), id_PB, the contraction PB → EP, the
    two dashed arrows, the four forgetful arrows, and the bottom PB → EP.
- **The label "contraction de B en un point"** runs along the long arrow PB → EP, as the transcription has it, not along
  the middle dashed arrow, as Hua has it.

**Working through.**
1. *α and β are isomorphisms once collars exist (re-derived).*
   - With a collar C ≅ B × I of B in X, (X ∪_B (B × I), B × {1}) ≅ (X, B): the external collar is absorbed into C, since
     C ∪_B (B × I) ≅ B × [−1, 1] ≅ C rel the outer end.
   - And X ∪_B Cone(B) ≅ X/B as pointed spaces. Indeed X/B = (X∖(C∖Ċ)) ∪_Ċ C/B, with C/B ≅ (B × I)/(B × {0}) =
     Cone(B), and Cone(B) ∪_B (B × I) ≅ Cone(B).
   - So α: forget∘recoller B × I ≅ id_PB and β: forget∘recoller Cône(B) ≅ the contraction. Both rest on I a).
2. *What the equivalences rest on.*
   - The forgetful functors PBCR → PBC → PB and EPCR → EPC → EP are equivalences of ∞-isotopic categories (mapping
     spaces of tame homeomorphisms) when the spaces of choices are contractible: I b), c) and II b), c).
   - By §2, the cone side is sound as far as II c) goes.
   - On the collar side, PBCR → PBC and PBC → PB cannot both be equivalences for B whose pseudo-isotopy space is not
     contractible. The composite PBCR → PB can be one: it needs only the space of rigidified collars to be contractible.
   - So the "système des 11 équivalences" holds after replacing collar automorphisms by fibrewise ones, or for B with a
     contractible concordance space (for example a point, or a disc, by the Alexander trick).
3. *The variants (150):*
   - "multipaires bordantes (B = ∐_{i∈I} Bᵢ), espaces multipointés (I ↪ X), I un ensemble fini variable", with the same
     diagram redrawn;
   - "Scholie: du pt de vue ∞-isotop., c'est pareil que de travailler avec l'une des six catégories équivalentes."
   - Consistent with the above, with the same caveat.

### 3.2 Tubular neighbourhoods (151)
**What he defines:**
- A tubular neighbourhood of a tame part Y of a tame space X "c'est par définition un cône strict autour de o de X/Y".
- **Construction:** f: X → ℝ⁺ tame with f⁻¹(0) = Y; V = f⁻¹([0, ε]) and V̇ = f⁻¹(ε), "ε petit [et admissible]".
- **Margin:** the choice of the tubular neighbourhood, or of the rigidified one, is anodyne; "V̇ bien défini en cat.
  ∞-isotopiques".
- **Rigidification "Y-admissible":** V − Y = V* ≃ V̇ × [0, 1[ and
  X ≃ ((X∖(V∖V̇)) ∐ (V̇ × I)) ∐_{V̇ × {1}, φ} Y, with φ: V̇ × {1} → Y tame and determined by the rigidification.
- **Claims:** a) a Y-admissible rigidification exists; b) the homotopy class of V̇ → Y is well determined. "Mais on
  voudrait plus précis."

**Reading decided (151):**
- The exterior is X∖(V∖V̇). On the ink the first V has a dark mark over it (a cancelled dot, or a dot), and the second
  carries its dot.
- The transcription's "X∖([V̇|V]∖V̇)" is resolved to V by the mathematics. Hua's "X∖(V̇∖V)" is not what the ink shows.
- The first "∐" must be read as a gluing along V̇ = V̇ × {0}.

**Working through.**
1. *The construction (re-derived; Hardt's triviality theorem cited from memory).*
   - For X compact and f tame, f restricted to X∖Y has finitely many bifurcation values.
   - For 0 < ε below the first one, f⁻¹((0, ε]) ≅ V̇ × (0, ε] over (0, ε]. So V/Y ≅ Cone(V̇).
   - V̇ = f⁻¹(ε) is collared in f⁻¹([ε, ε + δ]) ⊂ X∖(V∖V̇), so the cone is strict.
   - "Admissible" is then read as "no bifurcation value in (0, ε]". This is our reading; the page does not say.
2. *a) (route credited to Michel Hua's project).* Triangulate X definably with Y a full subcomplex. The simplicial
   neighbourhood of Y in the derived subdivision is the mapping cylinder of the simplicial retraction of its frontier
   onto Y (from memory: the standard structure of derived neighbourhoods). So a) is true for compact tame pairs.
3. *b) (re-derived).* V is the mapping cylinder M_φ, which deformation retracts onto Y, and φ is homotopic to the
   inclusion V̇ ↪ V followed by any retraction V → Y. So the homotopy class of φ depends only on (V, V̇, Y). The "plus
   précis" (a contractible space of choices) is open.

### 3.3 The equisingular case and the general case (152)
- **1°: X equisingular around Y.**
  - He presumes that φ can be taken fibrant, with an ∞-isotopic equivalence
    {(X′, B, Y, φ): (X′, B) a bordant pair, φ: B → Y fibrant} ⥲ {(X, Y) equisingular pairs},
    (X′, B, Y, φ) ↦ (X′ ∐_B Y, Y), glued by φ.
  - His NB: X∖Y and Y regular correspond to X′ a manifold with boundary B and Y regular.
  - *The NB, re-derived:* if X∖Y is a manifold and V a strict tubular neighbourhood, then X′ = X∖(V∖V̇) is a manifold
    with boundary V̇ (the strict collar of V̇).
  - *In today's terms:* the description of a two-stratum space by its resolution (unzipping): a manifold with boundary
    whose boundary fibres over the closed stratum, with the link as fibre. Compare Thom–Mather control data and
    Ayala–Francis–Tanaka (2017); these comparisons are credited to Michel Hua's project and Pro N28.
- **The margin:** "Je présume que X∖Y régulier implique que Y régulier (au moins si Y ⊂ closure(X∖Y))".
  - It is false without the equisingularity of 1°: X = ℝ², Y the two axes (Pro N29; re-derived).
  - It is true under it (re-derived). If the germ type of (X, Y) is constant along Y, every point of Y has the germ type
    of its generic points. These are manifold points of the top dimension of Y (tame: such points are dense in the
    top-dimensional part). So Y is a topological manifold.
  - In that setting the margin is nearly tautological.
- **2°: the general case.** He asks for conditions on φ: V̇ → Y "pour assurer l'unicité essentielle", for instance
  dim φ⁻¹(y) = c(y) − 1 with c(y) = codim_y(Y, X), local on V̇ instead of on Y.
  - *Checks of the condition (re-derived):*
    - for Y a submanifold, φ is the normal sphere bundle, with fibres of dimension c − 1;
    - for Y a point, the fibre is the link, of dimension n − 1 = c − 1;
    - for the cross Y = axes in X = ℝ², a mapping-cylinder structure whose fibre over the origin is 4 points (one corner
      per quadrant) satisfies it: dimension 0 = c(0) − 1.
  - Derived neighbourhoods of finer triangulations can have arcs over the origin, and they violate it. So the condition
    is a genuine constraint that selects among mapping-cylinder structures.
  - Whether it gives essential uniqueness is open (package R20-119_144-161_276-281-1, (d)).

### 3.4 Canonical filtrations (153)
**What he writes:**
- "On peut [bien] trouver encore une description de la catégorie ∞-isotopique des paires ⟨quelconques⟩ (X, Y) en termes
  d'une catégorie (X′, B, φ: B → Y)", with conditions on φ.
- "Filtrations équidimensionnelles et régulières d'un espace modéré. Il y en a je présume une qui est canonique, c'est la
  ~~plus~~ moins fine de toutes."
- "[Plus gén. pour filtration déjà donnée, il y a une filtration canonique, régulière équidimensionnelle, compatible avec
  celle-ci]"
- "Les invariants de dévissage de cette filtration sont donc des invariants de la structure modérée envisagée."

**Working through.**
1. *His correction of "plus" to "moins" is necessary (re-derived).* No finest regular equidimensional filtration exists.
   Adding a point p of the top stratum to every Xᵢ with i ≥ 0 gives a strictly finer filtration that is still regular
   and equidimensional. So only the coarsest one can be canonical.
2. *Existence:*
   - in the topological setting, the intrinsic stratification of King (1985) and its predecessors (Sullivan, Handel);
   - in the semialgebraic and subanalytic settings, the minimal Whitney stratification;
   - all from memory; King is cited by Michel Hua's project.
   - For tame spaces, with "regular" meaning manifold strata plus local triviality, it is open here.
3. *The last sentence:* if a canonical filtration exists, functoriality makes its dévissage invariants invariants of the
   tame structure. This is immediate.
4. *A caution* (Pro N34, re-derived): the choice of morphisms matters. The semialgebraic homeomorphism t ↦ (t², t³) from
   a line onto a cusp shows that "regular" must mean topological (manifold strata), not smooth.

**Status of SA20-4:**
- **Sound parts:** α and β; II c); the construction and b) of 151; the correction plus → moins.
- **Wrong:** the collar part as stated, through I c).
- **Open:** its main claims: the cascade as equivalences; 1°; 2°; the canonical filtration in tame topology.

### 4.2 The square torsors (156–157): checked by computation
**His formulas:**
- 156: "ε ∧ ε* ∧ ω(Q) = 1; ω(Q) ≃ ω(Q*); Q* ≃ ε ∧ Q; Q ≃ ε* ∧ Q*".
- 157, boxed: "Q* = ε ∧ Q^∨" and "ε* ∧ ε ∧ ω ≃ 1".
- 157, margin: "ε ∧ codiagQ ≃ ε* ∧ codiagQ* (≃ ω(S), ω(S*)), i.e. ε ∧ ε* ∧ codiagQ ∧ codiagQ* ≃ 1, d'où codiagQ* ≃ diagQ".

**The set-up.** Q is a square on four points of S (the cyclic order 0-1-2-3), and ε the complementary pair. Its stabilizer
has order 16, the Sylow 2-subgroup D₄ × 𝔽₂ of 157. Its image is the stabilizer of a square Q* on four totals, with
complementary pair ε*. For each two-element set the script computes the character of the stabilizer: ε, ε*, the
diagonals diag, the two pairs of opposite sides codiag, the sign of the four-point set, and the sense of rotation
rot = diag ∧ codiag.

**The relations that hold** (output §3):
- ε ∧ ε* ∧ rot(Q) = 1;
- rot(Q) = rot(Q*);
- codiag Q* = diag Q, and codiag Q = diag Q*;
- codiag Q = ω(4-set Q).

The orientations of the two four-point sets are **not** equal: they differ by rot.

**Conclusions:**
- In 156–157, **ω(Q) is the orientation of the square (its sense of rotation), not the orientation of the underlying
  four-element set.** With that reading:
  - his first two identities hold;
  - the 157 margin derivation is correct step by step (ε ∧ ε* ≃ ω(Q) = diag Q ∧ codiag Q, hence codiag Q* ≃ diag Q);
  - ω(S) ≃ ω(S*) holds because the outer automorphism preserves A₆.
- With the other reading the first two identities fail.
- "Q* ≃ ε ∧ Q; Q ≃ ε* ∧ Q*" cannot hold with Q read as diag or as codiag: together the two would give ε ∧ ε* ≃ 1,
  contradicting the first identity. The same goes for the boxed "Q* = ε ∧ Q^∨" (Q^∨ undefined). These are statements
  about squares twisted by ε, which the pages do not define (Hua and Pro N43 also leave them).

### 4.3 The affine plane over 𝔽₃, the "trilitaire" group and the three G_α (156): reconstructed and checked
**What he writes:**
- V is a plane over 𝔽₃; E = P(V) is its four directions, with 𝔖_{P(V)} ≃ Aut(V)(𝔽₃) ≃ GP(1, 𝔽₃).
- "une partition (2,2) de E (en antipodismes) i.e. Structure carrée sur E"; card Eᵢ = 3; Eᵢ × Eⱼ ⥲ V for i ≠ j.
- "Groupe d'automorphismes trilitaire d'ordre 3³ × 2⁴ = 3·144, opère sur 𝔖_ε (ε = ∐ Eᵢ), et laisse invariant un syst.
  de 3 sous-groupes G_α (α ∈ Car(E) = Antip.(E)). Le stabilisateur d'un G_α est le groupe des automorphismes d'une
  bi-ditriade. Opère fidèlement sur G_α, sous groupe d'ordre 10 de Aut(G_α) (d'ordre 2·(6!))."
- Margin: the G_α "ont un 3-sous-groupe de Sylow V₀ en commun"; "N(V₀)/V₀ … (groupe du carré)".

**Reconstruction:**
- **G_α ≅ S₆.** For α = {{i, j}, {k, l}}, a pairing of the four directions, the twelve lines split into the six-sets
  Eᵢ ∐ Eⱼ and E_k ∐ E_l. G_α is the graph of an outer isomorphism Sym(Eᵢ ∐ Eⱼ) → Sym(E_k ∐ E_l). The two six-sets play
  the roles of S and S*, and the ditriads {Eᵢ, Eⱼ} and {E_k, E_l} correspond under the duality.
- **The stabilizer.** The stabilizer of α in AGL₂(𝔽₃) is AΓL₁(𝔽₉), of order 144. It is the stabilizer of a point of
  P¹(𝔽₉) in PΓL₂(𝔽₉) ≅ Aut(S₆), hence of index 10.
- **The bi-ditriade** of 156 is the pair (ditriad of S, dual ditriad of S*). Its automorphism group, swaps of S and S*
  included, is that AΓL₁(𝔽₉).

**Checks** (output §4–6; computation):
- |AGL₂(𝔽₃)| = 432 = 3·144.
- The kernel of the action on the directions has order 18. It acts on (Eᵢ, Eⱼ) by pairs of equal sign: the
  (𝔖₃ × 𝔖₃)′ of III.5.
- Each pairing α has a stabilizer of order 144. Its linear part has order 16, with five involutions and elements of order
  8: semidihedral, the Sylow 2-subgroup ΓL₁(𝔽₉) of GL₂(𝔽₃).
- **G_α:** for each α there is exactly one graph subgroup of outer type that contains the nine translations and is
  normalized by the part of Stab(α) preserving the two six-sets. No graph of inner type qualifies. Each G_α has order
  720.
- AGL₂(𝔽₃) permutes the three G_α, and they are distinct.
- Stab(α) normalizes G_α. |Stab(α) ∩ G_α| = 72, the S₃ ≀ S₂ of a ditriad. Only the identity of Stab(α) centralizes G_α.
  - So Stab(α) embeds in Aut(G_α) ≅ Aut(S₆), of order 1440, **with index 10**.
- The translations V₀ form a Sylow 3-subgroup common to the three G_α. N_{G_α}(V₀) has order 72, and N/V₀ has one
  element of order 1, five of order 2 and two of order 4: D₄, "le groupe du carré".

**So the 156 passage is correct**, read with "d'ordre 10" as index 10 and 2·(6!) as written. "Trilitaire" names the
triality: S₄ acts on the four directions, and through S₄ → S₃ on the three pairings α, it permutes three S₆'s.

**The terminology moves between pages:**
- On 160, II.2 gives "1 → 𝔖₃ × 𝔖₃ → Aut d'une bi-di-triade → [ℤ₂|𝔊₂] → 1", of order 72. That is the part preserving S
  and S* (Stab(α) ∩ G_α, order 72).
- On 156 the full group, of order 144, includes the swaps.
- On 160, III.5 calls the affine-plane structure itself "quadritriades (ou bi-di-triades)".
- Pro N54 flagged the clash; the computation resolves it.

### 4.4 "Trialité des bi-di-triades" (159): re-derived
- **A) The structure on V:** a set E of twelve triples meeting pairwise in at most one point. The written "∀ a, b ∈ E"
  lacks a ≠ b (his slip; Pro N49). With distinct triples:
  - 12·3 = 36 = C(9,2), so every pair of points lies in exactly one triple;
  - this is a Steiner triple system on 9 points, unique up to isomorphism: AG(2,3) (from memory; Pro N50 enumerated it).
- **B) The structure on E:**
  - a partition into four classes of three;
  - V, a set of four-element subsets with a) each D meeting each class (exactly) once, b) two distinct D meeting in one
    point, c) two elements of different classes lying in a unique D.
  - His "b) résulte de a) et c)" is right. By c), two blocks share at most one point. By a) and c), each point lies on
    three blocks, so a block meets 4 × 2 = 8 others at its four points, and these are all the other blocks.
- **"NB c implique déjà que card V = 9":** false with c) alone. Take the 18 blocks Eᵢ ∪ {b}, i < j, b ∈ Eⱼ; each pair
  from different classes lies in exactly one of them (Pro N51, re-derived).
  - With a), card V = 9: there are 54 pairs from different classes and 6 in each block.
  - And three blocks pass through each point.
- **Margins:**
  - Ê = E ∐ {∞} and V̂ = V ∐ {Eᵢ ∪ {∞}}, with any two distinct lines of V̂ meeting in exactly one point.
    - Re-derived: the lines Eᵢ ∪ {∞} meet only at ∞; D ∈ V meets Eᵢ ∪ {∞} once by a); two D meet once by b).
    - So (Ê, V̂) is the projective plane of order 3, and "E est un plan projectif sur 𝔽₃ privé d'un de ses points" is
      right.

## 5. SA20-6: the Orsay commission report (119/276–281)

**What it is.**
- "Rapport de la Commission relative au recrutement des enseignants", Département de Mathématique, Faculté des Sciences
  d'Orsay.
- Pages 1–5 are handwritten copy in a neat hand that is not Grothendieck's (276–280). Page "-6-" is typed (281).
- **Dating:** 1968, before October ("en fonctions avant la rentrée d'Octobre 1968", 278). Per Michel Hua's project, the
  dossier keeps next to it a circulaire announcing a meeting "le mardi 11 juin". 11 June 1968 was a Tuesday (checked),
  which fits June 1968.
- It is a collective document, not his. The page carries no personal data: no names.

**Its content (read in full):**
- **I, the organisation.** An École de Mathématique (first and second cycles, non-classical teaching with other
  departments) and a Laboratoire de Recherche Mathématique (third cycle, research environment). Every teacher belongs
  to one of the two sections.
  - Decisions on teaching, appointments and organic changes belong to the Assemblée du département: maîtres de
    conférences and professeurs; assistants and maîtres-assistants or their elected representatives, at most one sixth;
    elected students, one third.
  - The Assembly decides by absolute majority, and by two thirds for appointments and organic changes.
  - Service: three hours weekly in the Laboratoire (half a third-cycle course) and six hours in the École. Staff in post
    before October 1968 choose their section.
- **II, criteria.** A Doctorat ès Sciences with the mention "Enseignement" or "Recherche", by section.
- **III, recruitment:**
  - by the Assembly;
  - junior posts by a commission with at least one third and at most one half students, deciding with two thirds of its
    members;
  - senior posts in plenary session with two thirds of the voters;
  - a student veto for École appointments, effective with two thirds of the student representatives;
  - Laboratoire appointments by a commission at least two thirds Laboratoire staff, if a majority of the Laboratoire asks.
- **IV, detachments:**
  - one semester every four years for École staff, without teaching;
  - one-year renewable detachments both ways;
  - permanent transfer "par les mêmes modalités qu'une première nomination".

**Checked:** the voting arithmetic of the Pro notes, re-derived:
- students one third plus assistants at most one sixth make at most one half (N64);
- a six-member commission with two student seats can reach four votes without the students, and with three seats it
  needs one student vote (N68);
- in an 18-member Assembly with six students, four student votes make the veto (N69).

**How it connects.** It has the architecture of the "B. Demain" part of his June 1968 text "Le maître-enseignant et le
maître-chercheur" (119/254–270, outside this stretch), as Michel Hua's project says ("c'est, sous d'autres noms,
l'architecture de la partie B"). The names and numbers differ:
- Laboratoire against Institut;
- a semester every four years against a year in five (Pro N63, N71).

It is not mathematics. For the edition it is a document by other authors, for the user's decision (DELIVERABLES).
