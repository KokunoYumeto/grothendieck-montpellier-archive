## A. SA20-13: the one-space axioms and cellular decompositions (121/002-024)

### A.1 The title sheet (002)

**What is there.** Faint pencil diagrams:
- cubes I^{n-1} -> I^n <- I^{n+1};
- a space X presented as the coequalizer of coprod_{alpha in Fl I} X(alpha) => coprod_{i in Ob I} X_i -> X;
- a boxed condition on beta: X -> M: a) beta o p_i moderate for all i; b) beta injective;
- a triangle X x_{Y'} X = X -> Y' with u: X -> Z, and its underlying-set version, with |u| marked "mod" and v marked
  "mod?";
- the graph square Gamma(|u|) ⊂ |X| x |Z| over Gamma(v) ⊂ |Y'| x |Z|, the left arrow circled "épi", with "cont";
- the equivalence relation R = X x_Y X ⊂ X x X => X -> Y.

**What it asks, and the answer the page sketches (re-derived).**
- Suppose g: X -> Y' is surjective on points and u = v o g with u moderate. Then Gamma(v) = (|g| x id)(Gamma(u)): the
  point (x, u(x)) goes to (g(x), v(g(x))), and surjectivity gives all of Gamma(v).
- So v is moderate as soon as images of moderate parts under moderate maps are moderate (the image axiom: M1 of 120/003).
- If the spaces are compact Hausdorff, Gamma(v) is compact, hence closed, and v is continuous (a closed graph into a
  compact space): his "cont".
- This is the graph lemma that the 121_049 reader found answering the effective-epimorphism question on 121/054.

So the sheet belongs with the third approach of the folder (121/054-073 and cote 120). Michel Hua's project says the
same ("les ingrédients de la troisième mouture").

### A.2 §1 "(Structure modérée sur un espace)" (004-010)

**004: the Boolean algebra and its function algebra.** F ⊂ P(X) with b) finite unions, b') finite intersections,
a) complements; A_F = the finitely-valued Z-valued functions with fibres in F. Re-derived:
- a) <=> a') (characteristic functions of members are in A_F), given ∅ ∈ F.
- The margin "moyennant b) A_F contient les constantes": constants need ∅, X ∈ F.
- F = {A : phi_A ∈ A_F}. Under a), b) <=> b') <=> b'') (A_F stable under products: phi_A phi_B = phi_{A∩B}).
- Then A_F is stable under sums and f -> -f: a unital subring of Z^X. His "X^Z" is a slip for Z^X (as the Pro notes and
  Michel Hua's project say).
- The same holds with any non-zero ring Lambda: A_F is a Lambda-subalgebra of the step functions Et(X, Lambda).

**005: saturation.**
- *His claim:* "Lorsque Lambda est intègre de corps de fractions K, on peut [prouver|penser] que les sous-algèbres A de
  Et(X,Lambda) ainsi obtenues sont les traces ... des sous-algèbres de Et(X,K)". These are the saturated ones
  (lambda ≠ 0, lambda phi ∈ A => phi ∈ A).
- *Check (re-derivation; the Lagrange argument is Michel Hua's project's):*
  - A_F is saturated: Lambda is a domain, so the fibres of lambda phi are those of phi.
  - Conversely, let A be a saturated **unital** subalgebra and f ∈ A with distinct values c_1, ..., c_k. Then
    prod_{l≠j}(f - c_l) = d_j phi_{f^{-1}(c_j)} with d_j = prod_{l≠j}(c_j - c_l) ≠ 0.
  - By saturation phi_{f^{-1}(c_j)} ∈ A. Hence A = A_F with F = {B : phi_B ∈ A}, a Boolean algebra.
- *Refinement:* "unital" is needed ({0} is saturated and is no A_F). With it the claim holds, so the verb can be
  "prouver": N03's missing proof is supplied.

**005: closure.**
- c) Ā ∈ F for A ∈ F <=> c') A° ∈ F (under a)). Hence Ȧ = Ā - A° ∈ F, and X = A° ⊔ Ȧ ⊔ (X - Ā) is a partition by
  members. Re-derived.
- A first d) (connected components "ouvertes, de nb fini et ∈ F") is struck here. It is the later f) of 010, under an
  earlier letter (see A.3).

**006: d), e) and the Prop.**

  - The relative reading is the one the rest uses (011: "son intérieur est ≠ ∅ dans X̄_i"; 007: "l'int. rel. de C dans
    D = C̄").

    member, which §2 needs.
- **e):** no infinite decreasing chain of closed members A_1 ⊃ A_2 ⊃ ... with A_{n+1} nowhere dense in A_n.

  - *Refinement:* true when Phi is closed under finite ∪, ∩ and contains ∅, X. (The Pro notes' N05 shows the literal
    formula fails for Phi = {{1}} on {1, 2}.)
  - In its use (Phi = the closed members F_0) these hold. Re-derived.
- **Prop: if F satisfies d and e, F is generated by its closed members.**

**007-008: when is a family of closed sets the closed part of its Boolean algebra?**

**008-009: a topology from F_0 alone.** For a bare set X, a topology exists in which the members of F_0 are closed and

**009, NB: under a)-d) the boundary Ȧ of a member is nowhere dense.**
- The page's proof is fragmentary: a reduction to Ā = X, A° = ∅, then cramped insertions.

  - U is open and inside Ā, so U ∩ A ≠ ∅. U ∩ A has empty interior, since an open subset of A lies in A°, which is
    disjoint from Ȧ.
  - Every point of U lies in cl(U ∩ A).
  - By d), U ∩ A contains a set W, open and dense in cl(U ∩ A). Then W ∩ U is a non-empty open set of X inside A and
    inside Ȧ: a contradiction.
- This is the step the Prop of 006 and the existence theorem of 015 use.

- If the open members form a basis, f) makes the locally closed members locally connected.
  - *Check:* take x ∈ A and a basic open member V ∋ x. Then V ∩ A = B - (C ∪ ∁V) is a locally closed member, and its
    finitely many components are open in it.
- "En fait cette restriction est inutile": true, once f) is known for all members (019-020, A.6). Finitely many
  components make each one open in V ∩ A.

**The setting.** P = (X_i)_{i∈I} is a finite partition, and F_P the family of unions of leaves. Re-derived:
- F_P satisfies a) and b), and A_{F_P} is the algebra of functions constant on the leaves.
- The margins: F_P ↔ P(X/R); P is the set of minimal non-empty members; the P correspond to the finite F.

isolé)".

  - Then induct on X minus that leaf, which is open. 1° and 2° pass to it, because cl(X̄_i ∩ X') = X̄_i. Re-derived.
- The margin NB of 012: a non-incident leaf misses the closure, and X̄_j ∩ X̄_i is nowhere dense in X̄_i. Re-derived.

  - The mathematics is f) (components). The letter d) is the **old lettering**: on 005 the components condition was

  - N12 and N19 kept the literal letters; this settles them.

  - f) => 3): a leaf is a locally closed minimal non-empty member, so it has one component.

**The finite quotient (013-014).** Y = X/R ≅ I is a finite space.
- The closure of {i} corresponds to X̄_i, so incidence is specialization.
- 2° says that I is T_0 (his "présobre"; for a finite space, T_0 and sober coincide, from memory).
- 1° says phi^{-1} commutes with closure, equivalently that phi: X -> I is open.
  - *Check (N80's open point):* for J ⊂ I, cl(phi^{-1}J) = ∪_{j∈J} X̄_j is saturated by 1°. So it is phi^{-1} of a
    closed set containing J, and phi^{-1}(cl J) = cl(phi^{-1}J).

    ways.
- So the finite cellular decompositions are the finite T_0 quotients with open quotient map. This is the now-standard
  picture of a stratification as an open continuous map to a finite poset with its Alexandrov topology. (Michel Hua's

### A.4 §3 the existence theorem (014-017)

**Definition (014).** P is F-admissible if its leaves are members (F_P ⊂ F).

**Prop (015).** Let F satisfy a)-e) and let Phi ⊂ F be finite. Then there is an F-admissible cellular decomposition in
which every Y ∈ Phi is a union of leaves, and among these a coarsest one, unique.

**Construction (015-016):**
- X' = ∪_{Y∈Phi} Ẏ is closed and nowhere dense, by the NB of 009.
- U = ∁X'. The Y ∩ U are open and closed in U, and the U_i are the atoms of the Boolean algebra they generate in U.
- Recurse on (X', Phi'), with Phi' = {Y ∩ X'} ∪ {Ū_i ∩ X'}. The recursion ends by e).

**Check (re-derivation, existence and minimality).**
- *The atoms need complements.* 015 says only "intersection des Y ∩ U" (N15); 018 adds "QPS Phi stable par ∁". With
  that, each U_i is a member, open and closed in U.
- *The decomposition (X'_j) ∪ (U_i):*
  - 1°: Ū_i = U_i ∪ (Ū_i ∩ X'), and Ū_i ∩ X' ∈ Phi' is a union of the X'_j.
  - 2°: Ū_i ∩ U = U_i and Ū_i ⊄ X'.
- *Minimality.* Let Q be any admissible decomposition in which the Y are unions of leaves.
  - Ȳ, Y° and Ẏ are unions of Q-leaves (by 1° for Q), so X' and U are as well.
  - Each Q-leaf in U lies in one atom.
  - Ū_i is the closure of a union of Q-leaves, so it is a union of Q-leaves. Hence Q|X' is admissible for (X', Phi').
  - By induction along the chain of e), Q|X' refines the decomposition built for (X', Phi').
- **The theorem holds as stated.** Michel Hua's project reports a Lean check of existence and of the coarsest
  decomposition (lean/Grothendieck/Folder121.lean); that is its claim, not re-run here.

**Corollaries (016).** Both re-derived:
- Cor 1: F is the filtered union of the F_P, P admissible.
- Cor 2: admissible decompositions have finite suprema (coarsest common refinements).
- The struck (Dim), chains of bounded length, is replaced by e), with the margin "résulte en particulier de l'existence
  d'une dimension finie".

**The Remarque (017): strict decompositions.** With f) as well, there is a coarsest admissible decomposition with
connected leaves.
- Replace the U_i by their components, which are finitely many members by f).
- Minimality holds as above, because connected Q-leaves lie in components of atoms. Re-derived.

### A.5 §4 regular decompositions (017-018)

**The setting.**
- F_r ⊂ F is a set of locally closed members, the "regular" ones.
- Condition (B|S): every closed member Y has a largest relatively open regular part Y_reg, dense in Y. Its complement
  Y_sing is nowhere dense.
- P is F-regular if, for every closed P-saturated Y, Y_sing is P-saturated (018). The page adds: "(Ceci implique que
  les feuilles sont ∈ F_r)".

**This last claim is wrong as written (the Pro notes' N79, verified).**
- Take the two-point Sierpiński space X = {0, 1}, with closed sets ∅, {0}, X. Let F = P(X) and F_r = {∅, {0}, X}.
- Then (S) holds, and the singleton partition is F-regular, since every closed saturated Y has Y_sing = ∅.
- But its leaf {1} is not in F_r.

**Refinement (mine).** The implication holds when F_r is stable under passage to relatively open members. Regular sets
are so for every usual notion (smooth points, manifold points).
- (X̄_i)_sing is saturated and nowhere dense in X̄_i, so it cannot contain the dense leaf X_i.
- Hence X_i misses it, lies in (X̄_i)_reg, and is relatively open there, so X_i ∈ F_r.

**The Prop of 018.** Under a)-e) and (S): for finite Phi there is an F-regular partition with the Y ∈ Phi unions of
leaves, and a coarsest one. With f) ("d)" in the ink, A.3) there is one with connected leaves.
- *The proof:* X' = ∪(Ẏ ∪ (Ȳ)_sing), reading the ring of the ink as the singular locus of the closure. Then recurse,
  with the (Ū_i)_sing added to Phi'.
- *The corollaries:* Cor 1-3 give the filtered union, the finite suprema and the **canonical F-regular partition** of X.
- *Checked:*
  - X' is closed and nowhere dense.
  - On U the Y ∩ U are regular (relatively open in (Ȳ)_reg), given the stability above and "Phi ∋ X, stable by ∁".
  - The recursion ends by e).
- *Open: a gap in the sketch.* The sketch secures (S) for the members of Phi and for the closures of the open leaves.
  The definition asks it for every closed saturated union of closures. Whether Y_sing is saturated for such a union
  depends on the germ of the union along each lower leaf, and the sketch adds no sets that control it.
- *Strongest reduction:* if regularity is local, add at each stage the singular loci of all the finitely many unions of
  the new closures. The recursion still ends by e), since each new X'' is a finite union of closed nowhere dense sets.

**023-024: the sheaf axioms.** He takes a subsheaf F̲ of the sheaf of germs of subsets with a)-d), and e) in local form:

## B. SA20-14: mobility, equisingular partitions, the two conjectures, gluing and reconstruction (121/026-048)

### B.1 Mobility (026-028, his "1.")

**Definitions (026).**
- Let X be a space and Y a "non-singular" subspace (a manifold).
- *Equisingularity:* X is equisingular along Y if every y ∈ Y has an open U and a homeomorphism U ≃ N x Y_U, sending
  Y_U to {a} x Y_U by the canonical map, for some pointed space (N, a).
- *Mobility:* x is "ν-mobile" if X is equisingular along some manifold Y ∋ x with dim_x Y ≥ ν. Its order of mobility
  ν(x) is the largest such ν; order 0 means "immobile" (élémentaire, over a struck "spéciale").
- NB: Y can then be chosen of dimension exactly ν.
  - *Re-derived:* restrict to a coordinate ν-plane Y' ⊂ Y_U ≅ R^m and use
    N x R^m = (N x R^{m-ν}) x R^ν, with the point (a, 0).
- If X is locally finite-dimensional, ν(x) exists. *Re-derived:* a manifold of dimension m embedded in X forces
  dim X ≥ m near x.

**The filtration (027).** Mob^i(X), the set of i-mobile points, is open.
- *Re-derived:* in a chart U ≃ N x Y_U, every point (n, y) is i-mobile along {n} x Y_U, with the pointed space (N, n).
- Mob^0 = X ⊃ Mob^1 ⊃ ... and the closed sets F_i = X - Mob^{i+1} increase.
- The intersection of the Mob^i is empty, and the union of the F_i is X, iff ν is defined everywhere. Re-derived.
- The ink's "F_{[3|2]}" is F_2 by the sequence.

**Props A-D**, posed as questions ("on se pose la question ... de la validité des propositions"):
- A. Y_i = ν^{-1}(i) is a manifold, purely i-dimensional, and X is equisingular along it.
  - Margin: "Appelons ``[dévissables|divisibles]'' les espaces loc de dim finie pour lesquels prop A soit vraie".
  - *My look at the margin (027):* the word has an i before -ble and no double s, which favours "divisible" (Michel Hua's
    project's reading); the Pro read "dévissables". This is for the user's eye; the mathematics is unaffected.

**Checks and corrections.**
- **D, "<=": true.** If n has a chart N ⊃ U ≃ N' x Z with dim Z = ν-i, then Y x U ≃ N' x (Z x Y). Re-derived.
- **D, "=>": false in the topological category.** The remark is Michel Hua's project's ("double suspension"); the
  following version is mine, re-derived.
  - Let H be a homology 3-sphere with π_1(H) ≠ 1 (the Poincaré sphere), cH its open cone, a the cone point.
  - (i) a is not a manifold point of cH: its deleted cone neighbourhoods are ≃ H at every scale, with π_1 ≠ 1, while
    those of a manifold point of dimension 4 are simply connected.
  - (ii) a is immobile. If cH ≃ N' x (-1, 1) near a, translation along the factor makes the points (a', t) all locally
    alike. For t ≠ 0 they are manifold points (cH - {a} is a manifold), so a would be one too.
  - (iii) R x cH is a 5-manifold. A point of the suspension circle of Σ²H = S¹ * H has a neighbourhood ≅ R x cH, and

  - With Y = R² one gets Michel Hua's project's form, R² x cH ≅ R^6.
- **B's first sentence follows from A, using only the true direction of D, in every context (TOP included).**
  - If X is equisingular along Y_i (A), then locally (X, Y_i) ≃ Y_i x (N, a).
  - If a were k-mobile with k ≥ 1, then by D's "<=" the points (y, a) would be (i+k)-mobile. But they have order
    exactly i, so a is immobile.
  - So "**sous** réserve de Prop A" is the reading that makes sense (the Pro's enlarged crop favours "sous").
- **B's bracket is not implied.** It says the local type of (N, a) depends only on the component of Y_i, i.e. the

- **C fails in TOP (new here).** In X = R x cH, a 5-manifold, so Y_5 = X, take the line Y = R x {a}.

  - Y is not locally flat in X: locally, X - Y ≃ H x (stuff) has non-trivial π_1, whereas a locally flat line in a

  - So if "régulièrement immergée" means locally flat, as for a "sous-variété régulièrement plongée", the germ of Y is
    an equisingular direction outside the correspondence of C. The wild suspension circles of the double suspension are

**Definitions (030).**

**Filtrations and leaves (031-032).** An increasing closed filtration is equisingular if the diagram (X_0 -> X_1 ->
... -> X) is equisingular along each V_i = X_i - X_{i-1}; the leaves are the components of the V_i.
- **Prop: the closure of a leaf is a union of leaves.**
  - *Re-derived:* let G be a leaf of V_k meeting F̄, F a leaf of V_i (so k ≤ i).
  - If k = i, then G = F, because the components of V_i are closed in V_i.
  - If k < i, take a product chart at a point of G ∩ F̄ with Y' connected. There F is a union of sets C x Y', with C
    a component of the local piece, so {a} x Y' ⊂ F̄. Hence G ∩ F̄ is open in G, and it is closed, so G ⊂ F̄.

- **Closed leaves.** F ↦ F̄ is injective, since F = F̄ ∩ V_i with i least such that F̄ ⊂ X_i. The bord F̄ - F =
  F̄ ∩ X_{i-1} is closed and is the union of the strictly incident leaves. Re-derived.
- **Prop (032): equisingular <=> a) frontier condition + b) the diagram of X and all the closed leaves is equisingular
  along each leaf.**
  - "=>": the chart of the filtration is a chart of every closed leaf, which is locally (∪C̄) x Y'.
  - "<=": the X_i are unions of closed leaves, so they are products in the same chart.
  - Re-derived, with local finiteness of the leaves assumed (true in the piecewise contexts).
- **Partitions.**
  - A partition is "cellulaire" (margin) if 1°) each leaf is a difference of closed sets and 2°) closures are unions of
    leaves. This is §2's notion (011-012) without finiteness, with local closedness in place of §2's 2°, equivalent to

  canoniques": by dimension, by iterated minimality, and the two duals. Their closedness is re-derived from the frontier
  condition and the drop of dimension.

  locally closed union of leaves is a product in each chart, since each leaf is F̄ minus a closed union of closed leaves.
- **Admissible systems on a diagram (033-034).** b) every arrow maps a leaf into a leaf, giving a diagram of incidence
  posets Φ_i -> Φ_j and of maps between closed leaves. The system is equisingular if that diagram is equisingular along

### C.2 SA20-9: the "remords"

**From cotes 120-121: there is no remords, no calculation answering it, and no date on these pages.**

**What the pages contribute to its subject.** The worry, as the Pro notes give it from 147/179-180 (not read here): the
sphere with one marked point, whose two strata are simply connected, is not recovered in homotopy by the ordinary
constructible topos (one arrow), which misses H²(S²). Three things on 121 bear on this.
- **039 keeps the link as a space.** In the recollement (𝓕, 𝓖, α: p^*𝓕 -> i^*𝓖), Z and p are kept as a space and a
  map.
  - For the marked sphere (X = D², Z = S¹ -> Y = pt), Mayer-Vietoris gives H²(S², Λ) = H¹(S¹, Λ) = Λ (re-derived, B.4).
  - Gluing data of this kind do not lose the class the remords is about. The loss happens only when the link is replaced
    by its π_0 (the poset of strata) or by a 1-categorical colimit.
- **041-047 rebuild the space up to homeomorphism.** They work from manifolds with fibred boundaries, by mapping-cylinder
  collapses (044: V = V° ∐_{U'} U, W = W° ∐_{V'} V). This keeps strictly more than any homotopy-level datum.
- **043 is a π_1-level sketch,** low-confidence reading:

  - edges with a ℤ/2-torsor of orientations;
  - "groupoïde 1-spécial";
  - for 2-leaves, "foncteurs admissibles" 𝓕 -> 𝓖 into "groupoïdes 2-spéciaux".
  - This is exactly the level of data the remords tests. On the marked sphere the datum is BZ -> 1 at one vertex:
    - glued on classifying spaces (homotopy pushout of * <- S¹ -> *) it gives S², with H² = Λ;
    - glued as groupoids (1 *_Z 1) it gives a point.
  - The Pro notes' Q2.7 has the same computation. The page shows the data but no gluing rule, so it cannot say on which
    side he stood when he wrote it.

### C.3 SA20-10: are his axioms those of an o-minimal structure?

**No, as written, for each of the systems on cotes 120 and 121.** The 152_120 and 121_049-073 readers showed it for
120's M0-M11 and for the five drafts of 121/054-073. What 121/004-024 adds is the other half of the comparison.

**1. The one-space axioms a)-f) (121/004-024).** They hold for the definable subsets of a definable X in an o-minimal
expansion of R. (Pro notes Q3.3, after van den Dries-Miller 1996 §§4.2-4.3, 4.7; not reopened.)
- They also hold for the constructible subsets of a Noetherian topological space (check, mine; EGA locators from
  memory):
  - Boolean; the closure of a constructible set is constructible;
  - d) a constructible C contains an open dense subset of C̄: in each irreducible component Z_k of C̄, a locally closed
    piece of C is open dense; remove the other components;
  - e) Noetherian chains are finite;
  - f) a constructible locally closed set has finitely many components, each a union of irreducible components, open in
    it and constructible.
- So a)-f) are a common abstraction of EGA's constructible sets (EGA 0_III §9, IV §1.8; from memory) and of o-minimal
  definable sets. They do not single out o-minimality.
- On X = R with its order topology, f) for all members (019-020) says that every member is a finite union of points and
  intervals: the o-minimal finiteness on the line. But here it is for one family on one space, not for a structure
  closed under projection.

**2. The all-arity axioms (120's M0-M11; 121/054-073).**
- They hold closure under coordinate images (projections), products, inverse images, the graphs of + and x, the points
  R_0 and finite unions, all for compact sets, with no finiteness axiom.
- The all-compact model satisfies them (the first pass's Cantor-set test; the 152_120 and 121_049-073 readers), so they
  are not o-minimal.

**3. The two halves together (mine).**
- Let S_n ⊂ P(R^n) be Boolean algebras closed under products with R, coordinate permutations and projections,
  containing the diagonals and the order.
- Such an S is o-minimal iff every member of S_1 has finitely many connected components: a subset of R with finitely
  many components is a finite union of points and intervals. That is 121's f) applied to S_1 on X = R.
- So **o-minimality is the conjunction of 120's all-arity closure (in Boolean form) and 121/010's f) on the line.** Each
  draft holds one half, and neither combines them on these pages.

**4. Why the compact form cannot get there by itself (mine; literature from memory).**
- 120 and 121/054-073 axiomatize compact members only. A structure and its open core (the reduct generated by the open,
  equivalently the closed, definable sets) have the same closed definable sets.
- So compact axioms can at best describe structures up to open core, and there are non-o-minimal structures with
  o-minimal open core: the dense pair (R, R_alg) (van den Dries 1998; Miller-Speissegger 1999; Dolich-Miller-Steinhorn
  2010, all from memory).
- Even 120's M0-M11 with "every member of M_1 is a finite union of points and closed intervals" added would characterize
  at most structures with o-minimal open core. Whether the Boolean-projection closure of such an M_* is o-minimal is a
  theorem-of-the-complement question (Gabrielov for the subanalytic case, from memory), open on the pages.
- This agrees with the 121_049-073 reader's hunch H20-121_049-073_153_155-1 and sharpens it.

**5. Local completion.**
- His sheafification F -> F̄ (020-024) is the passage from a global (finite) theory to a local one, as between o-minimal
  structures and the analytic-geometric categories of van den Dries-Miller (Pro notes Q3.6, checked there).
- The page sees where the two differ:
  - e) may fail for F̄ (020);
  - compactness gives F̄ = F under the basis hypothesis (020), and not without it (021);
  - the uniform dimension axiom e_n restores e) (024).
- In the van den Dries-Miller dictionary, compactness (the passage through P^n(R)) plays this role.

**6. Scalar extension and the real-closed-field test** (the title of SA20-10). These concern 120/021-024 (M11). For the
o-minimal comparison: once the order and f) on the line are added, the field R must be real closed, since an o-minimal
ordered field is real closed (Pillay-Steinhorn 1986, from memory). Nothing on 121/001-048 bears further on it.
