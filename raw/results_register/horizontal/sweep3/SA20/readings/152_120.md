2. **152/003, the first working sheet.** "disques découpés avec origine fixée ⟷ arbre circularisé pointé"; "type de mots = arbres [plans ou] pointés, … bouts libres, en l'origine seult et … plats à l'intérieur".

     - The condition: each cut component carries at least one, or at most one, vertex "(au choix)".
   - **The claim (l.77).** "La cat. des ``types de mots'' de long. n équivaut : la cat. \unsure{isotypique|isotopique} des systèmes formés d'un disque D [en général orienté] d'un ``découpage par cordes'' de ce disque, avec au moins un sommet sur chaque \unsure{arête|cpt.} découpée sur Ḋ par les extrémités des cordes".

   - **The reduced order and the conditions.**
     - "ordre réduit" = Σ_{α ⊂ R_0} (Card(α∩S) − 1), over the boundary arcs α.
     - Type trivial: reduced order 0. Type \unsure{totalitaire|réduit}: K = ∅, "(\unsure{non|i.e.} trivial)".
     - S ∩ α ≠ ∅ for every arc, and Card S∩α = 1 if α ⊄ R_0.
3. **152/004, "admissible" by recursion.** The structure is a "graphe \unsure{segment|segmenté}" with an involution σ on its edges.
   - Γ admissible of order n ≥ 1 iff σ = id, or there is an orbit ε of σ formed of two consecutive edges such that the graph Γ′ obtained by contracting to a point one of the edges of ε, with the involution σ′ deduced from σ, is admissible.
   - Margin: "Tout graphe inv. d'ordre 0 est adm. (ceux d'1 aussi)".
4. **152/005, "Arbres plans", his page 1.**
   - A tree is a connected, 1-connected graph, hence strict, so a structure (S, A) with A ⊂ 𝔓_2(S). A = ∅ ⟺ card S = 1.
   - A plane tree: a tree with a class of embeddings of |Γ| in a surface (germs: two embeddings are identified when neighbourhoods U, U′ are homeomorphic over the identity of |Γ|).
   - The classes of embeddings are the bicircularisations: pairs (ε, c), ε a two-element set and c: ε → circ(Γ) compatible with "passage à l'opposé".
   - In an oriented surface a class is a circularisation. It depends only on the A_s with card A_s ≥ 3, so "si les sommets de Γ sont d'ordre ≤ 1, il y en a un et un seul".
5. **152/007-008, his pages 1′, 2: the contour.**
   - Cutting X along a non-punctual plane tree gives a surface with connected boundary, a topological polygon whose combinatorial polygon depends only on Γ.
   - For a general circularised graph one gets a contour, not necessarily connected.
   - Ā is the set of arcs (oriented edges), σ_Γ the reversal and ρ_Γ the rotation round the origin vertex.
   - The block giving S ≃ Ā/ρ_Γ on 007 is cancelled.
   - 008 builds the contour C: rd identifies Ā with A(C) (the right side of each arc), Ā(C) ≃ Ā × {±1}, σ_0^C ≃ id × τ, and ρ_C = σ_1^C σ_0^C.
   - The boxed formulas for σ_1^C and ρ_C are heavily rewritten (d/g letters overwritten, exponents struck); the second pass left them unresolved.
6. **152/010, his page 2′.**
   - "Ces deux données … permettent de récupérer un graphe circularisé Γ, ayant Ā comme ens des arêtes orientées … σ_Γ = σ_C, ρ_Γ = ρ_C".
   - **"En résumé":** circularised graphs without isolated vertex, given as (Ā(Γ), ρ_Γ, σ_Γ), form a category equivalent to that of oriented contours with a fixed-point-free involution σ_C on the edges.
   - **The "correspondance fondamentale":** Ā(Γ) ≅ A(C); σ_Γ ⟷ \unsure{ρ_C|σ_C}; ρ_Γσ_Γ ↤ ρ_C; ρ_Γ ↤ ρ_Cσ_C.
   - **Margins:** "On appellera cela déploiement du graphe plan donné"; "ρ_Γ^{σ?} rotation renversée pour la \unsure{carte} actuelle définie par Γ".
   - **Boxed:** "Γ un arbre ⟺ C est un polygone comb. (non ponctuel), et l'involution σ_C de A(C) est totalement non pénétrante, i.e. pour deux orbites disjointes d'arêtes sous σ_C, \unsure{composition|leur position} est n.p.".
7. **152/011, his page 3.**
   - "``Arbres plans'' (non ponctuels) (dans un plan pas néc. ~~orienté~~) ⟷ polygones combinatoires, avec inv. sans pts fixes non pénétrante sur l'ens des ~~sommets~~ arêtes".

8. **152/013, his page 3′.**

### A2. Working through: what is proved, checked, wrong and open

**(a) Circularisations, 152/005.**
- **Count:** circ(Γ) = Π_s (cyclic orders on A_s), of order Π_s (card A_s − 1)!, which is 1 exactly when every vertex has degree ≤ 2. Re-derived.
- **His statement:** with "≤ 1" (the ink, settled in the second pass) it is true and weaker than his own preceding sentence gives (≤ 2). This is not an error; it is recorded as a refinement.
- **Bicircularisations:**
  - The regular neighbourhood of an embedded tree is a disc (his "d'où U ≃ ℝ²").
  - A germ of embedding is a rotation system up to the choice of one of the two orientations of the disc. That is a pair (ε, c) with c(−) the reversal of c(+).
  - Simultaneous reversal acts freely on circ(Γ) as soon as one vertex has degree ≥ 3, so there are Π(card A_s − 1)!/2 classes in that case and one otherwise.
  - Re-derived; this agrees with the first pass's P06.

**(b) The contour and the dictionary, 152/007-010.**
- **Cutting (007).** Cutting a surface along a non-punctual tree yields one boundary circle, the boundary walk of the tree, a polygon with 2E sides, one per arc. Re-derived.
- **The dictionary (010).** In the standard encoding of a map by darts Ā, the reversal σ (fixed-point free) and the rotation ρ, the faces (boundary components of the thickening) are the orbits of φ = ρσ: run along an arc, then turn to the next arc at its end vertex.
  - (σ, ρ) ↦ (σ_C, ρ_C) = (σ, ρσ) has the inverse (σ_C, ρ_C) ↦ (σ_C, ρ_Cσ_C). Bijections of Ā commuting with the structure correspond, so the categories are equivalent.
  - This is his "En résumé" with rows 2 and 3 of the dictionary (ρ_Γσ_Γ ↤ ρ_C, ρ_Γ ↤ ρ_Cσ_C). Re-derived; it agrees with the first pass's P11.
- **Row 1 (σ_Γ ⟷ \unsure{ρ_C|σ_C}).** Rows 2-3 force σ_Γ = σ_C: from ρ_C = ρ_Γσ_Γ and ρ_Γ = ρ_Cσ_C one gets σ_C = σ_Γ. The ink is unresolved (a ρ-like letter over another; Michel Hua's project reads ρ_C). The mathematics decides σ_C. This is a reading to settle on the image, with the mathematical answer recorded.
- **The head of 010 ("σ_Γ = σ_C, ρ_Γ = ρ_C").** It contradicts row 3, ρ_Γ = ρ_Cσ_C, unless σ_C = id, which is excluded (fixed-point free; first pass P10). The head belongs to an earlier identification (through rd/rg on 008, where ρ_C was defined on Ā itself) or is a slip. The dictionary is the coherent version. Michel Hua's project notes the same disagreement ("ce dictionnaire ne s'accorde pas avec les deux égalités données en tête du même feuillet").

**(c) The tree criterion (010, boxed).**
- For a connected map with one face (C a single polygon) and E edges, V − E + 1 = 2 − 2g. So Γ is a tree ⟺ g = 0.
- A one-face map is the gluing of the 2E-gon by the pairing σ_C. Its genus is 0 exactly when the pairing is non-crossing: a crossing pair of chords forces a handle, and a non-crossing pairing glues to a sphere whose dual graph is a tree.
- Re-derived (the gluing statement is standard: Walsh and Lehman 1972, from memory). His criterion is correct. "(non ponctuel)" excludes the one-vertex tree without contour; "sans sommet isolé" excludes invisible vertices.
- His wording "totalement non pénétrante, i.e. pour deux orbites disjointes … est n.p." is the pairwise non-crossing condition.

**(d) The quotient and the disc, 011 and 013.**
- **The quotient:** |Γ| = |C|/(a ∼ σ_C a, orientation reversed). The boundary of the thickened graph maps onto the graph two-to-one on open edges, the corners onto the vertices. Re-derived.
- **The disc picture:** n disjoint chords in a disc cut it into n+1 regions. The adjacency graph is connected, with n edges and n+1 vertices, so it is a tree (first pass's P12; re-derived).
- **The count:** discs with n non-crossing chords and an origin, up to isotopy, ↔ rooted plane trees with n edges. Both counts are the Catalan number C_n (from memory: Stanley, EC2, Ex. 6.19). This checks the equivalence of 013 on objects.
- **His duality sentence (013):** each region borders a packet of consecutive edges of the boundary polygon, which is the vertex of Γ. It agrees.

**(e) The recursion of 152/004 does not characterise non-penetrating involutions (correction 152_120-K3).** I worked out what the rule admits on a segment graph (a path).
- **Literal reading** (one edge of ε contracted; the partner then has no partner and becomes a fixed edge): the rule admits exactly the involutions whose two-element orbits are pairs of consecutive edges.
  - Proof: let {a, b} be an orbit with k ≥ 1 edges between them. A fixed edge is never contracted. Contracting one edge of a consecutive pair lying between a and b removes one edge and leaves its partner fixed between them. So at least one edge always remains between a and b, and {a, b} never becomes consecutive.
  - Example: σ = (1 4)(2 3) on four edges is rejected, although its chords are nested and do not cross. So it is non-penetrating in the sense of 152/010.
- **Contracting both edges of ε** ("en 1 point", the reading the struck "\deleted{deux}" suggests he was weighing): σ is admissible iff it is non-crossing and no fixed edge lies between the two edges of a two-element orbit. For fixed-point-free σ this is the usual recursive test for non-crossing pairings (every non-crossing pairing of points on a line has an adjacent pair, and removing it keeps the rest non-crossing).
- **The fixed edges (the first pass's P05 and @902, kept):** σ = (1 3) on three edges, with edge 2 fixed, is rejected under both readings although its one chord crosses nothing. The scan-10 criterion is stated for fixed-point-free involutions only (010 l.11; 011 l.5), so a fixed edge has no meaning there yet.
- **Status:** the scan-4 rule is a first attempt. In its literal form it is too strong. With both edges contracted and no fixed edges it agrees with the criterion of 010. Its purpose (word types?) is not stated on the page.

## Part B. Cote 120: "Topologie modérée" (SA20-12)

**Sources.** Title sheet 120/002: "Topologie [over struck "Structures"] modérée", "(Autour de l'Eloge)". The pencil above it reads \unsure{1978|1778}; Michel Hua's project reads 1978, a label, not the date of writing. There are two page sequences: 1-24, and 1-11 on alternate leaves.

### B1. What he does, in order, with the checks

**(I) Models (003-006).**
- **The axioms.** For n ∈ ℕ*, M_n ⊂ 𝔓(Rⁿ):
  - M1: images under u^*: R^m → Rⁿ for every u: [1,n] → [1,m];
  - M2: inverse images under u^* for u surjective, i.e. u^* injective;
  - M3: u^{*-1}(B) ∩ A;
  - M4: products.
- **Comment ①.** M_I is defined for finite non-empty I.
- **Reductions of M1-M3 (comment ② and the summary a)-c₂) on 004).** They generate M1-M3: every map of finite sets is a composite of permutations, degeneracies and faces. Re-derived.
- **Intersections (003 l.45-49).** "M1, M2, M4 impliquent que les [M_n] sont stables par intersection de deux". Re-derived: A ∩ B = δ^{-1}(A × B), with δ = u^* for the surjective fold u: [1,2n] → [1,n]; this uses M4, then M2, with M1 only for the reindexing. His inserted "(moyennant M1, M2, M4)" is exact. The page writes 𝔖_n for M_n three times, a slip kept in the text.
- **The M3 reduction (003 l.50).** "pour avoir M3, il suffit de le prouver pour u \unsure{surjectif|injectif}, et m̄ seulement pour u^* l'op. standard Rⁿ⁺¹ → Rⁿ".
  - Re-derived: write u = j∘s with s surjective and j injective; then u^{*-1}(B) = j^{*-1}(s^{*-1}(B)) and s^{*-1}(B) ∈ M by M2. So M3 is needed only for injective j, whose u^* is a projection. Projections compose from standard ones, and the induction uses M1 on the images of A.
  - So the word must be "injectif". If the ink is "surjectif" (Michel Hua's project; the first letter looks like his s), it is a slip.
- **The trivial cases (③, NB, (***), M_∅, 004-005).**
  - M1 makes "M_n = ∅", "∅ ∈ M_n" and "M_n = {∅}" each hold for all n as soon as they hold for one n.
  - If ∅ ∉ M_1, the M_n are filter bases.
  - M′_n = M_n ∪ {∅} again satisfies M1-M4 (u^*(∅) = u^{*-1}(∅) = ∅, ∅ × B = ∅).
  - Setting M_∅ = 𝔓(R^∅) = {∅, {e}} keeps M′1-M′4 for empty I and J; M′3 for u: ∅ → J needs ∅ ∈ M_J, i.e. (**).
  - All re-derived.
- **Points (④, 005).**
  - {x} ∈ M_n ⟺ every {x_i} ∈ M_1: M4 one way, the coordinate maps of M1 the other.
  - R_0 = {x : {x} ∈ M_1}, and R_0^I is the set of moderate points. The vertical sign under R_0^I reads R_0^I ⊂ R^I.
  - "si Card R_0 ≥ 2 … ∅ ∈ M_1": two distinct moderate points have empty intersection. Re-derived (@922).
  - His "{x} ∈ Rⁿ" (l.26) is a slip for M_n.
- **Examples (⑤-006).** Parts of cardinal ≤ 1, finite parts and all parts satisfy M1-M4 (re-derived). "Aucun de ces \unsure{exemples} … pour nous!"
  - The margin's "à l'exclusion de c₂)" matches no failure: c₂) holds in all three examples. Reading question.
- **(II) Espaces modérés (006-007).**
  - **Definitions.** f: A → B (A ∈ M_I, B ∈ M_J) is moderate if Γ_f ∈ M_{I∪J} (disjoint union meant).
  - **The Proposition, "via M2".**
    - a) The identity: Γ_id = δ(A), M1.
    - b) gf: Γ_{gf} is the projection of (Γ_f × C) ∩ (A × Γ_g), by M4, the intersection lemma (M2) and M1; so "via M2" fits b).
    - c) f bijective ⟹ f^{-1} moderate: a coordinate swap, which is conservativity.
    - Re-derived.
  - **The categories.** Mod_{M_*} ≃ Esp_{M_*}, with |·| faithful, conservative and "transportable" (transport of structure). This is standard.
  - **The Proposition of 007, as corrected.**
    - Finite products exist and |·| commutes with them. The pairing graph is an intersection of inverse images (M3).
    - So do fibre products and equalizers: the equalizer is the projection of Γ_f ∩ Γ_g, by M1.
    - So finite limits exist without any hypothesis on R_0.
    - The final object is the model {e} = R^∅ of 005.
    - This is why "(M5) R_0 ≠ ∅ … objet final" was struck (as the parallel block's O6 notes). Re-derived.
  - **The rest of 007.** "Rⁿ ∈ M_n for one n ⟹ for all I" (M1, M4). M0: R ∈ M_1 is "facultatif".

**The reconstruction (008; again 018). Open, and incomplete as stated (correction 152_120-K11).**
- "la donnée d'un M_* est équivalente à la donnée d'un système (à isom. près) (E, R, X ↦ |X|) de a) la catégorie E avec lim← finies, b) le foncteur … exact à gauche, c) l'objet R … tel que tout objet de [E] se plonge dans un Rⁿ".
- **The direction M_* ↦ system.** Re-derived:
  - the inclusion A ↪ R^I is moderate and monic;
  - a mono X → Rⁿ is injective, since |·| is faithful and left exact;
  - its image p(Γ) is in M_n (M1), and X ≅ image by conservativity.
  - So M_n is recovered as the subobjects of Rⁿ.
- **The converse needs more than a)-c):**
  - |·| faithful, conservative and transportable, so that subobjects are subsets. He states these on 006 and 018, not in 008.
  - An image axiom: the subobjects of the R^m must be stable under the coordinate morphisms R^m → Rⁿ (the categorical M1, existential quantification).
- **What finite limits give.** Pullbacks, intersections and products give M2-M4. Composites of monos give M1 for maps injective on the set. Nothing on these pages gives images of non-injective projections.
- **Open:** whether M1 follows. A counterexample needs a class closed under preimages, products and injective images, but not under all images. The natural candidates (Zariski-closed sets; closed countable sets; decidable sets; QF-definable sets over ℚ) all fail the injective images too (hunch H20-152_120-4).
- **The 018 converse** ("On a une réciproque quand R ∈ M_1 (donc R cpt) en termes d'un tel foncteur [fidèle conservatif transportable exact à gauche]…") has the same gap.
- His slip "tout objet de R" for E (008 c) is noted by the parallel block.

**Colimits (008-014).**
- **Finite sums (009).** "Si Card R_0 ≥ 2 [over struck "R_0 ≠ ∅"] … dans E il y a des sommes finies (moyennant M5)". Re-derived: X × {c_J} × {a} ∪ Y × {c_I} × {b}, with a ≠ b ∈ R_0, constants in R_0 and M5.
- **Why ≥ 2 is needed.** With Card R_0 = 1 the sum 1 ⊔ 1 cannot exist with |·| commuting: the images of the two points would be moderate singletons, hence both equal to the unique point (e, …, e) of R_0ⁿ.
- **So 015 is out of date.** 015 l.20 "Si R_0 ≠ ∅ (idem dans Esp_{M_*} il y a sommes disjointes finies)" keeps the old hypothesis (correction 152_120-K12; this confirms the parallel block's O2).

  - Corollary: the discrete structure on a finite set (a finite sum of points). Re-derived.
  - The Proposition on set-theoretic colimits that carry a structure making the g_i moderate; proof on 012, Γ_f = ∪ (g_i × 1)(Γ_{fg_i}), M1, M5. Re-derived.
  - Cor: X/ℛ_f ≅ f(X). Re-derived.

- **The question (010).** For X_1 ↩ Z ↪ X_2 (two hooks, settled in the parallel block), is there an amalgamated sum?

**À prouver (025).**
- **1).** For a morphism X → Y there are a rare moderate Y′, the components U_i of Y∖Y′ and moderate fibres Z_i with X|U_i ≅ U_i × Z_i, through moderate correspondences R_i ⊂ (Ū_i × Z_i) × X.
  - This is Hardt's generic triviality in the o-minimal setting (Hardt 1980; van den Dries, Tame topology and o-minimal structures, 1998, ch. 9; from memory). It holds also for finitely many definable subsets at once.
- **"Énoncé analogue pour un diagramme fini … ?!/??".** Re-derived:
  - It holds for diagrams of inclusions, his case Z ⊂ X ⊂ Y × [0,1], by Hardt for finitely many subsets.
  - It fails for parallel arrows. Take X = Y × S¹ over Y = (0,1), with f = id and g(y, z) = (y, R(y)z), where R(y) is the rotation with cos = (1−y²)/(1+y²) and sin = 2y/(1+y²) (semialgebraic). The rotation number of g on the fibre is a topological conjugacy invariant and varies with y, so no piece is trivial.
  - This agrees with the first pass's Q4-06 and Q4-07.
