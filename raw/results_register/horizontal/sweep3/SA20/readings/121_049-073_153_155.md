### 1.1 Admissible partitions (049-052)

**049, "Contextes" (L.P.M., S.A.P.M., Alg_C, An_C).** Claim: for X with a locally finite family of closed T_α there is a
strictly admissible partition with every T_α a union of leaves; among these, one coarsest; its formation commutes with
restriction to opens. Proof plan: by canonicity, local existence suffices; reduce to finite dimension and finitely many
T_α; induct on dim X. The first open stage is proposed as X^[1] = ∪ T̊_α, and the page stops at "Soit".
- **Wrong as written** (the Pro notes' test, R121-02: for X = R, T = {0}, the union of interiors is empty).
  **Correction (mine):** the union of the open leaves of the coarsest partition is W = X − ∪_α Fr(T_α) (minus Sing X if
  "strict" asks for non-singular leaves). Re-derived: an open leaf lies in T̊_α or in X − T_α, so it misses every
  frontier; conversely no leaf F meets both W and some Fr(T_α): F ⊂ T_α, some leaf G outside T_α has x ∈ Ḡ, so F ⊂ Ḡ,
  while at a point of F ∩ W the set T_α is a neighbourhood that G cannot approach. So the leaves split between W and
  X − W, and on W the coarsest choice is the pieces of constant type (or their components).
- **Open:** the induction itself (existence of a coarsest partition and its gluing) is not carried out (R121-01).

**050.** For U open meeting F_i: F̄_i ⊂ F̄_j iff F̄_i ∩ U ⊂ F̄_j ∩ U; so the incidence order of I_U is induced, and F_i
is open iff F_i ∩ U is (i maximal in I iff in I_U). **Checked:** his proof by the rareness of proper closed leaves is
right; a shorter one: a point x ∈ F_i ∩ U lies in F̄_j, and F̄_j is a union of leaves, so F_i ⊂ F̄_j. Then the filtration
III: X = X^0 ⊃ X^1 ⊃ …, locally finite by closed sets, with V^i the differences, and a local-finiteness and frontier
condition on the components of U ∩ V^i. **Read on the image:** the ink has X^{i−1} in "X^{i−1} rare dans X^i", in
V^i = X^i − X^{i−1} and in the bracket. For a descending filtration this is impossible (X^{i−1} ⊃ X^i). His slip: read
X^{i+1} throughout. The Pro notes (R121-04) left this for the facsimile; it is decided now.

**051.** Conditions (a) connected leaves, (b) non-singular, (c) equisingular; (a) is not stable under restriction to
opens, and he replaces it by (C): for every open U the components of the U ∩ F_i form an admissible partition (local
finiteness and the frontier condition component by component). **Checked:** F_i open in X iff F̄_i maximal (both
directions; the key step is F̄_j ∩ F_i = ∅ for j ≠ i when F̄_i is maximal); and the two reformulations of (C) b) agree
when the F_i are locally connected (an open-and-closed part of F_j ∩ U is a union of components).

**052, "Cellules".** Admissible partition I: (0) partition, (i) locally finite, (ii) F̄_i a union of leaves, (iii) F_i
locally closed. Then F̄_i = F̄_j implies F_i = F_j, and the data are equivalent to the closed leaves Φ_i with II: cover,
locally finite, closed, Φ_i ∩ Φ_j a union of Φ_k, proper inclusions rare; conversely F_i = Φ_i − ∪_{Φ_j⊊Φ_i} Φ_j.
**Re-derived in full:** the unique smallest Φ through a point exists by (i) and (iii); F_i is locally closed (a locally
finite union of closed sets is removed); F̄_i = Φ_i because Φ_i − F_i is a locally finite union of closed rare subsets,
hence rare; Φ_i is a union of leaves by minimality. Two provisos: the Φ_i must be non-empty (he deleted "non vides" in
II(0); an empty Φ_i gives an empty leaf), and the family must be indexed without repetition. **Read on the image:** "la
famille des Φ_i = F_i" has no bar in the ink (the F̄ of the line above has one). His slip: Φ_i = F̄_i.

## 3. Cote 155, scans 1-11: a tensor category with a fibre functor over k′/k (SA20-17)

**Read in full:** the transcription (his manuscript pages 1-5 on 002, 004, 006, 008, 010), the Pro notes T01-T25 and the
155 rows of the standing questions. No scan image was needed. **The sheets:** the versos 003, 005, 007, 009, 011 are
typed or printed university and publishing documents of June-October 1984 (see DELIVERABLES), so the notes are from
mid-1984 or later.

**002 (ms p. 1).** k a field, k′/k finite. The data of an abelian ⊗-category C over k and a k-linear, faithful ⊗-functor
F: C → Modf(k′) (he struck out "exact") are equivalent to 1°) a commutative k′-algebra U with an augmentation
ε: U → k′, and 2°) on the pro-object A = U^∨ = Hom_{k′}(U, k′) an associative ring structure with unit ε such that
(i) λ ↦ λε, k′ → A, is multiplicative, and (ii) the map A → A ⊗̂_{k′} A dual to the multiplication of U lands in the ring
J̃/J, J = 𝔧·(A ⊗̂_k A) the right ideal generated by 𝔧 = ker(k′ ⊗_k k′ → k′), J̃ its idealizer, and is a ring homomorphism.
- **Re-derived, and named (mine):** J̃ = {x : x(λ⊗1 − 1⊗λ) ∈ J for all λ ∈ k′}, so for x = Σ a_i ⊗ b_i the condition is
  Σ a_iλ ⊗ b_i = Σ a_i ⊗ b_iλ in A ⊗_{k′} A. That is Takeuchi's product A ×_{k′} A (Takeuchi 1977, from memory), and (ii)
  says that A is a ×_{k′}-bialgebra, a (pro-) bialgebroid over k′ with source equal to target. The Pro notes reach the
  idealizer (T04) and the bialgebroid literature (T23) but not this identification.
- **Open (his):** the equivalence is stated, not proved (T01); the pro-object setting must be kept (T02).

**004 (ms p. 2).** Example C = Modf(k), F = ⊗_k k′: U = k′ ⊗_k k′, ε the multiplication, A ≅ k′ ⊗ k′^{∨(k)} ≅ End_k(k′)
with its usual ring structure. **Re-derived:** Hom_{k′}(k′ ⊗_k k′, k′) ≅ Hom_k(k′, k′); End_k(k′) ≅ M_d(k) is Morita
equivalent to k, as the reconstruction requires (T06 agrees). Margin: if k′/k is étale, U ≅ A^{∨(k)}, and Δ_U, transpose
of the multiplication of A, is coassociative with Δ_U(1) ≠ 1 and not multiplicative (consistent: the comatrix coalgebra
of M_d(k) against the algebra ∏ k′; the identification needs the trace form, T16).
- **His question:** intrinsic conditions on C so that, for k′/k and F as above, M′ ↦ M′ ⊗_{k′⊗_k k′} k′ on finite
  A ⊗_k k′-modules is exact ("OK si k′/k est étale") and faithful. "Suffit-il que C soit rigide, et que
  k ≅ End_C(1_C)??? Exprimer la rigidité de C!"
- **Checked:** étale gives exactness (the diagonal is a direct factor of k′ ⊗ k′) but not faithfulness on all modules
  (T11); for k = F_p(t), k′ = k(t^{1/p}), k′ ⊗ k′ = k′[ε]/(ε^p) and the functor is not exact (T12; re-derived).
- **Answered later:** for a rigid abelian k-linear ⊗-category with End(1) = k and a fibre functor over k′, Deligne's
  recognition theorem (Catégories tannakiennes, 1990, §1.12) gives the reconstruction, and Deligne-Milne (1982, 3.10-3.11)
  the diagonal construction (T09-T10, from the notes; Deligne 1990 also names the gap in Saavedra's proof).

**006 (ms p. 3), rigidity.** M ↦ D(M) = M^∨, compatible with F′, is an anti-equivalence, hence exact, hence of the form
Hom_A(M, I) with I = D(A) = A^{∨(k′)} = U; to make D land in A-modules, U needs a second, commuting A-action, i.e. a
module structure over A ⊗ A; since End_A(U) ≅ End_A(A_d)^o ≅ A^o (the endomorphisms need not be k′-linear), this is a
map a: A → A^o, the antipode. **Re-derived:** End of A as a right module is A (left multiplications), so its opposite is
A^o; duality reverses composition. Margin: valid only if k′/k is finite, to identify U with D_k(A) ≅ D_{k′}(A).

**008 (ms p. 4).** D(M) = Hom_A(M, U) ≅ k-bilinear φ: A × M → k′ with φ(λu, x) = λφ(u, x), φ(uv, x) = φ(u, vx), i.e.
k′-linear maps A_d ⊗_A M → k′, and A_d ⊗_A M ≅ M: so F′(D(M)) = M^{∨(k′)}. **Re-derived** (tensor-hom adjunction; T18
agrees). Still needed: the pairing F′(M) ⊗ F′(D(M)) → k′ = F′(1_C) comes from M ⊗ D(M) → 1_C.

**010 (ms p. 5).** That is, M^{k′} ⊗_{k′} (M^{k′})^∨ → k′ is A-linear for the diagonal structure; it suffices to take
M = A. The diagram stops. **Checked:** the reduction to M = A is right if one passes through the finite quotients of A
