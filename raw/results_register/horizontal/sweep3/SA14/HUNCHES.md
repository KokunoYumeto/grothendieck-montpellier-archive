## From readings/c57.md (7. Hunches)

- **H1 (57:99-100).** The kernel of Pic^tau_X (x) Kbar -> Pic^tau_{Y_Kbar} (Y the generic hyperplane section of a normal X of
  dimension >= 2) has Lie algebra of dimension h^1(X, O_X(-1)) (computed above). So Grothendieck's "p-groupe fini
  unipotent purement infinitésimal" is nonzero exactly when degree-1 Kodaira vanishing fails for O_X(1). Whether this
  happens for a very ample O_X(1) on a smooth (or normal) X in characteristic p is the open point. Links: Raynaud's
  counterexamples to Kodaira vanishing (with ample, not very ample, sheaves); the Lefschetz table of 57:97.
- **H2 (57:110).** Seshadri's theorem without Pic: for f proper flat with geometrically integral S_2 fibres over a
  normal base, is a Weil divisor with no vertical part, Cartier on the generic fibre, Cartier? This is true for f smooth
  (Ramanujam-Samuel). It fails for non-proper relative complete intersections (my counterexample on 111). The proper
  case with singular fibres is open on the page; counterexamples of the 111 type need a non-Cartier divisor along a
  codimension-3 singular locus of the total space, which properness does not exclude.
- **H3 (57:97, Question).** Surjectivity of Pic(X) -> Pic(Y) for n >= 4 in characteristic p without the vanishing of
  H^2(Y, O_Y(-m)). The page reduces it to the p-part. The local analogue is Bhatt-de Jong, "Lefschetz for local Picard
  groups" (Ann. Sci. ENS 2014; locator unverified), which I recall as an isomorphism up to p-power torsion. Neither the
  exact state of the global question nor a counterexample is known to me.
- **H4 (57:97, boxed).** "Noyau nul si n >= 4" (injectivity of Pic(X) -> Pic(Y) for every smooth hyperplane section).
  The kernel is a finite p-group generated by layers H^1(Y, O_Y(-m)). Its vanishing reduces to H^0(X, Omega^1_X(-1)) = 0
  for the closed log-forms of p-torsion classes (Akizuki-Nakano type), which can fail in characteristic p.
- **H5 (57:227-231, 272).** The henselian questions of the 1962 letter: (i) universal images come from Pic(X); (ii)
  Pic(X) = Pic(X^) when R^1 f_* O has finite length; (iii) Pic(Y - y) = Pic(Y^ - y^) at isolated singularities. For
  henselizations of local rings of finite type these are approximation statements (Artin 1969). My cone computation
  (57:193-194) supports (iii) for henselizations, and also shows that it fails for the non-henselian local ring (plane
  curves of degree >= 4).
- **H6 (57:277, "Au concours").** In the category of algebraic spaces Nér = Pic/Pic^tau exists as soon as Pic^tau is flat,
  closed and of finite presentation. Being separated and unramified, it is then a scheme (Knutson). So the answer is
  probably yes, through algebraic spaces.
- **H7 (57:314-315).** Is Pic^0_{X/S} proper for X normal and proper over a trait (without projectivity)? Is Pic_{X/S}
  representable for X proper flat with geometrically normal or integral fibres? The second is no in general for schemes
  (Raynaud 1970) and yes for algebraic spaces (Artin). The first can fail if line bundles of the generic fibre do not
  extend as invertible sheaves on a non-factorial normal total space.
- **H8 (57:8).** His expectation (12), D -> NS(X_o) injective, holds for normal A by the negativity lemma. For
  non-normal A with regular U it reduces to the normalization (semi-local), componentwise.
- **H9 (57:22, 32).** "Corr_S(X, Y) is always separated" (proper, flat, f_* O = O universally for both factors). The
  page proves it when Pic of one factor is representable or pre-ét-representable. In general it is open on the page;
  Raynaud's existence of Corr (313) is the related known result.
- **H10 (57:64).** For X/S smooth projective in characteristic 0 over an arbitrary base, Pic^0 is an abelian scheme
  (Deligne 1968, degeneration and base change of R^q f_* O). His "au secours !" question is answered. His other
  question (64), whether omega_G locally free in residue characteristic 0 forces smoothness along the unit section
  without flatness, is open here.
- **H11 (57:73-79).** "Problem A" (the flattening functor for proper X/S) is representable by a monomorphism of finite
  presentation (Raynaud-Gruson 1971), as he expected. His "quasi-affine versus affine" question for f^*: Pic_X ->
  Pic_{X'} over a dense open of a one-dimensional base remains; he notes in the margin "impossible if X smooth!".
- **H12 (57:193).** In characteristic p, the local Néron-Severi groups of S and S^ (and S~) agree modulo p-groups in
  every dimension; the same p-killed obstruction argument works as on 57:97.
- **H13 (57:111, refined).** The refined form of Question 2 holds when S is regular and the fibres are R_2. Is R_2 also
  necessary in a sense? My examples have fibres singular in codimension 1 (non-normal) and 2 (normal), so the cut is
  between R_1 and R_2.

## From readings/c58_c60.md (5. Hunches)

1. **E^♮ is the inertia part of a Weil-Deligne pair with N forgotten** (60:39-57, SA14-10).
   - Grounds: by §2.5(d), E^{♮(x)} = [E^{ρ_0(I_x)}] for the pair (ρ_0, N), and E ↦ E^♮ commutes with duality.
   - His averaging over an open U acting unipotently is a precursor of the Weil-Deligne formalism and of the
     semisimplified local factors (Deligne, "Les constantes des équations fonctionnelles des fonctions L", 1973, §8;
     locator unverified).
   - The "constantes locales" of 60:59 are Deligne's ε-factors.
2. **The finite generation of local π_1** (58:19, SA14-3, SA14-6). Research package RP-S2.
3. **58:26: the preamble of Bourbaki draft n° 355** (typed) records that Grothendieck told the rédacteur that the main
   result generalizes "en remplaçant l'un des anneaux par un module". It dates his contact with that draft (SA14-13).
4. **58:57 and 58:61: two states of one statement.** They carry the same algebraization statement ("3.1", "Corollaire
   3.2"), one probably a fair copy of the other; the order is not settled.
5. **The margin word on 58:9 and 44:2.** The word read "Canulé" on both pages is one word of his. Both readings are open;
   one look at both scans side by side should settle it (SA19 `c43_c44` §2).
6. **"niveau" on 60:63-69.** His level, with the decomposition of L into factors whose inverse eigenvalues have absolute
   value p^{i/2}, anticipates the weight filtration of L-functions (Deligne, Weil II; locator unverified).
7. **Cote 59 as a construction of the theta divisor and of K from the determinant of cohomology** (SA14-14).
   - Grounds: §4.1-4.2 and §4.8.
   - This precedes Knudsen-Mumford and Moret-Bailly ("Pinceaux de variétés abéliennes", Astérisque 129, 1985) and
     Faltings-Chai ch. I (locators unverified). The two routes to K (determinant, 59:9; theta symmetry, 59:28) meet for
     g >= 3 through the strong Franchetta theorem (§4.2(e)).
8. **Canonical points of Pic^δ by the type of δ** (59:10-14, 59:26-28). Research package RP-S3.
9. **"mon petit topo de Albanese"** (59:21, 59:23-25). A planned text on Albanese torsors, to start from the sorite
   i ↔ ψ. It links to SA14-4 and SA14-7 and to his Bourbaki seminar on Picard schemes (FGA; locator unverified).
