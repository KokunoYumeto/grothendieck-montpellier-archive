## 2. What is new

Against the literature I know (I ran no corpus search for these rows; the run's own source census is its section F.2):
- **P^n, p odd: the full group.** For every n >= 2 and d >= n+2 the geometric and arithmetic monodromy of H^(n-1)_et(Y, F_p) over the ordinary smooth degree-d hypersurfaces is GL_C(d-1,n)(F_p), irreducible with full determinant (thm:plane-odd-all-groups, thm:pn-odd-full-groups; checked). Known inputs: the quartic case (Achter-Pries 2008, Prop. 4.8, through M_3) and McLaughlin's classification (Kantor-Seress 2001, Thm 3.6). The degeneration to hyperplane arrangements, the quadric reflections and the degree induction are, as far as I know, new. Ekedahl (1991) and Achter-Pries (2008) treat moduli of curves; plane curves and hypersurfaces are thin families to which their theorems do not apply.
- **P^n, p = 2: the exact group by parity.** GL_h(F_2) when d = n mod 2; the stabilizer of one canonical covector, F_2^(h-1) x| GL_(h-1)(F_2), when d = n+1 mod 2 and d >= n+3 (thm:pn-char2-all-groups; checked). The plane-curve covector is known (theta characteristics: Cais-Ellenberg-Zureick-Brown 2013, Thm 4.2; Castryck-Streng-Testa 2014, as the run cites); the exact group and the higher-dimensional case are new as far as I know.
- **Point counts.** Every residue mod p of #Y(k') occurs in every nonempty open: for P^n exactly from d = n+2 for every p (sharp: residue 1 only for d <= n, residue 1 missed by the ordinary open for d = n+1), and for every smooth projective X from an explicit, very large d_res (thm:all-X-residue-density; checked). This is the point-count consequence of 8.6, which Grothendieck (4:12) says was not proved "même si X = P^2".
- **Obstructions to full monodromy.** In characteristic 2 a canonical invariant covector whenever omega_X (x) L^(d*) has a square root (thm:general-adjunction-covector); a fixed quotient of dimension 2^n - 1 for ordinary abelian X of dimension n and even d; on products of ordinary elliptic curves the exact invariant covector space, of dimension 2^e - 1 for p = 2 (e = number of factors with a_i d even) and n for p odd when p \| a_i d for all i (thm:product-exact-fixed-covectors, thm:product-char2-all-parities-fixed-quotient); and an explicit surface over F_3 (E: y^2 = x^3 + x^2 + 1, X = E x E, L = O(3O) boxtimes O(3O)) whose monodromy fixes a 2-dimensional quotient in every degree. All checked.
- **Exact groups on one ordinary surface E x E over F_2** (E: Y^2Z + XYZ + X^3 + Z^3 = 0): GL_8(F_2) in degree 1 (my independent recomputation of the certificates agrees), the full pointwise stabilizer of three covectors (order 2^96 |GL_32(F_2)|) in degree 2, GL_80(F_2) in degree 3; transvection generation, reducedness of the Hasse divisor and the exact invariant quotient in every degree; the full image in every degree conditional on irreducible Hasse divisors (proved in rows I did not read).
- **Effective statements:** a terminating algorithm for the entire monodromy image of any specified X over F_q, and finite Galois data over any perfect field (checked; impractical, as the run says).

### 4.1 The point-count residue rows: zeta functions over finite fields (a real bridge, in both directions)

Rows: cor:pn-odd-all-residues-sharp (2923), cor:pn-all-p-sharp-residue-threshold (3224), thm:all-X-residue-density
(3706); and, through them, every monodromy theorem of my packets, since each is used only through the trace of Frobenius.

- **The objects.** For smooth projective Y of dimension n-1 over F_q, Z(Y,T) = ∏_i P_i(T)^((-1)^(i+1)), and on the vanishing
  part P_(n-1)(T) = ∏_j (1 - α_j T). Deligne's purity (Weil I, Thm 1.6): |ι(α_j)| = q^((n-1)/2) for every complex embedding ι.
  The unit-root factor U(T) = ∏_{α_j a p-adic unit}(1 - α_j T) has degree the stable rank h of the Hasse-Witt map, and
  modulo the prime above p it is det(1 - Frob·T \| V), V = H^(n-1)_ét(Y, F_p) (vanishing part). The coherent trace
  congruence (Katz 1973, SGA 7 II, XXII, Cor. 3.2, as the run cites) is the T-coefficient of this: #Y(F_q) ≡ 1 +
  (-1)^(n-1) Tr(Frob \| V) mod p for hypersurfaces in P^n. This is exactly Grothendieck's "Woods Hole formula" of 4:12.
- **What RH over finite fields gives there.** The rows need points of the parameter space, over F_(q^m), with a prescribed
  Frobenius conjugacy class in the finite monodromy group, inside every nonempty open. That is Chebotarev for the finite
  étale frame cover, which rests on the Lang-Weil estimate #U(F_(q^m)) = q^(m dim U) + O(q^(m(dim U - 1/2))), i.e. on the
  Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields (Weil 1948), extended by Lang-Weil (1954); the effective form the run
  cites (Katz-Sarnak 1999, Thm 9.7.13) uses Deligne's Weil II for the error term. So these rows depend on RH over finite
  fields (Weil, Deligne), not on Alpöge's RH or GRH: over F_q the Riemann hypothesis is a theorem and is what makes the
  existence step work. With it, the density of the residue a among smooth ordinary Y over F_(q^m) is
  #{g ∈ G : 1 + (-1)^(n-1) Tr g ≡ a} / \|G\| + O(q^(-m/2)), with G = GL_h(F_p) (P^n, p odd, d ≥ n+2; P^n, p = 2, d ≡ n mod 2)
  or the covector stabilizer (p = 2, d ≡ n+1 mod 2, d ≥ n+3).
- **What the rows give back.** A mod-p equidistribution law for the unit-root factor of the zeta function: as Y runs over
  the ordinary smooth degree-d hypersurfaces of P^n (p odd, d ≥ n+2), U_Y(T) mod p is distributed like the characteristic
  polynomial det(1 - gT) of a uniformly random g ∈ GL_h(F_p). This is the p-adic companion of Katz-Sarnak's archimedean
  equidistribution of the normalized α_j/q^((n-1)/2) (which needs purity for the normalization); purity says where the α_j
  lie on the circle, the run's theorems say how their unit-root part is distributed mod p. For every smooth projective X
  (thm:all-X-residue-density) the run gives the weaker statement that every value of the trace occurs, for d ≥ d_res.
- **What GRH (Alpöge) gives there.** Nothing these rows need: everything is over finite fields, where RH holds (Weil,
  Deligne). The number-field analogue would be a version of 8.6 for hypersurfaces over Q reduced modulo varying primes;
  effective Chebotarev under GRH (Lagarias-Odlyzko 1977) would then bound the least prime realizing a residue. No row
  states that.

- **lem:DE-A3-ppav-even-bound (10132), lem:DE-A3-mixed-polarization-blocks (11202).** The flag is "Tate module": the prime-to-2 l-adic Tate modules T_l(A) of abelian surfaces over an algebraically closed field, used with the polarization pairing T_lA x T_lA -> Z_l(1).
