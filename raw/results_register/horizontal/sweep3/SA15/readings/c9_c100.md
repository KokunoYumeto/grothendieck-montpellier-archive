## 1. The findings that matter

1. **100:3, part 2) of his Theorem is false for non-reduced S; true for S reduced.** The Theorem: S locally noetherian, U
   open, T = S - U of residue characteristic p; abelian schemes over S against triples (A_U, M over S, φ: M|U ≅ T_p(A_U));
   1) full faithfulness when U meets every connected component; 2) equivalence if S has a finite surjective geometrically
   unibranch S' and prof ét_T(S) ≥ 2. Counterexample (part 2, verified at the merge, check event M-1): S = Spec Z_p[ε]/(ε²),
   U = S[1/p]; A_U a non-trivial first-order deformation of an elliptic curve E_0/Q_p with good reduction, M the constant
   deformation of E[p^∞]; any A over S with these data is, by Serre-Tate over Z/p^n[ε] for all n, the constant
   deformation, whose generic fibre is not A_U. The gap is exactly his step a) on 100:7 ("réduction au cas S réduit"),
   which he marked "?" twice: it needs an isomorphism over U ×_S Spec Λ^ that nothing provides. With S reduced, cases b)-e)
   carry the proof, and his "Douteux, sans doute faux" lemma on 100:17 is not needed (noetherian induction, part 2 §3 e).
2. **9:14, "faux par Tate": his question answered both ways.** He asks whether constant _pA_K forces the trace on
   prime-to-p inertia to be 2 dim A (typed "n = dim A_K", a slip), i.e. whether Raynaud's semi-stable reduction criterion
   holds for ℓ = p, and names the case of Hasse invariant zero as the interesting one. Proved here: for potentially good
   **ordinary** reduction (p ≥ 3) the answer is yes (rigidity of the multiplicative part of the Néron model's p-torsion,
   then Serre's lemma). Constructed here: for **supersingular** reduction the answer is no, over K = Q_p(ζ_{p²}) (e =
   p(p-1)): a Kisin module gives an elliptic curve E_0/O_K with E_0[p] ≅ χη ⊕ χη (χ ramified quadratic), and its twist
   E = E_0 ⊗ χ has unramified p-torsion but additive reduction. So the pencil "faux par Tate" is right, in the case he
   singled out (part 1 §3; the construction uses Kisin's classification and Illusie's lifting theorem).

## 1. SA15-10: the reduced Dieudonné module and duality over the truncated rings A_n (9:2-8)

### 9:3: the two Hodge-type sequences and the corollary

**What he writes.** k a field of characteristic p, G p-divisible, G* its dual, g = dim G, g* = dim G*; G̃ the universal
extension ("grp enveloppant") 0 → V(t_{G*}) → G̃ → G → 0. Two pairs of rows: H(G) in 0 → ω_G = t_G^∨ → H(G) → t_{G*}
→ 0 (dimensions g, g + g*, g*) with F_0 = H(F): H(G)^(p) → H(G) and V_0 = H(V): H(G) → H(G)^(p); and the "dual" rows
0 → ω_{G*} → t_{G̃} → t_G → 0 with V_0, F_0. NB: H(G) = t_{G̃*} (the Lie algebra of the universal extension of G*).
1) the two sequences, for t_{G̃} and t_{G̃*}, are transposed by a canonical functorial pairing which exchanges F_0 and V_0.
2) Ker F_0 = Im V_0 = ω_G^(p); Ker V_0 = Im F_0 = "ce que ça peut (innovation intéressante)". 3) F_0, V_0 on H(G) are
the reduced Dieudonné module mod p. 4) the composite t_{G*}^(p) → H(G) → t_{G*} is "la puissance p-ième des
dérivations [et correspond: Frobenius dans H²(G, G_a)]". **Corollaire:** G purely infinitesimal ⟺ F_0 nilpotent
("i.e. G* sans tore"); G* purely infinitesimal ⟺ V_0 nilpotent ("i.e. G sans composante toroïdale"); both ⟺ both.

**Checked** (check events 9:3-a, 9:3-b). With the convention V(E) = Spec Sym E (EGA II 1.7.8), Lie V(t_{G*}) =
t_{G*}^∨ = ω_{G*}, which is the left term of his right-hand rows, so the two diagrams agree. Ker F_0 ⊇ ω_G^(p) because
Frobenius kills differentials (F*(df) = d(f^p) = 0), with equality by rank, and Im V_0 = Ker F_0 is the
standard F-V exactness of H¹_dR-type modules (Oda, Ann. Sci. ENS 2 (1969), locator unverified). Item 4 is right: F_0
factors through H(G)^(p)/ω^(p) = t_{G*}^(p), and the induced map to t_{G*} = Lie G* is the linearized p-power of the
p-Lie algebra; "H²(G, G_a)" (the ink is a 2) is the Hochschild group whose symmetric classes are Ext¹(G, G_a) = t_{G*}.
The corollary, tested on the two basic cases: for G = μ_{p^∞} (g = 1, g* = 0), H = ω_G and F_0 = 0; for G = Q_p/Z_p
(g = 0, g* = 1), H = t_{G*} and F_0 is bijective. In general F_0 is nilpotent iff G has no étale part, i.e. G connected,
i.e. G* has no multiplicative ("toric") part; V_0 nilpotent iff G* connected iff G has no multiplicative part. **The ink
reads "i.e. G* sans tore"** (500 dpi; K9-01 and Michel Hua's project read the star too); the Pro transcription drops the
star, which makes the line false (correction 1). The margin reads "des dérivations", not "dans l'algèbre" (correction 2).

**Status.** Known: the contravariant Dieudonné module mod p as Lie of the universal extension of the dual (Messing, LNM
264, 1972; Mazur-Messing, LNM 370, 1974; locators unverified). "Innovation intéressante": Im F_0 = Ker V_0 is the
conjugate filtration, a second flag next to the Hodge flag; the pair is what later became an F-zip (hunch H1-5).

### 9:5: the trace form on A_n

### 9:7-8: two dualizing functors, and the bimodule A_n ≅ its W-dual

left modules into right modules, repaired by the involution s of A exchanging F and V. "A_n est fini sur W_n." A second
dualizing functor for M killed by F^n, V^n: D'(M) = Hom_{W_n}(M, W_n) with the right A-structure "transposée", and
Hom_{W_n}(M, W_n) = Hom_{A_n}(M, Hom_{W_n}(A_n, W_n)). "Je dis qu'on a un iso I_n = A_n ≅ Hom_{W_n}(A_n, W_n)." On 9:8:
the right structure u.F = π^{-1}(u∘F), u.V = π(u∘F) [sic], u.λ = u∘λ, with u.F.V = u.V.F = pu; on D'(A_n) =
Hom_{W_n}((A_n)_g, W_n) the bimodule structure u.F = π^{-1}(u∘F_g), u.V = π(u∘V_g), u.λ = u∘λ_g, Fu = u∘F_d,
Vu = u∘F_g [sic], λu = u∘λ_d; "Attention les deux structures de W_n-module ... ne sont pas les mêmes"; A_n →? Hom
"iso. de bimodules" with a question mark; then the conditions on a bi-additive pairing A_n × A_n → W_n that make it
right A_n-linear (⟨x, yλ⟩ = ⟨λx, y⟩, ⟨x, yF⟩ = π^{-1}⟨Fx, y⟩, ⟨x, yV⟩ = π⟨Vx, y⟩) and left A_n-linear (⟨x, ay⟩ =
⟨xa, y⟩ for a = λ, F, V), with ⟨x, y⟩ = ⟨1, xy⟩ = ⟨xy, 1⟩.

  and so is "Vu = u∘F_g" (the left action is precomposition with right multiplication: Vu = u∘V_d). Corrections 3, 4.

- The pairing ⟨x, y⟩ = φ(xy) satisfies all six conditions: the right ones by b), c) and the right linearity of φ, the

  F^iW and V^iW is perfect. Bijective by length.

## 2. SA15-15: the struck lemma for the Gauss-Manin connection (9:9)

**What he writes** (the whole sheet struck; Michel Hua's project gives it too). "Question: Comment définir directement une

**Checked** (9:9, independent re-proof). The lemma is true. The data (P, Θ) are exactly a global section of the
two-term complex C = [𝒢 → Z⁰Hom(K, L)] (degrees -1, 0; differential δ∘i): a 𝒢-torsor with an equivariant map to Z⁰.

## 3. SA15-19: the character of a Barsotti-Tate group on inertia (9:10-14)

### 9:11-12: the construction

**What he writes.** V a complete DVR "à corps résiduel parfait" (inserted), residue characteristic p, K of characteristic
0 or p; M = (M(ν))_{ν≥0} a p-BT group over K (his M(ν) is the kernel of p^{ν+1}, so M(0) = _pM, as the text itself
writes and as Michel Hua's project notes). M satisfies the "condition de monodromie" if it has a composition series by
BT groups whose graded pieces N acquire good reduction over a finite separable K'; by Tate's theorem ("démontré pour
l'instant seulement dans le cas d'inégales caractéristiques") N_{K'} comes from a BT group N over S' unique up to unique
isomorphism, so G = Gal(K'/K) acts on N over its action on S'. Then (1) K_BT(K; K') → K_BT(S'; G), (2) → K_BT(s'; G),
(3) → K(W(k'), G) by the Dieudonné module (margin "et = vers Cris(k')"), (4) the composite; restricting to the inertia
I ⊂ G, K(W(k'), G) → K(W(k'), I)^G ≅ R_{W(k')}(I)^{G/I}, and (5) the trace to central functions on I: "le caractère
du groupe de B.T. M", independent of K' and viewed as a function on the inertia subgroup of Gal(K̄/K).

**Checked / remarks.** The construction is right granted two points he passes over (Pro notes #28, #30): (1) needs the
class of Σ[N_i] to be independent of the composition series, which follows from Tate's full faithfulness once one knows
that sub-BT groups of a BT group with good reduction over S' have good reduction (true in mixed characteristic, e.g. via
schematic closure and Raynaud for e < p-1 and Breuil-Kisin in general; not available in 1967); and "≅
R_{W(k')}(I)" should be a homomorphism, which is all the trace needs (Michel Hua's project weakens it the same way).
Why the character can be computed "via les T_ℓ" (9:13): on the special fibre, I acts by k'-automorphisms of N_{s'}, and
for an abelian variety the trace of an automorphism on the Dieudonné module equals its trace on T_ℓ (the characteristic
polynomial of an endomorphism is the same on both). Check event 9:11-12.

### 9:12-13: the comparison with the Brauer character of _pM, and its failure

**What he writes.** If M_{K'} is constant, T_p(M) is a locally constant p-adic sheaf and the character of M agrees, on the
elements of I of order prime to p, with the modular character of I on M(0). Is the character always determined by M(0)
= _pM? "Il n'en est rien": by Brauer's theory of virtual liftings it would then equal the modular character of M(0) for
every M; for M with good reduction the character is constant on I, so the modular character of M(0) would be trivial;
"or c'est déjà faux en prenant M = Z_p(1), donc M(0) = μ_p, en se plaçant dans le cas où K ne contient pas les racines
p-èmes de 1". If K contains them, no trouble for good reduction of constant or multiplicative type; the ind-unipotent
case remains. Margin NB: then the character would be Z_p-valued once μ_p ⊂ K (on a subgroup of I of index dividing p-1).

**Checked** (9:12-13 targeted checks).
- Constant case: the model N over S' is étale, D(N_{s'}) = T_p(M) ⊗ W(k') with I acting through T_p(M); the ordinary
  character of a lattice on p-regular elements is the Brauer character of its reduction. Correct.
- The Brauer argument: the decomposition map from Z_p-representations to F_p-representations of a finite group is
  surjective on Grothendieck groups (Serre, "Représentations linéaires des groupes finis", Part III, Thm. 33; locator
  unverified), every lattice representation of G occurs as T_p of an étale BT group over K constant over K', and both
  sides are additive (M ↦ M(0) is exact on BT groups). So "determined by M(0)" forces "equal to the Brauer character of
  M(0)", as he says.
- The counterexample needs more than "K ne contient pas les racines p-èmes de 1": the character of μ_{p^∞} on I is
  trivial, and the Brauer character of μ_p on I is that of ω|_I, so the contradiction needs **the inertia** to act
  non-trivially on μ_p, i.e. K(μ_p)/K ramified. If K = Q_p(π) with π^{p-1} = -pu, u a unit that is not a (p-1)-th power
  mod p, then μ_p ⊄ K but K(μ_p) = K(u^{1/(p-1)}) is unramified over K, and μ_p gives no contradiction. The hypothesis to
  write is "e(K/Q_p) not divisible by p-1" or simply "I acts non-trivially on μ_p" (e.g. K unramified over Q_p, p > 2).
  Michel Hua's project makes the same point. Correction 7.
- With μ_p ⊂ K: for multiplicative good reduction M(0) = X/p ⊗ μ_p = X/p as a Galois module and both characters are those
  of X; by additivity over the connected-étale sequence over S' (G-stable), the ordinary case follows too. The margin is
  right: the eigenvalue multiset of an F_p-representation is stable under x ↦ x^p, so its Brauer character is fixed by
  σ and lies in Z_p; without μ_p ⊂ K one restricts to the kernel of I → Aut(μ_p) = F_p^*, of index dividing p-1.

### 9:13-14: the "cas test", and the pencil "faux par Tate"

**What he writes.** Two elliptic curves over K with potentially good reduction, _pE_1 ≅ _pE_2, μ_p ⊂ K: are their trace
functions on inertia (computable "via les T_ℓ") equal, "sur les éléments premiers à p" (inserted)? "Le cas intéressant est
celui où l'une au moins des deux courbes, après extension K → K', se réduit en une courbe à invariant de Hasse nul." Or
again: if _pA_K is constant, is the trace "égale à n = dim A_K" (typed over a struck "nulle") on the elements of I prime
to p? "Seul le cas de réduction non semi-stable de A offre un problème; donc il faudrait ici trouver un exemple où le
critère de réduction semi-stable de Raynaud est en défaut pour ℓ = p !" For an elliptic curve with residue characteristic
≠ 2, 3, K' the smallest extension with semi-stable reduction, G ⊂ Aut(A_{s'}), "lequel est cyclique à 2, 4 ou 6
éléments donc est premier à p", and the question is whether φ_E is constant equal to 2 when _pA is constant;
"il suffit de regarder le cas où la courbe elliptique réduite ... est d'invariant de Hasse nul". In the left margin, in
pencil, beside this paragraph: **"faux par Tate"**.

**Slips.** The trace of the identity on T_p(A) is 2 dim A, so "n = dim A_K" should be 2n, as his own elliptic case
("égale à 2") shows (correction 8; Michel Hua's project reads 2g). "G est un sous-groupe de Aut(A_{s'})" holds for the
inertia subgroup (all of G if k is algebraically closed); its order divides 2, 4 or 6, and 3 occurs (correction 9).

**The answer, worked out (check events 9:14-a, 9:14-b).**
1. **Ordinary reduction gives no counterexample (proved).** Let A/K have potentially good reduction with ordinary
   reduction over K', residue characteristic p ≥ 3, and A[p] unramified (in particular if _pA_K is constant; then μ_p ⊂
   K by the Weil pairing). Work over the completion of K^ur; let L/K^ur be the minimal extension with good reduction,
   Φ = Gal(L/K^ur), which acts on the Néron model 𝒜 over O_L and faithfully on Ā by k̄-automorphisms (Serre-Tate,
   Ann. of Math. 88 (1968), §2; locator unverified). 𝒜[p]^0 has special fibre μ_p^g, so it is of multiplicative type
   (SGA 3 X, rigidity; locator unverified), ≅ μ_p^g over O_L. A semilinear automorphism of μ_p^g over φ ∈ Φ is the
   canonical one composed with some a ∈ GL_g(F_p); on generic points it acts by ζ ↦ ζ^a since μ_p ⊂ K, so the
   hypothesis forces a = 1, and the reduction of the canonical one is the identity because φ acts trivially on k̄. So Φ
   acts trivially on Ā[p]^0; on Ā[p]^ét it acts as on the generic points (étale specialization), trivially. Over k̄,
   Ā[p] = Ā[p]^0 × Ā[p]^ét, so each α ∈ Φ acts trivially on Ā[p], hence α = 1 + pβ in End(Ā), and an automorphism of
   finite order congruent to 1 mod n ≥ 3 is 1 (Serre's lemma; Mumford, Abelian Varieties, §21, Thm. 5; locator
   unverified). So Φ = 1: A has good reduction over K and the trace is 2g. For elliptic curves the last step can be
   replaced by the action on Lie(Ā) = Lie(Ā[p]^0): a non-trivial α ∈ Aut(Ē) acts on the invariant differential by a
   non-trivial root of unity (p ≥ 5). This is his own reduction ("il suffit de regarder ... invariant de Hasse nul"):
   the ordinary case is covered by additivity from the constant and multiplicative cases.
2. **Supersingular reduction does give counterexamples (construction).** Take p ≥ 3 and K = Q_p(ζ_{p²}), so e = e(K/Q_p)
   = p(p-1) and μ_p ⊂ K; χ the ramified quadratic character of K(√π)/K, whose restriction to I is θ^{(p-1)/2} (θ the
   tame character of level 1). Let m = (p-1)/2, c = pm (so e = 2c), and let 𝔐 be the free k[[u]]-module with basis
   f_1, f_2 and φ(f_1) = u^c f_1, φ(f_2) = (1 - u^{c-m}) f_1 + u^c f_2. Then:
   - the matrix of 1⊗φ has elementary divisors 1 and u^{2c} = u^e, so 𝔐 is a Kisin module of E-height ≤ 1, hence a
     finite flat group scheme 𝒢 over O_K killed by p (Kisin's classification, p > 2; locator unverified);
   - 𝔐[1/u] has basis e_1 = f_1, e_2 = f_2 - u^{-m}f_1 with φ(e_i) = u^c e_i, so the generic fibre restricted to
     G_{K_∞} is ψ ⊕ ψ with ψ given by u^c; solving x^{p-1} = u^{-c} gives x = u^{-p/2}, so ψ = χ on inertia; by full
     faithfulness of restriction to G_{K_∞} on flat representations (Breuil, Kisin; locator unverified) the generic
     fibre of 𝒢 is χη ⊕ χη with η unramified;
   - mod u, φ is f_1 ↦ 0, f_2 ↦ f_1 and V = E(u)(1⊗φ)^{-1} is f_1 ↦ 0, f_2 ↦ -f_1 (this uses e = 2c exactly), so Ker F
     = Im V and Ker V = Im F are lines: the special fibre is the BT_1 of a supersingular elliptic curve.
   Lift 𝒢 to a BT group over O_K (truncation BT_{n+1} → BT_n is smooth and surjective: Illusie, Astérisque 127 (1985),
   Thm. 4.4; locator unverified); by Serre-Tate and algebraization it is E_0[p^∞] for an elliptic curve E_0/O_K with
   supersingular reduction. Put E = E_0 ⊗ χ (quadratic twist): E[p] ≅ η ⊕ η is unramified (det = η² = 1, consistent
   with the Weil pairing), so _pE is constant over a finite unramified extension of K, which still contains μ_p; but E
   has additive, potentially good reduction, inertia acts on T_ℓ(E) through χ by ±1, and φ_E takes the value -2 on
   elements of order prime to p. So the expectation fails, in the supersingular case he singled out, and so does
   Raynaud's criterion with ℓ = p; the same E refutes "character = Brauer character of M(0) when μ_p ⊂ K" in the
   ind-unipotent case. This is presumably what "faux par Tate" records; Tate's own argument is not on the page and is not
   reconstructed (hunch H1-1). The construction rests on Kisin's theory and Illusie's lifting theorem; the Kisin-module
   dictionary (character of u^c, special fibre = 𝔐/u𝔐) is used, not re-derived.

**Status of 9:10-14.** The character is the inertial part of what Fontaine later attached to potentially crystalline
representations (the inertial type of the Weil-Deligne representation, Fontaine, Astérisque 223 (1994); locator
unverified); his question "is it determined by _pM?" is the question of how the inertial type relates to the reduction mod
p (hunch H1-2). Open on the page: the ind-unipotent case, which the construction above settles negatively.

## 1. 100:3-4 — the Theorem, and the Lemma, Complément and Corollary that give 1) (SA15-19)

**What he states (3).** S locally noetherian, U open, T = S - U, p prime with char k(t) = p for all t in T. C = abelian
schemes over S; C' = triples (A_U, M, phi) with A_U abelian over U, M p-divisible over S, phi: M|U -> T_p(A_U).

2) If a) there is a finite surjective S' -> S with S' geometrically unibranch (e.g. the normalization finite over S) and
b) prof et_T(S) >= 2, then F is an equivalence. (A cancelled earlier b): for t in T, any two irreducible components of
the strict localization meet outside the closed point; equivalently U'' is dense in S'' = S' x_S S'.)
Margin footnote: with L_n = pi_0 of S', S'', S''' and K_n the components whose image meets U, the condition b'): "la plus
petite partie de L_1 contenant K_1 et stable par 'troisième face' soit L_1 (c'est ce qui est assuré par la condition b))".

**What 4 proves.** Lemma (for any prime l): A, B abelian over S locally noetherian, u_l: T_l(A) -> T_l(B); there is a
closed subscheme T of S such that u_l becomes algebraic over S'/S iff S' -> S factors through T. "Cf Inventiones 1.2 (ou
démonstration cristalline)": presumably his own "Un théorème sur les homomorphismes de schémas abéliens", Invent. Math. 2
(1966) 59-78 (locator unverified). Complément: at t in T with char k(t) = l, T contains an open neighbourhood of t.
Corollary: if every connected component of S meets U, every point of S - U has characteristic l and u_l|U is
algebraic, then u_l is algebraic.

**Checked.**
- Corollary (check event 100:4a): T contains U as an open subscheme (hypothesis c), and a neighbourhood of each point of
  T in S - U (Complément and b); so T is open and closed, contains U, and meets every component: T = S. Correct.
- Complément (100:4b): over the infinitesimal neighbourhoods of t (l nilpotent there), Serre-Tate lifts the homomorphism

with A(t)|U(t) = A_U|U(t), U(t) = Spec O_(S,t) - {t}; spread A(t) to B over an affine V containing t; U(t) is the limit
of the U ∩ V' over affine V' containing t, with affine transition maps, so the isomorphism B|U(t) = A_U|U(t) and the one
on p-divisible groups spread to some V' (EGA IV 8.8.2); glue B|V' with A_U over U ∩ V'; T shrinks by a neighbourhood of
t in T; noetherian induction finishes. At each later step the local problem at a new maximal t is the old one, because
full faithfulness 1) over the punctured scheme U_k(t), with dense open U(t), identifies the extended A_(U_k) with the old
solution there (U is dense, since prof et_T(S) >= 1). So e) holds given d), and the "Douteux" lemma can be dropped; his
margin "inutile ?" beside it on 17 points the same way. The uniform-neighbourhood lemma begun on 19 ("Supposons d'abord S
excellent ... Alors", unfinished) is likewise not needed. Whether the local-system lemma itself is true is left open
(hunch H2-4).

## 4. 100:21 — when a homomorphism of p-divisible groups is algebraic (SA15-19)

"Question: quand [u_p: T_p(A) -> T_p(B)] provient-il d'un hom u: A -> B." a) (S noetherian) there is a largest subscheme
T on which u_p is algebraic; T is closed and its formation commutes with any base change. b) If t in T has
characteristic p, T contains an open neighbourhood of t. Cor.: "Soit Z l'ens des pts de S qui sont maximaux, ou qui sont
associés à O_S et de car. != p. Supposons que z in Z => closure(z) ∩ S_p ∩ T non vide. Alors u_p est algébrique." These
are the Lemma and Complément of 4 for l = p, and the Corollary in the form used on 9-11. The figure draws a family over S
with A_(S') -> B_(S').

**Checked (100:21).** The corollary holds when Z = Max(S) ∪ (Ass(O_S) ∩ S[1/p]): an associated point x of the ideal of T
is either in Z (then it generizes a point of T of characteristic p, so x has a neighbourhood in T) or an associated point
of characteristic p lying in the closure of a maximal point already in T (then b) applies at x); so the ideal is 0. The
other parse ("(maximaux ou associés) et de car != p") makes it false: S = Spec k, k an algebraic closure of F_p(t), E
ordinary with j = t, u_p the idempotent (1, 0) of End(E[p^infinity]) = Z_p x Z_p, not algebraic since End(E) = Z; Z is
empty and the hypothesis holds vacuously. His first wording, cancelled ("des éléments de Max S ... et de l'ens des"),
confirms the first parse. Correction row 12 (punctuation of the edition).

## 5. 100:23-27 — descent of morphisms along proper epimorphisms (SA15-19)

**Lemma 1 (23).** f: S' -> S a proper epimorphism of locally noetherian schemes (epimorphism: O_S -> f_* O_S' injective),
X formally unramified over S: Hom_S(S, X) -> Hom_S(S', X) => Hom_S(S'', X) is exact. Proof: g' with g' pr_1 = g' pr_2
factors topologically through S (f is submersive); the sheaf map lands in A = Ker(f_* O_S' => h_* O_S''), so g' factors
through S' -> Spec(A) = S'_1 -> X; "EGA III 3 indique aisément que S'_1 -> S' est fini, radiciel" — the ink reads S'
(checked); it must be S'_1 -> S (correction row 3); S'_1 -> S is an epimorphism since O_S ⊂ A ⊂ f_* O_S'.
**Checked (100:23a), independent re-proof:** S'_1 -> S is finite by coherence of f_* O_S' (EGA III 3.2.1, locator
unverified); radicial: for two points (or two Omega-points) of S' over the same point of S there is a point of S'' over
the pair, and the two composites S'' => S' -> S'_1 agree, so all of S' over s goes to one point of S'_1, and two
embeddings of its residue field over k(s) into an algebraically closed Omega agree; since S' -> S'_1 is surjective (A ⊂
f_* O_S'), S'_1 -> S is radicial.
**Corollary 1** (f entier radiciel epimorphism, X formally unramified; f finite and S locally noetherian, or X locally of
finite type): Hom_S(S, X) -> Hom_S(S', X) bijective. Checked (100:25): S' -> S is a homeomorphism; locally, replacing X
by the schematic image, O_(S,s) ⊂ O_(X,x) ⊂ O_(S',s') with O_(X,x) finite over O_(S,s), unramified, residue extension
radicial hence trivial; Nakayama gives equality. **Corollary 2:** a proper epimorphism of noetherian schemes is a descent
morphism (full faithfulness) for abelian schemes, since Hom_S(A, B) is representable and unramified. Correct.
**Corollary 3** (p-divisible groups): reduced to "Il faut prouver que X [= Hom_S(A, B)] est formellement net"; open on the
page. Check event 100:25b: formal unramifiedness of the Hom functor of p-divisible groups holds by rigidity
(Katz, LNM 868 (1981), Lemma 1.1.3, locator unverified), but Lemma 1's route through Corollary 1 needs it for the limit,
not for the finite levels Hom(A[p^n], B[p^n]), which are not unramified (Hom(alpha_p, alpha_p) = G_a); the page does not
say how to pass to the limit.

**Effectivity (27).** f as in Cor. 2 and an effective epimorphism. a) reduction to S local, "triviale, par passage à la
limite"; b) to S complete, by "le critère de descente plate des schémas abéliens de Raynaud", noting that Cor. 2 cannot
be applied to S_2 = Spec of the completed tensor square (not noetherian) and that flat base change carries the
radicial algebra A along. Margin: "donner remarque après lemme 1; donne aussi le cas de 0-descente". A cancelled c)
(dimension 0) is replaced by d) on 29.

## 6. 100:29-39 — a formal abelian scheme, and when it is algebraic (SA15-19)

**d) Construction of the formal abelian scheme (29-31).** "si S_0 = Spec k (k corps résiduel) alors S'_0/S_0 a une
section, donc S'_0 -> S_0 est un morphisme de descente effective universelle" — ink checked: "a une section" (Codex web
reads so; Pro has "une galér(?)", correction row 4); it needs k algebraically closed, as in the cancelled c) of 27. With
M the formal moduli of A_0 over Spf W(k), a formal deformation of A'_0 over S^' is a map S^' -> M; the descent datum is
"phi' p_1 = phi' p_2"; the sequence S^ <- S^' <= S^'' of formal schemes is exact (comparison theorem), so A^' descends to
a formal abelian scheme A^ over Spf R. Checked (100:29): maps S^' -> M are continuous maps of the universal deformation
ring into Gamma(S^', O), which is the completion of Gamma(S', O) by EGA III 4.1.5 (locator unverified); completion is
exact on finite modules, and the effective-epimorphism hypothesis gives O_S = Ker(f_* O_S' => h_* O_S''), so equalized
maps from S^' come from S^.

**Th. (31-35).** R complete local noetherian, S = Spec R, A^ a formal abelian scheme over Spf R ("Spf B" in the
transcription; R is meant). Equivalent: (i) A^ algebraic; (ii) A^ becomes algebraic after some proper surjective
S' -> S; (iii) for every integral quotient B "de A" (read: of R, correction row 7) of dimension 1, with normalization B',
A^ over Spf B' is algebraic. Margin NB: the proof only needs the one-dimensional points outside any given countable union
of closed sets T_i. Proof: (ii) => (iii) by a trait T_1 finite over the complete trait and descent along the finite
flat T_1 -> T (projective); (iii) => (ii'): a finite surjective unramified S' -> S over which A^ is algebraic — the
polarizations of the A_n form a formal scheme P^ = ⊔ P^alpha, a countable family of finite unramified S-schemes; (iii)
puts every one-dimensional point in some Z_alpha = Im(P^alpha); the Proposition gives finitely many; (ii') => (i'):
algebraic over S_red (closed immersions S'_i -> S after a finite étale base change, one of them an isomorphism on each
irreducible component; then a gluing lemma for components: O_S = O_S' x_(O_T) O_S'' for S = S' ∪ S'' reduced); (i') => (i):
Lemma: X^ proper smooth over Spf R, algebraic over S_red => algebraic, by deformation theory with the obstruction in
H^2 and the indeterminacy in H^1 of T ⊗ J on X_0 and on X^_0, identified by the comparison theorem ("sans doute cas
particulier d'un [lemme] vieillot à démontrer avec complexe cot. relatif"). Checked (100:31, 100:33-35, 100:35-37): each
implication holds as written; in (i') => (i) the two torsors of liftings correspond under the comparison isomorphism, so
the formal lifting is algebraic, and the group law algebraizes (EGA III 5.4.1, locator unverified). The second H in 2°
on 37 is H^1 (Codex web reads H^1; Pro leaves H^(1|2)); correction row 11.

**Proposition and Corollary (37-39). Independent re-proof, check event 100:37-39.** S local noetherian complete, (Z_i)
a countable family of closed sets whose union contains every x with dim closure(x) <= 1: finitely many Z_i cover S.
Reduce to S integral (components); if no Z_i is all of S they are all nowhere dense, and the Corollary gives a point of
dimension 1 outside their union. The Corollary: R finite over V[[T_1, ..., T_r]], V a complete discrete valuation ring
(Cohen); images of nowhere dense closed sets stay nowhere dense (R a domain); the V-homomorphisms
V[[T]] -> V form m^r, a complete metric space; for f != 0 the zero set of f in m^r is closed with empty interior (after
translating and scaling, a non-zero power series vanishing on all of m^r contradicts Weierstrass preparation in one
variable by induction on r, m being infinite); Baire gives phi outside all of them, and Ker(phi) is a point of dimension 1
avoiding every Z_i. Correct.

## 7. 100:41-45 — remarks: proper flat formal schemes, and descent of flat proper schemes (SA15-19)

## Hunches

- **H2-1 (status of the Theorem for reduced S).** With S reduced, the proof on 3-45 closes up to two points it leaves open:
  the gluing in a) is not needed (S reduced), Cor. 3's passage to the limit, and the non-finite normalization case of b).
  For S normal of generic characteristic 0 the statement follows from Tate's full faithfulness plus extension over
  points of codimension 1 and descent; I expect the reduced, geometrically unibranch case to be true as stated. Grounds:
  every step checked above; the counterexample needs nilpotents. Related later work on extending abelian schemes over
  bases of dimension >= 2: Faltings-Chai, Degeneration of Abelian Varieties (1990), chapter V §6, and Vasiu-Zink,
  "Purity results for p-divisible groups and abelian schemes over regular bases of mixed characteristic", Doc. Math. 15
  (2010) (both locators unverified).
- **H2-2 (dating).** The manuscript is from 1968, the year SGA 7 (1967-1969) was being written; 3-45 look like the
  generalization to bases of dimension > 1 of the trait case typed on 48-51 for SGA 7 IX. Grounds: the Illusie letter
  under 29, the forward references 13 and 15, the typescript 48-51 cited as "Raynaud" on 9.
- **H2-3 (the algebraization criterion of 31).** "A formal abelian scheme over a complete local noetherian ring is algebraic
  iff it is algebraic over the normalization of every one-dimensional integral quotient, even avoiding a countable union of
  closed sets" may not be in print in this form. Grounds: I know of no such statement; it would follow today from Artin's
  algebraization for formal abelian schemes with an ample class, through the same NS-and-Baire argument.
- **H2-4 (the local-system lemma of 17).** For S normal it is true (homomorphisms of abelian schemes extend over normal
  bases); for reduced non-normal S I could not decide it; his "Douteux, sans doute faux" may target non-reduced bases,
  where the counterexample to 2) suggests local data need not glue. It is not needed for the Theorem (section 3 e).
- **H2-5 (the uniform neighbourhood of 19).** True for S normal; for non-normal S the neighbourhood V must avoid the points
  where the punctured strict localization is disconnected (a nodal curve with a twist by -1 at the node: an isomorphism
  given at a smooth point does not extend across the node). The lemma is unfinished and not needed.
- **H2-6 (l != p, 3 on 11).** The overwritten digit was probably 2 ("l != p, 2"); 3 over it may be a slip.
