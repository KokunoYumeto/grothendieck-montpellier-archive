## Cote 161-1, "Catégories" (folder K161-1-01; scans 4-8, 10-14, 16-20; 15 pages)

The cote is not one text. It holds five groups of loose sheets on different subjects: adjunctions (4-8), symmetric
monoidal categories (10-13), theories and their constructions (14), exactness of extended functors (16), a stray
p-adic Hodge sheet (17), and extension of functors from a strictly generating subcategory (18-20).

### 1. Adjunctions and the largest subcategories where they are equivalences (scans 4-8; SA23-29)
- **What he does (shown).** For u: E -> F left adjoint to v, unit i: id -> vu, counit j: uv -> id:
  - a) for a full E' ⊂ E: u|E' fully faithful and vu(E') ⊂ (essential image of E') iff every i(Y), Y in E', is an
    isomorphism (condition (1)); b) the dual (2) with j;
  - c) strictly full subcategories satisfying (1) and (2) correspond bijectively by taking essential images;
  - d) E_0 = {Y : i(Y) iso}, F_0 = {P : j(P) iso} are the largest such; u and v restrict to inverse equivalences;
  - e) v(F) ⊂ E_0 iff i(v(P)) is iso for all P ("v(j(P)) sera alors l'inverse ??"); in the margin: iff v factors as
    F -> E_1 -> E with the second factor fully faithful and the first a passage to a category of fractions. He proves that
    then F_0 ⊂ F has the right adjoint u_0∘v', and dually (scan 6) that if u(E) ⊂ F_0 then E_0 ⊂ E has the left adjoint
    v_0∘u';
  - f) four pairs of equivalent conditions (Im v ⊂ E_0; v ≃ vuv; reflectivity; factorisation through a localisation and a
    fully faithful functor), and: such an adjunction is the same thing as a reflective E_0 ⊂ E, a coreflective F_0 ⊂ F and
    an equivalence E_0 ≃ F_0, with u = β u_0 α', v = α v_0 β';
  - scans 7-8: examples (E punctual: v has a left adjoint iff F has an initial object), then the question: if α' is a
    reflection onto E_1 and β: E_1 -> F fully faithful with βα' having a right adjoint, has β one? He reduces it (one
    extra object a, φ = ∅) to: is the subcategory of Σ-local objects (Σ = arrows inverted by α') the essential image of
    E_1? "Or c'est vrai sauf erreur ....".
- **Checked.** a)-f) re-derived; the margin's factorisation criterion holds (Gabriel-Zisman I.1.3: a right adjoint that is
  a localisation has a fully faithful left adjoint; locator unverified). His two open questions are both answered
  positively: (i) v(F) ⊂ E_0 makes the monad vu idempotent, and for an idempotent monad every algebra structure is the
  inverse of the unit, so v(F) ⊂ E_0 iff u(E) ⊂ F_0, and his scan 6 gives the reflector; (ii) on scans 7-8 the answer is
  yes in general, without his reduction: vP is Σ-local, so lies in the essential image of α, and α'∘v is right adjoint
  to β. The example his margin on scan 4 asks for: E = (Ens), F punctual, E' = {∅}.
- **Wrong.** f) 1') reads "Im u ⊂ E_0" in the ink (tilde under E_0); F_0 is meant (his 3') has F_0). His slip.
- **Open on the page.** Nothing that is not settled above; "à voir si on peut simplifier" (scan 6) is an editorial wish.
- **Today.** This is the theory of idempotent adjunctions (fixed points of an adjunction; idempotent monads). Standard
  now; no locator opened.

### 2. Free symmetric monoidal category, products of families, contraction, signs (scans 10-13)
- **What he does (shown).** Φ(A): finite families (I, (L_i)), morphisms a bijection τ and isomorphisms φ_i; ⊗ by disjoint
  union, structures AUC ("associativité, unité, commutativité"). Proposition: for C a ⊗-category AUC, restriction to Φ_1
  gives an equivalence Hom^{⊗AUC}(Φ(A), C) -> Hom(A, C) ("Dém ...."). Corollary: a unique ⊗-functor Π: Φ(C) -> C
  extending the identity, "le produit ⊗ de la famille"; converse: an AUC structure on (C, ⊗) is the same as such a Π
  compatible with ⊗ on Φ_2. Scan 12, "Contraction": Φ^c(C), families with a set of disjoint index pairs and pairings
  L_j' ⊗ L_j'' -> 1 ("qui soit un accouplement parfait i.e. une dualité"), two functors inc, res to Φ(C), and the
  contraction morphism Π∘inc -> Π∘res of ⊗-functors. Scan 13: no canonical isomorphism Π(L,...,L) ≃ L^{⊗n}; for
  invertible L the symmetry of L⊗L is ε(L) in Aut(1), of order 2, and a permutation acts by ε(L) to its parity; no
  ambiguity "dans le seul cas des catégories de Picard [tout objet inv, les ε(L) = 1]"; then inverses (L, L') with
  φ: L⊗L' ≃ 1 and the compatibility of the two contractions of L⊗L'⊗L (left as a question).
- **Reading.** The transcription's "Φ(e)", "Φ_1(e)" are Φ(C), Φ_1(C) (decided on the scans; correction filed).
- **Checked.** The Proposition is right for A a groupoid, as his Φ(A) has isomorphisms only; with arbitrary φ_i it is
  the free symmetric monoidal category on any A (Joyal-Street, Braided tensor categories, 1993; locator unverified). The
  sign statement is right with the exponent read as the parity in Z/2. "Catégorie de Picard" here means the strictly
  commutative ones (ε = 1), as in SGA 4 XVIII 1.4 (locator unverified).
- **Open on the page.** The proof of the Proposition ("Dém ...."); the relation between φ_{L',L} and ψ_{L',L} (scan 13).
  Both are routine coherence statements today.
- **Inferred.** The contraction formalism (scan 12) is the tensor calculus of compact closed categories (Kelly-Laplaza
  1980; locator unverified).

### 3. Theories with finite limits and their constructions (scan 14, his page "2")
- **Shown.** A diagram Hom_u(I; T, T') -> Hom(T, T') over its "0" version, "≃ si T-bar, T'-bar sont de prés. lim<-_0";
  theories with finite projective limits (resp. arbitrary ones) form 2-categories; with I fixed, 2-categories of
  I-morphisms. "Constructions" of a theory T: by definition morphisms T -> i, where i is the theory of a bare set
  (i_C = C), i.e. maps of 2-fibred categories over (Cat lim<- fin); they form a category Γ^T = Hom(T, i) with finite
  limits, with Γ^T × T -> i; for C with finite limits and ξ in T(C), u |-> u(ξ), Γ^T -> C, is left exact.
- **Inferred.** Γ^T is the category of definable constructions of T (its syntactic category in later terms), and
  ξ |-> (u |-> u(ξ)) is the functor T(C) -> Lex(Γ^T, C); a theory is "representable" when this is an equivalence. The
  sheet is page 2 of a set whose page 1 is not in this cote; it belongs with SA23-30 (cote 161-2, theories and
  2-representable theories). Nothing proved on the page beyond the left exactness, which is immediate since limits in
  Γ^T are computed objectwise.

### 4. Exactness of extended functors; topoi as left exact reflections (scan 16)
- **Shown.** (i) C small with finite inductive limits, φ: C -> Ens right exact, φ-bar(ψ) = Hom(ψ, φ) on
  Hom(C, Ens)^op: is it right exact? It would need φ injective; "Mais c'est tjrs faux": take ψ' = φ ⊔ ρ with ρ constant
  of non-empty value x; Hom(ρ, φ) = ∅ since φ(∅_C) = ∅. (ii) φ: C -> E left exact, C with finite limits, E with finite
  limits and small inductive limits; when is the extension φ-bar: C-hat -> E left exact? Never for E = Ens^op; true if
  E is a topos; converse: if φ is strictly generating, E -> C-hat is fully faithful with left adjoint φ-bar, so φ-bar
  left exact makes E a topos.
- **Checked.** Both arguments are right (check events).
- **Today.** The converse is the characterisation of Grothendieck topoi as left exact reflective subcategories of
  presheaf categories (SGA 4 IV; locator unverified). The page states it as a corollary of the setup, in two lines.

### 6. Extending functors from a strictly generating subcategory (scans 18-20; scan 19 is a small diagram)
- **Shown.** C ⊂ E full, strictly generating, E and F with small inductive limits; α = i_! (α(φ)(X) = lim-> over C/X of
  φ), β = restriction; βα ≃ id, α ⊣ β, α fully faithful. Necessary condition (*) for α(φ) to commute with small inductive
  limits: φ preserves the inductive limits of systems in C whose limit in E lies in C. Hom'(C,F), Hom'(E,F) defined
  accordingly; β': Hom'(E,F) -> Hom'(C,F) is fully faithful (since X = lim-> over C/X); it is an equivalence iff α maps
  Hom'(C,F) into Hom'(E,F); reduced to a **Lemma**: for φ satisfying (*) and any system Y_i in C with limit Y in E,
  lim-> φ(Y_i) ≃ α(φ)(Y). "I -> C/Y, cela est-il cofinal ? C'est vrai en tous cas si pour tout diagramme fini dans C,
  la lim-> dans E existe et est dans C."
- **Checked.** The setup and full faithfulness are right. The cofinality claim is **false** as stated (C = countable
  sets in E = Ens, I = finite subsets of N, Y = N: C/Y has the final object id_N, through which no Y_i factors). The
  closure hypothesis is not idle: for C = {2} ⊂ Ens (strictly generating), φ(2) = 1 with trivial End(2)-action satisfies
  (*) but α(φ)(2 ⊔ 2) = 1 while lim-> φ = 1 ⊔ 1, so β' is not an equivalence without it.
- **Wrong.** (*) is written with lim<- (his slip; inductive limit meant).
- **Open on the page.** The Lemma under his closure hypothesis. My reduction: with C closed under finite inductive limits
  in E, (*) reduces any system to a filtered one (limits of finite subsystems lie in C); what remains is whether
  lim->_K φ(Y_K) -> lim->_{C/Y} φ(Z) is an isomorphism for a filtered system Y_K with limit Y, which holds when the
  objects of C are finitely presentable in E (E = Ind(C)) and is not settled in general.
- **Today.** Dense subcategories and cocontinuous extensions (Kelly, Basic concepts of enriched category theory, ch. 5;
  locator unverified); the condition the page is after is the "C-canonical" colimit condition.

#### 7. The δ-exact envelope theorem (scans 4-7)
- **Shown.** d = (d', d'') small sets of diagram types; Δ = Δ_d the 2-category of categories with limits of type d' and
  inductive limits of type d'', and functors commuting with them; C essentially small with a small set δ = (δ', δ'') of
  projective and inductive cones. **Theorem:** there is C-bar in Δ, essentially small, and a δ-exact i: C -> C-bar such that
  φ |-> φ∘i is an equivalence Hom_{Δ,i(δ)}(C-bar, E) -> Hom_δ(C, E) for all E in Δ ("objet 2-initial"). NB: this solves
  **Pb 1** (δ = ∅: free completion under limits of type d) and **Pb 2** (C in Δ; δ' = exact cones of type d' plus the
  arrows of Σ as one-leg cones, δ'' = exact cones of type d'': the Δ-localisation at Σ). Proof: a transfinite sequence
  C_{i+1} = K(C_i, δ_i), inductive limits in Cat at limit ordinals; K is "préexact" (a'_1: pairs equalised by a cone are
  identified; a'_2: augmentations factor), adds cones to every diagram of type d (b), keeps track of the cones (c) and
  induces equivalences on Hom_δ (d); then a large ordinal ω gives 1°) C_ω in Δ, 2°) C -> C_ω δ-exact, 3°) Hom_{δ_ω} =
  Hom_{Δ,τ_ω(δ)}. K = σρ with ρ = ρ_3ρ_2ρ_1 (envelope, equalise, add factorisations) and σ its dual; "L'ordre ... n'a
  pas d'importance"; "La chose ayant une signification intrinsèque est la limite des itérés transfinis de K....".
- **Checked.** Outline right. **Gap:** ω is "le plus petit ordinal de cardinal π" with π only > card Fl Θ; π must be
  regular (successor cardinal), or S_ω need not be large. **His slip:** inside ρ_1-ρ_3 the projective side carries double
  primes (Δ'', δ'') in the ink; the steps are about projective cones.
- **Today.** Free completions of a category under given limit types subject to making given cones exact: the
  completion theorems for mixed sketches (Ehresmann's school, about 1966-72) and the transfinite-construction
  results of Gabriel-Ulmer 1971 and Kelly 1980 (locators unverified). The page proves it by hand, for limits and
  colimits at once.

#### 10. Every topos represents an algebraic theory, built in steps (scans 20-23)
- **Shown.** For a topos R with b: I -> R: 1°) the empty structure on base objects (X_i), represented by R_0 = presheaves

  cones exact); R_0 -> R_1 -> R_2 -> R_3 ≃ R; "Ĵ_1 -> R est un foncteur de localisation". "Re NB": for "R algebraic"
  it is shorter to start from C with finite limits, R ≃ C~, and add the continuity axioms. Then "il faut suivre pas à pas
  la construction transfinie": a) empty structure, b) axioms (Pb 2), c) adding arrows = a 2-fibred product
  T_R ×^2 τ_C over τ_I, hence the 2-fibred product of topoi R ×^2_T S, "± connue, redonnons en une autre" via small
  generating sites and the amalgamated sum U_0 (scan 22); d) passage to the limit: 2-inductive limits in Δ of topoi exist
  and are topoi, choosing small generating C_α stable under transition "à l'aide des filtrations cardinales, mais c'est
  un marteau pour une mouche". Scan 23 sums it up with "analyseurs": J_1 the free "analyseur" generated by J_0 and the
  arrows, J_2 the full sub-analyseur of R generated by I -> R; ρ' the envelope for Δ_π (categories with finite products).
- **Checked.** c) and d) (check events). **Wrong:** "surjectif sur les Hom" for R -> R' (scans 20-21) is false; the
  condensation of 3° and 4° needs only that arrows of R' are fractions.
- **Reading.** "filtrations cardinales" on scan 23, decided on the scan (the transcription offered three readings).
- **Inferred.** "Analyseur" is his word here for a category with finite products, i.e. a many-sorted Lawvere theory

  classifying topos (the theorem that every Grothendieck topos classifies a geometric theory; Makkai-Reyes 1977,
  Johnstone's Elephant D3; locators unverified).

## Hunches (with grounds)

- **H1 (161-2 scans 12, 64; 7-9).** His question "des conditions de type (i') peuvent-elles être exprimées comme
  conditions de type (ii') ?" has a positive answer in Cat: fullness (surjectivity on Hom) of restriction along φ is
  essential surjectivity of restriction along the inclusion of R-bar ⊔_{R ⊔ R} (R × [1]) ⊔ ... into R-bar × [1]
  (cylinders), and faithfulness likewise with a double cylinder; this is how the 0-, 1-, 2-faithful conditions of the Δ_f
  theorem (Schanuel's "conditions d'exactitudes généralisées", scan 64) reduce to one kind. Not proved here; the
  2-categorical orthogonality literature (Kelly 1989) would confirm.
- **H2 (dating, F13).** The accessible-category sheets (161-2 scans 55-62, 78) were written with the printed numbering
  of SGA 4 I §8-9 at hand (every locator I checked matches); "(modifié 9.4?)" and "9.12. (Condition c) renforcée)" read
  like proposals for the text of Exposé I, so a date during the preparation of the 1972 edition (about 1968-72) is the
  most economical reading. Checking whether the 1964 multigraphed Exposé I has §9 with these numbers would decide
  between "before printing" and "after".
- **H3 (priority, F13).** If H2 holds, his definition "π-accessible = L_π + a small strictly generating subcategory of
  π-accessible objects; in general Ind_π(C)" and the theorem "E π-accessible iff E ≃ Ind_π(C) iff E_π small and strictly
  generating" (scans 55, 60) predate Lair (1981) and Makkai-Paré (1989) by about a decade, and the cocomplete case meets
  Gabriel-Ulmer (1971). Worth a comparison with the printed SGA 4 I 9.4-9.11 to see how much of it reached print.
- **H4 (dating, SA23-54).** "Topos modulaire" on scan 112 says the 2-fibred product of topoi is "pas encore prouvé",
  while scan 22 gives a construction it calls "± connue": scan 112 likely precedes scan 22, and both precede or are
  contemporary with Giraud's "Classifying topos" (1972) and Hakim (1972).
- **H5 (161-2 scan 79).** His question on finite-product theories (which Σ give the same S) is the Morita problem for
  Lawvere theories; the answer should be: full subcategories of the perfectly presentable objects of S, closed under
  finite coproducts, whose idempotent completion contains them all (Adámek-Rosický-Vitale 2011, ch. 15; locator
  unverified).
- **H6 (161-2 scan 44-50, the ring catalogue).** The "topologie hensélienne" of item 3) is the Nisnevich topology (1989)
  and the fppf item 5) the topology of "ultralocal" rings; the catalogue may be the earliest systematic list of
  classifying topoi of ring-theoretic properties; to compare with Hakim (1972) and Wraith/Johnstone (1970s).
- **H7 (161-2 scan 93).** "La réciproque est vraie, et connue": the known result is Olivier's absolutely flat
  universal ring (Séminaire Samuel 1967-68), which would date the sheet after 1967 (locator unverified).
- **H8 (161-3 scan 15).** His "contre-exemple pour des produits infinis" of discrete spaces may need I uncountable: I
  recall (unverified) Isbell's results that countable localic products of complete metric spaces are spatial, while
  uncountable products of infinite discrete spaces are not.
- **H9 (161-3 scan 11).** The "Raffinement" (no non-empty locally closed part without relatively quasi-compact
  neighbourhoods suffices for Top(X × Y) ≃ Top(X) × Top(Y)) is sharper than the local-compactness criterion of the
  literature; I have not seen it stated (search needed).
- **H10 (dating).** The English sheets of 161-3 (scans 35-41, 49, 53-55) and 161-2 (scans 34-35) are notes for, or of,
  an English course or talk on topoi and triples, about 1966-73; Grothendieck's 1973 Buffalo lectures on topoi are a
  candidate for 161-3 (not established).
- **H11 (161-1 scan 20).** His Lemma (extension from a strictly generating C ⊂ E closed under finite inductive limits)
  is true when the objects of C are finitely presentable in E and is open in general; 161-2 scan 57 g) states a version
  with finite projective limits instead.
