**His objection on 3 is correct in substance.**
- **The statement.** The printed Lemma ('classique') says: A is local noetherian, analytically integral, with K_Â separable over K_A; B = C_p is an integral local ring with C finite over A and p ∩ A = M. Then B is analytically reduced.
- **Without injectivity it fails.** If A → B is not injective, then C = A/q and B = A/q are allowed. The lemma would then say that every quotient A/q of such an A is analytically reduced, while the hypothesis bears only on the generic formal fibre of A.
- **Counterexamples.** Regular local rings with a non-analytically-reduced quotient exist (Nagata's examples; locator unverified). In char 0 the separability hypothesis holds automatically, so the statement needs A ⊂ B.
- **Status.** Checked by argument, not by a written counterexample (check event below). The Pro note O07 (MPL_22+23_002-016#13) records the same point.

### 22/022-025: formal simplicity for the discrete topology (SA23-19, H0D7)
- **Scan 22: his question.** Is a complete local A ≅ K[[t_1..t_n]], formally simple over k for its usual topology, also formally simple for the discrete topology?

- **Scan 23: char 0.** "Je présume que si dim A ≥ [1], A n'est jamais form^t simple sur k pour sa topologie discrète". The test is whether Ω_k ⊗ A → Ω_A has a left inverse, for k = Q((x_i)_{i∈I}) and A = k[[t]].
- **Scans 24-25: his Proposition.** If Ω_{K/k} is infinite-dimensional and n > 0, then A = K[[t_1..t_n]] with the discrete topology is not formally simple over K relative to k, a fortiori not over k. He proves it.
- **The proof re-derived (correct).**
  - A left inverse D sends the t_i into V ⊗ A with V finite-dimensional. A finitely generated k' ⊃ k kills those components, and Ω_{K/k'} stays infinite-dimensional. So one may assume D t_i = 0.
  - Then D(Σ a_λ t^λ) = Σ (d a_λ) t^λ coefficientwise.
  - Choose a sequence of distinct coordinates i_n and a_n with d^{i_n} a_n ≠ 0. Then f = Σ a_n t^n has D f outside the direct sum A^{(I)}, a contradiction.
- **Open.** For Ω_{K/k} finite-dimensional in char 0, for example Q[[t]] over Q, the page leaves his presumption without proof. See the hunches.

### 23/006: the discriminant (SA23-21)
- **What he writes.**
  - δ_{r,k} ∈ Φ^{(k−1)(r+1)}(Φ^k(P^r)), "(discriminant) défini au signe près". The margin brace adds r+1 copies of k−1.
  - For a family φ of degree-k hypersurfaces over S (line M̌), δ pulls back to a section of M̌^{(k−1)(r+1)}, and P^r_φ is smooth over S iff δ is nowhere zero.
  - J ≅ g*(M̌)(−k).
- **His slip: the degree.** The discriminant of forms of degree k in r+1 variables has degree (r+1)(k−1)^r in the coefficients: it is the resultant of the r+1 partial derivatives, each of degree k−1, and each enters with degree (k−1)^r (Gelfand-Kapranov-Zelevinsky; locator unverified). His (k−1)(r+1) is right only for r = 1, binary forms, where both give 2k−2, and for k = 2, quadrics, where both give the degree r+1 of the symmetric determinant. For plane cubics the true degree is 12, not 6.
  **Read on the scan (23.pdf page 6, 200 dpi):** the ink has (k−1)(r+1) in the formula, under the brace in the margin, and in O((k−1)(r+1)). The reading is confirmed; the degree is his slip.
- **The weight.** Over a base with E not trivial, δ is a section of M̌^{(r+1)(k−1)^r} ⊗ (∧^{r+1}E)^{k(k−1)^r}. For the plane cubic of 23/005 this gives L^{−72} ⊗ L^{60} = L^{−12} = ω^{⊗12}, the classical weight of Δ. This confirms the corrected degree.
- **Link (my inference).** "Défini au signe près" and smoothness ⇔ δ invertible over any base is the programme Demazure carried out for the discriminant over Z (M. Demazure, "Résultant, discriminant", Enseign. Math. 58 (2012); locator unverified). There the integral normalization is the delicate point.

### 23/008-016: "Exponentielle": nilpotent exponential, hyperalgebras, Cartier (SA23-22, H0DB)
- **Scan 9.** For a separated complete linearly topological Q-algebra U, exp and log are inverse bijections between the topologically nilpotent elements and 1 + N(U). They are functorial, and closed subalgebras are stable.
- **Scans 10-11.** A k-hyperalgebra (augmented, with diagonal Δ). The group-like elements γ(U) form a group: they are the homomorphisms of k[T] with δT = T⊗T, "le dual top. de l'Algèbre du groupe Z". For k ⊃ Q, exp and log are inverse bijections between the primitives π(U) and γ(U), with the Campbell-Hausdorff group law on π(U).
- **Scans 12-13.** For an infinitesimal k-algebra Λ, the relative primitives and group-likes of U ⊗ Λ correspond. The map π(U) ⊗ Λ → π_0(U ⊗ Λ) is an isomorphism if Λ is k-flat or 0 → π(U) → I(U) → I(U)⊗I(U) is universally exact.
- **The margin of 13.** Cartier's theorem follows: U ≅ U(g) with g = π(U) (Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt). If g is finite-dimensional, the dual of U is a regular local ring. "Cor Un groupe alg. en car 0 est toujours lisse". Consequence: a Cartier hyperalgebra over a field is known from the functor π_0(U ⊗ Λ).
- **Scans 14-15.** In End(M), nilpotent ξ and unipotent exp ξ have the same fixed vectors, commutants and stable submodules, and derivations correspond to automorphisms. For Λ with I(Λ)^n = 0 and k ⊃ Q, the u with uM ⊂ I(Λ)M are nilpotent, and the bijection holds. Corollary: the automorphisms of a Λ-algebra B inducing the identity on B ⊗_Λ k ↔ the Λ-derivations D with DB ⊂ I(Λ)B.
- **Scan 16, Proposition.** For k ⊃ Q, A a commutative k-algebra and Λ infinitesimal, the infinitesimal Λ-automorphisms of A ⊗_k Λ form the group of k-derivations A → A ⊗_k I(Λ), with the Hausdorff-Campbell law.
- **Checked.**
  - The bijection on 14-15 rests on u^n = 0 (Work note R4 rechecked it). exp D is an automorphism for a derivation D with D^n = 0 by the Leibniz formula; log of an automorphism is a derivation, by the same identity read backwards.
  - The isomorphism Hom_B(Ω_{A/k} ⊗ Λ, A ⊗ Λ) ≅ Hom_A(Ω_{A/k}, A ⊗ Λ) on 16 is base change. All correct.
- **Link (F07).** In cote 68 he tried a version with J generated by the u(x) − x. There his NB "u induit l'identité sur gr_J(A)" fails, as sweep 2 rechecked. Here the hypothesis uM ⊂ I(Λ)M, with I(Λ) nilpotent, is the one that works.

### 23/053-054: his PS on regular immersions (for Dieudonné; date 1966-67, from the SGA 6 reference)
**What he says:**
- **The diagnosis.** In his Riemann-Roch seminar (SGA 6) regular immersions must be handled in the non-noetherian case, "et il apparait (hélas) que la notion introduite sous ce nom dans EGA 16.9.2. n'est pas la bonne notion". In a local ring, (x, y) can be a regular sequence while (y, x) is not.
- **The fix.** The serviceable notion is that the Koszul complex of (f_1..f_n) is acyclic (the transcription reads "nul en degrés 0"; a reading question for the scan, since the sense is acyclic in positive degrees). Then:
  - the sequence is quasi-regular;
  - every other system of n generators of J has the same property;
  - "idéal régulier" and "immersion régulière" are defined locally from it;
  - these notions descend by fpqc, and the composite of two regular immersions is regular;
  - the transitivities of 19.1.5 (iii) seem to hold without noetherian hypotheses ("Berthelot en a déjà vérifié une").
- **What he proposes for EGA.**

**What I checked:**

- **The Auslander-Buchsbaum argument re-derived: correct.** Ext^q(k, A) = 0 for q < a, Tor_{−p} = 0 for −p > d, and Tor_d(Ext^a(k, A), M) ≠ 0.

### 23/083-084: Lemma 1, "Rappels sur la dimension"
**What he writes.** X is noetherian, Y closed with components Y_i, and r = Codim(Y, X). I' is the set of i with Codim(Y_i, X) = r, and K_i the set of components X_k ⊃ Y_i with codim(Y_i, X_k) = r. For a section x of an invertible sheaf with V(x) ⊃ Y:
- **1°)** Codim(Y, V(x)) ≥ r − 1, "et pour qu'on ait égalité, il f. et s. qu'il existe i ∈ I', k ∈ K_i tels que V(x) ⊅ X_k".
- **2°)** On a quasi-affine X with r > 0 there is x with Codim(V(x), X) = 1, so that codim(Y, V(x)) = r − 1. It is chosen by avoiding the minimal primes.
- **Corollaries 1 and 2.** An ideal of height r contains x_1..x_r of height r, part of a system of parameters. The parameters of A_p extend to part of a system of parameters of A/p.

**His slip in 1°).**
- **The inclusions disagree.** The proof ends with "∃ k ∈ K_i t.q. V(x) ⊃ X_k", the opposite inclusion of the statement.
- **Neither form is right.** The correct condition is that for some i ∈ I', V(x) ⊅ X_k for every k ∈ K_i.
- **The two-component test.** Take O local with minimal primes P_1, P_2, dim O/P_j = r, and x ∈ P_1, x ∉ P_2. Then dim O/x = max(dim O/(P_1 + x), dim O/(P_2 + x)) = max(r, r − 1) = r: no drop.
- **2°) agrees.** The choice in 2°), avoiding all minimal primes, satisfies the corrected condition, so the corollaries stand.
- The Work note K23-05 S1 saw the ⊅/⊃ discrepancy; the quantifier is added here.
