### Working it through (each step a check event)

- **① ⇔ ③ ⇔ ⑦′.** X = V/Π. A closed geodesic of the flat metric is a translate of the image of ℝ·a for a primitive
  a ∈ Π. Its class modulo translations is the class of a, up to sign (the orientation). So ① ⇔ ③, and ⑦′ is the same
  datum written additively.
- **③ ⇔ ④ ⇔ ⑤.**
  - Write Π = ℤa ⊕ ℤc. The map w = exp(2πi z/a) identifies V/ℤa with ℂ*, and X with ℂ*/b^ℤ, where b = exp(2πi c/a).
    Here |b| ≠ 1 because Im(c/a) ≠ 0.
  - So X = (P ∖ {0, ∞})/u^ℤ, with u the homothety w ↦ bw, whose fixed points are 0 and ∞.
  - Choosing the generator with |b| < 1 (④′) or with |λ| > 1 (⑤) fixes the sign.
- **⑤ ⇔ ⑥.** A discrete subgroup ≅ ℤ of PGL₂(ℂ) made of semisimple elements is generated by a loxodromic element: an
  elliptic element of infinite order generates a non-discrete group, and a parabolic one is unipotent. So ⑥ ⇔ ⑤.
- **The two-element chain.** The following are all two-element sets, and the maps between them are the natural ones:
  - the orientations of Γ and of Γ₀;
  - the generators of Z;
  - the trivializations T ≅ G_m (z and z⁻¹);
  - the fixed points of u;
  - the Borel subgroups of G containing Z (the stabilizers of the two fixed points).

  Agrees.
- **{Γ} ≃ X/Γ₀ ≃ circles.** In the coordinate w the geodesics of the class are the circles |w| = r, modulo r ~ |b|r. So
  the set is ℝ*₊/|b|^ℤ, a circle, and X/Γ₀ is the same circle. The circles |w| = r are exactly the circles of P for which
  0 and ∞ are inverse points, his "bicentres". Agrees.
- **014, the trace criterion.**
  - If |λ| = 1, then λ̄ = λ⁻¹ and α = 2 Re λ ∈ [−2, 2].
  - Conversely, if α ∈ [−2, 2], the roots of λ² − αλ + 1 have discriminant α² − 4 ≤ 0. So they are complex conjugate
    with product 1, and |λ| = 1.
  - So |λ| ≠ 1 ⇔ α ∉ [−2, 2], and λ = λ⁻¹ ⇔ α = ±2 (parabolic or ±1). This is the elliptic, parabolic and loxodromic
    trichotomy for SL₂(ℂ). His chain on 014 is this argument.
- **A remark on the multiplier.** For u with eigenvalues λ, λ⁻¹ the action on P is w ↦ λ²w, so b = λ^{±2}. Draft 011
  identifies u with "hom. b". Nothing on the pages depends on the square.

### 2.2 The surgery programme (003–007)

**What he defines (003–004).**
1. *Découpage:* cutting a conformal surface (possibly with boundary) along a compact rectifiable sub-1-complex gives a
   conformal surface with boundary and a marked compact piece of boundary.
2. *Recollage:* a conformal surface with boundary, a compact piece of that boundary and an "admissible" equivalence
   relation ("au sens rectifiable") give a conformal surface.
3. *Rétrécissage:*
   - Let X̃ be cut from X along K, with distinguished boundary ∂′X̃, and choose a (multi-)annulus around ∂′X̃. A germ
     of such an annulus round a boundary component amounts to a "rectifiable" Riemannian structure of total length 1 on
     that component.
   - Remove the collar, transport the gluing relation by a rectifiable isomorphism ∂′X̃* ≅ ∂′X̃, and get K ⊂ X*.
4. *Élargissage:* the inverse operation, adding a collar.
5. *Panachage:*
   - Inside a standard tubular neighbourhood, take a circle Γ_i in each component T_i, with Γ_i ≅ (∂′X̃)_i, and remove
     the part of T_i enclosed by Γ_i. His margin calls this a composite, rétrécissage ∘ élargissage or élargissage ∘
     rétrécissage.
   - Then comes "Commutativité" of panachage at different components of K.
6. For an isolated real-analytic loop C, panachage is: remove a real-analytic annulus round C and reglue its two
   boundaries by a cylinder ("relations avec les petits plongements conformes d'une petite couronne…").

**The combinatorics (006).**
- The margin has |x′|, |y′| ≤ √(x′² + y′²) ≤ |x′| + |y′|, and the same for the integrals. So a curve is rectifiable iff
  its coordinates have bounded variation. Agrees.
- The display has K̃ = lim_{x ∈ K∖L} K_x with K_x = {y ≤ x}, a map π: K̃ → K with L̃ = π⁻¹(L), and
  "|K̃| − |L̃| ≅ |K| − |L|, (K̃, L̃) est pur". This is the cut complex: the closed cells off L, glued only along cells off
  L. Away from L nothing changes, and every point of L̃ lies in the closure of K̃ ∖ L̃. This is my reading of an
  unexplained display; it is consistent.

### 2.3 The letter to Lipman Bers, 15 April 1984 (016–021, carbon 022)

**What it asks.** I read it in full; this is my summary.
- With Yves Ladegaillerie he runs a microseminar on Teichmüller spaces and groups.
- **Two spaces.**
  - The algebraic Teichmüller space M̃_{g,ν} ≅ ℂ^d, with d = 3g − 3 + ν: curves with ν points and a "Teichmüller
    rigidification".
  - Thurston's space M̃B_{g,ν} ≅ ℂ^d × (ℝ*₊)^ν of compact bordered conformal surfaces. The extra factor is the boundary
    lengths ℓ_i for the canonical hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary.
- **The bridge.** Both sit in a space of surfaces with "generalized boundary", whose components may be points
  (ℓ_i = 0). It is a manifold with corners in which M̃ is a face.
- **The hope.**
  - An intrinsic retraction M̃B → M̃.
  - An action of the semigroup (ℝ⁺)^I, free on the open part, with fibres ≅ (ℝ⁺)^I.
  - In pants coordinates it would multiply the ℓ_i, "but it is not clear that this operation is intrinsic".
- **The retraction by surgery.** Fill each boundary circle by the cone on it, made conformal through its Riemannian
  structure. This gives a closed surface with ν points and a system of discs.
- **Admissibility.** A system is "admissible" if it comes from surgery. Intrinsically: the two circle actions on each
  Γ_i coincide, the one from Γ_i as the boundary of D_i and the one from Γ_i as a boundary component of the hyperbolic
  surface X^∧ ∖ ∪D_j°.
- **Radii.** A disc D round s sits canonically in T_s as a Euclidean disc (the shadow). Its "radius" r_i lies in a torsor
  under ℝ*₊.
- **His expectation (019).** Admissible systems are determined by their radii, and the admissible radii are exactly
  r_i < ρ_i for a fixed ρ. He then notes that this matches the earlier action only if all ρ_i = +∞.
- **The orange-slice model (019–020).**
  - Give the sphere the metric for which 0, 1, ∞ are equidistant on a great circle. The three cells Q_i ("orange
    slices") are bounded by three half great circles through the poles P^±.
  - The model says that the admissible systems are exactly the (λ_i Q_i) with 0 ≤ λ_i < 1, which would give
    M̃B ≅ M̃ × [0, 1[^I.
  - He asks "whether the model above is likely to hold, or is definitely false."
- **The last question (021).** How does the hyperbolic metric change when a collar is removed? He asks for an explicit
  formula in the λ_i.

**Working it through.**
1. **d and dimensions.** dim_ℝ TB_{g,ν} = 6g − 6 + 3ν = dim_ℝ (ℂ^{3g−3+ν} × ℝ^ν). In Fenchel–Nielsen terms these are
   the lengths and twists of 3g − 3 + ν interior curves plus ν boundary lengths (from memory). Agrees.
2. **"A homotopy equivalence" is too broad.** Michel Hua's project says so in a footnote, and Pro's C05 gives a witness,
   which I re-derived:
   - On the pants spine, with π₁ = F(a, b), the map φ(a) = ab, φ(b) = b has inverse a ↦ ab⁻¹, b ↦ b.
   - It sends the peripheral class (ab)⁻¹ to (ab²)⁻¹, with abelianization −(1, 2). That is none of ±(1,0), ±(0,1),
     ±(1,1).
   - So the rigidification must preserve the orientation and the peripheral classes (Dehn–Nielsen–Baer, from memory).
     Agrees.
3. **The equidistant metric and the cells.**
   - The metric g_{A₀}, with A₀ = (1, −½; −½, 1), is invariant under z ↦ 1 − z, z ↦ 1/z and z ↦ z̄.
   - So the reflections z ↦ 1 − z̄ and z ↦ 1/z̄ are isometries, and their fixed circles Re z = ½ and |z| = 1 are the
     bisectors.
   - Hence Q₀ = {|z| ≤ 1, Re z ≤ ½}: a lune with angles 2π/3, whose vertices e^{±iπ/3} are his poles. Agrees with Pro's
     C03a and with Michel Hua's project.
4. **The model is false.** The argument is the extremal-length argument of Michel Hua's project, which I re-derived with
   Pro's numbers (C03):
   - Take the discs at 1 and ∞ degenerate (cusps), and ℓ₀ = L. The model forces D₀ = λ₀Q₀ ⊂ Q₀.
   - Q₀ ⊂ {|z + ½|² ≤ 7/4}, since |z|² + Re z + ¼ ≤ 7/4 there, and √7/2 < 4/3 < 7/5 < 3/2. So the annulus
     A = {4/3 < |z + ½| < 7/5} lies in the pants, and its core is homotopic there to ∂D₀.
   - Hence Ext_P(∂D₀) ≤ Ext_A(core) = 2π/log(21/20) ≈ 128.8.
   - The hyperbolic metric has area 2π, and every curve of the class has length ≥ L in it. So Ext_P ≥ L²/2π.
   - For L = 32 this gives 1024/2π ≈ 163.0 > 128.8, a contradiction. Exactly: log(21/20) > 1/21, and 1024/21 > 4π².
   - So **the orange-slice model is false in the form the letter states it (degenerate discs allowed)**: D₀ leaves Q₀
     once ℓ₀ is large.
   - Credit: the argument is Michel Hua's project's; the explicit annulus and pants are Pro's (GPT-6 Astra, ChatGPT Pro
     mode).
5. **Why ρ must be finite.**
   - For any admissible system on (P¹; 0, 1, ∞), D₀ is simply connected in ℂ ∖ {1}. Koebe's quarter theorem then gives
     r(D₀, 0) ≤ 4·dist(0, ∂D₀) ≤ 4 in the coordinate z. The inequality is strict, since the complement of D₀ has
     interior.
   - So the shadows are bounded. The January "Conjecture" of 068–069, a bijection onto *all* systems of Euclidean discs
     in the T_{X,a_i} and hence a principal (ℝ*₊)^I-bundle, cannot hold as stated.
   - Credit: this is a footnote of Michel Hua's project, §VIII, which bounds the shadows for every type by the same
     argument (Schwarz's lemma in genus ≥ 2, Koebe in genus 1). I re-derived it for (0, 3).
   - The letter itself replaces the bijection by r_i < ρ_i (019). This bears on STUDY.md R22-2 (b) and H22-6, which
     treat the January conjecture as open (CORRECTIONS).

## 3. SA22-4: the metric dossier "Métriques canoniques sur surfaces conformes" (024–046)

Read in the order 027, 045, 028, 046, 029–044, after the loose sheets 025–026.

### 3.1 The loose sheets (025–026)

**025: equivalent data on a conformal sphere.**
- The data:
  - a) a compatible pair of an antipodism and an inversion;
  - b) a spherical metric and a great circle;
  - c) a circle with a compatible toric structure, i.e. a fixed-point-free anti-involution and a pair of points
    conjugate by it;
  - d) a compact torus T ⊂ Aut⁺(X) with an orbit of X ∖ X^T;
  - d′) a pair of points {z, z′} with an orbit of the unique compact torus fixing them.
- Re-derived:
  - A round metric is fixed by its antipodal map alone (034: a fixed-point-free anti-involution fixes one normalized
    metric).
  - An inversion commuting with that map is an isometric reflection, and its circle is a great circle.
  - A toric structure on a circle C is a simply transitive circle action by projective maps of C ≅ ℝP¹. It is the
    rotation group about a pair of points symmetric in C, i.e. the poles.
  - With poles 0, ∞, the rotation-invariant round metrics correspond one-to-one to their equators |z| = r, i.e. to the
    orbits.
  - All agree.

**026 (upside down): the lattice scratch.**
- The display:

### 3.2 Hyperbolic surfaces (027, 045)

**The theorem (027).** For X connected, the following are equivalent:
- (i) X is not ℙ¹, 𝔼¹ = ℂ, G_m = ℂ* or an elliptic curve;
- (ii) X̃ ≅ H;
- (iii) every holomorphic map ℂ → X is constant;
- (iv) for X open in ℙ¹ (resp. in ℂ), the complement has ≥ 3 (resp. ≥ 2) points;
- (v) for X = X̂ ∖ S of type (g, ν), 2g + ν ≥ 3, i.e. (g, ν) ∉ {(0,0), (0,1), (0,2), (1,0)}, i.e. χ_c(X) < 0.

This is uniformization and Liouville. The coefficient in (v) is **2** in the ink (on the image a 2, perhaps written over
a 3); with 3 the case (1, 0) would wrongly count as hyperbolic. Agrees.

**The corollaries.**
- **Corollary 1:** an open connected subset of a hyperbolic surface is hyperbolic, by (iii). Agrees.
- **Corollary 2:** the interior of a bordered surface with non-empty boundary is hyperbolic. Agrees: the interior is open
  in the double and misses a whole curve, so it is hyperbolic even when the double is ℙ¹, ℂ, ℂ* or a torus.
- **Corollary 3 (045):** X ⊂ X′ open and connected is hyperbolic except when
  - a) X′ ≅ ℙ¹ (resp. 𝔼¹, G_m) with at most 2 (resp. 1, 0) points removed;

### 3.3 The non-hyperbolic surfaces and their metrics (045, 028, 046, 029–031)

### 3.4 The sphere with a compact group (031–039)

**039: the NB on maps.** The sentence is "On pense notamment au cas où X est associé à une carte, et H est le groupe des

### 3.5 Surfaces with boundary (040–044)

**The equivalence (040).** Bordered conformal surfaces correspond to holomorphic surfaces with an anti-involution:
X ↦ (X^or, σ), with X = X^or/σ. These are Klein surfaces and their complex doubles (Alling–Greenleaf, LNM 219; from
memory).
- X is hyperbolic iff X^or is. For compact X this holds iff χ(X) < 0, since χ(X^or) = 2χ(X).
- For type (g, ν) the condition is 2g − 2 + ν ≥ 1, i.e. 2g + ν ≥ 3. The sign is "−" on the image; with "+" the
  condition would hold for every (g, ν).
- Int X is hyperbolic except when ν = 0 and g ∈ {0, 1}.
- Agrees.

**041.** The canonical metric of X^or descends to X with geodesic boundary, since the boundary is the fixed set of an
isometric involution. The remarks on ℝP² and on ℂ (a σ-invariant distinguished metric must be prescribed) agree.

**042–044: the new cases.** All agree.
- 𝒳 = ℂ: X is a closed half-plane, with a class of metrics modulo constants. Quasi-invariant metrics are invariant under
  automorphisms of finite order, since c(f)ⁿ = 1. Aut(H̄) is the real affine group.
- 𝒳 = ℙ¹ with an inversion: X is a disc, and its σ-invariant round metrics correspond to the points of the open disc.
- Summary: there is a unique distinguished metric except for ℂ and H̄ (a class modulo constants) and for ℙ¹ and the disc
  (families with 3 and 2 real parameters).
- The closed annulus and the Möbius band get their flat metrics, since σ preserves the flat area-1 metric of the torus.

### Working it through

- **The extension of V (084).** For four distinct points the cross-ratio is invariant under the double transpositions,
  so each is induced by a unique Möbius involution.
  - Any Klein subgroup is conjugate to the standard one: take σ = −z with fixed points 0, ∞. A commuting involution
    exchanges 0 and ∞, so it is c/z, and rescaling gives 1/z.
  - The fixed pairs {0, ∞}, {±1}, {±i} are disjoint, so card S = 6. Agrees.
- **The normalizer (084).**
  - z ↦ iz fixes −z and exchanges 1/z and −1/z.
  - (z + 1)/(z − 1) exchanges {0, ∞} and {±1} and preserves {±i}.
  - So Γ → 𝔖₃ (on the three pairs) is onto, and its kernel, the maps preserving each pair, is exactly V
    (z, −z, 1/z, −1/z). Hence |Γ| = 24.
  - Agrees (Pro's O02 says the same).
- **The splittings (085).**
  - The stabilizer of a pair of antipodal faces in the rotation group is S₃: the order-3 rotations about the face axis
    and three half-turns. It meets V trivially, since the vertex half-turns move every face pair. So it is a complement
    to V, and there are four of them, permuted simply transitively by V (H¹(S₃, V) = 0; Pro's O06, after Michel Hua's
    project).
  - **But the stabilizer of a single face ("un tel triplet") is C₃, which is not a splitting.** The sentence must read
    "pour une telle paire de faces antipodiques", as the sentence before it does. This is a slip of wording; Michel
    Hua's project and Pro's O05 correct it the same way. Re-derived.
- **Orientation and √−1 (085).**
  - Γ acts on the octahedron by rotations, so it preserves both orientations. An orientation is therefore extra datum.
  - In standard coordinates, the orientation for which (0, 1, i) is positive is independent of the standard coordinate,
    since two standard coordinates differ by an element of Γ.
  - The faces (0, 1, i) and (0, 1, −i) share the edge {0, 1} with the same order, so they carry opposite orientations.
  - So or(Oct(V)) ≅ {±√−1} canonically, and the margin's "Mieux" (X, V) ≃ (Oc, φ: {±i} ≅ or(Oc)) holds. Both groupoids
    are connected with automorphism group 𝔖₄. Agrees.
- **The Proposition (086).** a) ⇔ b) holds in every characteristic ≠ 2: Γ ≅ 𝔖₄ has a unique normal D₂, and Γ ⊂ Nor(V)
  with |Nor(V)| = 24 gives Γ = Nor(V). a) ⇒ c) is always defined.
  - **c) does not determine a) in characteristic 5.** There i = 2 and S = ℙ¹(F₅), z ↦ z + 1 preserves S, and it conjugates
    V to −z + 2 ∉ V, a different Klein group with the same S. Re-derived. This is the correction of sweep 2, made with
    octa.py for p ≤ 29: five Klein groups per S; Aut(ℙ¹, S) = PGL₂(F₅) ≅ 𝔖₅.
  - The margin Cor in its (X, S) form fails in characteristic 5 for the same reason. Pro's O04 repair is right: keep the
    antipodal pairing, whose even pair-flips recover V.
  - The NB's "6 + 12 + 8" ramification picture holds over ℂ. In characteristic 3 the elements of order 3 are unipotent,
    so the face centres give 4 points, not 8 (Pro's O08). His Proposition is unaffected.
  - "Droite projective [réelle]" (086): the image shows a word like "réelle" (sweep-2 crop). Over an algebraically closed
    field it makes no sense, so it is a slip of the pen (the real elliptic curves are of the same weeks) or a misreading.
    Keep the reading as uncertain and say so in a note.
- **087.** A V-set S of six points with |S^σ| = 2 for each σ and S = ⊔S^σ is the same as a fixed-point-free involution
  with orbits the S^σ. Each σ fixes S^σ and swaps the two points of each other pair, since it commutes with the other
  involutions. Agrees (Pro's O09).
- **The quotient (088).** In the standard model q(z) = z² + z⁻² is V-invariant of degree 4. Its branch fibres {0, ∞},
  {±1}, {±i} lie over ∞, 2, −2. So X/V ≅ Z(J), a projective line with the three branch points labelled by J. Agrees

- **The twisting identity (088, "à expliciter").** It holds formally once X(T) is defined by twisting a fixed model by

  - A canonical model exists: X(I) = ℙ¹ ×^{Γ₀} Isom(I, I₀), with Γ₀ ≅ 𝔖_{I₀} the rotation group of the standard model
    and I₀ its face-pair set. Then I(V(I)) ≅ I.
  - The page's own canonical construction (087) is mostly struck. So the page asserts but does not construct X(I). This
    is my reading; Pro's O11 says the same.
- **The Theorem (089).** Re-derived on objects and on automorphisms.
  - Four labelled points on X up to isomorphism correspond to points of Z(J)* (the cross-ratio line minus three points),

### Working it through

- **The module equivalence (091).**
  - M₊ ∩ M₋ = 0 by 2-regularity.
  - M′ ∩ P = 2P: if 2x ∈ P then 2(σx − x) = 0, so σx = x. Hence m ∩ p = 0, and likewise m ∩ q = 0.
  - Essential surjectivity: for (P, Q, m), let N be the preimage of m in P ⊕ Q, with σ = 1 ⊕ −1. Then N₊ = 2P and
    N₋ = 2Q, and multiplication by 2 identifies (N₊, N₋, m_N) with (P, Q, m).
  - Full faithfulness: a σ-map is determined on M′ = 2M by 2-regularity.
  - Agrees (Pro's E01–E02, after Michel Hua's project's findings register; morphisms of triples as there).
- **The Cor of 091 ("P, Q inv. de rg 1")** needs Spec k connected, e.g. k a domain. For k = ℚ × ℚ and σ = (diag(1, −1),
  1), P has rank (1, 2) (Pro's E03). Re-derived. A refinement; the hypotheses on the sheet are struck and uncertain.
- **The two (π, σ) (093, 100).** For k = ℤ, k₀ = F₂ and k₀^× = 1, so there are exactly two classes: diag(1, −1)
  (m = 0, the split case) and (1 1; 0 −1) (m the diagonal), the regular representation of C₂. This is the classical
  classification of ℤ[C₂]-lattices (trivial, sign, regular: Diederichsen and Reiner; from memory).
- **The proviso of 100 is substantive (Pro's E04–E05).**
  - The non-split classes correspond to k₀^×/im(k^×). Changing the bases of P and Q multiplies the slope of m by a ratio
    of reduced units.
  - For k = ℤ[(1 + √−11)/2]: a PID, with residue field F₄ at 2 since x² − x + 3 is irreducible mod 2, and units ±1
    reducing to 1. This gives three non-split classes, not one.

- **The hexagon (100).** For u = 1 and v = e^{iπ/3}, v̄ = 1 − v, so the reflection in ℝu has σu = u, σv = u − v, matrix
  (1 1; 0 −1). "M est le réseau engendré par l'ens des sommets": the six vertices generate the hexagonal lattice, so the
  reading is "l'ens" (one vertex would give rank 1).
- **The families (093, 099).**
  - The σ-invariant positive forms are x² + ay², and "isogènes" holds by the index-2 inclusion ℤe₊ ⊕ ℤe₋ ⊂
    ℤe₊ + ℤ(e₊ + e₋)/2 (Pro's E07).
  - τ = i√a and τ = (1 + i√a)/2 follow from the orthonormal basis (e₀, e₁/√a).
  - The real points: 2y ∈ ℤ gives two circles; y ∈ ℤ gives one (u − 2v is primitive).
  - Real automorphisms: a lattice isometry commuting with σ preserves ℤe₊, so it is ±1 up to −1 composed with σ, which
    is excluded by orientation. Hence ±1 only, even for the square and hexagonal lattices.

- **Real structures on a given curve (094–095, 097–098).**
  - A det −1 isometry of the plane is a reflection, so real structures exist iff X ≅ X̄ iff j ∈ ℝ. The reading "celles dont
    l'invariant modulaire est réel" was checked on the image as far as "dont l'invariant" (against the footnote of
