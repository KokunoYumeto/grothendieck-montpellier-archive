### SA08-4 (the PD de Rham complex of the simplices), scans 4-12
**What he constructs.** Sullivan's forms DRS_[n] = Q[X_0..X_n, dX_i]/(ΣX_i − 1, ΣdX_i) (scan 5). Over Z the
quotient by the PD ideal generated by ΣX_i − 1 makes no sense, because ΣX_i − 1 is not in a PD ideal (scan 6). Repair:
a PD triple (S, J, t), t ∈ J, and C_DRpd[n](S,J,t) = S{X_i}[dX_i]/(ΣX_i − t, ΣdX_i)_pd (scans 6-7); then the universal
choice S = k{T}, J = k{T}^+, t = T (scan 8), which gives the bigraded simplicial algebra
C^{••}_DRpd*(k) = Γ^•_k Φ_{k*} ⊗ Λ^• Ψ_{k*}, Φ_{k*}([n]) = k^{[n]}, Ψ = Φ/k_* (diagonal), d of bidegree (−1,+1),
d(x^{(n)}) = x^{(n−1)}dx, alternating for the exterior degree, PD on the augmentation ideal. Cochains:
C^{••}_DRpd(X_*,k) = Hom(X_*, C^{••}_DRpd*(k)) (scan 9).

**Re-derived (check events CE001-CE004).**
- Φ_{k*}([n]) = Map([n], k) with restriction by composition, so Φ = cosk_0(k) is contractible; X_i is the indicator
  of vertex i and T = ΣX_i is the constant section 1 (Hua's "la section constante égale à 1"). Γ^p of a contractible
  simplicial module is contractible (Dold-Puppe); 0 → k_* → Φ → Ψ → 0 gives Ψ ≃ K(k,1), and Λ^qΨ ≃ K(k,q) by décalage
  (LΛ^q(M[1]) ≃ LΓ^q(M)[q], Illusie, Complexe cotangent I, ch. I, 4.3.2 (to verify)).
- Weight-n strand: 0 → k_* → C^{n,0} → C^{n−1,1} → … → C^{0,n} → 0 is exact in each simplicial degree (relative PD
  Poincaré lemma: Γ(kT ⊕ V') = k{T} ⊗ Γ(V'), and the Koszul complex of V' is exact in positive weight); the terms with
  p ≥ 1 are contractible, hence acyclic in the topos of simplicial sets (see CE008 for the argument).
- Hence H^{p,q} = H^q(X,k) for p ≥ 0 and 0 for p < 0, also at the endpoint q = n (which is not acyclic): with Z^{n−1}
  the kernel at C^{1,n−1}, coker(Γ(C^{1,n−1}) → Γ(C^{0,n})) = H^1(X, Z^{n−1}) = H^n(X,k) by dimension shifting. This
  settles the endpoint obligation of the Pro notes L013.
- Module structure: multiplication by T^{(a)} maps the resolution of weight n to that of weight n+a over the map
  k → k, 1 ↦ T^{(a)}T^{(n)} = C(n+a,a)T^{(n+a)}; so on H'^{n,q} = H^q(X,k) it is multiplication by C(n+a,a), and
  H'^{•,q} ≅ τ_q(H^q(X,k) ⊗_k k{T}). The product covers C(n+m,n): the cup product appears weighted (Pro notes L015).

**Remark a), T = 1 (scan 12): proved for the literal cochains (CE008).** He claims C_DRS(X_*, k ⊗ Q) ≅
C^{••}_DRpd(X_*,k)/(T−1). Michel Hua's project says it is "vrai tel quel" for X_* with finitely many non-degenerate
simplices and was not checked beyond; the Pro notes L019 show it fails for the finite-weight (bigraded direct-sum)
cochains when X_* is infinite. With his own definition, Hom(X_*, C_*), where a cochain may have unbounded weight over
infinitely many simplices, it holds for every simplicial set X_*:
1. k{T}/(T−1) ≅ k ⊗ Q (T^{(m)} ↦ 1/m!; in the quotient m!·T^{(m)} = T^m = 1), so levelwise A^q/(T−1) = DRS^q_*(k ⊗ Q)
   with A^q = ⊕_p C^{p,q}_*; and T − 1 is injective on each A^q_n, a free k{T}-module.
2. The long exact sequence of H^*(X_*, −) for 0 → A^q → A^q → A^q/(T−1) → 0 shows that the comparison map is an
   isomorphism iff T − 1 is injective on H^1(X_*, A^q).
3. H^i(X_*, B) = 0 for i ≥ 1 when B is a simplicial abelian group with π_* = 0: embed B in an injective I; I is
   extendable, hence contractible; I → I/B is surjective with contractible kernel, so a trivial Kan fibration, so every
   X_* → I/B lifts; induct on i. This applies to ⊕_{p≥1} C^{p,q}.
4. So H^1(X_*, A^q) = H^1(X_*, C^{0,q}), and T maps C^{0,q} into ⊕_{p≥1} C^{p,q}; T acts by 0 on H^1, and T − 1 = −1
   is injective.
(Step 3 is for simplicial sets; the semi-simplicial variant is to verify.) The bracketed generalization with (S,J,t),
which carries his "?", holds levelwise by the same computation (t^m = m!·t^{(m)} = 1 forces m! invertible); the global
form needs T − 1 replaced by t − 1 in steps 1-4 and is not checked here.

**Divided powers on shadows (CE022; the same CP^2 test is in the Pro notes of MPL_136_047-094, R062, found there
first).** H^*(CP^2; Z) admits no PD structure (x^2 = 2γ_2(x) is impossible, x^2 being a
generator), so no integral PD-DGA can have it as cohomology ring. Its shadow algebra does: γ_m(x T^{(n)}) =
x^m (T^{(n)})^{(m)} (n ≥ q ≥ 1) satisfies (xT^{(n)})^m = m!·γ_m(xT^{(n)}). The weight parameter absorbs the factorials.
This shows the weighted reading is consistent; descent of the cochain-level PD to cohomology is not proved on the pages
(Pro notes L022) and is not proved here.

### SA08-9 (integration of forms and the cochain axioms), scans 48-72
**Representable cochains (48-52).** Axioms a) cohomology comparison, b) limits; then C^n(X_*) = Hom(X_*, C^n_*)
(scan 48, faded; the withdrawn argument "faux déjà pour M=0", Pro notes R002). Ex 1: C^n(K,M) = Hom(K, u_{n*}M);
Ex 2: normalized cochains.
- Re-derived (CE023): the normalized n-cochains of Δ^m are Λ^{n+1}(k^{[m]}) ⊗ M (non-degenerate n-simplices of Δ^m
  are the (n+1)-subsets of [m]), with coboundary T∧, T = Σe_i; 0 → k → Λ^1Φ → Λ^2Φ → … is exact (Koszul complex of
  the unimodular T). Hence Λ^qΨ ≅ Ker(T∧ on Λ^{q+1}Φ) = Z^q_*, the simplicial module of normalized q-cocycles of the
  simplices, which is the standard model of K(k,q). So C^{0,q}(X) = Hom(X_*, Λ^qΨ) = Z^q(X; k): the weight-zero
  corner of the PD complex is the cocycles. The margin formula of scan 50 with Hom(Δ_n, Δ_p) has the variance reversed
  for the right adjoint u_{n*} (Pro notes R003; ink not re-checked).

**Prop 1, integration (54-56), CE024.** For a semi-simplicial dg group A_* with A_n^p = 0 outside [0,n] and A_n a
resolution of A_0, there is a unique system K_n: A_n^n → A_0 with K_0 = id, K_n = 0 off degree n, and Stokes
K_n(dω) = K_{n−1}(∂ω). Re-derived: K_N(dω^{N−1}) := K_{N−1}(∂ω^{N−1}) is well defined because dω^{N−1} = 0 gives
ω^{N−1} = dα (N ≥ 2) and K_{N−1}(∂dα) = K_{N−1}(d∂α) = K_{N−2}(∂∂α) = 0, or ω^0 = ε_1(α) with ∂ε_1 = 0 (N = 1). The
hypotheses used are exactly H^N(A_N) = 0 and H^{N−1}(A_N) = 0 (N ≥ 2) (his Remarque 1; Pro notes R005). Remarque 3
(integrations commute with all endomorphisms) follows from uniqueness: K∘φ and φ_0∘K are both Stokes systems with
the same K_0. His NB that the K_n do not commute with the simplicial operators is right (Pro notes R006).

**The slice topos and coefficients (58-64).** "l'étude cohomologique de X est celle de ce topos induit" (58; the
display lacks /X, Pro notes R047); cochains with coefficients C^n(X,A) = ∏_{σ∈X_n} A(σ) (60); sheaves and cosheaves
with sums and products (62). Scan 64: for F locally constant, C^*(X_*,F) ≅ Γ(X_*, C^*_{X_*} ⊗ F) and C_X ⊗ F is a
flasque resolution; then "que F soit loc. constant ou non", with his margin "à vérifier ce point".
- Wrong for arbitrary F: the Pro notes R061 give a presheaf on the simplicial circle for which Γ(X, C^q_X ⊗ F) = 0 in
  every degree while H^1(X,F) = k. For locally constant F the statement is the classical one (cochains with local
  coefficients).

**Integration as a cochain map (65-69), CE025.** Degree-0 maps A^*_* → C^*_{*M} are systems K_n: A_n^n → M, and they
form a map of complexes iff K_n∂_{n+1} = K_{n+1}d (Stokes). Re-derived: under a) A_n^i = 0 for i > n, b), c), a Stokes
system is determined by K_0, and K_0 extends iff K_0(∂_1 Ker(d: A_1^0 → A_1^1)) = 0, so A_0/∂_1 Ker(d) is the universal
target; under c') H^0(A_1) = ε^*(A_0) it is A_0 itself. The image lies in the normalized cochains Λ^{*+1}Φ ⊗ M when

in S; "dont il faudrait étudier les propriétés multiplicatives" (open; Pro notes R015).

**The de Rham theorem (70-72).** Data ① a Stokes system, ② a sheaf resolution Ω^* of k_X, ③ a subcomplex S(X)' of
singular simplices computing integral homology on a basis of opens, ④ restriction maps to A_n; then Ω^* → S'^*_X and
the cube of scan 72, whose bottom and right faces are isomorphisms ("Cartan, S_X^* homot^t fin"). This is the
classical Weil-Cartan proof of de Rham's theorem in his integration language; no step is wrong, and the local
hypotheses (Pro notes R016-R018) are the classical ones (paracompactness, homotopically fine singular cochains).

**H3. Why "Non" for cosimplicial sets (scan 91).** In Δ̂ the object Φ = i_{0*}(Z) is coinduced from [0] and receives
Z diagonally. In the cosimplicial topos the right adjoint of evaluation at [0] is the constant functor
(Δ([n],[0]) is a point), so this candidate collapses to Z; the free object on the vertices maps onto Z instead of
receiving it. The structure dualizes. A proof of his "Non" should find a cohomological obstruction to any
universally flasque Φ ⊃ Z there.

**H4. Universal flasqueness implies contractibility.** For a simplicial abelian group, acyclicity on every slice
Δ̂/X probably forces π_* = 0 (test X = K(π_q, q+1) against H^1(X, K(π_q,q)) = H^{q+1}(X, π_q), as in CE003). If so,
scan 100's argument is repaired without "projectif", and the hypothesis of the Prop of scan 98 holds for Δ̂
(Γ^i of a contractible module is contractible, Dold-Puppe). Grounds: CE036, CE008 step 3.

**H5. The product convention is the right one.** Remark a) holds for every X with cochains of unbounded weight
(CE008) and fails with finite weights (Pro notes L019); the cochains of unbounded weight are exactly the duals of the
chain object C_*^DRpd(X) of scans 123-135 (completed series k{{T}}, Pro notes O11). So the "virtual PD de Rham
complexes" of scan 13 should be defined with completed (product) weights, and the shadow theory (scans 20-45) should
be checked in that setting, where the Ext corollary's grading assumption matters (CE018: ungraded Hom(S^+,S) ≠ S).
