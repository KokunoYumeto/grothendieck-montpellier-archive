### SA08-24 · polynomial laws, coalgebras and comodules (scans 144-159)
**What he sets up (144).** k a ring, C_k the k-algebras, the topos Ĉ_k of functors on C_k; O_k the forgetful functor (a
k-Algebra); for a k-module M, V(M)(A) = Hom_k(M, A), an O_k-Module represented by Sym*(M); later W(C)(k') = C ⊗ k'
(153).

**144-147: V is fully faithful; homogeneity.** Hom(V(N), F) = F(Sym* N) (Yoneda), so Hom(V(N), V(M)) = Hom_k(M, Sym* N).
For u = Σ u_i (u_i : M → Sym^i N), u*(λx*) = Σ λ^i x̃*(u_i(m)); u* is homogeneous of degree n iff u_i = 0 for i ≠ n.
- *Checked (re-derivation):* the test he writes, Σ_{i≠n} (T^i − T^n) u_i(x) = 0, is an identity in Sym*(N)[T], i.e. at
  the universal point over k[T] (A = Sym*(N)[T], x* the inclusion, λ = T), not at a point with values in k[T] itself;
  read so, comparing the coefficients of T^i gives u_i(x) = 0. Degree-one maps are the Hom_k(M, N), hence O_k-linear,
  and V is fully faithful. Correct.
- *146-147, many variables:* ∏ V(N_α) = V(⊕ N_α); multidegree n ⟺ u : M → ⊗ Sym^{n_α} N_α; multilinear ⟺
  u : M → ⊗ N_α. Checked for finite I; for infinite I the all-ones multidegree needs an infinite tensor product, which
  the page does not define (the Pro notes N013 flag the support convention).

**148-152: coalgebras.** Bilinear laws on V(M) ⟺ Δ : M → M ⊗ M; unit ⟺ counit M → k; multiplicative augmentation
V(M) → O_k ⟺ e ∈ M with Δe = e ⊗ e (the counit condition ε(e) = 1 is among the "conditions qu'on n'écrit pas").
"lim" on 148 is the inductive limit (V turns colimits into limits, 146); colimits of coalgebras are coalgebras. Gradings
of type D ⟺ V(M) = ∏ V(M_d) ⟺, for D a group, an action of the diagonalizable group Spec k[D]; Δ compatible with the
grading ⟺ multiplication on V(M) compatible ⟺ commuting with the action.
- *Checked:* alternating ⟹ anticommutative (151): x² = y² = (x+y)² = 0 gives xy + yx = 0. The converse fails in
  characteristic 2 (the Pro notes N017: F_2[u], u odd).
- **Wrong (152), his slip:** "l'anti-crochet de deux antidérivations est une antidérivation". *Reading decided on the
  image:* the ink has "antidérivation". The anticommutator DE + ED of two odd derivations is an even derivation (the
  cross terms (−1)^{|a|+1}E(a)D(b) and (−1)^{|a|}E(a)D(b) cancel, and likewise for D(a)E(b)); correct statement:
  "... est une dérivation" (standard; Bourbaki, Algèbre, ch. III, §10 (to verify)). The Pro notes N018 give an explicit
  example. Michel Hua's modern reading of his page 221 (our scan 222) states the correct form for the Euler operator
  dδ + δd ("anticommutateur de deux antidérivations, est une dérivation"); what scan 222 itself says is for Part B.
- *Checked:* an endomorphism of degree γ of M acts in degree −γ on V(M); for u ∈ (s^k)_n, u_C maps C_{m+n} to C_m (157),
  hence his reindexing V(C)^m = V(C_{−m}) (157, and the margin on the typescript scan 160).

**153-159: when a module over the dual algebra comes from a comodule.** For a coalgebra s and a left comodule C, u ∈ s^k
acts by u_C = (id ⊗ u)∘Δ_C, and the coaction is recovered if C ⊗ s → ∏_{u ∈ s^k} C is injective (153).
- *s free (155, 157): checked.* The coordinate functionals of a basis (e_α) make C ⊗ s = C^{(A)} → C^{s^k} injective for
  every C. A module structure comes from a coaction iff each orbit map u ↦ u.x is continuous for the product topology on
  s^k ≅ k^A and the discrete topology on C (a continuous linear map k^A → C factors through a finite projection, so
  u.x = Σ_{α∈F} u(e_α) x_α). Coassociativity and counit then follow from the module axioms, because
  C ⊗ s ⊗ s → Hom(s^k × s^k, C) is injective for s free (the check the Pro notes N022 ask for). The graded form on 159
  ("intégrables", with "loc." struck) is the same statement for s graded and free. These are the modules now called
  rational (Michel Hua's project, footnote: "rationnels (ou discrets)"); over a field, Sweedler, Hopf Algebras (1969),
  ch. II (to verify); over a ring, the α-condition in Brzeziński–Wisbauer, Corings and Comodules (2003), §4 (to verify).
- **Gap (155), corrected:** "Si C est plat ... C ⊗_k k^{(I)} → C^I est-il injectif ? Oui, par passage à la limite
  inductive sur I". The finite-I reduction proves injectivity for the direct sum k^{(I)}, where it is trivial, but the
  map needed is C ⊗ k^I → C^I, from the product (the Pro notes N021). *Re-proof of the needed statement for k
  Noetherian:* if Σ c_k ⊗ r_k (r_k ∈ k^I) maps to 0, every column (r_{k,i})_k lies in the relation module
  Rel = {a ∈ k^m : Σ a_k c_k = 0}, generated by finitely many ρ_1..ρ_s; the equational criterion of flatness gives
  c_k = Σ_l b_{kl} d_l with Σ_k b_{kl} ρ_{t,k} = 0, so Σ c_k ⊗ r_k = Σ_l d_l ⊗ (Σ_k b_{kl} r_k) = 0. For a general ring k,
  injectivity of C ⊗ k^I → C^I is a Mittag-Leffler-type condition (Raynaud–Gruson, Invent. Math. 13 (1971), seconde
  partie, §2 (to verify)), not a consequence of flatness by his argument. His free case (157) needs no flatness and is
  correct.
- *Checked (155):* for s = k[U] (the coordinate coalgebra of G_a), s^k = k{{T}}, and on C = k[U, Y_0..Y_n] the monomials
  T^{(i)}X^{(i_0)}... act by D_U^{(i)} D_{Y_0}^{(i_0)}...; D_f P = 0 when ord f > deg P, so all the operations are
  locally nilpotent, which is why the continuity condition holds there.
