### 76/3-9. "Complexes côniques et ensembles à opérateurs"

**What he does.** An n-complex is a family of finite sets F_0, ..., F_n (the α-faces) with incidence relations I_α ⊂ F_α × F_{α+1}; the order on F_* = ∐F_α is the one generated by incidence. Axioms:
- 1: I_α → F_{α+1} onto (every face has a face one below); 1' (margin, "faudrait-il ?"): I_α → F_α onto.
- 2 (the diamond condition): for x_α and x_{α-2} < x_α there are exactly 2 faces of rank α-1 between them.
- 3: an edge has 2 vertices; 3' (optional): an (n-1)-face lies in exactly 2 n-faces.
- 4: A/G_n^{(α)} → F_α is bijective, i.e. two maximal flags with the same α-face are joined by moves σ_β, β ≠ α.

On the set A of maximal flags he defines the involutions σ_α (change the α-face, keep the others), notes σ_ασ_β = σ_βσ_α for |α-β| ≠ 1, and lets G_n be the group they generate. Prop (76/5): the functor from complexes satisfying the axioms to G_n-sets is fully faithful; "Il faudrait déterminer l'image essentielle". He asks (76/4) whether bijectivity of A/G_n^{(δ)} → A_δ for every type δ follows from Axiom 4.

**What is right.**
- These are, in today's words, the abstract polytopes and their flag graphs. Axiom 2 is the diamond condition, Axiom 4 is flag-connectivity of residues, and G_n is the string Coxeter group with all labels ∞. See McMullen and Schulte, *Abstract Regular Polytopes* (Cambridge UP, 2002), §2A-2B (locator to be checked).
- The reconstruction of F_* from the G_n-set (faces = orbits of G_n^{(α)}; incidence = intersecting orbits) is standard; see Vince, "Combinatorial maps", J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 34 (1983) 1-21 (locator to be checked).
- "Fully faithful" holds once the morphisms of complexes are the rank-preserving order maps that are bijective on every diamond, i.e. those that commute with the σ_α. He does not say which morphisms he takes.

**His question on δ (76/4), answered (our proof, SA04-R76-01).**
- If Axiom 4 holds for F_* and for every lower section F(x), the δ-version follows by induction on |δ|. The flags through a chain with top x_k form a product: (flags of F(x_k) through the rest of the chain) × (flags above x_k). The σ_i with i < k act on the first factor only, and those with i > k on the second only. Axiom 4 at x_k makes the product connected, so each factor is connected; induction does the rest.
- Without heredity I see no reason for the δ-version to follow from Axiom 4 at singletons. The obstruction would be a middle section [x_α, x_β] that is not flag-connected, for instance two polygons (the vertex figure of a pinched sphere inside a 3-face). A global example with Axiom 4 at every singleton was sketched, not verified.
- His NB on 76/4 ("les F(x) satisfont les axiomes 1, 2, 3 et 4") is right for 1, 2, 3, which are local. For Axiom 4 it is not automatic. So the axiom has to be imposed hereditarily ("strongly flag-connected"; McMullen-Schulte §2A, locator to be checked).

**Slips.**
- 76/4: Axiom 2 reads "y_{α-1} ∈ F(x_1)_{α-1}"; it should be F(x_α)_{α-1}.
- The definition of σ_0 cites "cf Axiome 2"; the axiom that makes σ_0 well defined is Axiome 3 (an edge has two vertices).
- Both are minor, and neither was checked on the scan.

**76/7: the manifold criterion is wrong as stated (decided on the scan, SA04-K76-01).**
- The ink, crop at 200 dpi: "pour que (F_*) soit une variété top. de dim n, il f. et s. que (F_*) satisfasse 1,1',2,2',3,3',4, et que ∀ x ∈ F_α (α ≥ 3), la réalisation géom. de F(x) soit une sphère". F(x) is the lower section, the set of y < x.
- *Sufficiency fails.* Take the suspension of a triangulated torus with its face poset. Every section is flag-connected, the poset is thin with 3', and every |F(x)| is the boundary of a simplex. Yet the link of a suspension point is a torus, so the space is not a 3-manifold.
- *For topological manifolds, necessity fails too, in high dimension.* Take K, a triangulation of the suspension ΣP of Poincaré's homology 3-sphere, and add two top cells x, x' with F(x) = F(x') = K. The axioms hold, and |F_*| = Σ²P ≅ S^5 by the double suspension theorem of Edwards and Cannon. But |F(x)| = ΣP is not a sphere.
- The correct statement for the triangulation by the order complex: |F_*| is a PL n-manifold iff for every x of rank α, |F(x)| is a PL (α-1)-sphere and the realization of the upper section {y > x} is a PL (n-α-1)-sphere. The link of the barycentre of x is the join of the two, and a join is a PL sphere iff both factors are.
- The condition on |F(x)| alone says that F_* is the face poset of a regular CW complex: Björner, "Posets, regular CW complexes and Bruhat order", European J. Combin. 5 (1984) 7-16, Prop. 3.1 (locator to be checked).
- His bracket on recognizing spheres ("si on a l'hyp. de Poincaré, il suffit ... simplement connexe ... l'homologie d'une sphère") is right for the sphere question itself (generalized Poincaré conjecture, now proved in all dimensions).

**76/6.** A diagram of extensions Aut(X, id_∂X) → Aut(X) → Aut(∂X), with Z^I (I = π_0(∂X)) and groups SA(X), ST(X). These look like the boundary-twist extensions of mapping class groups of a surface with boundary. Read, not checked; the objects are not defined on the page.

**76/7-10. Combinatorial manifolds and conical maps.**
- "n-variété combinatoire finie" = a finite set D_n with fixed-point-free involutions σ_0, ..., σ_n, non-consecutive ones commuting. This is the n-dimensional combinatorial map (Vince 1983; Lienhardt's n-G-maps).
- "n-complexe combinatoire" = a cascade D_n → ... → D_0 of G_{i-1}-sets.
- 76/9: the skeleta δ_{i-1} = (D_n/⟨σ_n, ..., σ_{i+1}⟩; σ_0, ..., σ_{i-1}). The σ_j with j ≤ i-1 commute with σ_{i+1}, ..., σ_n, so they descend to the quotient (checked). Freeness on the quotient is not automatic, and the page does not claim it.
- 76/10: a "n-carte conique" is a compact space with a closed filtration whose découpages are finite unions of cones, with an equisingularity condition d). This is the topological counterpart, met again in 76/36.

**76/12-14. Flag types, paracartes.**
- D_{0...i} → D_I is onto when i = max I, and D_σ → D_{σ'} is onto when σ' is a final segment of σ. Both follow from Axiom 1, by extending a chain downwards.
- His question (76/14): is D(..., j-1, j, j+1, ...) → D(..., j-1, j+1, ...) always of degree 2? For the poset of a complex satisfying Axiom 2, yes: the fibre is the set of j-faces between x_{j-1} and x_{j+1}. For an abstract paracarte (a functor) it is an extra axiom.

### 76/16-34. Flag geometries, quasi-simplicial sets, orders on the vertices

**What he does.** A "géométrie de drapeaux" is a poset D whose lower sets D(d) are isomorphic to the posets P^*(I_d) of non-empty subsets of finite sets. "Stricte" (d = Sup I_d) ⇔ D is the poset of simplices of a simplicial complex ("maquette simpliciale"). With a "dimension function" δ: Φ → I it is equivalent to a contravariant functor on P_f^*(I). With compatible total orders on the simplices it is equivalent to a quasi-simplicial set (a Δ-set; injective monotone maps only) satisfying (*): for d ∈ D_m, u ↦ u^*(d) is injective on Hom_Δ(Δ_n, Δ_m).

**What is right.**
- These are the *simplicial posets* (Björner 1984 §3; Stanley, "f-vectors and h-vectors of simplicial posets", J. Pure Appl. Algebra 71 (1991) 319-331; locators to be checked).
- Strict ⇔ simplicial complex ⇔ card D(σ) ≤ 1: right.
- (*) says that every simplex has pairwise distinct faces, so its closed simplex embeds.

**76/22-23: transitivity of ≺ under ②bis (SA04-R76-02, targeted proof check).**
- The argument is right in his convention, in which δ_0, δ_1 on D_1 give the *first* and *second* vertex and δ_i on D_2 deletes vertex i.
- In the standard convention (δ_i deletes vertex i at every level), ②bis reads (δ_2, δ_0): D_2 → (D_1, δ_0) ×_{D_0} (D_1, δ_1) onto, with x ≺ y iff x = δ_1(z), y = δ_0(z). His identities "δ_0δ_1 = δ_0δ_2" and "δ_1δ_1 = δ_1δ_0" are false in the standard convention and true in his. Recorded as a convention note, SA04-K76-02.
- Antisymmetry from ① (an edge has two distinct vertices): right.
- His conclusion that the total orders of the I_d are induced by the order of D_0: right.
- *Sharpening.* ②bis (every composable pair of edges is filled by a 2-simplex) is sufficient, not necessary. The total orders of the simplices are induced by one order on D_0 iff the directed graph of oriented edges has no directed cycle; then take the transitive closure.

**76/24-26.** The two directions of the correspondence (Δ-sets with ① and ② ↔ posets with ⓐ and an order on D_0 with ⓑ, ⓒ) and the recovery of ≺ as ≺_*.
- On 76/25, "y_0 = δ_0(z_1)" is a slip for δ_1(z_1).
- Rmq: the opposite order gives another Δ-structure with canonically homeomorphic realization. Right: both realize the poset D_*.
- Ex 1: flags of an ordered set give the order complex, and ⓓ (d = Sup I_d) characterizes simplicial complexes. Right.

**76/27-29: Ex 2 and its Lemma (SA04-R76-03, independent re-proof).**
- *Data.* A group G with σ_0, ..., σ_n, where σ_i and σ_j commute when j ≥ i+2, a G-set X, and D(H) = X/N_H with N_H = ⟨σ_i : i ∉ H⟩.
- *Lemma.* If H ∩ H' ≠ ∅ is a final segment of H and an initial segment of H', then D(H ∪ H') → D(H) ×_{D(H∩H')} D(H') is onto.
- *His proof.* Put K = ∁H and K' = ∁H', and let i be the last element of H ∩ H'. Then K ∪ K' = (K ∪ K') ∩ [0, i-1] ⊔ (K ∪ K') ∩ [i+1, n]. The first part lies in K' and the second in K, and their generators commute, so G_{K∪K'} = G_K·G_{K'}.
- *Check.* I re-derived both inclusions: [i+1, n] ⊂ K because i is the last element of H; and j < i, j ∉ H ∩ H' forces j ∉ H' because H ∩ H' is an initial segment of H'. I also did the lifting: y = bax with b ∈ G_{K'}, a ∈ G_K, and z = ax lifts the pair.
- The proof is complete. σ_i² = 1 is not used. The lemma gives ②bis for the flag geometries of G_n-sets.

**76/30-34.** Programme: conditions for ②bis, and reconstruction from the cell structure. Presheaves on P_f(N): Θ(D) and its quasi-simplicial structure. All of it extends to Dr(N), the flags of an ordered set N. Read; definitions only.

### 76/36-43. "Cartes cellulaires n-dim et groupes cartographiques"

**Definitions.** n-admissible filtrations are defined by induction: X_i is obtained from X_{i-1} by attaching closed i-balls by a finite map whose pull-back filtration of the boundary is (i-1)-admissible and étale on strata. The category F_n has the finite, filtration-compatible, strata-étale maps; F_n^∘ is the subcategory of n-manifolds. **The n-cartographic group G_n** = ⟨σ_0, ..., σ_n | σ_i², (σ_iσ_j)² for j ≥ i+2⟩. For n = 2 this is the cartographic group C_2 of the *Esquisse d'un programme* (1984), §3 (published in Schneps-Lochak eds., *Geometric Galois Actions 1*, LMS LN 242, CUP 1997; locator to be checked). The functor E_n is the flags, by induction through ∂X̃_n.

**Th. 1 (76/39).** Fully faithful on the isotopy category, "dont l'image essentielle est formée des G_n-ens. tels que les σ_i y opèrent librement".
- For n ≤ 2 the essential-image claim is the classical fact that every finite G_2-set with free σ_i is the flag set of a map on a closed surface.
- For n ≥ 3 it is false as stated (SA04-K76-03). A G_3-set with free σ_i realizes a 3-pseudomanifold. Its 3-cells are bounded by the surfaces of the ⟨σ_0, σ_1, σ_2⟩-orbits, and these are spheres only when V - E + F = 2 on each orbit. The vertex links (⟨σ_1, σ_2, σ_3⟩-orbits) satisfy the same condition.
- He sees this himself on 76/43: "On est amené à étudier si un espace triangulé est homéom. à une sphère --- celà semble lié à l'hyp. de Poincaré !".
- *Hunch.* For n = 3 the image of F_3^∘ is decidable by these Euler characteristics, as in crystallization theory (Ferri, Gagliardi, Grasselli; locator to be checked). For n = 4 it needs 3-sphere recognition, which is decidable (Rubinstein 1995; Thompson 1994; locators to be checked). For large n the recognition of spheres is undecidable (Novikov), so a combinatorial characterization of the image cannot be effective in general.

**Th. 2, Th. 3, ψ_n (76/39-42).**
- Th. 2: cascades of G_{i-1}-sets, fully faithful.
- Th. 3: R_i(X_n) ≅ E_n(X_n)/⟨σ_n, ..., σ_{i+1}⟩, with the transition maps passing to the quotient. This is consistent with 76/9.
- ψ_n: the inverse construction by "multicônes".
- The programme Th. 4-7: adjunction, barycentric subdivision (the second subdivision triangulates), essential image, duality, products, fibrations.
- Stated, not proved on these pages.

### 76/50-63. The combinatorial cube C(B, σ)

**What he does.**
- The "maquette" of the cube is (B, σ): a set B with a fixed-point-free involution σ, I = B/σ, n = card I. C = {φ ∈ E : |φ(b)| ≤ 1 ∀ b}, where E is the space of odd functions on B.
- Prop: the faces F(I') ↔ the partial sections I' (I' ∩ σI' = ∅), order-reversing. The partial sections of a surjection determine it (fully faithful on the groupoid of surjections).
- Cor: Isom((B,σ), (B',σ')) ≅ Isom_aff(C, C') ≅ Isom_ord(Comb C, Comb C'). Faces of cubes are cubes.
- Th: Aut(C) is simply transitive on the flags ("repères"), via Lemma: an automorphism of a convex polytope fixing a flag is the identity. Drap(C) is a torsor; n-cubes ≃ G_0-torsors, G_0 ≃ {±1}^n ⋊ S_n.
- Antipodism φ ↦ -φ; centre {1, σ}; c_d = C(n,d) 2^{n-d}; Euler's relation.
- Orientation: σ acts by (-1)^n, so Aut(C) ≅ Aut^+(C) × {±1} for n odd. The "thème de méditation": the orientation of E is recovered from Comb(C) as the two classes of flags exchanged by every σ_i.
- Regular and homogeneous polytopes: card Rep(C) = d_0 ⋯ d_{n-1}; N | N_c | R.
- Conjugacy classes of Aut(C_n): from the orbits of u_I and the "tordu / non tordu" cycles. Fixed faces: 3^{Σα_i^+}, with generating function by codimension ∏(1 + 2t^i)^{α_i^+}.
- The ten classes for n = 3. Prop: ux = ±x for all vertices ⇒ u = ±1 (n ≥ 3). Cor: oriented 3-cubes ≃ 4-element sets.
- Skew cubes ("cubes gauches", the hemicubes): ∂C/σ ≅ P(E) with a cell decomposition whose cells meet in a cell or in two disjoint cells. His question: is Aut(C)/σ → Aut_proj(C̃) → Aut_ord(Φ(C̃)) onto? (n even: "à examiner").

**Checks.**
- SA04-R76-05, independent re-proof.
  - The flag count 2^n·n·(n-1)⋯1 = 2^n n!: a d-face lies in n - d faces of dimension d+1.
  - The Lemma's induction: an affine automorphism of finite order fixing a hyperplane pointwise is the identity or an affine reflection, and a reflection exchanges the sides of the facet's hyperplane.
  - Euler: Σ(-1)^d C(n,d) 2^{n-d} = (2-1)^n = 1, and for every convex polytope Σ_{d=0}^n (-1)^d f_d = 1.
  - The centre {±1}; σ acting on orientations by (-1)^n.
  - N | N_c because Aut(C) ⊂ Aut(Fac C); N_c | R because Aut(Fac C) acts freely on the flags.
  - In the "thème", the two classes D^± exist and are unique because the flag graph is connected and bipartite. Indices: the σ_i that move a flag of C are σ_0, ..., σ_{n-1}, not "1 ≤ i ≤ n".
- SA04-R76-06, computation (script `cube_classes.py` in my scratchpad, brute force over the hyperoctahedral group B_3, 48 elements).
  - There are 10 conjugacy classes, with the sizes and determinant signs in his margins: (3+) 8 +, (3-) 8 -, (2+,1+) 6 -, (2+,1-) 6 +, (2-,1+) 6 +, (2-,1-) 6 -, (1+1+1+) 1 +, (1+1+1-) 3 -, (1+1-1-) 3 +, (1-1-1-) 1 -.
  - The fixed-face polynomials ∏(1+2t^i)^{α_i^+} agree with a direct count of the stable faces for every class.
  - The Prop ux = ±x ⇒ u = ±1 holds for n = 3, 4 and fails for n = 2 (the swap of coordinates), as his "si n ≥ 3" says.

- SA04-R76-07, independent re-proof. **His open question on the hemicube (76/63) is answered: the composite is an isomorphism for every n ≥ 3, n even included.**
  - The hemi-n-cube poset Φ(C̃) is thin (diamond condition) and strongly flag-connected for n ≥ 3. An automorphism fixing one flag fixes every adjacent flag, so Aut_ord acts freely on the 2^{n-1} n! flags.

  - Hence Aut(C)/σ → Aut_ord(Φ(C̃)) is bijective. His orientation-cover argument covers only n odd, but this argument needs no orientation. Compare McMullen-Schulte §6C on hemi-cubes (locator to be checked).

### 77/3-6. "Digression quadratique": ten equivalent categories

**What he does.** Over a scheme S he lists ten categories and states "Toutes ces catégories sont canoniquement équivalentes":
- C_1, regular quadratic modules of rank 2;
- C_2, (A, V, ξ) with A finite étale of rank 2, V an invertible A-module and ξ ∈ Γ N_{A/S}(V)^*;
- C_3, (U, T) with U a form of G_m and T a U-torsor;
- C_4, a P^1-bundle with an étale divisor of degree 2;
- C_5, a conic and a non-tangent line in a plane bundle;
- C_6, an affine plane bundle with a non-parabolic conic;
- C_7, a special orthogonal module of rank 3 with a non-isotropic plane;
- C_8, a rank-4 Azumaya algebra with a commutative étale subalgebra of rank 2;
- C_9, a form of PGL_2 with a maximal torus;
- C_10, torsors under (Z/2)·G_m = O(2).

**Read; consistent with the classification by H^1(S, O(2))** (each is a groupoid of O(2)-torsors). The step C_3 ↔ C_10 is the general fact that for a split extension with abelian kernel, G-torsors ↔ (H-torsor, torsor under the twisted kernel), as groupoids. The margin "faux, même sur un corps, p.ex. sur R la forme a(x²+y²) n'est pas isom. à xy" is his own correction of a Zariski-local-triviality claim. It is right: U-torsors for a non-split torus are only étale-locally trivial.

### 79/4-27. §I: the 2-polyhedron SP_M of a free rank-2 module

**What he does.**

- Vertices: S_M = M^*/±1, the primitive vectors mod sign. Faces: F_M = F̃_M/±1 ⊂ P_3(S_M), which is injective when 2 ≠ 0 in Λ. Edges: A_M.
- Every edge lies in exactly two faces.
- With a "jauge" e_M of det M: SF_M, SA_M and SP_M = (S_M, SA_M, SF_M), the faces with x∧y = ±e_M.
- The star of a vertex ẋ is the affine Λ-line L_x = {y : x∧y = e_M}, with contour edges y - z = ±x. Its connected components are L_x/Λ_0 (Λ_0 = image of Z). So SP_M is a genuine 2-polyhedron iff Λ ≅ Z or Z/nZ, n ≥ 3. P_M itself is one iff in addition Λ^* = ±1, i.e. n ∈ {3, 4, 6} or Λ = Z.
