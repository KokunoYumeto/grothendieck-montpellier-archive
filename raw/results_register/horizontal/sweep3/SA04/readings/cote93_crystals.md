## 2. Cote 93, scans 47-54: the Barsotti-Tate programme and the "Rabiots" (SA04-1, SA04-3)

**What he does.** 47 finishes the deformation statement for abelian schemes: D*(A) = Lie(E(A*)) (E the universal vector extension), a lifting of the Hodge filtration Fil^1 ⊂ D*(A)_{S'} is the same as a smooth vector subgroup L of E(A*)_{S'} lifting t_A^∨, and E(A*)_{S'}/L is an abelian scheme whose dual is the wanted A'. The proof of the exponential step is deferred ("sur lequel nous reviendrons"). 48: the comparison D*(A(∞)) ≅ R^1 f_{cris*}(O_{A_cris}) is announced as "le résultat essentiel", and "en postulant un tel isomorphisme" is how one arrives heuristically at D*(G) for any BT group G; Serre-Tate follows by dévissage, with the handwritten "méthode suivie par Messing ds sa thèse". 49 ("Rabiots"): a) the canonical filtration of an F-V-crystal over a perfect ring, Fil^{1(p)} = Ker F = Im V; b) isogroups of BT, defined by Hom ⊗ Q ("Définition provisoire; elle a l'inconvénient de ne pas fournir un champ"); Proposition (proved later): for a nilpotent immersion S → S' whose ideal is killed by a power of p, IsoBT(S') → IsoBT(S) is an equivalence; Problem 4: the essential image of IsoBT → IsoF-V-cris, a valuative criterion, a Zariski-open subset of the Grassmannian. 51: Problems 5 and 6, the functor from (Dieudonné module, two-step filtration of M ⊗ L) to Galois representations, and its relative form toward constant twisted p-adic sheaves on Spec(A[1/p]). 53-54: Tate's theorem (Driebergen): for S normal noetherian integral with function field of characteristic 0, BT(S) → BT(η) is fully faithful; the questions a) characterize the Galois modules obtained (Hodge-Tate is necessary), b) find an explicit functor to filtered F-crystals; the "foncteur mystérieux" should be fully faithful with a quasi-inverse on a full subcategory "sans doute contenue dans celle des modules ... de Hodge-Tate"; the conjecture that characteristic 0 is unnecessary in Tate's theorem, with a route through the problem of full faithfulness of Cris loc lib(S) → Cris loc lib(η) in characteristic p, reduced "facilement" to k[[t]].

**Checked.** Nothing on these pages is a proof; they are a programme. What I checked is that the statements are right as written, against what is known now:
- the Proposition of 49 is right: quasi-isogenies lift uniquely along nilpotent thickenings on which p is nilpotent (Drinfeld's rigidity, N. Katz, "Serre-Tate local moduli", LNM 868, 1981, Lemma 1.1.3, locator to be checked), and BT groups lift (L. Illusie, "Déformations de groupes de Barsotti-Tate", Astérisque 127, 1985, Thm 4.4, locator to be checked);
- the conjecture of 53 was proved: A. J. de Jong, "Homomorphisms of Barsotti-Tate groups and crystals in positive characteristic", Invent. Math. 134 (1998), 301-333 (also the Pro notes' BT62);
- the "foncteur mystérieux" is Fontaine's D_cris: J.-M. Fontaine, "Sur certains types de représentations p-adiques du groupe de Galois d'un corps local; construction d'un anneau de Barsotti-Tate", Ann. of Math. 115 (1982), 529-577; its essential image is the weakly admissible modules (P. Colmez, J.-M. Fontaine, "Construction des représentations p-adiques semi-stables", Invent. Math. 140 (2000), 1-43), and BT groups correspond to the crystalline representations with Hodge-Tate weights in {0, 1} (M. Kisin, "Crystalline representations and F-crystals", Progr. Math. 253, 2006, 459-496). His guesses on 53 (full faithfulness, a quasi-inverse on a subcategory of Hodge-Tate modules) are what was proved.

## 3. Cote 93, scans 55-66: the Euler characteristic (SA04-4)

**What he does.** 56: for a field k, E_χ! (resp. E_χ) is the class of locally compact spaces with H*_!(X, k) (resp. H*(X, k)) finite-dimensional, singular cohomology in the margin. Axioms: (a) compact ⇒ χ_! = χ; (b) Y closed in X: two of X, Y, X − Y in E_χ! give the third, χ_!(X) = χ_!(Y) + χ_!(X − Y); (c) closed Mayer-Vietoris for χ, with the bracket "Même X, X' ∩ X'' ∈ E_χ ⇒ X', X'' ∈ E_χ"; (d) homotopy domination; (e) points. 58 ("Conclusions premières"): (1) disjoint sums; (2) χ(R^n) = χ(B^n) = 1, χ_!(R^n) = (−1)^n, χ(S^n) = 1 + (−1)^n; (3) a finite filtration with strata f_i × R^i gives χ_! = Σ(−1)^i f_i. 60: (f) Künneth; (g) for a locally trivial fibration f: X → Y with fibre F, χ_!(X) = χ_!(Y)χ_!(F) and χ(X) = χ(Y)χ(F) "cum léger grain de sel", proved by the Leray spectral sequence with compact supports modulo the lemma χ_!(Y, R) = b·χ_!(Y) for a local system R of rank b ("Il faut prouver"), which he proves for spaces with a finite cell filtration. 62: the cover condition (g_2); the homological lemma "pourvu qu'on fasse l'hyp. que Y est ∼ Y' satisfaisant (g_2)"; (h): C_{-1} = {∅}, C_n = compact spaces every point of which has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to the cone over a compact S ∈ E_{n−1} with vertex at the point, C_∞ = ∪ C_n, E_∞ = locally compact separated X with X̂ (one-point compactification) in C_∞; the margin lists E_0 to E_4 (E_4 "(Moïse)", "S'ils sont cpts, il n'est pas clair qu'ils sont triangulables"); Prop C_∞ ⊂ E_χ ∩ E_χ!, with a proof for X ∈ E_∞. 64: X' = X̂ − T̊ for a half cone T at ∞, "On vérifie" (margin "vérifier") X' ∈ C_∞ and X' ∼ X, and the formula χ(X) − χ_!(X) = χ(S_ω), "la structure locale de X à l'infini". 66: the theorem on E_∞, two functions χ_!, χ with (a)-(h), (g) "tout au moins si Y est un polyèdre relatif", with the margin "j'ignore si c'est vrai sans telle restriction", uniqueness on relative polyhedra with the Euler formula, and the margin "j'ignore si χ et χ_! sont caractérisés par a) à e) sur tout E_∞".

**Proved on the pages, and re-derived (SA04-R93-02, -05, -06, -10).** (a)-(e) follow from the exact sequences; the bracket of (c) holds because H^i(X') ⊕ H^i(X'') sits in an exact sequence between H^i(X) and H^i(X' ∩ X''), so two of the four suffice when they are X and X' ∩ X''. (2) and (3) are right. (g) is right once the E_2 term is finite, and the finite-cell lemma is right. 64 is right: X̂ = X' ∪ T with X' ∩ T = ∂T ≅ S_ω gives χ(X') = χ(X̂) − 1 + χ(S_ω), and χ_!(X) = χ(X̂) − 1 by (b) and (a); for R^n this is 1 − (−1)^n = χ(S^{n−1}). One qualification: with singular cohomology the closed Mayer-Vietoris sequence of (c) needs an excisive couple; with sheaf cohomology it always holds, and the two agree on the locally contractible spaces of E_∞.

**His two open questions on 66 are answered on E_∞ (SA04-R93-11, -12).**
1. *(g) without the polyhedral restriction.* Let Y, F ∈ E_∞ and L a local system of rank b on Y. A compact space of C_∞ is metrizable (Smirnov), locally the open cone over a compact ANR, hence an ANR (O. Hanner, "Some theorems on absolute neighborhood retracts", Ark. Mat. 1 (1951), 389-408), hence homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex (J. E. West, "Mapping Hilbert cube manifolds to ANR's: a solution of a conjecture of Borsuk", Ann. of Math. 106 (1977), 1-18). On a finite CW complex χ(W, L) = b·χ(W) by cellular cochains, and twisted cohomology is a homotopy invariant, so χ(K, L) = b·χ(K) for every compact K ∈ C_∞. For non-compact Y, cut at infinity: Y = Y' ⊔ U with Y' = Ŷ − T̊ compact in C_∞ (his 64) and U ≅ S × (0, 1/2) open, L|U pulled back from S; the compact-support sequence and Künneth give χ_!(Y, L) = χ(Y', L) − χ(S, L|S) = b(χ(Y') − χ(S)) = b·χ_!(Y). The Leray spectral sequence H^p_!(Y, R^q f_! k) ⇒ H^{p+q}_!(X), with R^q f_! k locally constant of fibre H^q_!(F), then gives χ_!(X) = χ_!(Y)χ_!(F); the homology Serre spectral sequence (f is a Hurewicz fibration over the metrizable Y) gives χ(X) = χ(Y)χ(F) in the same way, Y being homotopy equivalent to Y'. So (g) holds for every locally trivial fibration with base and fibre in E_∞. The Pro notes leave exactly this open (EC37); their telescope counterexample (EC21) lies outside E_∞.
2. *Uniqueness on E_∞.* If χ, χ_! satisfy (a)-(e) on E_∞, then for compact X ∈ C_∞ take a finite polyhedron P ∼ X (West, then a simplicial model); P ∈ E_∞, so (d) gives χ(X) = χ(P), which (c), (d), (e) fix by induction on simplices; for any X ∈ E_∞, χ(X) = χ(X̂ − T̊) by (d) and 64, and χ_!(X) = χ(X̂) − 1 by (b), (a), (e). So χ and χ_! are characterized by (a)-(e) on all of E_∞. This is the route of the Pro notes' EC39, which they leave conditional on the reading of the class; the condition is met by the definition as written: for compact S, "S ∈ E_{n−1}" and "S ∈ C_{n−1}" coincide (Ŝ = S ⊔ {pt}), so the reading question EC23 does not affect the mathematics, and E_0 is the class of finite discrete spaces (C_0 = finite discrete, cone over ∅ = a point), which settles EC24.

**Triangulation (SA04-R93-13).** The E_3 bracket "[ils sont triang. dans le cas cpt]" is struck through on the scan (Pro right, K93-08 wrong, SA04-K93-06). His doubt for E_4 is borne out: Freedman's E8 manifold is a compact topological 4-manifold, locally conical, not triangulable (M. H. Freedman, "The topology of four-dimensional manifolds", J. Differential Geom. 17 (1982), 357-453; non-triangulability by Casson's invariant, S. Akbulut, J. McCarthy, *Casson's invariant for oriented homology 3-spheres*, Princeton Math. Notes 36, 1990, locator to be checked); in every dimension ≥ 5 there are non-triangulable compact manifolds (C. Manolescu, "Pin(2)-equivariant Seiberg-Witten Floer homology and the triangulation conjecture", J. Amer. Math. Soc. 29 (2016), 147-176). So C_n for n ≥ 4 contains non-triangulable compacta, and the uniqueness above has to go through homotopy models, as it does.

## 4. Cote 93, scans 72-84: the Schwarzian and the "écarts" (SA04-7, SA04-8, SA04-6)

**What he does.** 72: Th. I: on a Riemann surface X, a morphism G: E_X × E_X → ω^{⊗2} (E_X the étale maps to the affine line, P_X to P^1) which is a differential operator in f, g, a cocycle (b), invariant under g ↦ ag, g + b, 1/g (c), rigid (d) and surjective in g (e), unique up to a unit; II: functorial in X under étale maps, unique up to c ∈ C*; the torsor T_X under ω^{⊗2} with c: H\P_X ⥲ T_X (projective structures). 73: G(f, g) = [(−2g'''/g' + 3(g''/g')^2) − (same for f)] dt^2; III: over a base S with Hasse derivatives, uniqueness up to ±1, and G_0 = G/12 because G vanishes identically in characteristics 2 and 3; the normalization G_0(t, t^2) = dt^2/(2t), and G_0(t, t^3) = (2/3) dt^2/t^2. 74: Remarks: ω^{⊗2} is universal; torsors for different G are multiples (T^G is the twelfth multiple of T^{G_0}); in characteristic p, a), b), c) and non-vanishing characterize G_0 up to F_p^*, with the margin "faux? pour un op. diff. d'ordre ≤ 3". 75-78, 84: the analogues for G_a (dg − df), G_m (dg/g − df/f), Aff(1) (D(f, g) = (g''/g' − f''/f') dt, the connections on ω), the affine group of G_m acting on G_m ((dg/g)^2 : (df/f)^2). 80, 82: n-differentials: for ω = f dt^n, c_n(ω) = c_t + [−(2/n) f''/f + ((2n+1)/n^2)(f'/f)^2] dt^2, the cocycles G_{m,n}, and the diagram of torsors c_a, c_{a,n}, c_af, c_pr with the maps α_n, β_n, γ, δ. 81: uniformization diagrams with question marks on real-analyticity; E = Ω^{−1} ⊕ Ω^0 and g ⊗ Ω^1. 83: X = ΩY, the first k-invariant k_1 ∈ H^3(π_1, π_2), "=? 0", and its desuspensions.

**Checked (SA04-R93-14 to -17).**
- G = −2(S(g) − S(f)) dt^2 with S the Schwarzian; (b) and (c) hold, and the chain rule makes G a quadratic differential independent of t. In Hasse derivatives, −D^{(3)}g/D^{(1)}g + (D^{(2)}g/D^{(1)}g)^2 = (1/12)(−2g'''/g' + 3(g''/g')^2): G = 12 G_0 holds.
- The test value is wrong in the ink, not misread: the scan (300 dpi) shows "1/(2t) dt^2" after a heavily cancelled first value. With f = t, g = t^2: D^{(1)}g = 2t, D^{(2)}g = 1, D^{(3)}g = 0, so G_0(t, t^2) = dt^2/(4t^2). This confirms SA04-C1 (sweep 1; Michel Hua's project, VI) on the ink. His second value G_0(t, t^3) = (2/3) dt^2/t^2 is right.
- 80/82: γβ_n(f dt^n) = c_pr(t) + (2(−f'/(nf))' + (f'/(nf))^2) dt^2 = c_pr(t) + [−(2/n) f''/f + ((2n+1)/n^2)(f'/f)^2] dt^2, his c_n; c_{nr}(ω^r) = c_n(ω) holds (the (r−1)-terms cancel), so G_{m,n}(ω_m, ω_n) = G_{m,nr}(ω_m, ω_n^r) is right; G_{m,n}(ω_m, (ds)^n) = G_{m,0}(ω_m, s) is right. The margin δ(ω_2) = c_pr + [f − f''/f + (5/4)(f'/f)^2] dt^2 and δα'_2(ω_1) = c_pr + [f^2 − 2f''/f + 3(f'/f)^2] dt^2 are consistent (δ(ω_1^2) = δα'_2(ω_1)).
- Two slips: in G_{m,n} on 80 the f-part carries (g'/g)^2 for (f'/f)^2; the third line of γβ_n on 82 is the case n = 1 written as if general (SA04-K93-10).

**Open.** 74 margin: whether G_0 is unique up to F_p^* among operators of all orders in characteristic p. In characteristic 0 the Schwarzian is the unique projective invariant (V. Ovsienko, S. Tabachnikov, *Projective differential geometry old and new*, CUP 2005, ch. 1, locator to be checked); in characteristic p, Hasse derivatives of order p^k may give further invariants, which is what his "faux?" suspects (hunch H1-4, package P1-2). 83 is carried from the Pro notes (EC47-EC54: a nonzero class after one delooping, vanishing after two by Arlettaz's theorem), not re-derived.

**Checked (SA04-R1615-02, -03).**

In c) on 24 the text has T_DR(M) = T_cr(M) ⊗_K L; it must be ⊗_L K (SA04-K1615-01; not decided on the scan).

Inflation-restriction makes the kernel of H^1(Γ, G_m(S)) → H^1(π, G_m(S)) equal to H^1(Γ/π, L^*) = L^*/{σ(x)/x}, and the valuation maps it onto Z (v(σ(x)/x) = 0, v(p) = 1). The correct statement fixes the slope data, which in Fontaine's theory is the condition of weak admissibility (Colmez-Fontaine 2000).

## 8. How the stretch connects

- 93/53 ("Le théorème de Tate suggère ... foncteur mystérieux") and 161-5/23-29 are one programme: 161-5 is the Tannakian, torsor-theoretic attempt at the functor whose problems are posed on 93/49-54, after Serre's 1968-69 course (161-5/18-22) and, by its marginal note, rechecked after Sen (1973).
