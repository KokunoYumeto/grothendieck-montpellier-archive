## Cote 69: the 27 lines of the cubic surface as a graph, and E6 (SA04-20)

Scans 3-120 (107 transcribed; 41, 65, 66, 73, 77, 89, 94, 95, 100, 105 and 107 are blank sheets, viewed at 14 dpi; 81 and 92 have no text). Seven Codex-web folders. His own pagination runs p.1-26 (scans 3-34, with "bis/ter" leaves), then a second numbered series p.1-17 (scans 42-58) headed "Les 27 droites de la surface cubique, et le syst. de racines E6", then unnumbered leaves.

The subject is one object: the **cubic graph** C, the graph on the 27 lines of a smooth cubic surface with "liés" meaning "meet". He characterizes it by graph axioms, describes it by torsors after pinning parts of it, counts every configuration in it against |W(E6)| = 51840, rebuilds the E6 root system and Weyl group from the graph alone, and ends with a classification of "minuscule-type" vectors in root systems and with group 3-nets (triples of torsors). Everything here is classical in substance (the 27 lines and W(E6): Coxeter 1940; Manin, *Cubic forms*, ch. IV, §§23-26; Dolgachev, *Classical algebraic geometry*, 2012, ch. 9; GQ(2,4): Payne-Thas, *Finite generalized quadrangles*, 1984, §6.1 and 5.3 — locators to be checked); what is his is the route: axioms on a graph, torsors, "épinglage", and the counting tables.

### 69/3-11 (p.1-9). The characterization theorem

- **Statement (69/3).** A graph S with (a) 27 vertices, (b) every vertex linked to 10 others, (c) any two linked vertices have exactly one common neighbour, is a cubic graph ("Alors (et alors seulement)").
- **Proof.** Fix a triangle T = {u, v, w}. Lemma 1: every other vertex is linked to exactly one vertex of T (the three sets E(u)*, E(v)*, E(w)* of size 8 are disjoint by (c) and fill the 24 remaining vertices). This gives the involution σ_T (x ↦ the third vertex of the triangle on x and its T-neighbour), an automorphism (69/4). Lemmas 3-6: each x ∈ E(u)* meets each σ-pair of E(v)* once; non-linked pairs of E(u)* correspond to non-linked pairs of E(v)* (the partitions Π22 of the 4-element quotients P_u, P_v, P_w are identified transitively, lemma 6 with the precise form φ_wv φ_vu = σ φ_wu). Lemma 7 (69/8-9): the 16 triangles disjoint from T, modulo σ, inject into P_u × P_v × P_w and form a torsor under Ψ(V) = {x + y + z = 0} ⊂ V^3, V of dimension 2 over F2. Pinning (69/10-11): a triangle (α4, β4, γ4) fixes names α_i, β_i, γ_i, α'_i = σα_i, ...; lemmas 8 and 9 give every link; "S est déterminé ... à isom. près".
- **Checked.** The proof is sound. Re-derived: Lemma 1 (counting), the injectivity in Lemma 7, Lemma 9's last step (the coincidence set of w_v(α1) and w_v(α4) lies in the partition (1,4)(2,3) and contains 4). The question mark of 69/9, "Ψ =? N_u + N_v", is answered yes: N_u + N_v = {(x, y, -x-y)} = Ψ for any abelian V (SA04-R69-04).
- **What it is.** Condition (c) with (b) makes every vertex lie on exactly 5 triangles, and Lemma 1 is the axiom of a generalized quadrangle: points = vertices, lines = triangles, order (2, 4). The theorem is therefore the uniqueness of GQ(2,4) (known; Payne-Thas §6.1, locator to be checked), proved from scratch by pinning. Computed: the 27-lines graph is strongly regular (27, 10, 1, 5) (SA04-R69-01).
