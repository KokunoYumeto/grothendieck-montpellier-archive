## Cote 94: representing a functor on modules (SA04-36)

**What he does.** "Construction des Modules cohérents par leur foncteur représentatif", with applications to prorepresenting a functor G/S along a section and to the invariants Ω^1, g:
- Lemma 1 (94/003): for B faithfully flat over A, F: Mod(A) → Ens is representable (by a module of finite type or finite presentation) iff F is left exact and F_B is.
- Lemma 2: for A noetherian and f ∈ A, F is representable by a module of finite type iff F is left exact, F(M) ≅ lim F(M/f^n M) on Â-modules of finite type, F commutes with filtered colimits, F_f is representable, and every F_{A/f^n} is.
- Proposition 3 (noetherian A, by noetherian induction); Proposition 4 (local A, using injective hulls of the A/m^{n+1}); Lemma 5 and Remark 5 bis (the rank function p ↦ rk F(k(p)) is constructible).
- Lemma 6 and Corollary 7: generic base change along regular sequences; the dualizing module on a dense open (011, 013).
- Corollary 11, and Theorem 12 (016): the representability criterion over a noetherian quotient of a regular ring; Remark 13 (excellent-type rings).
- Theorem 14 (018): for f: X → S proper of finite presentation and F, G of finite presentation with G flat over S, there is a P of finite presentation on S with Hom(P, M) ≅ f_* Hom(F, G ⊗ M), compatible with base change. Lemmas 15-17 give generic base change for Ext and R^j f_*; his margin on Lemma 15: "cette réduction ne sert à rien en fait".
- Remark 18 ("foncteur constructible"); Theorem 19 and Corollary 20 (022-024): for f proper, flat and of finite presentation, and Z affine over X, the Weil restriction Π_{X/S} Z is an affine S-scheme, and closed immersions are preserved.

**Checked:**
- **SA04-R94-01, -02:** Lemmas 1-2 and Proposition 3. Â × A_f is faithfully flat over A; P̂ = lim P_n is of finite type; the induction runs through B/f^n B for f non-nilpotent.
- **SA04-R94-03:** Lemma 6 b), which he calls almost trivial, has one real step. A sequence regular on O_s stays regular on O_s/J when gr_J is free: the last nonzero power J^k is a free O_s/J-module, so a zero-divisor on O_s/J is one on O_s. ## Cote 97: Chern classes of the regular representation, lifting Fuchsian groups, classifying spaces (SA04-37)

**What he does.**
- **97/005:** for G cyclic of order n, the even cohomology is H^{2*}(G, Z) = Z/n[ξ] and c(r_G) = Π_{λ∈Z/n}(1 + λξ_0). For n = p prime: c_p(r_G) = 0 and c_{p−1}(r_G) = ξ_0^{p−1}, "indépendant du choix de ξ_0 !". Hence, for G finite of order divisible by p, H^{Nk}(G)(p) ≠ 0 for all N, with k = 2n'(p−1).
- **97/007-013:** Γ ⊂ PSL_2(R) cocompact and torsion-free, X = D/Γ. The bundle E_0 comes from the standard representation of SL_2(R) if Γ lifts. The class γ_1^X ∈ H^2(X, Z) of the universal-cover extension is the Euler class: γ_1^X = 2 − 2g, by passing to a liftable subgroup of finite index. Its reduction mod 2 is the obstruction to lifting, so "On gagne ! Il existe tjrs un Γ̃ !". Margin: E_0 ⊗ E_0 ≅ T_X ⊕ 2·1_R.
- **97/016:** H^*(B_G, R) for G compact connected, through invariant forms; the theorem Sym(T) ≅ H^*(B_G, R), T the transgressive elements.
- **97/018:** for G complex reductive connected and a prime p, the torsion conditions (i)-(iii), (i bis), (ii bis), with the implications by Hopf, Borel's transgression and the Leray sequence of G/B → B_B → B_G.

**Checked:**
- **SA04-R97-01, decided on the scan.** The ink of 005 reads c_{p−1}(r_G) = (Π_{λ∈F_p^*} λ) ξ_0^{p−1} = ξ_0^{p−1}. By Wilson's theorem Π λ = (p−1)! = −1 in F_p, so c(r_G) = 1 − ξ_0^{p−1} and c_{p−1}(r_G) = −ξ_0^{p−1} for p odd (SA04-K97-01). The non-vanishing and his conclusion on H^{Nk}(G)(p) are unaffected; this is the classical non-vanishing of Chern classes of the regular representation (B. Venkov; M. F. Atiyah, "Characters and cohomology of finite groups", Publ. Math. IHÉS 9 (1961), locator to be checked).
- **SA04-R97-02:** the lifting argument is right. The obstruction lies in H^2(Γ, Z/2) = Z/2 and is γ_1 mod 2 = 2 − 2g mod 2 = 0. E_0 has Euler class 1 − g, a square root of T_X, i.e. a spin structure. χ = χ_0^2 − 2 gives E_0 ⊗ E_0 ≅ T_X ⊕ 2·1.
- **SA04-R97-03:** 018 is the matter of his "Torsion homologique et sections rationnelles" (Séminaire C. Chevalley 1958, *Anneaux de Chow et applications*, exposé 5; locator to be checked), carried without recheck.

## Cote 98: norms, hypersheaves on G-sets, bounded families, base change for Tor and Ext (SA04-17)

**What he does.**
- **"Formalisation des Normes, cf. SGA 4 XVII" (002-014):** the norm of a finite locally free morphism through symmetric powers, with functoriality, additivity and transitivity diagrams (005, 007, 009). Then an axiomatic theory (011-012): a class N of "normed morphisms" stable under identities, composition, base change, sums and quotients of fibre powers by subgroups of S_n; a degree that is multiplicative and additive, with (v bis): the degree of X^n_Y/G is card(E^n/G); norms Cor_{X/Y}: Y → Σ^r(X/Y), subject to transitivity, base change and additivity. Examples: G-sets, finite flat schemes, analytic spaces, topological coverings. Margin: "Expliquer la relation de cet exemple avec le cas général".
- **016-024:** hypersheaves on the category C_G of G-sets with finitely many orbits; the Leray-Čech spectral sequences (A) and (B), H^*(G, A) ⇐ H^p(T/e, H^q(A)); Shapiro, Hochschild-Serre, and fibrations of classifying spaces.
- **"Familles limitées, cf. SGA 6 XIII" (026-044):**
  - a refined Chow lemma: quotients of a fixed L whose cycles have bounded degree form a bounded family, by induction on the generic length;
  - equivalence of sheaves over field extensions and the set Φ(X/k), with a question on composite extensions;
  - constructions T(F_1, ..., F_n) compatible with base change; examples: Hom, ⊗, Tor, Ext, f^*, R^p f_*, primary decompositions;
  - stability of bounded families under ⊗, Hom, Tor and Ext;
  - the lemmas: if Tor_q^{O_Y}(F_1, ..., F_n), resp. Ext^q(F, G), are S-flat for q ≤ N, the base-change maps are isomorphisms for q ≤ N;
  - a plan of Propositions 1-7.

**Checked:**
- **SA04-R98-01:** (v bis) holds for finite locally free morphisms. B^{⊗n} has a basis indexed by E^n and permuted by S_n, so (B^{⊗n})^G is free on the orbit sums, of rank card(E^n/G), and compatible with base change.
- **SA04-R98-02:** the Tor lemma follows from the Künneth spectral sequence Tor_p^A(H_q(K), A') ⇒ H_{p+q}(K ⊗ A'). With H_q(K) flat for q ≤ N, every term with p > 0 in total degree ≤ N vanishes and no differential reaches E_{0,n}.
- **SA04-R98-03:** the Ext lemma holds in degrees ≤ N once the cokernel of K^N → K^{N+1} is flat, which his margin supplies. His "en tout degré" refers to the truncated complex.

## Cote 100: good reduction and p-divisible groups (SA04-38)

**What he does.**
- **Theorem (003).** S locally noetherian, U ⊂ S open, every point of T = S − U of characteristic p. The functor A ↦ (A_U, T_p(A), id) from abelian schemes over S to triples (A_U, M, φ), with M a p-divisible group over S:
  1. is fully faithful when U meets every connected component (e.g. depth along T ≥ 1);
  2. is an equivalence when some finite surjective S' → S has S' geometrically unibranch and the étale depth prof ét_T(S) is ≥ 2 (the profondeur étale of SGA 2 XIV, decided on the scan, SA04-R100-05). A footnote replaces 2 by a simplicial condition on the components of S', S'' and S'''. A boxed earlier form of b) is crossed out.
- **Full faithfulness (004-005, 021).** The locus where an ℓ-adic or p-adic homomorphism is algebraic is a closed subscheme T. At points of characteristic ℓ it contains a neighbourhood ("Cf Inventiones 1.2 (ou démonstration cristalline)"), whence the corollary.
- **Essential surjectivity (007-019).** Five steps:
  1. reduction to S reduced, by Serre-Tate and a formal abelian scheme;
  2. S geometrically unibranch with a Jacobi rigidification;
  3. a level-ℓ rigidification, ℓ ≠ p, 3, then extension and descent;
  4. S reduced with S' → S finite and S' geometrically unibranch;
  5. the general case, with a lemma: a "système local de schémas abéliens" comes from an abelian scheme.
- **Descent (023-027).** Lemma 1: a proper epimorphism S' → S is a descent morphism for maps into formally unramified X. Corollaries 2-3: descent for homomorphisms of abelian schemes and of p-divisible groups. Effectivity is reduced to the complete local case and Raynaud's flat descent criterion for abelian schemes.
- **Algebraization (029-045).** A formal abelian scheme over Spf R is algebraic iff it is algebraic over the normalizations of the one-dimensional integral quotients. The proof goes through the polarization schemes P_n and their countable decomposition P = ∐ P^α. The key Proposition (037-039): for S complete local, countably many closed Z_i whose union contains every x with dim of its closure equal to 1 already cover S with finitely many; the proof is by Baire category in the space m^r of V-homomorphisms V[[T_1, ..., T_r]] → V. Variants for proper flat formal schemes follow (041-045).

**Checked:**
- **SA04-R100-01:** the corollary of 004 (T open and closed, every component meets U, so T = S).
- **SA04-R100-02:** the Baire argument is right: nonzero power series have closed nowhere-dense zero sets in m^r, which is complete. One qualification: V must be a complete discrete valuation ring. In equal characteristic, write k[[T_1, ..., T_r]] = V[[T_2, ..., T_r]] with V = k[[T_1]]; with V the coefficient field, m^r = 0 and the argument gives only the closed point (SA04-K100-01).
- **SA04-R100-03:** Lemma 1 of 023, at the level of its sketch.
- **SA04-R100-04:** the theorem itself is carried as background. It is consistent with his "Un théorème sur les homomorphismes de schémas abéliens", Invent. Math. 2 (1966), 59-78 (cited on the page as "Inventiones 1.2"), and with Serre-Tate. For later published forms of the extension statement see G. Faltings, C.-L. Chai, *Degeneration of abelian varieties* (1990), ch. V (locator to be checked).

## Cote 101: local cohomology, "Notes antiques 1958" (SA04-39)

**What he does.**
- **002-005:** local cohomology against projective and global cohomology through the cones over X = Proj(S): Ext with supports on the punctured cone equals ⊕_n Ext(X; F, G(n)), and flat base change.
- **006-016:** "Dualité locale pour Modules non quasi cohérents". A is complete regular local of dimension n and F any O_X-module; the pairing H^i_x(F) × Ext^{n−i}(X; F, O_X) → H^n_x(O_X) = I is an isomorphism "à peu de choses près". He reduces to F = O_{U,X} (extension by zero) and works out n = 2 with adèles (K → Π K̂_p → Hom(K, I) → 0). Then: "Question : classifier -- -- --" and "Autres questions : Ext^i(K, A) = 0 pour tt i (A anneau local noeth régulier complet, de corps K)".
- **017, "Notes antiques 1958 (Caractérisation des Coh. M., des Gorensteins etc)":**
  - the plan: §1 the functors H^i_C; §2 dualizing functors and modules, local duality, Gorenstein rings;
  - the "module fondamental" (Ω of finite type with D(Ω) ≅ H^n(A), the canonical module);
  - Theorem 4: local duality holds in degrees ≥ k iff H^i(A) = 0 for k ≤ i < n;
  - corollaries: the Cohen-Macaulay case; Ext^i(k, Ω) = 0 for i ≠ n and k for i = n; Hom(Ω, Ω) = A;
  - the ideal N and faithfulness (024-026), with his "Non" to whether H^n(Ω) is always dualizing;
  - Proposition 10 (Ω/xΩ is fundamental for A/xA) and Proposition 11 (localization, for quotients of regular rings, "une démonstration directe ... semble impraticable");
  - the modules Ω^{(i)}_A = Ext^{n'−n+i}_{A'}(A, Ω_{A'}), whose vanishing for i ≥ 1 characterizes Cohen-Macaulay rings;
  - Lemma 6 (a change-of-rings spectral sequence);
  - Propositions 13-16 and the closing theorem (031-043): characterizations of canonical modules and Gorenstein rings by Ext^{n−1}, Ext^n and Ext^{n+1}(k, M), injectivity of H^n(M) and the "comonogène" conditions;
  - Problem C, and his margins that two of the three Ext conditions do not suffice.

**Checked:**
- **SA04-R101-01, a proof of his question on 016:** Ext^i_A(K, A) = 0 for all i ≥ 0, for every complete local noetherian domain A ≠ K, regular or not.
  - Hom(K, A) = 0 since A has no nonzero divisible elements (∩ m^n = 0).
  - For i ≥ 1: A, a finitely generated module over a complete local ring, is pure-injective (R. B. Warfield, "Purity and algebraic compactness for modules", Pacific J. Math. 28 (1969), locator to be checked), hence cotorsion (Ext^1(flat, A) = 0). The syzygies of the flat module K are flat, so Ext^i(K, A) = 0 for all i ≥ 1.
- **SA04-R101-02:** Corollary 2 and Hom(Ω, Ω) = A agree with the theory of canonical modules (W. Bruns, J. Herzog, *Cohen-Macaulay rings*, CUP 1993, 3.3.4, 3.3.10, locator to be checked).
- **SA04-R101-03:** Proposition 10 follows from the long exact sequence for x, as he writes.

**Dating.** These 1958 notes come before Bass's "On the ubiquity of Gorenstein rings" (Math. Z. 82, 1963) and before the Harvard seminar on local cohomology of 1961 (R. Hartshorne, *Local cohomology*, LNM 41, 1967). The canonical module appears here as the "module fondamental".

## How the stretch connects

- **Cote 94 and cote 102:** one programme. 94 represents functors on modules (Theorem 12, Theorem 14 = EGA III 7.7.6, Weil restriction); 102 glues objects of a site and plans properties of morphisms of functors. Both are drafts toward EGA V-VI.
- **Cote 98 and cote 94:** 98's bounded families and generic flatness of Tor and Ext are the finiteness side of the same programme (Hilbert and Quot schemes, SGA 6 XIII). Its norms are the symmetric-power side (SGA 4 XVII).
- **Cote 100:** the arithmetic continuation of cote 93's Barsotti-Tate programme (SA04-1), whose output is good reduction via p-divisible groups. Its proof uses Mumford's GIT moduli, and the GIT sheets are in the folder. The reused EGA IV_4 proofs date it to 1967 or later.
- **Cote 101:** a 1958 local-cohomology text, the earliest dated text of the stretch, written before SGA 2.
- **Cote 97:** the lifting of Fuchsian groups to SL_2(R), i.e. spin structures. It is the topological shadow of the sl_2 versus PGL_2 oper distinction of cote 93/086-091, where his "2 inv." mattered.
