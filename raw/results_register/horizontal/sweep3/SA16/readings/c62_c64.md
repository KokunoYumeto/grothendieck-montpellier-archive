### 2.6 Normality of quadratic extensions (64:153-157; SA16-15)

**What he does.** For A a DVR ("anneau jauge") and B = A[S]/(S^2 - a): in residue characteristic != 2, B is normal iff
a is not in m^2; in residue characteristic 2, B is normal iff a is in m - m^2, or a is a unit with a-bar not a square, or
a = alpha^2 - b with alpha a unit and b in m - m^2 (b mod m^2 independent of alpha) (64:155). Application to double
covers of the line over Z (64:154-156): P = epsilon q Q in Q(t)^*/Q(t)^*2; B_(p) is normal for p odd; at 2 it is
non-normal only if 2 does not divide q and epsilon q Q is a square mod 4; to be generically étale in characteristic 2 the
normalization needs exactly that, which fixes epsilon; example Q = T(T - lambda) with lambda = 2^alpha 3^beta
("l'invariant modulaire exceptionnel"): the condition is lambda = 0 mod 4, giving an étale cover of A^1_Z - {0, lambda}
and of P^1_Z - {0, lambda}.

**Checked (check events MPL_64 rows 17-18).** The DVR criteria: Eisenstein for a in m - m^2; for a = alpha^2 - b,
T = S - alpha gives T^2 + 2 alpha T + b, Eisenstein iff b is not in m^2, and if b is in m^2 then T/pi is integral
(v(2) >= 1); b mod m^2 is independent of alpha because 2 is in m. Correct. The example: T^2 - lambda T is a square mod 4
iff lambda = 0 mod 4 (T^2 - lambda T = (T + c)^2 forces c even); for lambda = 4 mu, S = T - 2U gives
U^2 - TU + mu T = 0, whose discriminant T(T - lambda) is invertible on the open set, so the normalization is a finite
étale double cover of A^1_Z - {0, lambda}, étale also at 2. Correct. With lambda = 1728 = 2^6 3^3 this is a double cover
of the j-line over Z minus {0, 1728}.

**What is wrong.** 64:156, ink: on the chart T' = 1/T he normalizes Z[T']_(lambda T' - 1) in the extension
delta^2 = lambda T' - 1. With S^2 = T(T - lambda) and delta = S T' one gets delta^2 = 1 - lambda T'; the cover
delta^2 = lambda T' - 1 is its twist by -1, which is not a square mod 4, so its normalization is not étale at 2 (A08 and
A10 agree).

**R1. Which invariants come from genus-zero triples (62:46 "quels i conviennent ??").**
- Question: for a normal scheme S over Z[1/6] and i in Gamma(S, O_S) with i and i - 1 not identically zero on any
  component, is i the invariant of a triple (X, X', s) iff the two codimension-one conditions of 62:19 hold (v(i) even
  where i vanishes, 3 | v(i - 1) where i - 1 vanishes)? Proved for regular S; to be proved or refuted for normal S.
- Why it matters: it is the question he leaves open on 62:46, and its answer describes the maps to [U/S_3], the
  rigidified M_{1,1} over Z[1/6].
- Rests on: 62:19, 62:42-50; SA16-5, SA16-4.
- Strongest reduction: on regular S, by Zariski-Nagata purity, the conditions are codimension one, and they are those of
  62:19 (section 1.5); on normal S the reduction is to the purity of the branch locus for the S_3-torsor, which can fail

  does not extend.

  purity (locator unverified).
