### From the reading cote72

- **H72-1 (from SA04's H3-3).** The bad characteristics of a pinned regular realization are those dividing the β_i, ρ' and σ'. Here ρ = L_{n+1}(β_0..β_{n-1}) is the determinant of the autodual matrix (72/57), and δ_V = Bρ, δ_E = Bσ (72/53). For n = 2 this is the discriminant δ' = -(α+2)(α+β)(β+2)² of 70/69. Grounds: the computations SA04-R72-01, R72-04, SA04-R70-03 and R70-06; 72/78 (smoothness of the circumscribed quadric iff the β_i and σ' are invertible).
- **H72-2 (new).** The invariant-subspace theorem of 72/80-83 is the analogue, over any field and with pinning, of Vinberg's irreducibility criterion for linear Coxeter groups. The tridiagonal a_i^∨ of 72/14-17 is a Cartan matrix: β_i = 0 is decomposability, and ρ is its determinant. Grounds: our comparison of the statements. Literature: É. B. Vinberg, "Discrete linear groups generated by reflections", Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser. Mat. 35 (1971), 1072-1112, §§1-2 (locator to be checked).
- **H72-3 (new, from the correction SA04-K72-05).** Over S_0 the relations η_i² = εη_i leave at most 7 independent classes among his 10. Whether Br(f_V) ≠ 0 at the generic point is a Hilbert-symbol computation over Q(β_0, β_1). It is the first test of his open problem on 72/113 (the total Stiefel-Whitney class of the generic smooth form). Grounds: SA04-R72-05 and his NB on 72/113.

### From the reading cote81

- **H81-1.** The "maquettes ordonnées" of 81/38-40 are the finite nested poc-sets, i.e. compatible split systems, and every one is the maquette of a unique finite tree.
  - The tree is recovered from the intervals, which are chains (SA01-R81-08). This is what his abandoned proof was heading for.
  - Literature, all locators to be checked: Buneman (1971) on compatible splits; Dunwoody (1979) on nested sets; Roller (1998), "Poc sets, median algebras and group actions"; Sageev (1995). His (28 a) is the form of nestedness in which exactly one of the four relations holds up to σ.
- **H81-2.** Parts 1, 3 and 4 fit together as a combinatorial preparation for the Galois action on plane trees.
  - 81/3 with Q = 1 is plane trees (Shabat polynomials).
  - Non-crossing partitions of n points correspond to plane trees (Kreweras 1972; Dershowitz-Zaks; locators to be checked).
  - Trees are recovered from compatible decompositions (H81-1).
  - Test: compare with the Esquisse's discussion of trees (cote 119) and with the other SA01 cotes.
- **H81-3 (speculative).** The margin "Dessin plié" (81/50) and the chord diagrams of 81/10-12 suggest that a folded disc is described by a non-crossing partition of its boundary points. That would make cote 81 the boundary combinatorics behind the folded maps and immersion models of 154/46-76.

### From the reading cotes71_75

- **H71-1 (new).** Hand B's sheets of 71/14-19, 30 and 32 restate the manuscript's results in compact form, among them the gluing of 71/19, the farthest-vertex diagonal and the hull chord. They are on sheets of 1977-78, so the cote documents a reworking of the 1976 manuscript, not its source. Grounds: the hands, the dated versos, and the margins pointing to these routes ("compliqué, prendre le sommet ... à dist. max.", 71/37).
- **H71-2 (to check in the literature).** "Dehn (?) non-publié" (71/21) refers to Max Dehn's unpublished proof of the polygonal Jordan theorem, described by H. Guggenheimer, Arch. Hist. Exact Sci. 17 (1977), 193-200 (locator to be checked). The three-good-vertices statement may be in it. Grounds: the attribution with its question mark, and the dates.
- **H75-1 (new, computed).** The "n-polygône régulier le plus général" of 75/33 lives over O_p = ℤ[2cos(2π/p)]. Its plane realization degenerates (α = ±2, i.e. 4 - α² = 0) exactly at the primes above q when p = q^k or 2q^k, and nowhere when p is of no such form. The norm of 4 - α² was computed for p = 3..30 (SA01-R75-05).
- **H75-2 (new, computed).** At the regular solids the discriminants δ', δ of 70/69 are:

  | Solid | δ' | δ |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | tetrahedron | 2 | -3 |
  | octahedron | 4 | -12 |
  | cube | 2 | -4 |
  | icosahedron {3,5} | φ² (a unit) | -3φ⁴ |
  | dodecahedron {5,3} | 1 | α² - 4, of norm 5 |

  For the icosahedral pair the special characteristics are therefore 2 (quadratic forms; A_5 ≅ SL(2, F_4)), 3 (the icosahedron's δ) and 5 (the dodecahedron's δ; S_5 ≅ GP(1, F_5); the icosahedron mod 5 fills P²(F_5), cote 70). 75/32 names 2 and 5; the computation adds 3. Grounds: SA01-R75-05 and SA04-R70-03.

### From the reading cotes83_93-92-134

- **H83-1 (composition).** The ∗-product of 127-177 is the composition of quadratic algebras: q_{c_1} ⊗ q_{c_2} = q_c with c = b_2²c_1 + b_1²c_2 − 4c_1c_2, and δ multiplicative. It should agree with Bhargava's composition, restricted to the "diagonal" case, and with cote 84 (SA02). The discriminant Q of a pair of quadratic subalgebras, whose roots are the commuting values of Tr(uv), is the same object.
- **H83-2 (n-alités are nets).** The n-alités in Ens of 048-051 are Bruck's nets, i.e. transversal designs TD(n, m) or n − 2 MOLS. References: Bruck, Canad. J. Math. 3 (1951); Colbourn-Dinitz, *Handbook of Combinatorial Designs*, III.3; locators to be checked.
- **H93-3 (characteristic p), sharpened.** 93/121-124 prove the local rigidity of G_0 = 0 in every characteristic, using the right truncation T^p or T^{p²}. The Pro notes D07-D14 extend this to nonreduced A and weaken "ad − bc = 1" to a unit determinant. The global injectivity H_{X^{(p)}}\𝒫 ↪ Conn_0 that he "hopes" for (122) remains open beyond this local model.
- **H93-5 (families).** The uniformizing projective connection is not holomorphic, hence not algebraic, in the moduli. For compact curves this is the Pro notes F06; for P^1 minus four points it is the Takhtajan-Zograf accessory parameters (locator to be checked). The answer to his "Sont-elles algébriques ?" (115) is therefore negative whenever the moduli vary. For P^1 − 3 points μ is rational: −(z² − z + 1)/(z²(z − 1)²).
