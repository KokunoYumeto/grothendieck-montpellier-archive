## 2. Scans 2-10 (18.1.84): five variants of the deployment

**What he defines.** For a system Σ = (X, Σ) of n pseudolines in the projective plane X, the "deployment" is a closed surface 𝒳 whose faces are the relative positions of an added pseudoline D. The five variants differ in how the supercritical (supersingular) positions D = D_i ∈ Σ are treated:
- 𝒳_0: the supercritical positions are not faces; each becomes a vertex of order 4n (all other vertices of order 4) (154/002);
- 𝒳_1: they are admitted as faces; the exceptional specialization arcs are chosen short (combinatorial length 1) and placed midway; 2(n−1) sub-stable faces fit around a supercritical face in the stable case, 2n_i in general (n_i = number of vertices on D_i) (154/002);
- 𝒳'_1: each supercritical vertex of 𝒳_0 is replaced by a small disk carrying the "affinisation" of Σ by D_i; vertices on these faces have order 3 (154/003-004);
- 𝒳_2: the sweeps "below" a supercritical position are added as edges of weight 2; all vertices have order 4 and those near D_i correspond to pairs (s, a), s a vertex and a an edge of D_i incident to s: 2(n−1) of them in the stable case (154/005);
- 𝒳_3: 2n_i supercritical positions per D_i, according to which of the 2n_i facets of the double cover D̃_i the virtual intersection point lies on; D_i ⊂ 𝒳 is replaced by a Möbius band; 𝒳_3 maps to X (154/009).
- Ultimate deployments: the principal D_{2n}-covers 𝒳̃_i defined by the polygons Pol(D) (ramified for the first three) (154/010).

**Checked.**
- The free-end count of 154/003: for n even, 2·2(n−1) + 2(n−1) = 6(n−1); for n odd, 2(n−1). The arithmetic holds (SA11-R154-01).
- 2(n−1) = 2n_i in the stable case (each D_i meets the other n−1 lines in distinct points); the pairs (s, a) on D_i number 2n_i, the edges of the barycentric subdivision of D_i (SA11-R154-01).
- The 2n_i supercritical positions of 𝒳_3 (154/009): a pseudoline D'_i close to D_i crosses it once, in one of its n_i edges, and on that edge it can pass from either side to the other; that gives 2n_i classes, the 2n_i edges of D̃_i (SA11-R154-02).
- 𝒳_2 is 𝒳_0 with the supersingular vertices blown up, or 𝒳_1 with the supersingular faces cut out and their boundaries glued antipodally (154/007). Blowing up a point of a surface (removing a disk and gluing its boundary by the antipodal map) adds a cross-cap. This is used in section 6 for the genus.

**Open, in his words:** whether 𝒳'_1 maps to X near the boundary of a supercritical face (154/010: "je ne suis pas tout à fait convaincu").

## 3. Scans 12-33 (23-25.12.83): the set of relative positions PR(Σ) and its polygons

### 3.1 Specialization arrows (154/012-016)
- **Definition.** A specialization arrow is a pair (D, T), T a "specialization triangle" for D: sweeping D over the apex s of T gives D', and D' ∩ S = (D ∩ S) ∪ {s} (S = the set of vertices of Σ). Generizations are the pairs (s, φ), s ∈ D ∩ S, φ an orientation of D at s, i.e. a point of D̃ over s.
- **His claim:** P̃ → P × P, (D, T) ↦ (D, D') is injective (154/012). His argument: s is the unique vertex in D' not in D; the ν+1 lines of Σ through s meet D in the base of T; the edges from s to the two end vertices are unique because no line of Σ' = Σ ∪ {D} through s is a biangle (Σ is not trivial). The argument is complete as far as it goes (SA11-R154-03).
- **The degree bound (154/013).** He wants every vertex D of the graph Γ(Σ) to have at least three arrows. Stable case: D borders at least three triangles of Σ ∪ {D}. That input is Levi's theorem that every pseudoline of a non-trivial arrangement borders at least three triangles (F. Levi, "Die Teilung der projektiven Ebene durch Gerade oder Pseudogerade", Ber. Math.-Phys. Kl. Sächs. Akad. Wiss. Leipzig 78 (1926), 256-267; locator to be checked). From one triangle he obtains T, and from the two "big triangles" A, B two further specialization triangles T', T''.
- **Sub-stable case: his counterexample (154/014).** With Σ = St_4 and D the sub-stable position through a vertex a that avoids the two corridors incident to a and cuts the third lengthwise, D has no specialization triangle. He then sees that he has forgotten the supercritical positions D = Δ ∈ Σ, and adds the "corridor" specializations: D through a vertex a of Δ, swept along a global biangle of D and Δ that contains no vertex. With the corridors the bound is restated on 154/016: at least 3 arrows at every vertex, at least 4 when D is not stable. The Pro notes (P04) say this correctly: the counterexample targets the first argument, not the amended bound.
- **Checked (154/015).** The immediate generizations of a supercritical Δ with ν vertices: two per vertex (the two orientations of X at it), a polygon of order 2ν, the double cover of the necklace of Δ; with the stable (non-immediate) ones, 4ν. He notes in the margin that the stable ones are not immediate. Consistent (SA11-R154-04).

### 3.2 Weights and the deployed polygon (154/017-019)
- **His claim (154/017, margin):** at every non-supercritical D, the weights of all arrows and all corners at D add up to 2n. **Checked** (SA11-R154-05): the parallel contour D̃ (the boundary of a thin Möbius band around D) meets each line of Σ twice, so it is cut into 2n arcs; specialization arcs, generization points (a generization point over a vertex where ν+1 lines meet carries ν short arcs) and corners partition these 2n arcs.
- **The supercritical case (154/018-019). Checked** (SA11-R154-06): for Δ = Δ_{i0} and a vertex s with ν lines of Σ other than Δ through it, the ordinary weight of the corridor arrow is ν_0 = n − ν (the n − ν − 1 lines not through s cross the corridor as disjoint chords, cutting its side on D into n − ν edges), so ν + ν_0 = n; and the deployed polygon P̂(Δ) has order Σ_s 2ν_s = 2(n−1), because every other line meets Δ exactly once.
- **The labelling of the deployed polygon (154/018).** P̂(D) has order 2n and its edges correspond to the oriented lines Ĩ = {±Δ_i} (each point of D̃ ∩ Δ_i is labelled by the orientation of Δ_i pointing away from D). He says this bijection "est compatible avec les antipodies", the half-turn of the 2n-gon on one side and Δ ↦ −Δ on the other.
  - **Refined (SA11-R154-07; refined statement SA11-K154-01).** This holds when D is stable, and fails when D passes through a vertex of Σ. Where k ≥ 2 lines of Σ meet at a vertex v of D, the contour crosses them in one order on one side of D and in the reverse order on the other side (a parallel at height +ε meets the line of angle θ at ε cot θ, one at height −ε at −ε cot θ). The label antipodism is then the half-turn followed by reversing each such cluster, not the half-turn.
  - Smallest example, computed in coordinates with the band y² ≤ ε²(x² + z²) around D = {y = 0}: Δ_1: y = x, Δ_2: x = 0, Δ_3: x = 5, D through v = Δ_1 ∩ Δ_2. Along D̃ the labelled crossings are (+2, +1, +3, −1, −2, −3). The half-turn sends +2 to −1, not to −2. For a generization of D (y = δ with 0 < ε < δ) one gets (+2, +1, +3, −2, −1, −3), which is compatible.
- **His expected injectivity (154/019):** "il ne devrait pas être difficile de montrer que cette application est injective", for the map from non-supercritical relative positions to polygon structures on Ĩ (the register's candidate `154-cyclic-order-determines-position`).
  - **Proved here, with the steps written** (SA11-R154-08). The labelled cyclic order on D̃ is the rank-2 oriented matroid (M ∪ D)/D, M the rank-3 oriented matroid of Σ. From it and M one reads off, for every vertex v = Δ_i ∩ Δ_j off D, on which side of D the vertex v lies:
    1. on the sphere, the half of Δ_i on the positive side of D is the semicircle from x_i (the crossing with D) in the direction of the label o_i;
    2. whether v lies in it follows from the side of Δ_j on which x_i lies, read from the cyclic position of x_i between ±x_j and the label at x_j, together with M's sign data along Δ_i.
    The vertices on D are recognized as the reversed clusters of the previous point. So the cocircuit signature of the extension is determined. By Las Vergnas's theorem a single-element extension is determined by its cocircuit signature (M. Las Vergnas, "Extensions ponctuelles d'une géométrie combinatoire orientée", Colloq. Internat. CNRS 260, 1978, 265-270; A. Björner, M. Las Vergnas, B. Sturmfels, N. White, G. M. Ziegler, *Oriented matroids*, 2nd ed., CUP 1999, §7.1, Thm. 7.1.8; locators to be checked).
    Hence D is determined by P̂(D) and Σ, for stable and non-stable D alike, provided P̂(D) is taken literally (with the reversed clusters), not as an antipody-compatible polygon.
  - **Credit.** Michel Hua's project states the oriented-matroid form: two single-element extensions of the same chirotope with the same contraction, up to sign, coincide up to reorientation. It reports a partial Lean check (`lean/Grothendieck/Folder154.lean`, `problem17_holds`), and its note says "nous ne savons pas si cet ordre la détermine", leaving the translation from the polygon open. What is ours: that translation (P̂(D) is the contraction, refined by the cluster orders), the failure of antipody-compatibility for non-stable D, and the recovery of the clusters. The step from Las Vergnas is short.
- **Not taken up by him:** surjectivity, and the description of the image (his question 2° on 154/020).

### 3.3 Transport along a specialization (154/020-026)
- On 154/020 he states a canonical isomorphism P̂(D) ≅ P̂(D') sending a vertex coming from the arrow a to the antipode of one coming from a; the coarse version (Pol(D) ≅ Pol(D') minus a) is struck "annulé; cf plus bas !".
- **The weight relation (154/022):** poids(α') + poids(β') + poids(a) = poids(γ). It follows from the 2n rule at D and D' when the two corners α, β cutting γ are the only ones not yet weighted. Consistent (SA11-R154-09).
- He enlarges Pol(D) to Pol*(D) by the antipodes of the specialization arrows, taken as free ends ("bouts libres"), and the pseudo-specialization arcs (154/022-023). Pol*(D) is stable under antipody, and specialization gives an embedding Pol*(D) → Pol*(D') that is not always onto (a square next to T creates new pseudo-specializations).
- **The local-system principle (154/025):** on a map whose total weight at each vertex is a constant N, the deployed local polygons are N-gons, and the transport along an edge is fixed by (a) equal order, (b) the effect on orientations, (c) the image of one vertex. So they form a local system of N-gons. This is right as a construction (SA11-R154-09); the work lies in (c), which he supplies.
- **24.12 (154/026):** "c'est le polygône Pol^∧(D) qui est le bon, ou encore mieux, le polygône dual". The surface is the gluing of the disks Δ(D) (the complement of a tubular neighbourhood of D), which leads to the disk model of 154/034-043 (read by a6d42bf6, STUDY §1). Folding the free edges creates artificial double points on the boundary, "sûrement rédhibitoires" (154/028).
- **25.12 (154/028):** gluing without folding gives a surface with boundary, every boundary point of order 2 or 3; the holes correspond exactly to the vertices of the closed surface.

### 3.4 The general surface of a graph with polygon structures (154/031-033)
- **Construction.** Γ = (S, A⃗, σ_0, α), with a polygon structure on the arrows at each vertex and transport isomorphisms φ_a: ω(A⃗_s) → ω(A⃗_t) with φ_{−a} = φ_a^{−1}. Each vertex gives a polygonal disk Dis_s, with 2ν_s flags. Edges are glued so that corresponding orientations induce opposite orientations on the glued edge. This is the standard rule for a coherent gluing.
- **Flag operations (154/032). Checked** (SA11-R154-10):
  - σ_0(a⃗, ω) = (−a⃗, −φ_a ω), σ_1(a⃗, ω) = (ρ_ω(a⃗), −ω), σ_2(a⃗, ω) = (a⃗, −ω);
  - ρ_som = σ_2σ_1 is the rotation (a⃗, ω) ↦ (ρ_ω(a⃗), ω) about s;
  - ρ_face = σ_1σ_0 is (a⃗, ω_s) ↦ (ρ_{ω_t}^{−1}(−a⃗), ω_t), with ω_t = φ_a ω_s, which is his formula for ρ_f;
  - ρ_som ρ_f = σ_2σ_1σ_1σ_0 = σ_2σ_0 = σ, with σ(a⃗, ω_s) = (−a⃗, φ_a ω_s).
  - This settles the reading question SA11-C2 on the mathematics: "ρ_som ρ_f = σ_2σ_0", as frozen. The reading "ρ_f = σ_2σ_0" in Michel Hua's project contradicts ρ_face = σ_1σ_0 on the same page. The ink is examined in section 9.
- **154/033:** boundary circuits are the orbits of the dihedral group ⟨σ_0, σ_1⟩, which contains ⟨ρ_f⟩ with index 1 or 2. Correct.

## 4. Scans 44-45 (31.11.83 [sic]): the axioms of the deployment (𝒳, K, L)

- **Axioms a-h.** 𝒳 is a compact surface with two graphs K, L cutting it into cells:
  - a) the vertices of K have order 4, so K has a deployment K̃' that is a curve;
  - b) every edge of K carries at most one vertex of L;
  - c) the exceptional edges of K (those without an L-vertex) form a finite union of circles K_0;
  - e-g) the critical points, multiplicities and the number ν(s);
  - h) every face F of K carries 2n points of L on ∂F, counted with reduced multiplicity; for ordinary F this is Σ_s (2ν(s) + ε(s)) + Σ_a ν(a).
  The axiom-level counts agree with 154/017 (2n per face).
- **The incidence dictionary of 154/045:**
  1. faces of K ↔ non-supercritical relative positions;
  2. edges ↔ immediate specializations D → D' (ordinary) or sweeps between positions next to a D_i (exceptional);
  3. ordinary vertices ↔ (D, t', t'', β), D stable, t', t'' specialization triangles not opposite at a vertex, β an arc of D̃ between their arcs, with the four faces D, D', D'', D''' (specialize over t', t'', or both);
  4. exceptional vertices ↔ flags (s, a) of type (0, 1) of Σ.
  - Corollary: the components of K_0 are indexed by I, with K(i) ≅ D̃_i.
- **My reading of the dictionary** (it is mine, not his). For an arrangement of straight lines whose relative positions are all straight, the relative positions are the cells of the dual arrangement A^∨: the lines dual to the vertices of Σ, with the points δ_i dual to the D_i as the supercritical cells. He sets up exactly this himself on 154/078 ("le système de droites dans X^∨ correspondant aux sommets de Σ"). Then:
  - Γ(Σ) with its diamonds is the cubical subdivision of A^∨;
  - (𝒳, K) is its dual, with the n supercritical points blown up into the circles K(i);
  - a vertex (D, t', t'', β) is a diamond "2-cell > two edges > vertex" of A^∨.
  Section 6 uses this for the genus.

- **154/056: the monodromy formula. Checked** (SA11-R154-13).
  - With σ_k(t) = ℓ_k − t on ℤ/Nℤ, one gets ρ_p(t) = σ_{p−1}⋯σ_0(t) = (−1)^p (t − χ_p), where χ_p = Σ_j (−1)^j ℓ_j (induction on p).
  - Around a component D_i the alternating sum is Σ_t (ν_t − 1) = n − 1, since every other line meets D_i exactly once.
  - So the transport around D_i is the translation by n − 1 (also on 154/064: "u(ℓ⃗_{D_i}) = translation par n−1").
  - In a 2n-gon this translation has order 2n for n even and n for n odd. This is the parity split of 154/003 and the "ramification" of 154/007.
- **154/060-064 (4.1.84).** The transport of polygons along paths, and the characters χ_X, χ_s, χ_a, χ_𝒳, χ_c with χ_X = χ_s χ_a.
  - **His claim (064): χ_c is trivial.** For a path c from D to D', par(c) ≡ sing_{D'} − sing_D (mod 2), where sing_D is the number of vertices of Σ on D, an immediate specialization counts one step, and a sweep over a D_i counts two (his margin on 063). **Checked** (SA11-R154-14): every step changes sing by 1, and every sweep leaves it unchanged. So χ_𝒳 = χ_X.
  - **The surjectivity of π_1 → Aut Pol(D) ≅ D_{2n} (064, "ouf!").** The half-turns of D about a point of X give, for every vertex and every edge of the 2n-gon, the reflection fixing it, and these generate the whole dihedral group. Checked as group theory; the existence of the loops is his geometric claim.
- **154/065-071 (3.1.84): the four ways of gluing Δ_D to Δ_{D'}** (eight a priori, four up to the antipodisms): A1 (an immersion 𝒳 → X), A2 (a fold along common edges), B1 and B2 (no natural map to X).
  - On 154/067 he finds "une erreur monumentale, commise dès le début": following an orientation of Δ_D through the transition arc gives on D' the orientation opposite to parallel transport.
  - 154/071: A1, A2 and B1 give the right circuits around a supersingular position; B2 "déconne complètement".
  - Collation points not yet decided on the scan: 066, "qui exprime l'orientation" (Pro) against "l'opération" (Codex), where the sense favours "l'opération de générisation"; 068, "recollage" (Pro) against "vrillage" (Codex), where the sense favours "recollage".
- **154/072-076: a surface from the stable positions only.** Gluing along (a, b), with transport (1) parallel, which gives an immersion but "les circuits sont stériles", or (2) opposite, which gives the right supersingular circuits.
  - 154/073: N = 4n, with ρ_i = σ_{i−1}σ_i. Checked: ρ_i² = 1 is the same as σ_{i−1} and σ_i commuting, since σ² = 1.
  - 154/075-076: the Möbius band, and the doubled graph with two arrows per adjacent pair (D, D').

### 6.1 The Euler characteristic and the genus (154/120): the test of H1

**His text, checked on the scan at 250 dpi.**
- "χ(𝒳) = s₀ − s₁ + s₂ = 1 − g"; "les sommets sont d'ordre 4"; "2s₁ = 4s₀ = 2Σ_{f∈F} n_f", where n_f is half the order of the face; "s₁ = 2s₀"; "χ(𝒳) = s₂ − s₀"; "g = 1 + s₀ − s₂".
- s₂ is the number of non-supersingular relative positions. s₀ is (a) the number of pairs (D, a), a an inertia arc of type gen-gen, plus (b) the number of flags of type (0,1) of Σ (2n(n−1) in the stable case).
- In the left margin the ink has "(n−2)+(n−3) = 2n−5", not "−" as frozen (SA11-K154-02).

**Checked** (SA11-R154-17): with all vertices of order 4, s₁ = 2s₀ and χ = s₂ − s₀. His g is 1 − χ (Michel Hua's project notes that this is not the usual genus).

**Computed: the value** (SA11-R154-18). Take a line arrangement Σ whose relative positions are all straight. Then the non-supersingular positions are the cells of the dual arrangement A^∨ other than the n points δ_i, so s₂ = (V − n) + E + F, where V, E, F count the cells of A^∨.
- The vertices (a) are the pairs (non-supersingular vertex w of A^∨, face at w). The critical position D_w passes through the m vertices of Σ whose dual lines meet at w. So D_w has 2m generization points on D̃ and 2m gen-gen inertia arcs, one for each of the 2m faces at w. A sub-stable position has none, because each of its two sides carries a specialization arc (the two ends of its edge in A^∨).
- The flags (b) of D_i number 2n_i, which is the number of faces at δ_i.
- So s₀ is the number of vertex-face incidences of A^∨, which is 2E, and χ(𝒳) = V − n + E + F − 2E = χ(RP²) − n = 1 − n.
- **His g is therefore n**, and 𝒳, being non-orientable (154/064: χ_𝒳 = χ_X ≠ 1), is the connected sum of n + 1 projective planes.
- Examples worked in full:
  - n = 3 in general position: A^∨ has 3 vertices (all δ_i), 6 edges and 4 faces; s₂ = 10, s₀ = 0 + 12, χ = −2.
  - n = 4 in general position: A^∨ has 7 vertices (4 δ_i and 3 diagonal points), 18 edges and 12 faces; s₂ = 33, s₀ = 12 + 24 = 36, χ = −3.
  - In both cases s₁ = 2s₀ was checked against the edge list: 24, and 36 + 12 + 24 = 72.
  - For n ≤ 4 in general position every pseudoline relative position is straight. Arrangements of at most 8 pseudolines are stretchable (J. E. Goodman, R. Pollack, "Proof of Grünbaum's conjecture on the stretchability of certain arrangements of pseudolines", J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 29 (1980), 385-390; for non-simple arrangements, locator to be checked), and 4 lines in general position are projectively unique.

**What this says about H1** (the hunch that the surface is the rank-3 extension space of Sturmfels and Ziegler).
- 𝒳 is not the extension space itself. In the realizable case it is the projectivized extension space (the dual plane, χ = 1) **blown up at the n supercritical points**:
  - each δ_i is replaced by the exceptional circle K(i) ≅ D̃_i, a Möbius-band neighbourhood;
  - 154/007 says 𝒳_2 is 𝒳_0 with the supersingular vertices "éclatés";
  - each real blow-up of a point lowers χ by one.
- His genus formula agrees with this: s₂ − s₀ = 1 − n.
- For pseudoline arrangements in general, Sturmfels and Ziegler prove that the extension space of a rank-3 oriented matroid is homotopy equivalent to S² (B. Sturmfels, G. M. Ziegler, "Extension spaces of oriented matroids", Discrete Comput. Geom. 10 (1993), 23-45; the rank-3 case is part of their theorem on strongly Euclidean oriented matroids; locator to be checked). That points to χ(𝒳) = 1 − n in general.
- **But 154/154 shows why this is not automatic.** A pseudoline relative position through ν vertices has 2^ν stable generizations, forming a ν-cube (SA11-R154-20); straight lines give only 2ν of them. So for ν ≥ 3 the extension poset has ν-dimensional cubes. 𝒳, built from polygons, keeps only the 2ν "cyclically consecutive" squares of each such cube (the gen-gen inertia arcs of D̃). Whether s₂ − s₀ = 1 − n survives this is open; it is the sharpened form of H1 (H154-1 in the integration file).

## 7. Scans 127-176 (1984, undated sheets): fuseaux, flags, counts, axioms with boundary

- **154/131.** (α+β+2σ+2)/2 ≤ α+β+σ+1, with difference (α+β)/2. Trivially true for α + β ≥ 0; the "*?" is his.
- **154/133-140: the flags of 𝒳̃ and the operations σ_0, σ_1, σ_2, σ.**
  - A "fuseau" (spindle) is a critical position with a contact element. The circuit D_1, …, D_8, D_9 = D_1 of stable positions around a non-stable D at a vertex s depends on (D, s, ρ, ω).
  - 136 is the dual-arrangement cell structure in his words: "faces de 𝒳̃ : p.r. stables; arêtes : p.r. semi-stables; sommets : p.r. critiques".
  - 135: σ_1, σ, σ_2, σ_0 with consecutive ones commuting.
  - **Checked** (SA11-R154-21): in the one-vertex, one-face, two-edge example of 140, with σ' = σ_0 = σ_2, one has σ_1 = σσ'. On the four flags a±, b± this gives a+ ↦ a− ↦ b+.
- **154/142: counts of positions in the simple straight case.** Stable, semi-stable and critical.
  - Critical: ½N(N−1) − n(n−1)(n−2)/2 with N = n(n−1)/2, which is 3·C(n,4) ~ n⁴/8 (3 for n = 4, 15 for n = 5). **Checked** (SA11-R154-19).
  - The ink has "(n−2)" in the subtracted term, as the count of pairs of vertices on a common line requires; the frozen "(n−1)(n−1)" is a misreading (SA11-K154-03).
- **154/151.** The cellular surface X* = Rel(Σ) with critical positions as vertices. His difficulty is to find "les deux (?) sommets incidents à a_L" for a semi-stable L: "il n'est pas sûr qu'aucun de ces choix ne marche!"
  - For pseudolines this is a real obstruction. A semi-stable L can have several specialization arcs on one side of D̃ \ {s′, s″} (154/016 allows it), so the "edge" L can have more than two critical endpoints.
  - The extension poset is then not the face poset of a regular cell complex, which is the same phenomenon as the cubes of 154/154.
- **154/153: fuseaux** (corridors, semi-corridors, trivial, ordinary). His claim: isotopy classes of fuseaux correspond one-to-one to semi-stable positions. Not checked; given 151, it needs the uniqueness of the endpoint, which is open for pseudolines.
- **154/154.** "Les générisations stbles de D correspondent aux sommets d'un cube canon. de dim ν, dont les arêtes sont les générisations semi-stbles": 2^ν vertices and ν2^{ν−1} edges. **Checked** (SA11-R154-20): the perturbations near the ν vertices are independent, each to either side. The frozen "2^{ν+}" should read 2^ν (not checked on the scan).
- **154/157-158.** ρ_s = σ_2σ_1, ρ_f = σ_1σ_0, ρ_sρ_f = σ, the convention of 032, which supports the reading decided there. The orientations of a triangle T in X and of its dual T' in X̌ correspond (158).
- **154/171: the axioms with boundary.** Interior vertices of K have order 4. Every edge of K now carries **exactly** one vertex of L, where 044 b) had "at most one" (the exceptional edges became boundary). The face sum Σ(2ν_s + ε_s) + Σν_a = 2n is the same as 044 h).
- **154/176.** The cycle 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 = 1 by specialization, generization, generization, specialization.

## 8. What is open, with the strongest reduction on the pages

1. **Injectivity of D ↦ P̂(D) (154/019).** Settled by section 3.2 (SA11-R154-08), with the literal labelling. Michel Hua's project states the chirotope form ("deux extensions à un élément d'un même chirotope qui ont, au signe près, la même contraction … coïncident") and checks it partly in Lean (`problem17_holds`). It leaves open the translation from the polygon to the contraction, which section 3.2 gives. Open: the image of the map (his 2°, 154/020).
2. **χ(𝒳) for pseudoline arrangements in general** (sections 6.1 and 7). Known: s₂ − s₀ = 1 − n when the extension poset is the dual line arrangement. Unknown: the effect of the ν-cubes (154/154) and of semi-stable positions with more than two critical endpoints (154/151).
3. **The endpoints of an edge a_L (154/151) and the fuseau correspondence (154/153)** for pseudolines.
4. **The local system of the Pol(D) on 𝒳 at exceptional vertices** (154/093, his doubt), and the connectedness of the D_{2n}-cover of 𝒳̃ (154/087, "trop conjectural").
5. **The discrepancy of one unit** between the exceptional index of 047 and ℓ_a of 056/073 (SA11-K154-04).
6. **Whether 𝒳'_1 maps to X** near a supercritical face (154/010).
