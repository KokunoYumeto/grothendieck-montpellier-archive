### Scans 2-4: Théorème 1 and its corollaries

**On the page.**
- **Théorème 1.** Let f: X → S be faithfully flat, quasi-compact and quasi-separated, with S affine; the revisions
  above the line amount to "covering, quasi-compact, quasi-separated, flat". For F quasi-coherent, Ȟ^i(X•/S, F) is 0
  for i ≠ 0 and H^0(S, F) for i = 0.
- **Cor. 1.** Without affineness on X, the cohomology sheaves H̲^i(X•/S, F) vanish for i ≠ 0, and F → H̲^0 is an
  isomorphism.
  - The proof: their formation commutes with flat base change, because the X^i/S are qcqs. One passes to affine
    S_λ over which X_λ has a section, where the claim is "immédiat".
- **Cor. 2 (margin).** S' ↦ Γ(S', F ⊗ O_{S'}) is an fpqc sheaf. It is enough to check Zariski families and
  faithfully flat morphisms of affines.
- **Cor. 3.** For any topology T between Zariski and fpqc, H*(X^T, F^T) ≅ H*(X, F).
  - The proof: Leray for (Sch)^T → (Sch)^Zar, then Cartan's spectral sequence reduce it to Ȟ^p of finite
    faithfully flat affine families. Since F turns sums into products, these are single morphisms, so Th. 1
    applies.
- **Cor. 4.** For S' → S fpqc-covering there is a spectral sequence Ȟ^p(S'/S, ℋ^q(F)) ⇒ H*(S, F), where
  ℋ^q(F) = (T ↦ H^q(T, F ⊗ O_T)). Scan 4 writes its low-degree exact sequence.

**Checked.**
- Th. 1 is the exactness of the Amitsur complex of a faithfully flat ring map (SGA 1 VIII 1.1; Stacks 023M, to
  verify).
- Cor. 1:
  - the H̲^i are the cohomology sheaves of the complex (f_n)_*f_n^*F, with f_n: X^{n+1} → S qcqs, so they are
    quasi-coherent and commute with flat base change;
  - with a section, the augmented complex is contractible by the extra degeneracy, naturally in the open of the
    base;
  - S_λ = the affine opens of X work.
- The low-degree sequence ends in H^2(S, F). It is the five-term sequence of a first-quadrant spectral sequence:
  0 → E_2^{1,0} → H^1 → E_2^{0,1} → E_2^{2,0} → H^2. So the doubtful "H^{0|2}" of the transcription must read H^2.
- Cor. 3 holds as proved once the finite affine T-families are cofinal among the T-coverings of an affine. This
  holds for the Zariski, étale, fppf and fpqc topologies, and for the topology of Prop. 2 below. It is not
  automatic for an arbitrary topology "intermédiaire", which needs this cofinality as a hypothesis (refinement,
  CORRECTIONS row 1).
- Cor. 2 is the fpqc sheaf property of quasi-coherent modules (SGA 1 VIII 1; Stacks 023Q, to verify).
- Slip, credited to the Work notes of K126-01 (their S02): in Th. 1 and Cor. 1, F "sur X" should be F on S, pulled
  back, and the last comparison "X'/X" should be X/S.

### Scan 4: Remarque 2, the doubtful vanishing, and why it is right

**On the page.** "La démonstration du Th. 1 montre aussi, pour S affine, que Ȟ^p(S'/S, ℋ^q(F)) = 0 pour tt p, si
q ≠ 0."
- A marginal "?" stands beside it. The next sentence, "Ce résultat est sans doute ...", is struck out.
- He draws the case p = 0: Ker(H^q(S', F) ⇉ H^q(S' ×_S S', F)) = 0.
- The slanted note over Th. 1 on scan 2, "Compléter, cf remarque 2 plus bas", points here.

**Checked: true for S' → S qcqs, faithfully flat, and S affine.**
1. For each q, the sheaves R^q(f_n)_*(f_n^*F) on S, with f_n: S'^{n+1} → S, form a complex of quasi-coherent
   sheaves. Its cohomology sheaves 𝒦^{p,q} are quasi-coherent and commute with flat base change (EGA III 1.4.10 for
   flat base change; to verify).
2. After the faithfully flat base change S_1 → S, with S_1 the affine opens of S', the covering has a section. The
   complex of presheaves U ↦ H^q(S'^{n+1} ×_S U, F) is then contractible, naturally in U. Its augmentation term is
   R^q id_* = 0 for q > 0. So the 𝒦^{p,q} vanish after S_1 → S, hence on S.
3. S is affine, so Γ is exact on quasi-coherent sheaves. Hence Ȟ^p(S'/S, ℋ^q(F)) = Γ(S, 𝒦^{p,q}) = 0.

A test case agrees. Take S = Spec k and S' = X proper with H^0(X, O) = k. Künneth puts p_1^*a = a ⊗ 1 and
p_2^*a = 1 ⊗ a in different summands of H^1(X × X, O), so the kernel is 0. The "?" can be lifted, with "qcqs"
written into the hypothesis. This matters, because Remark 2 is the step the next proposition needs.

### Scans 4-8: Proposition: effectivity of an equivalence relation through a nilpotent thickening

**On the page.**
- **Setting.** X a prescheme, R an equivalence relation on X, X_0 ⊂ X a closed subscheme defined by a nilpotent
  ideal and stable under R, R_0 the induced relation.
- **Hypotheses.** R_0 is M-effective, M = faithfully flat, quasi-compact, quasi-separated morphisms, and
  p_i^0: R_0 → X_0 are faithfully flat qcqs.
- **Conclusion.** R is M-effective.

**The proof, re-derived step by step.**
1. Reduce to I_{X_0} of square zero.
2. Y_0 = X_0/R_0, with R_0 ≅ X_0 ×_{Y_0} X_0. The ideal I_{X_0} carries a descent datum for X_0 → Y_0, effective
   by fpqc descent of quasi-coherent modules, giving I_{Y_0}.
3. Reduce to Y_0 affine with ring A_0, and put A = Ker(H^0(X, O_X) ⇉ H^0(R, O_R)). Then Ker(A → A_0) = I_{Y_0}.
4. **A → A_0 is onto.** Take f_0 on X_0 from φ_0 ∈ A_0.
   - The obstruction ζ ∈ H^1(X_0, I_{X_0}) to lifting f_0 has equal pullbacks along p_1^0 and p_2^0. Both are the
     obstruction to lifting the same section of O_{R_0}, by naturality of the connecting map.
   - So ζ ∈ Ȟ^0(X_0/Y_0, ℋ^1(I_{Y_0})), which is 0 by Remark 2.
   - A lift f then has y = p_1 f − p_2 f ∈ Z^1(X_0/Y_0, I_{Y_0}).
   - Ȟ^1(X_0/Y_0, I_{Y_0}) = 0 by Th. 1 and Cor. 1, so dλ = y has a solution, and f + λ lies in A.
5. Y = Spec A. One has I_{Y_0}O_X = I_{X_0} ≅ I_{Y_0} ⊗ O_{X_0}, so X_0 = X ×_Y Y_0.
6. **X → Y is flat.** This is the local flatness criterion for a nilpotent ideal: X_0/Y_0 is flat, and
   I_{Y_0} ⊗_A O_X → O_X is injective, being the descent isomorphism onto I_{X_0} (EGA 0_III 10.2.1, to verify).
7. **R → P = X ×_Y X is an isomorphism.** R is flat over Y and R_0 → P_0 is an isomorphism. The kernel K of
   O_P → O_R satisfies K/IK = 0 by flatness of R, so K = 0.

**Wrong: a hypothesis used but not stated.**
- The proof needs p_1, p_2: R → X flat, not only p_i^0: R_0 → X_0. Scan 8 says "Comme R → X est plat"; steps 2
  and 4 use p_i^*I_{X_0} = I_{R_0}; and the induction in step 1 needs flatness at every intermediate order.
- Flatness of R_0 → X_0 does not imply flatness of R → X over a nilpotent thickening.
- With "p_1, p_2: R → X flat" added, the proof is complete (CORRECTIONS row 2).
- On the reading: the inserted "Si X_0 → Y_0 est plate i.e. R_0 → X_0" on scan 5 is a separate, unfinished clause.

**Known.** This is the nilpotent-invariance of effectivity for flat equivalence relations, a standard step in the
construction of quotients by flat groupoids (cf. SGA 3 V; to verify the exact locator).

### Scans 10-15: the topology generated by T on C and T' on C' (Prop. 1, Cor. 1-2) and the fpqc topology (Prop. 2)

**On the page.**
- **Prop. 1.** C has fibre products and a pretopology T. C' is a full subcategory with: (a) every object has a
  T-covering by objects of C'; (b) C' is stable under isomorphism and under fibre products of its own diagrams. T'
  is a pretopology on C'. Condition (c) says that in a tower U_{ijh} → U_{ij} → U_i → U (T', T, T') a subfamily of
  the U_{ijh} → U is T'-covering.
  - Then a family X_i → X is covering for the generated topology 𝒯 iff it is refined by a two-step pattern: a
    T-covering U_α → X by objects of C', then T'-coverings U_{αβ} → U_α, each U_{αβ} mapping to some X_i.
- **Cor. 1.** The one-step criterion for X in C'.
- **Cor. 2.** F is a 𝒯-sheaf iff it is a T-sheaf whose restriction to C' is a T'-sheaf.
- **Prop. 2.** C = (Sch), T = Zariski, C' = affine schemes, T' = finite, jointly surjective, flat families. This
  gives:
  - (i) the description of fpqc coverings;
  - (ii) F is an fpqc sheaf iff it is a Zariski sheaf and F(S) → F(S') ⇉ F(S' ×_S S') is exact for S' → S
    faithfully flat with S, S' affine;
  - (iii) faithfully flat quasi-compact morphisms, and families of flat open morphisms whose images cover, are
    coverings.

**Checked.**
- **Base change** (scan 14). U'_{αβλ} = U_{αβ} ×_{U_α} U'_{αλ} lies in C' by (b), and is T'-covering by base change
  in C'.
- **Transitivity** (scans 14 and 12). The tower U_{αβγδ} → U_{αβγ} → U_{αβ} → U_α is of type T', T, T', and (c)
  extracts a T'-covering V_{αλ} → U_α. These close the two axioms that matter; the isomorphism and saturation axioms
  are as written.
- **(c) for Prop. 2.** Composites of flat maps are flat, affines are quasi-compact so finitely many Zariski pieces
  suffice, and the union of the images is U.
- **(b) for affines.** "Produits fibrés" must be read as fibre products of diagrams of affines, which are affine.
  U ×_X V for an arbitrary X is not affine in general: take the plane with a doubled origin.
- **Cor. 2.** The margin argument, that the families of universal F-descent form a topology containing T and T', is
  correct (SGA 4 II; to verify). To see that T'-coverings are of universal F-descent along an arbitrary X → U, cover
  X by affines and use the T-sheaf property.
- **(ii)** is the standard criterion (Stacks 023Q, to verify).
- **(iii)** "pas de problèmes": for an affine U ⊂ X, finitely many affine opens of the sources, or of f_i^{-1}(U),
  have images covering U.

### Scans 18-24: Proposition (canonical factorisation, "B")

**On the page (scan 18).**
- f: X → Y is finite and universally open, Y locally noetherian, and d(y), the number of geometric points of
  f^{-1}(y), is locally constant.
- Then X → X_0 → Y with X → X_0 finite, surjective and radicial, and X_0 → Y finite étale.
- Every Y-morphism X → Z with Z étale over Y factors uniquely through X_0. The page writes X'_0 in the
  factorisation and X' in the universal property: one object.
- Heading A, "comparaison avec les revêtements étales holomorphes", is announced and not written.

**The proof.**
- **Scans 19 and 21**, a first draft that is struck through: the locus Y'' ⊂ Y' = X^I where I × Spec k(y') →
  X'_{y'} is bijective, said to be open and closed.
- **Scan 20, Lemme.** If f has a section s, then s(Y) is open and closed; hence X ×_Y X = X_(1) ⊔ X^(1) with
  degrees 1 and n − 1.
- **Scan 22, Lemme 2.** Iterating, a finite surjective Y' → Y splits X' = X ×_Y Y' into n pieces of separable
  degree 1.
- **Descent of Y' × I.** The trivial cover Y' × I has a descent datum along Y' → Y, which gives the étale cover
  X_0.
- **Lemma 4.** An étale Y* splitting X_0 gives X* = ⊔ pieces radicial over Y*. The canonical X* → Y* × I descends
  to X → X_0.
- **The universal property** reduces to X* → Y* finite, radicial and surjective.

**Wrong: the step "Y irréductible ⇒ les composantes irréductibles de X sont disjointes" (scan 20).**
- The page flags it "[vérif]". It is false when Y is irreducible but not geometrically unibranch.
- **Counterexample.** Take Y the nodal cubic and X its connected étale double cover: two copies of P^1 glued at
  two points into a cycle. X → Y is finite étale and d ≡ 2. Add a section: X ⊔ Y → Y is finite étale with d ≡ 3,
  and its irreducible components still meet.
- The **Lemma itself is true**. Re-derived (CHECK_EVENTS):
  1. Over O^sh_{Y,y}, X^sh splits into d(y) local pieces, one for each geometric point of f^{-1}(y).
  2. Each piece is finite and universally open over the connected Spec O^sh, so it is surjective.
  3. Over every point of Spec O^sh there are exactly d = n geometric points, so each piece contributes exactly one
     and is radicial, hence homeomorphic to Spec O^sh.
  4. Going down for the flat X^sh → X then shows that every generisation of s(y) lies in s(Y).
  5. s(Y) is closed, because finite maps are separated. A closed set stable under generisation in a locally
     noetherian space is open.
- The page's argument is valid for Y geometrically unibranch, for instance normal (CORRECTIONS row 3).
- Michel Hua's project supplies a justification "par la chute de d", flagged there as not written in detail. On the
  nodal cubic the number of geometric points does not drop, so that route also needs unibranchness. The
  strict-henselisation proof above needs nothing.

**A non-flat descent step used silently (scan 22).**
- "Il est trivial que X'_0 = Y' × I est muni d'une donnée de descente ... d'où un rev. étale X_0": the descent
  datum is trivial, as the page says.
- Its effectivity is not trivial: Y' → Y is finite and surjective but not flat. It is the descent of finite étale
  covers along finite surjective morphisms of finite presentation (SGA 1 IX 4.12, to verify). Michel Hua's project
  cites SGA 1 IX §4 "de mémoire".
- So cote 126 already uses non-flat descent, the subject of cote 127.

**Checked.**
- **Lemma 2:** the iteration keeps every Y_k → Y_{k−1} finite, surjective and universally open.
- **Lemma 4:** an open-and-closed piece of X'* compatible with the trivial descent datum is the preimage of an
  open-and-closed piece of X*, because X'* → X* is surjective and universally open, hence a quotient map.
- **The universal property** for every étale Z, not only finite ones: X → X_0 is a universal homeomorphism, so
  their étale sites agree (SGA 1 IX 4.10; EGA IV 18.1.2; to verify).

**Open on the page.**
- **(a) Scan 24, "À généraliser".** f proper and universally open, with the number of geometric connected
  components of the fibres locally constant. This is the representability of π_0(X/Y) by a finite étale Y-scheme.
  Known in the literature for flat proper morphisms of finite presentation with this local constancy (Romagny,
  "Composantes connexes et irréductibles en familles", manuscripta math. 2011; EGA IV 15.6; to verify).
- **(b) Scan 24, "Question: Caractérisation des foncteurs représentables par des rev. étales ???"** Cote 131 answers
  it in a stronger form. Its Corollaire 1, on scan 14 of cote 131, says F is representable by an étale separated
  X iff 1°-4° and 6° hold and F is étale on artinian arguments (sweep 3 SA17 STUDY, SA17-14). Finite étale adds
  properness through the valuative existence part.

### Scans 27-30: Proposition: a descent datum given on a dense open extends (the scheme form of Weil's theorem)

**On the page.**
- **Setting.** S noetherian, T/S flat of finite type, U ⊂ T×_S T and V ⊂ T×_S T×_S T dense opens with V ⊂ ∩
  p_{ij}^{-1}(U). F is a fibred category over S-schemes of finite type in which (reading uncertain) the flat
  surjective morphisms of finite type are of F-descent.
- **Data.** X ∈ F_T (the page writes F_S, a slip), and φ: X ×_S T ≅ T ×_S X over U satisfying the cocycle over V.
- **Proposition.** There are a dense open T' ⊂ T and a descent datum φ' on X|T' with φ' = φ over U ∩ (T' × T').
- **Corollary.** If every such T' → S' is of strict F-descent, X|T' comes from some Y ∈ F_{S'}.

**The proof, re-derived.**
1. Make U and V invariant under the symmetric groups. This is legitimate: from the cocycle at (a, b, c) and at
   (b, a, c), φ_{ab}φ_{ba} = id.
2. The fibre-density lemma ("cf. un lemme qui vient plus bas", "La démonstration des lemmes est facile"), checked
   here:
   - for a flat morphism of finite type and a dense open V, the fibres meeting V densely include all generic points
     of the target, because a dense open contains every generic point and flat maps send generic points of fibres
     over generic points to generic points;
   - the locus is constructible (EGA IV 9.5, to verify), so it contains a dense open.
3. W = {(t_1, t', t_2): (t_1, t'), (t', t_2) ∈ U} → T × T is flat, and surjective by fibre density. So it is of
   F-descent. ψ = φ_{t_2t'}φ_{t't_1} on W descends to φ' once it is compatible over W ×_{T×T} W.
4. The compatibility is pulled back to W' ⊂ T^5. There
   φ_{t_2t'}φ_{t't_1} = φ_{t_2t'''}φ_{t'''t_1} = φ_{t_2t''}φ_{t''t_1}, by the cocycle at the four triples
   (t_1, t''', t'), (t''', t', t_2), (t_1, t''', t''), (t''', t'', t_2).
5. The cocycle for φ' is pulled back to W''' ⊂ T^6, and φ' = φ over U through V → U (p_13), which is onto by
   fibre density.

**Wrong: the fifth triple in W' (scan 28; decided on the image).**
- The ink adds, small and late at the line end, "(t''', t_1, t_2) ∈ V" to the four triples.
- It is not used in the identity of scan 29. It forces (t_1, t_2) ∈ U, since V ⊂ p_23^{-1}(U). Then W' → W ×_{T×T}
  W is not onto over the points with (t_1, t_2) ∉ U, which are exactly the points the construction exists to
  reach. With it the lemma "W' → W ×_{T×_S T} W est surjectif" is false.
- With the four triples the map is onto: four dense opens of a fibre meet.
- So the fifth triple must be deleted (CORRECTIONS row 4). Michel Hua's project keeps all five triples without
  comment.

**Slip in W''' (scan 29; decided on the image).**
- The ink reads "(t_1,t'), (t',t_2), (t_2,t'''), (t''',t_3) ∈ U, et (t_1,t',t'''), (t''',t'',t_3) ∈ V".
- Scan 30 computes φ'_{t_3t_2} through t'', so the pairs must be (t_2, t''), (t'', t_3). Then (t''', t_3) is
  already implied by V.
- With that list the computation of scan 30 is right, and W''' → T^3 is onto by successive choice of t', t'' and
  t''' in dense opens (CORRECTIONS row 5). Michel Hua's project notes the same exchange of t'' and t'''.

**Open reading.**
- Scan 29's insertion "W ×_{T×_S T} W ≃ T^4": the sign is ambiguous on the image, between ≃ and ⊆. The mathematics
  needs ⊂, as scan 28 itself writes "W ×_{T×_S T} W ⊂ T ×_S T ×_S T ×_S T".
- Michel Hua's project reads a "sauf erreur" at this surjectivity. Our transcription does not have it. Left open.

**Known.** Weil's theorem on rational descent data and birational group laws (Weil, "The field of definition of a
variety", Amer. J. Math. 78, 1956; M. Artin, SGA 3 XVIII; to verify). The page's version for an arbitrary fibred
category with flat descent is the scheme-theoretic form.

### Scans 32-35: "Question de la possibilité de descente par un morphisme fid. plat non fini"

**On the page.**
- T → S is finite étale Galois with group G, s a closed point with f^{-1}(s) of n points, t one of them, and
  T' = T − (f^{-1}(s) − {t}). T' → S is faithfully flat, étale and not finite.
- T' ×_S T' = Δ(T') ⊔ ⊔_{g≠e} U'_g with U'_g ≅ T'' = T − f^{-1}(s). T'^3 = ⊔ V'_{g,g'}, all but V'_{e,e} ≅ T''.
- Conclusion: a descent datum on X/T' for T' → S is the same as a descent datum on X|T'' for T'' → S'' = S − s,
  that is, G acting on X|T'' compatibly with its action on T''.
- If X is quasi-projective, this is an isomorphism X|T'' ≅ X_0 ×_{S''} T''.
- Effectivity becomes: given X'_0 on S − s and an extension X of X'_0 ×_{S''} T'' across t, quasi-projective over
  T', show that X comes from an X_0 over S. The page ends there, unanswered. Michel Hua's project: "Le dossier
  s'arrête sur cette question".

**Checked.**
- φ_g^{-1}(U_g ∩ T'^2) = g^{-1}T' ∩ T', which equals T'' for every g ≠ e only when no g ≠ e fixes t. That is,
  s must be totally split, so n must be the order of G (CORRECTIONS row 6). Otherwise U'_g ≅ T' for g in the
  stabiliser of t.
- The decomposition of T'^3 and the reduction of the cocycle to the pieces ≅ T'' are as written.

**Reduction, mine.**
- When s is totally split, T' ×_S {s} = {t} with k(t) = k(s). So (T'' ⊂ T', S'' ⊂ S) is an elementary distinguished
  (Nisnevich) square.
- The question is therefore Nisnevich gluing. The data (X'_0 on S'', X on T', the isomorphism over T'') always glue
  to an algebraic space X_0 over S, separated if X and X'_0 are.
- X_0 is a scheme, so the descent is effective:
  - when X → T' is affine, by fpqc descent of affine morphisms (SGA 1 VIII 2.1);
  - more generally when X → T' is quasi-affine (SGA 1 VIII 7.9, to verify).
- For quasi-projective X what remains open is whether X_0 is a scheme, for instance whether an ample sheaf can be
  glued. The page's example isolates exactly this: the descent datum carries no information at t. Cf. Moret-Bailly,
  "Un problème de descente", Bull. SMF 124 (1996), to verify.

## SA17-29 · cote 127: non-flat descent

K127-01 (scans 2-13) and K127-02 (scans 14-24). The cover (scan 2) reads "Application de la descente non plate :

### Scans 7, 8, 10, 11, 13: Th. 1.8 ("lemme de Hironaka")

### Scans 4-6: "Formules de Néron": the local symbol as intersection plus a correction in (1/m)Z

**On the page.**
- **Setting.** A_K an abelian variety over K = Frac(V), V a DVR (residue field finite "?").
- **The correction v(X, a) ∈ Q,** for X ∈ D_a (divisors algebraically equivalent to 0) and a ∈ Z_0^* (0-cycles
  of degree 0) with disjoint supports. It is fixed by four properties:
  - (i) bilinear;
  - (ii) translation-invariant;
  - (iii) for X = div f, v = Σ i(div_{s_0} f̄ · ā), the vertical part of div f̄ on the Néron model met with the
    closure of a;
  - (iv) bounded in x for X fixed.
- **Further properties.**
  - (iv bis) v depends on x only modulo the identity component.
  - (v) v ∈ Z if X ∈ D_ℓ or a ∈ Z_ℓ^*.
  - (vi) m·v ∈ Z, where m is the order of the component group A_0/A_0^0.
- **Hence a pairing.** A^*(K) × A(K) → Z/mZ.
- **(vii)** (X, a)_v = v(X, a) + Σ i(X̄ · ā). So the local symbol lies in (1/m)Z.
- **Cor.** v(X_a, b) = v(X_b^−, a).
- **The global case.** For discrete valuations with the product formula, Σ_v (X, a)_v gives A^*(K) × A(K) → (1/m)Z,
  "la forme de Néron-Tate".

**Checked.**
- **Uniqueness of v.** Two solutions differ by δ, which vanishes on D_ℓ. For a fixed class, x ↦ δ((x) − (0)) is a
  homomorphism A(K) → Q by (ii) and bilinearity, and it is bounded by (iv), so it is 0.
- **(vii) agrees with (iii) on D_ℓ.** v(f(a)) = i(div f̄ · ā) = i(X̄ · ā) + i(div_{s_0} f̄ · ā) on the smooth,
  hence regular, Néron model.
- **The margin "si m = 1 ... v = 0".** When A_0 is connected, vertical divisors are multiples of A_0, and
  i(A_0 · ā) = deg a = 0. Then 0 satisfies (i)-(iv), so v = 0 by uniqueness.
- **The global pairing is Q-valued** with denominators bounded by the component-group orders, when only discrete
  places occur (function fields). This is the structure of Shioda's height formula with its local terms
  contr_v ∈ (1/m_v)Z (Shioda, Comment. Math. Univ. St. Paul. 39, 1990; to verify).
- **Known.** The pairing A^*(K) × A(K) → Z/mZ through component groups is Grothendieck's pairing on the component
  groups of Néron models (SGA 7 I, exposé IX; to verify). (iv bis), (vi) and the Cor. are stated, not proved, on
  the page. They are consistent with that pairing.

### Scan 7: Pic^0 of a regular model is a Néron model

**On the page.** For X → S proper with X and S regular, if the schematic closure A of Pic^0_{X_η} in Pic_{X/S} is
flat (smooth), then A is a Néron model. The reason given: X ×_S S' is regular for S' smooth, so divisors on the
generic fibre extend by closure. The margin warns "Attention s'il n'y a pas de section locale (ét.) de X sur S".

**Checked.**
- **Existence of extensions.** Pic(X') → Pic(X'_η) is onto for regular X'. A section from S' flat over S lands in
  the closure, because S'_η is schematically dense.
- **Two points needed, and noted.**
  - Pic(X')/Pic(S') = Pic_{X/S}(S') needs a section étale-locally, which is the margin's own caution.
  - Uniqueness needs A separated.
- "sous-schéma en groupes plat de X" should read "de Pic_{X/S}" (slip, CORRECTIONS row 17).
- **Known.** Raynaud, "Spécialisation du foncteur de Picard", Publ. IHÉS 38 (1970); BLR, Néron Models §9.5; to verify.

### Scans 10-15: Picard numbers through a pencil, and the height form on the mobile part

**On the page.**
- **Setting.** X is a smooth projective surface. A pencil of hyperplane sections has base locus Z, X̃ is the
  blow-up of Z, and X̃ → P^1.
- **Kernels.**
  - The kernel of Pic(X̃) → Pic(X̃_η̄) consists of the vertical classes.
  - If all members are integral, it is generated by ξ̃_0 = φ^*ξ_0 − Σ z̃.
  - Hence Pic(X) → Pic(Y_η̄) is injective.
- **Fixed part.** Pic^0(X) ≅ J^fixe(k), where J^fixe is the K/k-trace.
- **The mobile part.** NS(X̃)' → J'(k(η)), J' = J/J^fixe, has kernel of rank 1 and is onto modulo a finite group.
  The page argues through H^1(K, J'') torsion and Tsen.
- **The boxed formula.** ρ(X̃) − 2 = ρ(X) + z − 2 = ρ(J'), "z = nb de sections singulières du pinceau", valid if
  all members are irreducible (integrality is not needed).
- **Scan 15.** A positive-definite Néron-Tate form Q on J'(k(η)). The formula "Q(i(ξ), i(η)) = −ξ·η" is marked
  "À prouver" and "?", "valable si les Y_t sont tous intègres ?".

**Checked.**
- **Injectivity of Pic(C) → Pic(C_K̄)** for a geometrically integral curve, by Hilbert 90.
- **The vertical classes.** If all fibres are irreducible, the vertical classes are multiples of the fibre class.
  A multiple fibre m·C gives the class C, still of rank 1, so "irréductibles" suffices, as the page itself corrects.
  ξ̃_0 ∉ φ^*Pic(X), because Pic(X̃) = φ^*Pic(X) ⊕ ⊕Z·z̃.
- **"Surjectif mod groupe fini".** J'(K) is finitely generated (Lang-Néron), so a torsion cokernel is finite. Br(k(t)) = 0
  (Tsen) gives Pic(Y_η) = Pic_{Y_η}(K). Divisors extend by closure.
- **The boxed formula (decided on the image).** The first term is ρ(X̃) − 2, a 2 written over a 1. The
  transcription's "ρ(X̃) − 1" must read −2 (CORRECTIONS row 18).
  - rank NS(X̃)' = ρ(X̃) − 1, and the kernel has rank 1, so ρ(J') = ρ(X̃) − 2. This is the Shioda-Tate formula with
    irreducible fibres.
- **The definition of z is wrong (checked on the image).**
  - The ink reads "nb de sections singulières du pinceau". But ρ(X̃) = ρ(X) + card Z, so the middle term needs
    z = card Z, the number of base points (Y_0² = the degree of X), not the number of singular members.
  - With that, ρ(J') = ρ(X) + deg X − 2 when all members are irreducible (CORRECTIONS row 19).
- **Scan 15's "?" is answered yes, up to the normalisation of Q.**
  - For ξ ∈ NS(X)', φ^*ξ is orthogonal to the fibre class (ξ·Y_0 = 0) and to every exceptional curve z̃. These are
    sections of X̃ → P^1. So φ^*ξ lies in the orthogonal complement of the trivial lattice.
  - Shioda's formula then gives ⟨i(ξ), i(η)⟩ = −ξ·η when all fibres are irreducible. Otherwise there are correction
    terms, which are the local terms v of scans 4-6.
  - Normalisation: the Néron-Tate pairing of the canonical principal polarisation (Shioda 1990; for higher genus
    fibrations Shioda 1999; to verify).

### Scans 17, 19, 22, 26, 28, 30, 32: the local symbol of two cycles, i(C·D) on a regular model

**On the page.**
- **Scan 17.** For X/S projective and regular over a DVR, with X_s geometrically integral, take horizontal cycles C
  and D of complementary dimension, algebraically equivalent to 0 on X_η. Then i(C·D) depends only on C_η and D_η.
  Wanted: a symbol ⟨C_η, D_η⟩_v independent of the model or of its existence.
- **Scan 19.** The same for k cycles, with the properties (i) multilinearity, (ii) invariance, (iibis) transposition,
  (iii) normalisation by v(f(·)), (iv) continuity, (V) base extension.
- **Scan 22.** Globally, −⟨D_η, C_η⟩ = D·C, as a sum of local terms. Locally, with X_0 smooth except at one
  point where X is regular, the boxed ⟨C_η, D_η⟩_v = i(C·D).
- **The proof, scans 26-32.**
  - Through the Albanese α: X → A, with good reduction and a section. C' = α_*C, and the projection formula gives
    i(C·α^*D') = i(C'·D').
  - D = α^*(D') + div f, with "Lemme : dégager un" for moving D'.
  - This reduces to ⟨C_η, div f_η⟩_v = i(C·div f) = v(f_η(C_η)).
  - Scan 32 starts the general case through the Néron model of Alb X_η on X − x_0.

**Checked.**
- The projection formula step.
- The final reduction. i(C·div f) = Σ v(f(c_i)) for C horizontal meeting div f properly. A vertical part n·X_0 of
  div f adds n·deg C_η = 0, since X_0 is irreducible and C_η has degree 0. So the margin condition on components of
  div f is not needed in this setting.
- The sign convention: the local symbols are local intersection numbers, and the global height is minus their sum.
  This agrees with scan 15.
- **Open.** The moving lemma ("dégager"), the general case at the singular point x_0, and the continuity
  principle (iv) of scan 19.
- **Known.** Bloch's height pairing and Beilinson's local heights for homologically trivial cycles (Bloch,
  Contemp. Math. 1984; Beilinson 1987; Gillet-Soulé; to verify). Deligne's pairing for several line bundles is the
  multilinear analogue of scan 19 (Deligne 1987; to verify).

**Checked.**
- M_K̄/Γ = M_K: extensions of an absolute value to a normal extension are conjugate (Bourbaki AC VI §8; to verify).
  X(K̄)^Γ = X(K^{p^{−∞}}).
- The radicial and quasi-Galois cases of scan 37.
- **The Question of scan 38: yes.**
  - If F × M_K̄ is M_K-bounded in P^r, each (P, v) lies in one of finitely many E(U_j; x; c_j).
  - So the local heights satisfy h_v(P) ≤ max_j max(0, −c_j(v)), which is 0 outside a finite S.
  - So h(P) is bounded (cf. Lang, Fundamentals of Diophantine Geometry, Ch. 10; to verify).
- **The point "Admettons ce point" (scan 42), proved here.**
  - Refine the cover of affine X to principal opens D(g_i) with Σ a_i g_i = 1. For (P, v) in a bounded set, some i
    has v(g_i(P)) ≤ −v(a_i) up to a bounded term at the finitely many archimedean or bad places.
  - So 1/g_i is bounded below there, and (P, v) lies in a bounded part of D(g_i). Hence Φ_X is a sheaf.
- **The admissibility of φ_f.** Units on an affine open are polynomials in bounded coordinates, and so are their
  inverses.
- **The kernels.**
  - Φ_X → QF_X is injective.
  - Ker(R_X^* → QF_X) consists of the units g with every g(P) an M_K̄-unit. A non-constant g takes cofinitely many

    M_K-units.
- Scan 44 writes "noyau de R_X^× → Φ_X^*" for R_X^× → QF_X (slip, CORRECTIONS row 20).
- **The quasi-projective claim** is Néron's existence of Weil functions for divisors (Néron, Ann. of Math. 82, 1965;
  Lang, Fundamentals, Ch. 10; to verify). The page reduces it correctly to O(1) on P^r, where
  −log max_i |x_i/x_j|_v is a Weil function.

### Scans 6-9: the 2-category (C, D, φ) and "Teichmüller groups with holes"

**On the page.**
- **The model.** A surface with boundary is modelled by C = Cat G, with G = π_1, and D = ⊔_{i∈I} Cat Z, the
  boundary loops h_i ∈ G.
- **π_0(End)** is the group of outer automorphisms ṽ of G sending each h_i to a conjugate of some h_j.
- **π_1(End)** is 0, except for the annulus (Z, not canonically) and the disc (Z, canonically).
- **With D fixed,** there is an exact sequence 0 → Z^I → TS ("Teichmüller spécial") → T ("Teichmüller à trous")
  → S_I → 1. Ker ψ = Z^I in general, Z (by the sum) for the annulus, and TS = 0 for the disc.

**Checked.**
- The centraliser of a boundary element h_j is ⟨h_j⟩: G is free of rank 2g + |I| − 1, and boundary elements are
  primitive. The disc is the exception (G = 1).
- **"h_i, h_j non conjugués (... leurs images dans G_ab sont distinctes !)".**
  - In H_1 the [h_i] satisfy exactly one relation, Σ[h_i] = 0.
  - For |I| = 2, [h_1] = [h_2] would force 2[h_1] = 0, hence [h_1] = 0, which is false. For |I| ≥ 3 they are
    distinct elements of a basis plus their negated sum.
- The centres and the λ_i in the three cases.
- The annulus: (λ_1, λ_2) ~ (λ_1 + μ, λ_2 − μ), so Ker ψ ≅ Z by the sum. In mapping-class terms, the two boundary
  twists of the annulus coincide.
- **Known.** π_0(End) is the Dehn-Nielsen-Baer description of the mapping class group of a bordered surface. The
  kernel Z^I is generated by the boundary Dehn twists, the capping sequence (Farb-Margalit, A Primer on Mapping
  Class Groups, §3.6 and §8.2; to verify).

## Hunches

1. **Step 3 of cote 131 is settled by quasi-affineness for quasi-compact pieces; the locally quasi-finite case needs
   one more idea.**
   - Grounds:
     - 126 scans 32-35 show that a descent datum along a non-finite map carries no information at the missing
       points. Effectivity is a gluing question.
     - For X separated and quasi-finite over Ŝ, X is quasi-affine (EGA IV 18.12.12), and fpqc descent is effective
       (SGA 1 VIII 7.9; to verify).
     - For locally quasi-finite X, glue as an algebraic space and use "separated, locally quasi-finite algebraic
       spaces are schemes" (Stacks, to verify). The obstacle is that fpqc descent of algebraic spaces is not always
       effective. Here, though, the functor F is given over S, so only representability needs descending.
   - Link: cote 131 SA17-15, step 3.
