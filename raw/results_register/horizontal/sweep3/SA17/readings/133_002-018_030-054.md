### Scan 3: an associativity obstruction (scratch on the institute's letterhead)

**On the page:**

  "q' → q'' = q'''|q'''' = −1" and "q''' = q' ∘ q'' ∘ 1"; Michel Hua's project reads "q' + q'' + q''' = −1" and

  blot; not decided. Nothing on the sheet uses these relations.
- A product p: L•⊗L• → L and the associativity square through a: (L•⊗L•)⊗L• → L•⊗(L•⊗L•).
- An arrow δ: Ext(π₀,π₀;π₁) → Ext¹(π₀,π₀,π₀;π₁). **Image:** the superscript of δ is "(1)" (the Pro transcription
  left 1|2 open; Michel Hua's project reads (1)).
- The pentagon on x, y, z, t, with a double-headed vertical arrow between (x⊗y)⊗(z⊗t) and x⊗(y⊗(z⊗t)).

**My reading** (the page defines none of the groups; Michel Hua's project gives the same reading, credited):
L• is a two-term complex with homology π₀ (degree 0) and π₁ (degree 1); p is associative up to homotopy. The two
composites p(p⊗1) and p(1⊗p)a agree on π₀ and on π₁, so their difference is a class in Ext¹(π₀⊗π₀⊗π₀, π₁).
Changing p by a class of Ext¹(π₀⊗π₀, π₁) moves it by the Hochschild-type differential δ⁽¹⁾. The pentagon is the
next coherence condition.

**Checked:** for complexes K, L concentrated in degrees 0 and 1, with K K-flat, a morphism in the derived category that
induces zero on H₀ and H₁ comes from Ext¹(H₀K, H₁L). With K = L•^{⊗3} and H₀K = π₀^{⊗3} (right exactness of ⊗), the
associativity defect is a class in Ext¹(π₀^{⊗3}, π₁) as soon as the two composites agree on H₁. The class depends on
p only through δ⁽¹⁾ of the change of p.

**Open:** the page writes no statement; what the obstruction is for is not said. Michel Hua's project links the
pattern (two invariants, a constraint, a class one degree up) to the Gr-categories of cote 135 and to scans 46-54,
credited. Scan 46 has the same pattern: "Obstruction dans H³, indétermination dans H²".

### Scan 4: the Stiefel-Whitney classes of a hyperbolic plane

**On the page:**
- P' = W*(L^{⊗2}); E = L + L⁻¹ with Q(x,x') = xx'; "L_{P'}^{⊗2} ≃ O_{P'}" gives η ∈ H¹(P', F₂); η² + w₁η + w₂ = 0.
- "Je dis que w₁ = α := ∂(−1), w₂ ≡ c₁ mod 2", with ∂: H⁰(S, G_m) → H¹(S, μ₂). **Image:** the sign is "≡"
  (three strokes; the Pro transcription left ≡|= open).
- ∏ᵢ(1 + α + ξᵢ), and (1 + y)(1 + y + α) = 1 + α + y(y + α), with "c₁ ?" under y(y + α), joined by a vertical
  equality sign.
- E₂^{pq} = Ext_B^p(Tor_q^A(M, B), N) for A → B, M, N.
- A small sketch and a shopping list (Wednesday 22: épicerie 15,20; pain 12,30; beurre, bananes, œufs 9,40;
  "J. et D.": bus 22). Not mathematics; placed.

**Checked (my re-derivation; it settles his "c₁ ?"):** assume 2 invertible and Stiefel-Whitney classes in étale
cohomology with the Whitney formula and rank-one normalisation (the inputs of scan 9).
1. P' is the scheme of non-isotropic lines of H(L) = (L ⊕ L⁻¹, xx'). A line other than L and L⁻¹ is the graph of an
   isomorphism u: L → L⁻¹, a nowhere-zero section of L^{⊗−2}; inverting u identifies this with W*(L^{⊗2}). The
   tautological line N = graph(u) ≅ L_{P'} carries the form x·u(x), a trivialisation of N^{⊗2}. That is his
   "L_{P'}^{⊗2} ≃ O_{P'}", and its μ₂-torsor class is η.
2. N^⊥ = graph(−u), with form −x·u(x), so N^⊥ ≅ N ⊗ ⟨−1⟩ has class η + α. The Whitney formula on P' gives
   w(H(L)) = (1 + η)(1 + η + α). So w₁ = α (true: H(L) is locally ⟨1, −1⟩), w₂ = η(η + α), which is his y(y + α)
   with y = η, and η² + w₁η + w₂ = 0 holds mod 2.
3. **w₂(H(L)) = c₁(L) mod 2**, meaning the Kummer image ∂[L] ∈ H²(S, μ₂) of [L] ∈ Pic(S). The class η maps to
   [L_{P'}] in Pic(P'). The composite H¹(μ₂) → H¹(G_m) → H²(μ₂) is the coboundary δ₄ of the pulled-back extension
   1 → μ₂ → μ₄ → μ₂ → 1 (squaring). As an extension of Z/2 by Z/2, μ₄ is the Baer sum of Z/4 (coboundary η ↦ η²)
   and of the extension twisted by the cyclotomic class α (coboundary η ↦ αη). So δ₄(η) = η² + αη = η(η + α).
   - Test 1: over a field every class is a Kummer class (a); it lifts to the μ₄-Kummer class of a, so δ₄ = 0, and
     indeed (a)(a) = (a)(−1).
   - Test 2: over an algebraically closed field α = 0 and δ₄ = Sq¹.

   Hence w₂(H(L)) = ∂[L] after pulling back to P'. The pull-back is injective on H*(−, μ₂): P' is the G_m-torsor of
   L^{⊗2}, and the Gysin sequence's connecting map is the cup product with ∂[L^{⊗2}] = 2∂[L] = 0. So
   w₂(H(L)) = c₁(L) mod 2 on S.
4. Consequence, matching his ∏ᵢ(1 + α + ξᵢ): for a vector bundle E of rank r with mod-2 Chern roots ξᵢ,
   w(H(E)) = ∏ᵢ(1 + α + ξᵢ) = Σ_k (1 + α)^{r−k} c̄_k(E). In particular w∘α ≠ 1. This is why "w∘α = 1" is struck on
   scan 9; Michel Hua's project made this link first, credited.
5. The E₂ term: from RHom_A(M, N) = RHom_B(M ⊗^L_A B, N), the hypercohomology spectral sequence has
   E₂^{pq} = Ext_B^p(Tor_q^A(M, B), N) ⇒ Ext_A^{p+q}(M, N). The abutment is not on the page. The Pro notes W15 and
   Michel Hua's project supply the same abutment, credited. Known: Cartan-Eilenberg, *Homological Algebra*, ch. XVI
   (to verify).

**Known:** the complex-analytic case of 3 is the Pro notes' W12 (credited). Michel Hua's project checks L trivial and
the complex case and leaves the étale statement unproved. The étale classes and their axioms: Laborde, "Classes de
Stiefel-Whitney en cohomologie étale", Mém. SMF 48 (1976) 47-51, §§II-III (locator from the Pro notes; to verify);
Delzant (1962) over fields (to verify).

### Scan 6: WG(S) and the λ-ring question

**On the page:**
- S a locally ringed topos. WG(S) is the group completion of the monoid of isomorphism classes of quadratic modules
  (locally free of finite rank) that are strictly non-degenerate (the polar bilinear form is non-degenerate).
- NB: WG(S) is a pre-λ-ring ("sans unité en général"), augmented to "the binomial ring" H⁰(S, Z).
- If S is connected and 2 is not invertible, the rank takes values in 2Z. If 2 is invertible, WG(S) has a unit.
- Even then he does not know whether WG(S) is a λ-ring, i.e. whether the universal formulas
  λⁱ(xy) = Pᵢ(x, …, λ^{i−1}x; y, …, λ^{i−1}y) and λⁱ(λʲx) = Q_{i,j}(x, λ²x, …, λ^{ij}x) hold.
  **Image (600 dpi):** both exponents read i−1.
- Reduction: the first formula can be tested on the classifying topos of O(m) × O(n), on the pull-backs of the
  universal modules; the second on B_{O(m)}. "D'où la tâche de déterminer WG(B_{O(m)})".

**Checked:**
- **Rank parity** (scheme case). At a point of residue characteristic 2 the polar form satisfies b(x, x) = 2q(x) = 0,
  so it is alternating and non-degenerate, of even rank. The rank is locally constant. On a locally ringed topos the
  argument needs a point of residue characteristic 2 (the Pro notes W04 and Michel Hua's project, credited).
- **The unit.** The unit form ⟨1⟩ has polar form 2xy, strictly non-degenerate exactly where 2 is invertible. If 2 is
  not invertible on a connected S, every rank is even, so no element has rank 1 and there is no unit.
- **The product without 2 invertible exists.** Lift q₁ to a bilinear β₁ with β₁(x, x) = q₁(x); then
  z ↦ (β₁ ⊗ b₂)(z, z) does not depend on the lift (alternating forms contribute zero), has polar form b₁ ⊗ b₂, and
  gives 2q₁(x)q₂(y) on x ⊗ y. Michel Hua's project states this, credited.

**Wrong:**
- **The pre-λ-structure needs 2 invertible.** On a connected S with a point of residue characteristic 2, his own
  parity statement forbids an augmented pre-λ-structure: the augmentation of a pre-λ-ring to the binomial ring
  satisfies ε(λ²x) = C(ε(x), 2). For x the hyperbolic plane this gives ε(λ²x) = 1, which is not in 2Z.
  Concretely, λ² of the hyperbolic plane has rank 1, and there is no strictly non-degenerate quadratic module of rank
  1 at a point of characteristic 2. Correction: the NB holds where 2 is invertible; without that, WG(S) is a
  non-unital ring with no exterior-power operations. Neither the Pro notes (W04 only raises the λ⁰ convention) nor
  Michel Hua's project says this.
- **The arguments of Pᵢ stop one short.** The universal polynomial needs λⁱ:
  P₂ = x²λ²y + y²λ²x − 2λ²xλ²y. Checked on sums of line elements: for x = a + b, y = c + d, both sides equal
  cd(a² + b²) + ab(c² + d²) + 2abcd. Michel Hua's project corrects the same slip, credited. Q_{i,j} is written in
  the standard form.

**The reduction:** sound in outline. The product identity λ_t(xy) = λ_t(x) * λ_t(y) is bi-additive in the ring
Λ(WG(S)), so effective classes suffice, and every pair of quadratic modules of ranks m, n is a pull-back of the
universal pair. That needs quadratic modules to be locally standard (forms locally ⟨1, …, 1⟩: O_S strictly local
with 2 invertible, the hypothesis he adopts on scan 9). Otherwise the classifying topos of the theory "rank-m
quadratic module" replaces B_{O(m)}.

**Known and open:**
- The page's WG(S) is the split group: no metabolic = hyperbolic relation. It agrees with the modern
  Grothendieck-Witt group GW(S) when S is affine and 2 is invertible, not in general (the Pro notes W02, Michel Hua's
  project).
- The λ-ring property is known for GW of fields of characteristic ≠ 2 (McGarraghy, cited from memory by Michel Hua's
  project; to verify) and for GW of schemes over such fields (Zibrowius 2015, NYJM 21, Introduction and Theorem;
  Zibrowius 2018, Theorem 2; Fasel-Haution 2025, Theorem 1 over Z[1/2]; locators from the Pro notes W03; to verify).
- **Open:** his question for the split WG(S) on non-affine S. See hunch H-133-1.

### Scan 7: the hyperbolic map and W(S)

**On the page:** α: K_s(S) → WG(S), E ↦ E ⊕ Ě with the evaluation form; K_s(S) is the "special" K-group (isomorphism
classes only). "Il n'est pas clair" that α factors through K(S), i.e. that φ(E) = φ(E') + φ(E'') for a non-split
extension. W(S) := Coker α. NB: if 2 is invertible, (M, q) ⊕ (M, −q) ≅ E ⊕ Ě with E = M, via the diagonal and the
antidiagonal.

**Checked:**
- **α does not factor through K(S)** (his doubt, answered in the negative). The forgetful map WG(S) → K_s(S),
  (M, q) ↦ [M], is additive and sends α(E) to [E] + [Ě]. On P¹_k, K_s is free on the [O(n)] (Grothendieck's
  splitting and Krull-Schmidt). α(O²) ↦ 4[O], while α(O(−1)) + α(O(1)) ↦ 2[O(−1)] + 2[O(1)]. Yet
  [O²] = [O(−1)] + [O(1)] in K(P¹) by 0 → O(−1) → O² → O(1) → 0. First found by the Pro notes W05 (another
  invariant on P¹) and by Michel Hua's project (Atiyah's F₂ on an elliptic curve), credited.
- **The NB.** Δ = {(x, x)} and ∇ = {(x, −x)} are isotropic, Δ ⊕ ∇ = M ⊕ M when 2 is invertible, and the polar
  form pairs them by 2b(x, y), perfectly.

### Scan 8: "Lazard dixit"; W(S) directly; the quotient

**On the page:** over an affine scheme, "il paraît (Lazard dixit)" that, without 2 invertible, (M, q) ⊕ (M, −q) is of
the form α(E); not so on every locally ringed topos, "p.ex. ... pour le topos classifiant B_{O(n)} si n ≥ 1". In
either case (2 invertible, or S affine) W(S) is the set of classes of quadratic modules modulo
(M, q) ∼ (M', q') ⇔ ∃ E, F with (M, q) ⊕ (M', −q') ⊕ α(F) ≃ α(E) (margin "à vérifier"). With a Witt theorem (a field)
one may take F = 0. α(K(S)) is an ideal, so W(S) is a ring, augmented to H⁰(S, F₂). Does the pre-λ-structure pass
to the quotient?

**Image (600 dpi): the name reads "Lazard", not "Laborde".**
- The third letter sits at x-height with a z-shaped descending curve. His b has a tall ascender (compare "bil." on
  scan 6, line 4).
- The word ends in a d with his usual tail and no final e.

The Pro transcription had Laborde|Lazard, Laborde first; Michel Hua's project reads Laborde. The page does not say which
Lazard. The mathematics below does not depend on the name.

**Checked:**
- **The affine statement holds for finite projective M over any ring.** Δ is a Lagrangian on which q ⊥ −q vanishes.
  Lift q to a bilinear β with β(y, y) = q(y) (possible on a projective module). Define g by b(x, g(y)) = −β(y, x).
  Then Λ = {(y + g(y), g(y))} is a complement of Δ on which the form vanishes, and the polar form pairs Δ and Λ
  perfectly, so (M, q) ⊥ (M, −q) ≅ H(M). Same proof in the Pro notes W06 and Michel Hua's project, credited.
- **"Pas vrai pour B_{O(n)}, n ≥ 1".** I read O(n) as the orthogonal group of the hyperbolic form of rank 2n over F₂;
  the page does not say.
  - n = 1 is the Pro notes' W07 (credited): C₂ swapping the coordinates of (F₂², xy). The fixed subspace of
    F = (M, q) ⊕ (M, q) carries the non-zero form (a + b)², but every C₂-hyperbolic H(E) of the same representation
    has zero form on its fixed subspace.
  - All n ≥ 1 (mine): let C₂ act on the first hyperbolic plane only. On the fixed subspace of F, the radical of the
    polar form is the 2-dimensional fixed part of the first summand, and q restricted to it is the non-zero linear
    form a + b. For any H(E) with E ⊕ Ě ≅ F as representations, E ≅ F₂[C₂] ⊕ (trivial), the radical is spanned by
    the two norm lines, and q vanishes there. An equivariant isometry would preserve q on that radical. Pulling back
    along B_{C₂} → B_{O(n)} gives the claim.
- **"À vérifier" holds.** Every class of W(S) is a single module: [M] − [M'] = [M ⊥ (M', −q')] − α(M'). Equality in
  the group completion means isomorphism after adding some X, and X ⊥ (X, −q_X) ≅ α(X) moves into the hyperbolic
  terms. Michel Hua's project checks the same, credited. With Witt cancellation over a field (to verify for
  characteristic 2: Elman-Karpenko-Merkurjev, Thm 8.4), F can be cancelled.
- **The ideal.** N ⊗ H(E) ≅ H(N ⊗ E): N ⊗ E and N ⊗ Ě are Lagrangians on which the product form of scan 6 vanishes,
  paired by b_N ⊗ ev. The ideal is the image of K_s(S); the page writes α(K(S)). That is notation, given scan 7 (the
  Pro notes W09 and Michel Hua's project read it so).
- **The quotient question: no.** For S ≠ ∅ with 2 invertible, λ²(α(O)) = λ²⟨1, −1⟩ = ⟨−1⟩ has rank 1, so it is
  non-zero in W(S) under the mod-2 rank, while α(O) = 0 there. First answered by the Pro notes W08 and Michel Hua's
  project, credited. By the scan-6 correction, the question only arises where 2 is invertible.
- Remark: where 2 is not invertible on a connected S, all ranks are even, and the augmentation to F₂ is zero.

### Scan 36: splitting statements, and a group seen through its centre and its derived group

**On the page:**
- **(I)** "Extension d'un p-divisible par un lisse (ou unipotent) est triviale".
- **Cor.** "Extension d'un p-divisible a-torique par un lisse (ou unipotent) est un produit".
- In brackets: every a-toric p-divisible subgroup is central; a p-divisible a-toric quotient is abelian, with a largest
  a-toric p-divisible subgroup, necessarily central; the properties of the largest such subgroups are still to be
  formulated. NB: for a formal group to be a-toric, its linear representation must be unipotent.
- **Rem.** π₀ = (G_ab)/Im δ has no a-toric part, i.e. is linear.
- **(II)** an extension of a smooth formal group by a p-divisible one is a product, "à isogén. près du moins".
  - **Cor.** DG ∩ Z(G) is quasi-unipotent, "i.e. annulé par une puiss. de p", and even Cent(Dér G) is.
  - The bracket's argument: the p-divisible part T of Cent Dér G lies in Z(G), G ≃ T × G/T, so DG ∩ T = {e}.
  - **Cor.** G smooth is, up to isogeny, built from "la pure partie a-torique" and a linear|fibré group, with a
    question mark.
  - **Question:** is DG ∩ Z(G) unipotent?
- **"Solution"**: the diagram 1 → π₁ → L₁ → L₀ → π₀ → 1, with D₁ →δ D₀ below and θ: D₀ → Aut D₁.
  - p^∞π₁ = e and at(π₀) = {e}.
  - L₁ = Z(G), D₁ = D(G), L₀ = G/Z(G), D₀ = G/D(G), π₁ = L₁ ∩ D₁, π₀ = G/L₁D₁.
  - D₁^{D₀} = π₁ and D_{1,D₀} = {e}.
  - G is to be rebuilt from π₁, Im d × Im δ and π₀, through D' = D₁ ⊔_{π₁} L₁ and L' = L₀ ×_{π₀} D₀.

- **The dévissage.** ψ_G is injective iff ψ_{0G} is injective on ζ₀ and ψ̄ is injective on ζ/ζ₀.
- **What b) expresses.** Ker ψ_{0G} = (Z(G) ∩ NN')/N', so ψ_{0G} injective ⇔ N' = H ∩ Z(G), not N' = Cent(H).
  Michel Hua's project made this correction, credited.
- **The cancellation is right.** N' = Cent(H) is not necessary for N' = Z(G): take G = S₃, N = A₃, N' = 1 (the Pro
  notes G17, credited; re-derived: Z(S₃) = 1, Cent(A₃) = A₃).

**Scans 53-54 (pp. 6-7), on the page:**
- λ_G: M' × M' → η, (x, y) ↦ [x̄, ȳ] mod N₀ (lifts in G).
- Zero when x̄ or ȳ lies in d'(N'), so λ_G: π₀ ⊗ π₀ → η = (N_comm)_M, alternating.
- "On a évidemment N/DG ≃ η/λ_G(π₀ ⊗ π₀)", so N = DG ⇔ λ_G onto.
- λ_{G₁} = λ_G + βc_E with β: π₁ → η.
- η₀ = Coker(π₁ → η); λ₀: π₀ ⊗ π₀ → η₀ is independent of G and built in K.
- b) λ₀ onto, "car signifie aussi que N/π₁ dans K ⊂ M × M' est = DK, ou encore que dN = DM exactement", margin
  "Vérifier".
- P = Ker λ₀; λ̄_G: P → π₁* = Im(π₁ → η), drawn with a double head; c) λ̄_G onto;
  λ̄_{G₁} = λ̄_G + p c_E i.

**Checked:**
- **λ_G.** M' is commutative, so [x̄, ȳ] ∈ N. Changing a lift by n ∈ N changes it by an element of [G, N] = N₀, so λ_G
  is well defined. It is bilinear and alternating modulo N₀. It vanishes on d'(N') because N' is central.
  - N₀ = [G, N] ⊃ [N, N], so η = (N_ab)_M (scan 53: "(N_comm)_M").
  - Scan 53's lifts must be taken in G, since M' = G/N. The overwritten G|N of the Pro doubts is G.
- **N/DG ≃ η/λ_G(π₀ ⊗ π₀):** DG ⊃ N₀, and DG/N₀ is generated by the λ_G(x, y). The Pro notes G18 and Michel Hua's
  project have the same, credited.
- **λ_{G₁} = λ_G + βc_E:** the commutators multiply as for ψ.
- **"Vérifier" holds.** λ₀ onto ⇔ N = DG·π₁ ⇔ N/π₁ = DK ⇔ dN = DM. The last step: NN' = DG·N', intersected with N,
  gives N = DG·(N ∩ N') by the modular law, since DG ⊂ N. The Pro notes G19 and Michel Hua's project agree, credited.
- **c) is a real condition.** Given b), λ_G is onto iff λ_G(P) = π₁*. The double-headed arrow is not automatic: for
  G = C_p × C_p and N = N' = C_p × 1, λ₀ is onto 0 but λ̄_G = 0 ≠ π₁* (the Pro notes G20, credited; re-derived).

**Open:**
- Which E satisfy the centre condition and c) together. Michel Hua's project lists this as never established.
- For discrete abelian π₀ and π₁ the problem is linear algebra: every alternating map Λ²π₀ → π₁ is some c_E, since
  H²(π₀, π₁) → Hom(Λ²π₀, π₁) is onto (universal coefficients, H₂(π₀) = Λ²π₀). One seeks an alternating c with
  ψ_G + c̃∘α injective and λ̄_G + p∘c∘i onto.
- For formal groups over a base X, the analogue of that surjectivity, and the representability of every object
  above, are open. See package SA17-RP6.

## SA17-26 · the game of go (scans 30-34)

His notation throughout:
- The board is ν × ν. The letter is v|ν in the Pro transcription, unresolved; I write ν.
- N, N' are the numbers of moves of black and white.
- P, P' are the stones on the board at the end; Q, Q' the black and white prisoners; T, T' the territories.

### Scan 30: the scoring identity

**On the page:**
- N = P + Q, N' = P' + Q', and ε = N − N' = 0 (white plays last) or 1.
- P + P' + T + T' = ν².
- The points of black and white are S = Q' + T and S' = Q + T', and D = S − S' = (Q' − Q) + (T − T').
- D = (P − P') + (T − T') − ε and D + ν² = 2(P + T) − ε, "ou ν² − D' = 2(P' + T') + ε", with D' := −D = S' − S.
- Boxed: D = 2(P + T) − ν² − ε. Also D' = 2(P' + T') − ν² + ε.

**Image (300 dpi):**
- The three operators before ε are thick horizontal bars, i.e. minus signs. The Pro transcription left −|+ open.
- In "ν² − D'" the D carries a prime. The Pro transcription has "ν² − D", without the prime.

**Checked:** Q' − Q = (N' − P') − (N − P) = (P − P') − ε, so D = (P − P') + (T − T') − ε = 2(P + T) − ν² − ε, using
the partition; and D' = 2(P' + T') − ν² + ε. The Pro notes J01 have the same, credited.

**Wrong:** "ν² − D' = 2(P' + T') + ε" is a slip. With D' = −D it would say ν² + D = 2(P' + T') + ε, which is false;
the right form is ν² − D = ν² + D' = 2(P' + T') + ε. Michel Hua's project reads the prime and corrects the line the
same way, credited.

**Hypotheses:** the partition P + P' + T + T' = ν² needs every point to be a stone or someone's territory at the end:
no neutral points, no seki, every dead stone removed. With U unassigned points the identity becomes
D = 2(P + T) + U − ν² − ε (the Pro notes J02, credited; Michel Hua's project states the same hypotheses).
Under them the identity is exact: territory scoring differs from area scoring 2(P + T) − ν² by the last-move term ε
only.

### Scan 31: settled territories, and his question

**On the page:**
- Territories of both colours are considered unassailable; white has just played (black to move); n = p + q,
  n' = p' + q', n = n'. Here p counts black stones that are not virtual prisoners and q the virtual prisoners.
- t, t' are the territories with the prisoners removed, and p + p' + t + t' = ν².
- s = t + q' and s' = t' + q; d = s − s' = (t − t') + (q' − q) = (t − t') + (p − p'); d = 2(p + t) − ν².
- D = d ∗ ε with ε = 0, 1 ("NB p + t = P + T").
- "Comme on ne peut prévoir la valeur de ε (est-elle indépendante de la suite du jeu?) il est raisonnable de
  compenser l'avantage de noir (de commencer) en posant ε = 1". A question mark in the margin.

**Image (300 dpi):** the operator in "D = d ∗ ε" is a solid square blot and cannot be decided from the ink. Scan 34
writes "D = d − 1" for ε = 1, and the arithmetic gives minus.

**Checked:**
- n = n' gives q' − q = p − p', so d = 2(p + t) − ν².
- With p + t = P + T, the boxed identity of scan 30 gives D = d − ε. The area p + t does not change while each side
  fills its own territory and removes dead stones.

**His question, answered (mine): ε is not determined by the position alone.**
- A stone sacrificed in the opponent's territory adds one move to the sacrificing side. The opponent's capturing moves
  fill points of its own territory that it fills anyway, and the captured point becomes empty again.
- When the two sides' remaining moves are equal, such a sacrifice changes who plays last. It moves ε by one, and moves
  D by one through the extra prisoner. D = 2(P + T) − ν² − ε stays exact throughout.
- Under territory scoring such a sacrifice costs its author one point, the extra prisoner. So voluntary sacrifices
  gain nothing, and his proposal to fix ε = 1 is a convention (a compensation for black's first move), not a
  consequence.

### Scans 32-33: the rules

**On the page:**
- **No suicide**, except 1°) the Ko ("cf plus bas") and 2°) "remplissage d'un trou": A fills the last liberty of a
  groupement E of B whose stones take all liberties of A's group.
- Moves alternate and passing is not allowed.
- **End of the game.** The obligation to play ends only when A has no legal free point left, except points c that are
  one of the last two interior liberties i₁, i₂ of a groupement E of A.
  - NB: if A played there, B would capture E' = E ∪ {c}, "et de proche en proche il capturerait tous les pions de A".
  - E must be "indélogeable": B cannot play at c or c', neither as the last liberty of a sub-groupement nor by the
    Ko rule.

**Remarks (not check events):**
- 2°) is the capture exception.
- A Ko capture is itself a capture, i.e. a case of 2°, restricted by a repetition rule. Listing it as a separate
  exception does not match the usual treatment, and the promised rule never comes (the Pro notes J04 and Michel Hua's
  project, credited).
- The NB motivates the exemption: without it the obligation to play would force A to fill his own eyes one group
  after another. That the capture would then spread to all of A's stones is asserted, not argued; open (J04).

### Scan 34: who plays last

**On the page:**
- At the end black has ρ groupements Gᵢ, each with γᵢ ≥ 2 eyes, and white ρ' groupements.
- Struck: "Si blanc joue dernier (ε = 0) alors les ρ' groupements ont chacun exactement 2 yeux, donc T' = 2ρ'".
- Live: "Si t − 2ρ ≤ t' − 2ρ' alors c'est blanc qui joue le dernier (i.e. ε = 0, i.e. D = d)"; "Si t' − 2ρ' < t − 2ρ,
  c'est noir qui joue le dernier (i.e. ε = 1, i.e. D = d − 1)".

**Checked:** suppose each side fills only its own territory and ends with exactly two single-point eyes per
groupement (no seki, no neutral points), with black to move.
- Black has a = t − 2ρ moves left and white b = t' − 2ρ'.
- If a ≤ b, black is stuck after both have played a moves: white played last, ε = 0.
- If a > b, white is stuck after black's (b + 1)-th move: black played last, ε = 1.
- The rule holds, and "D = d − 1" confirms the minus sign of scans 30-31.

**His deletion is right (my reason; the page gives none):** when white plays last it is black who is stuck, so it is
black's groupements that are down to two eyes (T = 2ρ), not white's.

**Open:** the rule is not proved for his literal rules. With no passing, and any non-suicidal point in the opponent's
territory a legal move, a player whose own territory is used up must play into the opponent's territory, and the rule
does not count those moves. The Pro notes J03 and Michel Hua's project ("the rule presupposes that each side only fills
its own territory") say the same, credited.

## Hunches

**H-133-1. His λ-ring question has a positive answer for his split WG(S) over a field of characteristic 0.**
- **Grounds:**
  - His own reduction (scan 6) to the universal pair on B_{O(m)×O(n)}.
  - Over a field k of characteristic 0, quadratic modules on B_G are representations of G with an invariant form, and
    they are completely reducible (G = O(m) × O(n) is reductive). So split and metabolic Grothendieck-Witt groups
    agree on B_G.
  - The symmetric representation ring GW(G; k) is a λ-ring by Zibrowius (2015) (to verify: the class of groups G his
    theorem covers).
- **Hunch:** the λ-identities then hold in WG(S) for every S over k satisfying scan 9's hypotheses (O_S strictly
  local, 2 invertible), non-affine S included, where WG(S) ≠ GW(S).
- **Open part:** characteristic p > 2, where representations of O(m) are not completely reducible and the split
  WG(B_{O(m)}) differs from the metabolic one.
- **Leads to:** SA17-RP5.

**H-133-2. Scans 36 and 46-54 are a crossed-module form of P. Hall's isoclinism.**
- **Grounds:** a group is seen through G/Z(G), G' and the commutator map; the two crossed modules (Z(G) → G_ab) and
  (DG → G/Z(G)) share π₁ = Z ∩ DG and π₀ = G/Z·DG; changing the realisation by a central extension E changes the
  commutator data by c_E.
- **Where this sits (to verify):** P. Hall, "The classification of prime-power groups", J. reine angew. Math. 182
  (1940); Beyl-Tappe, LNM 958 (1982), on isoclinism and central extensions; Aldrovandi-Noohi (2009) on butterflies,
  which the Pro notes cite.
- Michel Hua's project lists the finding as unsearched; neither the Pro notes nor Michel Hua's project names
  isoclinism.
- **Leads to:** SA17-RP6.

**H-133-3. The statements on scan 36 are a formal, characteristic-p counterpart of Rosenlicht's theorem.**
- **Grounds:** "p-divisible a-torique" plays the part of the anti-affine (abelian-variety) part, "linéaire" that of the
  affine quotient, and scans 42-44 are the algebraic version.
