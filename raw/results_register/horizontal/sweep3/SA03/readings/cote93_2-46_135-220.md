## 93/135-138. The canonical projective connection and "régulière à l'infini"

**What he does.**
- 93/135: the first-jet extension of ω by ω² is P^1(ω), and the universal operator d^1_ω; the indeterminacy of the construction is a section of ω^{⊗2} ("on retombe sur ses pattes !"). Over C, the transcendental construction "indiquée plus haut" therefore gives a canonical projective connection, a canonical section of the torsor C_{X/C} of projective connections under ω².
- He asks when it is "régulière à l'infini", i.e. when the connection on P_X ≅ P(J/J³) is regular at infinity, and writes: "Sauf erreur, cela doit signifier que cette section du torseur ... n'admet 'à l'infini' X̂ - X que des pôles simples au plus."
- 93/136-138: the indeterminacy group of the regular ones is then H^0(X̂, ω²(D)), D the reduced divisor of the punctures. From 0 → λ → λ(D) → λ(D)/λ → 0 (λ = ω²):
  - g ≥ 2: 0 → H^0(ω²) → H^0(ω²(D)) → H^0(D, ω|D) → 0, of rank 3g - 3 + ν, identified with Ω^1_{M_{g,ν}}(s), the arrow marked "can. ?";
  - g = 1, D ≠ 0: the cokernel Q(X) ≅ t_X̂ (the Lie algebra of the Jacobian), rank ν; the moduli read as elliptic curves with ν points of sum zero;
  - g = 0: rank 0 for ν ≤ 3 ("Il n'y a qu'une seule connexion projective régulière à l'∞ sur X si X est de genre 0 et à ν ≤ 3 trous", the one induced from X̂ = P^1, "n'a rien de transcendant !"), and ν - 3 for ν ≥ 4, the dimension of M_{0,ν}.
- Two margins on 93/135:
  - "sur une courbe elliptique privée d'un pt, la connex. projective canonique n'est pas régulière (autrement elle ne dépendrait pas du dit trou, par raison d'homogénéité -- trop rapide !)";
  - "Ce n'est pas le cas sur E^1_C à 0|1 trou, ni sur P^1_C privée de 3 pts (car sur P^1_C privé de ν ≤ 3 pts, il n'y a au plus qu'une seule conn. proj. régulière (à l'∞ !))". K93-01 reads `\unsure{0|1}`.

**Checks.**
- **SA03-R93-07 (computation, `c93b.py`).** At a cusp the uniformizing coordinate is z = log q/2πi, and its Schwarzian is S(z, q) = 1/(2q²): a double pole. More generally a local exponent difference α gives (1 - α²)/(2q²). The dimensions h^0(X̂, ω²(D)) by Riemann-Roch are 3g - 3 + ν (g ≥ 2), ν (g = 1, ν ≥ 1) and max(0, ν - 3) (g = 0), as he finds.
- **SA03-R93-08 (targeted proof check).** His "can. ?" has the answer yes: Serre duality identifies H^0(X̂, ω²(D)) with the dual of H^1(X̂, T(-D)), the tangent space of M_{g,ν} at s (Kodaira-Spencer).
- **Correction SA03-K93-02.** "Pôles simples au plus" is not regularity.
  - By Fuchs's criterion the connection on P(J/J³) is regular singular at a puncture iff the difference with a holomorphic local projective connection has at most a double pole (Deligne, LNM 163, 1970; locator to be checked).
  - The uniformizing connection of a hyperbolic X has at each cusp the principal part (1/2)dq²/q² (in the normalization of S).
  - So it is regular in the usual sense on every hyperbolic X, and regular in his simple-pole sense at no puncture.
  - His count is right for a different object, the regular singular connections with fixed double-pole principal parts: they form a torsor under H^0(X̂, ω²(D)). For cusps this is the classical space of accessory parameters.
  - With the usual notion and cusp principal parts, the unique such connection on P^1 minus 3 points is the uniformizing one (the hypergeometric equation with exponent differences 0, 0, 0), which is transcendental. "N'a rien de transcendant" holds only for his simple-pole notion, where the unique regular one is the restriction of the connection of P^1.
  - The dossier, §3.5, makes the same distinction and warns against using one meaning to refute the other. The correction does not do that: it states both answers.
- **The margins.**
  - Under his own criterion the first margin is right: a simple-pole section on E minus a point extends to E, since H^0(E, O(P)) = constants, and the holomorphic projective connections of E have monodromy in the affine group, never Fuchsian. The homogeneity argument is the "trop rapide" step.
  - The second margin is consistent with his criterion if it says "is not regular" on E with one hole and on P^1 minus 3 points.
  - "à 0|1 trou" is therefore an open reading that matters (SA03-R93-09: my look at the scan at 250 dpi, rotated, did not decide the oblique margin).

## 93/142-153. Connections and stratifications on torsors

**What he does.**
- A connection on E is a splitting of the principal parts P^1(E). The formula (4) is f(d^1x + ω) = d^1(fx) + (fω - x ⊗ df).
- Integrability is the vanishing of the curvature; the Leibniz rule is (7).
- H^1_DR(S, E) classifies E-torsors with integrable connection.
- Stratified torsors are given by λ: P → (P^∞(E)/δE)^♮.
- The canonical maps (24), H^*_strat(S, E) → H^*_DR(S, E), take the stratification class to the de Rham class (margin "faire la vérification", 93/153).
- Corollary: "J de car. nulle et Ω_{S/T} plat [p. ex. S form^t lisse sur T] de sorte que (24) est un isomorphisme", so stratified torsors ↔ torsors with integrable connection.

**Check (SA03-R93-11, targeted proof check).**
- (4), (7) and the classification by H^1_DR are right: the difference of two splittings is a 1-form, and gluing data give a Čech-de Rham 1-cocycle.
- The Corollary is right for S smooth over T, his example; the comparison of stratifying and de Rham cohomology in characteristic 0 is Grothendieck, "Crystals and the de Rham cohomology of schemes", Dix exposés (1968), §4 (locator to be checked).
- With "Ω_{S/T} plat" alone it is not a standard theorem; the dossier, §3.6, says the same. Open: a proof or a counterexample.

## 93/168-187. "Opération de Cartier"

**What he does, and what is right.**
- 93/169: with a p-basis, H^*_DR(B/A) = H^*_DR(B/Λ), Λ = A[B^p], with basis 1, [x^{p-1}dx] for one variable, and Künneth for several. Right.
- 93/170: "Il y a un hom unique ψ: Ω^*_{B/A} → Z^*_{B/A}" with ψ(x) = x^p and ψ(dx) = x^{p-1}dx. He checks only the multiplicativity, (xy)^{p-1}d(xy) = x^p y^{p-1}dy + y^p x^{p-1}dx. He then composes with Z → H_DR ("Nous serons intéressé surtout par l'hom composé ... φ: Ω^* → H^*_DR").
- 93/171-176: the p-curvature of d + ω on O, ω = Σ c_i dx_i.
  - By Jacobson's formula (cited "Sém. Chevalley 58/59, exp. 6"), (Δ_i^0 + c_i)^p = Δ_i^{0p} + c_i^p + D_i^{p-1}c_i.
  - So zero p-curvature is c_i^p + D_i^{p-1}c_i = 0, i.e. C̃ω = ω̃, with C̃(Σ c_i dx_i) = -Σ D_i^{p-1}(c_i) dx̃_i.
- 93/177, Théorème: the image of dlog is the subsheaf of closed ω with C̃ω = ω̃, with kernel Λ^* (O_X^{*p} when S is perfect).
- 93/178, Théorème 3: on top-degree forms, C̃: f_*ω_{X/S} → ω_{X^{p/S}/S} "est l'homomorphisme trace (ou de Gysin)" of the relative Frobenius, with a residue formula for f_1, ..., f_n.
- 93/179-181: for proper smooth X/S, the p-th power on R^nf_*O and C on f_*Ω^n are transposed. Pic(X^{(p)}) → Pic(X) is V, F_Pic V = p, V F_Pic = p, and on Lie it is the p-th power, "(est-ce dans le livre de Demazure-Gabriel ?)".
- 93/182-185, for curves: on Q = F_*O/Λ the pairing φ(f, g) = C̃(f dg) is a perfect duality, so det(Q)^{⊗2} ≅ Ω^{1⊗(p-1)} ⊗ Λ (**).
  - p = 2: Ω^1 ⊗ Λ is canonically a square.
  - p odd: he asks for a canonical square root ψ: det Q ≅ Ω^{1⊗(p-1)/2} ⊗ Λ (***), and proves it independent of the p-base by the connectedness of the scheme of p-bases.
- 93/186: the self-dual diagram for a proper smooth curve. 0 → R^1f_*(O)^{(p)} → H^1_DR → f_*(Ω^1)^{(p)} → 0, with F and C transposed. Right.

## 93/194-198. "Questions": the p-curvature conjecture in his hand

**What he does.**
- S of finite type over Z, integral and dominant, with generic point η; X smooth over S; E locally free with a connection of zero curvature.
- E_η is "de niveau zéro ou intégrable" if a finite étale surjective cover trivializes it. Over C this means the transcendental π_1 acts through finite groups; his margin adds "localement trivial (pour la top. étale)".
- He notes that then, on an open U ∋ η, every closed fibre of characteristic p has zero p-curvature. Then: "Question La réciproque est-elle vraie ?"
- Rank one: E_η integrable means a Kummer cover, a trivialization of E^{⊗n}. He splits the condition into a) torsion in Pic and b) logarithmic forms with C̃ω_s = ω̃_s.
- For an abelian scheme, with the dual A and its universal vectorial extension E, the rank-one question becomes c): if ω ∈ E(S) and ω(s) ∈ pE_s at every closed s, is the image of ω in A of finite order? His margin: the translation holds more generally for X/S proper smooth.
- d): the same for any group scheme of finite type: subgroups proper connected, of unipotent rank zero, affine or unipotent.
- e): "Soit A schéma abélien sur S, ω section de A, telle que pour tt pt fermé s ∈ S, ω(s) se remonte à A_s^{(p)} via Verschiebung A_s^{(p/s)} → A_s. Est-il vrai qu'alors ω est d'ordre fini ?"

**Checks.**
- **SA03-R93-22 (targeted proof check).**
  - The easy direction is right: spread the finite étale trivialization over an open of S.
  - The converse is the Grothendieck-Katz p-curvature conjecture, here in his own hand with the arithmetic base and the group-scheme variants. Katz states it in Invent. Math. 18 (1972), and Publ. Math. IHES 39 (1970), §5, proves it for Gauss-Manin connections (locators to be checked).
  - It is open in general. The rank-one case (his a), b), c)) is known: D. V. and G. V. Chudnovsky, LNM 1135 (1985), for curves; J.-B. Bost, Publ. Math. IHES 93 (2001); Y. André (2004), Cor. 4.3.5-4.3.6 per the dossier (locators to be checked).
- **SA03-R93-23 (computation, `c93b.py`).** Question e) has the answer no, by the dossier's example, recomputed.
  - Take E: y² = x³ - 2x over Spec Z[1/10] (Δ = 512) and P = (2, 2).
  - P has infinite order: x(2P) = 9/4 is not integral (Nagell-Lutz).
  - At every closed point (p = 3 and p ≥ 7), p ∤ #E(F_p): the Hasse interval lies in (0, 2p), and the 2-torsion point (0, 0) makes the order even; checked for p < 400. So [p] = V∘F is bijective on E(F_p), and P(s) lifts by V.
  - c) keeps the connection data and is the known rank-one case; e) drops that data and fails. The dossier, §3.7, draws the same line.

## 93/199-204. The iterated Cartier operator; logarithmic forms and p-torsion

**What he does.**

- Logarithmic differentials correspond to the eigenvalue 1, i.e. to the Frobenius eigenvalues (ℓ-adic, or crystalline) that are p-adic units "(Katz)".

**Check (SA03-R93-24, targeted proof check).** The equality with dim_{F_p} {}_pA(k) is right: a logarithmic form over k is dlog of a p-th root of a trivialization of some L with L^{⊗p} ≅ O. ## 93/205-213. "Variantes connexifiantes des foncteurs de Picard relatifs (Katz)"

**What he does.**
- P^c_{X/S}: invertible modules with connection. P^ci: with integrable connection. (P^c)^0 is the universal vectorial extension of the Picard scheme.
- When p·1_S = 0, P^cip: zero p-curvature, with the exact sequence 0 → P^cip → P^ci → (p-curvature) f_*^fl(Ω^{1⊗p}), and the "faisceau de Cartier".

- P^cin (nilpotent p-curvature) is the formal completion of P^cip in P^ci, by (4.1)-(4.3).

**Check (SA03-R93-25, targeted proof check).**
