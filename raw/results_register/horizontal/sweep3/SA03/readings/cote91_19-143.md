## 91/19-32: the relative unramified Hom theorem, its corollaries, and the letter to Anantharaman (SA03-1, SA03-2)

### 91/19-21: end of the obstruction proof begun on 91/16-18

- The obstruction to extending u_0 is ξ ∈ Ext¹_{O_{P_0}}(I_{Γ_0}, O_{Γ_0} ⊗ J), killed by π^n.
- The sequence (×) 0 → J →π^n J → J_n → 0 is exact because, after the reduction of the margin of 91/12, O_T has a single associated point, so π is a non-zero-divisor on J.
- Via I/I² ≅ u_0^*Ω¹ and the representing modules, ξ becomes ξ'' ∈ Hom(M_{u_0}, J_n)/Im Hom(M_{u_0}, J) ⊂ Ext¹(M_u, J). Then, from 0 → N → M_{v_0} → M_{u_0} → 0, it becomes ξ''' ∈ Hom(N, J)/Im Hom(M_{v_0}, J). The comparison maps into the η-localized quotients are injective, because J → J_η is.
- Two compatibilities are explicitly admitted ("nous admettrons"): that the obstruction for v is the image of that for u, and that the coherence class γ equals ξ'''.
- The algebra as written checks; the two admitted steps are not proved on the page (SA03-R91-01).
- 91/22, "Pb": simplify by replacing properness and the modules M_u, M_v with an injectivity hypothesis on Hom(u_0^*Ω¹, O ⊗ G) → Hom(v_0^*Ω¹, O_Z ⊗ G) for all quasi-coherent G.

### 91/23-25: a second route, splittings of extensions (SGA 3 III 4.2)

- Lifts correspond to splittings of 0 → O_{Γ_0} ⊗ J → E → I_{Γ_0} → 0 compatible with the given splitting for v. This gives the diagram (D) of representing modules, σ: L → J, and the splitting π': M' → L'.
- He states the condition as λ vanishing on R = R_1 + R_2, the two separate kernel conditions. **This is wrong** (SA03-K91-01):
  - a homomorphism μ: M → G defined on the images of L and M' exists iff λ∘(σ·pr_1 + σαπ'·pr_2) vanishes on the whole kernel of L ⊕ M' → M;
  - the two separate conditions miss mixed relations, where i(l) = -β(m') ≠ 0.
- The margin "le foncteur en G est repr. par Coker ρ" points to a single cokernel. ρ is not defined on the page.
- The stage-A dossier reaches the same correction (CL-SA03-003–005). It also says that a second displayed cokernel fails; we cannot identify which object that is from the transcriptions.
- The flatness of the base change of J/R, which gives conditions 7° and 8°, stands for the corrected R as well (SA03-R91-02).

### 91/25-29: corollaries

- **Corollary 1:** over a separably closed k with X proper, ∏_{X/k}P/X is representable, and essentially quasi-projective for quasi-projective fibres.
- **The theorem of 91/26.** X proper, flat and of finite presentation; probes Z_i ⊂ X flat with (a) ∏_{Z_i} representable, (b) X normally flat along Z_i, (c) every associated point of every geometric fibre meeting some Z_i.
  - (i) ∏_{X/S}P/X is representable, locally of finite presentation and **separated**.
  - (ii) Quasi-compact opens are quasi-affine over ∏_i ∏_{Z_i}.
  - (iii) It is quasi-projective when these are.
  - "Séparé" needs P → X separated, as the letter itself assumes for Y (SA03-K91-02). The stage-A dossier says the same.
- **The question of 91/27**, "Les composantes connexes de ∏_{X/k}P/X sont-elles de type fini?", has a negative answer for P merely locally of finite type. Take X = Spec k and P an infinite connected chain of P¹'s. This is the stage-A dossier's example, checked (SA03-R91-03).
- **Corollaries 2-9:**
  - radicial specializations of the associated points;
  - after a finite separable extension;
  - quasi-projective fibres;
  - generic representability after a finite étale cover;
  - integral geometric fibres with a smooth section ("5[?6]", i.e. 6);
  - smooth proper X: representable étale-locally, with an fpqf variant proposed;
  - Isom_k(X, Y) and Aut_k(X) for proper X and Y over a field (Matsumura and Oort, Invent. Math. 4 (1967), 1-25; locator to be checked);
  - Aut_S(X) after U' → U finite étale ("Il est plausible qu'on puisse prendre U' = U ... cf Raynaud").

  Consistent with the letter. Not re-proved in detail.

### 91/35-36: the formalism and Nagata's counterexample

- The formalism: f_*(X/T) = Hom_{T/S}(T/S, X/S), Hom_S(X, Y) = φ_*(X × Y/X), and the descent formula.
- Nagata's counterexample: a free ℤ/2-action on a smooth X with an orbit in no affine open. The Galois descent for L/K is then not effective, and R_{L/K}(X_L) does not exist. Right in substance (SA03-R91-04; S. Bosch, W. Lütkebohmert, M. Raynaud, *Néron Models*, 1990, §7.6; locator to be checked).
- The stage-A dossier: the general obstruction is identifiable, the particular Nagata example is not fully recovered from the leaves.

### 91/38-49: existence and properties of f_*(X/T)

- **91/38:** f_*(V̌(F)) = V̌(f_*F) for T → S finite locally free. Right.
- **91/39:** the smallest coherent F_1 with f^*(F_1) ⊃ F'_1, for f flat and proper over a local noetherian base. It rests on f^* commuting with filtered intersections, which his own margin questions. **Open**, not decided here.
- **91/40-43:** Propositions 2 and 2 bis (gluing) and the Theorem: if every multisection of X_s/T_s over a finite extension lies in an affine open, f_*(X/T) exists and is the union of the f_*(U_i/T).
  - This is BLR §7.6, Theorem 4.
  - Cor. 1: T/S geometrically injective, or X locally quasi-projective. Cor. 2: closed subschemes.
  - Slip on 91/41: "définit T comme un f_*(X/T)" should read H (SA03-K91-09).
- **91/44-46:** f_* is left exact. It preserves open immersions, closed immersions (with a reserve), affine and quasi-projective morphisms, and projective or proper ones for T/S separable.
  - His margin asks whether properness descends along a faithfully flat T → S. It does in general by fpqc descent (EGA IV 2.7.1), without Chow's lemma.
- **91/45: Proposition.** det f_*(L) is ample (very ample) for f_*(X/T) → f_*(Y/T). The reduction to Grassmannians (91/45-48) gives:
  - a closed condition (stability of F_1 under B);
  - an open condition (det u_ξ invertible).

  The stage-A dossier notes that this gives a locally closed condition, not yet the closed projective embedding. We agree.
- **91/48-49:** f_*(g) is of finite type when g is, through the noetherian fibre powers X^ν. Remark: the fibre dimension of f_*(X/T) is not bounded by ν times that of X/T. For T = Spec k[ε]/(ε²) and X = T × T, Hom(T, T) contains a copy of A¹. Right (SA03-R91-04).

### 91/50-55: smoothness, étaleness, dimension, homogeneity

- **The Theorem:**
  - (i) for T/S affine, g smooth ⇒ f_*(g) smooth (infinitesimal lifting over T');
  - (ii) g étale ⇒ f_*(g) étale.
  - Right (BLR §7.6, Proposition 5; locator to be checked).
- **Corollary 1:** the bound n^ν on the number of points in a fibre. It uses "the lift is known once the image of one point is known", which **needs g separated** (SA03-K91-04, as in the stage-A dossier).
- **Corollary 2:** "D(s) = n D'(s)" **is wrong** (SA03-K91-03).
  - Reversing it does not repair mixed dimensions either. Over a geometric fibre made of several points, the components of the Weil restriction have dimensions that are sums of component dimensions of X (sum-sets), not n times one of them.
  - Only his "en particulier" holds: pure relative dimension d gives pure relative dimension nd. That is what the proof of 91/53 shows: f_*(A^d_T) = A^{nd}.
- **Corollary 1 bis and Corollary 3.** Finite étale of degree λ goes to degree λ^{ν_s}, with ν_s the separable degree. "ν locally constant" ⇔ f_* preserves finite étale ⇔ ... Right.
- **91/55: "homogène"; Corollary 4,** "si g est surjectif, f_*(g) est surjectif".
  - Right under the smoothness of (i): points, then infinitesimal lifting.
  - False for a bare surjective g (stage-A: the closed point of the dual numbers). This is a precision (SA03-K91-10).
- **The Proposition on principal fibrations:** a G-covering goes to a torsor under f_*(T × G/T).

### 91/56-59: the étale envelope

- S' ↦ Hom_S(T, S') on étale S-schemes, and Hom_S(f_*^{ét}(T), S') = Hom_S(T, S') (*), i.e. π_0(T/S).
- The general claim of 91/58, "un tel foncteur [left exact on finite π_1-sets] est nécessairement de la forme Hom_{π_1}(A, E)", **is false as stated** under either reading (SA03-K91-05):
  - E ↦ E^G is left exact and is not an internal Hom Map(A, E);
  - the identity functor is not an equivariant Hom_G(A, −).
- It holds for functors compatible with restriction to open subgroups, i.e. with étale localization, which is the case of Hom_S(T, −). The stage-A dossier gives a counterexample of its own (not checked here).
- 91/59: T → f_*^{ét}(T) is surjective and geometrically injective, and conversely. Right.

## 91/61-80: local functors 𝕎 and their right adjoint 𝕎^a: Weil restriction and Greenberg in one frame (SA03-6)

- **The set-up.** 𝕎 is "de nature locale" (open immersions, unions, intersections).
  - Examples: I, T ×_S S'; I', S' finite locally free; II, a ring scheme W, the Greenberg realization; III, a universal homeomorphism S' → S; III', an augmented W with nilpotent kernel.
  - 𝕎^a(𝔛) represents T ↦ Hom_{𝕎(S)}(𝕎(T), 𝔛).
- **Properties of 𝕎^a.** It commutes with finite projective limits and with base change, and preserves group and ring schemes. The generating ring scheme is W = 𝕎^a(𝕎(S)[t]). A closed 𝔛 given by F_1..F_s is the inverse image of zero under Λ^r → Λ^s. It is bifunctorial in 𝕎' → 𝕎.
- **§2, the affine case.** 𝕎(B) = Hom(Λ, B) gives 𝕎^a(affine) affine, with rs generators; the general case is a direct limit of finitely generated subalgebras. Corollary: finite type (for 𝕎 of finite type) and closed immersions are preserved.
- **§3, gluing.**
  - Lemma 1 (condition α) and Lemma 2 (condition β, triple intersections).
  - Lemma 3: β is automatic when |T| ≅ |𝕎(T)|.
  - Lemma 4: α is automatic in cases I' and II*.
- **Theorem 1:** 𝕎^a exists for every 𝔛 when 𝕎 is a special Greenberg functor, or 𝕎_{S'} with S' → S finite, locally free and radicial.
- **Theorem 1 bis and its corollaries:** existence when the finite subsets with ≤ n(s) points of the fibres lie in affine opens, and finite type. This is BLR §7.6, Theorem 4.
- **91/78-80:** closed immersions, immersions (quasi-projective case), separatedness, finite type and affine morphisms are preserved.
- All checked as far as stated (SA03-R91-07). Modern counterparts for specified Greenberg and restriction functors: A. Bertapelle and C. González-Avilés (locators to be checked), as the stage-A dossier records.

## 91/81-122: schematic sets and the constructible topology (SA03-7)

**Constructibility (91/81-82, 84).** In a quasi-compact X with retrocompact neighbourhoods: constructible ⇔ quasi-compact and locally constructible. The locally closed case, and the qcqs example (finitely many equations locally). Right.

**Schematic sets (91/87-99, odd scans).**
- Atomic preschemes |X| = ∐ Spec k(x).
- Constructible maps: residue-field maps that are morphic on the pieces of a partition into locally closed constructible subsets. "Ensemble schématique" = a qcqs preschema with constructible maps as morphisms.
- Statements:
  - the refining partition lemma;
  - f is constructible iff morphic on a finite partition;
  - preimages of constructible sets are constructible (Chevalley, piece by piece; his margin: "fragile pour l'argument!");
  - isomorphisms are piecewise isomorphisms;
  - Cor. 1: every schematic set is |X| with X affine;
  - Cor. 2: every morphism is a morphism of affine schemes.
  - Right (SA03-R91-13).
- **Cor. 0, "Si X noethérien Y l'est" (91/93),** is true for the underlying spaces and false for schemes, even reduced ones (SA03-K91-06).
  - The counterexample: B = k + x·k[x, y] is reduced and not noetherian, while Spec B = D(x) ⊔ {m} with D(x) ≅ Spec k[x, x⁻¹, y]. We verified that every prime containing x is m = x·k[x, y].
  - The stage-A dossier gives the same example (CL-SA03-230).
- **The question of 91/97,** "A-t-on Cons X ≅ lim Cons(X_i)?", has a negative answer: the comparison map is not surjective.
  - Take T = A¹_ℚ, X_n = Aⁿ and coordinates the indicator functions of distinct rational points a_m. The finite tuples are constructible and compatible.
  - No single finite partition carries them all: the generic piece is a dense open on which every coordinate vanishes, yet it contains almost all a_m.
  - The stage-A dossier's example; we checked the argument (SA03-R91-08).
- 91/99: "Ensembles quasi-schématiques ... forment une catégorie géométrique (?)". 91/101: categorical axioms (finite limits, effective universal epimorphisms, the dual axioms, "X × ∐Y_i ≅ ∐ X × Y_i").

**The constructible topology (91/104-111).**
- Prop. 1: a base of constructibles; X^cons → X continuous; T_0 ⇒ X^cons Hausdorff and totally disconnected; induced topologies (EGA 0_III 9.1.8, 9.1.13).
- Corollaries 1-3:
  - his "parties antitypiques" (reading uncertain) are the closed sets of X^cons, the pro-constructible sets of EGA IV 1.9;
  - E and X - E pro-constructible ⇔ E constructible.
- Prop. 2 (preimages).
- 91/110:
  - f quasi-compact ⇔ f^cons proper ⇔ f maps pro-constructible sets to pro-constructible sets and has quasi-compact fibres;
  - constructible images ⇔ f^cons open.
  - Slip in Cor. 3 (iii): "f^{-1}(Y)" should read f^{-1}(E) (SA03-K91-07; not viewed).
  - The stage-A dossier reports a witness conflict on 91/110 (a positive claim against an explicit warning). We note it without deciding.
- 91/111: Spec(A)^cons is compact, and pro-constructible sets are the images of quasi-compact (mono)morphisms. Right (SA03-R91-09).

**91/113-122.**
- The category (Cons aff) and fully faithful embeddings into ind-objects and presheaves.
- 91/115: A^cons = colim over standard partitions of ∏ A(X_{i,red}), whose Spec is X with the constructible topology. This is the absolutely flat hull (D. Olivier, 1967-68; and EGA IV 1.9.16, as the stage-A dossier specifies).
- 91/118: a plan (compact and locally compact preschemes, flasque pro-schemes, schematic spaces, quasi-schematic spaces, thick points, "Espaces de Néron") with an adjunction diagram; 91/120: the locally compact version.
- 91/122: compact schemes (quasi-compact, Hausdorff) are totally disconnected and affine, with constructible = clopen. Right.

## 91/124-142: thick points, the Greenberg realization over a DVR, Laurent units, jets (SA03-9, SA03-10)

**91/126-128: "points épais" of an essentially affine pro-scheme.** Conditions (Ep), (Ep)', (Ep)'', (Ep)''' and a diagram of implications marked "???". Open on the page.

**91/131-132: the Greenberg realization X̄ over a DVR with perfect residue field.**
- A set map φ̄: X̄ → Ȳ from φ: X_K → Y_K, for Y proper, via perfect closures.
- Proposition 1: thick points map to thick points, with a promorphism of finite index on a dense constructible open.
- Proposition 2: constructible images under smoothness and a bounded invariant l(N, x).
- These are stated, not proved. The stage-A dossier notes that "finite index", "simple" and the bounded invariant cannot be securely identified. Comparison: M. J. Greenberg, Ann. of Math. 73 (1961) and 78 (1963); J. Sebag (locators to be checked).

**91/134-135: the loop group A ↦ A((t))^*.**
- It is ind-representable by 𝒢 = lim 𝒢_n, with 𝒢_0 = G_m × Spec ℤ[(a_i)_{i≥1}], and there is a map 𝒢' = ℤ × 𝒢_0 → 𝒢.
- **The Theorem**, checked (SA03-R91-10):
  - (i) 𝒢'_n → 𝒢_n is a surjective closed immersion, a homeomorphism, and 𝒢_n is non-reduced;
  - (ii) it is an open immersion on the components ν = ±n only. For |ν| < n, the dual-number point t^ν + εt^{ν-1}, with inverse t^{-ν} - εt^{-ν-1}, lies in 𝒢_n^ν without being t^ν times a power-series unit;
  - (iii) 𝒢_n^i → 𝒢_{n'}^i is not an isomorphism for n' > n. The text's "n' ≥ n" includes the identity (SA03-K91-08, as in the stage-A dossier).
- In the condition defining 𝒢_n, "a_i = 0, b_i = 0 pour i < n" must mean i < -n: the ambient ring is ℤ[(a_i, b_i)_{i ≥ -n}] (reading to check).
- **Witnesses:** the Pro text of 91/135 lacks the statements (i)-(iii) that K91 carries (not viewed).

**91/136: pro-categories.**
- Pro ⊃ Pro' (essentially affine) ⊃ Pro'' (quasi-compact, finitely presented, affine transitions), with Hom_{Pro}(𝒴, 𝒳) = Hom(lim 𝒴, lim 𝒳) (EGA IV 8.13-8.14). The precise version needs quasi-separatedness as well (stage-A dossier).
- "Dans Serre et Néron, on utilise des systèmes projectifs ... avantage à les interpréter comme de véritables schémas".

**91/138, 140, 142: jets.**
- Coordinate changes f_i = a_i + Σ b_ij t_j + Σ c_ip t^p acting on m^n for A of infinitesimal order N; 𝔾_{n,N} = Aut of the augmented Z[t]/(t)^{N+1}, and its Greenberg realization; affine-space rows over T⁰ ⊗ V_i.
- The boxed composition (a'_0 + a'_1 t + a'_2 t²)∘(a_0 + a_1 t + a_2 t²), expanded through t⁴, is right as a polynomial composition (re-derived by hand, SA03-R91-11). With a_0 ≠ 0 it is not an automorphism of the augmented k[t]/(t³) (stage-A dossier).
