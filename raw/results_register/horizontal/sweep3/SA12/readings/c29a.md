**What he claims and what I checked.**
- 1.16 (scan 8). His counterexample: H = e, one section a = b^n, Y' = Y[T]/(T^n - a), Y = S. A morphism Y -> Y'
  compatible with e -> H' is a section of Y', and T -> b is one; but e -> H' = mu_n is not surjective. Re-derived:
  correct, for the statement as he paraphrases it (some form of "a morphism of Kummer coverings compatible with the
  groups forces H -> H' onto"). Murre's 1.16 itself is not on these pages.
- Murre's correction in example 3a (scan 3). A morphism Z^{n'}_a -> Z^n_a of Kummer coverings over S exists iff
  n_i | n'_i for all i: at a generic point of V(a_i), T_i must go to an element of valuation 1/n_i in a ring where
  T'_i has valuation 1/n'_i, so 1/n_i lies in (1/n'_i)Z; conversely T_i -> T'_i^{n'_i/n_i}. So Murre's "n' = m.n" is
  the exact condition. Grothendieck's "n' >= n" is right only if >= is the divisibility order of the index set (his
  usual convention for Kummer systems; his sketch is not on these pages).
- The regularity lemma (scans 11-12): "if A is a regular local ring, (x_i)_{1<=i<=r} part of a regular system of
  parameters, and (n_i) integers >= 1, not necessarily prime to the residue characteristic, then
  B = A[T_1..T_r]/(T_i^{n_i} - x_i) is again regular." Re-proved: B is finite free over A, so dim B = dim A = d;
  B/m_A B = k[T]/(T_i^{n_i}) is local, so B is local; its maximal ideal is (T_1..T_r, x_{r+1}..x_d), d generators,
  since x_i = T_i^{n_i}. So B is regular, in every characteristic. Correct.
- The a posteriori statement (scan 11): if at every maximal point s of every D_i the inertia is prime to char k(s),
  then the inertia order is prime to the residue characteristic at every specialization of s. I checked the mechanism
  in the only non-empty case (s of characteristic 0, s' of characteristic p): if no component of D through s' is of
  characteristic p, the fibre V(p) meets U = S - D near s', the covering is étale there, and a Kummer exponent
  divisible by p is not étale in characteristic p. I tried to build a counterexample over Z_p[zeta_p][x] with
  D = (x) + (pi) and a = x^p + pi^p c x (unramified at (pi), index p at (x)); it fails because a vanishes on a third
  divisor through the closed point, singular there for p >= 3, so the covering is not étale on S - D. No
  counterexample found; the statement is the one later proved in LNM 208 with Abhyankar's lemma (locator unverified).
- 6.4 "correct only if the D_i are regular": for a non-regular D_i (a nodal curve on a surface) the two local branches
  at the node are not seen separately by tameness "along D_i" at its generic point; his repair (tameness along the
  étale-local components of one normal-crossings divisor D) is the notion LNM 208 adopts (locator unverified).

### 29/013-034. "Données de ramification": the sketch sent to Murre on 24.4.67 (Codex C29-01..03, K29-01; SA12-31, SA12-30)
**Identification (new).** The French typescript 13-34 is the "sketch which I promised on ramification data" enclosed
with the letter of 24.4.67 (29/010), and the text Murre was reading on 16 May (29/003). Three internal proofs:
(1) Murre quotes on 29/003 "On peut enfin l'expliciter en revenant à la definition de Rev^R(S), et en exigeant que les
revêtements des s(M) correspondants à X soient principaux de groupe G": this sentence stands word for word on 29/031;
(2) Murre quotes "Hom_{s'}(M',M) est vide sauf si n' >= n, auquel cas ...", which is 29/021 (the relation is a blank on
the typescript, checked on the scan); (3) the letter of 24.4 says "the paragraphs 2 to 5 in this plan correspond
approximately to the sections 1. to 4. in my sketchy notes" and cites "particular case b)": the typescript has §1
"Données de ramification sur un schéma" (13), §2 "Propriétés de la catégorie Rev^R(S). Le groupe fondamental" (25),
§3 "Fonctorialités pour S et R variables" (26), §4 "Cas des revêtements galoisiens" (31), and "Cas particuliers du type
kummérien: a) ... b)" (20-22). On 32 the text turns to English ("This condition is satisfied in the case of Kummer data,
as one sees by combining 1.10. and 1.15. of Murre's notes"), with the counterexample to "prop. 1.16. of Murre's notes"
and "Murre's lemma 4.3": the sketch was written against Murre's March manuscript. 33-34 add "Remords concernant
l'exemple 3": axioms a)-e).

**What he defines and claims.**
- A ramification datum R = (C, s): a fibred category C over the étale site Et_S (not assumed a stack) and a cartesian
  functor s to relative schemes; models M, geometric realizations s(M); Rev^R(S) = the 2-colimit over covering sieves
  of the cartesian sections of the pulled-back category of finite étale covers; the realization functor
  r: Rev^R(S) -> (Sch)/S, faithful but not fully faithful in general (13-15).
- Example 1: (Z, G), Rev^R(S) = G-equivariant finite étale covers of Z, r(Z') = Z'/G; faithful if Z|U is G-principal
  and dense; fully faithful if moreover Z normal (15-17). Example 2: projective systems (Z_i, G_i) (17-19). Example 3:
  locally given models with finite automorphism groups, realization by descent (19-20). Kummer data a) (a family
  (D_i) with equations, models (a, n, G, u), realization Z^n_a) and b) (a divisor D with local decompositions; 1°
  local regular components of a normal-crossings D, 2° local components smooth over a base T) (20-22). Rev^R in case
  1° is the category of covers "modérément ramifiés au sens large" along D; by Abhyankar they are the covers étale off
  supp D, normal over supp D, dominating, tame at the maximal points of D (22). Specialization of the tame pi_1 "peut
  se démontrer, par la technique de SGA 1" with this notion (23).
- §2 (25-26): Rev^R(S) satisfies G1-G3 of SGA 1 V 4; r commutes with finite colimits but not with finite limits, not
  with products; a fibre functor at a geometric point outside the ramification set; pi_1^R(S, sbar) profinite.
- §3 (26-30): functoriality for f: T -> S; an isomorphism on realizations when quotients commute with base change
  (T -> S flat, or Kummer a)); transitivity for U -> T -> S declined ("jetant provisoirement un voile pudique sur ces
  abîmes"); the étale finite case (an open subgroup) and the entire radicial surjective case (an equivalence);
  fpqc descent and passage to the limit announced, "utilisé (sans démonstration) à la page 38 des notes de Murre".
- §4 (31-32): Galois coverings of type R; the necessary and sufficient condition (strict localizations, normal
  subgroups H_1, H_2 of the stabilizer H, an isomorphism u of quotients compatible with phi: H/H_1 -> H/H_2 must come
  from an element g of H) for the realization to be fully faithful on Galois coverings (isomorphisms only).

**What I checked.**
- The full-faithfulness criterion of Kummer a) (21): r is fully faithful if S is locally noetherian, normal at the
  points of supp D_i, the D_i reduced and without common components. Re-proved through Example 1's criterion: Z^n_a
  is finite flat over S, so S_2 where S is; at a codimension-1 point on exactly one D_i the local ring is
  O[T]/(T^n - pi u), Eisenstein, hence a DVR; off the D_i it is étale. So Z^n_a is normal (Serre), and Z|U is dense by
  flatness. With a common component (T_1^2 = pi = T_2^2) the element T_1/T_2 is integral and not in the ring: the
  hypothesis is needed.
- "r ne commute pas aux produits" (25): over a strictly henselian DVR with uniformizer a, Z = Z^2_a corresponds to
  the pi_1-set mu_2; Z x Z in Rev^R corresponds to mu_2 x mu_2, realized by Z disjoint-union Z, while Z x_S Z =
  Spec O[T,T']/(T^2 - a, T'^2 - a) is connected (one closed point) and not normal. Correct; this is also what his
  English remark on 32 says about Murre's lemma 4.3.
- The criterion of §4 holds for Kummer data, as he says (32): for H = mu_n abelian, Z/mu_d is the Kummer cover of
  degree n/d, so Z/H_1 = Z/H_2 forces H_1 = H_2; its automorphisms over a strictly local normal base are mu_{n/d},
  which lift to mu_n; equivariance forces phi = id. Checked.
- Signs: the morphisms M' -> M are "les b tels que b^n = a' a^{-1}" (21). With the realization T -> b T'^{n'/n} one
  gets b^n = a a'^{-1}; the typescript's convention corresponds to T -> b^{-1} T'^{n'/n}. A convention, not an error.

**What is wrong or loose.**
- 29/021-022, case b): the decompositions (D_i) are required to have "les restrictions à Sêc(O_{S',s'}) des D_i qui
  sont non nulles ... deux à deux disjointes" for every s' in S' (ink checked: "Sêc", "deux à deux disjointes"). Taken
  literally this allows at most one nonzero D_i through each point, which excludes the crossings that case 1° is
  made for. The condition that works is "no common component" (equivalently: the condition only at points s' of
  codimension 1). He calls it himself "la condition canularesque" (22).
- 29/031, the number of the section "Cas des revêtements galoisiens": the Codex reading "3(?)" is an overtype of 3
  and 4 (crop checked). The ink does not decide the order of the strikes; the numbering (§3 is "Fonctorialités", 26)
  and the letter of 24.4 ("sections 1. to 4.") make it §4. Correction proposed.
- 29/022: "Lorsque T est régulier, les notions 1° et 2° coïncident" needs S smooth over T along D (implicit in
  "croisements normaux relativement à T"): then S is regular and the local components V(t_i), smooth over T, are the
  local regular components. Under that reading correct.

**Open on the pages.** In case 2° "sauf erreur (il faudrait le vérifier)" that Rev^R(S) is a full subcategory of the
coverings (22); transitivity of functoriality (28); fpqc descent and passage to the limit for coverings of type R
(30), announced and not proved. All three are settled in Grothendieck-Murre LNM 208 for the tame case (locator
unverified).

### 29/047-058. "Th. de finitude relatif pour pi_1": Lemma 1 (descent) and the finiteness of H^1 (K29-01 for 47-48; Pro for 50-58; SA12-1, SA12-2)
Six numbered leaves I-VI: 48 (I), 50 (II), 52 (III), 54 (IV), 56 (V), 58 (VI); the odd scans 49-59 are the reused
typescript n° 385 (see Reused material).
**Lemma 1 (48, 50).** f: X -> Y faithfully flat [hypothesis word], Y normal integral with generic point y, X',X''
étale covers of X, X_ybar connected. For every homomorphism (isomorphism) u: X'_ybar -> X''_ybar, the smallest
extension K'_1 of k(y) over which it is defined is unramified over Y (its normalization Y_1 is étale) and u comes
from a unique X x_Y Y_1-morphism. NB on 50: this gives the exact sequence pi_1(X_ybar) -> pi_1(X) -> pi_1(Y) -> 0,
"généralise ... SGA IX 6.1, 6.2", not to be confused with SGA X (any fibre, but f proper).
- **Reading decided on the scan (new).** The hypothesis word read "biréduit(?)" by K29-01 is "réduit", written after a
  heavily cancelled word in the top-right insertion "fid. plat [cancelled] réduit"; under it "plat normal" is struck.
  "Morphisme réduit" is EGA IV's term (6.8.1, locator unverified): flat with geometrically reduced fibres.
- **The hypothesis is needed (counterexample, new).** R a DVR, uniformizer t, residue characteristic not 2;
  X = Spec R[x, x^{-1}, z]/(z^2 - t x) (flat over R, special fibre z^2 = 0 not reduced, generic fibre z^2 = t x, x != 0,
  geometrically integral); X' = X[w]/(w^2 - x) (étale, x a unit), X'' = X disjoint-union X. Over K = k(y), x = z^2/t,
  so X'_K = X_K x_K K(sqrt t): X'_ybar and X''_ybar are isomorphic, the isomorphism is defined exactly over K(sqrt t),
  which is ramified over R. So the lemma fails for a faithfully flat f with a non-reduced fibre, and the sequence is
  not exact in the middle (X' is split on X_ybar and does not come from an étale cover of Y). With "réduit" this
  example is excluded.
- **With "réduit", the steps I checked.** At a generic point xi of a fibre over a codimension-1 point of Y, O_{X,xi}
  is a DVR with the same uniformizer (fibre reduced), so a ramified Y_1 would ramify X x_Y Y_1 at xi: Y_1 is
  unramified in codimension 1. X is S_2 at every point outside the generic fibre (depth formula for flat local maps:
  depth O_Y >= 2 at codim >= 2, fibres S_1), so the integral elements of K'_1 extend to X' (normal in codimension 1,
  Hartogs beyond): X' -> Y_1 exists. The last step, X' = X x_Y Y_1 hence Y_1 étale by fpqc descent, is his
  "considérations de profondeur", not re-derived. Status: plausible, no counterexample with "réduit"; the needed
  hypothesis is now on the page.
**Lemmas 2-4 and the Proposition (52-54).** Lemma 2 (f of finite type, Y universally japanese: f_* of a constructible
sheaf of sets is constructible) is cited "SGA au début", not proved; the japanese hypothesis is what makes local branch
counts finite. Lemma 3 (partition into locally closed X_i: H^1(X,G) -> prod H^1(X_i,G) has finite fibres) and Lemma 4
(Hom(F,G) finite for constructible F, G on noetherian X) re-derived: a constructible F is a quotient of a finite sum of
representables h_{U_i} (U_i quasi-compact étale), so Hom(F,G) injects into prod G(U_i), finite; for X = U union Z the
gluing datum of a torsor is a morphism P_Z -> i^* j_* P_U between constructible sheaves (Lemma 2), so finitely many.
The Proposition: (i) H^1(X,G) finite for all X of finite type over S, (ii) the same for smooth affine X with
geometrically connected fibres over finite étale covers of normal affine S_0, (iii) the same over a dense open, are
equivalent (Lemma 3), and follow from (iv) a) finiteness over the algebraically closed residue fields, b) H^1(S',H)
finite for subgroups H of G x G (Lemma 1 compares two torsors through their Isom). Consistent; not re-proved in full.
**Cor. 1, 2, the NB and the curve reduction (56-58).** For G prime to the residue characteristics the condition b)
suffices. The NB (H^1(X,G) finite over an algebraically closed field, G prime to p) by induction on dim X: Lemma 3,
smooth connected X, dim 1 "dû à Serre-Lang" (via Abhyankar), dim >= 2 an elementary fibration in curves and Lemma 1.
Checked: an extension of profinite groups whose kernel image and quotient have finitely many open subgroups of each
index has the same property, so the fibration step works with Lemma 1 for a smooth fibration (réduit).
- **The "Prop" on 58, with his margin "?" (new remark).** "k un corps; si pour tt courbe alg. lisse X sur k le nb de
  rev. étales de X de degré donné est fini, alors la même chose est vraie pour tt X de t.f. sur k." The hypothesis
  forces char k = 0: in characteristic p, y^p - y = x^m (m prime to p) are infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic
  étale covers of degree p of A^1_k. In characteristic 0 the induction needs the curve statement over the algebraic

### 29/071-077. Birational invariance of Pic^tau; dim Pic bounded on normal models (Pro; SA12-9, SA12-10)
**71 Théorème.** f: X -> Y dominant birational morphism of integral normal proper k-schemes, Y regular: Pic^tau_Y ->
Pic^tau_X is an isomorphism. Margins: "X normal 'sur k' ?", "Y projectif ?? ou proj. sur k", "N.B. factoriel ne suffit
pas ...".
**Proof on the page, step by step, with what I checked.**
1. Monomorphism, since f_* O_X = O_Y universally (flat base change over k). Checked: projection formula L = f_*f^*L
   on every X_T.
2. Points: Y factorial, so Pic(Y) = Pic(U) (U the domain of f^{-1}, complement of codim >= 2); g^* f^* = i^* makes
   f^* split injective, Pic(X) = f^*Pic(Y) + ker g^*, and ker g^* consists of the divisors sum n_i X_i on the
   exceptional ("singuliers") divisors. tau-equivalence passes from X to Y through U (algebraic families on U x T
   extend to Y x T for T smooth, Y x T regular). Checked.
3. "Il reste à voir que si [sum n_i X_i] est tau-équivalente à 0, elle est nulle. C'est là un lemme bien connu ..."
   (margins "vérifier ? On peut se ramener au cas projectif", "insuffisant"). The lemma is true: tau-trivial implies
   numerically trivial, and a numerically trivial combination of f-exceptional divisors is 0 by the negativity lemma
   (Kollár-Mori 3.39, locator unverified; its surface case rests on the negative definiteness of exceptional curves,
   valid in every characteristic). So f^*: Pic^tau(Y) -> Pic^tau(X) is bijective on points over k algebraically closed.
4. The Corollary, H^1(Y, O_Y) -> H^1(X, O_X) bijective (72-75). Injective by f_*O_X = O_Y (Leray). Characteristic p:
   split H^1(O) into the F-bijective and F-nilpotent parts; the first is H^1(Z/p) x k, and H^1(Y, Z/p) = H^1(X, Z/p)
   because pi_1(X) = pi_1(Y) (onto as Y is normal; injective by purity on the regular Y); the second is the union of
   ker F^nu = H^0(O^{p^-nu}/O) (k perfect, H^0(O) = k), and T(Y) -> T(U) is bijective because O_Y^{p^-nu}/O_Y is
   locally free on the regular Y (Kunz; his margin "Y lisse sur k (!)"), while T(X) -> T(U) is injective because
   O_X^{p^-nu}/O_X is torsion-free on the normal X (an element a with a^{p^nu} in O_X and s a in O_X is integral, so
   in O_X). Re-derived: correct, and a resolution-free proof. Characteristic 0: spreading out over S of finite type over
   Z, the cokernel dimension r of the generic fibre persists on a dense open of S, in particular at closed points of
   characteristic p > 0, where it is 0. Re-derived: correct (Y smooth and X normal over S give the hypotheses on the
   fibres; "X normal 'sur k'" is needed for this, as his margin asks).
5. "Ainsi f est une immersion surjective. Il reste à prouver qu'elle induit un isom sur les espaces tangents": the
   passage from (closed immersion, bijective on points, isomorphism on tangent spaces) to an isomorphism is valid when
   Pic^tau_Y is smooth (always in characteristic 0: equal dimensions force smoothness of Pic^tau_X). In characteristic
   p it is not a valid principle: alpha_p in alpha_{p^2} is a closed immersion, bijective, with equal 1-dimensional Lie
   algebras, and not an isomorphism. What closes the gap is the comparison at all infinitesimal orders: with the
   Corollary, H^1 agrees, and if H^2(Y, O_Y) -> H^2(X, O_X) is injective (by Leray, iff H^0(Y, R^1 f_* O_X) = 0) the
   deformation functors of the trivial bundle agree and the immersion is an isomorphism near 0. That injectivity holds
   when R^1 f_* O_X = 0, which a resolution of X gives (characteristic 0; in characteristic p via
   Chatzistamatiou-Rülling for birational maps of smooth varieties, locator unverified). Status: the theorem is
   proved on the page in characteristic 0; in characteristic p the last step is a gap when Pic^tau_Y is not smooth.
   His own margin on 71 about non-reduced points ("... pts ... non-réduits", reading uncertain) may be this.
**75 Cor. 1, Cor. 2, the Question.** Cor. 1 (Pic^tau birational for X proper smooth) follows by passing through a

## Hunches
1. **Proposition 4 and the base.** The pages' own fix is the right one: minimality should be taken in a category of
   admissible groups closed under equalizers of the morphisms allowed (constant groups over a field, as in the letter
   to Serre). Over a non-normal reduced base with morphisms varying along S, the cusp shows the theory needs another
   notion. Grounds: 139-142 and the cusp. Links: Nori's fundamental group scheme; the fundamental group scheme over
   Dedekind bases (Gasbarri; Antei-Emsalem-Gasbarri), which treat the relative question of 131 (literature, locators
   unverified); SA12-22, SA12-35, SA12-37 (reader B's stretch 141-187).
2. **Pic^tau in characteristic p.** The theorem of 71 is probably true: the gap is at the infinitesimal level, and his
   Frobenius method for H^1 (72-73) suggests the same split (F-semisimple / F-nilpotent) for H^0(Y, R^1 f_* O_X) or for
   H^2, which would give the injectivity H^2(Y, O) -> H^2(X, O) that closes it. Grounds: 72-75; for smooth X and Y,
   R f_* O_X = O_Y (Chatzistamatiou-Rülling, locator unverified). Links: SA12-9, SA12-10.
3. **pi_1(Z - Y), Z proper non-projective, characteristic p.** Probably still finitely generated: abelian p-covers
   are finite in number in each degree (the Frobenius-stable part of H^1(X, O) is finite-dimensional, as in the
   Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik finiteness for local cohomology). Grounds: the cone computation I did for a projective
   cone minus its vertex (H^1(X, Z/p) = H^1(C, Z/p)). Weak; links SA12-5, F12.
4. **Pi_2^t(Spec Z - S) (98, "Mais qu'est Pi_2^t ???").** His question is the K(pi,1) question for arithmetic curves:
   A. Schmidt's results that Spec O_K - S is often of type K(pi,1) for pro-p coefficients answer it in many cases
   (literature, locator unverified). Links: SA12-18, the cd material of cote 31 (F11).
5. **The a posteriori tameness** (29/011, 29/100 "Il faut prouver que I est premier aux car. résiduelles") is proved in
   LNM 208 with Abhyankar's lemma (locator unverified). Links: SA12-19, SA12-31.
6. **What LNM 208 dropped.** Murre's 1969 draft had a chapter X "plumbing" and an unwritten chapter on "the analogue of
   the theorem of Mumford" (79-81), which Grothendieck asked to apply to the finite generation of the local prime-to-p
   pi_1 (82). Comparing LNM 208's chapters with Murre's I-X would show which parts never appeared. Links: SA12-11,
   SA12-16, SA12-17, the "Finitudes" programme item 2) (43).
7. **The 1959 "module de Weil" for p (129-130)** is an early written statement of the Dieudonné-module programme (the
   p-part of lim _nX as a Witt-vector module with three pieces), seven years before crystals. Links: SA12-22; the
   crystals topics of the archive (SA05-SA07 cluster).
8. **"Finitudes" 3) b)** (constructibility of pi'_1 of geometric fibres) is the theorem of Orgogozo 2003 (locator
   unverified); 1) b) finite presentation of pi'_1 is settled for the tame group of smooth varieties with good
   compactification by Esnault-Schusterman-Srinivas (as Pro BR01-BR09 cite) and, I think, open in general without
   resolution. Links: SA12-32, SA12-3.
