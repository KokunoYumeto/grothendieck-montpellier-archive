### 1. 29/141-142: Proposition 4 restated and its proof (SA12-22; F7, F23)
- **Places.** 141: the setting (S connected and reduced, pointed by a; C a category of finite flat S-groups stable
  under products, kernels of pairs and images; the G in C admissible; Z(G) = Z(S,a;G) the classes of a-pointed
  principal homogeneous bundles), "Éléments minimaux", Proposition 4 and the proof. 142: the end of the proof, with
  his warning "[Attention, on perd le fait que S est réduit, S_1 ne l'est plus]" (confirmed on the ink in sweep 2).
- **What he does.** Proposition 4: for α ∈ Z(G) minimal, u ↦ u_*(α), Hom_S^gr(G,G') → Z(G'), is injective. Given
  u_*α = v_*α he forms (Q,η) = (P,ξ) ×_G (G'×G') = (P×_u G') × (P×_v G'), the (unique) pointed map (P',ξ') → (Q,η)
  over the diagonal of G', the fibre product (R,λ) = P ×_Q P', and H = Ker(u,v: G ⇉ G'). He claims (R,λ) is a
  pointed H-torsor; then minimality gives H = G, so u = v. The claim is checked after the base change S_1 = P, where
  everything is trivial, and reduces to the commutativity of P →can P×_u G' →w P×_v G' with P →can P×_v G', which
  "résulte de l'unicité de P → P×_v G' transformant pt marqué en pt marqué".
- **What I checked (independent re-proof of the reduction).** Write w([p,1]_u) = [p,c(p)]_v with c: P → G'. The
  G-compatibility of w gives c(pg) = v(g)^{-1} c(p) u(g), and pointedness gives c(ξ) = 1. Then R ≅ {p ∈ P : c(p) = 1},
  stable under H. Over S_1 = P, R_1 = {g ∈ G_1 : v(g) = c_0 u(g)} with c_0 = c|_{S_1}; it is the trivial H-torsor iff
  c_0 = 1, i.e. iff c ≡ 1, which is his diagonal claim. But c ≡ 1 forces v(g)^{-1}u(g) = 1 for every point g (take p
  fppf-locally), i.e. **u = v at once**. Conversely u = v gives w = id (the rigidity he uses in "(d'ailleurs unique)")
  and c ≡ 1. So the diagonal claim is equivalent to the conclusion, and **minimality is never used**: the bracket "du
  caractère minimal … résultera alors que H = G" is idle, and the whole weight of the proposition lies on the final
  "unicité".
- **What is wrong.** The "unicité" as written cannot hold without minimality, since it would prove injectivity for
  every α. Counterexample (computation): S connected, G = G' = Z/2 constant, α trivial (P = S ⊔ S, ξ in the first copy),
  u = id, v = 0: u_*α = v_*α (both trivial), u ≠ v, and there are two pointed S-maps P → P×_v G' (the second copy
  goes to either copy). The two maps compared on 142 are u-equivariant and v-equivariant respectively, so the
  uniqueness of pointed v-equivariant maps does not apply to the first (this agrees with P29 N142.1).
- **What is true (repair, own work).** If G and G' are finite étale, minimality of α is equivalent to P connected
  (the component of ξ is a torsor under its stabiliser, which is in C when C has all subgroups, as in Exemple I), and
  a pointed S-morphism from a connected P to a finite étale (separated) S-scheme is unique (the equaliser is open and
  closed). Then c ≡ 1 and u = v: Proposition 4 holds in the étale case, and minimality enters exactly at the
  "unicité". For non-étale G the same step needs a rigidity that fails: the Pro counterexample over the cusp (package
  MPL_29_095-140, N033, rechecked in sweep 1, F7) and the non-reduced S_1 of his own warning are both at this step.
- **Reading.** P_1 ≅ G_1 (transcription) is right: P_1 is a G-torsor trivialised by the diagonal; Hua's G'_1 (per P29
  N142.1) is a slip of his reading. The "(?)" on 141-142 (Z-symbol, "classes", "résultera", "remplacer", "naturelle")
  do not touch the mathematics.
- **Open.** Proposition 4 for finite flat non-étale G in C: the strongest reduction on the page is "c ≡ 1", i.e. the
  uniqueness of pointed maps from a minimal P; his conditions of admissibility (29/137-140, reader A's stretch) decide
  whether it can hold.

### 2. 29/143: the pro-object π_1^C(S,ξ); Examples I and II (SA12-22, SA12-35; F10)
- **What he does.** Z(G×G') = Z(G)×Z(G'), and the subgroups in C of a G in C satisfy the minimal condition; "Ces
  propriétés formelles suffisent" to build a pro-object of C with epimorphic transitions, π_1^C(S,ξ). Exemple I: C =
  the groups defined by ordinary finite groups, giving the usual fundamental groups. Exemple II: S over a field k, C
  from a category C_0 of finite k-groups stable under products, kernels of pairs and images.
- **Checked (independent re-proof).** Given Prop. 4, the construction is formal: index the pairs (G,α), α ∈ Z(G)
  minimal; a map (G_1,α_1) → (G_2,α_2) with u_*α_1 = α_2 is unique (Prop. 4) and an epimorphism (α_2 comes from the
  image u(G_1) ∈ C, so minimality forces u(G_1) = G_2); two pairs are dominated by a minimal reduction (H,β) of
  (α_1,α_2) ∈ Z(G_1×G_2), which exists by the minimal condition; then lim_→ Hom(G_i,G') → Z(G') is onto (every
  β ∈ Z(G') has a minimal reduction) and injective (dominate, then Prop. 4). Only products, images, the minimal
  condition and Prop. 4 are used; left exactness on kernels is not needed. This agrees with P29 N143.1.
- **Reading corrected on the ink.** The parenthesis of Exemple II reads "(par exemple ~~[one word]~~ les k-groupes
  finis — ce qui ~~est~~ semble raisonnable surtout si k sep. clos)", as Codex K29-09 has it. The Pro reading "ce qui
  rend vraisemblt(?) inutiles(?) les hyp. dessus(?)" is a misreading. So he says nothing about hypotheses becoming
  unnecessary, and P29 N143.2 comments a misreading (its α_p example over k[ε] stays true, but bears on no claim of
  his).
- **Remark (own).** For C from k-groups, rigidity of pointed sections over S reduced and connected is automatic: a
  morphism from S reduced connected to a finite k-scheme G_0 with a ↦ e factors through Spec κ(e) = Spec k. But
  Hom_S(G_0 ×_k S, G'_0 ×_k S) contains non-constant maps (End_S(α_p) = O(S)); the cusp counterexample N033 (package
  MPL_29_095-140) uses [x] ∈ End_S(α_p). Whether Exemple II means Hom_k (constant maps only) decides whether N033
  touches it (open; the page does not say).

### 5. 29/149, 151: the π_1 of a fibre product, "Compléments aux notes IHES" (SA12-35)
- **What he does.** X, Y over S, locally noetherian; X → S proper, surjective, separable, with geometrically connected
  fibres; Y connected. The canonical map π_1(X×_S Y) → π_1(X) ×_{π_1(S)} π_1(Y) is surjective; it is bijective if Y
  has a section, or more generally if some S' over S, connected and nonempty, has an S-map S' → Y with π_1(X') → π_1(X)
  injective. Over S = Spec k: π_1(X×_k Y) ≅ π_1(X) ×_{π_1(k)} π_1(Y) for X proper geometrically connected, "Y connexe
  sur k sans plus". 151 restates it with S' → S finite étale surjective, and draws the two exact rows.
- **Checked (independent re-proof).** With A = π_1(X̄_s), the rows A → π_1(X×_S Y) → π_1(Y) → 1 and A → π_1(X) →
  π_1(S) → 1 (SGA 1 X 1.4, locator unverified) give surjectivity by lifting, and injectivity iff Ker[A → π_1(X)] ⊂
  Ker[A → π_1(X×_S Y)] (as P29 N151.1). A section σ, or an S-map S' → Y with π_1(X') ↪ π_1(X), factors A →
  π_1(X×_S Y) through π_1(X) or π_1(X'), which gives the inclusion. For S' finite étale and connected, X' is connected
  (proper, surjective, connected fibres) and finite étale over X, so π_1(X') ↪ π_1(X). Over a field, A → π_1(X) is
  already injective (SGA 1 IX 6.1, locator unverified), so "Y connexe sans plus" is right; through his criterion one
  takes S' = Spec Ω, Ω algebraically closed over a residue field of Y, using π_1(X_Ω) = π_1(X_k̄) for X proper (SGA 1
  X 1.8, locator unverified).
- **Reading corrected on the ink.** The bracket is "[par exemple fini / S' étale sur S]": the ink has "fini" (four
  letters, two i-dots), as Codex K29-09 reads; the Pro transcription has "si(?)". The word matters: for S' étale but
  not finite the clause is false (S = X = A^1_C, S' = G_m: π_1(X') = Ẑ → π_1(X) = 1 is not injective), and with
  "fini" it is right and agrees with 151.

### 7. 29/153-157: finite étale covers of rank n, quotients X/G (SA12-34)
- **What he does.** 153: "3. Application à la caractérisation …". Prop. 3.2: for f: X → Y finite, (i) f étale of constant
  rank n; (ii) X becomes Y_1 × I_n after a faithfully flat Y_1 → Y; (ii bis) the same with Y_1 a Galois étale cover
  (group G_n); (iii) X ≅ P/H with P a Galois étale cover of group G and H ⊂ G; (iii bis) the same with G = G_n,
  H = G_{n-1} the stabiliser of a point; all equivalent. (i) ⇒ (iii bis) is "due à J.P. Serre": G_n acts on X^n (fibre

  fibre, a G_n-torsor (by a Prop. 2.5 not on these leaves), and P/H → X is an isomorphism. 155-156: the remaining
  implications, (ii) ⇒ (i) by descent of local freeness and of separability, and the corollary "si X est connexe, on
  peut prendre P connexe". 157: Prop. 3.1: X étale over Y, G finite acting by Y-automorphisms: (i) the no-inertia locus
  is open and closed; (ii) X/G exists and X → X/G, X/G → Y are étale; 155 carries its proof (reduction to one orbit of
  components, faithful action, completion) and the corollary "X étale sur Y en x, q: X → X' étale en x ⇒ X' étale
  sur Y en q(x)".
- **Checked (independent re-proof).** Serre's argument: X^n is finite étale over Y, so for s ∈ G_n the fixed locus
  (equaliser of s and id, pullback of the open and closed diagonal) is open and closed; P is the complement of their
  union, finite étale; on a geometric fibre E ≅ I_n, P is the set of injective n-tuples, n! of them, on which G_n acts

  stabiliser G_0, P/H = ⊔_{G_0\G/H} P_0/(G_0 ∩ gHg^{-1}), and X connected forces one double coset. Corollary of 155:
  flatness descends along the faithfully flat local map O_{X',q(x)} → O_{X,x}, and q^*Ω_{X'/Y} ≅ Ω_{X/Y} = 0 at x, so

### 8. 29/158-161: axioms for Galois categories and for a fundamental group in categories (SA12-33)
- **What he does.** 158: "Catégorie galoisienne": (C, F: C → finite sets) with Gal 1 (finite sums and products), Gal 4
  (F commutes with them), connectedness, Gal 2 (decomposition into components, unique), Gal 3 (sections of f: X → Y,
  Y connected, ↔ components mapped isomorphically onto Y), Gal 5 (f between connected objects with F(f) bijective is
  an isomorphism, "[implique F(X) = ∅ → X = 0]"), Gal 6 (quotients X/H of a Galois object, F(X/H) ≅ F(X)/H). Two
  margins propose to replace Gal 3 and Gal 5 by "F(f) injective (X connected) ⇒ f an isomorphism onto a component".
  159: a second list, 1°-9°, with a full subcategory P of "points" and a class of "non ramifiés" morphisms, then π_1(X,x)
  for x ∈ Hom(ξ,X). 160-161: "Groupe fondamental dans les catégories": a class (D) of descent morphisms with (i)-(v),
  example preschemes with (D) "plats et affines [ou plats et quasi-projectifs]", and admissible structural data.
- **Checked (targeted).** "[implique F(X) = ∅ → X = 0]" holds: by his definition 0 is connected (Gal 2 with I = ∅);
  for connected X ≠ 0 with F(X) = ∅, Gal 5 applied to 0 → X would make it an isomorphism; for general X apply Gal 2
  and Gal 4 (as P29 N158.1).
- **What is wrong (small).** Because 0 counts as connected, the two margin axioms fail for X = 0: F(0) = ∅ → F(Y) is
  injective, but 0 → Y is neither an isomorphism nor onto a component (components are ≠ 0 by Gal 2). They need
  "X connexe ≠ 0". In the same way 6° on 159 fails for X = 0, which 5° makes vacuously "non ramifié" (P29 N159.1 says
  the same in étale terms). The example on 160 needs "fidèlement plats": a flat affine open immersion is not a descent
  morphism (iii) (P29 N160.1); and for "plats et quasi-projectifs", condition (iv) (descent of a morphism of D along
  one of D) is not effective in general without a compatible relatively ample sheaf (SGA 1 VIII 7.7-7.9, locator
  unverified).
- **Open.** Whether the 158 list (without fibre products) implies the SGA 1 V 4 axioms: not settled on the page; the
  later "Catégories multigaloisiennes" (209-217) take it up.

### 9. 29/162-163: proper morphisms, finite cokernel, the nodal curve (SA12-34)
- **What he does.** f: X → Y proper dominant, Y locally noetherian, normal, connected: π_1(X) → π_1(Y) has finite
  cokernel (reduce to the generic point, π_1(η) → π_1(Y) being onto; make X geometrically connected over a finite
  separable K'/K). N.B.: false for Y not normal, e.g. X the normalisation of an integral curve with an ordinary double

  y_0, one nearby); for f universally open "[p. ex. Y normale], je crois que [l'on] peut alors construire les f_*(X_1)
  pour X_1 étale sur X" (163).
- **Checked (independent re-proof).** A closed point of the (nonempty) generic fibre of the dominating component has
  residue field L finite over K, and G_L → G_K has open image; with π_1(η) ↠ π_1(Y) (Y normal) the image of π_1(X) is

- **Computation, sharpening N162.2.** The refutation works for every integral curve Y with one ordinary node, not only a

  "π_1(X') = 0" needs X' = P^1.
- **What is wrong.** "[p. ex. Y normale]" does not give universal openness for a proper dominant f (the blow-up of A^2,

  unverified).
