### 33/100-110 (sheets 99-109): "Formulaire faisant intervenir ⊗^L et D^-" (SA13-2, SA13-3)
- **What he does.** (1) On any ringed topos: F flat iff Hom(F, I) is injective for every injective I; every module is a
  quotient of a flat one (the A_U); hence flat resolutions in D^-, D^- = K^-(flat)/quasi-isomorphisms, and ⊗^L.
  Hypertor; Lf^* commutes with ⊗^L, first for flat f, then (33/104, "Remarque sur changement de base non plat") for any
  f; transitivity L(gf)^* = Lf^*Lg^*; adjunction Hom(Lf^*L, K) = Hom(L, Rf_*K) and its internal form
  Rf_*RHom(Lf^*L, K) = RHom(L, Rf_*K) (he calls it "formule de projection"). (2) Why the coefficients A = Z/nZ force D^-
  and unbounded arguments; a table of where Rf_*, Rf_!, Lf^*, Rf^! are defined (33/110-112).
- **Checked (re-derived).** (a) The flatness criterion in both directions (for the converse, embed M'⊗F in an injective I
  and extend along M'⊗F → M⊗F). (b) ⊗ of bounded-above flat complexes respects quasi-isomorphisms, so ⊗^L is well
  defined. (c) His margin question at 33/102, "est-ce vrai dans le contexte des topos généraux ?": yes. For any morphism
  of ringed topoi, f^* = O_X ⊗_{f^{-1}O_Y} f^{-1}(-) sends flats to flats and acyclic bounded-above flat complexes to
  acyclic ones, which is what he writes himself on 33/104. (d) His margin at 33/106 is right: the quick proof of the
  adjunction (K injective, L flat, Hom^•(f^*L, K) = Hom^•(L, f_*K)) computes Hom_D(L, Rf_*K) only when f_* keeps
  injectives, i.e. when f is flat. His dévissage to L = A_{Y'} and the margin computation with the localization
  i: Y' → Y are correct (Rf'_*(K|X') = (Rf_*K)|Y'). (e) The table on 33/110-112: Rf_! has finite cohomological dimension
  for Y quasi-compact by proper base change (fibre dimension bounded); Lf^* = f^* is exact for constant coefficient rings.
- **Wrong / open.** 33/110: "C'est peut-être vrai dès que Y est noethérien" (finite cohomological dimension of Rf_*).
  This does not hold as stated for Z/n with n divisible by a residue characteristic: hunch H2 gives a candidate

### 33/112-120 (sheets 111-119): projection formula and Künneth (SA13-3, SA13-4)
- **What he does.** "Prop.": the projection formula for Rf_*, with M locally constant constructible, or f qcqs and M
  locally constant, under finite cohomological dimension of f_*. "Théorème": f compactifiable, Y quasi-compact,
  L ∈ D(X), M ∈ D^-(Y): Rf_!(L) ⊗^L M = Rf_!(L ⊗^L f^*M). Proof: reduce to f proper; define f_*(L) ⊗ M → f_*(L ⊗ M) →
  f_*(C(L⊗M)); reduce to L, M in degree 0, Y affine, M = i_!A_{Y'} with i affine étale; compute with
  i_!(Q) ⊗ P = i_!(Q ⊗ i^*P) and base change for étale i (33/116, margin "O.K."); a margin offers the reduction to a point
  by proper base change instead. Corollary (Künneth): Rh_!(g'^*L ⊗^L f'^*M) = Rf_!L ⊗^L Rg_!M for f: X → S, g: Y → S
  compactifiable, S quasi-compact. Special case in each degree when F or G is flat and the R^if_!F or the R^jg_!G are
  flat. 33/118: no better Künneth is to be hoped for (Tor terms). For cohomology without supports over an algebraically
  closed k there is no good Künneth with Z/p coefficients (p = char k), "même pour H^1". But if X or Y is proper,
  R(f×g)_*(L ⊠^L M) = Rf_*L ⊠^L Rg_*M. "Je pense" the same holds with neither proper when n is prime to the
  characteristic, "démontrable à l'aide du th. de dualité locale". 33/120: these Künneth results need neither n prime to
  p nor duality (Verdier proved Künneth first with global duality); the projection formula fails for Rf_* in place of
  Rf_!; but it holds for Rf_* when M has locally constant cohomology of finite type.
- **Checked (re-derived).** (a) The proof of the Théorème on 33/114-116, step by step, including
  i_!A_{Y'} ⊗ Rf_!L = Ri_!Li^*Rf_!L = Ri_!Rf'_!Lj^*L = Rf_!(j_!A_{X'} ⊗ L) = Rf_!(f^*(i_!A_{Y'}) ⊗ L). In print: SGA 4
  XVII 5.2.9 (locator unverified). (b) The Künneth corollary from two projection formulas and proper base change; in print
  SGA 4 XVII 5.4.3 (cited so by SGA 5 III 1.6.4, s5.tex). (c) The special case on 33/116. If F or G is flat, the
  external tensor product is underived. If all R^if_!F are flat, a bounded-above flat representative K of Rf_!F has flat
  cycles and boundaries (descending induction), so the classical Künneth sequence has no Tor_1 term and
  H^n = ⊕ H^i ⊗ H^j. Correct. (d) "même pour H^1" (33/118; the Pro pass put "(?)"): true. Independent proof: over k
  algebraically closed of characteristic p, H^1(A^n, Z/p) = k[x_1..x_n]/℘(k[x]), ℘(c) = c^p − c. Modulo ℘, every class is
  uniquely a k-combination of monomials x^a whose exponents are not all divisible by p (λm ≡ λ^p m^p, and the constants
  lie in ℘(k)). The class of st in H^1(A^2) is such a basis monomial, while H^1(A^1)⊗H^0 + H^0⊗H^1(A^1) is spanned by
  the monomials in s alone and in t alone. So the Künneth map is not onto in degree 1. (e) The proof of (*) when f is
  proper: projection formula for f' proper, proper base change, and the projection formula over S = Spec k, where every
  sheaf is constant. (f) The failure of the projection formula for Rf_* (33/120): with M = j_!A_U the formula would force
  f_*F|(Y−U) = 0 for every F vanishing off f^{-1}U; for f: G_m ⊂ A^1, U = G_m, F = A one has (f_*A)_0 = A ≠ 0. His
  example and his reason are right.
- **His conjecture on 33/118 is a theorem.** Künneth for Rf_* with neither map proper, coefficients prime to p, over a
  field: SGA 4 1/2 Th. finitude 1.9, as SGA 5 III 1.6.4 states (s5.tex: "D'après (SGA 4 1/2 Th. Finitude 1.9), 1.6.4 est
  un isomorphisme"). Deligne proved it with his finiteness theorems and generic base change, not with local duality as
  the page expects.
- **Wrong.** 33/112: the "Prop." writes the same expression Rf_*(L.) ⊗^L M. on both sides. Decided on the scan image
  (check event): the ink has it so, with a small insertion "M." under the second f_*. The intended right side is

- **Open on the page.** The Prop.'s hypotheses are run together ("M à ch. bornée ... loc. cte et constructible
  (= pseudo-cohérent) ou f qu. qs qu. cp. et M à ch. loc. constante --- sous réserve f_* de dim. ch. finie"). The clean

  local terms). The coefficient problem: A = Z/ℓ^ν is not a field, the H^i are not free, Tr f^(i) is undefined. The
  ℓ-adic way out was "indiquée dans mon exposé Bourbaki" (Séminaire Bourbaki exp. 279, Dec. 1964; my identification,
  locator unverified). He wants the ℓ-adic formula to be the formal limit of formulas at each finite level, with
  RΓ_X(A_X) and RΓ(f) in place of the H^i and the f^(i). 33/130-136: pseudo-coherent and finite-Tor-dimension complexes
  over a commutative ring A; perfect = both. The trace Tr_*u of an endomorphism in D(A) is unique under (α)
  Σ(−1)^i Tr u_i for strictly perfect complexes and (β) invariance under isomorphism in D(A), or under (a) degree-0 free
  plus (b) additivity on mapping cylinders; Tr uv = Tr vu; Tr(u ⊗^L v) = Tr u Tr v; existence is reduced to vanishing on
  acyclic complexes. The intrinsic form: L^∨ = RHom(L, A), L = L^∨∨, (L⊗L')^∨ = L^∨⊗L'^∨, RHom(L,M) = L^∨⊗^L M; the
  pairing Hom_D(L,M) × Hom_D(M,L) → A with ⟨u,v⟩ = ⟨v,u⟩ = Σ(−1)^i Tr(vu)^i = Σ(−1)^i Tr(uv)^i, and
  Σ(−1)^i Tr u^i = ⟨u, id⟩.
- **Checked (re-derived).** Uniqueness and existence of Tr_*: homotopy invariance (the alternating trace of dh + hd
  telescopes to 0); independence of the strictly perfect model (Tr(g u' f) = Tr(u' f g), with fg homotopic to the
  identity); vanishing on a bounded acyclic complex of finite projectives (it splits into short exact sequences of
  projectives; the alternating sum telescopes). The symmetry of ⟨u,v⟩: Tr(v_i u_i) = Tr(u_i v_i) for maps between

  5.8.1; locator unverified). The "additivité pour un mapping cylindre" is at the level of complexes: the drawing on

  cohomology and trace 0) re-derived.
- **Open.** Nothing internal; the ℓ-adic passage is a wish here (SGA 5 VI and XV in print).

## Hunches

- **H1 (36/29-33): the [M → G] duality is Deligne's later duality of 1-motives, drafted for SGA 7.** On these sheets
  A = [M → G] with G an extension of an abelian scheme by a torus, the "biextension" of (A, A') as a class in
  Ext^1(A ⊗^L A', G_m), the torsion _nA = Ext^0(Z/n, A) with 0 → _nG → _nA → M/nM → 0, and a perfect pairing proved by
  filtration. That is the content of Deligne, Théorie de Hodge III (1974), §10 (1-motives, the dual 1-motive, the Weil
  pairing on T_ℓ) (locator unverified), and of the "faux schémas abéliens" and mixed motives of weight 1 that cote 37
  pairs (sweep-2 flag on K37-03). Grounds: the objects and the pairing coincide; SGA 7 IX §§9-10 uses [M → G]-type data
  for semistable reduction. What would settle it: compare with Hodge III §10.2 and with SGA 7 IX 9-10.
- **H2 (33/110): "peut-être vrai dès que Y est noethérien" fails for coefficients divisible by a residue characteristic.**
  Candidate: Y the spectrum of a strictly henselian DVR of mixed characteristic (0, p) whose residue field has infinite
  p-degree, j the inclusion of the generic point, F = Z/p; the stalk of R^qj_*Z/p at the closed point is H^q(K, Z/p), and
  cd_p(K) is infinite when [k:k^p] is (Kato; Gabber-Orgogozo, Invent. Math. 174 (2008); locator unverified). For n
  invertible the positive results need finite dimension and quasi-excellence (Gabber).
- **H3 (33/172-174): the global 2n bound for sheaves with injective fibres.** His question "est-il vrai que C est limite
  ind. filtrante des faisceaux const. à fibres injectives ??" is a Lazard theorem for étale sheaves of Z/N-modules (for
  this A, injective fibres = flat). If it holds, Ext^i(F, C) = 0 for i > 2n follows from the constructible case; print
  (SGA 5 I 1.5) proves only dim.inj ≤ 4n. I expect the Lazard statement to hold for noetherian schemes (each flat
  constructible sheaf is locally a sum of j_!-type pieces), but I have not found it in print.
- **H4 (33/166): the research list maps onto the next decade.** a) Illusie's derived exterior powers (LNM 239, 283);
  b) analytic duality (Ramis-Ruget-Verdier); c) SGA 4 Vbis and Deligne's Hodge III; e) "site stratifiant" as the
  crystalline/stratification programme (Berthelot, LNM 407). Identification by subject only; locators unverified.
- **H5 (36/10-27): the strong form of 7.1 is the open part, and the printed exposés took the weak form.** The margins
  "pas prouvé sous cette forme forte" and print's SGA 7 XXI Théorème 1.4 (non-nilpotence only) agree. The strong form
  (Frobenius elements of G_0 with every prescribed trace on all of V_0) would say that the monodromy of the unit-root part
  of H^{n−1}(Y, O) of large-degree sections is big; links: Katz's later work on monodromy of families and on
  ordinarity of generic hypersurfaces (Deligne, Illusie; locators unverified).
- **H6 (36/4, 36/82-84): the Artin-Mazur question was later settled by others.** Deligne's "As tu vérifié si
  l'accouplement d'A. M. coïncide avec le tien ?" and Grothendieck's unproved sentence on 36/84 (the discriminant form of
  the intersection matrix is the obstruction pairing) are the statement that Grothendieck's pairing on component groups
  of Jacobians equals the intersection-matrix pairing, proved by Bosch and Lorenzini (Invent. Math. 148, 2002); for
  imperfect residue fields the pairing can fail to be perfect (Bertapelle-Bosch, 2000). Locators unverified.
- **H7 (36/71): Ker(Γ → NS(X_0)) = Z/d in all relative dimensions?** True in relative dimension 1 (multidegree argument).
  In general the class O(Σ(d_i/d)D_i)|X_0 is d-torsion and numerically trivial; the question is whether it is in
  Pic^0(X_0). Links: Raynaud, Spécialisation du foncteur de Picard (Publ. IHES 38, 1970); Gabber-Liu-Lorenzini on the
  index (2013) for 36/61 a). Locators unverified.
- **H8 (cote 39): the axioms are Weierstrass systems.** Conditions a)-d) plus the formal preparation theorem of 39/19-20
  are close to the Weierstrass systems of Denef and Lipshitz (Math. Ann. 1984; locator unverified), for which noetherianity,
  faithful flatness of the completion and the implicit function theorem hold; that would answer both margin questions of
  39/4 and 39/13. The flow and Pfaff closures (g), h)) go beyond them, towards Pfaffian/o-minimal function classes
  (Khovanskii). Grounds: the same list of closure properties.
