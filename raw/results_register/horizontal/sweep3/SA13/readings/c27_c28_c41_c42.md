## Cote 42, "Réseaux d'anneaux locaux": scans 2-7 (K42-01) (SA13-68)

**42:2-3, networks of local rings.**
- A "réseau d'anneaux locaux" is an ordered set with an inductive system of local rings, localizations O_x → O_y, and a bijection {y ≥ x} ↔ Spec O_x.
- There are morphisms and a functor ρ from preschemes to networks.
- **Claim (42:3), stated without proof:** for S locally noetherian, ρ is fully faithful on S-preschemes locally of finite type. Margin: "est-il inutile de mettre des conditions de finitude sur Y ??"

**42:4, the Proposition.** On a sober locally noetherian space, a sheaf F whose generization maps are locally injective along closures has Γ(X, F) = lim F̃.
- **Checked:** the agreement set of a section with a coherent family is stable under generization and contains an open of each x̄ at its points, so it is open (constructibility); then glue.

**42:5-6, Cor. 1 (Γ(X, O_X) as coherent families) and Lemma 2.** If A is noetherian, then A_f → A_p is injective for some f ∉ p, and (A_f)_q → A_p is injective for q ⊃ p. **Checked.**

**42:6-7, Cor. 2 and Lemma 3: morphisms X → Y as coherent families of germs.**
- **Checked, with a hypothesis.** It is true for Y separated over S. That is what "S-schéma" means in EGA I (préschéma = today's scheme).
- For a non-separated Y it fails: take the line with doubled origin and a DVR.
- So Cor. 3 (S-morphisms = S-morphisms of networks) needs Y separated, or another argument using the point map. The full-faithfulness claim of 42:3 for préschémas therefore remains open.

**42:7, the Remark: the hypothesis of the Proposition is not superfluous.**
- Example: X = Spec k[T], Φ a set of closed points, F = ∐_{x∈Φ} ε_x with F_a = e at the generic point a, and the family (ξ_x = unit, ξ_a = e).
- **Decided on the scan (42.pdf page 7, 300 dpi): the ink reads "finie".** With Φ finite, the hypothesis holds (U = X − Φ at a), and the family is a section, the finite sum.
- **Refuted as written.** The example needs Φ infinite: then the hypothesis fails at a, and the family is not a section, because sections of the direct sum have finite support on the quasi-compact X. "finie" is his slip for "infinie".
