### 2.2 Scans 21-29: §5, quasi-unipotent representations

- **What he does.** Definition 5.1: u unipotent if every u(g) is unipotent, "ou encore son polynôme car.
  det(T id − u) est égal à T^n − 1"; quasi-unipotent if unipotent on an open subgroup. 5.2: G = Zariski closure of
  u(π) (reduced); u unipotent iff G unipotent; margin: G/G° ≅ π/U, U = u^{-1}(G°(R)). 5.3: for R topological and u
  continuous, u quasi-unipotent iff G° unipotent (both directions proved). 5.4: in characteristic 0, g ∈ G(R) is
  unipotent iff g ∈ G°(R); so U is the largest subgroup acting unipotently. 5.5: G° unipotent has a commutator
  filtration with vector-group quotients; H^i(Γ, V) = 0 for Γ finite and V uniquely divisible, so G ≅ Γ·G°
  (semi-direct, "de façon unique") and Γ acts naturally on E. 5.6: R = Q_ℓ, G° commutative ("quasi-abélienne"),
  G° ≅ G_a^d: u(U) is a lattice Z_ℓ^d spanning G°(Q_ℓ); G(R) is the push-out of the extension π/N of π/U by U/N along
  U/N → U/N ⊗ Q_ℓ; a universal extension of Γ by U^{ab}(ℓ) ⊗ Q_ℓ is announced. 5.7: in the strictly local case,
  U^{ab}(ℓ) ≅ Z_ℓ(1) canonically (5.7.1), Γ acts on it trivially "(3.15)", so G ≅ G° × Γ with G° = 0 or the vector
  group of Q_ℓ(1) (5.7.2-3); a quasi-unipotent ℓ-adic representation of I is given by U and an algebraic
  representation of Γ × W(Q_ℓ(1)); margin: the projective system Γ_i × W(Q_ℓ(1)) (Γ_i = I/V_i) has a ⊗-category of
  algebraic representations equivalent to that of the quasi-unipotent ℓ-adic representations of I.
- **Print.** Not in Deligne's summary, which defines quasi-unipotence elementwise (0.0.7). The ⊗-category statement
  of the margin is, in later language, the description of a quasi-unipotent representation of I as a pair
  (ρ_0, N), ρ_0 with finite image and N nilpotent commuting with it, ρ(σ) = ρ_0(σ) exp(t_ℓ(σ)N) (Deligne, "Les
  constantes des équations fonctionnelles des fonctions L", 1973, §8; locator unverified).
- **Checked.**
  1. Scan 21, reading decided on the scan (250 dpi): the ink has "det(T id − u) est égal à T^n − 1". His slip: the
     characteristic polynomial of a unipotent automorphism of an n-dimensional space is (T − 1)^n. Correction row.
  2. 5.3 and 5.4 (targeted proof check): right as written. In 5.3 the converse uses that u(π) is a finite union of
     cosets of u(U), so the closure H of u(U) has finite index in G and H = G°. In 5.4 a unipotent element maps to a
     unipotent element of the finite group Γ, trivial in characteristic 0. The parenthesis "(i.e. G est unipotent)" of
     5.4 should read G° (Work notes; not checked on the scan).
  3. 5.5 (targeted proof check): H^i(Γ, V) = 0 holds for i > 0, not i ≥ 0 (H^0 = V^Γ); with that, the extension
     splits; the splitting is unique only up to conjugation by G°(R) (H^1 = 0), so "Γ opère de façon naturelle sur E"
     holds up to that conjugacy. In 5.7 the splitting is unique: Γ acts trivially on G°, and conjugating a complement
     by v ∈ G° changes γ to (v − γv)γ = γ. Both qualifications agree with the Work notes and, for the degree, with
     Michel Hua's project.
  4. 5.6 (targeted proof check): a compact subgroup of Q_ℓ^d is a closed bounded Z_ℓ-submodule, free of rank d' ≤ d,
     and Zariski density forces d' = d; the push-out description of G(R) is right (the comparison map is an
     isomorphism on the kernel V ⥲ G°(R) and on the quotient).
  5. 5.7 and the margin, independent re-proof: for U ⊂ I open normal, U^{ab}(ℓ) ≅ Z_ℓ(1) (3.14.2), and I acts on it
     trivially (Corollary 3.16), so G ≅ G° × Γ. For the ⊗-equivalence: on an open normal V ⊂ I the action is
