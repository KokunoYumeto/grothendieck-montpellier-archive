### The notes on representations (even scans)

**90/2, Lemma 1.** A is a Dedekind ring and M a flat A-module; Ñ is the saturation of a submodule N. He lists "conditions équivalentes":
- a) Ñ is of finite type for every N of finite type;
- b) M has no submodule isomorphic to A^{(p)} = ∪_n p^{-n} = Γ(S - {p}, O_S);
- c) no M_p contains a copy of K;
- d) M = lim M_i over saturated finite M_i, each a direct factor of the next;
- e) M ↪ A^I, which is sufficient, and necessary when M is countably generated.

What holds (SA02-R90-01):
- a) ⇔ d); b) ⇔ c); a) ⇒ b); e) ⇒ a); a) together with countable generation ⇒ e), since M is then a countable increasing union of direct factors, hence projective.
- b) ⇒ a) **fails** as soon as A has infinitely many maximal ideals. Take M = Σ_p p^{-1}·Z ⊂ ℚ over A = Z: it has no submodule ≅ Z[1/p], yet the saturation of Z ⊂ M is M, which is not of finite type.
- b) ⇒ a) holds for A semi-local.

The ink (200 dpi) has a "⇕" beside c) and a vertical double arrow beside a), read "⇕" or "⇑". Either reading claims b) ⇒ a) (SA02-K90-01). We found the counterexample independently. The stage-A dossier has the same one (its §3, CL-SA02-500), so on this point it is confirmed. Everything later in the cote uses condition a)/d), so nothing downstream is affected.

**90/4, Lemma 2.** If the algebra B of an affine A-group G satisfies these conditions, every finite submodule of B lies in a finite N with Ñ = N and ΔN ⊂ N ⊗ N.
- The proof: G × G acts on B. Invariance under G × e (ΔN ⊂ N ⊗ B) and under e × G is stable under saturation, because (B/Ñ) ⊗ B is torsion-free. Then (N ⊗ B) ∩ (B ⊗ N) = N ⊗ N when B/N is flat.
- Right (SA02-R90-02).

**90/6.**
- If G is of finite type, a saturated finite N that generates B and is stable gives a faithful representation (on points; not a closed immersion). The stage-A dossier notes that a stronger, crossed-out proposition stood here.
- Over a field, every affine group is the projective limit of affine groups of finite type with faithfully flat transitions (standard).

**90/8-10, 90/12.** Suppose B = lim B_i with B/B_i flat, ΔB_i ⊂ B_i ⊗ B_i and B_i projective of finite type.
- The G-modules of finite type are the union of the categories of "i-admissible" ones, and these are the modules over the algebras U_i = B̌_i (a strict projective system).
- Hence every G-module of finite type is a quotient of a G-module that is projective of finite type: U_i ⊗ F → E for any projective F → E. Right (SA02-R90-02).
- He then states the same over a scheme S on which every coherent module is a quotient of a locally free one (for instance S with an ample invertible sheaf, or S noetherian regular).
- His NB on 90/10: for G of finite type the conditions of Lemma 1 mean that every irreducible component of G has all its fibres non-empty. This is consistent with the example G = (identity) ⊔ (a generic point) inside (ℤ/2)_A, whose algebra A × K violates b). Not proved here.

**90/14-24: the sheaf version.** G is flat affine over S as above, and X is an S-scheme with a G-action and a G-linearized relatively ample L.
- 1) Every quasi-coherent F ⊂ E has a smallest G-stable quasi-coherent hull F', compatible with flat base change, and of finite type if F is.
- 2) For X qcqs, E is the union of its G-stable submodules of finite type.
- 3) If moreover S has the resolution property, every E of finite type is a quotient of a G-module f^*(H)(-n) that is locally free of finite type.
- The proof passes through Γ^•(E) = ⊕_n f_*(E(n)), a fully faithful functor (via M ↦ M̃), and the smallest G-stable sub-O_S-module N ⊃ Γ^•(F), which is graded and an A^•-submodule. F' is then Ñ, and its finite type comes from the G-span N^n of finitely many generating sections in degree n (90/24).
- His own margin on 90/24 says "Rédiger mieux en début de la démonstration ...". The argument is right as sketched (SA02-R90-02).
- This is the equivariant resolution property. It is the setting of J.-P. Serre, "Groupe de Grothendieck des schémas en groupes réductifs déployés", Publ. Math. IHÉS 34 (1968), 37-52, §§1-2, and of R. W. Thomason, "Equivariant resolution, linearization, and Hilbert's fourteenth problem over arbitrary base schemes", Adv. Math. 65 (1987), 16-34 (locators to be checked). The title "KG(X)" on the cover fits that use.

## Cote 91, scans 2-18: representability of Hom functors by non-projective methods (SA02-12)

The archive's cover (91/1) reads "Autour de Néron. [Greenberg]-Néron. Foncteurs Hom (méthodes non-projectives) : notes manuscrites (s.d.), lettre (1967). Cote n° 91, 142 pages". Its note lists Bourbaki exposés n° 285 (1964-1965), 288 (1966), 316 (1966-1968) and 371 (1969-19..), and manuscript notes (1962-1964), on the versos. 91/2-18: 15 scans are transcribed (K91-01 and the Pro); 91/4 and 91/15 are blank (viewed).

**91/2-3: titles.**
- "Représentabilité des Foncteurs Hom, ∏_{T/S} X/T de Greenberg etc."
- A plan: "Représentabilité relative de ∏_{X/S} P/X (sans hyp. projectives)"; "Flattening functor"; "Passage au quotient"; Hom_S(X, Y); Aut_S(X).

**91/5-7: Theorem.**
- *Statement.* f: X → S is flat of finite type over a locally noetherian S, and Y → X is a monomorphism ("mono", K91; "mor.", Pro) of finite type. Then ∏_{X/S} Y/X, the functor T ↦ [X_T = Y_T], is representable iff it commutes with adic projective limits of artinian rings when Y → X is a (closed) immersion, and it is then represented by a (closed) subscheme of S. Margin: "il suffit que Y → X soit un monomorphisme".
- *The proof* checks Grothendieck's conditions 1°-8° for representability by unramified separated morphisms:
  - 4°-6°: X_A flat, so O_{X_A} → O_{X_A} ⊗ A' is injective;
  - 7°: by flatness X_T is the schematic closure of X_U, and Y_T is closed and contains it;
  - 8°: generic freeness (EGA IV 11).
- All right (SA02-R91-01). This is the setting of SGA 3 VIII 6.4 (essentially free morphisms) and of Raynaud-Gruson's purity (M. Raynaud, L. Gruson, Invent. Math. 13 (1971), 1-89, part I, §4; locators to be checked).

**91/8-9: the adic condition (L) and condition R.**
- (L): for T local noetherian (complete, with algebraically closed residue field), if Y_T contains an open neighbourhood of the special fibre of X_T, then X_T = Y_T.
- Corollaries: X proper, OK. For X a group and Y a subgroup the check reduces to discrete valuation rings. The condition holds a) when X has connected fibres, and b) when finitely many subschemes G_i, finite over S, have G_{i,s̄}·G⁰_{s̄} covering G_{s̄}.
- Re-proved:
  - a) by density of the generic fibre;
  - b) because finite G_i have specializations, an open set is stable under generization, and Y_T is a subgroup.
- Condition R (91/9) is the group form of purity: every open subgroup of G_T containing the special fibre is G_T.
- The corollary on 91/9 is stated with a gap. The group containing Y is missing ("un sous-groupe fermé de, ."), and the flatness of X, the theorem's hypothesis, is not restated (SA02-K91-03).

**91/10: Cent, Transp, Norm.**
- For u: H → G with H flat of finite type satisfying R (for instance with connected fibres): Cent_G(u) is representable by a subscheme, closed if G is separated.
- Transp_G(u, K) and Norm_G(H) are representable as well.
- The stage-A dossier notes that an unconditional closedness was crossed out. Consistent with SGA 3 VIII 6.5-6.7 and XI 6.11 for the flat cases (locators to be checked).

**91/11-12: the flattening functor.**
- *Statement.* f is of finite type, E is coherent on X, and F(S') = {∅} iff E_{S'} is flat (resp. faithfully flat) over S'. F is representable iff it commutes with adic limits of artinian rings.
- *The checks:*
  - 4°-6°, descent of flatness along an injective local homomorphism of artinian rings: true for all modules, since flat means free over an artinian local ring and A^{(I)} ⊂ A'^{(I)};
  - 7°: the associated points of J lie in U, because T is the schematic closure of U;
  - 8°: generic flatness.
  - All right (SA02-R91-02).
- **The reformulation (L) of 91/12 is wrong as written.** It reads: "pour tt T ... tel que F_T sur X_T/T soit plat sur T dans un voisinage de la fibre générique ..., F_T soit plat sur T". The ink (viewed) and both witnesses have "générique".
  - *Why it is wrong:* commuting with adic limits means "E_T flat along the closed fibre ⇒ E_T flat". By the local flatness criterion, flatness along the closed fibre is flatness on a neighbourhood of the special (closed) fibre. So "générique" must read "spéciale", as in the (L) of 91/8 (SA02-K91-01).
  - *Counterexample with "générique":* E = k(x), for a closed point x of the special fibre of P¹ over a complete discrete valuation ring. E is flat near the generic fibre and not flat, yet its functor commutes with adic limits.
  - With "générique", Corollary 1 ("O.K. si f est propre") would also fail. With "spéciale" it follows: the image of the non-flat locus is closed and misses the closed point.
  - The stage-A dossier does not note this.
- His margin N.B. (K91; absent from the Pro): one may assume dim T = 1, T irreducible, and T_red the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring.

**91/12-13: Corollaries 2 and 3.**
- Corollary 2: the case E = O_X with X a group satisfying R.
- Corollary 3: for f of finite presentation, the following are equivalent: (i) f = f'f'' with f'' faithfully flat and f' a monomorphism ("mm"); (i bis) the same with f'' flat; (ii) pr_1: X ×_S X → X is flat. The factorization is unique, with S' = X/R, R = X ×_S X.
  - (i) ⇒ (i bis) ⇒ (ii) holds, because pr_1 is a base change of f'' when f' is a monomorphism.
  - (ii) ⇒ (i) comes from the theorem in its faithfully flat version: under (L), X_T → T is covering.
  - Right (SA02-R91-03).

**91/14: two corollaries.**
- *The pseudo-torsor.* G of finite presentation acts on P with P formally principal homogeneous; then there should be a monomorphism S' ↪ S with P → S' faithfully flat and P a G_{S'}-torsor. This **needs G flat**, which both witnesses lack (SA02-K91-02).
  - Counterexample: S = Spec k[t] and G = S ⊔ V(t) ⊂ (ℤ/2)_S (the identity and, over t = 0, the other point), acting on P = G. G is finitely presented and P is formally principal homogeneous. The unit section forces S' = S, but G → S is not flat.
  - The stage-A dossier gives the same correction with G = Spec Z[x]/(2x, x²) (CL-SA02-545).
  - With G flat, pr_1: P ×_S P ≅ G ×_S P → P is flat and Corollary 3 applies.
- *Quotients.* If u: G → H is of finite presentation with N = Ker u flat, then G/N exists. Right: pr_1: G ×_H G ≅ N ×_S G → G is flat, and Corollary 3 applies over H (SA02-R91-03).

**91/16-18: the relative unramified Hom theorem.**
- *Setting.* Z → X → Y over S, with f, h flat and proper and g of finite type. His margins: "Énoncer plutôt en termes de ∏_{X/S} P/X → ∏_{Z/S} Q/Z"; "inutile si Z → X est surjectif"; "l'hypothèse S loc. noeth peut être remplacée par des hypothèses de présentation finie".
- *(a)* The locus where Hom_S(X, Y) → Hom_S(Z, Y), u ↦ u∘φ, is unramified is open.
  - The modules M_u and M_w corepresent J ↦ Hom(u^*Ω¹, O ⊗ J), by flatness and properness (EGA III 7.7.6).
  - Unramifiedness at s means M_w ⊗ k(s) → M_u ⊗ k(s) is surjective, and Nakayama gives openness.
  - Right (SA02-R91-05).
- *The theorem.* Hom_S(X, Y; φ) → Hom_S(Z, Y) is representable by unramified morphisms; hence Hom_S(X, Y; φ) is representable when Hom_S(Z, Y) is.
  - The principle of proof: the criterion for unramified separated preschemes. Only the last two "conditions de modération" need work: the extension problem over a one-dimensional local base with sets of extensions that are formally principal homogeneous under Hom(M_{u_0}, J).
  - The page breaks off at "Par hypothèse on tient des éléments aux coins". The rest is on 91/19 ff. (SA03).
  - His margin: "Tout serait à simplifier".
  - The stage-A dossier records the obstruction step to be corrected on 91/20-21, outside this stretch. It also records that a letter to Anantharaman of 11 September 1967 is a dated witness of this Hom theorem.
