#### 45:34-36. Hyperalgebra of an affine group scheme; descent of distributions and functions (SA18-1)

- **What he does.** G affine group scheme over A, "A-simple" (smooth), Aff_A(G) its affine algebra, U_A(G) its
  hyperalgebra (distributions at the origin), the pairing <f, X>, U_A(G) = A-linear forms on Aff_A(G) killing a power of
  the augmentation ideal; the bimodule actions X*f, f*X and the action f ⌟ X of Aff on U.
  - **Prop. 1 (45:35).** For A ⊂ B and X in U_B(G) = U_A(G) ⊗_A B: X in U_A(G) ⟺ X*Aff_A ⊂ Aff_A ⟺ Aff_A*X ⊂ Aff_A ⟺
    <X, Aff_A> ⊂ A.
  - **Prop. 2 (45:36).** G A-connected, f in Aff_B(G): f in Aff_A ⟺ f ⌟ U_A ⊂ U_A ⟺ <f, U_A> ⊂ A.
- **Checked.** Prop. 1 in full: (ii)⇒(iii) by <X, f> = ε(X*f); (iii)⇒(i) because X kills I_B^n ⊇ I_A^n, so X|Aff_A lies
  in Hom_A(Aff_A/I_A^n, A) ⊂ U_A, and its B-extension is X. This uses that Aff_A/I_A^n is finite projective (smoothness,
  his "A-simple"). Prop. 2, (iii)⇒(ii): his three-line argument through Prop. 1 is right.
- **Open on the page.** (iii)⇒(i) of Prop. 2: he sets up the square C → C_B, Ĉ = Hom_A(U, A) → Ĉ_B and stops. What is
  needed is C_B ∩ Ĉ = C inside Ĉ_B. I re-derived it for A a field (a k-basis of B containing 1 and injectivity of
  C → Ĉ for G connected) and for G_a and G_m over any A ⊂ B (coefficientwise: B[u] ∩ A[[u]] = A[u]). For a general
  extension A ⊂ B it needs a flatness hypothesis on B/A that the page does not state. The statement is the Kostant
  criterion for Z-forms (Kostant, "Groups over Z", Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 9, 1966) (locator unverified).

#### 45:37-40. Lie algebra cohomology and the rigidity of group schemes (SA18-1)

- 45:37 is a heading sheet: "Cohomologie de l'algèbre de Lie et coh. des groupes".
- **45:38.** G smooth over S = Spec A, A Artin local, g = Lie(G).
  - (1) H^0(g_0, g_0) = 0 ⟺ Ad: G → Aut_S(g) is unramified, and a closed immersion if G_0 has no nontrivial étale
    subgroup in the kernel.

  - (3) Given only g, H = Aut_S(g) has Lie(H_0) = Der(g_0). It is smooth over S if moreover H^2(g_0, g_0) = 0, which
    "signifie sauf erreur qu'il n'y a pas de variations de structure infinitésimales non triviales de g_0".
  - Margin: then Aut_S(g) ≅ Aut_S(G), and "cela suffirait à construire les Tchevalley sur Z".
- **45:40.** Under (1)-(3), G has no nontrivial infinitesimal deformations. Lifting G (connected) is equivalent to
  lifting g, with the obstruction in H^3(g, g). Two questions: (a) a map H*(G, g) → H*(g, g) and a spectral sequence
  between them ("la différence entre le global et l'infinitésimal"); (b) for G semisimple adjoint, is
  H*(G, g) = H*(g, g) = 0 "??".
- **Checked.**
  - Lie(ker Ad) = ker(ad) = z(g) = H^0(g, g), so (1) holds.
  - For (2) I re-derived the step he leaves implicit, that Aut(g_0) is smooth: dim G_0 ≤ dim Aut(g_0) ≤
    dim Der(g_0) = dim g_0, so equality holds and Aut(g_0) is smooth. Then Ad_0 is étale, and étaleness over the Artin
    base follows by flatness.
  - H^2 as the space of infinitesimal deformations and H^3 as the obstruction space is the Nijenhuis-Richardson theory
    (Bull. AMS 72, 1966; J. Math. Mech. 17, 1967) (locator unverified).
- **Question (b) settled.** Question (b) is answered "no" in characteristic 2 by his own example on 45:42.
  - g = Lie(PGL_2) = gl_2/k·1 has basis h', e, f with [h', e] = e, [h', f] = f, [e, f] = 0.
  - Der(g) = {D : D(h') ∈ ke + kf, D|(ke+kf) arbitrary} has dimension 6, and the inner derivations have dimension 3, so
    dim H^1(g, g) = 3 (computation, check event).
  - So (2) fails, and Aut(g) (which contains GL_2 acting on ke + kf) has dimension 6 > 3. The Chevalley construction of
    the 38 margin therefore fails at p = 2 already for A_1 adjoint.
  - In characteristic 0 both sides vanish: Whitehead's lemmas, and Hochschild's vanishing for reductive groups.
  - The spectral sequence he asks for in (a) exists in characteristic p: the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre sequence for the
    Frobenius kernel G_1 ⊂ G, with H*(G_1, M) the restricted Lie algebra cohomology (Jantzen, Representations of
    Algebraic Groups, I.9 and II.12) (locator unverified).

#### 46:5-9. p-structures, Lang's theorem, and unit F-modules (SA18-6)

- **1) (46:5-7).** G a sheaf of groups on a site, f an endomorphism. He considers the stack Σ of pairs (P, φ), with P a
  G-torsor and φ: P → P such that φ(xg) = φ(x)f(g).
  - Locally φ = φ_a with φ_a(g) = af(g).
  - Σ is a gerbe iff u ↦ f(u)u^{-1} is an epimorphism. It is then the gerbe of N-torsors, N = Ker(f, id).
- **Checked** (targeted proof check).
  - A morphism (G, φ_a) → (G, φ_b) is a left translation by u with ua = bf(u). So the page's "b^{-1}a = f(u)u^{-1}"
    holds as written only for commutative G; in general a = u^{-1}bf(u).
  - The gerbe criterion is unaffected, since u ↦ u^{-1}f(u) and u ↦ f(u)u^{-1} differ by inversion.
  - The automorphism sheaf of (G, f) is the fixed-point sheaf N.
- **2) (46:7-8), Lang.** For G smooth over F_q and f the q-Frobenius, u ↦ f(u)u^{-1} is étale (because Lie(f) = 0) and
  surjective when G is connected.
  - Margin "axiomatique": the same holds for G smooth over S and Lie(f) = 0, surjective if the fibres are connected.
  - **Checked.** Étaleness follows from the fibre criterion, the differential being minus a translation. Surjectivity
    holds because every twisted-conjugacy orbit is open in each connected geometric fibre.
  - Corollary: over S/F_q, (P, φ) ↔ torsors under the constant group G(F_q). Lang (Amer. J. Math. 78, 1956); his
    citation is Serre, Groupes algébriques et corps de classes, p. 119 (locator unverified).
- **3) (46:8-9).** Non-degenerate p-structures φ: E^(p) ≅ E on E locally free of rank n ↔ GL(n, F_p)-torsors ↔ étale
  F_p-local systems ℒ of rank n, with E = ℒ ⊗ O_S.
  - This is Katz's equivalence for unit-root F-crystals mod p (Katz, "p-adic properties of modular schemes", LNM 350,
    Prop. 4.1.1) (locator unverified).

## Hunches (with grounds)

- **H1 (45:38 margin, 45:42).** Constructing the Chevalley groups over Z as Aut^0 of the Chevalley Lie algebra works over
  Z[1/N] exactly where H^0 = H^1 = H^2 (𝔤_{F_p}, 𝔤_{F_p}) = 0, and fails at small primes.
  - Grounds: his (1)-(3); my dim H^1 = 3 for pgl_2 in characteristic 2.
  - Expected bad primes: 2 for A_1, B, C, F_4; 2 and 3 for G_2; perhaps p | n+1 for A_n.
  - Literature: Hogeweij on Chevalley Lie algebras in bad characteristic, Hiss on H^1(G, 𝔤) (locators unverified).
  - Package R1.
- **H2 (45:55, Question 1).** For non-split ρ, a maximal torus T ≅ T_1 need not lie in a Borel.
  - Grounds: his N.B. covers only split T_1. Over F_q the question is whether wτ conjugate to τ in GL(X) forces w
    τ-conjugate to 1 in W. Over U_3 this holds (checked). Triality ^3D_4 is the natural candidate for failure.
  - Package R7.
- **H3 (45:59).** In "vrai pour les formes intérieures" he was thinking of inner forms with a Killing couple. The real
  obstruction to Aut(G) → Out(G) being onto, for inner forms of SL_n, is [D] ≠ [D^op] (the Tits class).
- **H4 (46:9).** The Fitting-ideal argument makes every finitely presented unit F-module locally free on any base. The
  infinite-type counterexample shows the Katz equivalence needs finiteness. This links to the mod-p Riemann-Hilbert
  correspondence for unit F-crystals (Emerton-Kisin; Böckle-Pink) (locators unverified).
- **H5 (46:19).** The object he means is the Frobenius neighbourhood V(I^[p^ν]). His margin ("ne fait intervenir que la
  section marquée") is exactly true for it.
- **H6 (47:10-11).** Over a base, the Chow-theoretic Fourier transform satisfies F(ab) = (-1)^g F(a)*F(b) with no Todd
  factor iff Todd(H^1_dR(A/S)) = 1 in CH_Q(S).
  - In characteristic 0 this holds by Esnault-Viehweg. In characteristic p, van der Geer's relation c(E)c(E^∨) = 1 on
    A_g suggests it too.
  - His pages anticipate the Deninger-Murre decomposition (Crelle 422, 1991), which needs this identity. Links SA07.
  - Package R2.
- **H7 (54:7, SA15).** Oda's theorem answers his question. The full Hodge-de Rham structure of an abelian scheme in
  characteristic p is determined by the BT_1 A[p] through its Dieudonné crystal. This is the bridge to SA15-16
  (`sweep3/SA15/STUDY.md` §2), where 2.7 shows why divided powers are needed for the crystal.
- **H8 (51:19-22).** The abelian subscheme of Pic_{X/S} should exist for X/S smooth projective over a reduced S in every
  characteristic, since the Picard variety of the fibres has constant dimension b_1/2. Over non-normal bases a
  non-reduced Pic^0 (Igusa) could break uniqueness. Package R4.
- **H9 (51:23).** His "analogue th. de pureté pour la bonne réduction" is the purity question for abelian schemes.
  - Mixed characteristic with small ramification: yes (Vasiu-Zink).
  - Otherwise there are counterexamples.
  - The torsor version d) should hold over regular bases where purity holds for the abelian scheme.
  - Links SA15-19 (Néron models and p-divisible groups, 100:48-53). Package R5.
- **H10 (52:10).** His "conoyau de dim 1, fibre Spec k[t]/t^2" is the genus-3 case. He probably had the
  quartic/hyperelliptic dichotomy of genus 3 in mind.
- **H11 (55:8).** His question (is the contraction J ∩ A of a finitely generated ideal of A[T] finitely generated, for A
  coherent) has a negative answer. A counterexample should exist in Soublin's coherent ring with A[T] not coherent.
  Package R6.
- **H12 (45:4-15).** Over imperfect fields of dimension ≤ 1 (F̄_p(t)), extensions by non-solvable infinitesimal
  kernels are the only open case of his surjectivity and H^2 theorems. The Cartan-subalgebra route cannot work, and a
  different argument, perhaps by Weil restriction or by passing to G/G_1, is needed. Package R3.
- **H13 (56:15).** Both degree slips on 56:15 shift by the same amount (2ν-2 for 2ν, 2ν-1 for 2ν). This fits an
  indexing of the cone over Q^n with the cone over Q^{n-1} mixed up. The other rows were computed correctly.
- **H14 (dating, SA18-19).** These are new datings, drawn from the pages:

## Research packages (concepts)

- **R1. Rigidity of Chevalley Lie algebras in small characteristic.**
  - Question: for each simple root system Φ and prime p, and for the adjoint and simply connected Chevalley Lie
    algebras 𝔤 over F_p, decide whether H^i(𝔤, 𝔤) = 0 for i = 0, 1, 2. Decide whether Aut^0_{Z[1/N]}(𝔤_Z) is the adjoint
    Chevalley group scheme exactly over the primes where these vanish.
  - Why: his 45:38 programme ("cela suffirait à construire les Tchevalley sur Z") and his 45:40 question.
  - Rests on: 45:37-42 and 45:18-26; item SA18-1.
  - Strongest reduction: on the page, (1)+(2)+(3) ⇒ Aut^0 smooth and equal to G. In this reading, dim H^1(pgl_2, pgl_2)
    = 3 in characteristic 2, so the construction fails there.
  - Material: the pages; Chevalley bases; a computer algebra computation of Der and H^2 for rank ≤ 4 and p ≤ 7;
    Hogeweij, Hiss, Strade.
- **R2. Fourier identities over a base.**
  - Question: for an abelian scheme A/S of relative dimension g over a smooth base S over a field (characteristic 0, then
    p), prove or refute ψ_D(ab) = (-1)^g ψ_D(a) * ψ_D(b) in CH(Â)_Q with no Todd factor. Equivalently, decide whether
    Todd(H^1_dR(A/S)) = 1 in CH_Q(S).
  - Why: the corrected box of 47:11, and the base-change form of Beauville's identities used in motivic decompositions.
  - Rests on: 47:3-11; `sweep2/REPORTS/cote47_scan009_chi_factor.md`; SA18-13, SA18-14, SA07.
  - Strongest reduction: this reading reduces it to Todd(H^1_dR) = 1. Characteristic 0 follows from Esnault-Viehweg
    (2002). Characteristic p is open to me (van der Geer).
  - Material: cote 47 and 54 transcriptions; Mukai (1981), Beauville (1983), Deninger-Murre (1991), Esnault-Viehweg
    (2002), van der Geer (1999).
- **R3. Infinitesimal kernels over fields of dimension ≤ 1.**
  - Question: let k have characteristic p with Br(K') = 0 for all finite separable K'/k (e.g. F̄_p(t)). Prove or refute
    that H^1_fppf(k, G) → H^1_fppf(k, G/K) is onto for every affine algebraic G and infinitesimal normal K, and that every
    nonabelian H^2 class of an algebraic lien over k is neutral.
  - Why: Lemma 4 of 45:8-9 and case (ii) of 45:14 rest on a false Frattini step. The rest of his theorems stands.
  - Rests on: 45:2-17; SA18-2, SA18-6.
  - Strongest reduction: by his Lemmas 3 and 5 and the commutator dévissage, to K of height one with perfect restricted
    Lie algebra, e.g. Frobenius kernels of semisimple groups and their twisted forms.
  - Material: the pages; Giraud, Cohomologie non abélienne; Springer (1966); Demazure-Gabriel; results on fppf
    cohomology of infinitesimal groups over imperfect fields.
- **R4. The abelian subscheme of Pic in mixed and positive characteristic.**
  - Question: for f: X → S smooth projective with geometrically connected fibres over a reduced locally noetherian S,
    prove or refute that there is a unique abelian subscheme A ⊂ Pic_{X/S} with |A| = ∪_s |Pic^0(X_s)|.
  - Why: proved on 51:19-22 only in residue characteristic 0 ("Sinon, il faut regarder de plus près").
  - Rests on: 51:17-22 and 49:3-10; SA18-17.
  - Strongest reduction: on the page, to the triviality of the finite p-group kernel of A → P over a DVR.
  - Material: the cote 49 and 51 transcriptions; FGA VI; Kleiman, The Picard scheme (§9.5); Igusa's example.
- **R5. Purity for good reduction.**
  - Question: decide his 51:23 d) for torsors under abelian schemes over regular S: is the set of points where P_K has
    good reduction the complement of a pure codimension-one closed set? Decide also the underlying purity statement for
    abelian schemes, separately in the cases where it is known and where counterexamples exist.
  - Why: his question "Analogue th. de pureté pour la bonne réduction des schémas abéliens?". It links SA15 (p-divisible
    groups, Néron models).
  - Rests on: 51:23, 51:9-12; SA15-19.
  - Strongest reduction: c) on the page, H^1(S, G) ⊂ H^1(K, G) for regular S (Weil extension). Known for abelian schemes
    in mixed characteristic with e ≤ p - 1 (Vasiu-Zink 2010, from memory).
  - Material: the pages; `sweep3/SA15/readings/c9_c100.md`; Faltings-Chai V.6; Vasiu-Zink; de Jong-Oort.
- **R6. Contractions of finitely generated ideals over coherent rings.**
  - Question: settle 55:8. If A is coherent and J ⊂ A[T] is finitely generated, is J ∩ A finitely generated? Prove it,
    or give an explicit counterexample, e.g. in Soublin's ring ∏_n Q[[x, y]].
  - Why: his coherence question for compact Stein algebras of finite presentation.
  - Rests on: 55:2-12; SA18-18.
  - Strongest reduction: on the page, the question is reduced to pure commutative algebra. It holds when A is
    noetherian (compact semianalytic Stein sets, Frisch).
  - Material: the pages; Glaz, Commutative coherent rings (LNM 1371); Soublin (1970).
- **R7. Maximal tori of the quasi-split type outside Borel subgroups.**
  - Question: for G quasi-split over a field k with quasi-split torus T_1, decide whether a maximal torus T ≅ T_1 (as
    k-tori) must lie in a Borel subgroup over k. Decide it first over finite fields (twisted classes of W·τ that are
    conjugate in GL(X) but not under W), then in general.
  - Why: his Question 1 on 45:55 ("J'en doute"); his N.B. on 45:53 settles split T_1 over connected S.
  - Rests on: 45:45-57; SA18-2.
  - Strongest reduction: over F_q, the condition is a statement about twisted conjugacy classes in W(Φ) ⋊ ⟨τ⟩, a finite
    computation for each type (start with ^3D_4 and ^2E_6).
  - Material: the pages; Carter, Finite groups of Lie type; a computation in GAP/CHEVIE.
