## What his pages show, as read in this sweep (transcription passages)
- **29/066.** The Chow step asserts "Y' de codim ≥2" for an arbitrary proper surjective Z'->Z. The blow-up of P^2 at a point refutes this (Pro BR04).
  - (My inference) The clause is used at 29/068, so the gap reaches the proof (F12).
- **29/070.** The margin asks whether pi1(U;Omega)->pi1(K/k;Omega) is bijective for the regular locus U of a proper normal model. Pro BR13's negative answer with (P1xP1)/±1 is correct.
- **29/076.** dim Pic = 1/2 rank_{Z_l}, with no l≠p on the page. The formula is false for l=p (Pro P12).
- **29/088 and 29/094.** The setup states no properness. The unique n-divisibility of ker(Pic X_m -> Pic X_0) fails for X_0 with nonconstant units (Pro FORMAL-10).
  - (My inference) It holds for X_0 proper over the base field, so sweep 2 must settle the intended setting (F13).
- **29/096.** His own boxed claim: for X0-D'0≠P1 the inertia extension is "triviale". If "triviale" means split, it is false (Heisenberg). The two Pro notes disagree on whose claim it is (F1).
- **29/138-139.** The definition of minimal pointed torsors (the literal formula has ×_G G') and Proposition 4, "α minimal => injective", with the margin \unsure{Sans rigueur}.
  - Pro N033's cusp counterexample for the repaired definition was rechecked by computation (F7).
- **31/020.** "cd(X)<2dim X+1" is strict as frozen. It fails for Spec F_3, and the deleted line above has ≤ (F4).
- **33/023.** The E6 equation with "(12+1)" is impossible mod 13. With "(11+1)" it gives the exponents 4 and 8 (F2).
- **33/049.** "V=R^4" appears in an eight-coordinate E8 calculation (F3).
- **29/008, Grothendieck to Murre.** "I am afraid 6.4. is false as stated, and that the statement is correct only if the D_i are regular" (F17).
- **29/113.** Théorème de Bertini 1, with "On n'affirme pas, cependant, que (*) soit toujours surjectif (sans hypothèse de propreté sur X)". 29/115-116 cite SGA 7 XVIII for Lefschetz pencils and the conjugacy of vanishing cycles.
- **29/125 and 127-128.** The letter "Mon cher Serre", dated "18.6|8.1959".
  - It begins with Tate's question on a global definition of analytic varieties over complete valued fields, and he is "encore sceptique".
  - It turns to the infinitesimal part of the fundamental group: pi1^G(S,a); for k not algebraically closed, "sans-doute" replaces a deleted "peut-être"; then the homotopy sequence (F16).
