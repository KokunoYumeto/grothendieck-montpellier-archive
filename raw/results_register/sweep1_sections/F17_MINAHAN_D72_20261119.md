## The three questions

### 1. Is σ an edge of weight two?

**Yes.** The definitions are in Minahan §6.1 "The complex of minimizing cycles" (TeX lines 793-815, pp. 19-20):
- a *basic cycle* is a multicurve with unique positive coefficients λ_i such that x = Σ λ_i [a_i];
- a *cycle* is a multicurve that satisfies two conditions: (1) each curve lies in a basic cycle contained in the
  multicurve; (2) no non-trivial non-negative integral combination of the classes of its curves vanishes;
- P_M is the convex hull of the basic cycles in M;
- W(v) = Σ λ_i.

Applied to M = a ⊔ b ⊔ c, with [a] = [b] = v, [c] = w and x = v + w:
- the only basic cycles in M are a + c and b + c;
- condition (2) holds because v and w are independent (D.606-D.607);
- so P_M = σ has dimension 1, and W = 2 at both ends.

I re-derived D.605, the statement that no lower curve lies in P. A curve c* dual to c can be chosen inside Q, and
then disjoint from P. Every curve in P has algebraic intersection 0 with c*, while x has intersection ±1 with it.

### 2. What is the adjacent descending link?

**It is the infinite discrete set D.** The definitions are in Minahan §5 (TeX lines 676-695, pp. 14-15): R_σ,
d^face, d^adj = ∪_{ρ ∈ R_σ} Hull(V(ρ)), and d^tot = ConvJoin(d^face, d^adj).
- Every vertex outside σ of a cell in R_σ has weight 1. It is therefore a single curve δ ⊂ Q of class x: a curve
  separating {Q_b, Q_{c,+}} from {Q_a, Q_{c,−}} (D.608-D.609).
- Any two distinct curves of this kind intersect, because S_{0,4} has complexity 1. The twists T_η^m δ_0 give
  infinitely many of them (D.612).
- So R_σ consists of σ and the triangles conv{a + c, b + c, δ}, and d^adj(σ) = d^face(σ) = D (D.613), which has
  H̃_0 ≠ 0.

Minahan's own Lemma 6.6 (TeX line 1051, pp. 28-29) gives the same set: C_dr(P) * C_dr(Q) = ∅ * D = D.

### 3. Is the bound he uses stated for this case?

**No.** The final proof of Theorem A (TeX lines ~1063-1080, p. 29) argues as follows:
- it writes d^adj = C_dr(Σ_0) * ... * C_dr(Σ_k);
- it applies Lemma 6.1 to every component Σ_i;
- it concludes that d_W(σ) is (g − 3 − k)-acyclic.

**What Lemma 6.1 requires** (TeX line 858, p. 22): |B_+|, |B_−| ≥ 2 and |B_+| ≤ |B_−| + 1.

**What the proof supplies:** Lemma 6.5 (p. 28) gives only that each Σ_i is balanced. The component P = S_{2,2} has
|B_+| = |B_−| = 1, so Lemma 6.1 does not cover it.

**Why C_dr(P) is empty:** a draining Σ_N would need at least two incoming boundary curves, and P has one.

The join is therefore just C_dr(Q) = D. That is (−1)-acyclic (non-empty) and not 0-acyclic, while
g − 3 − k = 4 − 3 − 1 = 0.

## D.614: the global convex join

**This also holds.** As printed (p. 15), ConvJoin is the global convex join in R^𝔖. Here it is the union of all
segments [δ_i, δ_j], which is connected. So the inclusions of Lemma 5.3 (TeX line 714, p. 16) are not weak
equivalences at σ.

The proof of Lemma 5.3 covers d^†(σ) by the sets ρ ∩ d^†(σ). Those sets miss the segments [δ_0, δ_1], which lie
in no cell. So under the printed total link, σ passes the n-acyclicity test of pp. 16-17, and Lemma 5.3 fails at σ
instead. Either way, the chain Lemma 6.1 → final proof → Lemma 5.6 does not go through at σ.

## Two remarks of mine (not checked independently)

1. **"This latter summand is equal to g" (p. 29) is a miscount.** The correct value is g + k.
   - Each curve of M is one incoming boundary curve on one side and one outgoing on the other.
   - The complement has k + 1 components, in the paper's own labelling.
   - The Euler characteristic then gives Σ_i (g(Σ_i) + |B_+(Σ_i)|) = g + k. In D.72's example this is
     (2 + 1) + (0 + 2) = 5.
   - The slip only weakens the stated bound: where Lemma 6.1 covers every component, the join bound is g − 3.
     It is not the gap.
2. **The size of the gap.** Every balanced component either has one incoming and one outgoing curve, so that its
   C_dr is empty, or is covered by Lemma 6.1.
   - Leaving out the empty factors, the bound that survives is g − 3 − Σ_j g(P_j), summed over the 1-in-1-out
     components P_j.
   - That reaches the required g − 3 − k only when Σ_j g(P_j) ≤ k.
   - D.72 is the case g(P) = 2 > k = 1.
   - This does not show that the link is less acyclic than required in general, since the bound is only a lower
     bound; in D.72 the link is computed exactly.
