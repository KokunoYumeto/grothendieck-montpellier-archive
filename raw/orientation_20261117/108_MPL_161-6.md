## Orientation for this package (curated 17 Nov 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max from our records; leads, unrefereed)

- **What these pages are:** cote 161-6 (scans 2-34), "Graphes, icosaèdre [etc.]" (from 1973 to c. 1977): loose leaves on graphs and the icosahedron, in the group "Dossiers rassemblés par Grothendieck sur différentes thématiques" (161-1 to 162-6).
- **Where they sit:** maps on surfaces and the icosahedron (plan rank 10), years before La Longue Marche (1981). Same rank: cote 83 on icosahedra and Azumaya algebras (115_MPL_83_090-133 and the other cote 83 packages) and cote 64 on moduli of elliptic curves (118_MPL_64_002-039 to 121_MPL_64_116-157).
- **Our open questions they may bear on:** **LM4 F.7** (the centralizer of a geometric A_5 in a genus-zero mapping class group, and in its completion). **LM4_SUCCESSOR B.39** (the nonsolvable direction of that comparison). **LM4_SUCCESSOR B.41** (extension along the icosahedral marked-orbit loci). **LM4_SUCCESSOR B.38** (K_5 = 1, the level-five congruence kernel). **PUSH_20261114 Part2Succe 26** (icosahedral characters on the cover of the sphere branched at twelve cusps, through the centralizer of A_5).
- **Our results to use and check:** none named in our digest.
- **What other packages' notes say about these cotes:** none yet.
- **Michel Hua's project (credit it to his project whenever you use it):** Hua, modernised reading of cote 161-6: the folder bears the two words "Graphes, icosaèdre", the inventory adds "etc.", and the leaves are not a continuous text, opening with a fair copy in ink (our digest cuts the rest). No finding of his is listed for these scans.
- **What the notes should look for:** (1) which graphs and maps are drawn (the icosahedron, its dual, quotients) and which groups he computes; (2) the orbits of the icosahedral group on the sphere (vertices, faces, edge midpoints: 12, 20, 30 points) and any stabilizer, centralizer or normalizer, as data for LM4 F.7 and LM4_SUCCESSOR B.39, B.41; (3) any field of definition or Galois action on these maps; (4) the dates on the leaves, and what in them anticipates La Longue Marche.
