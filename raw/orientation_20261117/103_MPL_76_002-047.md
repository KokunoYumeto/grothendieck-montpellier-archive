## Orientation for this package (curated 17 Nov 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max from our records; leads, unrefereed)

- **What these pages are:** cote 76, scans 2-47, "n-cartes cellulaires": undated handwritten notes, about 1977. The rest of the cote is in package 104_MPL_76_048-063+88+70_002-016.
- **Where they sit:** plan rank 10 (maps on surfaces and the icosahedron): maps generalised to dimension n, beside cote 70 (n-polyhedra), cotes 85-86 and cote 138 (maps).
- **Our open questions they may bear on:** none of ours concerns n-dimensional maps; the 31 rows matched by subject concern GT̂, sections and congruence kernels. Possibly:
  - **FINMON C2-CP2** (programme map): finite categories with classifying space CP² (bounds 17 to 22); if these pages give finite combinatorial models of cell complexes.
  - **EXIT_TOWER E.24**: for n = 2, the Galois action on maps is its (0,4) level.
- **Our results to use and check:** **FINMON** (programme map): the least finite monoid with classifying space CP² has 23 elements.
- **What other packages' notes say about these cotes:** none in our digest.
- **Michel Hua's project (credit it to his project whenever you use it):** Hua, findings register, **76-semisimplicial-vertex-order** (their pp. 20-24, our scans 021-025; no reference found by his search; new to us): semi-simplicial sets whose simplices have pairwise distinct faces and whose edge relation on vertices is transitive are equivalent to posets in which every principal ideal is a Boolean lattice of non-empty subsets (simplicial posets without the empty face), with an order on the minimal elements (cut in our digest).
- **What the notes should look for:**
  1. Scans 021-025: the equivalence in full, its conditions, and a proof or a counterexample.
  2. The definition of an n-cellular map: flags, the operators σ_0, …, σ_n, and the conditions for a cellular decomposition of a manifold.
  3. What Galois-theoretic content survives for n > 2; links to cote 70's n-polyhedra (packages 105, 106).
  4. Whether these complexes serve as finite models of homotopy types; compare FINMON.
