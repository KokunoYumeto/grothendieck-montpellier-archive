## Orientation for this package (curated 17 Nov 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max from our records; leads, unrefereed)

- **What these pages are:** cote 1, scans 99-150: the last part of "Analyse fonctionnelle" (1953; notes and a letter): singular values of operators, Schatten classes and Fredholm determinants.
- **Where they sit:** analysis (plan rank 25: functional analysis, early notes, documents). Scans 51-98 of cote 1 are in 244_MPL_1_051-098.
- **Our open questions they may bear on:** none of our runs meets this folder. What the pages may open up: inequalities for Fredholm coefficients and determinants, and a route to the Schatten-class theorems that avoids Weyl's lemma.
- **Our results to use and check:** none.
- **What other packages' notes say about these cotes:** only incidental citations (MPL_147_138-190 190-04; register sections of MPL_33_147-181+34_002-017, MPL_36_002-052 and MPL_36_053-085+39; MPL_7_061-072+8_001-078 at scan 078); none treats these pages.
- **Michel Hua's project (credit it to his project whenever you use it):** Hua, modernised reading of cote 1: an operator measured by its sequence of singular values; Weyl's comparison in mean and the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya lemma give the Schatten theory, redone in ten pages (their pp. 81-104, our scans 82-105); an older version (their pp. 123-149, our scans 124-150) does not yet know Weyl's lemma and goes through the determinant det(1 + zu). Hua, findings register, 1-mixed-schwarz-fredholm-coefficients (our scans 124-125; unsearched): for trace-class A, B, |α_n(A+B)|² ≤ α_n(|A|+|B|)·α_n(|A*|+|B*|) for every n, hence a bound on |det(1+A+B)|. 1-schatten-p-via-fredholm-determinant (our scans 127-140; candidate): the three parts of the S_p theorem for 0 < p ≤ ∞ through Fredholm determinants and Jensen's formula, without Weyl's lemma.
- **What the notes should look for:** (1) Hua's coefficient inequality against scans 124-125, with a numerical test on small matrices; (2) whether the determinant route at scans 127-140 is complete for p < 1, step by step; (3) which version is older, from internal evidence (references to Weyl, notation); (4) the 1953 letter, if in this range: correspondent and content; (5) possibly a source of his published work on Fredholm theory (our guess from the subject and date).
