## Orientation for this package (curated 17 Nov 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max from our records; leads, unrefereed)

- **What these pages are:** the whole of cote 87 (scans 2-41), "Polyèdres convexes", undated handwritten notes; the inventory group "Géométrie et topologie combinatoire" (cotes 69-102) is dated 1976 to about 1986 (Hua's reading).
- **Where they sit:** plan rank 18, Book IV (polygons, quadrics, graphs): the convex side of the combinatorics of polyhedra, beside cotes 85-86 (regular polyhedra), cote 70 (realisations) and cote 138 (maps).
- **Our open questions they may bear on:** none of ours concerns convex polyhedra; the 39 rows matched by subject are about GT̂, sections and icosahedral tests. Possibly **FERMAT_SUCCESSOR C.20** and **LM4_SUCCESSOR B.41** (icosahedral tests and loci), if the regular convex polyhedra are treated. The pages may open instead: when an incidence table is the face structure of a convex polyhedron.
- **Our results to use and check:** none.
- **What other packages' notes say about these cotes:** none in our digest.
- **Michel Hua's project (credit it to his project whenever you use it):** Hua, modernised reading of cote 87: what remains of a convex polyhedron when only the incidences of its faces are kept, and whether a table of incidences is the shadow of a real polyhedron. A short course on convex sets comes first: every convex set cut into "facettes" (relative interiors of faces) ordered by closure; polarity (the dual polyhedron, cube and octahedron exchanged) reverses that order; from it a simple rigid property, which his reading names in today's terms (cut in our digest; possibly the diamond property of face lattices). Our digest lists no findings of his project here.
- **What the notes should look for:**
  1. The facet decomposition and polarity: exact statements and proofs; identify the rigid property.
  2. Any criterion for an incidence table to come from a convex polyhedron: in dimension 3 compare Steinitz's theorem; in general realizability is hard (Mnëv's universality, 1988; our pointer).
  3. The boundary of a 3-polytope as a map on the sphere: links to cote 138 and cotes 85-86.
  4. Any count (Euler relation, face numbers) or classification proposed, with its proof.
