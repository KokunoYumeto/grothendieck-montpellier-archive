## Orientation for this package (curated 17 Nov 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max from our records; leads, unrefereed)

- **What these pages are:** the opening (scans 2-48) of cote 29, "Groupe fondamental [Autour de SGA 4 et SGA 7]": letters (1959, 1967, 1969), annotated typescripts and copies, handwritten notes.
- **Where they sit:** rank 24 of the plan (SGA 3 and the re-editions; tag "groupschemes"). Cote 29 goes on in 238_MPL_29_049-094, 239_MPL_29_095-140, 240_MPL_29_141-187 and 241_MPL_29_188-217+37_002-022.
- **Our open questions they may bear on:** the one matched row, **LM1 E.11** (arbitrary separated locally finitely presented group schemes), came through the tag and bears only on general group schemes. What the pages may open up instead: the tame fundamental group, with ramification as a structure rather than a property.
- **Our results to use and check:** **LM1 E.2** (claimed: fibrewise-unipotent sections of closed linear group schemes over Q-algebras are uniquely exp(t log g)), only if such sections appear.
- **What other packages' notes say about these cotes:** none read cote 29; MPL_11_043-058+56 N063 and N100 (cote 56) match by subject only.
- **Michel Hua's project (credit it to his project whenever you use it):** Hua, modernised reading of cote 29: the first eleven pages record a discovery made by two: Murre, writing the SGA 1 exposé on the tame fundamental group, does not see where the structure could come from; Grothendieck answers that his own notes were wrong, lists five false statements and proposes to start again; the method that follows is to study the category of coverings, and the 1967 "donnée de ramification" is an arbitrary catalogue giving the same category. No finding of his register is listed for these scans.
- **What the notes should look for:**
  1. The Murre-Grothendieck exchange in full: dates, writer of each piece, the five false statements and the plan to start again.
  2. The definition of a tame covering with its ramification data, and the examples where two data have the same geometric realization.
  3. Possibly the root of the Grothendieck-Murre tame fundamental group (LNM 208, 1971); and the later parts of this cote, whose findings (ramification-domain axioms, the Kummer gerbe) are listed for 241_MPL_29_188-217+37_002-022.
  4. The 1959 letter: correspondent, subject, and its place before SGA 1.
