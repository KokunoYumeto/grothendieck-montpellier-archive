## Whole endomorphism-field argument

Howe–Zhu, *On the existence of absolutely simple abelian varieties of a given dimension over an arbitrary field*, arXiv:math/0002205v1, §2 p.3, Proposition 3 and Lemma 4 p.4, and Theorem 6 p.5 with proof pp.5–6, were read in full for this check. Both polynomials are ordinary because their middle coefficients are prime to 37. The four exceptions in Theorem 6 are `a=0`, `a²=q+b`, `a²=2b`, and `a²=3b−3q` for a polynomial `T⁴+aT³+bT²+aqT+q²`; neither example satisfies any. Therefore both reductions are absolutely simple ordinary surfaces.

For each reduction, §2 says the endomorphism algebra over its ground finite field is the quartic CM field generated by Frobenius π. Absolute simplicity and Proposition 3 imply `Q(π^r)=Q(π)` for every r, and Lemma 4 together with its proof implies that the rational endomorphism algebra stays unchanged under every finite extension of the finite field. Taking the union over finite extensions therefore proves that the **geometric** rational endomorphism algebra is this same quartic CM field. This distinction is needed when extending the number field to define all endomorphisms of the characteristic-zero Jacobian.

Conrad, *Gross–Zagier Revisited*, MSRI Publications 49 (2004), Theorem 2.1(2), printed p.70, gives injective specialization of homomorphisms for abelian schemes with good reduction. Exact primary URL: <https://library.slmath.org/books/Book49/files/05conrad.pdf>. Consequently, after any necessary finite extension of the number field, the geometric rational endomorphism algebra of J injects into each of the two quartic geometric reduction algebras.

Here is the field argument in full. An algebra embedded into a field is commutative and has no zero divisors; a finite-dimensional rational algebra of this kind is itself a number field F. Its degree divides 4. Each quartic CM field K_i has its canonical real quadratic subfield generated by `π+37/π`, giving `Q(sqrt(433))` and `Q(sqrt(313))`. If a quartic field has two different quadratic subfields, their compositum is the entire field and is biquadratic. A primitive element of a biquadratic field has square polynomial discriminant because its Galois group acts by even permutations on the four conjugates. The nonsquare discriminants just computed therefore prove that each K_i has exactly one quadratic subfield. The two real quadratic fields are nonisomorphic. Hence F cannot have degree 2; if F had degree 4, it would identify K_1 with K_2 and their unique quadratic subfields, also impossible. Thus F=Q. The integral endomorphism ring, being an order in Q containing Z, is Z. In particular J is geometrically simple and non-CM.

The cover-degree calculation is compatible with C16: the six branch points are the four x-coordinates of nonzero 3-torsion, the chosen x-coordinate of 7-torsion, and infinity. They all belong to pi(E[21]). Taking the degree-two hyperelliptic cover in the C16 torsion-branch lemma gives a ball-cover degree at most `2 * 2 * 21² = 1764`.

## Backup bad-reduction observation

Direct enumeration gives #E(F7)=6, not 12, for E:y²=x³−30x−25. It is ordinary since a7=2. If a is chosen as the x-coordinate of a nonzero formal 7-torsion point, then v7(a)<0. Writing b=1/a and z=1/x, after a finite extension and rescaling the hyperelliptic coordinate, H_a has the model

`w² = z(z−b)(1−60z²−100z³−300z⁴)`.

The reversed quartic is squarefree with unit value at z=0 modulo 7. The special fiber has the unique ordinary double point at z=w=0 and has genus-one normalization `v²=1−60z²−100z³−300z⁴`. Locally, dividing by the square root of the quartic unit gives `uv=b²/4`, so this is a stable nodal degeneration and its Jacobian has toric rank one. This observation is not needed for the stronger two-reduction End=Z proof.
