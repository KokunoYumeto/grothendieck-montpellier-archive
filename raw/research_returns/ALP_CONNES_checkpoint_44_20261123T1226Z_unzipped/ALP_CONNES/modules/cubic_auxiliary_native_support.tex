\begin{proof}
\proofstep{1. A split residual lattice with the correct polarization coordinates.}
The compact image of \(V\) admits a stable lattice over a finite
coefficient extension containing its matrices and residual characters.
For completeness, start with any lattice, bound its compact orbit
inside a fixed fractional lattice, and take the sum of that orbit;
Noetherianity makes the sum a lattice and it is stable.
Write its coefficient uniformizer as \(\pi\).
Since \(p\ne2\), the integral idempotents
\((1\pm V(c))/2\) split this lattice into two free rank-one
summands.  They have rank one because the determinant at complex
conjugation is \(-1\).  In the corresponding ordered basis,
\[
                        V(c)=D_c=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1).
 \tag{AS.6}
\]
The two residual characters in (AS.2) have respectively these
values at \(c\), since \(\gamma(c)=1\).

The residual representation has a one-dimensional submodule;
its restriction to \(c\) is one of the two displayed eigenspaces.
Thus, in this basis, either every lower off-diagonal entry or
every upper off-diagonal entry belongs to \(\pi\mathcal O\).
Adjoin a square root \(\varpi\) of \(\pi\).
If the lower entries are in \(\pi\mathcal O\), conjugate by
\(\operatorname{diag}(\varpi^{-1},1)\) as a basis change:
the new upper and lower entries are respectively
\(\varpi b\) and \(\varpi^{-1}c\).
If the upper entries are in \(\pi\mathcal O\), use
\(\operatorname{diag}(\varpi,1)\) instead.
In either case all off-diagonal entries are in the new maximal
ideal and all matrices and their inverses remain integral.
The diagonal entries and (AS.6) are unchanged.
After any further finite coefficient extension this still gives
\[
              \bar V=\operatorname{diag}(\eta_K\gamma,\gamma^{-1})
 \quad\text{as an actual matrix representation}.
 \tag{AS.7}
\]
This argument does not infer a split residual lattice merely from
semisimplification; it constructs one using a ramified value extension.

Choose the final \(\mathcal O\) to contain this finite extension,
the fixed \(\mathcal O_0\), and the other finitely many coefficient
fields specified above.  For native \path{Large}, it suffices to
include the images of an integral basis of \(H\) under its finitely
many \(p\)-adic embeddings.  This is again a finite extension.
All residual characters are now regarded in the one field \(k\).
