# Primary inputs for AQ-14-NONPROPER

This is the source companion to the separate candidate proof. It is not by itself a promotion of that proof into the settled register.

Deligne, P. (1974). La conjecture de Weil: I. *Publications Mathématiques de l’IHÉS, 43*, 273–307. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02684373

- Original primary scan: https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_1974__43__273_0.pdf . Theorem (1.6), printed p. 276, is the smooth-projective Frobenius purity theorem. Twisting H^{2i−1} by i gives geometric weight −1; a torus Tate module has geometric weight −2. The candidate proves the resulting vanishing without requiring semisimplicity.

Geisser, T. (2015). Rojtman’s theorem for normal schemes. *Mathematical Research Letters, 22*(4), 1129–1144. https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.1831

- Operative directly inspected primary version: arXiv:1402.1831v2, February 25, 2015, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.1831 . Theorem 1.1, preprint p. 2, gives prime-to-characteristic torsion Suslin–Albanese isomorphism and H₁^S(T,Z)⊗Q_l/Z_l=0 for normal separated finite-type schemes, without assuming smooth compactification. Theorem 1.3, preprint p. 3, supplies the smooth-curve special case H₀^S(C,Z)^0≅Alb_sa(C)(k). Lemma 2.2, p. 5, records the Albanese map on Suslin H₀. The exact theorem statements were read; the entire Geisser proof is a cited input, not a new whole-proof certification.

Geisser, T., & Schmidt, A. (2016). Tame class field theory for singular varieties over algebraically closed fields. *Documenta Mathematica, 21*, 91–123. https://doi.org/10.4171/DM/528

- Original journal PDF: https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/26306?nt=1 . Theorem 1.1, printed p. 92, gives the finite perfect pairing/reciprocity isomorphism for coefficients prime to the characteristic. Theorem 7.1, printed p. 118, identifies the pairing with the Suslin–Voevodsky comparison. The candidate passes through direct and inverse coefficient limits explicitly; it does not claim that every finite-coefficient H¹ of a variety equals that of its Albanese.
- The published PDF lists authors Thomas Geisser and Alexander Schmidt in that order; the publisher metadata happens to reverse them. The citation follows the paper itself.

Kahn, B. (2014). Algebraic tori as Nisnevich sheaves with transfers. *Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Toulouse: Mathématiques, 23*(3), 699–715. https://doi.org/10.5802/afst.1421

- Directly inspected final journal PDF: https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bruno.kahn/preprints/tores-nisnevich.pdf . Lemma 4.1(a),(b), printed p. 707, proves that a pointed morphism of semiabelian varieties is a homomorphism and that a semiabelian variety is its own Serre Albanese. Equivalent earlier locator: July 4, 2013 author version, https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bruno.kahn/preprints/tores-nisnevich8.pdf , Lemma 3, p. 8. Both exact statements and the short proof were read.

Sun, S. (2017). Generic base change, Artin’s comparison theorem, and the decomposition theorem for complex Artin stacks. *Journal of Algebraic Geometry, 26*(3), 513–555. https://doi.org/10.1090/JAG/683

- Operative inspected primary version: arXiv:1511.01393v1, November 4, 2015, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01393 . Section 1.3.1, p. 2, permits Λ=Z_l. Section 2.1, p. 3, gives condition (LO) and the integral stratifiable adic category. Definition 2.2, p. 3, specifies one dense open valid for all subsequent base changes. Lemma 2.3(iii), pp. 4–5, and its proof explicitly obtain one dense open for all finite coefficient levels by finite Jordan–Hölder dévissage. Theorem 2.5(i), p. 7, states generic base change; Remark 2.5.1(ii), p. 8, recalls preservation of stratifiability. The candidate uses only finite-type F_p bases with l≠p, so (LO) applies. A family of separate opens, one for each l^n, would not suffice and is not the argument used.

Bloch correspondence and specialization inputs are those already precisely cited in AQ-14-FORWARD: Bloch (1979), Propositions 3.5 and 3.8, and Achter–Casalaina-Martin–Vial, arXiv:2007.07180v2, Propositions A.23, A.24 and A.26. The latter paper's (0.2)–(0.3) and §A.3 also identify the rational Tate module of the torsion-coefficient cohomology with ordinary Q_l-cohomology.
