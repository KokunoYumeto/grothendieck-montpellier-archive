# Finite transitions and exact rational descent in the abelian sector

This section sharpens the coefficient and base-field statements in
cote 15. It uses the all-abelian Tate inputs already recorded in
CC-TATE01--02 and AQ-ABELIAN-RECOGNITION, together with the established
equivalences CC-AG01 and CC-AG04--06. The arbitrary-variety Tate
conjecture is not an assumption of these scoped conclusions. The
rational classification applied below is classical: Milne (1994),
corrected author version dated 2 November 2019, Propositions 2.4, 2.6,
2.8, 2.17 and Theorems 2.15, 2.16, 2.18, 2.19. We prove the precise
consequences for the categories already established here; we do not
reprove the cited classification of central division algebras.

Write $\mathcal A_q$ for the rational numerical category generated by
abelian varieties, finite étale schemes, and Tate motives over
$\mathbb F_q$, and $\mathcal A_{\bar k}$ for its counterpart over
$\bar k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. As before, use the parity-corrected
symmetry, $E=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$ with $\ell\ne p$, and a fixed
embedding $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow E$. Coefficient
extension includes idempotent completion.

## CC-AG07: the exact finite-transition sequence

For every prime power $q=p^a$ and positive integer $m$, there is an
exact sequence of abstract abelian groups with their natural Galois
actions $$1\longrightarrow\mu_m\longrightarrow W(q)
   \xrightarrow{\alpha\mapsto\alpha^m}W(q^m)
   \longrightarrow1.                                      \tag{CC-AG.21}$$
Here $\mu_m$ consists of the algebraic $m$-th roots of unity; $W(q)$
consists of the algebraic $p$-units of integral $q$-weight, as in
CC-AG01. In particular, the power map has *zero cokernel*.

Under the established grading equivalences, extension of the base field
$\mathbb F_q\subset\mathbb F_{q^m}$ sends a label $\alpha$ to
$\alpha^m$. This functor on the $E$-coefficient categories is
essentially surjective. Its associated diagonalizable groups have the
fpqc exact sequence $$1\longrightarrow P_{q^m,E}\longrightarrow P_{q,E}
   \longrightarrow D_E(\mu_m)\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad P_{q,E}=D_E(W(q)).                                \tag{CC-AG.22}$$
The last group is finite étale of order $m$. After the identifications
of component groups in CC-AG06, the first arrow induces multiplication
by $m$ on $\widehat{\mathbb Z}_E$.

#### Complete proof.

The power map preserves the defining conditions: if every complex
conjugate of $\alpha$ has absolute value $q^{w/2}$, then those of
$\alpha^m$ have absolute value $(q^m)^{w/2}$; valuations away from $p$
remain zero. Conversely, given $\beta\in W(q^m)$ of weight $w$, choose
any algebraic root $\alpha$ with $\alpha^m=\beta$. Every complex
embedding of $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ sends this equation to the
corresponding equation for conjugates. Therefore
$$|\sigma\alpha|^m=|\sigma\beta|=q^{mw/2},
        \qquad |\sigma\alpha|=q^{w/2}.$$ At any finite place away from
$p$, taking valuations in $\alpha^m=\beta$ gives valuation zero for
$\alpha$. Thus $\alpha\in W(q)$. The kernel is precisely $\mu_m$; all
its elements have weight zero and are $p$-units. The map commutes with
every element of
$\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)$. This proves
(CC-AG.21).

A finite $W(q^m)$-graded $E$-vector space can now be lifted by choosing
a root label for each of its finitely many nonzero graded pieces,
retaining the vector space and its multiplicity. The grading
equivalences identify this construction with an object in
$\mathcal A_{q,E}$, whose base extension is the given object. No
assertion of essential surjectivity before extending coefficients is
used.

For completeness, diagonalizable-group exactness here can be checked on
group algebras. The surjection $W(q)\to W(q^m)$ gives a quotient
$$E[W(q)]\longrightarrow E[W(q^m)].$$ Its kernel is the ideal generated
by $e_\zeta-1$ for $\zeta\in\mu_m$, because its quotient identifies
precisely the cosets of $\mu_m$. Consequently its spectrum is the kernel
of $P_{q,E}\to D_E(\mu_m)$. Moreover $E[W(q)]$ is free, and faithfully
flat, over $E[\mu_m]$: choose one representative for each coset of
$\mu_m$ in $W(q)$. This proves the asserted fpqc exactness. The finite
group algebra $E[\mu_m]$ is a product of $m$ copies of $E$, since $E$ is
algebraically closed of characteristic zero; thus its diagonalizable
group is finite étale of order $m$.

The component quotient has character group $\mu_\infty\subset W(q)$. The
map on these torsion character groups is again $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^m$.
Taking its diagonalizable dual is multiplication by $m$ on
$D_E(\mu_\infty)$, which is the identified constant profinite group
$\widehat{\mathbb Z}_E$. This also treats $m=1$. $\square$

## The exact classical rational classification used

Let $B$ denote either the fixed field $\mathbb F_q$ or $\bar k$, and put
$$\Lambda_B=
 \begin{cases}
      W(q),&B=\mathbb F_q,\\
      \mathcal W_p,&B=\bar k.
 \end{cases}$$ The group $\mathcal W_p$ is the Weil-germ group of
CC-AG05. Every $\Gamma$-orbit $O$ in $\Lambda_B$ is finite.

For a label $\lambda$, define its centre field $$K_\lambda=
        \overline{\mathbb Q}^{\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(\lambda)}.
                                                               \tag{CC-AG.23}$$
For a fixed-field label $\alpha$, this is $\mathbb Q(\alpha)$. For a
germ $\xi=[\alpha,a]$, choose a sufficiently divisible positive integer
$N$; then $$K_\xi=\mathbb Q(\alpha^N), \qquad
       \xi=[\alpha^N,aN].                                    \tag{CC-AG.24}$$
To see this stabilization directly, list the finitely many conjugates of
$\alpha$. An automorphism fixes its germ exactly when its image of
$\alpha$, divided by $\alpha$, is a root of unity. Choose $N$ divisible
by the orders of all such ratios. An automorphism then fixes $\alpha^N$
exactly when it fixes the germ, proving (CC-AG.24). All larger multiples
of this $N$ give the same field.

Milne's cited classification assigns to each orbit $O$ a unique simple
rational motive $S_O$. Its endomorphism ring
$$D_O=\operatorname{End}(S_O)$$ is a central division algebra over
$K_\lambda$, for any $\lambda\in O$. Here and below End is the
endomorphism algebra of the *cohomological motive*, and the local Brauer
invariant uses Milne's geometric-Frobenius normalization on printed
p. 17. These conventions fix the following sign.

Choose $\pi\in K_\lambda$, $q_0=p^b$, and weight $w\in\mathbb Z$ as
follows. For a fixed-field label take $(\pi,q_0)=(\alpha,q)$. For a germ
take the stabilized representative in (CC-AG.24), so
$(\pi,q_0)=(\alpha^N,p^{aN})$. The local invariants, viewed in
$\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$, are $$\operatorname{inv}_v(D_O)=
 \begin{cases}
   w/2,&v\text{ real},\\[2pt]
   -\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{ord}_v(\pi)}
                         {\operatorname{ord}_v(q_0)}
        [K_{\lambda,v}:\mathbb Q_p],&v\mid p,\\[7pt]
   0,&\text{all other places}.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{CC-AG.25}$$
The displayed negative sign is the one in Milne's corrected 2019 author
text, Theorem 2.16, printed p. 18, carried to germs by Theorem 2.19,
p. 20. Replacing the cohomological endomorphism algebra by its opposite
reverses all its local invariants. Using the opposite normalization for
the local Brauer invariant also reverses their signs. Neither operation
changes the index used below.

Set $$d_O=\operatorname{lcm}_{v}
            \bigl(\text{denominator of }
                        \operatorname{inv}_v(D_O)\bigr),
                                                               \tag{CC-AG.26}$$
where the denominator of zero is one. Then
$$[D_O:K_\lambda]=d_O^2,\qquad
        \operatorname{rank}S_O=d_O\,|O|.                       \tag{CC-AG.27}$$
Only finitely many local invariants are nonzero. The formulas are
independent of taking a further power of a stabilized germ
representative, since numerator and denominator in its $p$-adic slope
are both multiplied by the same integer. Galois conjugation just
permutes the places and the labels.

The exact primary locators are as follows: Proposition 2.4, p. 13,
identifies the centre; Proposition 2.6, pp. 13--14, classifies the
simple objects; Proposition 2.8, p. 14, gives their ranks; Theorem 2.15,
p. 18, gives the local-invariant classification and index; Theorem 2.16,
p. 18, computes the invariants; Proposition 2.17, p. 19, states the
integral Grothendieck-ring image; and Theorems 2.18--2.19 with
Corollary 2.20, p. 20, give the germ versions.

In Milne's paper these results are written under the full Tate
hypothesis. Their application here is restricted to the abelian, Artin,
and Tate category: the full realization on its products and summands is
the already established all-abelian input, and occurrence of every Weil
label and germ is CC-AG01 and CC-AG05. No occurrence or fullness
assertion for an arbitrary smooth projective variety is being inferred.
The classical division-algebra formula (CC-AG.25), rather than an
independent reconstruction of its crystalline argument, is the cited
input in the next deduction.

## CC-AG08: rational descent as an explicit divisibility test

Under the established equivalence $$\mathcal A_{B,E}\simeq
                    \mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\Lambda_B}(E),
                                                               \tag{CC-AG.28}$$
let $V$ have multiplicity $n_\lambda=\dim_E V_\lambda$. There exists an
actual rational object $M\in\mathcal A_B$ and an isomorphism
$M_E\simeq V$ if and only if $$\begin{split}
 n_{\sigma\lambda}&=n_\lambda
                 &&(\sigma\in\Gamma,\ \lambda\in\Lambda_B),\\
 d_O&\mid n_\lambda
                 &&(\lambda\in O).
 \end{split}                                                \tag{CC-AG.29}$$
When these conditions hold, the rational isomorphism class is unique,
and explicitly $$M\simeq\bigoplus_O S_O^{\oplus n_O/d_O},\qquad
       \operatorname{End}(M)
          \simeq\prod_{O:n_O>0}M_{n_O/d_O}(D_O).               \tag{CC-AG.30}$$
Here $n_O$ is the common multiplicity on $O$. The zero object
corresponds to empty support.

Equivalently, coefficient extension embeds the Grothendieck ring as
$$\begin{split}
 K_0(\mathcal A_B)&\hookrightarrow
                         \mathbb Z[\Lambda_B]^\Gamma,\\
 \operatorname{im}&=
     \left\{\sum_O n_O\!\sum_{\lambda\in O}e_\lambda:
                   d_O\mid n_O,\ \text{finite support}\right\}.
 \end{split}                                                \tag{CC-AG.31}$$
Its actual-object cone is obtained by requiring $n_O\ge0$. The
multiplication on the target is convolution in the group ring, so this
also specifies the rational tensor multiplicities once the $d_O$ are
known.

#### Complete proof of the scoped deduction.

The category $\mathcal A_B$ is semisimple. The cited classification
identifies its simple isomorphism classes with the orbits $O$, and its
scalar extension has the one-dimensional simple labels of (CC-AG.28).
For a simple $S_O$, scalar extension and the finite dimensionality of
its endomorphism algebra give $$\begin{split}
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A_{B,E}}((S_O)_E)
       &=D_O\otimes_{\mathbb Q}E\\
       &\simeq\prod_{\tau:K_\lambda\hookrightarrow E}M_{d_O}(E).
 \end{split}                                                \tag{CC-AG.32}$$
The equality of the endomorphism algebra after coefficient extension is
literal in the scalar-extended category, and idempotent completion does
not alter the endomorphisms of an existing object. The second equality
follows because the separable centre splits into one $E$-factor per
embedding and a central simple algebra splits over an algebraically
closed field.

The centre embeddings correspond to the labels in $O$; for germs this
uses the stabilized centre in (CC-AG.24). The associated central
idempotents are the projectors onto those label components. In the
graded-vector-space category, a component of dimension $r$ has
endomorphism ring $M_r(E)$. Comparing the corresponding factors in
(CC-AG.32) therefore proves $$(S_O)_E
                  \simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\in O}
                                E_\lambda^{\oplus d_O}.      \tag{CC-AG.33}$$
This argument identifies each multiplicity, not just the sum of the
dimensions.

Every rational object is a finite direct sum
$\bigoplus_O S_O^{\oplus a_O}$, with uniquely determined nonnegative
integers $a_O$. Equation (CC-AG.33) gives $n_\lambda=a_Od_O$ on $O$,
proving the necessity of (CC-AG.29). Conversely, if (CC-AG.29) holds,
the integers $a_O=n_O/d_O$ are nonnegative and have finite support. The
direct sum in (CC-AG.30) exists in $\mathcal A_B$; its coefficient
extension has exactly the prescribed graded dimensions. Finite graded
vector spaces with the same dimensions are isomorphic, so it extends to
$V$. Uniqueness of the simple multiplicities proves uniqueness of the
rational isomorphism class. Schur's lemma gives zero morphisms between
different simple classes and endomorphism algebra $M_{a_O}(D_O)$ on an
isotypic sum, proving the second formula in (CC-AG.30).

For the Grothendieck ring, the classes of the simple objects are a free
integral basis by semisimplicity. Equation (CC-AG.33) sends that basis
to the disjointly supported nonzero elements
$d_O\sum_{\lambda\in O}e_\lambda$. They are linearly independent, and
their integral span is exactly the displayed image. Coefficient
extension is a tensor functor and the grading tensor product multiplies
labels, so the map respects convolution. Nonnegative simple
multiplicities give precisely the stated actual-object cone. This proves
all assertions. $\square$

#### Why coefficient extension matters for CC-AG07.

For $m=2$, let $V$ be the one-dimensional sign Artin motive over
$\mathbb F_{q^2}$; it is the minus summand of
$h(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_{q^4})$, so its sole Frobenius label is
$-1$. It is not the base extension of any rational object of
$\mathcal A_q$. Indeed such an object would have rank one, hence a
single label $\alpha\in W(q)$; (CC-AG.29) forces that label to be fixed
by $\Gamma$, so $\alpha\in\mathbb Q$. Base extension would require
$\alpha^2=-1$, a contradiction. Over $E$, either label $i$ or $-i$
supplies a preimage, exactly as CC-AG07 asserts.

## What this resolves, and what it does not identify

For the established abelian sector, the rational coefficient condition
is now an exact orbitwise arithmetic obstruction, rather than an
unspecified descent requirement. The supersingular quaternion example
AQ-RATIONAL-FIBRE is its smallest illustrative case: over
$\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ the supersingular weight-one germ has centre
$\mathbb Q$, invariants $1/2$ at $p$ and the real place, and index two.
Its single split label must therefore occur with even multiplicity in a
rational object.

The orbitwise test concerns descent of an *object*. It does not choose
coherent rational vector spaces for all objects and tensor products, and
it does not produce a $\mathbb Q$-valued fibre functor. In fact,
AQ-RATIONAL-FIBRE already proves that the full rational abelian category
has no such fibre functor, by its supersingular quaternion endomorphism
algebra. This nonexistence is a conclusion, not a remaining question.
The nonneutral Tannakian description remains appropriate: Milne,
Corollary 2.25 and Remark 2.26, p. 22, identify its multiplicative-type
band from the natural Galois action on Weil labels or germs. A band is
not a choice of neutral fibre functor. In particular, (CC-AG.29) is not
a claim that the rational category is the ordinary representation
category of that band over $\mathbb Q$.

Likewise the test does not lift numerical projectors to Chow projectors.
Applying any of these classifications to all numerical motives would
still require the arbitrary-motive Tate and realization inputs isolated
in CC-XC01--04 and BR-EFF01. No such implication follows from the
adopted RH and Dirichlet GRH alone.
