## Proposition

Let L be a rank-N lattice whose minimal vectors span L tensor R.
Let r be the dimension of the span of their rank-one quadratic
forms v v^t. If r-N is at most four, these quadratic forms, taking
one vector from each minimal pair, are linearly independent.
Equivalently, its perfect cone is simplicial.

Consequently, each projective degree-twelve target cell for rank nine
or ten has exactly thirteen minimal-vector pairs. Combined with
`k12_bimodular_attempt.md` and `k12_glue_index_two.md`, this removes
all rank-ten target cells of maximal index at most two. It does not
remove rank-ten cells of larger maximal index or all rank-nine cells.

## Known background theorem

Dutour Sikirić--Hulek--Schürmann, *Smoothness and singularities of the
perfect form and the second Voronoi compactification of A_g*,
Algebraic Geometry 2(5) (2015), 642--653, Theorem 1.1(i), printed p.644:
every cone in the perfect-cone decomposition of dimension at most
nine is basic, in particular simplicial. The cone dimension here is
the dimension of the linear span of the rank-one forms, not the
projective cell dimension. The explicit convention is stated at
the beginning of section 2, printed p.645.

Primary publication: https://content.algebraicgeometry.nl/2015-5/2015-5-027.pdf
DOI: 10.14231/AG-2015-027.

We invoke this as a known theorem. Its statement, conventions, and
complete printed proof of (i) in section 2 have been read. That proof
uses classified cones and computational checks; those computations
have not been independently rerun here.

## Proof of the proposition

Put d=r-N, so d is nonnegative and at most four. Suppose the quadratic
forms are dependent, and select an inclusion-minimal dependent subset

    p(v_1),...,p(v_k), where p(v)=v v^t.

The rank of this subset is k-1, and it has a relation with every
coefficient nonzero. Let W be the real vector span of v_1,...,v_k,
and put m=dim W.

Separate the positive and negative coefficients of the relation:

    sum_{i in I+} a_i p(v_i) = sum_{j in I-} b_j p(v_j),
    with a_i,b_j>0.

Both sides are positive semidefinite and their images are respectively
the spans of their supporting vectors. Equality implies that both
spans are W. Therefore both I+ and I- have at least m members, and

    k >= 2m.                                             (1)

Let sigma_W be the cone generated by all minimal-vector rays lying
in W, and let r_W be the dimension of its linear span. This is a face
of the original perfect cone: the linear functional on symmetric
matrices given by trace(P q), where P is orthogonal projection onto
W-perpendicular, is nonnegative on every generator p(v) and vanishes
exactly when v belongs to W. The space W is rational because it is
spanned by lattice vectors. Alternatively, sigma_W is precisely the
perfect cone of the rank-m lattice L intersect W, with its restricted
positive-definite form. That lattice has the same minimum as L and
has all its minimal vectors among those of L lying in W.

The face sigma_W contains the selected circuit, so

    r_W >= k-1.                                          (2)

Choose N-m minimal vectors w_1,...,w_(N-m) whose images form a basis
of (L tensor R)/W. Their projected rank-one quadratic forms are
linearly independent: after a linear change of quotient coordinates,
these are the diagonal elementary matrices. Projection annihilates
the span of sigma_W. Hence the forms p(w_i) are independent modulo
that span, giving

    r >= r_W+(N-m).                                     (3)

Combining (1)--(3) yields

    N+d = r >= k-1+N-m >= N+m-1,
    so m <= d+1.

Equation (3) also gives

    r_W <= r-N+m = d+m <= 2d+1 <= 9.

The known theorem now implies that sigma_W is simplicial. Distinct
minimal pairs give distinct rank-one positive-semidefinite rays, and
each is an extremal ray: a positive sum of rank-one positive-semidefinite
matrices can have rank one only if all summands have the same image.
The circuit therefore gives a dependence among distinct extremal rays
of sigma_W, contradicting simpliciality. Thus the original quadratic
forms were independent, as claimed.

## Interpretation and limits

This argument uses the basicness theorem only through its simpliciality
consequence. It neither assumes nor proves basicness of the larger
cone in dimension thirteen. In particular, lattice indices and glue
are still present and must be addressed by the separate orientation
arguments.

DEKM section 3.3 calls simpliciality for r=N+3 and N+4 expected.
The deduction above was therefore subjected to two independent
whole-proof checks; it is not silently attributed to a theorem stated
in DEKM.
It is compatible with DEKM Example 2.7: that nonsimplicial example
has N=6 and r=11, hence r-N=5, outside the asserted range.
