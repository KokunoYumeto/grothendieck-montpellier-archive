     These notes concern the arithmetic of the pn th cyclotomic fields Fn = Q(µpn ) for an odd
prime p and a positive integer n. Here, µpn denotes the group of pn th roots of unity inside the
complex numbers. When we speak of arithmetic, we speak not just of field elements but of
algebraic integers in this field, which is to say elements of the ring On = Z[µpn ] generated by the
pn th roots of unity. This ring is of course also generated by the single primitive pn th root of unity

     The cusp forms used in the main conjecture can be placed in a family of cusp forms of varying
level and weight, and if one likes, may be viewed as a single modular form with coefficients in a
finite flat algebra over Λ, with the variable coming from certain diamond operators. The Galois
representations may be similarly encapsulated in a single two-dimensional representation over
the Hecke algebra acting on such Λ-adic modular forms. This is made possible by the fact that
the modular forms under consideration are ordinary: their pth Fourier coefficients are units, and
the good control one has over the growth of spaces of such forms is the subject of Hida theory,
which has much of the flavor of Iwasawa theory.
     The Mazur-Wiles proof of the main conjecture indicates that the geometry of modular curves
“near the cusps” has much to say about the arithmetic of cyclotomic fields. That is, the “Eisenstein
ideal” determining the congruences between our Λ-adic cusp forms and Eisenstein series is
essentially the annihilator of the cusp at ∞. So, when we look at residual representations attached
to our cusp forms, we are essentially exploring the geometry of a modular curve near ∞.
     The characteristic ideal of an Iwasawa module being a rather rough invariant of its structure,
one might ask how far one can push this connection between geometry and arithmetic. The
construction of the unramified extension in the Mazur-Wiles proof is rather far from canonical,

exists a pseudo-isomorphism M → N or a pseudo-isomorphism N → M .

imply the existence of a pseudo-isomorphism in the opposite direction.

while there exists no pseudo-isomorphism Λ → (p, T ).

                             3.2. Moduli-theoretic interpretation
    The modular curves we have defined are not just complex manifolds, but in fact varieties, cut
out by polynomial equations. Even better, for small enough Γ, we can define them as the C-points
of schemes over Z.
    For N ≥ 4, the modular curve Y1 (N ) is the moduli space with S-points for a Z[ N1 ]-scheme
S equal to the set of isomorphism classes of pairs (E, P ), where E is an elliptic curve over
S and P is a point of E of order N , which we take to mean given by an injective morphism
(Z/N Z)/S → E[N ]/S of S-schemes from the constant group scheme Z/N Z over S to the
28                                 3. THEORY OF MODULAR FORMS

subgroup scheme E[N ] of E of N -torsion points, viewed as an S-scheme. In fact, it is a fine
moduli scheme, which is to say that it represents the functor that takes S to the aforementioned
set, and it is smooth of finite type over Z[ N1 ].
     Similarly, Y0 (N ) over Z[ N1 ] is the “course” moduli space with S-points for a Z[ N1 ]-scheme S
equal to the set of isomorphism classes (E, C), where E is an elliptic curve over S and C is a

to (Z/N Z)/S . (We shall not define these notions more precisely.)

                                          A.3. Third project
    This project has as its goal the exploration of the relationship between Massey products of
units of cyclotomic fields and the Iwasawa theory of Kummer extensions (see [Sha2]). There are
numerous possibilities here. First, let us recall the definition of Massey products.
    For a profinite group G and a commutative ring R, a defining system for an n-fold Massey
product (χ1 , . . . , χn ) of n continuous homomorphisms χi : G → R is a homomorphism ρ : G →
Nn+1 /Zn+1 , where Nn+1 denotes the group of upper-triangular unipotent matrices in GLn+1 (R)
and Zn+1 denotes the subgroup of matrices which are zero above the diagonal aside from the
(1, n + 1)-entry, such that the character ρi,i+1 given by the (i, i + 1)-entry of ρ is χi for 1 ≤ i ≤ n.
Given such a defining system, the Massey product (χ1 , . . . , χn ) is taken to be the class of the
2-cocycle
                                                   n
                                                  ∑︂
                                  κ1,n (σ, τ ) ↦→    ρ1,i (σ)ρn+2−i,n+1 (τ ).
                                               i=2

The Massey product is therefore only defined if there exists such a defining system, and if so, its
value depends upon the choice of defining system. Thus, it may be viewed as an element of a
quotient of H 2 (G, R). Note that we may also extend the definition to R-coefficients upon which
G acts by a character to R× by placing products of the characters along the diagonal and requiring
that the resulting map to the upper-triangular Borel be a homomorphism.
     Now, let us focus for the moment on Massey triple products (a1 , a2 , a3 ) of elements of EF,S
for a number field F containing µp and S a set of primes containing those dividing p. (We omit
subscripts for simplicity of notation.) Taken with Zp (1)-coefficients, such a Massey product will
exist if and only if (a1 , a2 ) = (a2 , a3 ) = 0, in which case it will have image in the quotient of
H 2 (GF,S , Zp (3)) by all cup products of the form (a1 , c) and (c, a3 ) where c ∈ H 1 (GF,S , Zp (2)).
     The project:
     (1) By definition, the Massey product (a1 , a2 , a3 ) provides an obstruction to the existence
         of a certain p-ramified extension. Under what circumstance does the vanishing of such
         products correspond to the existence of a (different) unramified extension of a p-extension
         of F in the sense of Corollary 5.1.20 (for F = Q(µp ))? What about higher Massey
         products? Study the implications for the structure of unramified Iwasawa modules.
     (2) Consider Massey products of the form (χp , . . . , χp , a1 , . . . , an ) as elements of quotients
         of H 2 (GF,S , Zp (n)), where χp ∈ H 1 (GF,S , Zp ) is the cyclotomic character. What are
         these measuring? (For n = 1, one might compare with [McSh, Section 4] for F = Q(µp )
         and µp -coefficients.)
                                                                                               −
     (3) In Iwasawa theory (taking F = Q(µp )), we have a duality between X+           ∞ and X∞ (see, e.g.,
         (2.3.18)). Do we have related duality for the second graded quotient of an unramified
         Iwasawa module of a Kummer extension that is controlled by cup products with a
         Kummer generator? What about for higher Massey products? The paper [LiSh] might
         be a good reference for duality.
    For an extra fun challenge, see if n-fold Massey products of cyclotomic units can be interpreted
in terms of some variant of modular symbols for GLn . (This can be discussed if we come to it.)
