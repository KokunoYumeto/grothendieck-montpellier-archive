## SN-44-01: Gram correction

I independently reconstruct the linear algebra on numerical cycle classes. The vertical spaces from distinct fibres are orthogonal. For a smooth fibre the self-intersection formula has its normal line bundle pulled back from a point of the base, hence trivial; its whole vertical space is radical. If a sum of elements from singular-fibre spaces is radical in the total vertical space, pairing with each one of those spaces separately shows that each term is in its individual radical. This proves the direct-sum description after quotienting radicals, including any relations between different fibre classes.

The algebraically trivial generic class has an admissible rational extension by the cited Kahn theorem. Localization shows that the prescribed pullback and that extension differ by a finite vertical class. Therefore the prescribed pullback annihilates the whole vertical radical, a step needed before an arbitrary Gram inverse is used. Solving G_s c_s=a_s is consequently legitimate and produces actual rational vertical Chow classes. Subtracting them gives an admissible extension. Orthogonality then gives raw intersection = admissible intersection + sum of the Gram scalars. The projection formula replaces raw intersection on the blowup by that on X. This proves exactly the stated sign and correction, and proves invariance under radical representatives as well as changes of basis.

The proof uses finite-dimensional numerical spaces, not finite-dimensional Chow groups. It does not require homological=numerical. Rational coefficients correctly handle the saturation in Kahn's existence statement and finite-extension transfer. The passage from geometric algebraic triviality to an admissible extension is consistent with the cited finite-base-change rules. The other-codimension argument also has the correct weak-Lefschetz endpoint: when 2i<dim X, restriction is injective even if 2i equals dim Y. One original class is then homologically trivial, and both its height and raw intersection vanish.

The final text makes the numerical dimension bound explicit: numerically independent rational cycles admit a full-rank rational matrix of intersection evaluations against complementary cycles. A nonzero minor stays nonzero over Q_ell, forcing the same number of independent cohomology classes. This correctly avoids treating a finite-dimensional Q_ell vector space as a finite-dimensional Q vector space.

## SN-44-02: node intersection and m=1

Let delta=u+v. Restriction of the strict transform of D to the exceptional projective space is delta h^m, since each ruling plane has degree1. The blowup kernel is the line generated by E^m. Because p^*D restricts to zero on E and E restricts as -h, the exceptional correction is exactly (-1)^(m+1) delta E^m.

The fibre has multiplicity2 at its node, so the strict transform has normal bundle O(-2Q). Self-intersection inside that smooth strict transform gives -2 times the quadric pairing. I independently compute E^(2m)=-1 and Dbar E^m=(-1)^(m-1) delta. The two cross terms in p^*D p^*D' contribute 2 delta delta', and the exceptional square subtracts delta delta', leaving delta delta' in total. The cited quadric table then gives

    D D' = (u+v)(u'+v') - 2 (ua+vb)(u'a+v'b)
          = (-1)^m (u-v)(u'-v').

Thus the local normalization has no missing factor2 or1/2. The self-square formula makes a numerically zero class have zero defect, so the functional really descends to V_s. Its kernel is the radical, and a nonzero rational defect can be scaled to1 without assuming an integral algebraic generator.

For m=1, the exceptional quadric is two reduced points. Its classes a,b are orthogonal idempotents, a+b=1, and the formula gives -(u-v)(u'-v'). An irreducible nodal fibre has equal branch coefficients; two components meeting at one node have component square-1. This agrees with the usual fibre intersection matrix. The strict transform may be disconnected; none of the self-intersection argument requires connectedness. The revised rational-equivalence argument correctly uses CH^(-1)(Q)=0 in this case.

## SN-44-03: local defect and global E=0

### Local map

The dual of Deligne's local specialization sequence has the segment

    H_(2m)(Y_t) -> H_(2m)(F_s) -> Q -> H_(2m-1)(Y_t),

where the last map sends1 to the vanishing cycle. For m>=2, the Gysin sequence of the circle bundle of O_Q(-1), or its etale counterpart with twists, identifies the relevant local quotient with

    H^(2m-2)(Q) / h H^(2m-4)(Q)
      = (Q a + Q b) / Q(a+b).

Restriction of a strict transform therefore sends ua+vb to u-v, after choosing the sign of the generator. For m=1 the reduced quotient H^0(Q)/Q1 gives the same formula. This verifies the bridge between the intersection-theoretic defect and the cohomological vanishing cycle. If the latter is nonzero, the map out of Q is injective, so every vertical algebraic defect is zero. Global conjugacy then gives the E!=0 assertion simultaneously at every singular fibre. No cycle-surjectivity assertion is needed.

### Global zero-height deduction over C

If E=0, the fixed-part theorem and weak Lefschetz make the middle cohomology variation the image of the constant cohomology of X, hence constant as a rational Hodge variation. This last qualification matters: constancy of only the underlying local system would not alone identify an arbitrary variation's Hodge filtration.

I check the Poincare-cycle maps by degree. For dim P=g and dim Y=2m-1, a codimension-m correspondence gives u:H^(2g-1)(P)->H^(2m-1)(Y), and its transpose gives v:H^(2m-1)(Y)->H^1(P). Their composite is a divisor correspondence on P x P because 2m-dim Y=1. On Picard varieties it is a nonzero integer multiple of the incidence duality isogeny. Its cohomological map is therefore invertible. Thus H^1(P), with the appropriate twist, is a direct summand of the constant variation. The finite trace of a Poincare cycle provides maps over the original perfect function field with a possibly larger nonzero multiplicity, which cancels in the normalized duality.

The resulting constant weight-one variation makes P isogenous over C(t) to a constant abelian variety. Every rational point of a constant abelian variety over C(t) is constant, since a rational map from P1 extends and P1 has zero Albanese. The Poincare Neron--Tate pairing consequently vanishes modulo isogeny. The separately cited height comparison then proves zero geometric height. The argument works for m=1 as well; then P is the usual Jacobian.

## SN-44-04 and SN-44-05: the three units and explicit projectors

I independently check the regular-section construction. The functional (d0x0,d1x1,d2x2) annihilates the tautological y-line and gives a section of Q_y^dual tensor O(a+t). Its zero set is exactly the three stated surfaces. At (e_i,s_i), the two nonvanishing coefficient differences and the simple zero of d_i make the three functions local parameters; the incidence equation removes one on every y-chart. Thus the zero scheme is reduced and regular, not merely set-theoretically equal to those surfaces.

The top Chern class is b^2-(a+t)b+(a+t)^2=Z+t(2a-b), since t^2=0. Restriction of the normal bundle to each surface is Q_y^dual, giving self-square1; restriction of Z is b^2, giving pairing1. The three surfaces are disjoint. The residual extension t(b-2a) is vertical and annihilates every vertical class because the base point class restricts to zero there. Its square is zero. Together with the carried AR-01 values this yields the exact three terms1+1+1.

For a normalized nodal class, the product-correspondence composition gives p_s^2=p_s because D_s^2=(-1)^m. Products between distinct fibres vanish. The two maps through the Tate motive compose to identity on that Tate motive, so the summand and projector formula are actual Chow-motive statements. No semisimplicity theorem is used. The Koszul K0 identity and its degree-two Chern character have the stated signs. Over Z[1/2] the three parameter differences remain units, and the same local regularity check applies. The finite-field Tate weights and the three skyscraper terms agree with Deligne's n+1 local specialization term.

## SN-44-06: arbitrary characteristic comparison and fixed part

### Finite fields of definition and normalization

The two heights have the same finite-base-change rule: degrees multiply by the full extension degree. This includes purely inseparable extensions. Dividing by that degree gives a value independent of the chosen finite field of definition, by comparison over a common extension. The perfect-field incidence-Picard descent uses its uniqueness property, so its conjugate isomorphisms satisfy the cocycle identity. A Poincare correspondence defined over a finite extension controls continuity; an automorphism fixing that correspondence and its abelian variety acts trivially because it acts trivially after a nonzero multiplication isogeny. Effective descent is therefore applicable.

Over K^perf, tracing a Poincare cycle from a finite Galois extension gives a rational cycle inducing a nonzero multiple of identity. All varieties, cycles, rational equivalences, maps and the finitely many points in the calculation involve finite data. They descend to a finite purely inseparable K_r/K. For K=k(t), enlarging to k(t^(1/p^r)) retains P1 as the base. This is the key reason the proof does not need descent over the imperfect field k(t) itself or a separable cover of positive genus.

### Comparison by curves

After a finite extension, algebraic triviality writes z as a sum of correspondence images of degree-zero divisors on smooth curves, with rational coefficients absorbing the possible inseparable integer multiplier. A codimension-m correspondence on C x Y sends CH^1(C) to CH^m(Y) and transposes CH^m(Y) back to CH^1(C), so the degrees in the adjunction are correct. With admissible lifts, both height expressions are the pushforward of the same product on the fibre product of models. Refined Gysin commutativity and the projection formula prove the identity for this nonzero correspondence degree. The argument does not misuse the degree-zero-only formulation of Kahn Proposition3.8. Kahn Proposition3.6 supplies admissibility in every degree; the alteration construction extends the calculation when a smooth high-dimensional model is unavailable.

For a curve, the admissible rational divisor is exactly one with zero degree on every fibre component. Pepin's local pairing comparison then identifies its intersection with the usual Neron pairing. Summing gives the global Poincare pairing, with incidence duality the negative theta polarization. Saito's functorial identity gives k_Y lambda_C b=a^dual lambda_Y. These three equalities give the displayed comparison with factor1/k_Y. Tracing the Poincare cycle multiplies both k_Y and lambda_Y by the same integer. There is consequently no hidden multiplicity or extra1/2 in the argument.

### Constant up to isogeny in characteristic p

Deligne gives trivial middle Galois representation when E=0. The same Poincare-cycle composition makes H^1(P) a quotient of it. Tate twists are trivial because the constant field is algebraically closed. A trivial rational Tate representation has trivial action on its integral Tate lattice; hence Neron--Ogg--Shafarevich gives good reduction at every point of P1. The cited quasi-projectivity of the global Neron model, together with properness under good reduction, makes its total space smooth projective.

For its abelian scheme g, the Leray five-term sequence gives H^1(total)->H^0(R^1g_*). The preceding H^1(P1) vanishes, and the next differential vanishes because the zero section splits g^*:H^2(P1)->H^2(total). Thus restriction of H^1(total) to every fibre is an isomorphism. Restriction of the total-space Poincare line bundle defines Pic^0(total)_red x P1 -> A^dual. Kummer identifies its rational Tate maps with those restriction isomorphisms. Its generic fibre is an isogeny, including the possibility of inseparability. Dualizing makes P isogenous to a constant variety over K_r.

Every K_r-point of a constant abelian variety is constant by Alb(P1)=0; this argument includes inseparable morphisms. A quasi-inverse isogeny then makes P(K_r) modulo trace torsion. The canonical pairing kills trace and torsion, giving zero height before and after division by [K_r:K]. This proves the new characteristic-not-two E=0 assertion. Characteristic2 is correctly excluded only from the ordinary-node package; the general Gram and comparison arguments are stated separately.
