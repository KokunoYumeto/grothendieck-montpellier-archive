\subsection{SF-Q6-02: an actual rational theta-jet construction, and its size obstruction}

The archive does contain an inhabited, genuine two-step theta-jet
construction.  This is stronger than a numerical \texttt{NumDatum} or
an arbitrarily assigned one-dimensional kernel.  Its displayed
small-weight construction does not, however, supply a
\texttt{SizeData$_2$} datum, and therefore cannot be used as a closed
\texttt{DivInputs} specialization.

\paragraph{Exact constructive statement and normalization.}
In
\texttt{Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/GlobalInterpolation/
ExistsHypJetsAll.lean}, \texttt{JetsInhab.exists\_colIn\_jets}
constructs
\[
 \exists B,\ X:\texttt{PreX}(B),\ I:\texttt{ColIn}(B,X),\qquad
 \texttt{HasLetters}(B)\ \wedge\ I.\texttt{Ok}\ \wedge\ I.\texttt{JetsIn}.
\]
The proof invokes the actual Hecke-character and column constructors,
adjoins a finite set of algebraic coefficients to the coefficient field,
and invokes \texttt{JetEx.exists\_jetsIn}.  The latter replaces the
ambient normalization by the chosen \texttt{amb0}, and resets the
relevant index exponents to $A=B=m$; it preserves the Hecke frame,
the excluded prime set, and the range of twisting characters.  It
does not assert preservation of every initial index or ambient datum.

The resulting rationality is exactly the degree-restricted statement
in \texttt{JetsInIffStepField.lean}: for each used step $i<m$, each
admitted lattice/coset datum, and each multi-index $\alpha$ with
the prescribed multidegree $\nu_W$,
\[
 \chi_F(N,N')^{-1}\,
 \biggl(\prod_{\tau,r,s}c_D(\tau)^{\alpha_{\tau rs}}\biggr)
 \partial^\alpha\operatorname{thInd}(0)
 \ \in\ B.\texttt{embLL}.\texttt{fieldRange}.
\]
Here \texttt{jetC} is the ordinary iterated derivative defined in
\texttt{Formalism/LocalToGlobal/KernelLattices/ThDistC.lean},
\texttt{derivAt0} and \texttt{jetC}, with no division by
$\alpha!$.  This proves rationality in the finite homogeneous
degrees used by the kernel, rather than the stronger all-degree
predicate \texttt{Amb.JetsRat}.  It consequently supplies real
coefficient data for a finite homogeneous realization that explicitly
retains this derivative normalization.

\paragraph{The parameters of the displayed construction.}
Tracing the proof through \texttt{exists\_pre\_sized} and
\texttt{exists\_pre\_two\_words} gives the following refinement
of its selected witness:
\[
 (k,m,t,f)=(8,2,15,8),\qquad N_i=8+16i,
\]
and, at every $\tau$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 v&=(24,20,16,12,8,4,0),\\
 u&=(49,45,37,29,21,13,5,-1)/2,\\

 \end{split}
\]
Indeed \texttt{exists\_pre\_two\_words} selects
\texttt{Formalism.MainNotions.Inhab.Rt 0}, takes
$\Delta=1$, $h_h=t+3=18$, $u_F=24$, $u_L=-1$ and $b_1=1$,
and uses the word with all Boolean letters true.  The prescribed
Harish--Chandra construction gives $b_j=1+36j$, $e_j=b_j+18$,
$u_0=24+1/2$, $u_7=-1+1/2$, and
$u_{\ell+1}=v_\ell-1/2-1$.
The subsequent \texttt{exists\_pre\_sized} restricts to this base
word (\texttt{Word := Unit}); it changes $d_u^*$ and $D^*$ so
that its $T$ window holds.  It does not change these weights or
the nonempty set \texttt{host.TT}, originally chosen to be $\{0\}$.
Thus the word ``sized'' in this constructor is not the full
\texttt{SizeData$_2$} assertion.

This is a statement about the witnesses obtained by the displayed
construction, or equivalently about the refinement of that proof
retaining its parameter equalities.  The unrefined existential
theorem does not say that every possible witness has these parameters.

\paragraph{Statement SF-Q6-02.}
Let $D$ be a global datum with $k=8$ and
$\texttt{host.TT}\ne\varnothing$.  Suppose the last
Harish--Chandra coordinate at every top embedding is $u_7=-1/2$.
Then there is no $L:\texttt{SizeData$_2}(D)$.
In particular, the selected small-weight witnesses just displayed,
and records retaining their host and Harish--Chandra data, do not
provide such an $L$.

\begin{proof}
Choose $t\in\texttt{host.TT}$ and its corresponding top embedding
$\tau$ (using the equivalence \texttt{D.eTau} and
\texttt{tau1P}).  The \texttt{top\_u} field of
\texttt{SizeData$_2$} gives
\[
 -\frac12=-b_T(t)-\frac{k-1}{2}
             =-b_T(t)-\frac72,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad b_T(t)=-3.
\]
On the other hand \texttt{bT\_ge} gives
$b_T(t)\ge b'>0$.  The positivity is immediate from
$x>1$, $b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)>0$,
$a=xb>0$ and
$b'=a/\exp(x^{\varepsilon/3})>0$.
This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What remains available.}
The rational two-step coefficient construction is useful in its own
right.  The general theorem
\texttt{exists\_preCh\_of\_prescribed} also permits other
Harish--Chandra weights; the obstruction above concerns the specific
small-weight specialization, not all $m=2$ global data.  A full
arithmetic specialization must choose weights satisfying every
\texttt{SizeData$_2$} condition, assemble the gain and error data,
retain the stabilizer contribution, and establish the relevant
composed period or positive full bound.
