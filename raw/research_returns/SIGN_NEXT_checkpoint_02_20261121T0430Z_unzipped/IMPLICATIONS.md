## Cote 16: the next step after sign-only purity

**Scans 049–050 now have a usable, correctly indexed route.** Historical `C_G(X)` is algebraicity of the Lefschetz lowering operator, hence modern standard conjecture B. It is not modern Künneth C. The literal lower-critical ordinary-Gysin reading fails already on `P1 × P1`: a `(1,1)` section pushes its degree-zero class to `a+b`, which does not span both divisor classes. This is a counterexample to that stated reading, with the ambiguity of the handwritten symbol kept separate.

SN-C03–04 prove the replacement. For `T=X×X` and its actual smooth hyperplane `W`, the critical algebraic Lefschetz condition `A0(T)` is equivalent to critical restriction reflection `R0(T,W)` together with weighted Gysin surjectivity `L_T j_*`. Replacing that weighted map by ordinary upper Gysin introduces exactly `A0(W)`. The latter is on `W`, whose dimension is `2 dim(X)-1`; it is not on the square of the hyperplane section of `X`. Kleiman 1968, Theorem 2.13(i),(ii′), supplies the classical equivalence with `C_G(X)`, and Proposition 3.6 shows that `D(W)` suffices for the missing algebraic Lefschetz condition. The formal countermodel SN-C05 establishes a failure of a proposed formal deduction, not a counterexample to the geometric standard conjectures.

**The abelian branch closes.** Classical algebraic Lefschetz operators give `C_G(A)`. SN-10 writes the required critical inverse maps explicitly. The stipulated 032 CM Hodge theorem, Milne 2002 Theorem 3.3(a),(b), and 032 Corollary 8.4 supply `I(A)` for all abelian varieties in the stated realizations, and also equality of numerical and homological equivalence. Applying this to all abelian products supplies the hypotheses of Kleiman Theorem 3.11 and Corollaries 3.12 and 3.14. Thus the positive-adjoint semisimplicity and splitting route succeeds for abelian varieties and for the specified powers of CM elliptic curves. The latter also have the separate SG-04 / Milne Remarks 3.7–3.8 proof.

**The sign-only theorem alone still supplies a different kind of information.** SN-14 constructs a rigid tensor countermodel with pure Frobenius eigenvalues and the full induced coefficient trace/λ identities, but with a nonzero nilpotent radical. Its algebra is `Q[N]/(N^3)` and its Frobenius is `c exp(N)`. All relevant trace and local-factor data agree with the associated graded. This refutes the formal inference from these data to semisimplicity. For the scan-049 positive-adjoint method, the additional assertion is an algebraic involution and the positive definite rational form `tau(u^dagger u)>0` for every nonzero correspondence in the image algebra. SN-13 gives explicit algebraic splitting maps once that positivity is present. No claim is made that this is the only possible method of proving semisimplicity.

## Cote 44: corrected Theorem 5, scan 004, manuscript page 2

The missing term is a vertical intersection correction. SN-44-01 gives the general Gram formula on the quotient of vertical numerical cycles by its radical. With `a_s(Z)` the intersection vector and `G_s` the nondegenerate vertical Gram matrix, the normalized signed height is

`(-1)^m [ Z·Z' - sum_s a_s(Z)^t G_s^(-1) a_s(Z') ]`.

The height is identified with the canonical incidence self-duality, including its factor `1/k`, rather than an unspecified metric normalization. SN-44-06 extends this comparison over algebraically closed fields in all characteristics for the stated smooth models; the explicit ordinary-node analysis requires characteristic different from `2`.

For a Lefschetz pencil, each active node has a normalized vertical class `D_s` with square `(-1)^m`, and the signed height is `(-1)^m Z·Z' - sum_s c_s(Z)c_s(Z')`. In the `E=0` case the height vanishes. The positive-characteristic proof uses the lisse constant middle cohomology, Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich, the total abelian scheme over `P1`, and its constant Picard variety to obtain an isogeny to a constant abelian variety. It does not assert that every abelian scheme over `P1` is itself constant.

In the supplied `P2×P2` diagonal pencil, the three nodal fibres are at `[0:1]`, `[-1:1]`, and `[-2:1]`. Their actual surfaces `D_i` satisfy `D_i²=1`, `D_iD_j=0` for `i≠j`, and `Z D_i=1`. Each contribution is exactly `1`, so the corrected equality is `0=3-(1+1+1)`. The stronger integral Chow relation `sum D_i=Z+t(2a-b)` identifies the corrected extension as the vertical-radical class `t(b-2a)`.

This closes the geometric height-correction portion of HODGE_SIGN U-03. It does not prove the separate general higher arithmetic Hodge-index theorem of cote 44 or remove metric hypotheses from an arithmetic statement.

## Schemes, K-theory, sheaves, motives, and L-functions

The framework sections of the paper provide the individual translations. For numerical motives, Jannsen 1992 Theorem 1 already gives unconditional rational semisimplicity. The extra abelian conclusion here identifies that category with the specified homological realization and supplies its positive Lefschetz-adjoint form. For general smooth projective varieties, the algebraicity and positivity standard conjectures remain outside the proved scope. No false counterexample to those geometric conjectures is inferred from a formal module or a pure nonsplit sheaf at a point.
