## G-01 — Universal integral secant equations

**Statement (checked below).** Let V_i=Z^{n_i}, V=⊗_{i=1}^d V_i, and let A(V)=Spec Sym(V^*) be the affine space with its universal tensor. For each r, the map

φ_r : (∏_{i=1}^d A(V_i))^r → A(V), ((v_{ij})) ↦ Σ_{j=1}^r v_{1j}⊗...⊗v_{dj}

is a morphism of Z-schemes, defined by polynomials with integer coefficients. Its scheme-theoretic image Σ_{r,Z} is defined by ker φ_r^*; its coordinate ring is a subring of the polynomial ring of the source and therefore is torsion-free over Z (hence flat over Z). Over C its underlying reduced subscheme is exactly the affine cone of the usual rth secant variety of the Segre variety. Every polynomial identity in ker φ_r^* remains an identity on sums of r decomposable tensors over every commutative ring and vanishes on the corresponding secant closure over every field. We do not assert that formation of the scheme-theoretic image commutes with reduction modulo every prime.

**Proof.** In bases, each coordinate of φ_r is Σ_j ∏_i x_{ij,k_i}, an integer polynomial. Thus φ_r induces the stated homomorphism from Sym(V*) into a polynomial Z-algebra P. Its image B≅Sym(V*)/ker φ_r* is a subring of P. If 0≠m∈Z and mb=0 in B, the same equality holds in P, which is torsion-free, so b=0. Every torsion-free Z-module is flat. The source is integral, so B is a domain, and localization to Q and then scalar extension to C gives the Zariski closure of the set of complex sums of r decomposable tensors. More explicitly, C is flat over Z and P/B has no relevance in characteristic zero after localization: ker(φ_r*⊗C)=ker(φ_r*)⊗C, since C is flat over Z. This identifies the complex image scheme. A polynomial in the kernel is a universal polynomial identity; substituting in any ring proves the last assertion. Over a nonflat base change the kernel can grow, so the integral construction alone does not identify every special fiber with its independently formed secant scheme. ∎

**Check notes.** Whole proof reread. The characteristic-zero kernel base change uses flatness of C over Z, not just flatness of B. The warning for F_p is essential. The source is affine and finite type; scheme-image formula is elementary. ## G-02 — Flattenings and Koszul flattenings as morphisms of bundles

**Statement (checked below).** Let S be any scheme and A,B,C finite locally free O_S-modules, of ranks a,b,c. A tensor section t∈A⊗B⊗C defines, for 0≤p<a, a bundle morphism

K_p(t): B^*⊗Λ^p A → Λ^{p+1} A⊗C,

β⊗ω ↦ Σ_i β(b_i)(a_i∧ω)⊗c_i when t=Σ_i a_i⊗b_i⊗c_i. This is canonical and compatible with arbitrary base change. Put κ=binom(a−1,p). On the universal integral sum of r decomposable tensors all (rκ+1)-minors of K_p vanish. Consequently, over any field, rank K_p(t)>rκ certifies that t has border rank greater than r. For an ordinary flattening κ=1.

**Proof.** K_p is the composition of tensor contraction, tensoring with id_{Λ^p A}, and exterior multiplication A⊗Λ^p A→Λ^{p+1} A. These constructions are canonical for locally free sheaves and commute with base change; in a frame the entries are linear functions of t with coefficients 0,1,−1. Over a field, for a nonzero pure tensor a_0⊗b_0⊗c_0, K_p is (ω↦a_0∧ω) tensored with the rank-one map β↦β(b_0)c_0. Extend a_0 to a basis. Exactly the wedge basis vectors not containing a_0 survive, giving κ independent image vectors. Thus rank on a pure tensor is κ. Subadditivity of matrix rank gives rank≤rκ on sums of r pure tensors. Apply this over Q to the universal polynomial-coordinate sum: all indicated minors are zero polynomials over Q, hence, since their coefficients lie in Z, zero over Z. They remain zero after every base change. Over a field, matrix rank≤rκ is a Zariski-closed determinantal condition, so it also holds on the border-rank≤r closure. For ordinary flattenings a nonzero pure tensor gives a rank-one matrix. ∎

**Dictionary for the root paper's norm certificates.** The maps Φ used there become morphisms of finite locally free sheaves on the affine tensor bundle. The condition Λ^{s+1}Φ=0 is the vanishing of its (s+1)-minors, equivalently the relevant Fitting-ideal condition on coker Φ. With target rank f, it is Fitt_{f-s-1}(coker Φ)=0 when the index is meaningful. The matrix singular-value inequality used after selecting a complex point and Hermitian metrics is an analytic certificate of a rank condition on this morphism. The numerical inequality does not define an algebraic norm over S; the resulting nonvanishing minor is the algebraic information.

**Proof check.** Entire argument checked once by this agent. This is an integral sheaf formulation of a known rank-one calculation rather than a new flattening method. Avoid claiming novelty. Sources: Landsberg–Ottaviani (2015), Theorem 2.1; equation and border rank bound are also in Landsberg–Michałek arXiv:1608.07486, §2, equations (2)–(4). Exact finite-dimensional version verified in the primary PDF, URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.07486 (tool ref turn615271189view2).

## G-03 — A precise FGA parameter space for limits

**Statement (checked below).** Let X⊂P(V) be an integral, nondegenerate projective variety over an algebraically closed field, and 1≤r≤dim V. Let U⊂Hilb^r(X) be the open locus of reduced length-r subschemes consisting of r distinct linearly independent points. On U the linear span defines a morphism U→Gr(r,V). Let Γ be the closure of its graph in Hilb^r(X)×Gr(r,V), and let S_r be the pullback to Γ of the tautological rank-r subbundle on Gr(r,V). The image of the proper morphism P(S_r)→P(V) is exactly σ_r(X).

**Proof.** Hilb^r(X) and Gr(r,V) are projective, so Γ is projective. The projective bundle P(S_r) is projective over Γ, hence its map to P(V) is proper and its image is closed. Over U its image is the union of the (r−1)-planes spanned by r linearly independent distinct points. This union is dense in the secant variety defined using arbitrary r-tuples: X^r is irreducible, and the open subset of distinct linearly independent tuples is nonempty because X is nondegenerate and r≤dim V; the sum/span parameterization over this open is dense in the whole parameterized secant closure. Since Γ is by definition the closure of the graph over U, the corresponding open part of P(S_r) is dense in P(S_r). Continuity shows that the image of P(S_r) is contained in the closure of the image of this dense open; the reverse containment follows from properness. The result follows. ∎

**Important limitation.** This statement retains the limiting r-plane as well as the length-r scheme. It does not identify border rank with membership in the actual span of a limiting smoothable length-r scheme, which can fail without suitable hypotheses on the embedding because the evaluation map V*→H^0(Z,O_Z(1)) can drop rank. At a boundary point (Z,L) of Γ, one has Z⊂P(L), but P(L) need not equal the actual span of Z. The incidence formulation is the safe exact dictionary. For r>dim V, the ambient space is already the secant variety and this construction is unnecessary.

## G-04 — Group schemes and orbit closures

**Dictionary.** G=∏_i GL(V_i) is a group scheme over Z; GL_n=Spec Z[x_ij,det(x_ij)^{-1}]. Its tensor representation acts on A(V). For a fixed integral tensor t the orbit map G→A(V), g↦g·t, has an affine scheme-theoretic image with ideal ker(Sym(V*)→Z[G]). It is an integral model of the orbit closure and its complex fiber is the usual complex orbit closure. As in G-01, no automatic identification of every special fiber is asserted. For homogeneous polynomials over C one uses GL(W) acting on Sym^d(W*) and the Veronese variety; for CP rank one uses ∏GL(V_i) and the Segre variety. These are different representations and different secant varieties. Their integral symmetric/divided-power distinction is made explicit in G-07.

**Exact SGA locator.** SGA 3, Exposé I, §4.5 (“Autres exemples de groupes”), explicit GL_n construction; §4.6 discusses module schemes associated to O_S-modules. The original scan has both on printed p.29, PDF zero-based page41 / one-based scan leaf42. Scan URL https://library.slmath.org/nonmsri/sga/sga/pdf/sga3-1.pdf; visually inspected. French searchable transcription: https://grothendiecksga.com/read/sga3/fr/I-4.html. Source attribution should name Demazure–Grothendieck et al., not imply Grothendieck personally authored every exposé.

**Norm obstruction, a complete elementary proof.** No nonzero norm on V_C can be invariant under the full product GL action when any factor contains scalar multiplication by arbitrary nonzero complex numbers. If a norm N were invariant, for t≠0 and real λ>1 the action (λI,id,...,id) gives N(λt)=N(t), whereas norm homogeneity gives N(λt)=λN(t), contradiction. Even scale-free norm ratios generally vary on a single GL orbit: for d≥2 and 0<ε≤1, t_ε=e_1^{⊗d}+εe_2^{⊗d} has F²=1+ε², projective norm 1+ε, and injective norm 1, so π²/F²=(1+ε)²/(1+ε²). All t_ε with ε≠0 lie in the same product-GL orbit. The norms follow from the displayed orthogonal decomposition, the diagonal injective maximization, and duality with e_1^{⊗d}+e_2^{⊗d}; alternatively one can omit the ratio example and retain the scalar proof.

**Implication.** Norm findings transfer exactly to each chosen archimedean fiber equipped with specified Euclidean/Hermitian metrics. They are statements about a vector bundle plus metric data. Ordinary EGA/SGA schemes and bundles alone have no distinguished positive definite Hermitian metric. This does not prohibit optimizing certificates over a GL orbit; it says that such an optimization is an extra mathematical step, not an automatic change of terminology.

## G-05 — K0, λ-operations, Schur functors and their information content

**Exact dictionary.** For finite locally free sheaves E, λ^i[E]=[Λ^iE] in K0(S), and λ_t([E]+[F])=λ_t[E]λ_t[F]. For algebraic group representations, the analogous construction lives in the Grothendieck ring of the exact tensor category of finite locally free representations; over C for reductive G this recovers the usual representation ring. The Koszul source and target have classes [B*]λ^p[A] and [C]λ^{p+1}[A]. Their dimensions and characters can be computed in this ring. Exterior, symmetric, and integral Schur functors provide the associated bundles; over Q one may realize Schur functors using Young idempotents. Over Z one must use integral Schur-functor constructions and must not silently divide by hook lengths or d!.

**Precise limitation with proof.** The class of the source and target of a morphism, even together with all λ- and γ-operations on those classes, does not determine its rank. On S=Spec C, compare the two maps 0 and id from E=C^N to E. The ordered pair of K0 classes is ([E],[E]) for both; every expression built from those classes by ring and λ/γ operations has the same value. Their ranks are respectively 0 and N. The two-term complexes also both have virtual class [E]−[E]=0. Hence the rank of K_p(t), or nonvanishing of its minors, requires the actual morphism, not just the virtual classes of its bundles. This makes no claim that K0 of an image, or richer equivariant data incorporating t, is useless; computing such data can already involve the sought rank information. ∎

**Extension example.** In Rep_C(G_a), the trivial two-dimensional representation and t↦[[1,t],[0,1]] have the same K0 class 2[1] from their filtrations, but are not isomorphic (one has trivial action and the other does not). This is an exact sense in which a Grothendieck group forgets extension data. It is not a universal barrier to all uses of K-theory in GCT.

**Rank-jump family.** Over A^1_C, let E=O^N and use the morphism z·id_E. Its fiber rank is N for z≠0 and0 for z=0, while its source and target, their K0 classes, and every λ/γ expression in those two classes remain constant. Thus these bundle classes also fail to record this concrete degeneration. The determinant z^N, or the actual map and its degeneracy ideal, does record it. This is a statement about those specified class invariants, not a theorem that no enriched K-theoretic construction detects degenerations.

## G-06 — ℓ-adic sheaves, weights and motives: exact applicability

The affine tensor space, Segre/Veronese secant schemes, and integral orbit models can be reduced modulo primes and supplied with étale sheaves. That is a legitimate new object to study, but none of the norm arguments constructs an ℓ-adic sheaf whose Frobenius weights or purity would certify the required tensor lower bound. No weight statement follows merely by replacing “vector space” with “sheaf.”

**Small formal nonimplication.** Over F_q, with ℓ different from the characteristic, choose two rational points 0 and e_1⊗...⊗e_d in the same affine tensor space. Their tensor ranks are 0 and 1. As abstract schemes each point is Spec F_q, and the pullback of the constant Q_l-sheaf has the same one-dimensional H^0, Frobenius eigenvalue 1, and weight 0 at both. Thus these constant-sheaf stalk data do not determine tensor rank. Likewise the absolute motive of either abstract point is the unit motive. This does not rule out sheaves or relative motives constructed from secant geometry, or tensor-dependent objects with additional data. ∎

## G-07 — Integral polynomials versus normalized symmetric Hilbert tensors

**Statement and exact dictionary (checked below).** Let E=Z^{n²}. The determinant and permanent polynomials lie integrally in Sym^n(E*). Their symmetric coefficient tensors S_F used for Hilbert norms over C are characterized by F(x)=⟨S_F,x^{⊗n}⟩ (bilinear coefficient convention, conjugations adjusted for Hermitian notation). Since every monomial of det_n or per_n uses n distinct variables, each of its n! ordered tensor slots has coefficient ±1/n!. Thus S_F is naturally defined over Z[1/n!], and n!S_F is integral. One must not call the normalized S_F an integral section of the tensor bundle. The polynomial and symmetric tensor models are canonically identified after inverting n! by symmetrization; divided powers provide the integral alternative.

**Proof of an integral catalecticant form.** The degree-k part of the coproduct F(x+y) defines a canonical map C_k(F): Γ^k E→Sym^{n−k}(E*). In coordinates its columns are Hasse derivatives ∂^[α]F, where ∂^[α](x^β)=binom(β,α)x^{β−α}. This is integral and commutes with base change. For det_n and per_n, every nonzero such derivative is, up to a sign for the determinant, the determinant or permanent of the submatrix complementary to specified sets I,J of k rows and k columns. A nonzero derivative can use no repeated variable, row, or column; conversely, any matching between I and J gives the corresponding complementary polynomial. There are binom(n,k)² distinct pairs(I,J). Their monomial supports are disjoint because a monomial records its row set and column set; each polynomial has coefficients±1, or1, and is nonzero over every field. Hence these columns span a free direct-sum space of dimension binom(n,k)² over every field. In particular C_k(F) has that rank integrally fiberwise, even in characteristic2 when determinant and permanent coincide.

For a polynomial pure power ℓ^n the same coproduct gives C_k(ℓ^n) of rank at most1 (possibly0 in a bad characteristic). Thus the (r+1)-minors vanish on every sum of r polynomial pure powers and on its closure. This gives an integral determinantal statement independent of any metric. In characteristic0, the map is the usual catalecticant up to the conventional nonzero factorial scalings, so it yields exactly the root paper's binom(n,k)² lower bound. ∎

**Characteristic warning.** In characteristic p≤n, the map ℓ↦ℓ^n into the polynomial module and the usual complete-linear-system Veronese embedding must not be identified using characteristic-zero symmetrization; some multinomial coefficients vanish. The integral scheme/minor identity survives, but the intended rank notion and the correct symmetric/divided-power model must be specified anew. In general matrix ranks can drop on reduction; the complementary-minor calculation above proves no such drop for this particular Hasse matrix. The equal-singular-value assertions and exact nuclear/injective norms live only on the chosen complex or real Hilbert fiber. Whole proof reread and checked.

## B-02 — No-occurrence theorem scope

The attached Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova source proves that for n≥m^25, every highest weight λ occurring in the coordinate ring C[Z_{n,m}] of the padded permanent orbit closure also occurs in C[Ω_n], the determinant orbit closure. This rules out occurrence obstructions in that range. It does not prove inclusion of the orbit closures, equality/inequality of multiplicities in the needed direction, P=NP, or failure of all geometric obstructions. The root paper should not identify this with a tensor-rank/norm barrier.

In Grothendieck-language terms, an actual equivariant closed inclusion Z↪Ω induces a surjection of graded O[G]-comodules on coordinate rings; over C, semisimplicity of polynomial GL-representations imposes inequalities of multiplicities in each degree. The theorem supplies only the corresponding support inclusion, a strictly weaker condition on the representation semiring. K0 retains multiplicities, so replacing the character by its support discards information beyond K0 itself.
