# Coordinate restrictions identify the minimum border rank in the image

Candidate dictionary for a possible F-23. This note supplies a complete proof and distinguishes the integral maps from their normalized characteristic-zero tensor realizations. It does not assert historical priority.

## CI-01 — Minimum residual rank by coefficient isolation

Fix a field K of characteristic zero, 1≤b≤n, and use ordinary CP border rank over an algebraic closure, or real Euclidean border rank when K=R. Let E_n=K^{n×n}. Let U^-_{n,b} (respectively U^+_{n,b}) be the subspace of Sym^b(E_n) generated by the normalized symmetric coefficient tensors of all b×b minors (respectively permanents). Then

\[
\min_{0\ne u\in U^-_{n,b}}\underline R_{\rm CP}(u)
=\underline R_{\rm CP}(\mathcal D_b),\qquad
\min_{0\ne u\in U^+_{n,b}}\underline R_{\rm CP}(u)
=\underline R_{\rm CP}(\mathcal P_b).
\]

For the residual row-tensor image V^-_{n,b} generated by the alternating b-tensors on b-element coordinate subsets, and the residual row-permanent image V^+_{n,b} generated by the sums of all orders of those subsets, likewise

\[
\min_{0\ne u\in V^-_{n,b}}\underline R_{\rm CP}(u)
=\underline R_{\rm CP}(D_b),\qquad
\min_{0\ne u\in V^+_{n,b}}\underline R_{\rm CP}(u)
=\underline R_{\rm CP}(P_b).
\]

**Proof.** For the polynomial determinant case write

\[
u=\sum_{|I|=|J|=b}a_{I,J}\,\mathcal D_{I,J},
\]

with some a_{I_0,J_0}≠0. Define the coordinate quotient p:E_n→E_b that retains exactly the matrix units indexed by I_0×J_0, identifying those rows and columns in increasing order with [b]. Every monomial of a b×b minor uses all of its b rows and all of its b columns. Thus a minor on another pair (I,J) is killed by this restriction: for it to survive, its b rows must be contained in I_0 and its b columns in J_0, forcing I=I_0 and J=J_0. Consequently

\[
p^{\otimes b}(u)=a_{I_0,J_0}\mathcal D_b.
\]

A product of linear maps sends a pure tensor to a pure tensor or zero and is continuous. It therefore does not increase CP rank or border rank. Nonzero scalar multiplication does not change either rank. Hence every nonzero u has border rank at least that of D_b's polynomial tensor. Conversely, any generator is the coordinate inclusion j^{⊗b}(D_b), where p∘j=id. Applying the inclusion and then its left inverse proves equality of its border rank with that of D_b. This generator attains the minimum. For permanents precisely the same support restriction applies, with all coefficients positive instead of alternating signs.

For the row spaces write u as a linear combination of the generators indexed by b-subsets I. Retain the coordinates of an index set I_0 with nonzero coefficient. A generator indexed by a different b-subset uses at least one deleted coordinate and is killed. The selected generator becomes D_b or P_b. The same monotonicity and split inclusion argument gives both row identities. ∎

The proof also works for ordinary CP rank. For Waring border rank of the symmetric polynomial tensors, all b factors use the same quotient p, so a pure power maps to a pure power or zero; the same minimum identity holds with Waring border rank throughout. When applying ordinary CP border substitution to a symmetric n-tensor, however, one must use the CP version: its approximating atoms need not be symmetric.

## Consequence of the incidence/substitution lemma

The k|(n−k) residual images, with b=n−k, are exactly the spaces above. Their dimensions are q=binomial(n,b)^2 in the polynomial cases and q=binomial(n,b) in the row cases. Combining CI-01 with F-13/GI-02 gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\underline R_{\rm CP}(\mathcal D_n)&\geq\binom nb^2+\underline R_{\rm CP}(\mathcal D_b)-1,\\
\underline R_{\rm CP}(\mathcal P_n)&\geq\binom nb^2+\underline R_{\rm CP}(\mathcal P_b)-1,\\
\underline R_{\rm CP}(D_n)&\geq\binom nb+\underline R_{\rm CP}(D_b)-1,\\
\underline R_{\rm CP}(P_n)&\geq\binom nb+\underline R_{\rm CP}(P_b)-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

For b=2, both normalized polynomial tensors are 4×4 symmetric bilinear matrices of rank4 (two nonzero off-diagonal 2×2 blocks). Hence both polynomial inequalities give binomial(n,2)^2+3, in particular39 for n=4. This makes the separate quadratic-image argument QI-01 unnecessary for that numerical consequence, though QI-01 remains a valid independent proof. Any larger-b numerical input must retain its actual scope (ordinary CP versus Waring, and field): the dictionary does not supply an unverified rank value.

## Exact scheme dictionary

| Tensor statement | Scheme/bundle statement | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Retain selected matrix coordinates | A split quotient of free Z-modules, hence of trivial vector bundles, E_n→E_b | Defined integrally with entries0 or1; not a GL orbit operation because it is generally noninvertible. |
| Apply the quotient in each tensor factor | The natural tensor-power morphism p^{⊗b}; for polynomials, restriction along the dual coordinate inclusion | Sends the Segre cone into the Segre cone (allowing zero) and sends pure powers into pure powers. |
| Isolate one coefficient | The pullback of every other minor/permanent section vanishes, while the chosen one becomes the standard b×b section | An integral polynomial identity. The row versions are also integral tensor identities. |
| Rank/border rank is monotone | Products of linear maps preserve sums of r atoms and their closures | In the projective formulation the induced projection may be undefined on its kernel; the affine cone formulation avoids that issue. |
| Minimum image rank equals a smaller target rank | The projectivized image subbundle avoids the residual secants up to exactly the first secant met by the smaller target | The statement is fiberwise over the chosen algebraically closed field; in real Euclidean form it uses Euclidean closure. |
| Combine with the image substitution lemma | The image point in Gr(q,W) avoids the proper incidence image I_{q,s} for s below the smaller target border rank | This is the closed incidence construction of F-18/GI-01, using projectivity/properness rather than a single fixed linear flattening matrix. |

The coordinate quotients and polynomial identities exist over Z. The normalized polynomial coefficient tensors have 1/b! coefficients, so their tensor realization in this note is over Z[1/b!] (and the original normalized degree-n tensor over Z[1/n!]). Equivalently, the unnormalized integral polynomial side can be kept in the divided-power/Hasse dictionary of F-22. One must not declare the normalized Hilbert tensor integral or assert that every independently formed secant commutes with nonflat special-fiber reduction. The row-tensor identities themselves need no factorial inversion.

For a family of images over a nonreduced base, work on the determinantal rank-q stratum, where (q+1)-minors vanish as functions and the q-minors generate the unit ideal. This supplies the actual image subbundle and the morphism to the Grassmannian. Constant geometric-fiber rank alone is insufficient.

## Proposed crosswalk entry

**F-23 (if adopted):** Split coordinate quotients of locally free modules isolate one complementary minor/permanent section. Tensor powers of these quotients preserve the relative Segre cone and its r-fold sum morphisms. Rank monotonicity identifies the least residual image border rank with the corresponding smaller tensor rank; the proper Grassmannian incidence from F-18 then supplies the recursive bound. The coordinate identities are integral; normalized symmetric tensors use F-22's localization convention. Anchors: SGA3 I §§4.5–4.6 for module schemes; EGA I §9.5 for scheme-theoretic images; EGA II Definition5.4.1, Proposition5.4.2, Theorem5.5.3(i) for proper incidence; FGA exposé221 Theorems3.1–3.2 for the Hilbert parameter-space context. These are construction anchors, not claims that Grothendieck stated the modern rank recursion.

This strengthening is algebraic and norm-free after the image is identified. It should remain distinguished from the genuinely analytic projector-norm certificate of F-14, and from the scoped linear flattening barriers.
