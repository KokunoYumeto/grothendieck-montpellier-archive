                                                    Abstract. We investigate the Galois cohomology of ﬁnitely generated max-
                                                    imal pro-p quotients of absolute Galois groups. Assuming the well-known
                                                    conjectural description of these groups, we show that Galois cohomology has
                                                    the PBW property. Hence in particular it is a Koszul algebra. This answers
                                                    positively a conjecture by Positselski in this case. We also provide an anal-
                                                    ogous unconditional result about Pythagorean ﬁelds. Moreover, we establish
                                                    some results that relate the quadratic dual of Galois cohomology with the p-
                                                    Zassenhaus ﬁltration on the group. This paper also contains a survey of Koszul
                                                    property in Galois cohomology and its relation with absolute Galois groups.




                                                                                1. Introduction
                                              Let p be a prime number and F be a field. The absolute Galois group GF =
                                           Gal(Fsep /F ) is the Galois group of the maximal separable extension Fsep of F . This
                                           group contains all the Galois-theoretic information about F , as any finite Galois
                                           group of an extension of F is a quotient of GF .
                                              Despite such a central role in Galois theory, absolute Galois groups of fields
                                           remain rather mysterious. They are profinite groups, and one of the main problems
                                           in current research in Galois theory is to determine which profinite groups are
                                           absolute Galois groups of fields. One way to investigate the structure of profinite
                                           groups is Galois cohomology. This is a cohomology theory based on continuous
                                           cochains and coboundaries (cf. [Ser13, Ch. 2]). The Galois cohomology of F with
                                           coefficients in the finite field Fp = Z/pZ is a graded algebra

Standing Hypothesis 1.2. For p = 2, F contains −1. Equivalently, in purely
group-theoretic terms, the image of the orientation associated to a pro-2 group G is
contained in {1 + 4λ | λ ∈ Z2 } (see Definition 4.1).
   Recently, M. Rost and V. Voevodsky completed the proof of the long-standing
Bloch-Kato conjecture (cf. [SJ06, HW09, Voe10, Voe11], see also [NSW08, § VI.4]).
Under our Hypothesis 1.1 this theorem implies that H • (GF , Fp ) is generated by
elements of degree 1 and its relations are generated by homogeneous relations of
degree 2. Such algebras are called quadratic algebras (cf. [PP05, § 1.2]).
   Locally finite-dimensional (cf. [PP05, Page 1] and Definition 2.1) quadratic al-
gebras come equipped with a duality. The quadratic dual of such an algebra A• is
a quadratic algebra generated over the same field by the dual space of the space of
generators of A• . Its relators form the orthogonal complement of the space of the
relators of A• (see Definition 2.3). The double quadratic dual (A!• )! is isomorphic
to A• as a graded algebra.
   Among quadratic algebras, the significant class of Koszul algebras has been sin-
gled out in [Pri70]. These are the graded algebras for which the ground field has a
linear free resolution as trivial module, that is a resolution of free graded modules
(Pi , di ) with each Pi generated in degree i. They are characterised by an uncom-
monly nice cohomological behaviour (cf. L § 2).
   For a general graded algebra A• = n≥0 An over a field k, the k-cohomology is
defined as the direct sum of the derived functors of the functor HomA• ( , k) evalu-
                                   L
ated on k, that is ExtA• (k, k) = i∈N ExtiA• (k, k). Finding an explicit description
of the cohomology of an arbitrary graded algebra is generally a hopeless battle. But
the cohomology of a Koszul k-algebra A• is just its quadratic dual A!• . This also
means that Koszul algebras can be reconstructed from their cohomology. There is
not the typical loss of information in passing from an algebraic or topological object
to its cohomology.
   Koszul algebras arise in various fields of mathematics, such as representation
theory, noncommutative algebra, quantum groups, noncommutative geometry, al-
gebraic geometry and topology (cf., e.g., [BGS96, Frö75, Koh85, Koh83, LV12]).
Moreover, Koszul algebras have been studied in the context of Galois cohomol-
ogy by L. Positselski and A. Vishik (cf. [PV95]). This was considerably extended
in further remarkable papers by Positselski (cf. [Pos05, Pos14]). In particular, in
[Pos14, § 0.1] he explicitly formulated a general conjecture, of which the following
is a special case.
Conjecture 1.3 (Positselski). Let F be a field containing a primitive pth root of
unity. Then the algebra H • (GF , Fp ) is Koszul.
   In [Pos05, Theorem 1.3] it is shown that the Koszulity — i.e., the property of
being Koszul — of the reduction modulo p of the Milnor K-theory, along with the
bijectivity of the corresponding norm residue map in degree 2 and the injectivity of
the one in degree 3 would give an easier and natural proof of Bloch-Kato conjecture
for the prime p. Positselski’s Conjecture can be seen as a strenghtening of Bloch-
Kato Conjecture.

   In this paper we prove that in several cases the Galois cohomology and its qua-
dratic dual have the stronger PBW property (cf. [LV12, Chapter 4]).
   There is a conjectural description of maximal pro-p quotients of absolute Galois
groups that are finitely generated and satisfy our Hypothesis 1.1. This description
roughly says that all such groups can be constructed inductively from finitely gen-
erated free pro-p groups and Galois groups of maximal p-extensions of local fields
using free products and certain semidirect products. Slightly more general groups
constructed this way are called elementary type pro-p groups (see § 4.3 for details)
and the former conjecture is known as Elementary Type Conjecture, in short ETC,
formulated by I. Efrat (cf. [Efr95]).
   Our main results are the following.
Theorem A. If G is an elementary type pro-p group, then H • (G, Fp ) is PBW,
and hence Koszul.
Remark 1.4. In Theorem A we can replace G by any absolute Galois group GF
such that its maximal pro-p quotient GF (p) is of elementary type. This follows
from the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence associated to the exact sequence
                          / Ker(π)       / GF   π   / GF (p)      / 1,
                  1
where π is the canonical projection. Indeed, Ker(π) ∼    = Gal(Fsep /F (p)), where F (p)
is the compositum of all Galois extensions of F of degree a power of p. By Bloch-
Kato Conjecture, H n (Gal(Fsep /F (p)), Fp ) = 0 for all n > 0, so the Hochschild-Serre
spectral sequence collapses at the second page. As a consequence, the inflation
map inf : H • (GF (p), Fp ) → H • (GF , Fp ) is an isomorphism. (This argument is well-
known to the experts, cf., e.g., [AEJ84, Proposition 5.9, p. 45] and [Efr06, § 24.3].)
   The quadratic dual of cohomology is related to the successive factors of a de-
scending series on G, the p-Zassenhaus filtration (see Definition 3.1). Let U(L(G))
be the restricted universal enveloping algebra of the restricted Lie algebra L(G)
given by the p-Zassenhaus filtration on G (see § 3.3).
Theorem B. If G is an elementary type pro-p group, satisfying Hypothesis 1.2 if
p = 2, then the quadratic dual of H • (G, Fp ) is U(L(G)) and it is PBW, and hence
Koszul.
   By a result of Jennings (Theorem 3.10), for a finitely generated pro-p group G,
U(L(G)) is isomorphic to the graded object grFp [[G]] associated to the filtration on
the complete group algebra of G by the successive powers of the complete augmen-
tation ideal (see Definition 3.3). Thus, Theorem B gives a partial positive answer
to a question of T. Weigel (cf. [Wei15]):
Question 1.5 (Weigel). Let F be a field containing a primitive pth root of unity.
Is the algebra U(L(GF (p))) Koszul?
   Theorem B can be proved without Hypothesis 1.2 for the class of Demushkin
groups (see § 4). In particular, this applies to maximal pro-p quotients of abso-
lute Galois groups of local fields that contain a primitive pth root of unity. In
combination with Theorem A, this gives:
Theorem C. If G is a Demushkin pro-p group, then the algebras H • (G, Fp ) and
U(L(G)) are quadratic dual to each other and PBW, hence Koszul.

   There are numerous families of fields for which a suitable version of the Elemen-
tary Type Conjecture is known to hold. For each of these families, the results of
this paper guarantee the Koszulity of Galois cohomology unconditionally. In many
cases, also a description of U(L(GF )) is available and the duality of the two algebras
can be proved. The notion of Pythagorean field will be recalled in § 6.2, the notion
of p-rigid field in § 6.1. Pseudo algebraically closed fields (PAC fields) are studied
in [FJ08, Chapter 11].
Theorem D. Let F be a field such that the quotient F × /(F × )p is finite. The
algebra H • (GF , Fp ) is PBW, and hence Koszul, in the following cases:
    (a) F is finite;
    (b) F is a pseudo algebraically closed (PAC) field, or an extension of relative
        trascendence degree 1 of a PAC field;
    (c) F is an algebraic extension of Q;
    (d) F is an extension of trascendence degree 1 of a local field;
    (e) F is p-rigid.
Moreover, if p = 2 the above also holds in the cases:
    (f) F is algebraic extension of a global field of characteristic not 2;
    (g) F is a Pythagorean field.
In cases (a) to (f ), U(L(GF )) is quadratic dual to H • (GF , Fp ) and hence it is
Koszul as well.
  As Positselski proved in [Pos14], the cohomology of local and global fields also has
PBW property. These facts corroborate a strenghtening of Positselski’s Conjecture.
Conjecture 1.6. Let F be a field containing a primitive pth root of unity. Then
the algebra H • (GF , Fp ) is PBW.
   Koszulity of an algebra can be paraphrased as the fact that there are not sur-
prising ways in which an element of the algebra can turn to be 0, or in other words
“only what is expected to be 0 is actually 0”. Our next results go in the same
philosophical direction. When a minimal presentation G = hx1 , . . . , xd | r1 , . . . , rm i
of a finitely generated pro-p group is given (see § 7), commutators and powers
applied to a given relator ri provide automatically some higher degree relators of
grFp [[G]]. Let us say that these are the “predictable” higher degree relations. But
a priori, grFp [[G]] might be subject to other, “unpredictable” relations. Under the
mild assumption that Galois cohomology is quadratic, the predictable relations are
orthogonal to the relations in Galois cohomology:

              2. Quadratic algebras and the Koszul property
   This section provides the basic definitions, notable facts and cohomological back-
ground for the classes of Koszul algebras and PBW algebras. In this section k
denotes an arbitrary field.
Definition 2.1. An associative, unital L k-algebra A is graded if it decomposes as the
direct sum of k-vector spaces A• = i∈Z Ai such that, for all i, j ∈ Z, Ai Aj ⊆ Ai+j .
The summand Ai is the homogeneous component of degree i. A graded k-algebra
A• is connected if Ai = 0 for all i < 0 and A0 = k · 1A ∼  = k; generated in degree 1 if
all its (algebra) generators are in A1 ; locally finite-dimensional if dimk Ai < ∞ for
all i ∈ Z (another common terminology for this is finite-type). A module M over a
graded L algebra A• is graded if it decomposes as the direct sum of k-vector spaces
M• =       i∈Z Mi s.t., for all i, j ∈ Z, Ai Mj ⊆ Mi+j . A graded A• -module M• is
finite-type if dimk Mi < ∞ for all i ∈ Z.

    The injectivity of ρ in degree 3 can be algorithmically checked with the Rewriting
Method (cf. [LV12, § 4.1]).
    First, one chooses a basis B = {r1 , . . . , rm } of Ω. By elementary linear algebra
over a field, one can always suppose that the coefficients of lm(r1 ), . . . , lm(rm ) are
1 and that, for each i, lm(ri ) does not appear as a monomial in any other relator
rj , j 6= i. A basis with these additional properties is said to be normalized.
    Each relation ri = 0 can be interpreted as a rewriting rule
                   lm(ri )    −(sum of non maximal monomials).
Rewriting rules can be subsequently applied to an initial monomial until the result
does not contain any more leading terms. This final shape is usually called a normal
form of the initial monomial. The process can be depicted with a directed graph,
whose vertices are the polynomials obtained as steps of the aforementioned process
and whose edges connect a polynomial to a second polynomial that is obtained
applying a single rewriting rule to the first. Normal forms are then the terminal
vertices of these rewriting graphs.
    A degree 3 monomial xa xb xc is critical if both xa xb and xb xc are leading terms
of relators in B. This implies that there are two possible rewriting processes on
it, starting with either the rewriting rule on xa xb or that on xb xc . The resulting
rewriting graph may then have two distinct terminal vertices, that is, the critical
monomial may have two distinct normal forms. A critical monomial is confluent if
its rewriting graph has only one terminal vertex.
    The (cosets of the) terminal vertices of the rewriting graph of a critical monomial
are equal in gr• A, but distinct in T• (V )/(Ωlead ). Since this is the only obstruction
to the injectivity of ρ in degree 3, this injectivity is equivalent to the confluence of
every critical monomial. A• is called a PBW algebra if there exist bases for V and
Ω and an admissible order as above with respect to which every critical monomial
is confluent. In that case, the above basis of V is called a set of PBW generators
of A• .
    The whole discussion can be then summarised in the fact that a (quadratic)
PBW algebra is Koszul.

             3. Filtrations on groups and associated algebras
   In this section we define a filtration on an arbitrary group G. It provides a
stratification of G into layers in which the group product has a simpler description.
The resulting graded object has a natural structure of a restricted Lie algebra (see
Definition 3.6). This structure is intimately related to the ring-theoretic structure
of the group algebra of G over Fp . Since group algebras are the basic ingredients
from which group cohomology is defined, the restricted Lie algebra of a pro-p group
is expected to play a significant role in Galois cohomology. This will be addressed
in the following sections.
   Even though the contents of this section applies to any group, we are mostly
interested in pro-p or profinite groups. Exercise (6.d) in [Ser13, Ch. I, § 4.2] asks
to prove that the topology of a finitely generated pro-p group is determined by
the group structure: more precisely, any subgroup of finite index is open. A full
proof of this can be found in [DdSMS99, Theorem 1.17]. The much more difficult
statement that the same is true for every finitely generated profinite group was
proved in [NS07a, NS07b]. However, it is not true that every subgroup of a finitely
generated profinite (or pro-p) group is closed. We shall therefore adopt the usual
Convention. When dealing with profinite (or pro-p) groups, every generating set
is to be intended in the topological sense, every subgroup is understood to be closed
and every map is understood to be continuous.

Definition 3.6. A restricted Lie algebra L over Fp is a Lie Fp -algebra equipped
with an additional unary operation [p] : L → L, called p-operation, satisfying
     (i) (αa)[p] = αp a[p] for all α ∈ Fp , a ∈ L;
                              Pp−1
    (ii) (a + b)[p] = a[p] + i=1 i−1 si (a, b) + b[p] for all a, b ∈ L, where si (a, b) is
         the coefficient of T i−1 in ad(T a + b)p−1 (a);
   (iii) ad(a[p] ) = ad(a)p for all a ∈ L.
We recall that the map ad : L → End(L) is defined as ad(a) = ada : b 7→ [a, b].
   A morphism of restricted Lie algebras is a morphism of Lie algebras that pre-
serves the p-operations.
    For any associative Fp -algebra A, the bracket operation [a, b] := ab−ba and the p-
operation a[p] := ap make the Fp -module underlying A into a restricted Lie algebra
AL . This defines a functor from associative algebras to restricted Lie algebras,
which has a left adjoint functor U. For a restricted Lie algebra L, U(L) is called the
universal restricted enveloping algebra of L. Concretely, U(L) is the quotient of the
tensor algebra T (L) by the two-sided ideal generated by the elements ab − ba − [a, b]
and a[p] − ap , a, b ∈ L. It comes equipped with an injective function, the universal
embedding ϑ : L → U(L), that is a monomorphism of restricted Lie algebras from
L to U(L)L (cf. [Jac79, § V.5]). This makes precise the idea that the p-operation
is the Lie analogue of the pth power map in the associative framework.

with the further condition that a[p] ∈ M for every a ∈ M , respectively a[p] ∈ r for

3.4. Cohomology of pro-p groups. The natural cohomology theory to be used
with profinite groups is Galois cohomology. It is defined in complete analogy with
classical group cohomology, except for the requirement that all maps be continu-
ous. Good accounts on Galois cohomology can be found for example in [NSW08],
[Ser13]. We content ourselves to quickly recall some useful facts, with a focus
on finitely generated pro-p groups, although the results are essentially valid in a
broader context.
   If G is a pro-p group, its Frattini subgroup is Φ(G) = Gp [G, G] and the quo-
tient G/G(2) = G/Φ(G) is a compact elementary abelian p-group. Its Pontrya-
gin dual is H 1 (G, Fp ) and the cardinality d(G) of a minimal generating set of G
equals dimFp H 1 (G, Fp ) (cf. [Ser13, Ch. I, § 4.2]). Thus, if G is finitely generated,
G/Φ(G) ∼       = (Z/pZ)d(G) . Moreover, if G is a finitely generated pro-p group, and
{x1 , . . . , xd } is a minimal generating set of G, then the minimal number of defining
relations between the xi is equal to dimFp H 2 (G, FpL     ) (cf. [Ser13, Ch. I,§ 4.3]).
   For a profinite group G, the space H • (G, Fp ) = n≥0 H n (G, Fp ) is a connected
graded Fp -algebra with respect to the cup product
(3.5)        ∪ : H r (G, Fp ) × H s (G, Fp ) −→ H r+s (G, Fp ),      r, s ≥ 0
(cf. [NSW08, Proposition 1.4.4]). The cup product is graded-commutative, that is
             a ∪ b = (−1)rs b ∪ a      for a ∈ H r (G, Fp ), b ∈ H s (G, Fp ).
   This is of special interest when G is a Galois group. If F contains a primitive
pth root of unity, the Kummer map κ : F × → H 1 (GF , Fp ) induces an isomorphism
F × /(F × )p → H 1 (GF , Fp ), thereby giving a concrete description of the first coho-
mology group of GF . The possibility to extend this to higher cohomology groups
was first suggested by J. Milnor for p = 2 (cf. [Mil70]) and then conjectured by
S. Bloch and K. Kato for any prime (cf. [BK86]). In detail, the Milnor K-theory
of F is the quotient K•M (F ) of the tensor algebra (see §1.1) of the multiplicative
group F × by the two-sided ideal generated by the tensors a ⊗ (1 − a), a ∈ F \ {0, 1}.
The tensor powers κ ⊗ · · · ⊗ κ factor through the aforementioned ideal (Stein-
berg relations, cf. [GS17, Proposition 4.6.1] or [Tat76, proof of Theorem 3.1]) and

is that, if F contains a primitive pth root of unity, then the algebra H • (GF , Fp ) is
quadratic. From this and the graded-commutativity of the cup product, it follows
that there is an epimorphism of quadratic Fp -algebras Λ• (H 1 (G, Fp )) → H • (G, Fp ).

                       4. Elementary type pro-p groups
    Few groups are known to be realizable as maximal pro-p quotients of absolute

4.3. Elementary type groups. Any free pro-p group is realizable (this is a conse-

Remark 6.1. By Kummer theory one has an isomorphism of discrete Fp -vector

Proposition 6.2. Let F be a field as in items (a) or (b) of Theorem D. Then
(GF (p), χp ) is a cyclotomic pro-p pair of elementary type.
Proof. In the cases when F is a finite field, or F is PAC field one knows that for
every finite Galois extension K of F the norm map K ∗ → F ∗ is surjective. Since
this condition implies that the Brauer group of each finite Galois extension of F is
trivial, H 2 (GF (p), Fp ) = 0, i.e., GF (p) is a free pro-p group, and hence (GF (p), χp )
is of elementary type.
   For finite fields this is the well-known Chevalley Theorem ([Sha72, Theorem 25]).
If F is PAC then this follows from [FJ08, Corollary 11.6.8]. Note that an algebraic
extension of a PAC field is again PAC (cf. [FJ08, Corollary 11.2.5]).
   Finally, if F is an extension of a PAC field of relative trascendence degree 1, then
the cyclotomic pro-p pair (GF (p), χp ) is of elementary type by [Efr01, Corollary 5.7].
