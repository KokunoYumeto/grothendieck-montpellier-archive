## 2. Finite Satake possibilities really do supply integrality

Let M be the exponent of the finite image of Calegari's spin candidate xi, and retain the exact cyclic base-change identity Pi_E = Sym^3(pi_E). At a good rational prime split in E, the Satake roots of Pi are roots of unity of order dividing M. At an inert good prime, their squares are the roots of the corresponding finite-image parameter over E, so every Satake root has order dividing 2M. Consequently the unramified Hecke polynomials of Pi lie in a fixed finite subset of Z[zeta_(2M)][T]. They are integral and have a finite Hecke field. Every coefficient's algebraic conjugates satisfy the elementary binomial bound. None of these assertions supplies a representation of the absolute Galois group whose Frobenius polynomials are those Hecke polynomials.

Exact inherited source: Calegari, section 4, supplied S5ArtinWeb.tex lines 510–540, especially the cyclic base-change construction and the split/inert Satake relation. The degree-five comparison has an even more precise integral alternative, below.

For the degree-five A, deleting the fixed common bad set gives a retained Hecke-polynomial field Q, integral coefficients, and no nontrivial coefficient-field conjugation. Every coefficient a_j(p) satisfies |a_j(p)| <= binom(5,j), hence the prime mean-square bound in Kim–Yamauchi assumption (5) follows from the Euler logarithm of zeta. This verifies the finite-set versions of their non-Galois hypotheses; it makes no rationality assertion at omitted places. The new checked proposition and full argument are now in s5_residual_realization.tex, MA-RB-010.

## 3. The exact degree-five residual test

Fix the automorphic comparison A and the five-dimensional Artin representation W. Enlarge a fixed finite set S to contain all their bad primes. The already audited Calegari comparison says that, for p outside S, the Hecke polynomial H_(A,p) equals H_(W,p) except on the Dirichlet-density-zero set Omega. At every p in Omega,

H_(A,p)(T) = (1−T)^3(1+T^2),
H_(W,p)(T) = (1−T)(1+T)^2(1+T^2),
H_(A,p)(T)−H_(W,p)(T) = −4T(1−T)(1+T^2).

These are det(1−T·parameter), not the reciprocal local L-factors. Their orientation is the source's actual exceptional eigenvalue lists: {1,i,−i,1,1} versus {1,i,−i,−1,−1}. Exact locator: Calegari's theorem immediately following the similitude calculation, supplied S5ArtinWeb.tex lines 736–765; source-PDF p.12 (PDF pagination, not journal pagination). This is direct multiplication of already verified local factors, not a new finite-representation certificate.

Here is the full residual scope argument. Let ell be an odd prime, and suppose a continuous five-dimensional residual representation r of G_Q exists, unramified away from a finite set, with characteristic polynomial H_(A,p) modulo a place above ell at every prime outside that finite set. A semisimple representation, as required by the cited theorem, is sufficient; semisimplicity is not needed for the characteristic-polynomial conclusion below. Reduce a stable integral lattice for W at the same residual characteristic, and, if desired, take its semisimplification; characteristic polynomials are unchanged. Such a stable lattice exists because the image of W is finite: sum the translates of an initial lattice and localize at the chosen prime.

The two residual representations are continuous into a discrete algebraic closure of a finite field, hence have finite image. Their product factors through one finite Galois extension. The set of elements of this finite Galois group for which their characteristic polynomials differ is a union of conjugacy classes. If it were nonempty, Chebotarev would give the corresponding rational primes a strictly positive Dirichlet density. Outside finitely many primes, however, this set is contained in Omega, because both representations there have the same characteristic polynomial as W. Since Omega has Dirichlet density zero, the difference set is empty. Thus H_(A,p) and H_(W,p) are congruent at every common good prime. The displayed difference polynomial is nonzero in every odd characteristic, so none of these common good primes belongs to Omega. Therefore Omega is finite.

Conversely, if the good-prime comparison is exact outside a finite set, the reductions of W give such compatible residual representations in every characteristic, outside an enlarged finite set. In characteristic 2 the displayed difference vanishes, so the known reduction of W already matches every good Hecke polynomial even before comparison is known. Thus a mod-2 congruence contains no information about the exceptional branch, whereas existence in any one odd characteristic is equivalent to good-prime comparison outside a finite set.

The existing finite-exception closure in the S5 analysis then relates this to exact comparison. No claim is made that odd-characteristic compatibility is equivalent to the stronger nonvanishing assertion R_B itself: no implication from exact comparison to absence of zeros of the quadratic Hecke product is established here. Nor has any theorem above supplied the odd-characteristic representation. This is a logically equivalent reformulation of the comparison obstruction, not a smaller arithmetic gate and not additional evidence of a shared real zero.
