# Geometric dictionary

This dictionary states the proved bridges between computational presentations and algebraic geometry. Archive identifiers occur only when the attached materials establish that antecedent. It does not infer motivic or K-theoretic consequences merely from a point-count congruence.

## The coherent line and universal stabilization

The main fixed-prime reduction turns a Boolean coefficient into the action of absolute Frobenius on the canonical coherent line of a smooth anticanonical hypersurface. The incidence part extends the binary mechanism NN-C023; the deterministic universal smoothing is separately proved and is not attributed to the generic-pencil argument NN-C024. Katz supplies the Laurent-residue coefficient formula, Algorithm(2.3.7.14) and Special Case(2.3.7.17), pp35–36. The geometric bridge is the hypersurface exact sequence and its Frobenius-compatible connecting map. The point-count congruence is independently obtained by summing a finite-field indicator.

The stabilization is an affine-linear map of entire coefficient spaces. Smoothness of every geometric fibre, relative flatness and the residue trivialization give a line bundle with a Frobenius-semilinear operator whose coefficient pulls back to the original polynomial coefficient. This is a family over Fp; no single smooth integral model over Spec Z is inferred from the separate fixed-prime constructions. Frobenius splitting is equivalent to nonvanishing of this scalar on the stated smooth anticanonical family, by duality of the Frobenius trace.

The shifted-count and Chinese-remainder reductions concern access to this geometric scalar through yes/no oracles. Parsimonious gate variables preserve the zero-dimensional Boolean counting scheme, including the explicitly counted additional branch. These are computational reductions, not morphisms asserted between the resulting smooth projective varieties.

## The higher-codimension annihilator line

For J contained in the input ideal K, with J a regular sequence of total degree N+1+e and K saturated to an anticanonical complete-intersection ideal I_X, Wiebe duality gives (J:K)/J isomorphic to (S/I_X)(-e). Its degree-e part is therefore one-dimensional. The Koszul--Cartier adjoint on S_e/J_e acts on that line by the desired scalar. This is a calculation with dualizing modules and closed-immersion functoriality; it does not require X to be a reduced component of a smooth intermediate scheme.

Fixed degree gaps make the multiplication relations directly computable by bounded-degree linear algebra. Bounded maximum-degree excess permits deterministic regular combinations over an explicit finite extension. A stronger randomized theorem needs only a bounded-excess regular sequence inside the actual input ideal after scalar extension. A finite free projection, followed by finite-algebra evaluations retaining nilpotents, recovers the kernel without expanding any of the other binary-degree inputs. A graded free residual quotient makes a fixed total-degree-e Newton grid recover the kernel exactly. The regular tuples form a deterministic candidate list, so only finding a certified good projection centre remains random. For an extension-defined regular tuple, Cartier is inverse-Frobenius semilinear; its norm is alpha^h, and a degree h coprime to p-1 permits recovery by an inverse exponent. These are the new bounded-excess findings; no earlier archive cote is assigned without evidence.

## Coarse invariants and total cohomology

A parameter line of smooth hypersurfaces has varying coherent Frobenius scalar but constant polarized Hilbert polynomial, Chern numbers, coherent dimensions and geometric Betti numbers. The proof is the explicit stabilization together with the hypersurface and tangent sequences. Those fixed numerical invariants therefore lose information needed for the scalar. This does not assert equality of the full motives or of the Frobenius representations.

The total-Betti information bound uses integral Weil polynomials and nonzero ample powers. It limits exact zeta-only encoding over a fixed field; it does not turn a finite set of possible zeta functions into an efficient classification of succinct equations. The distinction between a rank-one coherent group and the much larger total etale cohomology is explicit in the hypersurface Betti formula.


## Dictionary for the presentation findings

These records use mathematical labels only for cross-reference. No archive connection is asserted beyond the sources actually read.

### Trinomial equations for the same scheme

In algebraic geometry, adding gate-value coordinates is a change of projective embedding by a high tensor power of the hyperplane bundle. The affine chart rings and their gluing identify the new scheme with the original hypersurface, rather than merely giving a bijection on rational points. Therefore all functorial coherent cohomology and Frobenius actions are unchanged. In the programme concerning Grothendieck's schemes, the relevant distinction is between a presentation by equations and the scheme it represents. This construction makes that distinction algorithmically visible. Its source is coherent_scalar.tex, Theorem4; no additional archive cote has been inferred.

### Picard rigidity and secant centers

SGA2, XII3.7 identifies the Picard group of a projective complete intersection of dimension at least3 with the integer powers of its hyperplane bundle. It forces any new embedding of the smooth hypersurface to use aH. The a=1 case changes linear coordinates; a≥2 raises the least ambient dimension to2n+1 by the secant calculation. This is a direct use of Grothendieck's local-to-global Lefschetz and parafactoriality results, specifically XII3.6–3.7 and XI3.12–3.13. The proof of the secant bound is multiplication of sections and finite intersections with original lines, valid in every characteristic. No assertion about motives or K-theory is needed for it.

### A residue from sparse ideal generators

The anticanonical Koszul resolution identifies top structure-sheaf cohomology with the reciprocal-coordinate residue line. Changing a regular sequence changes its generator by a determinant. The truncated-polynomial identity is the concrete determinant transformation on the Frobenius coefficient. Over a prime field its nonzero determinant factor has (p−1)st power1, allowing a sparse generating list to yield the intrinsic scalar. Scheme saturation is not a harmless input operation: the same sheaf of ideals can be presented by higher multiples that do not contain a minimal ideal generator. The plane cubic makes this distinction explicit.

### Recovering bounded-degree cofactors

For a principal saturated ideal, a long list f_i=g h_i with no common projective cofactor zero determines the cofactor vector up to one scalar. Low-degree syzygies recover that vector without expanding g. Frobenius makes a unit cofactor especially simple in the Artinian quotient by pth powers: its pth power is its constant. Thus a coherent Frobenius coefficient can be recovered from a sheaf-theoretic presentation under a quantitative degree condition. This is a concrete calculation in a Frobenius-truncated local algebra, not a general effective saturation theorem or a factorization algorithm. The unbounded-degree problem remains.

### Monomial support and additive syndromes

The general support theorem uses the quotient of a linear solution space seen by the coordinates of a monomial. A positive-dimensional fibre contributes a factorp and disappears modulo p. Under the anticanonical degree equality, a nonzero top coefficient uses at leastm ambient coordinate forms but can use at mostm+K. Choosing a basis leaves at mostK extra linear forms; their joint value is an additive syndrome. Convolution on that small finite vector space computes the residue. The invariant being evaluated is still one Frobenius scalar, but the cost is governed by its presentation. No new cohomological rank or weight theorem is claimed. Group-algebra dynamic programming has an explicit precedent in Kaski, Koivisto and Nederlof (2012).

### Effective projection

The Chow equation of the secant variety gives a concrete finite-field obstruction to choosing a projection center. Its multidegree yields a sufficient extension degree. Scheme-theoretic image formation then uses saturation and elimination, with an explicit but very large output bound. This separates the existence of a projective embedding from an efficient list of defining equations. CRW's radical compression preserves points and zeta functions but need not preserve a scheme's nilpotent structure, so it is a different functorial operation.

### Separate cubic check

The scoped independent cubic check in independent_cubic.md does connect to schemes over Z, motives and lambda rings: a split finite center produces a wedge of motivic spheres, conditional on the cited ADO cofiber theorem. It gives K0=Z⊕Z^S with square-zero ideal and Adams weightv. This was checked for the other contribution; it is not an inference from any sparse-projective presentation theorem above.

### Why one small cofactor is enough

Saturation becomes equality of the dehomogenized ideal with a principal ideal. Thus the hidden cofactors generate the unit ideal, an identity preserved by restriction to every affine line. The univariate gcd of the restricted equations recovers the restriction of the principal generator without unlucky projection factors. Computing all other rows modulo one small-degree input makes their binary degrees harmless. Low-degree or sparse interpolation then recovers a single cofactor, and the Frobenius-truncated residue reads the scalar. This is stronger than using only the abstract gcd of the multivariate inputs. It is a direct computational consequence of the sheaf-theoretic saturation promise.

### Removing Frobenius multiplicities visible in sparse digits

The base-p digit coefficient matrix detects a factorization f=UV^(p^e) when it has rank one. Passing toUV reduces multiplicities while preserving membership in a radical target ideal. The old ideal remains contained in the new one, so the same saturated ideal results. This uses the reduced scheme structure and Frobenius; it does not assert that an individual nonreduced hypersurface is unchanged. A quantitative degree-decrease argument makes the preprocessing polynomial in binary exponent size.

### Selecting an unknown good cofactor

The line-gcd oracle evaluates every cofactor of polynomial numerical degree, whether or not it is sparse. Thus a sparse-interpolation candidate can be compared against a polynomial function of known bounded degree. Random evaluations certify that equality with controllably small error, while the genuinely sparse row always reconstructs correctly. Trying every eligible row converts an existential good-cofactor promise into a Monte Carlo scalar algorithm. This uses elementary polynomial identity testing after the geometric saturation argument has supplied an exact oracle; it is not deterministic recognition of hidden sparsity.

### Random projections and smoothness in coordinate lower bounds

The nonzero Chow obstruction has bounded total degree, so random matrix entries over a sufficiently large extension avoid it with an explicit probability. This produces a linear map with a Monte Carlo closed-immersion guarantee without computing image equations or certifying success. A separate coordinate-sparsity lower-bound mechanism from Bhargava–Sengupta uses large derivative spaces of shifted initial forms. Smooth homogeneous equations have only constant or linear initial forms away from the cone vertex, so that mechanism alone supplies no growing lower bound for this class. The characteristic-zero hypotheses of their PIT and explicit lower-bound theorems remain separate restrictions.

### Saturated complete intersections through finite algebras

A bounded-excess regular sequence J inside the actual input ideal K defines an intermediate complete intersection, which may be singular or nonreduced. Its graded annihilator line (J:K)_e/J_e is the dualizing-sheaf line carrying the target scalar. A finite linear projection makes the coordinate ring a finite free module over a parameter polynomial ring. Specializing the parameters gives small finite algebras retaining multiplicities. Evaluating the long sparse list there imposes linear conditions on the unknown degree-e annihilator. Wiebe identities and a special-fibre basis extension show that the residual quotient is graded free over the same parameter ring. Thus every affine fibre is valid and a total-degree-e Newton grid recovers the annihilator exactly, independently of the large input degrees.

The regular sequence itself can be found without expanding full graded pieces: pure-coordinate-power multiples of the low-degree input equations have the same projective base loci as those pieces. Prime-degree field towers choose sparse combinations deterministically for every degree profile; at least one candidate is regular under the existential promise. A fixed-variable Macaulay test certifies its centre and regularity. When this construction uses an extension field, Cartier is inverse-Frobenius semilinear; its entry is basis-dependent, but its norm is the iterate scalar. Choosing the total extension degree coprime to p−1 recovers the prime-field value by an inverse exponent. The remaining quantitative condition is bounded complete-intersection excess in K, not just in its saturation.


## Geometric dictionary for the structured-count findings

This supplement describes the newly checked algorithmic statements in geometric language. It assigns no archival cote: the supplied sources do not establish a specific archival connection for these new algorithmic refinements.

### A partition as effective geometric data

The grouped-gate theorem supplies a disjoint union of affine binary subspaces whose rational points are exactly the legal wire assignments. Its exact integer cardinality is the sum of powers of two determined by their dimensions. The Boolean encoding theorem carries that cardinality, reduced modulo a fixed prime, to Katz's coefficient on the top coherent line of a smooth projective hypersurface. The partition is data in the *presentation*: an isomorphism class of smooth varieties does not specify its affine wire domain or its groups of defects. Recovering the partition from an unmarked equation is therefore a separate effective problem. The paired chain proves that a unique legal primary assignment cannot replace the low-degree selector hypothesis.

### Split finite etale schemes and invisible traces

The equations `x_i^2=x_i`, `Z_j^2=1` over an odd prime field define a product of rational points. An additional indicator equation selects a union of factors of this finite reduced algebra. Its projection to the base is finite etale; the fibre's coherent `H^0` has dimension equal to its population. Frobenius on a split rational fibre is the identity, so its trace sees only that population modulo the characteristic. Multiplying a fibre population by three annihilates every positive coherent Frobenius trace in characteristic three without changing which fibres are empty. Thus the failure of traces to detect emptiness is caused by variable multiplicity on a split zero-dimensional scheme, not by nilpotents or nonsemisimplicity of the l-adic Frobenius representation.

For a split fibre of size `H`, the l-adic representation is `H` copies of the trivial representation and its zeta function is `(1-T)^(-H)`. Modulo three, the reciprocal polynomial first sees the nonzero integer `H` in degree `3^(v_3(H))`. The constructed private multiplier realizes prescribed valuations using a short Walsh expression, and therefore postpones this first visible degree at logarithmic description cost. This is an effective multiplicity construction. The argument does not identify an arbitrary finite scheme from its zeta function and does not keep the coherent rank fixed.

### Row powers in the finite coordinate algebra

On the displayed split finite scheme, mixed primary monomials and private characters form a basis of functions. The joint solution indicator `B=product_l(1-P_l^(p-1))` decomposes into private-character rows. A base fibre is empty if and only if all rows vanish there. Replacing each row by its `(p-1)`st power makes it a zero-one function with the same zero set. If their span has rank `R`, choose `R` original rows as a basis; `product_i(1-U_i)` is exactly the empty-fibre indicator. This proves the algorithm inside the coordinate algebra of the split scheme and explains why bounded rank is useful.

The rank is attached to the character basis. The explicit eight-point permutation changes rank one to rank four while preserving the finite map up to isomorphism. Thus no statement about an unmarked motive, Grothendieck class, or abstract finite scheme supplies this rank automatically. Binary affine exponent changes preserve it because they only permute rows and multiply them by characters and nonzero scalars; `(p-1)`st powers remove those units.

### Recovering a marked affine presentation

The designated span of quadrics `(A_i y+B_i x+c_i)^2-1` determines a diagonal algebra in the private quadratic matrices. The deterministic two-pencil argument recovers its one-dimensional eigenspaces, descends them to the original finite field, and then uses the mixed and linear terms to recover the full affine coordinates. Coefficientwise verification of the entire quadratic span gives an explicit certificate. The proof is an effective reconstruction within a specified presentation class; it neither assumes nor constructs an arbitrary scheme isomorphism.

The inverse affine substitution may expand monomials. Fixed monomial support controls this cost: values in the prime field permit reduction of each positive exponent to at most `p-1`, after which expansion costs at most `(n+t+1)^((p-1)sigma)` per term. This identity lives in the finite split coordinate algebra. It does not authorize replacing an ambient ideal or a projective saturation by the reduced exponent list.

### Arrangement complements, Tate roots, and codes

An affine arrangement complement has an inclusion–exclusion count polynomial `C(s)=sum_j c_j s^j`, valid over every finite extension of its field of definition. Its zeta function is `product_j(1-q^j T)^(-c_j)`. The possible weights and reciprocal roots are therefore fixed in advance, whereas their signed multiplicities contain the hard ground-field count. The coefficients are not all positive, so the expression should not be mistaken for an actual direct-sum decomposition of a motive with those coefficients as nonnegative ranks. It is an alternating cohomological or virtual expression.

Homogenization and the additional coordinate `t` identify the complement with the full-support part of a linear image after division by the scalar `t`. The central matroid polynomial is `(s-1)C(s)`. In particular its constant term is the negative of the affine arrangement constant term, and reducing modulo `q` extracts the negative satisfying-assignment residue. The number of linear constraints and the support of the one inverse-product binomial both grow. The construction consequently does not establish hardness for a bounded number of sparse equations with bounded monomial support.

### Finite étale schemes and equal Frobenius orbits

The fixed-field construction replaces each Boolean bit by a balanced pair of quadratic-period fibers among roots of unity. The relation equations and a tag cut out a reduced finite subscheme of the multiplicative group. Every accepted geometric point has the same orbit length L under arithmetic Frobenius. Thus its coordinate algebra is a product of K*S/L copies of F_(p^L), where S is the witness count. Over characteristic-zero cohomological coefficients, its representation is a direct sum of regular representations of the cyclic group of order L. The zeta function is `(1-T^L)^(-K*S/L)`. This is an actual finite scheme with an actual permutation representation, including the empty case.

The general-prime normalization chooses primes a congruent to -1 modulo 4p and uses the integral quadratic-period product `(a+1)/4` before reduction. Every factor `(a-1)/2` in K is a unit modulo p. This is what permits residue decoding. Prime search is polynomial for each fixed p; this construction alone does not give a uniform polynomial algorithm in log p. The source is the complete attached `research/Fixed_fields_and_Frobenius/fixed_field_multiplicities.tex`; the paper gives the period calculation, bit bounds and both normalizations explicitly.

The sign-only comparison concerns the pair `U=G_m×Z`, `Y={1}×Z`. Restriction in degree zero is an isomorphism and the Gysin kernel is zero. The remaining trace is exactly the permutation trace of Z. Consequently a sharp purity or sign bound controls the possible size and sign of a term whose multiplicity remains a counting problem. The cited antecedent is Proposition 4 of the attached sign-only preprint, not a claim that that preprint asserted an efficient counting algorithm.

### Ordinary coherent trace and its iterate

On each finite residue field a normal basis makes p-Frobenius a cyclic shift. If L>1 its trace is zero; its L-th iterate is the identity and has trace L. Therefore the unit-normalized finite scheme has ordinary coherent trace zero and iterated trace K*S. The trace distinction is essential. A separate split Boolean projective scheme has H0 of dimension S and ordinary trace S, but it gives no bounded-dimensional coherent realization. These statements are direct algebraic calculations in the attached fixed-field construction, rather than consequences of the characteristic-zero eigenvalue bound.

### Artin–Schreier covers and the location of the hard count

Reducing the numerator A modulo B=X^N-1 removes a possible pole at infinity. The rational function A/B has exactly N-K*S simple poles, one of which is the point 1. A simple pole precludes an Artin–Schreier coboundary after algebraic closure. The resulting smooth projective connected curve has genus and p-rank both `(p-1)(N-1-K*S)`. The geometric completion is determined by the function field. A polynomial-size ordinary-projective equation list for that completion is not asserted.

The distinct varying-binary-field construction from NN-C026 uses trace-dual coordinates and an odd-degree polynomial. It counts exactly `1+2S` rational points, while the complete based coherent Frobenius map depends only on field size and degree. Over F4, two formulas with two declared bits and one clause have S=2 and S=3 but the same genus 12 and the same coherent operator. The explicit pair and exhaustive point enumeration were produced in this run. This is a failure of recovery from specified invariants, not a claim that the curves are isomorphic or have the same integer zeta function.

### An integral cubic and actual Tate multiplicities

The Boolean verifier equations define S disjoint sections of affine space over Spec Z. Their smooth cubic suspension

`t(t-1)=Σ y_i f_i(x)`

has an affine-space fiber off those sections and two affine-space fibers over each section. Its Grothendieck class and point counts follow by stratification. Its actual compactly supported cohomology follows from a different argument: relative trace and the involution `t↦1-t` split the sole nonzero higher direct image into a constant summand and a summand supported on those sections. Thus the multiplicity S occurs in actual pure Tate cohomology, rather than merely in an alternating class.

This is the geometric construction NN-C027. The current proof checks the sheaf-theoretic decomposition itself. NN-C028 compares two such multiplicities through existence of a Frobenius-equivariant linear injection. The problem is PP-complete on the verifier-bearing encoded family; no assertion identifies that injection with a morphism or an algebraic correspondence.

### Motives, Chow groups and the lambda-ring

The Boolean sections have an integral normal framing. The section of the cubic chooses one of the two branches over each Boolean section. The Asok–Dubouloz–Østvær cofiber theorem therefore identifies the pointed cubic with a wedge of S motivic v-spheres. This is an unstable integral statement over Z, preserved by base change. Over a perfect field its motive is `Z ⊕ Z(v)[2v]^S`, and its higher Chow groups split accordingly. Ordinary Chow groups over arbitrary fields are independently generated by the branch cycles, with their sole positive codimension group in degree v.

These are the actual antecedents NN-S016 and NN-S017. Their link to Grothendieck's programme is the explicitly constructed integral scheme, normal framing, motivic cofiber and realization functors. The paper does not use analogy with motives as a substitute for the cofiber argument.

For NN-S018, the same spheres identify `K0=Z⊕I`, with `I≅Z^S` and `I²=0`. A Bott generator has Adams eigenvalue m^v. Newton identities give the entire lambda structure, and substitution `t/(1-t)` gives the Stirling gamma coefficients. The gamma filtration is a gcd multiple of I. In particular, torsion in a gamma-graded quotient is compatible with torsion-free K0; for odd v the penultimate quotient contains S copies of Z/2. The original gamma calculation was replayed from its generating series for v=1,…,9, not simply copied as a table.

The empty-case affine isomorphism is given by integral polynomial inverse maps from an ordinary Bézout identity. NN-C029 also asserts E-dependent circuit-construction bounds. Those running-time bounds and their E dependencies are outside this contribution's theorem set. The ordinary algebraic identity, constant-scope correction and inverse coordinate formulas are checked here; a polynomial monomial-size certificate is not claimed.

### Total Betti number and information in a zeta function

The current-run finite-range argument combines nonvanishing ample powers with Deligne's integral Weil polynomials. With total Betti bound B over Fq, at most

`(B+1)^(2B-1) · 3^B · 2^(B²) · q^((B³-B)/2)`

zeta functions occur. A comparator on n input bits realizes every count from 0 through 2^n using O(n) gates. Thus exact decoding from the zeta function alone forces `B>(n/(6 log2 q))^(1/3)`. If the variety dimension and q are fixed, the same coefficient count forces `B=Ω(sqrt n)`. These explicit bounds are current-run deductions, not assigned an earlier archive cote.

The degree-d hypersurface in P^(d-1) has primitive middle Betti number `((d-1)^d+(-1)^d(d-1))/d`. Thus the single coherent line in the leading construction coexists with exponentially large total étale cohomology. Neither this formula nor the finite-range argument settles the smallest total Betti bound for modular-count hardness: classifying succinct equations among finitely many possible zeta functions can remain computationally difficult.

## Bounded-degree private multiplier

The triangular-transfer construction realizes every prescribed positive 3-adic population valuation a with a ternary Walsh polynomial on 4(a+1) bits, at most 32a+16 terms and masks of weight at most five. A chain of explicit five-variable phase gadgets has transfer matrices conjugate to triangular matrices with diagonal 1,-3; its off-diagonal entry encodes a balanced base-minus-three integer. The chosen residue gives the exact valuation, and ordinary finite-field Fourier inversion gives all masks in polynomial bit time.

Geometrically, multiplying a split finite family by this private indicator preserves precisely the empty fibres and scales every nonempty fibre population by the same integer. In its reciprocal zeta polynomial, Frobenius raises the first nonzero positive coefficient degree by the corresponding power of three. The private polynomial degree is bounded independently of the erased segment. The rank of H^0 still grows; no fixed-rank cohomological realization follows. The connection is to the existing split-fibre construction, not to an asserted new archive cote or an inferred motive isomorphism.


## Certified coordinate division on the projective scheme

A coordinate can be removed from a displayed monomial content only when it does not vanish identically on the integral projective scheme. The certificate is a nonzero coordinate residue in a finite quotient of S/(K,t_0-1), where K is the original ideal and t_0 is a linear form. If the coordinate belonged to K^sat, a power of the irrelevant ideal would carry it into K; multiplication by the corresponding power of t_0 would then make its residue zero. Thus a nonzero residue certifies nonmembership even on a speculative branch. The affine chart condition t_0=1 is essential. Speculative coordinate divisions are never themselves treated as certificates. On the branch with the true coordinate-hyperplane set, the residual quotient is free over its parameter ring, and a degree-one Newton grid detects every nonzero coordinate. Thus the sheaf-theoretic distinction between a zero section and a section that is nonzero somewhere can be decided in the promised finite-algebra setting.

The ideal after certified content removal and the other safe replacements contains the original ideal and is contained in its saturation. This sandwich proves equality after saturation. Bounded CI excess is then a condition on the processed ideal. The Fermat cover examples show that this can be a strictly weaker hypothesis than bounded excess before coordinate division. The construction uses the scheme and its graded dualizing module; it does not follow from a virtual class or a weight bound. No archive cote is assigned to this current deduction.

## Geometric constants and hidden linearized relations

Geometric integrality makes the prime field algebraically closed inside the fraction field of the homogeneous coordinate ring. Indeed, an algebraic constant extension of degree greater than one would acquire zero divisors after tensoring with an algebraic closure, whereas the geometrically integral ring and its localization remain domains. For an exact homogeneous relation F=V^Q L(U/V), with L additive over Fp and L(1) nonzero, this fact forces U into the saturated prime ideal whenever F belongs to it. If V vanishes, the top Frobenius power gives the same conclusion. Replacing F by U consequently preserves the projective scheme after saturation.

The computational content is recognition from the displayed sparse equation, where L, U and the monomial V are all unknown. The gap (d+1)p^s<=Q isolates the pure Q section. A support candidate or a bounded remainder carry determines V; a sparse linear system determines the additive coefficients. This uses polynomial time in binary exponent length, with no fixed bound on the number of Frobenius blocks. The earlier two- and three-block relations are special cases. Giesbrecht's classical additive-polynomial decomposition already has polynomial cost in Frobenius degree at fixed characteristic; the hidden multivariate argument is the additional input issue here.

The growing-dimensional Fermat family has unchanged unbounded least CI excess under coordinate-content and rank-one digit processing, but the recognized relation recovers its anticanonical equation and reduces that excess to zero. This is a strict comparison of presentation algorithms. It neither proves hardness of the family nor gives an isomorphism of motives from a congruence. The scheme-preserving ideal sandwich ensures that the usual functorial coherent, etale and motivic invariants of the resulting projective scheme are unchanged; no extra lambda-ring or K-theoretic identity is inferred. No earlier archive cote is asserted for this recognizer.

### From the coherent line to the first formal height

For a smooth geometrically integral degree-d hypersurface in projective (d-1)-space, with d>=3 and n=d-2, the top coherent cohomology is a line and the top Artin–Mazur functor is a one-dimensional formal group. The curve endpoint uses the formally smooth degree-zero principal-unit functor or the formal Picard variety; it does not satisfy a false vanishing of H^0(O_X). Multiplication by p on a principal-unit cocycle identifies the degree-p coefficient of the formal p-series with the coherent Frobenius scalar. Over the prime field this scalar is the target coefficient H_p(F), which equals the encoded assignment count modulo p for the main construction. Thus nonzero scalar is height one, and zero scalar is height at least two, including infinity. The point-count congruence has its separate parity sign. ### The numerator and the literal logarithm coefficient

For the chosen homogeneous integral lift, Stienstra's logarithm is

\[
L(T)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{b_m}{m}T^m,\qquad
b_m=[(x_1\cdots x_d)^{m-1}]\widetilde F^{m-1}.
\]

The integer numerator b_p reduces to the coherent scalar; the literal logarithm coefficient is b_p/p. Write B_j=b_{p^j}. Then height at least r means B_j is divisible by p^j for every j<r. Under those earlier conditions, B_r is automatically divisible by p^{r-1}, and exact height r means its quotient by that power is nonzero modulo p. In particular, on the first zero locus the next residue is b_{p^2}/p modulo p, requiring the numerator modulo p^2. There is no reduction modulo p of a nonintegral logarithm coefficient. The unrestricted second Hazewinkel parameter includes the correction (b_{p^2}-b_p^{p+1})/p. These are conditional height tests, independent of the chosen homogeneous lift in their stated scope; numerical tangent scalars still have the usual rescaling weight. See `fg:lem:height-log`, `fg:cor:second`, and the Stienstra/Hazewinkel source audit.

### Integral invariant derivatives and the exact base ring

For a one-dimensional commutative lift over an unramified Witt ring, with normalized invariant derivation D and reduction height at least h, the proved coefficientwise operator bounds include

\[
D^aR[[T]]\subseteq p^{E_h(a)}R[[T]],\qquad
E_h(a)=\sum_{\substack{j\ge1\\h\nmid j}}\left\lfloor\frac a{p^j}\right\rfloor.
\]

For a group of exact finite height h, the optimal affine slope is c_h=1/(p-1)-1/(p^h-1), with error h-1; the lower bounds hold under height at least h. The proof supplies separate witnesses for slope and error. At infinite height the integral logarithm and exponential give the factorial ideal p^{v_p(a!)}, with no finite affine error at the limiting slope. Under the earlier height conditions, the next height is equivalent to the appropriate normalized derivative vanishing, and to the whole-operator factorial containment; a constant-term calculation alone is not the operator proof.

The generic DP1/DP3 deductions use an actual unramified integral lift and verified divisibility by p. High height at a closed deformation point does not imply the same p-divisibility throughout its parameter ring. Reduction modulo a ramified uniformizer is also insufficient. DP34 concerns specified directions in an identified integral Honda-dual model; height or rational slope alone supplies neither that model identification nor the direction data.
