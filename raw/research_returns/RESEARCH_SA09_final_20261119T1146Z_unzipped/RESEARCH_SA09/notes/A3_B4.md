### A.3: the precise action and why it suffices

Let T consist of simplicial abelian groups whose normalized complexes are bounded finite free. The cochain test functor is F_X(M) = Hom(Z[X], M). The required action consists of natural linear maps `rho_(p,M): Gamma^p F_X(M) -> F_X(Gamma^p M)` for all tests M and all p ≥ 0. These exist for the actual functor: at x in X_m use Gamma^p of evaluation at x. The evaluation maps assemble simplicially, without commuting scalar extension with infinite products.

The degree-(1,1) summand of Gamma²(F_X(M) ⊕ F_X(N)), followed by rho_(2,M⊕N) and projection to F_X(M tensor N), recovers the complete tensor pairing. The degree-zero map recovers the unit. Thus the tensor structure is obtained from the natural Gamma action itself; it is not an extra hypothesis appended to L2.

The tests R_n have `(R_n)_m = Z^{Hom_Delta([n],[m])}`. Their normalization has ranks binomial(n+1,i), is zero above n+1 and is split acyclic. The explicit extra degeneracy contracts the unnormalized complex; the natural chain inclusion/projection gives a normalized contraction. Yoneda identifies R_* with the Dold–Kan denormalization of the manuscript's walking complex of disks D_i. Hence F_X(R_n) is exactly Z^{X_n}, naturally in the cosimplicial index. The recovered tensor structure and the multiplication R_n tensor R_n → R_n reconstruct the full cosimplicial function ring A_X.

Conversely A_X reconstructs F_X by a fully typed equalizer of finite-free coefficient modules, including its tensor and Gamma maps. Its underlying normalized complex is C*(X,Z); conversely the underlying complex reconstructs the additive functor through Dold–Kan, or through A2.2's relatively left-exact universal property. This identifies the specified data **on the image of X → F_X**; it is not an assertion about arbitrary additive functors.

Horel's Theorem 5.4 then recovers every connected nilpotent finite-type space from its cosimplicial binomial function ring. Derived adjunction gives full mapping-space recovery. A3.3 also proves object detection for finitely many nilpotent components by the primitive idempotents of H⁰ and their canonical cosimplicial lifts. Mandell's integral E-infinity theorem detects the same object types but is faithful rather than full; his S²/S³ example witnesses the extra maps. Toën's simply connected affinization has a natural retraction onto X, but its integral derived points have an additional product of the p-completions of pi_(i+1) in pi_i. The comparison is inside A3.3 and its proof, with every theorem number and index range stated.

The weak counterexample is L(5,1), L(5,2). Both are nilpotent finite CW spaces, have quasi-isomorphic integral cochain complexes and identical graded cohomology rings and degree-compatible positive divided powers, yet their linking forms cannot agree up to a generator and orientation change. All eight signed-unit-square possibilities mod 5 are checked. This is **not** a counterexample to a coherent chain-level cup product or the full rho action.

Binomial numerical operations are distinct from strict polynomial laws: a → binomial(a,2) fails naturality under reduction mod 2, and no PD ideal in Z contains 1. The Gamma maps here change the test object; they are not internal PD operations on the unital cochain algebra or Horel's binomial operations.

### B.4: exact images and hypotheses

Put d_N = 2 if 8 divides N, and d_N = 1 otherwise. With compatible positive roots of 2, the full adelic image is `H_2 = {(a,b): (-1)^b = chi_2(a)}`, where chi_2(a) is +1 for a = 1,7 mod 8 and -1 for a = 3,5 mod 8. Its adelic index is 2. Its level-N order is N phi(N)/d_N; if 8 does not divide N the finite image is full. K(1/2) replaces b by -b and has the same indices.

The proof excludes every other obstruction: a positive radical of 2 lying in a cyclotomic field generates a real abelian field, whereas Eisenstein irreducibility and nonreal conjugates force its degree to be at most 2. A cyclotomic intersection calculation decides exactly when sqrt(2) lies in Q(zeta_N).

For K(-1/2), take `r_N = zeta_(2N) theta_N^(-1)`. Its translation is `c = (a-1)/2 - b` and its image is `(-1)^c = chi_(-2)(a)`, with the same d_N indices. At a finite even level this requires a cyclotomic lift modulo 2N; it must not be computed from a mod N alone.

Each Fermat fibre E_N(alpha'(i)) is K_N(-1/2)². Equal origins give `(a,(c,c))`; the manuscript's opposite phases give `(a,(c-a+1,c))`. The order remains N phi(N)/d_N, so its scalar affine index is N d_N and its full GL₂ affine index is `d_N N^4 product_(ell|N)(1-ell^(-2))`. Both adelic ambient indices are infinite. The scalar index at level 8 is 16, not 2. The equal radicands do not satisfy rank-two independence.

For independent elements of a number field K, the image is open in `Zhat^r semidirect Zhat^*` with common scalar cyclotomic action. Let e annihilate their saturation quotient inside an S-unit lattice modulo torsion, and set D = e |mu(K)|. The translation kernel over K(mu_infinity) contains D Zhat^r. The proof derives this from Schinzel's opened Theorem 2 and the S-unit theorem using the finite Kummer character pairing and annihilator duality. An independent derivation uses Perucca–Sgobba's opened Theorem 1: its uniform degree bound yields uniformly bounded finite affine indices and hence finite profinite index. For r ≥ 2, the image is not open with the full GL_r linear part.
