## P02. Exact ordinary/supersingular dichotomy on the E^4 isogeny orbit

Let E have j=0, and let A over a number field be geometrically isogenous to E^4. At common good places of residue characteristic p>3,

- p=1 modulo 3: A is ordinary;
- p=2 modulo 3: A is supersingular (all slopes 1/2).

**Proof.** One may take E:y^2=x^3+1. The Hasse-invariant criterion says that E modulo p is supersingular exactly when the coefficient of x^(p-1) in (x^3+1)^((p-1)/2) is zero modulo p. If p=2 modulo 3 there is no such term. If p=1 modulo 3 its coefficient is binomial((p-1)/2,(p-1)/3), which is nonzero modulo p since all factorial arguments are less than p. For an elliptic curve these alternatives are supersingular and ordinary. The slopes of a power are the repeated slopes of E. Newton polygons are unchanged by extension of residue field and by isogeny; after a finite extension realizing the isogeny it specializes between good abelian schemes. Therefore the same statement holds for A. QED.

Source for the Hasse-invariant criterion: Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, second edition, Chapter V, Theorem 4.1(a), printed p.148 (a copy was located at https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~obenoist/refs/Silverman.pdf). The application to x^3+1 is proved above. Isogeny invariance of the rational Dieudonne module is background; no new proof is claimed.

This statement is about rational residue characteristics. Counting places by their norms in an arbitrary number field is a different counting problem, especially when inert rational primes have residue degree two.

## P03. Why splitting in K does not determine the Newton polygon on the whole Weil family

For any elliptic curve C, put A=C^4 and act on each of the two C^2 blocks by

M = [[0,-1],[1,-1]], so that M^2+M+I=0.

Use the polarization represented on C^2 by H=[[2,-1],[-1,2]], and its product on A. H is positive definite and M^t H M=H. Therefore the Rosati involution induces complex conjugation on Q[M]. Over C the two eigenvalues of M on Lie(C^2) are omega and bar(omega), once each. Thus (A,Q[M]) has Weil signature (2,2).

Take C:y^2=x^3-x. At p=5 it is ordinary, even though p is inert in Q(omega). At p=7 it is supersingular, even though p splits in Q(omega). These facts also follow directly from the Hasse-invariant coefficient criterion in P02: for p=5 the x^4 coefficient of (x^3-x)^2 is -2, nonzero modulo 5; for p=7 the x^6 coefficient of (x^3-x)^3 is zero. Both primes are good. This is a concrete counterexample to extending P02 from the E^4 isogeny orbit to all K-Weil fourfolds.

Taking C to be non-CM shows that the arithmetic of non-CM supersingular-prime questions is already present inside the class of Weil fourfolds. A theorem about all Hecke characters of number fields is not, by itself, a theorem giving effective supersingular-prime counts for these C.

## P05. A fixed algebraic class has a prime-independent upper bound

Let (A,H) be a polarized abelian variety over a number field, t an absolute Hodge class, and suppose N t=[Z^+]-[Z^-] on A over the algebraic closure, with the two effective degrees at most D. All finitely many cycles, equations and comparison data are defined over a finite extension. Their closures in the good abelian scheme have specializations as effective cycles of the same degree; proper pushforward, specialization and the cycle class map commute. Thus c_v(t_v)<=max(N,D) at every place where this simultaneous model is defined. Enlarging a finite exceptional set gives a uniform bound for all remaining good places. If t_v is known algebraic at each of the finitely many omitted good places as well, taking the maximum of finitely many additional complexities bounds all good places. No GRH enters.

This proves existence, not a numerical value. Nor does it permit interchanging the quantifiers 'for every fiber there exists a bound' and 'there exists a bound for every fiber'.

## P06. An explicit projector onto the Weil plane on E^4

Fix E=C/(Z+Z omega), A=E^4, the product principal polarization theta=sum theta_i, and the Weil action eta(k)=diag(k,k,bar(k),bar(k)). Put

R=eta(omega)^*, S=eta(sqrt(-3))^* on H^4(A,Q),

e_W = (9+S)(2-R-R^2)/54.

Then e_W is the projector onto W=Lambda_K^4 H^1(A,Q), regarded as its two-dimensional Q-subspace in H^4.

**Proof.** Over K, write H^1=V_sigma direct-sum V_barsigma, each of dimension 4. On Lambda^a V_sigma tensor Lambda^(4-a) V_barsigma, S has eigenvalue 9(-1)^a. Therefore (9+S)/18 kills odd a and is identity on even a. On the surviving a=0,2,4 terms, R has eigenvalues omega^2,1,omega, respectively. Hence (2-R-R^2)/3 is identity on a=0,4 and zero on a=2. The two commuting factors give precisely the projector claimed. QED.

Let Z={(x,y,x,y)}. Put w=e_W[Z]. Then w is nonzero. In the conventions of P07 below its normalized Weil period is z(w)=1. The classes w and Rw form a Q-basis of W, since R has minimal polynomial X^2+X+1 there.

An initial cycle realization of 54w by the displayed polynomial has effective degrees at most 288 on each side: deg_theta Z=8, pullback by eta(sqrt(-3)) multiplies degree of codimension-two cycles by 9, and R preserves theta. This is improved below.

## P07. A smaller exact cycle identity: c(w)<=24

For norm-one a,b in Z[omega], let Z_(a,b)={(x,y,ax,by)}; its theta-degree is 8. On the factors (i,j), write D_ij(a) for the cross Kunneth part of the divisor class of the graph x_j=a x_i, namely its class minus theta_i+theta_j. Let P=D_13(1), Q=D_13(omega), U=D_24(1), V=D_24(omega). The map a -> D_ij(a) is additive, since the cross Kunneth part is the linear action of a on H^1. In particular D(omega^2)=-D(1)-D(omega), and D(-a)=-D(a).

Then

3w = P U - P V - Q U - 2 Q V

and, as a cohomology identity,

3w = [Z_(1,-omega)]+[Z_(1,-omega^2)]+[Z_(omega,-1)]
      -[Z_(-omega,-omega)]-[Z_(-omega^2,-1)]-[Z_(-omega^2,-omega^2)].

**Proof.** The odd Kunneth parts of [Z] are killed by (9+S)/18 and the even non-cross parts are killed by (2-R-R^2)/3. The surviving cross part is PU. R(P)=-P-Q, R(Q)=P, and the same formulas hold for U,V. Thus (2-R-R^2)PU=PU-PV-QU-2QV, giving the first identity. For the second identity, write [Z_(a,b)]=(theta_1+theta_3+D_13(a))(theta_2+theta_4+D_24(b)). On subtracting the indicated six terms, the constant and linear terms cancel. The remaining bilinear expression is PU-PV-QU-2QV. QED.

There are three degree-eight surfaces on each side, proving c(w)<=24 using N=3. The identity is realized by endomorphisms and divisors over Q(omega), hence specializes at every good place with p not dividing 6. The exterior-algebra script `weil_explicit.py` independently verifies all 70 coefficients in Lambda^4 Z^8, finds w has denominator exactly 3, that 3w is primitive integral, and that w^2=2 and w.Rw=-1. These are computation checks, not formal proof certificates.

For an arbitrary class t=(a w+b Rw)/m with integers a,b and m>0, the same formula gives N=3m and effective degrees at most 24(|a|+|b|). This is an explicit bound for a specified class at the fixed product, independent of its ordinary/supersingular reduction. It is not asserted minimal.

## P08. Period-degree estimate on complex effective surfaces

Normalize holomorphic forms dz_i on E so that integral_E (i/2) dz_i wedge dbar(z_i)=1, and put

Psi=dz_1 wedge dz_2 wedge dbar(z_3) wedge dbar(z_4),

z(V)=(1/4) integral_V Psi.

For an irreducible surface V in B x B=E^2 x E^2, resolve V. Let a,b be the degrees of its projections to the two B factors, setting a or b to zero for nondominant projection, and set c=integral_V theta_plus theta_minus. Then

|z(V)| <= sqrt(ab), c >= 2 sqrt(ab), deg_theta V = 2(a+b+c).

**Proof.** Pull back the holomorphic two-forms alpha=dz_1 wedge dz_2 and beta=dz_3 wedge dz_4 to the resolution. Their positive Hermitian Hodge norms squared are 4a and 4b; if the projection is nondominant its two-form vanishes. Cauchy--Schwarz gives |integral alpha wedge bar(beta)|<=4 sqrt(ab), which is the first inequality. The pullbacks L_plus,L_minus of the two polarizations are nef divisors on the resolution, with squares 2a and 2b. The Hodge index inequality for nef divisors gives c^2>=(2a)(2b); c>=0 gives the second inequality. The last identity is the expansion (L_plus+L_minus)^2. QED.

In particular every effective surface has even degree. Any irreducible component with nonzero z has a,b>=1, hence degree at least 8. If an effective cycle T has degree <=14, it has at most one component, with multiplicity one, whose z is nonzero. For that component ab<=2: if ab>=3, then a+b>=4 and the integral c>=ceil(2 sqrt(3))=4, so its degree is at least 16. Consequently

                 deg_theta T<=14 implies |z(T)|<=sqrt(2).          (P08.1)

The proof allows singular, reducible and nonreduced effective cycles via resolution and multiplicities. It does not assume that the surface is an abelian subvariety.

## P09. An exact least complexity: c_C(t_star)=16

This example was discovered by the root agent and checked here; attribution is joint, with the lower-bound mechanism P08.

Let Gamma_a={(x,y,ax,ay)} and define

t_star=[Gamma_1]+[Gamma_-1]-[Gamma_omega]-[Gamma_-omega].

Then t_star is a nonzero Weil class and its least complexity for the product principal polarization is exactly 16.

**Proof of purity.** Regard each graph as a correspondence on B=E^2. The sum Gamma_a+Gamma_-a kills the odd Kunneth components because [-1] acts by (-1)^j on H^j(B). The H^0 and H^4 components for a=1 and a=omega agree, since both are automorphisms. On H^2(B), the scalar automorphism omega acts as omega^2 on H^(2,0), as 1 on H^(1,1), and as bar(omega)^2 on H^(0,2). Thus the difference is supported precisely on the two rationally conjugate lines H^(2,0)(B) tensor H^(0,2)(B) and H^(0,2)(B) tensor H^(2,0)(B). These are exactly the Weil lines for eta(k)=diag(k,k,bar(k),bar(k)). The period computation below proves nonvanishing.

**Upper bound.** Every Gamma_a here has theta-degree 8: the pullback of theta to its E^2 is 2(theta_1+theta_2), whose square has degree 8. The displayed difference has two components on each side, so N=1,D=16 gives c_C(t_star)<=16.

**Lower bound.** In P08's normalization z(Gamma_a)=bar(a^2). Therefore z(t_star)=2(1-omega) and |z(t_star)|=2 sqrt(3). If N t_star=[T^+]-[T^-] with N>=1 and both effective degrees <=14, then P08.1 gives

2N sqrt(3)=|z(T^+)-z(T^-)|<=2 sqrt(2),

a contradiction. Effective degrees are even, so any representation with max(N,D)<16 would have both degrees at most 14, and has just been excluded. Thus c_C(t_star)=16. QED.

All four graph cycles are defined over Q(omega). Their specializations have the same degree and represent t_star,v, so c_v(t_star,v)<=16 at every good place where the CM endomorphisms and model extend. Applying the bounded-specialization eventual-constancy theorem under independent review gives c_v(t_star,v)=16 outside a finite set of good places. Hence the eventual least complexity is 16 on both the ordinary (p=1 mod3) and supersingular (p=2 mod3) subsequences. This last inference depends on that theorem and does not compute its exceptional set.

## P11. Prime counts cannot supply a missing size estimate

Let P be either infinite prime set from P02. Knowing its counting function, even exactly, imposes no condition on an unrelated positive integer function d:P->Z_{>0}; for example d(p)=p and d(p)=1 have identical underlying prime counts. This elementary logical observation does NOT refute a possible geometric bound for actual cycle complexity. It identifies the additional statement one must prove: a geometric inequality or construction relating the primes counted to bounded-degree points of the relevant relative Chow scheme.

Once the bounded-specialization theorem is available, a fixed class with finite generic complexity has eventually constant local minimum; a nonalgebraic class (if one exists) has only finitely many primes of any given bounded complexity. Thus the needed arithmetic input is an infinite intersection with one fixed bounded-degree locus, not merely an infinite or positive-density set of ordinary or supersingular primes.

## P12. Exact dependence on the polarization, and a normalization obstruction

For the explicit t_star of P09 and every integer k>=1,

                     c_(theta^tensor k)(t_star) = 16 k^2.

**Proof.** The degree of a surface for the polarization theta^tensor k is k^2 times its theta-degree. The lower-bound proof of P09 excluded every representation of every positive multiple N t_star with both theta-degrees below 16; it did not merely exclude N=1. ## P13. Characteristic 2: exact local minimum 16 by a small norm-pattern argument

This is the completed strengthening of the previously open characteristic-2 step. The root, bounded_specialization, and cm_transport agents supplied independent checks of parts of this argument. The deep background is only the usual description of the endomorphism ring of a supersingular elliptic curve as a maximal quaternion order; all low-degree estimates below are elementary.

Let E be the j=0 elliptic curve in characteristic 2. Its endomorphism order is the Hurwitz order

O_H = Z[1,i,j,(1+i+j+k)/2], i^2=j^2=k^2=ijk=-1.

Choose omega=(-1+i+j+k)/2 and s=i+j+k, so s^2=-3 and K=Q(s). The K-linear projection of a quaternion q=q_0+q_1 i+q_2 j+q_3 k is

                  q_K=q_0+(q_1+q_2+q_3)s/3.

It is orthogonal for the positive reduced norm. The 24 units are the eight elements +/-1,+/-i,+/-j,+/-k and the sixteen elements (+/-1+/-i+/-j+/-k)/2. Exactly six lie in K; their K-projection modulus is 1. Each other unit has squared K-projection modulus 1/3. A norm-2 Hurwitz integer has integral coordinates with exactly two coordinates equal to +/-1 and the others zero, so its squared K-projection modulus is either 0 or 4/3. In particular no norm-2 element lies in K.

Define z on cycle classes by projection to the two-dimensional Weil subspace and the normalization z(Gamma_a)=bar(a^2). Equivalently, use the algebraic projector of P06 and the basis w,Rw; the nondegenerate rational intersection matrix makes the coordinates rational, so z takes its values in K. This definition agrees with P08 after lifting to C. For h in M_2(O_H), exterior algebra on the two K-eigenspaces gives

                     |z(Gamma_h)|=|det(h_K)|,

where h_K is obtained by entrywise K-projection. A transpose graph has the conjugate coordinate and the same absolute value.

### Dominant surfaces

Let V be an irreducible surface in E^2 x E^2 of theta-degree at most 14, with both projections dominant, and denote the two projection degrees by a,b. The intersection-theoretic part of P08 remains valid in every characteristic: a,b>=1, c>=2sqrt(ab), and deg_theta V=2(a+b+c). Hence ab<=2. One projection has degree1. A rational map from an abelian variety to an abelian variety extends, and every such map is a homomorphism followed by a translation. Therefore V is a translate of a graph or transpose graph of an isogeny h:E^2->E^2 of degree d=1 or2.

Writing T=sum Norm(h_ij), intersection with the product polarization gives

                  deg_theta Gamma_h=2(1+d+T).

Thus T<=5 if d=1, and T<=4 if d=2. If h has at most three nonzero entries, row/column permutations make it triangular; then |det(h_K)|^2<=d<=2. If all four entries are nonzero, their positive integral norms have pattern (1,1,1,1) or (2,1,1,1). The latter occurs only when d=1.

A useful general observation is that if one entire row or column lies in K, then

                         |det(h_K)|^2 <= deg h.                    (P13.1)

Indeed Gaussian elimination with a nonzero K pivot reduces the reduced determinant to Norm(k) Norm(q), where q is the corresponding quaternionic Schur complement. Its K projection is the ordinary determinant of h_K divided by k. Orthogonal projection decreases reduced norm. A zero pivot is handled by a permutation or the triangular case.

For norm pattern (1,1,1,1), if every entry lies in K use (P13.1). Otherwise one determinant product contains a non-K unit, so the triangle inequality gives |det(h_K)|<=1+1/sqrt(3)<sqrt(3).

For norm pattern (2,1,1,1), the norm-2 entry is non-K. If a row or column lies in K use (P13.1). Otherwise, either the opposite unit is non-K, in which case

                 |det(h_K)| <= (2/sqrt(3))(1/sqrt(3))+1=5/3,

or the opposite unit is in K and both adjacent units are non-K, in which case

                 |det(h_K)| <= 2/sqrt(3)+1/3 < 5/3.

Since sqrt(2)<5/3 and 1+1/sqrt(3)<5/3, every dominant irreducible surface of degree<=14 has

                              |z(V)|<=5/3<sqrt(3).                 (P13.2)

### Nondominant surfaces

If a projection has point image, its Weil component is zero. Otherwise let its image be a curve C in E^2 and let F be the effective cycle class of a general fiber in the second E^2. Resolving the base curve and viewing V as a divisor in its product with E^2 shows that the (2,2) Kunneth component is [C] tensor [F]. The other Kunneth components do not contribute to W. The mixed intersection is deg_theta(C) deg_theta(F), hence

                    deg_theta V >= 2 deg_theta(C) deg_theta(F).

A degree-1 curve in E^2 is a coordinate elliptic curve, so its non-K Weil component is zero. For z(V) nonzero both curve degrees are at least2; since V has degree<=14 they must be (2,2), (2,3) or (3,2).

An irreducible degree-2 curve is a translate of a graph of a unit (both projection degrees are1); a reducible degree-2 cycle is either such a graph or a sum of coordinate fibers. A degree-3 effective curve is a graph/transpose graph of a norm-2 endomorphism, or a degree-2 graph plus coordinate fibers, or a sum of coordinate fibers. Thus the squared norm of its non-K offdiagonal component is at most2/3 in degree2, and at most2 in degree3.

The normalization can be checked by writing the quaternion algebra as K+Kj with j^2=-delta and jk=bar(k)j. For curves whose non-K offdiagonal components are beta j and gamma j, the product Weil coordinate is, up to sign and conjugation, delta beta bar(gamma). Therefore its squared absolute value is precisely the product of the two non-K reduced norms. It follows that

                       |z(V)| <= sqrt((2/3)*2)=2/sqrt(3)<sqrt(3).

Every irreducible component with nonzero z has degree at least8, by the dominant estimate or the product-of-curve-degrees bound. Hence an effective cycle of degree<=14 has at most one such component, with multiplicity one. Combining the two cases gives |z(T)|<=5/3 for every effective T of degree<=14.

Since |z(Nt_star)|=2N sqrt(3)>10/3 for every N>=1, such a class cannot be a difference of two effective cycles of degree<=14. All surface degrees are even. The graph upper bound N=1,D=16 survives in characteristic2. Consequently

                              c_2(t_star)=16.

QED.
