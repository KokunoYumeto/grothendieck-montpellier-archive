                       1.1. Overview. For a smooth projective variety X over C and an integer p ≥ 0
                       there is a cycle class map CHp (X) → H2p (X, Q) from the space of codimension p
                       cycles on X to the 2p-th Betti cohomology of X. This cycle class map lands in
                       Hdgp (X, Q) := H2p (X, Q) ∩ Hp,p (X, C), where Hp,p (X, C) ⊂ H2p (X, C) is part of the
                       Hodge decomposition. The Hodge conjecture for X asserts that the cycle class map
                       surjects onto Hdgp (X, Q) for all p, see e.g. [Del06], [Hod52]. The goal of this note
                       is to explain the following result of [Ope26].
                       Theorem I. If X is an abelian variety admitting complex multiplication, then the
                       Hodge conjecture holds for X.
                         For previous work on the Hodge conjecture for abelian varieties, see [Gor97]
                       and the references therein for a survey of work done up to 1997. For subsequent
                       work, see e.g.      [Mil99a],[MZ99],[Abd02a],[Abd99],[Abd02b],[Haz03b],[Haz03a],
                       [Abd04],[Koi04],[Haz06],[RM08],[O’S11],[Abd12], [LPS13],[Abd15],[Abd16],[Mil20c],
                       [Anc21],[VV22],[vG22],[vG23],[Mar23], [Flo25], [Agu25],[Mar25], [vGR26], [FF26],
                       [Mar26] for an incomplete list.

                       1.1.1. Consequences for CM abelian varieties. By [Abd05, Main result], Theorem
                       I implies the generalized Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties (cf. [Haz03b,
                       Theorem 8.3]). Since the Mumford–Tate conjecture is known for CM abelian vari-
                       eties by work of Pohlmann [Poh68], Theorem I implies the Tate conjecture for CM
                       abelian varieties over finitely generated fields of characteristic zero.

                       1.1.2. Consequences for all abelian varieties. Let k be an algebraically closed field.
                       By [Mil02, Theorem 4.6.(a)], Theorem I implies that numerical equivalence coincides
                       with ℓ-adic homological equivalence for abelian varieties over k with ℓ coprime to
                       the characteristic of k. By [Mil02, Theorem 4.6.(b)], Theorem I implies that the
                       Hodge standard conjecture holds for abelian varieties over k with respect to any
                       good (in the sense of [Mil02, §4]) Weil cohomology theory.

1.2.3. Of course it is not at all clear how Proposition 1.2.2 is useful. Another key
idea of [Ope26] is to take for S a Picard modular surface associated to E. More
precisely, we take a Shimura variety associated to a Hermitian space V over E with
signature (2, 1) at the distinguished embedding E ⊂ C and (3, 0) at all other places.
The first cohomology of these two-dimensional Shimura varieties can be completely
understood using theta lifts, see Theorem 3.1.11, which we explain following [Liu21].
It turns out to be essentially immediate from this that the Hodge structures of CM
abelian varieties with CM by E appear in this first cohomology, see Corollary 3.1.13.

1.2.4. To carry out the cup product computation, we produce explicit cohomology
classes with complex coefficients. More precisely, the work of Bergeron–Millson–
Moeglin [BMM16] shows that the first cohomology of S is spanned by theta lifts,
see Proposition 4.1.8. Theorem I thus reduces to showing that there are theta lifts
whose cup-product is nonzero. To do this, we consider the fourfold cup-product as
a functional on a Schwartz space, and then argue that it is enough to show that this
functional is nonzero. This we finally do using the doubling method in Section 4.2.
Remark 1.2.5. We recently learned that Wei Zhang had considered the rough
strategy followed in this paper and in [Ope26] many years ago, and also tried to use

this to prove the Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties. A key point missing to
him was a nonvanishing result for the surface period analogous to Theorem 4.0.2.
1.3. Comparison to [Ope26]. Our proof of Theorem I is heavily inspired by the
proof given in [Ope26]. Nevertheless, we do not claim to understand (or even to
have read) the details of the arguments written in [Ope26]. The goal of this note
is to give a variant of the proofs in [Ope26] that relies as much as possible on the
written literature. This has the benefit of making the proofs shorter, and possibly
also easier to understand. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the argument
for Theorem 4.0.2 is a departure from the argument for the analogous statement in
[Ope26]. We leave the task of a careful exegesis of the original proof to others.

  The goal of this section is to explain a reduction for the Hodge conjecture for
abelian varieties of CM type.

2.0.1. Recollections on the Hodge conjecture. For a smooth projective variety X over
C we let CH∗ (X) denote the Q-linear Chow ring of cycles on X modulo rational
equivalence, with CHi (X) the codimension i cycles. We similarly let H∗ (X, Q)

Hdgp (X, Q), see e.g. [Del06], [Hod52].
