## 1. Validity, grading and the canonical point-jet map

Its size is \(5(d+b+x+k-2)\), its second-row excess over

\[
\alpha=(a+c,b+c,c),\qquad a=b+x,\quad c=2k-2.
\]
Thus \(N=|\alpha|\ge5k\) is precisely the same validity
inequality. The determinant identity for \(Q\), of rank three,

The bundle \(S_{(a,b)}Q\) is generated by the quotient of
\(S_{(a,b)}E\). At a fixed point, select global sections spanning
its fiber. Every homogeneous tangent monomial of degree \(j<k\)
is the leading jet of a scalar degree-\(c\) section that vanishes
to order \(j\), because \(c\ge k-1\). Multiplication by the
selected bundle sections spans the entire degree-\(j\) jet
quotient. Correcting jets in increasing order changes none of
the already prescribed lower degrees. This proves surjectivity
onto the full one-point jet space.

The map itself is the canonical restriction map. G-027's
least-normal-degree substitution identifies its kernel with

## 2. The pair term and its actual maps

At subset size three, the complementary space has dimension one.
Its Cauchy factor contributes a single row. Consequently the
truncated tensor factors would need to supply at least
\[
\alpha_2+\alpha_3=b+4k-4>3(k-1).
\]
Their total available degree is at most \(3(k-1)\), so this
term is zero. The larger subset terms vanish in degree \(N\ge5k\)
as already proved in G-027.

At size two, the complementary factor has at most two rows.
It must omit the third row of \(\alpha\), of length
\(c=2(k-1)\). This already uses the entire maximum degree of
the two truncated factors. Thus their degrees are each \(k-1\);
the complementary partition is exactly \((a+c,b+c)\);
and the removed skew shape is a single horizontal row of
length \(c\). Its only Schur type is \((c)\).

The multiplication map
\[

\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^{c}F

on a pure power; normalized symmetric comultiplication supplies
an explicit inverse on the summand. Exchanging the two factors
fixes multiplication, so no extra pair sign occurs here.
