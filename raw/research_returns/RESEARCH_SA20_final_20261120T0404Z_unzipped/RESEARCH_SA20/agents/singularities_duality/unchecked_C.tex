\subsection{Part C: the remaining original-source comparison gates}
The mathematical statements C.001--C.023 have proofs or complete
counterexamples in the checked file. Part C.2(b) remains incomplete
because two requested original articles have not been opened in full.
The checked source comparison C.020 is explicitly restricted to the
texts actually opened; it does not silently attribute the same
hypotheses to these two papers.

\paragraph{C.U001: Hartshorne (1968).}
The exact gate is the original wording, hypotheses and endpoint
clause of Proposition~4.3 in \emph{Cohomological Dimension of
Algebraic Varieties}, Annals of Mathematics~88 (1968), 403--450.
Speiser's opened 1973 article, p.~147, invokes that proposition
after reducing arbitrary coherent sheaves to negative twists
tensored with the canonical line bundle. This locates a relevant
result, but does not establish whether the original proposition
has every stated direction or the boundary injection. The smallest
remaining source check is that single proposition and its notation.
Theorem~7.5 is independently restated in the opened Hartshorne
(1971), Theorem~1.3, p.~258; its original page is still unchecked.
No mathematical implication in C.014 depends on guessing either
original clause.

\paragraph{C.U002: Ogus (1973).}
The exact gate is the original comparison theorem, its index
range and endpoint injection in \emph{Local Cohomological
Dimension of Algebraic Varieties}, Annals of Mathematics~98
(1973), 327--365. The publisher abstract establishes the
smooth characteristic-zero ambient setting. The fully opened
Szpiro Bourbaki exposition~458, especially \S1.2 and \S2.3,
derives the local/formal comparison and explicitly refers to
Ogus's \S3 for the comparison argument. The fully opened
Speiser (1973), p.~144, attributes the local-complete-intersection
extension of $(I_s^*)$ to Ogus. These are precise secondary
attributions by contemporaneous researchers, not a reading of
Ogus's original theorem. The smallest remaining source check
is the original \S3 statement and its cited hypotheses; the
relation with the projective application must then be checked
in the same paper.

\paragraph{Access approaches tried and their failure points.}
The Annals publisher pages for both papers were opened. Their
DOI links and the JSTOR PDF and PDFplus links returned a
JavaScript access page instead of the article. SciSpace's
advertised PDF led to the same JSTOR target. The Ogus author
page did not expose this 1973 article. OpenAlex and Crossref
metadata searches supplied no accessible repository PDF.
Searches with both available search engines, exact titles,
DOIs, author names and theorem numbers found later citations
but no original full text. The Goettingen archive did supply
the fully opened Hartshorne 1970 book and Speiser 1973
Inventiones article; they settle the mathematical criteria
under their printed hypotheses, but cannot certify the text
of a different publication. The next useful step is an
accessible original scan from an authorized repository or
the publisher, followed by direct inspection of the two
smallest source gates above.

\paragraph{Undefined manuscript notation is not an unproved theorem.}
The adic fragment at 122/035 never defines its proposed category
or the crossed-out module $Q$; C.010 records exactly this limit
of the concordance. Scan 125/006 never defines $X'$ or states
an induction conclusion. C.021--C.022 complete the
punctured-local reading with every map and limit, while
C.016 and C.018 give the complete, different Cartier reading.
Explicit counterexamples in C.021 rule out treating a Cartier
restriction as that localization row or dropping the canonical
module and boundedness hypotheses. These results specify the
mathematics without claiming a uniquely recoverable historical
intention that the page does not state.
