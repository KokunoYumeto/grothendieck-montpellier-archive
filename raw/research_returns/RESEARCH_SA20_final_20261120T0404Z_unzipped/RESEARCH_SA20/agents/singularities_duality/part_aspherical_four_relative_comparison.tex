\subsection{A.163. Relative comparison over aspherical four-manifolds}

\begin{saresult}{A.163}{Proved under stated hypotheses}{Assembly, the relative cylinder comparison, and the four-torus}

Let $B$ be a closed connected oriented real PL four-manifold which is
aspherical, and put $\pi=\pi_1B$ and $N=B\times I$.
Write
\[
 H_C=\operatorname{Aut}_C(N\operatorname{ rel}\partial N),
 \qquad C\in\{\mathrm{PL},\mathrm{TOP}\}.
\]
PL families are jointly PL and preserve their parameter projection.
TOP groups have their compact-open topology and are replaced by their
singular complexes when taking comparison fibres. All homotopy groups
below are based at the identity. Assume $\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)=0$.
Let
\[
 A_j:H_j(B;\mathbb L\langle1\rangle)
                 \longrightarrow L_j(\mathbb Z[\pi])                 \tag{A.163.1}
\]
be the quadratic surgery assembly maps, with the orientation character
trivial and the decoration used by the geometric surgery sequence.
The Whitehead-group hypothesis removes the simple versus homotopy
decoration distinction needed here. No assembly isomorphism is assumed
without being stated.

If $A_7$ is injective, then
\[
 \ker\{\pi_0H_{\mathrm{PL}}\longrightarrow\pi_0H_{\mathrm{TOP}}\}
                      \cong H^1(B;\mathbb Z/2).                     \tag{A.163.2}
\]
This is an isomorphism of groups, using the natural relative structure
boundary map. If also $A_8$ is injective, there is an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow H^0(B;\mathbb Z/2)
  \longrightarrow\pi_1H_{\mathrm{PL}}
  \longrightarrow\pi_1H_{\mathrm{TOP}}\longrightarrow0,             \tag{A.163.3}
\]
and the comparison is an isomorphism on $\pi_j$ for every $j\ge2$.
No splitting of (A.163.3) is asserted.

Suppose in addition that $A_6$ is injective and every element of
$Z(\pi)$ is the evaluation class of a based jointly PL loop of
automorphisms of $B$. Then the comparison on $\pi_0H$ is onto, so
(A.163.2) extends to
\[
 0\longrightarrow H^1(B;\mathbb Z/2)
  \longrightarrow\pi_0H_{\mathrm{PL}}
  \longrightarrow\pi_0H_{\mathrm{TOP}}\longrightarrow0.             \tag{A.163.4}
\]
The central evaluation hypothesis is automatic when $Z(\pi)=0$.
Under these additional assumptions the ordinary-loop contribution
\[
 \eta_B:\pi_1\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)
       \longrightarrow\pi_0F_A
       \xrightarrow{\pi_0\beta_B}H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)                 \tag{A.163.5}
\]
from A.136 is zero. In fact it is enough in (A.163.5) that the evaluation
class of the loop in question be represented by a jointly PL loop.

For the standard $T^4$, all the stated hypotheses hold. Thus its
fully boundary-fixed component comparison has kernel
$(\mathbb Z/2)^4$ and is surjective; its fundamental-group comparison
is surjective with kernel $\mathbb Z/2$, and its comparisons in degrees
at least two are isomorphisms. Also $\eta_{T^4}=0$.
More generally, the assembly and Whitehead-group hypotheses for
(A.163.2)--(A.163.3) hold for every such $B$ whose fundamental group is
word-hyperbolic or acts properly, cocompactly and isometrically on a
finite-dimensional CAT(0) space. Thus the stated relative kernel,
fundamental-group exact sequence, and higher isomorphisms hold on this
entire explicitly specified class. The extra conclusion (A.163.4) still
requires the stated realization of central elements by PL loops; it
holds in particular when the centre is trivial.
These statements concern the fully boundary-fixed cylinder and the
ordinary-loop part of $\beta_B$. They do not compute the free-top
comparison fibre $F_P$, the whole map $\beta_B$, or the endpoint-image
condition in A.137.

\end{saresult}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{A relative mapping-cylinder lemma in the actual surgery dimension.}
For $r\ge1$, put $V=B\times I^r$. Let $F:V\to V$ be a TOP
homeomorphism which is the identity on $\partial V$ and is homotopic
to the identity relative to that boundary. Its relative PL difference
class is
\[
 \kappa(F)\in H^3(V,\partial V;\mathbb Z/2)
                   \cong H^{3-r}(B;\mathbb Z/2).                   \tag{A.163.6}
\]
This is the stable universal PL reduction difference for the standard
PL structure; it is not an assertion that $F$ is PL.

Choose a PL collar of the entire boundary, rounding the product corners
in compatible PL coordinates if necessary. Use the explicit boundary
compression of A.135 to obtain an embedding $c_s:V\to V$ whose image
omits a small boundary collar. Extend $c_sFc_s^{-1}$ by the identity
on the omitted collar. This is isotopic to $F$ relative to the boundary.
Apply the same formula to an existing relative homotopy of maps:
extend $c_sk_uc_s^{-1}$ by the identity. The formulas agree on the
gluing boundary because every $k_u$ fixes $\partial V$. Thus the
compressed map has a homotopy to the identity fixed on a common collar,
without requiring any intermediate map to be a homeomorphism.
Reparameterize its interval so it
is constant near both endpoints. Write this homotopy as $k_u$, with
$k_0=1$ and $k_1=F$. None of the intermediate $k_u$ is required to be
a homeomorphism.

Form the labeled mapping cylinder
\[
 M_F=(V\times I)\cup_F V,\qquad W=V\times I.
\]
The upper boundary has label $y=F(x)$, while bottom and side boundary
labels are the standard ones. Define
\[
 U:M_F\longrightarrow W,\qquad [x,u]\longmapsto(k_u(x),u).          \tag{A.163.7}
\]
At the top this is well-defined and equals the identity in the $y$
label. It is the identity in the bottom and side labels as well.
The chosen collars make it a product in all boundary coordinates,
including the corners. Both bottom inclusions are homotopy
equivalences, and $U$ restricts to their common identity, so $U$ is a
homotopy equivalence of the full boundary pair. Its relative TOP normal
invariant satisfies
\[
 \nu(U)\in[W,\partial W;G/\mathrm{TOP},*]
       \cong H_{5+r}(B;\mathbb L\langle1\rangle),
 \qquad \theta\nu(U)=0.                                          \tag{A.163.8}
\]
The normal data are the source stable TOP normal bundle and the
induced fibre-homotopy equivalence of Spivak normal fibrations, with
the specified boundary identification from (A.163.7).

This uses surgery only in dimension $5+r\ge6$. Ranicki's
Definition~18.2, printed pp.~197--198 \cite{Ranicki1992TopologicalManifolds}, explicitly treats compact
manifolds with boundary; the relative-version paragraph on p.~198,
Proposition~18.3 and Theorem~18.5, pp.~198--201, identify the normal
invariant and surgery obstruction with the homology group and
assembly in (A.163.8). If $A_{5+r}$ is injective, it follows that
$\nu(U)=0$.

Here is the required relative bundle implication, rather than merely
an analogy of zero tests. The zero normal invariant identifies the
source stable TOP normal reduction, with its fixed boundary
comparison, with the pullback of the target's standard PL normal
reduction. Pulling that PL lift back along the relative homotopy gives
a stable PL normal reduction on $M_F$ agreeing with all its prescribed
boundary reductions. Equivalently the natural universal PL-obstruction
map applied to the relative normal invariant is exactly the relative
Kirby--Siebenmann class of the labeled source. The target has zero
class, and the boundary labels were retained in (A.163.7). Stable tangent
and normal reductions have the same mod-two obstruction, since their
sum is stably trivial. Hence
\[
 k(M_F,\partial M_F)=0.
\]
Changing only the top boundary reduction from the standard one to its
$F$-transported reduction identifies this class with the interval
suspension of $\kappa(F)$. The suspension
\[
 H^3(V,\partial V;\mathbb Z/2)
       \xrightarrow{\cong}H^4(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z/2)
\]
is an isomorphism, so $\kappa(F)=0$. This is the same relative
normal-bundle argument as in A.146, now in dimension $5+r$.
No stable lift is converted into an ordinary isotopy.

\textbf{Why the relevant higher traces are homotopic to the identity.}
For $r\ge2$, the boundary $A=B\times\partial I^r$ is connected and
$\pi_1A\to\pi_1V$ is onto. Consequently every homeomorphism $F$ of
$V$ fixed on $A$ is homotopic to the identity relative to $A$.
One direct justification is to lift to the universal cover. Since
$F$ is the identity on $A$, it induces the identity on $\pi_1V$.
The preimage of $A$ in the universal cover is connected by the
surjectivity just noted. Choose the lift of $F$ which is the identity
there. The universal cover of $V$ is contractible by asphericity.
A relative cellular obstruction construction, choosing one cell from
each deck orbit, joins the lifted map to the identity while fixing
the preimage of $A$; every obstruction vanishes because the target
cover is contractible. The homotopy is deck-equivariant and descends.
The same construction works with a fixed closed collar in place of
$A$. This proves the precise relative assertion needed for (A.163.8).

\textbf{The comparison connecting maps are the trace obstructions.}
Let $\mathcal S_N$ be the sliced relative PL structure space used in
A.135. Its component of the standard structure is the base component
of the automorphism quotient, and
\[
 \mathcal S_N\simeq
 \operatorname{Map}_*(N/\partial N,K(\mathbb Z/2,3)),
 \quad \pi_q\mathcal S_N=H^{2-q}(B;\mathbb Z/2)
 \quad(q=1,2).                                                     \tag{A.163.9}
\]
The orbit quotient contains this entire base component, which is all
that is used for positive homotopy groups. The natural map
$H_{\mathrm{TOP}}\to\mathcal S_N$ sends a homeomorphism to the
transported standard structure.

Represent a class in $\pi_qH_{\mathrm{TOP}}$, $q=1$ or $2$, by a based
cube family $h_p$, $p\in I^q$. A.135 gives one common spatial collar
fixed by the whole family. Precompose each parameter coordinate by
a PL function constant near its endpoints; the family is then the
identity on a parameter-boundary collar. Its trace
\[
 \mathcal T_h:N\times I^q\longrightarrow N\times I^q,
                 (x,p)\longmapsto(h_p(x),p)                        \tag{A.163.10}
\]
is an actual TOP homeomorphism fixed near the full boundary. The
class induced in (A.163.9) is its relative PL difference class, under
the product suspension identification
\[
 H^3(N\times I^q,\partial(N\times I^q);\mathbb Z/2)
                         \cong H^{2-q}(B;\mathbb Z/2).             \tag{A.163.11}
\]

For completeness, this equality retains the ordinary-family tangent
data. In tangent-microbundle pair coordinates keep the base $(x,p)$
mapped to $(h_p(x),p)$ and send a nearby second point $(y,p')$ to
\[
                (h_{p+t(p'-p)}(y),p'),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
Extend the family constantly outside the parameter cube using the
identity collar. For each fixed base this is a homeomorphism germ,
with inverse using the inverse of the same $h$ slice. At $t=0$ it
is the vertical tangent map with trivial parameter directions; at
$t=1$ it is the full tangent map of (A.163.10). It preserves the zero
section over the base homeomorphism and is constant on all fixed
boundary germs. Thus universal relative PL obstruction identifies
the sliced structure class with (A.163.11), exactly as in the trace
verification in A.136.

In (A.163.10), $r=q+1\ge2$. The preceding relative homotopy argument and
mapping-cylinder lemma therefore show that
\[
 \pi_1H_{\mathrm{TOP}}\longrightarrow H^1(B;\mathbb Z/2)
       \text{ is zero if }A_7\text{ is injective},                 \tag{A.163.12}
\]
and
\[
 \pi_2H_{\mathrm{TOP}}\longrightarrow H^0(B;\mathbb Z/2)
       \text{ is zero if }A_8\text{ is injective}.                 \tag{A.163.13}
\]

\textbf{The actual relative kernel.}
A.135 identifies the based comparison fibre with
$\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,1))$. Its homotopy exact sequence
contains
\[
\begin{split}
0\longrightarrow\pi_2H_{\mathrm{PL}}
 \longrightarrow\pi_2H_{\mathrm{TOP}}
 \longrightarrow H^0(B;\mathbb Z/2)
 \longrightarrow\pi_1H_{\mathrm{PL}}\\
 \longrightarrow\pi_1H_{\mathrm{TOP}}
 \longrightarrow H^1(B;\mathbb Z/2)
 \longrightarrow\pi_0H_{\mathrm{PL}}
 \longrightarrow\pi_0H_{\mathrm{TOP}}.
\end{split}                                                       \tag{A.163.14}
\]
Equations (A.163.12)--(A.163.13) prove (A.163.2)--(A.163.3) and the degree-two
isomorphism. Higher isomorphisms are already immediate from the
fibre in A.135.

The identification of the component kernel is a group identification.
The path-model comparison fibre is the homotopy fibre of a map of
groups, and its boundary to $\pi_0H_{\mathrm{PL}}$ respects composition.
Under (A.163.9), composition of relative structure loops adds their
universal PL difference classes, with the usual pullback action.
That action is trivial here. Any element of $H_C$ is homotopic to
the identity as an unrestricted map of $N$, since its bottom
restriction is the identity. On relative cohomology the connecting
description
\[
 H^j(N,\partial N;\mathbb Z/2)
  \cong \operatorname{coker}\{H^{j-1}(B;\mathbb Z/2)
     \xrightarrow{\Delta}H^{j-1}(B;\mathbb Z/2)^{\oplus2}\}
\]
shows the action is also the identity, because both boundary maps
are fixed pointwise. Thus the cohomology operation in (A.163.14) is
ordinary addition, and the injective map in (A.163.2) is a homomorphism.

\textbf{Components and the ordinary-loop contribution when the centre is realized.}
For $r=1$, the two boundary components are disconnected. The relative
homotopy class of $F\in H_{\mathrm{TOP}}$ is instead the class of the
loop $a_t(x)=\operatorname{pr}_B F(x,t)$ in the identity component of
$\operatorname{Map}(B,B)$. Each $a_t$ is a homotopy equivalence.
For an aspherical $B$, evaluation identifies
\[
 \pi_1\operatorname{Map}(B,B)_{\mathrm{id}}\cong Z(\pi).           \tag{A.163.15}
\]
Indeed a loop is a map $B\times S^1\to B$ restricting to the identity
on $B\times\{1\}$; its fundamental-group map sends the circle
generator to a central element. Conversely each such homomorphism
is realized uniquely up to homotopy relative to $B\times\{1\}$,
by the elementary relative $K(\pi,1)$ obstruction construction.

Choose a jointly PL automorphism loop $b_t$ with the same central
class. Its trace $T_b(x,t)=(b_t(x),t)$ is a PL homeomorphism fixing
both ends, and $T_b^{-1}F$ is homotopic to the identity relative to
both ends, by (A.163.15) and interpolation of the interval coordinate.
Apply (A.163.8) for $r=1$ and the injectivity of $A_6$ to obtain
$\kappa(T_b^{-1}F)=0$. Since a literal PL trace has zero PL
difference, additivity gives $\kappa(F)=0$.

In ambient dimension five the relative structure classification
already used in A.135 identifies the zero difference component
with the standard component. The orbit quotient contains that
component and is a Kan fibration. A path from the structure of $F$
to the standard structure therefore lifts to a TOP isotopy, fixed
near $\partial N$, ending at a PL homeomorphism. This proves
surjectivity in (A.163.4). It uses the proved five-dimensional relative
classification, not a four-dimensional smoothing assertion.

For an ordinary loop $\gamma$ of automorphisms of $B$, (A.163.5) is
the PL difference of its trace, by A.136. Removing a PL loop with
the same evaluation class gives a trace homotopic to the identity
relative to both ends. The same argument proves $\eta_B([\gamma])=0$.
In particular $\eta_B$ always vanishes on the kernel of evaluation
under the injectivity of $A_6$. The conclusion concerns this
ordinary-loop contribution only; points of $F_A$ ending at other
PL components can still have an uncomputed $\beta_B$ value.

\textbf{The standard four-torus.}
For $B=T^4$, $\operatorname{Wh}(\mathbb Z^4)=0$, and Ranicki,
Example~24.16, printed p.~278 \cite{Ranicki1992TopologicalManifolds}, gives the zero-connective assembly
isomorphism in all degrees $j\ge4$. The map from one-connective to
zero-connective homology is an isomorphism in degrees $j=6,7,8$:
the adjacent ordinary homology groups $H_j(T^4;\mathbb Z)$ and
$H_{j-1}(T^4;\mathbb Z)$ vanish. Thus (A.163.1) is injective in all
three required degrees. For $z\in\mathbb Z^4$, the translation loop
$b_t([x])=[x+tz]$ is jointly PL in the standard torus coordinates,
has both endpoints the identity, and has evaluation class $z$.
Finally $H^1(T^4;\mathbb Z/2)=(\mathbb Z/2)^4$ and
$H^0(T^4;\mathbb Z/2)=\mathbb Z/2$. This proves the stated
consequences for the actual relative comparison.

\textbf{The hyperbolic and CAT(0) scope, with decorations and covers checked.}
For the more general explicit class in the statement we use the
published original of Bartels--L\"uck, \emph{The Borel Conjecture for
hyperbolic and CAT(0)-groups}, Annals of Mathematics~175 (2012),
631--689, Theorem~B and Proposition~0.3, printed pp.~633--635
\cite{BartelsLuck2012Borel}.
Their group class contains word-hyperbolic groups and groups acting
properly, cocompactly and isometrically on finite-dimensional CAT(0)
spaces. We use the algebraic assembly result, not their separate
dimension-at-least-five geometric Borel conclusion.

The group $\pi$ here is torsion-free. Indeed the finite aspherical
four-complex $B$ gives a finite-length free $\mathbb Z\pi$ resolution
of $\mathbb Z$. Restricting it to a finite cyclic subgroup of prime
order would give a finite-length free resolution there, contradicting
the nonvanishing periodic cohomology of that cyclic group. Every
nontrivial finite-order element has a prime-order power, so none exists.

Theorem~B gives the coefficient K-theoretic assembly isomorphisms in
degrees at most zero, surjectivity in degree one, and the ultimate
L-theoretic assembly isomorphism in every degree. Proposition~0.3(i)
and its proof explicitly convert these to
\[
 \operatorname{Wh}(\pi)=0,
 \qquad H_j(B\pi;\mathbb L^s_{\mathbb Z})
                \xrightarrow{\cong} L_j^s(\mathbb Z\pi)
                \quad\text{for all }j.                            \tag{A.163.16}
\]
In particular, this is not an implicit replacement of an ultimate
decoration by the simple one. The proof of Proposition~0.3 obtains
the vanishing of the reduced projective class group and all negative
K-groups as well as the Whitehead group, and uses those vanishings
for the decoration comparison and the passage from virtually cyclic
assembly to ordinary $B\pi$ assembly. We take the trivial orientation
character, as in (A.163.1).

Since $B$ is a finite four-dimensional $K(\pi,1)$, compare the
one-connective coefficient spectrum to the full spectrum in (A.163.16).
The cofiber has homotopy groups only in degrees at most zero.
Its homology over $B$ vanishes in every degree greater than four:
filter $B$ by its four-dimensional finite cell structure, or use the
finite cellular spectral sequence with total degrees $p+q$, where
$0\le p\le4$ and $q\le0$. The homology exact sequence of that
cofiber therefore makes
\[
 H_j(B;\mathbb L\langle1\rangle)
             \longrightarrow H_j(B;\mathbb L^s_{\mathbb Z})
\]
an isomorphism for $j\ge6$. It follows from (A.163.16) that $A_6,A_7,A_8$
are isomorphisms. This verifies all the assembly hypotheses used
above, on the stated full group class, independently of any
four-dimensional geometric surgery or Borel theorem.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope and proof provenance.}
