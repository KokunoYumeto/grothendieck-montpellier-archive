\begin{saresult}{A.136}{Proved under stated hypotheses}{The base-four comparison fibre is a fibre of an explicit stabilization map}
For a closed compact real PL four-manifold $B$, use the based comparison
fibres $F_A$ and $F_P$ of A.135. There is a specified map of based
homotopy types
\[
 \beta_B:F_A\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,2))
                                                        \tag{A.136.1}
\]
obtained by looping product with a line on sliced PL structures and
then applying the open five-dimensional classification with its
standard PL lift. It satisfies
\[
 F_P\simeq\operatorname{hofib}_0(\beta_B).                \tag{A.136.2}
\]
In particular the last line of (A.135.3) extends to the exact
pointed-set sequence
\[
 H^1(B;\mathbb Z/2)\longrightarrow\pi_0F_P
 \longrightarrow\pi_0F_A
 \xrightarrow{\pi_0\beta_B}H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2).           \tag{A.136.3}
\]
The image of $\pi_0F_P$ consists exactly of the classes with zero
obstruction $\pi_0\beta_B$.
A class of $F_A$ represented by a PL automorphism $a$ and a TOP isotopy
$\gamma$ from the identity to $a$ has obstruction equal to the relative
PL-structure class of the cylinder homeomorphism
$(x,u)\mapsto(\gamma_u(x),u)$, in
$H^3(B\times I,B\times\partial I;\mathbb Z/2)
=H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)$, using the standard PL structure as origin.

This describes all components and all higher homotopy groups of the
\emph{based comparison fibre} in terms of the actual map $\beta_B$.
It does not evaluate that map in general, determine the abstract
four-manifold automorphism comparison, or determine the cokernel of
$\pi_0P_{\mathrm{PL}}(B)\to\pi_0P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)$.
In particular it does not supply a model for TOP concordance components
that have no PL representative. The homotopy type of $F_A$ alone is
insufficient without $\beta_B$.
\end{saresult}
\begin{proof}
The empty case is immediate, so suppose $B$ is nonempty. All structure
complexes in the proof consist of \emph{sliced} PL structures: a
$q$-simplex is a PL structure on $\Delta^q\times X$ for which projection
to $\Delta^q$ is a PL submersion. No block automorphism complex is used.
Whenever a collar is fixed or is a product in one simplex, one common
collar works for its entire parameter simplex. Finite families admit a
common smaller collar. Near a closed subset the structures and maps
are germs on such neighbourhoods.

\textbf{Compact structure spaces and their orbit components.}
Let $\mathcal S_B$ be the complex of all sliced PL structures on $B$,
pointed by its standard PL structure $\alpha$. Write
$Y_B=\operatorname{Sing}\operatorname{Homeo}(B)$ and
$X_B=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{PL}}(B)$. The orbit map
\[
 Y_B/X_B\longrightarrow\mathcal S_B,
 \qquad hX_B\longmapsto h_*(\alpha),                     \tag{A.136.4}
\]
is injective and identifies its image with a union of components
containing $\alpha$.
Here is why the assertion applies to a compact four-manifold, without
claiming the open four-dimensional bundle theorem. The footnote to
Kirby--Siebenmann, Essay~V, Bundle Theorem~0.1, p.~217, explicitly says
that a proper CAT submersion is a locally trivial bundle, making that
bundle assertion immediate for compact fibres in every dimension
\cite{KirbySiebenmann1977}. A sliced structure on $\Delta^q\times B$
is proper, so its PL bundle is trivial over the simplex. If its fibre
at one vertex has the original PL isomorphism type, this gives a
projection-preserving trivialization from the standard fibre and thus
a lift through (A.136.4). A prescribed horn trivialization can be
retained: its difference from the chosen trivialization is an
$X_B$-valued horn, filled because $X_B$ is a simplicial group.
This verifies the required orbit-component and relative lifting
statements. The same reasoning shows that the entire component of
$\alpha$ is captured. It does not say that every PL structure on $B$
has the original PL isomorphism type.

The quotient map $Y_B\to Y_B/X_B$ is a Kan fibration. Taking its
homotopy-fibre sequence and then looping the standard structure
component gives
\[
 F_A\simeq\Omega(\mathcal S_B,\alpha).                  \tag{A.136.5}
\]
All components of the based loop space occur in this equivalence.
The notation does not mean only its constant-loop component.

\textbf{Product collars for ordinary concordance families.}
Put $N=B\times I$. Let $P_C^{\mathrm{pc}}$ be the subgroup complex of
concordances that are the identity near the bottom and have the form
$e(h)\times\operatorname{id}$ near the top, where $e(h)$ is their top
restriction. A simplex has one common such collar.
There is a weak equivalence
$P_C^{\mathrm{pc}}\to P_C$ for $C=\mathrm{PL},\mathrm{TOP}$.
Use the embeddings $c_s:N\to B\times[s,1-s]$ from (A.135.7).
For an ordinary bottom-fixed concordance $h$ with top restriction $a$,
define
\[
 R_s(h)=
 \begin{cases}
 c_s h c_s^{-1}&\text{on }B\times[s,1-s],\\
 \operatorname{id}&\text{on }B\times[0,s],\\
 a\times\operatorname{id}&\text{on }B\times[1-s,1].
 \end{cases}                                           \tag{A.136.6}
\]
The boundary values at the inner faces are respectively the identity
and $(x,1-s)\mapsto(a(x),1-s)$, so the pieces glue exactly.
At $s=0$ this is $h$ and at positive $s$ it has uniform product collars.
The formula is continuous for TOP families and jointly PL for PL
families: $c_s$ and its inverse on the moving image are jointly PL,
and so is the family $a$. Previously existing product collars remain
product because $\sigma_s(t)\ge t$ and collar compression commutes
with $a\times\operatorname{id}$. Relative identity parameter faces
stay fixed. Thus the finite-family argument of A.135 proves the
claimed weak equivalence, including injectivity using relative
nullhomotopies. These are ordinary concordances, with their top free.

Let $\mathcal S_N^{\mathrm{pc}}$ be the space of sliced PL structures
on $N$ that are standard near the bottom and are products near the
fixed top collar, with an arbitrary induced PL structure on $B$ at the
top. It is pointed by the standard cylinder structure. The orbit map
\[
 P_{\mathrm{TOP}}^{\mathrm{pc}}/
 P_{\mathrm{PL}}^{\mathrm{pc}}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal S_N^{\mathrm{pc}}              \tag{A.136.7}
\]
again has image a union of components containing the standard one.
The compact proper-submersion theorem proves this with the required
relative and collar conditions as follows. Trivialize a simplex of
structures as a PL bundle on the compact fibre $N$. If one vertex
is in the original orbit, compose with its given PL identification
with the standard cylinder. Initially the
trivializing homeomorphism may move both boundary maps. Choose a
collar width inside the common product collars of both its source
and target structures. Compress as in (A.136.6), extending on the two
removed collars by the corresponding bottom and top boundary maps.
Here $c_s$ is PL in \emph{both} the source and target structures,
because those structures are products on the collars and $c_s$ is
supported there. The new trivialization is therefore still a PL
isomorphism from the standard source to the varying target, and is
product on both collars. Its bottom map $b_t$ is a jointly PL map
of the standard $B$, since the target structure is standard there.
Precomposing by $b_t^{-1}\times\operatorname{id}$ makes the bottom
restriction the identity, leaving a product at the top.
This proves the claimed relative trivialization for one simplex.
If it was prescribed on a horn, the difference is a horn in the
common-collar simplicial group $P_{\mathrm{PL}}^{\mathrm{pc}}$;
filling that horn corrects the trivialization exactly. This supplies
the orbit-component statement without applying the completed
boundary classification (4.4) to a four-dimensional boundary.
Consequently (A.136.6)--(A.136.7) give
\[
 F_P\simeq\Omega(\mathcal S_N^{\mathrm{pc}},\alpha_N).
                                                        \tag{A.136.8}
\]
Again only the orbit containing the base structure is used before
looping. Other isomorphism types are not silently placed in that orbit.

\textbf{The strict collar square, before classifying the boundary.}
Let
\[
 M=B\times\mathbb R,\qquad
 C_-=B\times(-\infty,0],\qquad C_+=B\times[1,\infty).
\]
Let $\mathcal S_-$ be the space of sliced PL structures on $M$ equal
to the standard structure near $C_-$. Let $\mathcal T$ be the space
of germs of sliced PL structures near $C_+$ in $M$. There is a
restriction map $r:\mathcal S_-\to\mathcal T$ and a product map
\[
 s:\mathcal S_B\longrightarrow\mathcal T,
 \qquad L\longmapsto L\times\mathbb R\text{ near }C_+.
\]

a) The natural square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathcal S_N^{\mathrm{pc}}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal S_-\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow r\\
\mathcal S_B&\xrightarrow{\ s\ }&\mathcal T
\end{array}                                             \tag{A.136.9}
\]
is a strict pullback. A product structure on the top collar extends
uniquely as that product on the whole exterior half-cylinder, while
the standard bottom extends to the negative half-cylinder. Conversely
a structure in the indicated pullback restricts to exactly such a
structure on $N$. Equality of germs near $C_+$ and compactness of
$\Delta^q\times B$ give one common top collar for each simplex.
This is the collar construction of Kirby--Siebenmann, Essay~V,
(4.1), pp.~242--243, with the bottom structure held fixed; the
four-dimensional boundary space remains unclassified.

b) The right vertical map in (A.136.9) is a Kan fibration.
The restriction theorem in Essay~V, proof of (1.4), property~(4),
pp.~224--225, and the relative formulation (3.1)--(3.2), p.~240,
apply to the boundaryless five-manifold $M$. Apply restriction to
$C_-\cup C_+$, and then take the fibre over the standard germ on
$C_-$. The two closed sets have disjoint neighbourhoods, so their
structure germ spaces split as the corresponding product. The
resulting fibre map is precisely $r$. No four-dimensional relative
extension theorem is invoked. The fibration need not be surjective
on every component of $\mathcal T$.

c) The classification of open five-manifolds, with the chosen
standard PL structure, gives
\[
 \mathcal S_-\simeq *,\qquad
 \eta:\mathcal T\simeq
 \operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,3)),                 \tag{A.136.10}
\]
where $\eta$ sends the standard germ to the constant map.
For the first assertion the relative lift space is contractible
because $C_-\hookrightarrow M$ is a collared homotopy equivalence.
For the second, the neighbourhoods $B\times(1-\varepsilon,\infty)$
are cofinal near $C_+$ for each finite family: compactness of $B$
and its parameter simplices gives a common $\varepsilon>0$ near
$B\times\{1\}$, and the entire remaining positive half-cylinder
is already contained in $C_+$. Each such neighbourhood is an open
five-manifold homotopy equivalent to $B$. The natural classification
on these open neighbourhoods, including its germ formulation
(2.2.1)--(2.3.1), makes restriction along smaller collars a weak
equivalence of the corresponding lift spaces. Passing to the
finite-family germ colimit therefore gives the mapping space in
(A.136.10).
Precisely, apply Essay~V, relative classification~(3.2) and
Theorem~5.5(I), followed by the universal Kirby--Siebenmann square
and chosen-lift untwisting of \S5.6, pp.~252--253, just as in A.135.
This uses a rank-five tangent microbundle and the standard PL lift;
it does not assert a classification of compact four-manifold PL
structures.

Since $r$ is a Kan fibration, the strict pullback (A.136.9) is a
homotopy pullback. Its upper-right corner is weakly contractible,
so it yields a based equivalence
\[
 \mathcal S_N^{\mathrm{pc}}
 \simeq\operatorname{hofib}_0
 \bigl(\eta s:\mathcal S_B\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,3))\bigr).            \tag{A.136.11}
\]
The homotopy fibre is taken at the standard germ. This statement
includes only components of $\mathcal S_B$ whose stabilized germ
can be joined to that germ, as the homotopy-fibre definition requires.

\textbf{Definition of the map and all loop shifts.}
Fix the tangent classifying maps, the standard PL lifts, and the
comparison choices in Kirby--Siebenmann (2.3.1)--(2.3.2). With these
choices define $\beta_B$ to be the composite of the based equivalence
(A.136.5) with the loop of $\eta s$:
\[
 F_A\simeq\Omega\mathcal S_B
 \xrightarrow{\Omega(\eta s)}
 \Omega\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,3))
 \simeq\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,2)).            \tag{A.136.12}
\]
The quotient-fibre equivalence is interpreted as a map of based
homotopy types, or is realized by choosing its based homotopy inverse
in CW models. Such inverses are unique up to based homotopy.
The natural classification zigzags and Complement~2.3.2 make
compatible changes of the classifying choices give identified
homotopy-fibre models. Thus (A.136.12) specifies the map up to these
based homotopies; no preferred strict point-set formula is claimed.

Looping (A.136.11), using that based loops preserve homotopy fibres,
and applying (A.136.8), now gives exactly (A.136.2). All based loops
are retained, including those representing nontrivial fundamental
group classes of a structure component. Its fibre sequence contains
\[
 \Omega\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,2))
 \simeq\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,1)),
\]
in agreement with A.135. The long exact sequence extends to
(A.136.3), with
$\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(B,K(\mathbb Z/2,2))=H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)$.

For the stated geometric component description, use the path model
of $F_A$ in which a point is a PL map $a$ together with a TOP path
$\gamma$ from the identity to $a$. Reparameterize the path to be
constant near its two ends. Its pushed-forward PL structures on $B$
form a based loop in $\mathcal S_B$, since both endpoint maps are PL.
Product with the normal line and the natural relative classification
identify the resulting cohomology class with the cylinder structure
pushed forward by $(x,u)\mapsto(\gamma_u(x),u)$. This structure is
standard near both ends. Adjunction gives the suspension isomorphism
$H^3(B\times I,B\times\partial I;\mathbb Z/2)=H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)$,
which is precisely the value of $\pi_0\beta_B$.
For completeness, the stabilized fibrewise tangent map and the full
cylinder tangent map have the same relative microbundle homotopy
class. Extend $\gamma$ constantly past the interval ends. In the
pair-coordinate model for the tangent microbundle, keep the base
$(x,u)$ mapped to $(\gamma_u(x),u)$, and send the nearby second point
$(y,v)$ to
\[
 (\gamma_{u+t(v-u)}(y),v),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
For each fixed base this is a homeomorphism germ, with inverse obtained
by using the inverse of the same $\gamma$ slice. At $t=0$ it is the
fibrewise tangent map with a trivial normal line, and at $t=1$ it is
the tangent map of the cylinder homeomorphism. It preserves the zero
section over the base homeomorphism and is constant as a germ near both ends, where $\gamma$ was
made constant. Thus the relative classes used in the two descriptions
agree.

Finally, neither (A.136.5) nor (A.136.8) claims that every structure
component lies in the automorphism orbit. The loops suffice to model
all components of the based comparison fibres. They cannot determine
TOP concordance components outside the image of PL. The map
$\beta_B$ itself, including its induced connecting maps, remains an
unevaluated part of the model in general.
\end{proof}
