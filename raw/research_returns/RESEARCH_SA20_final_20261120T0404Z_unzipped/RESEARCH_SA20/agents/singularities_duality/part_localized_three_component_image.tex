\subsection{A.145. Local support and connected-sum obstruction summands}

\begin{saresult}{A.145}{Proved under stated hypotheses}{Supported realizations of the ordinary concordance obstruction}
Let $B$ be a closed, connected real PL three-manifold; it need not be
orientable. Let $W_1,\ldots,W_s\subset B$ be disjoint compact
codimension-zero PL submanifolds. Suppose each $W_i$ is PL homeomorphic
to $C_i\setminus\operatorname{int}D^3$, where $C_i$ is a closed,
connected, oriented PL three-manifold with finite fundamental group.
Write $j_i:W_i\hookrightarrow B$. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{im}c_B
   &\supset\sum_i(j_i)_!H^2(W_i,\partial W_i;\mathbb Z/2)\\
   &=\operatorname{PD}_B^{-1}
        \left(\sum_i(j_i)_*H_1(W_i;\mathbb Z/2)\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{145.1}
\]
Every term on the right is represented by a concordance supported in
$W_i\times I$, equal to the identity in a neighborhood of its entire
spatial and time boundary.

In particular, for
$B=C_1\#\cdots\#C_s\#D$, with these $C_i$ and any closed connected
PL three-manifold $D$, the composite
\[
 \pi_0P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)\xrightarrow{\,c_B\,}H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)
 \xrightarrow{\,\operatorname{PD}_B\,}H_1(B;\mathbb Z/2)
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_i H_1(C_i;\mathbb Z/2)
 \tag{145.2}
\]
is onto. Its kernel contains the PL and every real definable image.
This specifies a quotient by that homomorphism's kernel; it does
not identify the quotient by the PL image or all TOP components.
\end{saresult}

\begin{proof}
It suffices first to treat a single $W=C\setminus\operatorname{int}D^3$.
Van Kampen gives $\pi_1W\cong\pi_1C$, since the gluing sphere is simply
connected. The finite group has periodic cohomology and satisfies
the Sylow-$SK_1$ condition by the \emph{closed} manifold $C$ and
(144.9)--(144.10). We do not apply that homology-sphere argument to
the universal cover of the punctured manifold $W$.

Choose a compatible smoothing of $W$; only one smoothing is needed,
with no independently prescribed smoothing on its boundary. Apply
the relative realization assertion proved in A.144 to
\[
       X=W\times I,\qquad Z=X\times I=W\times I^2.
\]
Corners are treated through compatible product collars, or rounded
and then transported back through such collars. The normal invariant
is relative to the full boundary of $Z$. The two suspension
identifications are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^3(X,\partial X;\mathbb Z/2)
          &\cong H^2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z/2),\\
 H^4(Z,\partial Z;\mathbb Z/2)
          &\cong H^2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z/2).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{145.3}
\]
The first normal coordinate is in
$H^2(Z,\partial Z;\mathbb Z/2)\cong
 H^0(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z/2)=0$.
This observation is optional: choosing that coordinate to be zero
already suffices in A.144.

The integral lift in degree four exists because $Z$ is oriented and
$H^5(Z,\partial Z;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. The simple surgery
obstruction of $(0,y)$ is zero by the relative HMTW formula, with
the group condition imported from $C$. The relative product
construction (144.7)--(144.8) therefore gives a TOP homeomorphism
\[
 F_W:W\times I\longrightarrow W\times I,\qquad
                  F_W|_{\partial(W\times I)}=1
 \tag{145.4}
\]
realizing any prescribed class in (145.3). In particular, it fixes
the vertical side $\partial W\times I$, as well as both time ends.
No surgery theorem has been applied to the ambient manifold $B$.

We record explicitly how to obtain support away from the full
boundary. Let $c:\partial X\times[0,\delta]\to X$ be a collar, with
depth coordinate $t$. For $0\le u\le\delta/2$ put
\[
 \sigma_u(t)=\min\{t+u,(t+\delta)/2\},\qquad
 \sigma_u^{-1}(v)=\max\{v-u,2v-\delta\},
       \quad u\le v\le\delta.
 \tag{145.5}
\]
It is an increasing PL homeomorphism $[0,\delta]\to[u,\delta]$,
with $\sigma_0(t)=t$ and $\sigma_u(\delta)=\delta$.
Use it in the collar and the identity outside to obtain a jointly
PL family of embeddings $e_u:X\to X$. On $e_u(X)$ define
$F_{W,u}=e_uF_We_u^{-1}$, and on the removed collar define
$F_{W,u}=1$. The formulas agree on their common boundary because
$F_W$ fixes $\partial X$. The inverse is given by the same
construction using $F_W^{-1}$.

For $u>0$ this map is the identity on a full collar of $\partial X$.
The pasting is continuous jointly through $u=0$: on the moving
collar interface both formulas agree, and when a point tends to the
original boundary the boundary-fixed maps $F_W$ and $F_W^{-1}$
tend to the identity there. Compactness makes this assertion uniform.
Equivalently the closed moving regions give the same pasting
argument. Thus $F_{W,u}$ is an isotopy fixed on the entire boundary,
starting at $F_W$. With corners one uses a collar of the rounded
boundary and transports the result back in compatible PL product
coordinates. It still fixes every original face pointwise. Its
relative obstruction is unchanged. Fix any $u>0$.

Extend this map by the identity on $(B\setminus\operatorname{int}W)
\times I$; call the resulting ordinary concordance $\widehat F_W$.
The collar identity makes the extension a homeomorphism with a
continuous inverse. We now check its class, not merely the support
of its representing map.

The relative mapping-cylinder PL obstruction of $\widehat F_W$ is
zero off the indicated spatial support, with the canonical zero
trivialization there. Its supported cohomology class lies in
\[
 \begin{split}
 &H^4\bigl(B\times I^2,\,
 ((B\setminus\operatorname{int}W)\times I^2)
                  \cup(B\times\partial I^2);\mathbb Z/2\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\cong
 H^4\bigl(W\times I^2,\,
     (\partial W\times I^2)\cup(W\times\partial I^2);
                          \mathbb Z/2\bigr).
 \end{split}
 \tag{145.6}
\]
The isomorphism is excision in the displayed direction, from the
supported ambient group to the local group. Naturality of the
universal relative PL obstruction identifies its image with the
obstruction of $F_{W,u}$. Forgetting the extra relative spatial
complement gives the obstruction in
$H^4(B\times I^2,B\times\partial I^2;\mathbb Z/2)$.
This is the precise zero-extension operation, with both time-square
faces and all spatial-side faces retained.

After double interval suspension, the map in (145.6) is
\[
          j_!:H^2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z/2)
                             \longrightarrow H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2).
 \tag{145.7}
\]
Mod-two Poincare--Lefschetz duality makes it equal to
$\operatorname{PD}_B^{-1}j_*\operatorname{PD}_{W,\partial W}$.
This description is valid when $B$ is nonorientable. Thus
$c_B([\widehat F_W])=j_!(\eta)$ exactly, proving the one-support
claim in (145.1).

For disjoint supports these extensions commute, and in any case
the homomorphism property of A.143 makes their obstruction classes
add. This proves the sum assertion. The connected-sum
Mayer--Vietoris decomposition gives
\[
 H_1(B;\mathbb Z/2)\cong
       \bigoplus_i H_1(C_i;\mathbb Z/2)\oplus H_1(D;\mathbb Z/2).
 \tag{145.8}
\]
Deleting a ball does not change $H_1(C_i)$, and the inclusions of
the punctured summands identify precisely the displayed summands.
The support realizations therefore prove surjectivity of (145.2).
The kernel contains the PL and real definable images by A.143.

This is a general proof of supported realization. It adds no
computer-verified finite example beyond the explicitly checked
projective-three-space base in A.142, and asserts no algorithmic
construction of the TOP homeomorphisms produced by surgery.
\end{proof}
