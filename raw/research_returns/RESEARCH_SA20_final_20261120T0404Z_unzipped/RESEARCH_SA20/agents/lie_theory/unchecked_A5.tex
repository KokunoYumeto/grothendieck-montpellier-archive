\subsection*{Outstanding A.5 gates}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Definability of the maximal open local-triviality locus for a
general proper definable map with base dimension at least three needs a
proof beyond the existence of some Hardt partition.  A5.008 proves
the dense-open and nowhere-dense-complement assertions in general,
and maximal-locus definability for curves and finitely triangulable
maps. A5.012 settles every proper map with finite fibres, and
A5.013 settles every proper map whose target has dimension at most
two, over every real closed field.  A5.014 settles every proper
map whose fibres have dimension at most one, with no restriction
on the target dimension, and also identifies the corresponding
part of the maximal locus for arbitrary maps.  The remaining gate
requires fibre dimension at least two and target dimension at
least three.
The remaining general definability statement is not inferred
from those restricted results.
\end{enumerate}
The strict-partition claim of 121/049 is no longer an open gate:
A5.009 and A5.010 refute it in all four named contexts, including
pure-dimensional complex algebraic spaces and a Zariski-open
witness.  The proposed proper-map recursion is independently
refuted by A5.002.

The exact maximal-axis TOP-B uniqueness gate is closed by A5.011.
The homeomorphism fixes the axis pointwise, while the two transverse
germs have different end obstructions.

A5.015 gives a proper polynomial global product with smooth algebraic
source and closed sphere fibres that admits no finite ambient Thom
$a_f$ stratification near its central axis. It refutes the proposed
necessity of such a stratification for local bundle recognition.
Its own maximal bundle locus is the whole base, so the original
definability gate above remains unchanged.

A5.018 closes continuous sewing once one finite approximation format is
available. A5.019 gives the exact remaining logical gate: after the
finite radial normalization, every fixed angular-homeomorphism format
has a definable witness locus, and all-model definability of the exact
locus is equivalent to covering it by one finite pool of those formats.
The pool has not been proved to exist. Neither a surface-only TOP
recognition criterion nor a finite folded-disk extension supplies it.
