\subsection{Finite radial models and the exact angular certificate gate}

\begin{saresult}{A5.019}{Proved reduction; recovered text freshly rechecked}{A finite radial normalization of the exact local-bundle locus}
Fix one continuous definably proper map $f:X\to Y$ in an o-minimal
expansion of a real closed field. Name its finitely many coefficients.
On the definable manifold locus of $Y$, its exact local definable bundle
locus, with any prescribed finite fibre-manifold filter, admits the
following reduction. There are finitely many definable scalar-certificate
families. On each admissible family the source is a fixed product
$L\times[-1,1]^m$ and the unresolved condition is the existence of
homeomorphisms of $L\times\partial[-1,1]^m$ satisfying one exact angular
equation and tending to the identity in the $L$ coordinate. In particular,
all varying PL attachment maps can be replaced by the standard projection
to $L$.

For the fixed named-coefficient complete theory, the all-model definability
assertion for this exact locus is equivalent to a finite pool of the
explicit angular formula certificates described below. This is a strict
normalization of the remaining geometry, not a proof that such a finite
pool exists. It does not close original A5 or establish the separate
rarity and privileged-recursion assertions.
\end{saresult}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{1. Fixed-map and empty-case conventions.}\quad
First let $Y=B\subset R^m$ be open, with $m\ge1$. Choose a positive
continuous definable $r(a)$ such that
$a+[-r(a),r(a)]^m\subset B$. Put
\[
 D_a=f^{-1}(a+[-r(a),r(a)]^m),\qquad
 h_a(x)=\|f(x)-a\|_\infty.
 \tag{A5.19.1}
\]
Properness makes $D_a$ definably compact. Its compactness and the displayed
scalar formula are first-order assertions in the fixed input. Empty
central fibres cause no difficulty: a continuous definably proper map is
closed, by definable curve selection and compactness of the inverse image
of a compact curve. Thus $B\setminus f(X)$ is an open definable empty-bundle
locus. We henceforth discuss nonempty central fibres. Dimension-zero
regular target points are isolated and are treated separately in Step~7.

\textbf{2. A finite list of scalar-triangulation formats.}\quad
For a finite abstract complex $K$ with $N$ vertices use its standard
realization in $R^N$, whose vertices are the standard basis vectors.
For a fixed graph formula $\varphi_i(a,z,x;b)$, let
\[
 \Gamma_i(a,b;z,x)\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 z\in|K_i|\ \wedge\ x\in D_a\ \wedge\varphi_i(a,z,x;b).
\]
The index $i$ includes the finite complex, its finite source ambient
dimension $N$, and the finite graph formula. The tuple $b$ contains all
additional coefficients. Let $\operatorname{Homeo}(\Gamma_i)$ be the
first-order assertion that this is a bijective graph and that evaluation
and inverse evaluation are continuous. Continuity is expressed by the
usual finite-tuple epsilon--delta quantifiers. Define
\[
\begin{split}
 C_i(a)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \exists b\,\exists(c_v)_{v\in K_i^{(0)}}\;
 [&\operatorname{Homeo}(\Gamma_i)\ \wedge\ c_v\ge0\text{ for all }v\\
 &\wedge\!\bigwedge_{\sigma\in K_i}\ \forall\lambda\in\Delta_\sigma\,
 \forall x\bigl(\Gamma_i(a,b;\textstyle\sum_{v\in\sigma}\lambda_vv,x)\\
 &\hspace{32mm}\Rightarrow
 h_a(x)=\textstyle\sum_{v\in\sigma}\lambda_vc_v\bigr)].
\end{split}
\tag{A5.19.2}
\]
Here $\Delta_\sigma$ is the ordinary barycentric simplex. Every quantifier
ranges over a finite tuple of field elements.

Shiota's exact scalar triangulation theorem in the inspected version~3,
\cite[Theorem~2.2(1), printed p.~6]{Shiota2010}, applies to $h_a$ in every model of the complete
named-coefficient theory. All such models remain o-minimal: for each
fixed formula, the uniform finite component bound furnished in the
original o-minimal model is a first-order sentence and transfers.
Consequently each $a$ satisfies some $C_i$ in every model.

If no finite sublist covered all centres, compactness applied to
\[
 \{a\in B\}\cup\{\neg C_i(a):i\text{ any finite certificate format}\}
\]
and the complete theory would give a model with a centre admitting no
scalar triangulation certificate. Exact scalar triangulation in that
model contradicts this. Thus a finite list suffices, in every model of
the fixed theory. The index set comprises arbitrary finite formulas and
finite complexes; no countability, decidability, or effective enumeration
is asserted.

It is convenient to retain the certificate coefficients as parameters.
Write $w=(a,b,c)$ and let $W_i$ be their definable validity set. Refining
by the finitely many zero-height vertex patterns, one has a fixed pair
$(K,L)$ and a homeomorphism
\[
 \pi_w:|K|\longrightarrow D_a,\qquad
 \ell_w=h_a\pi_w,
 \qquad L=\ell_w^{-1}(0).
 \tag{A5.19.3}
\]
The function $\ell_w$ is the affine interpolation of the $c_v$ on every
simplex. Keeping $w$ avoids any continuity assumption on a choice of
triangulations as $a$ varies. Existential quantification over $w$ later
returns the centre locus.

\textbf{3. Matched stellar bands and a finite PL pair filter.}\quad
Fix once and for all $\beta=1/4$; only $0<\beta<1/2$ is needed.
Let $\ell_0$ have vertex heights zero on $L$ and one otherwise.
If positive vertices exist, put
\[
 c_*(w)=\min_{c_v>0}c_v,\qquad \epsilon(w)=\beta c_*(w).
\]
For an original mixed face $\sigma$, let $z_\sigma$ be the barycentre of
its zero vertices and $p_\sigma$ that of its positive vertices. The point
\[
 v_\sigma(w)=
 \left(1-\frac{\epsilon(w)}{\ell_w(p_\sigma)}\right)z_\sigma+
 \frac{\epsilon(w)}{\ell_w(p_\sigma)}p_\sigma
 \tag{A5.19.4}
\]
lies in its relative interior and has height $\epsilon(w)$. Use the
analogous point with heights $(\ell_0,\beta)$ for the fixed model.
Star all original mixed faces in decreasing dimension. Faces still to
be starred remain subcomplexes. Every original zero--positive edge is
starred, so no final simplex contains both a zero vertex and an old
positive vertex. All new vertices have the chosen intermediate height.
It follows that each lower band is exactly the subcomplex on zero and
new vertices: a simplex containing an old positive vertex contributes
only its face on new vertices. The two subdivisions have the same finite
combinatorics. Their matched vertex map is therefore a PL homeomorphism
\[
 t_w:N\longrightarrow \ell_w^{-1}([0,\epsilon(w)]),
 \quad N=\ell_0^{-1}([0,\beta]),
 \quad \ell_w t_w=\frac{\epsilon(w)}\beta\ell_0,
 \quad t_w|_L=\operatorname{id}.
 \tag{A5.19.5}
\]
For fixed $w$ this is PL. Its joint dependence on $w$ is rational definable
on finitely many pieces; it is not asserted to be jointly PL in $w$.
The same argument compares any two sufficiently small positive bands.

A necessary condition for a local product at $a$ is
\[
 (N,L)\cong_{\rm PL}
 (|L|\times[-1,1]^m,|L|\times\{0\}).
 \tag{A5.19.6}
\]
Indeed, a local definable chart identifies a sufficiently small compact
band with the right-hand product as a marked pair. A smaller band than
$\epsilon(w)$ has the same PL pair type by the preceding matching.
The relative definable Hauptvermutung, Shiota Theorem~2.1, printed p.~5,
turns the resulting definable homeomorphism of compact polyhedral pairs
into a PL homeomorphism. This proves necessity, without assuming the
chosen band already lies inside the unknown chart.

There are only finitely many pairs $(K,L)$, so (A5.19.6) is a finite
labelled-pair filter. A desired fibre-manifold or nonsingularity condition
is another finite filter on $L$ and its links. These filters are
combinatorial, not differential critical-value conditions. Any required
finite PL witnesses may be chosen over the real algebraic field: the
actual finite subdivisions and affine maps give finite ordered-field
certificates, which transfer by real-closed-field completeness.
If there are no positive vertices, then $K=L$ and $N=L$; for nonempty $L$
and $m\ge1$ the pair cannot be a product by dimension. Such pieces are
therefore inadmissible.

\textbf{4. A fixed exact radial product model.}\quad
On an admissible pair choose a fixed PL homeomorphism
$P:L\times[-1,1]^m\to N$, arranged to be the identity on the central
copy of $L$. On its source the two functions
\[
 g=\ell_0P,\qquad r(z,v)=\|v\|_\infty
\]
are nonnegative PL functions after one common subdivision, with the same
zero set $L\times\{0\}$. Apply the matched-stellar-band construction to
these two functions on this fixed finite complex. For some fixed
$\eta,\delta>0$ it gives a PL homeomorphism
\[
 J:\{r\le\eta\}\longrightarrow\{g\le\delta\},
 \qquad gJ=(\delta/\eta)r,
 \qquad J|_{L\times\{0\}}=\operatorname{id}.
\]
Take $\eta\le1$ and $\delta<\beta$. Define the fixed map
\[
 \Psi(z,v)=P J(z,\eta v).
\]
Then $\Psi$ maps $L\times[-1,1]^m$ onto
$\ell_0^{-1}([0,\delta])$, fixes $L$ at zero, and satisfies
$\ell_0\Psi(z,v)=\delta\|v\|_\infty$. Therefore
\[
\begin{split}
 \rho(w)&=\epsilon(w)\delta/\beta,\\
 G_w(z,v)&=\pi_w t_w\Psi(z,v),\\
 G_w:L\times[-1,1]^m&\longrightarrow
 f^{-1}(a+[-\rho(w),\rho(w)]^m),\\
 \|f(G_w(z,v))-a\|_\infty&=\rho(w)\|v\|_\infty,
 \qquad G_w(z,0)=\pi_w(z).
\end{split}
\tag{A5.19.7}
\]
These are a fixed finite family of definable formulas. Each $G_w$ is a
homeomorphism. No joint continuity in $w$ is required or claimed.

Put $\Sigma=\partial[-1,1]^m$. For $0<t\le1$ define
\[
 \Theta_w(t,z,u)=
 \frac{f(G_w(z,tu))-a}{\rho(w)t}\in\Sigma.
 \tag{A5.19.8}
\]
The remaining exact condition is the existence, for some $0<t_0\le1$, of a
jointly continuous definable family of homeomorphisms
$A_t:L\times\Sigma\to L\times\Sigma$, $0<t\le t_0$, such that
\[
 \Theta_w(t,A_t(z,u))=u,
 \qquad
 \sup_{z,u}\|\operatorname{pr}_L A_t(z,u)-z\|\longrightarrow0
 \quad(t\downarrow0).
 \tag{A5.19.9}
\]
This is now an angular problem on a fixed product, with the standard
projection supplying the control map.

\textbf{5. Proof of the exact angular equivalence.}\quad
If $H$ is a chart near $a$, identify its central fibre with $L$ so that
$H(z,a)=\pi_w(z)$. Shrink to a closed target cube in its domain. Write
\[
 G_w^{-1}H(z,a+\rho(w)tu)=(z'(t,z,u),t u'(t,z,u)).
\]
The second coordinate has norm exactly $t$ by (A5.19.7). Consequently
$A_t=(z',u')$ is a homeomorphism of $L\times\Sigma$ and satisfies the
angular identity. Continuity of $G_w^{-1}H$ on the compact central band
makes $z'\to z$ uniformly, proving the control assertion.

Conversely, write $A_t(z,u)=(z',u')$ and define
\[
 H(z,a+\rho(w)tu)=G_w(z',tu')\quad(t>0),
 \qquad H(z,a)=\pi_w(z).
 \tag{A5.19.10}
\]
The control in (A5.19.9), boundedness of $\Sigma$, and uniform continuity
of $G_w$ give continuity at the central fibre. The angular identity gives
$fH(z,b)=b$. The homeomorphism property of $A_t$ gives bijectivity on
whole positive-radius levels, hence on each fibre and on the entire band.
On a smaller closed cube the domain is definably compact, so this
continuous definable bijection is a homeomorphism. Restrict to its
interior to obtain a local bundle chart. This proves both directions.

\textbf{6. Exact comparison with the earlier PL attachment model.}\quad
For completeness the reduction also preserves the earlier control
condition quantitatively. Let $E=\ell_0^{-1}(1/2)$ and, using barycentric
coordinates on $K$, put
\[
 q(e)=2\sum_{c_v=0}\lambda_v(e)v,\qquad
 B_w(e)=\sum_{c_v>0}c_v\lambda_v(e).
\]
Thus $q:E\to L$ is the fixed PL attachment map and $B_w\ge c_*(w)/2$.
For $0<s<c_*(w)$ define $R_{w,s}:E\to\ell_w^{-1}(s)$ by the barycentric
coordinates
\[
 \mu_v=
 \begin{cases}
 (s/B_w(e))\lambda_v(e),&c_v>0,\\
 2(1-s/(2B_w(e)))\lambda_v(e),&c_v=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{A5.19.11}
\]
This is a definable homeomorphism. If $P(x)=\sum_{c_v>0}\lambda_v(x)$,
its inverse divides the positive barycentric coordinates by $2P(x)$ and
the zero ones by $2(1-P(x))$. The inequalities
$0<P(x)\le s/c_*(w)<1$ justify both divisions. As $s\to0$,
$R_{w,s}\to q$ uniformly. On a sufficiently small band the continuous
retraction
\[
 Q(x)=\frac{\sum_{c_v=0}\lambda_v(x)v}{1-P(x)}
\]
satisfies $Q R_{w,s}=q$.

For $0<s\le\rho(w)<c_*(w)$ define, with $t=s/\rho(w)$,
\[
 T_{w,s}(z,u)=R_{w,s}^{-1}\pi_w^{-1}G_w(z,tu).
\]
Then $T_{w,s}:L\times\Sigma\to E$ is a homeomorphism, jointly continuous
for $s>0$, and
\[
 d_w(s)=\sup_{z,u}\|qT_{w,s}(z,u)-z\|\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{A5.19.12}
\]
This follows by applying $Q$ to the continuously extending radial model.
The old angular map
$\theta_w(s,e)=(f(\pi_wR_{w,s}(e))-a)/s$ satisfies
$\theta_w(s,T_{w,s}(z,u))=\Theta_w(t,z,u)$.
If an old solution is $h_s:L\times\Sigma\to E$, set
$A_t=T_{w,s}^{-1}h_s$; conversely set $h_s=T_{w,s}A_t$.
The angular equations transfer exactly. If $e_{\rm old}$ and
$e_{\rm new}$ denote the sup errors measured by $q$ and $\operatorname{pr}_L$
respectively, the triangle inequality gives
\[
 |e_{\rm old}(s)-e_{\rm new}(s/\rho(w))|\le d_w(s).
 \tag{A5.19.13}
\]
Thus neither direction loses or strengthens the required limiting control.
The rational formulas in (A5.19.11) are not asserted to be jointly PL.

\textbf{7. A general definable target has finitely many moving chart formats.}\quad
The auxiliary target maps can be kept over the original named coefficients.
For any one finite graph format used below, its validity as the stated
homeomorphism, with the stated image, markings, and center, is first-order.
Choose its coefficient tuple by parameter-preserving definable choice
from that nonempty validity set. The chosen tuple is therefore definable
over the original named coefficients, and its definition transfers to
every model of the fixed complete theory. No extra unnamed constants
are introduced by fixing the finitely many auxiliary maps.

Compactify the ambient space of $Y$ by a fixed semialgebraic homeomorphism
$\kappa:R^n\to(-1,1)^n$. Triangulate $\overline{\kappa(Y)}$ compatibly
with $\kappa(Y)$, writing
$\alpha:|K_Y|\to\overline{\kappa(Y)}$ and
$S=\alpha^{-1}(\kappa(Y))$, a union of open simplices. On each open
simplex $\operatorname{Int}\sigma\subset S$, choose its barycentre
$c_\sigma$. The stellar triangulations at $c_\sigma$ and at
$a'\in\operatorname{Int}\sigma$ have the same combinatorics. Their
vertex map gives a homeomorphism $T_{a'}:|K_Y|\to|K_Y|$, fixing all old
vertices and carrying $c_\sigma$ to $a'$. It preserves every original
simplex setwise, hence preserves $S$. Evaluation and inverse evaluation
are jointly definable and continuous as $a'$ varies in the open simplex.
In particular local manifold type is constant on it.

For each simplex in the regular manifold locus of positive local dimension
$m$ (which need not equal the dimension of that simplex), fix one definable chart
$\eta_\sigma:(-2,2)^m\to V_\sigma\subset S$, with
$\eta_\sigma(0)=c_\sigma$ and $V_\sigma$ relatively open in $S$.
The moving chart at $a=\kappa^{-1}\alpha(a')$ is
\[
 b_a(v)=\kappa^{-1}\alpha T_{a'}\eta_\sigma(v).
 \tag{A5.19.14}
\]
It is a finite definable family of target-coordinate homeomorphisms,
with $b_a(0)=a$. The image of $[-1,1]^m$ is compact; properness supplies
compact $D_a=f^{-1}(b_a([-1,1]^m))$. Apply Steps~2--6 to the scalar
$\|b_a^{-1}f\|_\infty$ on this set. All coefficients and domain
conditions are still first-order in one fixed family. Thus the same
finite-format argument applies. A product in these coordinates is
exactly a product over a relatively open neighborhood in $Y$.

If $m=0$, the regular target point is isolated, so the map over its
singleton neighborhood is already a product; retain precisely those
fibres satisfying the finite prescribed manifold filter. Empty fibres
are handled by the open complement of $f(X)$, with whichever empty-fibre
convention the locus uses. Target singular points are excluded when the
locus requires a manifold target. This completes the general-target
extension without asserting uniformity for an external family of maps
$f_u$.

\textbf{8. Explicit first-order angular certificates.}\quad
Fix one admissible finite model and let $M=L\times\Sigma$; all scalar
parameters in this step satisfy $0<t\le1$. For every
finite graph formula $\gamma_j(w,t,e,e';b)$ let $V_j(w,t,b)$ assert that
it defines a homeomorphism $A:M\to M$ satisfying
$\Theta_w(t,A(e))=\operatorname{pr}_\Sigma(e)$ for every $e$.
The maximum
\[
 E_j(w,t,b)=\max_{e\in M}
 \|\operatorname{pr}_L A(e)-\operatorname{pr}_L e\|
\]
is definable on this validity set. Put
\[
 e_j(w,t)=\inf\bigl(\{\min(1,E_j(w,t,b)):V_j(w,t,b)\}\cup\{1\}\bigr).
 \tag{A5.19.15}
\]
The sentinel $1$ prevents the absence of any map from satisfying a
zero-error condition. Define $D_j(w)$ by
\[
 \liminf_{t\downarrow0}e_j(w,t)=0.
 \tag{A5.19.16}
\]
Equivalently, for every positive error and every positive radius, there
is a smaller positive $t$ and a valid coefficient tuple with that error.
This is a first-order predicate in field variables. Since $e_j$ is a
bounded definable one-variable function, its limit exists; thus (A5.19.16)
implies $e_j(w,t)\to0$.

Definable choice now selects $b(t)$ on a sufficiently small interval with
$V_j(w,t,b(t))$ and $E_j(w,t,b(t))<e_j(w,t)+t<1$.
There is no assumption that this selection is continuous. Instead form
its compact graph fibres and apply Hardt triviality over the scalar
parameter $t$. After shortening to a terminal interval $(0,t_0)$, the
graph family is homeomorphic over that interval to $(0,t_0)\times K$
for one compact graph fibre $K$. Projection to $(t,e)$ is a continuous
bijection onto $(0,t_0)\times M$. On every closed interval strictly
inside $(0,t_0)$ it is a compact continuous definable bijection and hence
a homeomorphism. Therefore its inverse, and then evaluation of $A_t$,
are jointly continuous locally throughout the interval. Projection to
$(t,e')$ gives joint inverse evaluation in the same way. This proves
that $D_j$ supplies the single coherent family required in (A5.19.9).
Conversely, any such definable family has one finite graph formula with
a finite tuple of coefficients and therefore gives one $D_j$.

For each original centre quantify existentially over the corresponding
scalar certificate $w$ lying above it. Combining the finitely many model
indices with the arbitrary finite angular-formula index gives definable
sets, still denoted $D_j$, in the original target. Let $D_*$ collect the
separately settled empty and zero-dimensional cases with the stipulated
filters. In every model of the fixed named-coefficient theory the exact
locus is
\[
 U_f=D_*\ \cup\ \bigcup_j D_j.
 \tag{A5.19.17}
\]
This is an equality of sets; the union is not presumed definable.

\textbf{9. The exact finite-pool equivalence across all models.}\quad
If finitely many of the $D_j$ cover the union in every model, (A5.19.17)
is a definable formula for $U_f$ in every model.
Conversely suppose the exact locus is definable in every model of the
fixed complete theory. Take a $\kappa$-saturated model with
$\kappa$ larger than the language/formula-index cardinal and all the
parameters involved. Include also the finitely many parameters of a
formula defining $U_f$ in this saturated model. If no finite union of
$D_*$ and $D_j$ covered it, the type
\[
 x\in U_f,\qquad x\notin D_*,\qquad x\notin D_j\quad\text{for every }j
\]
would be finitely satisfiable and, by saturation, realized. This
contradicts (A5.19.17). Thus a finite pool covers $U_f$ in that model.
For every individual $j$, the implication
$D_j\subset D_*\cup\bigcup_{i\in I_0}D_i$ is first-order over the original
named coefficients; it no longer mentions the parameters defining $U_f$.
Completeness transfers each such implication to every model. Equation
(A5.19.17) then gives the same finite pool everywhere.

A local chart in a saturated model is itself a definable map, hence is
defined by a finite formula with finitely many additional coefficients;
that is why it belongs to the certificate indexing set. The argument does
not assert that definability in one unsaturated model alone supplies a
finite pool, and it does not assert uniformity in an external parameter
indexing different maps. The fibre-manifold and target-manifold filters
remain present throughout.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Strict reduction and remaining gate.}
The geometric reduction leaves finitely many fixed product links and the
standard projection $L\times\Sigma\to L$. It eliminates varying PL
attachment maps, arbitrary scalar vertex heights, and unrestricted local
source geometries. What remains is the finite-pool assertion for exact
angular straighteners in (A5.19.9), equivalently the first-order certificates
(A5.19.15)--(A5.19.17). Neither ordinary Hardt triviality, the homotopy
comparison A154--A156, nor contraction of a supplied chart space proves
that pool exists. Original A5 therefore remains open.
