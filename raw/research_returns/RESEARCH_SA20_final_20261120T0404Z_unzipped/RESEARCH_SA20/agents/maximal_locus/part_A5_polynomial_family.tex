\subsection{A sign-qualified real proper polynomial family with infinitely many map types}

\begin{saresult}{A5.020}{Proved method obstruction; no original A5 movement}{A proper degree-twelve real family and the necessary sign qualification}
For $m\in\mathbb R$, put $Y=x^2+y^2-1$, $A=xY-2yz$ and
$B_m=(x+my)Y-2(y-mx)z$, and define
\[
 f_m:\mathbb R^3\longrightarrow\mathbb R^3,
 \qquad f_m(x,y,z)=(A^2B_m^2,Y,z).
\]
Every $f_m$ is proper and has degree twelve. The maps $f_m$ and $f_{-m}$
are polynomially right-left equivalent, whereas the subfamily $m>0$
is pairwise topologically right-left inequivalent. In particular the
subfamily $m>1$ already has infinitely many topological map types.
This corrects the all-distinct-real-parameters wording attached to the
displayed family in Jelonek's original introduction. Its exceptional
circle fibre has nearby finite fibres, so this is neither a constant-fibre
counterexample nor a counterexample to original A5.
\end{saresult}

\begin{proof}

\textbf{Source and scope.}\quad
The complete original Z.~Jelonek, \emph{The Thom conjecture for proper
polynomial mappings}, arXiv:1404.7463v4 (8 February 2015), was reopened
at \url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.7463}. Printed p.~2 displays the real
family below and asserts inequivalence for all distinct real parameters.
That last assertion needs a sign qualification. The paper's positive
Theorem~3.5 concerns complex smooth irreducible affine varieties;
it does not prove real or arbitrary definable map-germ finiteness.
The proof below is a direct verification for the displayed real family,
not an attribution of these missing details to that source.

Normalize the source's fixed nonzero radius to one and put
\[
\begin{split}
 Y(x,y,z)&=x^2+y^2-1,\qquad Z(x,y,z)=z,\\
 A(x,y,z)&=xY-2yz,\\
 B_m(x,y,z)&=(x+my)Y-2(y-mx)z,\\
 f_m(x,y,z)&=(A(x,y,z)^2B_m(x,y,z)^2,Y,z).
\end{split}
\tag{J.1}
\]
These are degree-twelve polynomial maps $\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$.
Each is proper: bounds on the last two output coordinates bound $z$
and $x^2+y^2=1+Y$, so the inverse image of a compact set is closed and
bounded. The joint map $(m,x,y,z)\mapsto(m,f_m(x,y,z))$ is proper by
the same argument.

\textbf{The exact sign equivalence.}\quad
Let
\[
 \phi(x,y,z)=(x,-y,-z),\qquad
 \psi(X,Y,Z)=(X,Y,-Z).
\]
Direct substitution gives
\[
 A\phi=A,\qquad B_m\phi=B_{-m},\qquad
 \psi\circ f_m\circ\phi=f_{-m}.
\tag{J.2}
\]
Thus the literal all-distinct-real-parameters claim cannot hold under
unrestricted right-left topological equivalence.

\textbf{A rigorous infinite family.}\quad
The subfamily $m>0$ is pairwise topologically inequivalent. We give
all details, including an intrinsic choice of the relation used to
distinguish its members.

Fix $m>0$, and write $d=\sqrt{1+m^2}>1$ and
$\alpha=\arctan m\in(0,\pi/2)$. Trigonometric notation in this
topological proof is used only to describe a circle; it is not asserted
to belong to the chosen definable language. For fixed output coordinates
$Y>-1$ and $Z$, let
\[
 r=\sqrt{1+Y},\qquad R=\sqrt{Y^2+4Z^2}.
\]
When $R>0$, choose $\varphi$ modulo $2\pi$ with
$Y=R\cos\varphi$ and $2Z=R\sin\varphi$, and write
$(x,y)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)$. With $s=\theta+\varphi$,
\[
 A=rR\cos s,\qquad
 B_m=rR(\cos s+m\sin s)=rRd\cos(s-\alpha).
\]
Consequently
\[
 A B_m=\frac{r^2R^2}{2}\{1+d\cos(2s-\alpha)\},
\quad
 X=\frac{r^4R^4}{4}\{1+d\cos(2s-\alpha)\}^2.
\tag{J.3}
\]
The image at these fixed $(Y,Z)$ is the whole interval $[0,M_m(Y,Z)]$,
where
\[
 M_m(Y,Z)=\frac{(1+Y)^2(Y^2+4Z^2)^2(1+d)^2}{4}.
\tag{J.4}
\]
Indeed the continuous factor in braces ranges from $1-d<0$ to
$1+d$, and $(1+d)^2>(1-d)^2$. The maximum in (J.4) is attained
exactly when $\cos(2s-\alpha)=1$, giving two distinct points on the
source circle. The value $X=0$ is attained exactly where $A=0$ or
$B_m=0$. These are four distinct points because
$0<\alpha<\pi/2$.

In a target neighborhood with $Y>-1/2$, the boundary of the image of
$f_m$ therefore consists of the two sheets
\[
 S_{0,m}=\{(0,Y,Z)\},\qquad
 S_{+,m}=\{(M_m(Y,Z),Y,Z)\},
\tag{J.5}
\]
meeting only at the origin. Away from the origin, the first sheet has
four-point fibres and the second has two-point fibres. Hence the germ
of $S_{0,m}\setminus\{0\}$ is intrinsically specified by the boundary
of the map's image and fibre cardinality four. A target homeomorphism
in a map equivalence preserves both of these properties.

The origin has the fibre
\[
 C=f_m^{-1}(0,0,0)=\{x^2+y^2=1,\ z=0\}.
\tag{J.6}
\]
It is the unique positive-dimensional fibre. To check this globally,
if $Y<-1$ the fibre is empty, if $Y=-1$ it has at most one point,
and if $Y>-1$ and $R>0$ then (J.3) is a nonconstant trigonometric
polynomial on a circle and every level is finite. The remaining case
$R=0$ is $Y=Z=0$, giving (J.6). Thus every global map equivalence
must carry the origin to the origin and $C$ to $C$.

Let $\mathcal R_m\subset C\times C$ be the set of limits of pairs
$(p_i,q_i)$ such that $f_m(p_i)=f_m(q_i)$ lies in the punctured lower
boundary sheet, and the common image tends to the origin. Equivalently,
take the closure of the corresponding equal-fibre relation and intersect
with $C\times C$. The preceding intrinsic characterization shows that
any map equivalence induces one circle homeomorphism $h:C\to C$ with
\[
 (h\times h)(\mathcal R_m)=\mathcal R_{m'}.
\tag{J.7}
\]

Identify $C$ with angles modulo $2\pi$. For a fixed direction
$\varphi$, the four points of the lower-boundary fibre have angles
\[
 \pi/2-\varphi,\quad 3\pi/2-\varphi,\quad
 \pi/2+\alpha-\varphi,\quad 3\pi/2+\alpha-\varphi.
\]
Their pairwise differences belong to
$\{0,\pi,\alpha,-\alpha,\pi+\alpha,\pi-\alpha\}$.
Conversely, every angle and every listed difference is realized by
choosing that direction and letting $R\downarrow0$, so
\[
 \mathcal R_m=\bigcup_{\beta\in
 \{0,\pi,\alpha,-\alpha,\pi+\alpha,\pi-\alpha\}}
                  \operatorname{graph}(R_\beta),
\tag{J.8}
\]
where $R_\beta$ denotes rotation of $C$ through $\beta$.
The six graphs are pairwise disjoint circles, hence precisely the
connected components of this relation.

It follows from (J.7) that $hR_\alpha h^{-1}$ is one of the six
rotations in (J.8) for $m'$. We recall the elementary angle invariant
to avoid an unspoken orientation convention. A lift $H:\mathbb R\to
\mathbb R$ of a circle homeomorphism has
$H(t+1)=H(t)+\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$.
If it conjugates rotations of normalized angles $a,b$, then for one
integer $k$,
\[
 H(t+a)=H(t)+b+k.
\]
Iterating this identity and using boundedness of $H(t)-\varepsilon t$
on the whole line gives $\varepsilon a=b+k$. Thus conjugate rotations
have angles equal up to sign modulo $2\pi$. Since both
$\alpha,\alpha'$ lie strictly between $0$ and $\pi/2$, the only
possible term in the target list is $\alpha'$ or $-\alpha'$.
Therefore $\alpha=\alpha'$ and $m=m'$. This proves the claimed
infinite family, including the subfamily $m>1$.

\end{proof}

\textbf{What this does not say about A5.}\quad
The exceptional fibre (J.6) is one-dimensional and nearby fibres are
finite. Thus the exceptional target value is never a bundle point,
for any parameter. This example refutes a general finiteness premise
for proper real polynomial map types; it supplies no nondefinable
maximal local-bundle locus and no constant-fibre counterexample.

\textbf{Separate pointed-germ source.}\quad
The complete original M.~Kwieci\'nski, \emph{Remarks on the local
geometry of analytic maps}, RIMS K\^oky\^uroku 1033 (1998), 140--147,
was independently reopened at
\url{https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/1033-14.pdf}.
Example~7.1 and Proposition~7.4 on printed p.~145, with the proof on
p.~146, use
\[
 X=\{x_1x_2=0\}\subset\mathbb C^4,\qquad
 f(x_1,x_2,z,t)=(x_1+x_2,(x_1+t x_2)z,t).
\]
At $(0,0,0,t)$ the intrinsic limiting relation is
$(z-z')(z-tz')(tz-z')=0$. For unit parameters the proof distinguishes
rotation angles up to inversion; it explicitly assumes one irrational
angle and excludes the equal and inverse cases. Restriction to a fixed
compact semialgebraic neighborhood containing a small unit-parameter
arc preserves these germs and makes the restriction proper. This is
a rigorous obstruction to general finite pointed map-germ types,
again with a positive fibre-dimension jump and no A5 counterexample.
