\section*{Torus correspondences: two necessary scope checks}

This note concerns the proposed use of Kronecker-foliation slopes or torus
translations to encode the local-bundle locus of one fixed proper
semialgebraic map. The arguments below are over the real field. They do not
exclude every possible dynamical encoding.

\textbf{Openness excludes one countable orbit as the exact centre test.}
For any continuous map $f:X\to B$, let $U_f$ be the set of target points
having an open neighbourhood on which $f$ is a locally trivial bundle in
the stipulated category. The set $U_f$ is open: every such neighbourhood
consists entirely of bundle points, by restricting the same chart.
Consequently, for any continuous centre map $y:J\to B$ from a
nondegenerate interval,
$y^{-1}(U_f)$ is open in $J$. For a fixed real slope $\alpha$, the set
\[
 \mathcal O_\alpha=
 \left\{\frac{a\alpha+b}{c\alpha+d}:
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb Z),\
 c\alpha+d\ne0\right\}
\]
is countable. Thus a nonempty subset of $\mathcal O_\alpha$ cannot be an
open subset of $\mathbb R$. In particular, on a nondegenerate interval meeting the
orbit, the equivalence
$y(s)\in U_f\Longleftrightarrow s\in\mathcal O_\alpha$ is impossible.
This argument uses no definability of $U_f$. A predicate for a collection
of separate maps $f_s$ need not be open in $s$; replacing that collection
by a single joint map imposes an additional condition and cannot silently
preserve this orbit test. An encoding relevant to a nondefinable open
locus must instead produce an open parameter set.

\textbf{Intrinsic finite correspondences disappear at a torus product.}
Let $F$ be a two-torus and let $\pi:F\times B\to B$ be the product
projection near $b\in B$. Suppose an assignment to unmarked map germs
associates a relation $R_{f,b}\subseteq F_b\times F_b$, and suppose that
every map-germ isomorphism carries its relation to the relation of the
isomorphic germ. Every homeomorphism $h:F\to F$ gives the product-germ
automorphism $h\times\mathrm{id}_B$. Hence $R_{\pi,b}$ is invariant under
the diagonal action of all homeomorphisms of $F$.

That action has exactly two orbits on $F\times F$: the diagonal and the
set of ordered distinct pairs. Here is a direct proof of the only
nontrivial assertion. Given ordered pairs $(x,y)$ and $(x',y')$ with
distinct entries, first use a torus translation to carry $x$ to $x'$.
Its image of $y$ is distinct from $x'$. The punctured torus
$F\setminus\{x'\}$ is path connected. Choose a path from that image to
$y'$ and subdivide it into finitely many portions lying in coordinate
disks whose closures avoid $x'$. Each required short move is realized by
a homeomorphism supported in its disk: choose a convex polygon in the
disk containing both points in its interior, cone its boundary triangulation from the
first point and from the second, and map corresponding triangles
affinely. This fixes the polygon boundary and extends by the identity.
Composing these finitely many maps fixes $x'$ and moves the second point
to $y'$. For the usual finite PL torus model the path and disks can be
chosen piecewise linear, so these automorphisms can be semialgebraic.

It follows that an invariant relation is a union of the two stated
orbits. If the relation is closed and reflexive, it is either the
diagonal or all of $F\times F$. In particular, a proper finite-valued
closed reflexive correspondence containing a nontrivial translation
graph cannot be an intrinsic relation at a product germ. Changing the
basis by $\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb Z)$ does not alter this obstruction.
This conclusion is conditional on intrinsic invariance: an externally
marked foliation or correspondence is additional data, and its
classification does not alone classify unmarked local bundles.

\textbf{The unrestricted equal-fibre limit relation is already full.}
Let $b\in B$, assume that $f$ is open at every point of $F_b$, and let
$D\subseteq B\setminus\{b\}$ have $b$ in its closure. For any
$x,y\in F_b$ and every $n\ge1$, openness at $x$ and $y$ supplies a
neighbourhood of $b$ contained in both
$f(B_{1/n}(x)\cap X)$ and $f(B_{1/n}(y)\cap X)$. Choose
$b_n\in D$ in that neighbourhood with $\|b_n-b\|<1/n$, and choose
$x_n,y_n$ in the indicated source balls with
$f(x_n)=f(y_n)=b_n$. Then $x_n\to x$ and $y_n\to y$.
Thus every ordered pair in $F_b\times F_b$ occurs as a limit of a pair
of points in a common nearby fibre. To obtain a proper finite relation,
one must restrict the approaching points by further conditions. At a
bundle germ any such conditions claimed to be intrinsic must satisfy
the symmetry test above.

These observations explain why finite-fibre rotation-modulus examples
cannot simply be padded with an unmarked torus and interpreted as a
constant-fibre counterexample to A5. They neither settle the finite
angular-certificate pool nor rule out a different construction with an
open nondefinable stable set.
