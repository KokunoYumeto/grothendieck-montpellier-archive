\subsection*{A finite pool for coherent scalar near-homeomorphisms}

This note concerns a supplied family of maps and one scalar approximation
parameter. It does not solve the exact angular projection constraint in
A5.019. Throughout, definable means definable in a fixed o-minimal expansion
of a real closed field, with parameters allowed. Compact means definably
compact unless the field is explicitly the real field.

\textbf{The coherent approximation predicate.}
Let $F\subset R^r$ and $G\subset R^s$ be compact definable sets, and let
$q_a:F\to G$, $a\in A$, be a definable family of continuous surjections.
Joint continuity in $a$ is not assumed. Say that $q_a$ has property $\mathcal N$
if there are $\epsilon>0$ and a jointly definable continuous family
$h_t:F\to G$, $0<t\leq\epsilon$, of homeomorphisms such that
\[
 \lim_{t\to0+}\sup_{x\in F}\|h_t(x)-q_a(x)\|=0.                 \tag{NH.1}
\]
This means one finite formula for the entire family. We do not substitute
the weaker infinitary assertion that for each tolerance some homeomorphism,
possibly in an unrelated formula, exists. The inverse family is jointly
continuous: restrict the parameter to a compact interval about any positive
time and use the compact definable continuous-bijection theorem.

\textbf{Claim.}
The set of $a$ with property $\mathcal N$ is definable. Moreover, there is
one finite pool of formula formats which supplies a homeomorphism
$F\to G$ within any prescribed positive uniform error of every $q_a$ in
this set. This assertion, for the fixed named-coefficient family, holds in
every model of its complete theory. The choice of approximants need not be
continuous in $a$.

If $A$ is empty there is nothing to prove. If $F=G=\varnothing$, the unique
maps satisfy the assertion trivially. Surjectivity excludes the two
incompatible empty cases when $A\ne\varnothing$. We henceforth suppose
that both sets are nonempty.

\textbf{1. A typed scalar cylinder and necessity.}
For one $a$ set
\[
 C_a=\{(t,tx,q_a(x)):x\in F,\ 0<t\leq1\}
       \ \cup\ \{(0,0,y):y\in G\}.                              \tag{NH.2}
\]
This is a compact definable set. Boundedness follows from compactness of
$F,G$. Its closure at height zero is contained in the displayed central
copy of $G$, and this copy is included; closure at a positive height follows
from compactness of $F$ and continuity of $q_a$. Write $p:C_a\to[0,1]$
for the first coordinate and $G_0=p^{-1}(0)$.

If (NH.1) holds, shrink $\epsilon\leq1$ and extend $h_t$ constantly for
$t\in[\epsilon,1]$. Define
\[
 \Phi:G\times[0,1]\longrightarrow C_a,\qquad
 \Phi(y,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 (t,t h_t^{-1}(y),q_a(h_t^{-1}(y))),&t>0,\\
 (0,0,y),&t=0.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{NH.3}
\]
At positive height this is a bijective homeomorphism onto that level, and
at zero it is the identity under the identification with $G$. Its continuity
at zero follows from boundedness of $F$ and the exact equality
\[
 \sup_{y\in G}\|q_a(h_t^{-1}(y))-y\|
       =\sup_{x\in F}\|q_a(x)-h_t(x)\|\longrightarrow0.          \tag{NH.4}
\]
Thus $\Phi$ is a continuous definable bijection from a compact definable
set to a Hausdorff definable set, hence a definable homeomorphism. This
argument is valid over every real closed field; ordinary sequential
compactness is not used. The cylinder pair $(C_a,G_0)$ is therefore
definably homeomorphic to $(G\times[0,1],G\times\{0\})$.

Take any exact scalar triangulation
\[
 \tau:|K|\longrightarrow C_a,\qquad g=p\tau\text{ PL},\qquad
 |L|=g^{-1}(0).                                                   \tag{NH.5}
\]
Relative definable Hauptvermutung applied to the preceding compact pair
homeomorphism says that $(|K|,|L|)$ is PL homeomorphic to a triangulated
product pair. In particular $|L|$ has a PL collar in $|K|$.

\textbf{2. Sufficiency uses the actual height.}
Conversely, suppose the zero subpolyhedron in (NH.5) has a PL collar.
Lemma 1 of A5.016, equation (A5.16.1), applies to arbitrary compact
polyhedra, with no manifold assumption. It gives $\delta>0$ and a PL
homeomorphism
\[
 c:|L|\times[0,\delta]\longrightarrow g^{-1}([0,\delta]),
 \qquad c(z,0)=z,\qquad g(c(z,t))=t.                              \tag{NH.6}
\]
This is the essential leveling step. Projecting an unlevelled arbitrary
pair homeomorphism would not suffice.

Let $\ell:G\to|L|$ be the inverse of $\tau|_{|L|}$ after identifying
$G_0$ with $G$. For $t>0$ write
\[
 \tau(c(\ell(y),t))=(t,t k_t(y),q_a(k_t(y))).                    \tag{NH.7}
\]
The formula defines a jointly continuous definable family of homeomorphisms
$k_t:G\to F$: each height level of (NH.2) is homeomorphic to $F$ by
projection of its second coordinate followed by division by $t$.
Compactness and continuity of the left side on $G\times[0,\delta]$ imply
\[
 \sup_{y\in G}\|q_a(k_t(y))-y\|\longrightarrow0.                \tag{NH.8}
\]
The inverse family $h_t=k_t^{-1}$ is jointly continuous at positive times,
and (NH.4), now read in reverse, proves (NH.1). We have proved the exact
equivalence between $\mathcal N$ and the PL-collar label of any scalar
triangulation pair (NH.5).

\textbf{3. Finite formats in every model.}
Name the finitely many coefficients defining $F,G,A,q$ and let $T$ be the
resulting complete theory. For every model of $T$ and every $a\in A$,
Shiota's exact scalar triangulation theorem supplies (NH.5). Enumerate all
finite standard abstract pairs $(K_i,L_i)$, their ambient dimensions, and
all finite formula formats for $\tau$ and its auxiliary coefficients.
The condition $C_i(a)$ that one such certificate exists is first order:
it asserts the homeomorphism, the cylinder and zero-set identities, and
linearity of $p\tau$ on every simplex with a vertex-height tuple $v$.
The zero vertices are exactly those of $L_i$; all other vertex heights
are positive. Every $a$ in every model satisfies some $C_i$. Compactness
therefore gives a finite sublist covering all of $A$ in all models of $T$.
This argument uses an arbitrary set of finite formulas; no countability
or decidability of the language or of the labels is assumed.

Label a standard pair positive if $|L_i|$ has a PL collar in $|K_i|$.
This label is independent of the real closed field. Any one PL collar
has finite triangulations, affine pieces, and an ordered-field formula
asserting its embedding, its restriction to the bottom, and openness of
the image of the half-open product near that bottom. A witness over one
real closed field gives a witness over the real algebraic field by
completeness of real closed fields, and such a witness transfers to every
real closed field. Thus a positive pair admits one fixed collar over the
real algebraic field. Steps 1--2 show in every model that
\[
 \{a:q_a\text{ has }\mathcal N\}
       =\bigcup_{i\text{ positive}} C_i.                         \tag{NH.9}
\]

For clarity the finite-format conclusion needs a little more than (NH.9).
Fix the algebraic collar for each positive pair. Choose a fixed common
refinement making this collar, the domain product height, and every
height $g_v$ linear on the relevant simplices. Such a common refinement
is fixed because $g_v$ is already linear on the fixed complex $K_i$;
only its vertex values move. Take the compact complement of the image
of a fixed open half-collar. It is disjoint from $|L_i|$, so the minimum
of $g_v$ on it is positive. Choose the working height $e(v)>0$ less than
this minimum and less than every positive vertex height in the domain
and range refinements, and less than the fixed collar height. A finite
minimum, followed by multiplication by $1/2$, is one definable choice.

The matched-stellar construction of A5.16.1 can be made uniformly by
explicit rational choices. On a mixed face with $l$ positive vertices
of respective heights $v_p$ and $k$ zero vertices, put barycentric weight
\[
 \frac{e(v)}{l v_p}\quad\hbox{at positive vertex }p,
 \qquad
 \frac{1-\sum_p e(v)/(l v_p)}{k}
       \quad\hbox{at each zero vertex}.                         \tag{NH.10}
\]
All weights are positive, sum to one, and have weighted height $e(v)$.
Apply the same recipe in the domain using its fixed product heights.
Star all original mixed faces in decreasing dimension. The finite paired
subdivisions are combinatorially identical. Every zero--positive original
edge has been starred, so the respective $[0,e(v)]$ bands are exactly
the zero-and-new-vertex subcomplexes. The simplicial matching preserves
their heights. This is precisely the proof of (NH.6), now in one fixed
definable format as $v$ varies; no growing mesh or rational approximation
to moving coefficients is used.

Select a certificate and its coefficients on each member of the finite
cover by definable choice. Formulas (NH.7) and (NH.10) give one finite
pool of coherent inverse approximants, with no continuity in $a$ asserted.
For every $a$ in (NH.9) and $\eta>0$, (NH.8) and definable choice supply
a time in its positive collar interval with uniform error less than
$\eta$. Taking inverses gives the asserted finite pool of homeomorphism
approximants. This completes the claim.

\textbf{4. Fixed-format limits and the conditional matcher estimate.}
Walsberg, \emph{On the Topology of Metric Spaces Definable in o-minimal
expansions of fields}, arXiv:1510.07291v2, Lemma 2.8 and Corollary 2.9,
printed pp.~5--6, constructs a definable family of all uniform limits
along definable parameter paths of any fixed definable family of functions.
The result is over arbitrary o-minimal real closed fields. Over the real
field it also contains exactly the uniform sequential limits. For vectors
one applies the scalar statement to the disjoint union of finitely many
copies of the domain and filters for simultaneous approximation. Fact 6.1,
pp.~13--14, and Proposition 6.2, p.~14, give the corresponding definable
completion. These statements were freshly read in the full original at
\url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.07291}; no copy was downloaded inside the
authored project. They give limit maps, not projection-preserving anchors.

Here is the precise conditional use. Suppose $r_t:F\to X_t$ is a
jointly continuous definable family of homeomorphisms onto compact
subsets of one fixed Euclidean space,
$r_t\to q:F\to X_0$ uniformly, and a genuine joint definable bundle
chart $H_t:X_0\to X_t$ satisfies $H_t\to\mathrm{id}_{X_0}$ uniformly.
Then $H_t^{-1}r_t:F\to X_0$ is a coherent approximation to $q$, since
\[
 \sup_{z\in X_t}\|H_t^{-1}(z)-z\|
       =\sup_{y\in X_0}\|y-H_t(y)\|\longrightarrow0.            \tag{NH.11}
\]
The scalar pool applies to $q$. If $a_s:F\to X_0$ is one of its
approximants, the matcher $r_ta_s^{-1}:X_0\to X_t$ satisfies
\[
 \sup_{y\in X_0}\|r_ta_s^{-1}(y)-y\|
 \leq \|r_t-q\|_\infty+\|a_s-q\|_\infty.                      \tag{NH.12}
\]
This is a bound for supplied limit maps on known bundle germs. It does
not manufacture the chart $H_t$ used to prove positivity of the limit.

For the spherical blow-up of A5.019 the ambient points carry both a
fibre coordinate and an angular coordinate $u\in\Sigma$. Applying
(NH.12) in that space gives matchers close to the identity in both
coordinates, but does not make their angular coordinate exactly $u$.
The identity $\Theta_tA_t=\operatorname{pr}_{\Sigma}$ remains an
additional constraint. For ordinary fibres, independent pointwise
matchers also do not by themselves supply coherent choices across
different Hardt pieces with varying target fibres. A5.018 sews a
bounded family of maps with fixed source and target; that hypothesis
must be established before using it here.
