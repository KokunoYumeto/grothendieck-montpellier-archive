\subsection*{Unnumbered real TOP recognition criterion for two-sphere fibres}

\textbf{Proposition.}\quad
Let $f:X\to B$ be a continuous proper definable map in a fixed
 o-minimal expansion of $\mathbb R$, where $X\subset\mathbb R^N$
 and $B\subset\mathbb R^m$ is open. The set of points admitting an
 ordinary topological local bundle chart with fibre $S^2$ is definable.
 More precisely, the finite and first-order conditions below define a
 set $G\subset B$ for which this exact ordinary TOP locus is
 $\operatorname{Int}_B G$.
 This assertion does not claim that the resulting charts are definable.

\begin{proof}
\textbf{The finite fibre-type condition.}\quad
Definable Hardt triviality partitions $B$ into finitely many definable
sets on each of which the fibres are definably homeomorphic to one fixed
fibre. Select exactly those pieces whose fixed fibre is topologically
homeomorphic to $S^2$, and denote their union by $S$. This is a definable
set, since only finitely many fixed fibre types are involved. No
algorithm deciding an arbitrary topological fibre type is needed for
this finite selection. Fibres over $S$ are nonempty compact surfaces.

\textbf{Two first-order local conditions.}\quad
For $x\in X$, let $O(x)$ be the assertion
\[
 \begin{split}
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\eta>0\ \forall b\in B\quad
 \|b-f(x)\|_2<\eta\ \Longrightarrow\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\\
 \exists u\in X\quad f(u)=b\quad\hbox{and}\quad
                                  \|u-x\|_2<\varepsilon.
 \end{split}
 \tag{S2.1}
\]
This is exactly the statement that $f$ is open at $x$.

For $b\in B$, $x\in X$, and $\varepsilon>0$, put
\[
 F_{b,x,\varepsilon}=f^{-1}(b)\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x).
\]
There is a definable relation
$\mathcal C(b,x,\varepsilon;u,v)$ saying that $u,v$ lie in the same
connected component of $F_{b,x,\varepsilon}$. To see this, apply Hardt
triviality to the definable family $F_{b,x,\varepsilon}$ over its
parameter space. On each of finitely many parameter pieces the fibre
is homeomorphic to a fixed definable set with finitely many connected
components. Membership in the same corresponding component is therefore
definable, and the finite union of these relations gives $\mathcal C$.
Over the real field these definable connected components are the
ordinary topological connected components.

Let $E(x)$ be the assertion
\[
 \begin{split}
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta\ (0<\delta\le\varepsilon)\quad
 \forall b\in B\ \forall u,v\in X:\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\\
 [f(u)=f(v)=b, \|u-x\|_2<\delta,\ \|v-x\|_2<\delta]
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal C(b,x,\varepsilon;u,v).
 \end{split}
 \tag{S2.2}
\]
Thus sufficiently close points in one fibre are connected in the
prescribed ambient ball. Define
\[
 G=\{b\in S:\ \forall x\in f^{-1}(b),\ O(x)\ \hbox{and}\ E(x)\}.
 \tag{S2.3}
\]
All the displayed quantifiers range over finite tuples of real field
elements, and $\mathcal C$ is definable, so $G$ and its interior are
definable.

\textbf{Necessity on an entire bundle neighborhood.}\quad
Suppose $a$ has a TOP chart
\[
 H:S^2\times V\longrightarrow f^{-1}(V),\qquad fH(z,b)=b,
\]
where $V$ is open in $B$ and contains $a$. Every fibre over $V$ is a
sphere, and projection in a product is open, proving $O(x)$ for each
$x\in f^{-1}(V)$.

Fix such an $x=H(z_0,b_0)$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Choose a small coordinate
disc $D\subset S^2$ about $z_0$ and a neighborhood $W\subset V$ of
$b_0$ such that $H(D\times W)\subset B_\varepsilon(x)$. Choose a
smaller coordinate disc $D_0$ with closure in $D$ and a smaller
neighborhood $W_0$ of $b_0$ inside $W$. The set $H(D_0\times W_0)$
is a relative neighborhood of $x$ in $X$. Thus some $\delta>0$ has
\[
 B_\delta(x)\cap X\subset H(D_0\times W_0).
\]
Two points in this ball and the same fibre correspond to points of
$D_0$ with one common parameter in $W_0$. An arc inside the coordinate
disc joins them, and its image lies in $B_\varepsilon(x)$ in that
same fibre. This proves $E(x)$. Consequently $V\subset G$, so
$a\in\operatorname{Int}_B G$.

\textbf{Sufficiency and every Dyer--Hamstrom hypothesis.}\quad
Take $a\in\operatorname{Int}_B G$, with $m\ge1$, and choose a closed
cube $C\subset\operatorname{Int}_B G$ with $a$ in its interior.
Let $X_C=f^{-1}(C)$ and $f_C=f|_{X_C}$. Properness makes $X_C$ compact,
hence a complete metric space in its inherited Euclidean metric.
The cube $C$ is a finite-dimensional compact, contractible, locally
compact, separable metric $m$-cell. The map is onto, and every fibre
is a topological two-sphere, because $C\subset S$.

The map $f_C$ is open in the relative topology. Indeed, at each
$x\in X_C$ condition $O(x)$ supplies a target neighborhood covered by
any sufficiently small source neighborhood. Intersect that target
neighborhood with $C$; a preimage of its point automatically belongs
to $X_C$. This proves relative openness even at the boundary of $C$.

Condition $E(x)$ supplies the arc condition in Dyer--Hamstrom's
Definition~5. In fact, an open subset of a two-sphere is locally
path connected, and each of its connected components is arc connected.
For $b\in C$, the set $f^{-1}(b)\cap B_\varepsilon(x)$ is such an open
subset. Therefore membership in the component asserted by (S2.2)
gives an arc joining the two points inside that fibre and ball.
For coincident points the assertion is immediate. Thus $f_C$ is
0-regular in precisely the sense of the original Definition~5.

Dyer--Hamstrom's Theorem~6, printed pp.~114--115, makes this map
completely regular, since the fixed fibre is a two-sphere. Their
Theorem~7, including its explicit two-sphere over an $n$-cell clause
on printed p.~115, then gives a homeomorphism
\[
 X_C\ \cong\ C\times S^2
\]
commuting with projection to $C$; the proof continues on p.~116.
Equivalently, after Theorem~6 one may use their Theorem~5's cell-base
conclusion. Restriction to the interior of $C$ is the required local
TOP chart at $a$.

If $m=0$, each nonempty target component is an isolated point, and its
inverse image is already a product over that singleton. The sphere
filter gives the asserted locus directly. An empty target gives an
empty locus; empty fibres are excluded from $S$ and are not sphere
bundles. This completes the equality
\[
 U_{\mathrm{TOP},S^2}(f)=\operatorname{Int}_B G.
 \tag{S2.4}
\]
\end{proof}
