\subsection{A.155. Real comparison ranges and transfer of higher surjectivity}

\begin{saresult}{A.155}{Proved under stated hypotheses}{Real weak equivalences and higher surjectivity in theories with a real model}
Over any fixed o-minimal expansion of the real field, let \(B\) be a
compact closed PL manifold of dimension at most \(2\), or of dimension
at least \(5\). Then both maps in
\[
 P_{\mathrm{PL}}(B)\longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{def}}(B)
       \longrightarrow P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)
 \tag{155.1}
\]
are weak homotopy equivalences.

More generally, let \(M\) be an o-minimal expansion of a real closed
field whose complete theory has an o-minimal model expanding the real
field. For every compact closed PL manifold \(B\) over \(M\), the map
\[
 \pi_kP_{\mathrm{PL}}(B)\longrightarrow\pi_kP_{\mathrm{def}}(B)
 \tag{155.2}
\]
is surjective for every \(k\geq1\), and it is bijective for \(k=0\).
In particular this surjectivity statement applies to the semialgebraic
category over every real closed field.
The transfer assertion here is a surjectivity assertion; no transfer
of positive-degree injectivity is inferred from a path of PL
coefficient data.
\end{saresult}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{The real comparison triangle.}\quad
A.154 proves surjectivity of the first map of (155.1) on all positive
homotopy groups, and A.113 supplies its component bijection.
The composite from PL to TOP is a weak equivalence in the stated
dimensions: use A.134 in dimensions at most \(2\), and A.113 in
dimensions at least \(5\). If a PL homotopy class vanishes after
mapping to the definable space, it also vanishes after mapping to
TOP, and hence was zero. Thus the first map is also injective on
all positive homotopy groups. The composite and the first map being
isomorphisms, the second map is as well. The same conclusion holds
at every component, using translation and the component bijection.
This argument does not use the ordinary automorphism comparison in
dimension \(3\) as a concordance comparison.

\textbf{A fixed finite spatial model.}\quad
For the transfer statement, choose a finite triangulation of \(B\)
and replace it by the standard realization of its abstract simplicial
complex. The vertex correspondence is a PL homeomorphism, so conjugacy
preserves the question. This standard polyhedron and its product with
the interval have real-algebraic coordinates.
It is a PL manifold over the real model as well. Indeed, fix the
finite triangulations and affine descriptors of link sphere or ball
charts over \(M\). Existence of charts with these fixed finite
descriptors is first-order in real closed fields; the same
certificates therefore exist over the real algebraic numbers.
This is a finite certificate for the spatial PL model. It is not
an assertion about a path in a parameterized PL mapping space.
It suffices to work on this one standard model \(N\).

\textbf{Joint continuity on coefficient cells.}\quad
Let \(\mathcal R\) be a real o-minimal model of the complete theory
of \(M\), in the same language. Place elementary copies of \(M\)
and \(\mathcal R\) in one sufficiently large common elementary
extension \(\mathcal E\).
Fix a graph formula describing a based definable family over \(M\),
with its finite tuple \(c\) of coefficients made into variables.
Let \(S\) be the coefficient set on which this formula describes a
valid continuous based concordance cube on the fixed domain
\[
 D=N\times[0,1]^k.
\]
Functionality, continuity, homeomorphism of every slice, the endpoint
conditions, and the boundary identity are all first-order conditions.
Thus \(S\) is definable. Its definition is over the fixed model
coefficients and the fixed language, and is the same in all three
structures.

We need joint continuity in a varying coefficient, not just
continuity for each fixed coefficient. Over \(\mathcal R\), apply
definable Hardt trivialization to the graph
\[
 \Gamma=\{(a,z,U_a(z)):a\in S,\ z\in D\}\longrightarrow S
\]
and refine the finite partition to cells. On a nonempty cell \(C\)
there is a definable homeomorphism
\(C\times F\to\Gamma|_C\) over \(C\), where \(F\) is a compact graph
fibre. Composing with graph projection gives a continuous bijection
\[
 C\times F\longrightarrow C\times D .
 \tag{155.3}
\]
Every point of the locally closed cell \(C\) has a compact relative
neighborhood in \(C\). On its product with \(F\), (155.3) is a
continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space and
is a homeomorphism. These neighborhoods prove that its inverse is
continuous on \(C\times D\). Hence evaluation \(U_a(z)\) is jointly
continuous there. The finite partition and these joint-continuity
assertions are first-order and transfer to \(\mathcal E\).
If inverse evaluation is included in the validity convention, the
same compact total-map argument gives it as well.

Let \(C_{\mathcal E}\) be a cell of this partition containing the
given \(c\). Since the cell is defined over \(\mathcal R\), its
nonemptiness in the elementary extension implies that it has a point
\(c_0\in C(\mathcal R)\). A cell is definably path connected;
choose a definable path from \(c\) to \(c_0\) in \(C_{\mathcal E}\).
Joint continuity just proved makes evaluation along this path a
based definable homotopy of concordance families.
This is a homotopy in the definable category. No PL property of
the coefficient path is claimed or needed.

\textbf{A finite witness reflected to the original model.}\quad
The family \(U_{c_0}\) is defined over the real model.
By A.154 it has a jointly PL representative and a based definable
homotopy to it over \(\mathcal R\). Interpret that finite data in
\(\mathcal E\), and concatenate with the coefficient-cell homotopy.
This gives a PL representative of \(U_c\) over \(\mathcal E\).

Now fix the actual finite formulas of this homotopy and the actual
finite triangulations and affine descriptors of its PL endpoint.
Their coefficients are a finite tuple. Their existence, with the
given \(U_c\) at the initial end, is one first-order statement with
an existential block for these finitely many coefficients, followed
by their first-order validity conditions. Those conditions include
continuity, agreement on faces, the slice homeomorphism conditions,
preservation of the parameter projection, the literal initial
family, and the fixed bottom and parameter-boundary data.
Continuity itself has quantified epsilon--delta clauses; we do not
describe the entire formula as quantifier-free or purely existential.

This first-order statement has parameters in \(M\), namely the input
coefficients \(c\) and the fixed standard model. It holds in the
elementary extension \(\mathcal E\), so it holds in \(M\).
The reflected witnesses are a genuine jointly PL endpoint and a
based definable homotopy over \(M\). This proves (155.2).
The component statement is the already established A.113.
The complete theory of real closed fields has the real field as a
model, giving the asserted semialgebraic consequence.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Scope.}\quad
The first assertion has real ground field and uses the specified
PL-to-TOP comparison ranges. The transfer assertion only requires
a real model of the fixed complete o-minimal theory and proves
surjectivity by reflecting one finite witness. It does not identify
whole TOP concordance spaces in dimensions \(3\) or \(4\), does not
claim that every o-minimal theory has a real model, and does not
replace parameter-preserving PL field invariance by a coefficient
cell path.
