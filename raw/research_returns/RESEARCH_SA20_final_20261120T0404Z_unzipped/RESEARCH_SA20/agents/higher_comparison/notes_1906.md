## Three possible shortcuts examined, with their failures

### Scalar coordinates in succession

A.114 encodes a polygonal circle by a scalar PL function that is injective on each marked edge. A higher-dimensional parameter cell cannot be encoded this way by one continuous scalar function. Straightening a second coordinate after the first would need a theorem preserving the entire first coordinate pointwise, not merely its finite marked subpolyhedra. The quoted scalar triangulation complement does not give this additional relative function condition. We therefore do not iterate it as if it did.

### Moving one parameter into the manifold

A q-parameter family on B can be regarded as a `(q−1)`-parameter family on a larger product involving B and an interval. Forgetting that the new interval is a parameter enlarges the homeomorphism group: the resulting homeomorphisms may move that interval coordinate. Returning to the original family space would require a relative level-preserving comparison, exactly the missing type of result. Stability of pseudo-isotopy under products is not a retraction to the original parameter-preserving subgroup. No injection or weak equivalence for this forgetful map has been proved in the required scope.

### Contractibility in a fixed formula format

A finite semialgebraic coefficient space of homeomorphisms can be partitioned into cells with joint evaluation, as A.116 proves. Within a cell, contracting coefficient paths supplies families. This does not produce a neighborhood of the identity in the union of all formats on which every family contracts. The required neighborhood may depend on the format of a candidate PL approximation; refining the approximation changes that format. Choosing the approximation before its contraction radius is known, and then refining it to meet that radius, is a circular argument. We found no uniform stabilization theorem that removes this dependence.
