\subsection*{The zero-obstruction PL-image criterion and the Friedman--Witt endpoint question}

This note records a conditional implication, not an evaluation of a
concordance component group. All manifolds and maps here are over the
real numbers. Write $P_{\mathrm{CAT}}(B)$ for homeomorphisms of $B\times I$
which fix $B\times\{0\}$ pointwise, with ordinary isotopies through such
maps. In the PL category these isotopies are jointly PL. Let
\[
 c_B:\pi_0P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)\longrightarrow H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)
\]
be the homomorphism of A.143. Its kernel contains the PL image; equality
is an outstanding geometric assertion, not a conclusion of the formal
Kirby--Siebenmann obstruction theory.

\textbf{Conditional statement.}\quad
Let $B=C_1\#\cdots\#C_s$ be a closed connected oriented smooth
three-manifold, where every $C_i$ has finite fundamental group, and fix
compatible PL structures. If
\[
 \ker c_B=
 \operatorname{im}\bigl(\pi_0P_{\mathrm{PL}}(B)
                    \longrightarrow\pi_0P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)\bigr),
 \tag{FW.1}
\]
then every diffeomorphism $f:B\to B$ which is topologically
pseudo-isotopic to the identity is smoothly pseudo-isotopic to the
identity. In particular, applying (FW.1) to the connected sums occurring
in the Friedman--Witt construction would answer the specific endpoint
question in Remark~(1) of Problem~4.69 of the 2026 problem list.
The converse is not asserted.

\textbf{Single-map smoothing fact used in the implication.}\quad
If $G\in P_{\mathrm{PL}}(B)$, there is a smooth concordance $\widetilde G$
whose top diffeomorphism is TOP-isotopic to $G|_{B\times\{1\}}$.
This requires neither a smooth family comparison in dimension four nor
an identification of smooth and PL isotopy groups of four-manifolds.

Here are the relative details. Put $N=B\times I$ with its product
smoothing $\sigma$. First replace $G$ by a PL-isotopic map which is the
identity on a bottom collar, without changing its top map. One may use
the collar compression already checked in A.145, applied only at the
bottom: conjugate on the image of the collar-shrinking embedding and
extend by the identity over the deleted collar. Let
$\sigma'=G^*\sigma$. These are two compatible smoothings of the same
PL manifold $N$, and they agree on a bottom collar. The stable
smoothing fibre $\mathrm{PL}/O$ has zero homotopy groups through degree
six; relative obstruction theory on this four-dimensional pair makes
the two smooth reductions homotopic relative to a smaller bottom
collar. Equivalently they are stably concordant there. The
$\mathrm{DIFF}\subset\mathrm{PL}$ product structure theorem converts
this into a smoothing concordance and its isotopy conclusion supplies
an isomorphism
\[
 \tau:(N,\sigma)\longrightarrow(N,\sigma')
\]
which is the identity on a smaller bottom collar and whose underlying
PL map is PL-isotopic to the identity relative to that collar. This is
exactly the category for which Quinn's Theorem~2.2 has no dimension
restriction, by Note~2.3(2); its relative-open-set version is
Note~2.3(5). The source explicitly allows the non-rel-entire-boundary
version, so no smoothing of the free top boundary has been silently
prescribed. Now $\widetilde G=G\tau$ is a smooth concordance. If
$a=G|_{B\times\{1\}}$, then
$\widetilde G|_{B\times\{1\}}=a\tau_1$ is TOP-isotopic to $a$.

The required stable connectivity is the already inspected original
Kirby--Siebenmann Annex~C, printed p.~318; compatible low-dimensional
smoothings are also stated in Essay~I, printed p.~39. The precise
product/isotopy source is F.~Quinn, \emph{A controlled-topology proof of
the product structure theorem}, Geom. Dedicata~148 (2010), 303--308,
Definition~2.1 and Theorem~2.2, pp.~304--305, Notes~2.3(2),(5), p.~305.
The obstruction-theoretic and isotopy assertions just used concern a
single supplied PL map.

\textbf{Proof of the conditional statement.}\quad
Choose a TOP concordance $F$ from the identity to the specified smooth
$f$. The connected-sum Mayer--Vietoris decomposition and A.145 show
that every class in $H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)$ is represented by a TOP
concordance $R$ which is the identity near both time ends. In detail,
the punctured finite-group summands realize their relative $H^2$
classes, and extension by zero identifies their sum with all of
$H^2(B;\mathbb Z/2)$ by mod-two Poincare duality. Choose
$c_B([R])=c_B([F])$. Then
\[
 F_0=FR^{-1},\qquad (F_0)_1=f,\qquad c_B([F_0])=0.
\]
The middle equality uses the literal identity of $R$ on its top,
not merely its membership in an identity component. The last equality
uses the actual homomorphism property from A.143.

By (FW.1), $F_0$ is joined within $P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)$ to a PL
concordance $G$. Restriction of that path to the top gives a TOP
isotopy from $f$ to $a=G_1$. The preceding single-map fact gives a
smooth concordance $\widetilde G$ with smooth endpoint $a'$ which is
TOP-isotopic to $a$, hence to $f$. The natural map
\[
 \pi_0\operatorname{Diff}(B)\longrightarrow
                    \pi_0\operatorname{Homeo}(B)
\]
is injective for a closed smooth three-manifold. Therefore $a'$ and
$f$ are smoothly isotopic. Append the trace of that smooth isotopy to
$\widetilde G$, using product collars at the join. This gives a smooth
pseudo-isotopy from the identity to the exact prescribed endpoint $f$.

The ordinary three-dimensional comparison in this last step is
Cerf, \emph{Groupes d'automorphismes et groupes de diff\'eomorphismes des
vari\'et\'es compactes de dimension 3}, BSMF~87 (1959), Theorem~5,
p.~327, Corollary~1, p.~328. His definition of the comparison in
\S1.2, p.~320, expressly includes the bijection on components; his
\S3.2, p.~326, supplies the compact-open and smooth topology conventions.
For a closed manifold the boundary conditions there are vacuous.
The sphere Smale assertion used in his Theorem~4 is now proved, as
already checked in A.162. Thus no assumption that TOP isotopies in
four dimensions can be smoothed is present.

\textbf{Exact relation to the published examples.}\quad
Kwasik--Schultz, \emph{Pseudo-isotopies of 3-manifolds}, Topology~35
(1996), 363--376, states the punctured proper-metacyclic-prism result
and the closed connected-sum corollary on printed p.~364. The latter
concerns $M\#N$ with proper metacyclic prism factors, and its remark
identifies these maps with the Friedman--Witt examples. The original
Friedman--Witt paper, Topology~25 (1986), 35--44, gives its relevant
connected-sum Corollary~2.2 on printed p.~40. These are spherical
prime factors with finite fundamental groups. Their connected sum
usually has infinite fundamental group; it is A.145's supported
realization, not an incorrect finite-group claim about the sum, that
applies above.

The authorized 2026 problem list, Problem~4.69, printed pp.~247--248,
asks whether a closed three-manifold diffeomorphism can be TOP but not
smoothly pseudo-isotopic to the identity. Remark~(1) asks the smooth
question specifically for the Friedman--Witt maps, citing their TOP
pseudo-isotopy from Kwasik--Schultz. Consequently an affirmative
answer to (FW.1) for those connected sums would force a positive answer
to that specific smooth-pseudo-isotopy question. Conversely, a negative
answer for one of those examples would, by the construction of $F_0$
above, exhibit a zero-$c_B$ TOP concordance component missing the PL
image. Neither answer is proved here. This does not identify the
component group of $P_{\mathrm{TOP}}(B)$, the kernel of its PL comparison,
or the smallest projective-space image gate. In particular, this is
not a claim that a computational presentation decides an open problem,
and it is not an impossibility claim about the original task.
