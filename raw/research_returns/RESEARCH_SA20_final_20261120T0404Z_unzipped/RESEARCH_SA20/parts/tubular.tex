\subsection{Tubular mapping cylinders and the dimension proposal}

For a closed definable $Y$ in a compact definable $X$, a
$Y$-admissible rigidification of a tubular neighborhood $V$ with outer
face $B$ means a definable homeomorphism
\[
 M_\varphi=(B\times[0,1]\amalg Y)/((b,0)\sim\varphi(b))
 \xrightarrow{\sim}V
\]
which is the identity on $Y$ and identifies $B\times\{1\}$ with the
specified outer face. This gives a precise interpretation of 119/151.
The quotient by $Y$ is then a rigid cone, but continuity before this
quotient is an additional requirement.

\begin{saresult}{A.201}{Proved}{Existence of admissible mapping-cylinder rigidifications}
Let $f:X\to K_{\ge0}$ be definable and continuous, with $X$ compact
and $Y=f^{-1}(0)$. For all sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$,
$V=f^{-1}([0,\epsilon])$ is a strict tubular neighborhood and has a
$Y$-admissible rigidification with outer face $B=f^{-1}(\epsilon)$.
More generally, any strict tubular neighborhood in the sense of
119/151, whose quotient by $Y$ is a definable rigid cone, admits a
$Y$-admissible rigidification, possibly different from the initial
cone rigidification. For a fixed pair $(V,B)$, the homotopy class of
$\varphi:B\to Y$ is independent of the admissible rigidification.
\end{saresult}
\begin{proof}
Apply the triangulation-of-functions theorem
\cite[Theorem 2.2(1)]{Shiota2010} to $f$. It gives a finite polyhedron
$P$, a definable homeomorphism $h:P\to X$, and a nonnegative PL
function $g=fh$. Subdivide so $g$ is affine on every simplex and its
zero set is a subcomplex. Choose $2\epsilon$ smaller than every
positive vertex value of $g$. On a simplex meeting $g^{-1}(0)$,
write a point uniquely as
\[
 x=t y+(1-t)z,
\]
where $y$ lies in the face spanned by zero-valued vertices and $z$
lies in the face spanned by positive-valued vertices. On the level
$g=\epsilon$, the zero-coordinate sum is positive, since every
positive vertex value exceeds $2\epsilon$. Thus the assignment
$\varphi(x)=y$ is continuous on that level; the formula agrees on
common simplex faces. The map
\[
 (b,r)\longmapsto\varphi(b)+(r/\epsilon)(b-\varphi(b)),
 \qquad 0\le r\le\epsilon,
\]
together with the identity on $g^{-1}(0)$, induces a continuous
bijection from its mapping cylinder to $g^{-1}([0,\epsilon])$.
For $r>0$ the inverse is obtained by following the same segment to
level $\epsilon$, so it is unique. Compactness gives a homeomorphism,
and all formulas are definable over $K$. Continuing these segments to
level $2\epsilon$ supplies the exterior collar. Components contained
entirely in $Y$ simply have no outer-face preimage. If $Y=X$, the
mapping cylinder is $Y$ with $B=\varnothing$; if $Y=\varnothing$,
choose $\epsilon<\min f$ and the neighborhood is empty. These are
compatible with the pointed-quotient convention of A.109.

For an already specified tube $V$, its cone radius gives a continuous
definable $f:V\to[0,1]$ with zero set $Y$, and the chosen cone
rigidification trivializes $f^{-1}((0,1])$ over $(0,1]$. The preceding
construction gives a mapping-cylinder description below some
$\eta>0$. Transport its level-$\eta$ base to $B=f^{-1}(1)$ using the
given trivialization, and retain that trivialization on $[\eta,1]$.
The two formulas agree at $\eta$, and their concatenation is the
required admissible rigidification of the entire $V$. This step is
why triviality on positive radii alone was insufficient without the
triangulation argument at radius zero.

Each admissible rigidification gives a strong deformation retraction
$H_i:V\times[0,1]\to V$ onto $Y$, with final retraction $r_i$.
The formula $r_0H_1(-,s)$ is a homotopy from $r_0$ to $r_1$, fixed
on $Y$, since $r_0|_Y=\id$. Restriction to $B$ gives a homotopy
from $\varphi_0$ to $\varphi_1$. We claim independence of this
homotopy class, not uniqueness of the homotopy or contractibility of
the full neighborhood choice space.
\end{proof}

\begin{saresult}{A.202}{Refuted}{The proposed fibre-dimension equality need not be attainable}
The requirement at 119/152
\[
 \dim\varphi^{-1}(y)=\dim_yX-\dim_yY-1
 \tag{A.2.1}
\]
cannot always be met by a $Y$-admissible rigidification. It fails even
for a compact semialgebraic nowhere dense $Y$ in the smooth compact
three-manifold $X=S^3$, with $Y\subset\overline{X\setminus Y}$.
For a locally trivial conical bundle over a pure-dimensional manifold
$Y$, the equality does hold, with the dimension of the fibre link.
The equality alone does not imply the categorical uniqueness asserted
on the page.
\end{saresult}
\begin{proof}
In a Euclidean chart of $S^3$, let
\[
 Y=\{(x,y,0):x^2+y^2\le1\}
 \ \cup\ \{(0,0,z):0\le z\le1\},\qquad a=(0,0,0).
\]
Stereographic inclusion makes this a completely specified compact
semialgebraic subset of $S^3$. It has local dimension two at $a$,
so (A.2.1) requires $\dim\varphi^{-1}(a)=0$.

Suppose $V=M_\varphi$ were any admissible strict neighborhood of $Y$,
with compact outer face $B$. The product $B\times(0,1)$ is open in
the three-manifold $S^3$, so $B$ is a closed definable surface.
Here is the low-dimensional PL justification for this cancellation:
triangulate $B$ and use definable Hauptvermutung on compact local
charts of the displayed product. In its product triangulation, the
link of a vertical edge over a vertex of $B$ is exactly that vertex
link in $B$. Because the product is a PL three-manifold, this link
is a PL circle. Links of edges and faces give the remaining surface
charts. Thus $B$ is a PL two-manifold; no higher-dimensional TOP
cancellation assertion is needed.

The surface $B$ is connected. Choose a sufficiently small $\delta>0$
and let $N$ be the union of
\[
 \{x^2+y^2<(1+\delta)^2,\ |z|<\delta\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \{x^2+y^2<\delta^2,\ -\delta<z<1+\delta\}.
\]
Compactness of $Y$ lets us choose $\delta$ so that its closure lies
in the interior of $V$. The complement $N\setminus Y$ is connected:
the two sides of the disk join around its boundary, and the punctured
normal disks about the segment join to the upper side. Equivalently
one can take the union of a slightly enlarged solid disk and a thin
solid tube about the segment and make these paths inside that union.
Every point of $V\setminus Y$ can be joined to $N\setminus Y$ along
its mapping-cylinder radial segment, since its limiting point lies
in $Y$. Hence $V\setminus Y$, and therefore $B$, is connected.

The map $\varphi$ is onto. Otherwise its compact image misses a
relative neighborhood of some $y\in Y$; the mapping cylinder would
then have, near $y$, only the at-most-two-dimensional set $Y$. This
contradicts that $V$ contains a three-dimensional neighborhood of
every point of $Y$. The set $Y\setminus\{a\}$ has two nonempty
clopen parts: the punctured disk and the open attached segment.
Their inverse images disconnect $B\setminus\varphi^{-1}(a)$.
If (A.2.1) held, this fibre would be a finite set, since it is
zero-dimensional and definable. Removing finitely many points from
a connected surface does not disconnect it: any path can be diverted
inside finitely many disk charts around the removed points. This is
a contradiction. In fact every admissible $\varphi$ here has a
positive-dimensional fibre over $a$.

For the positive statement, a local product
$Y\times CL$ has dimension $\dim Y+\dim L+1$ at its base and
link projection with fibre $L$; this proves (A.2.1) immediately,
including the empty-link case under $\dim\varnothing=-1$.
Finally take $Y$ to be one point and $X=CB$ for $B=(S^1)^6$.
Every standard tube has fibre dimension $\dim B=\dim X-1$, so the
dimension condition is satisfied, but the derived choice space has
the nontrivial homotopy type in A.111. Thus this numerical condition
does not restore categorical uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{saresult}{A.203}{Refuted}{The equisingular-pair equivalence needs retained tubular data}
The collapse functor in 119/152 from a bordant pair with a locally
trivial boundary map $B\to Y$ to an equisingular pair $(X,Y)$ is not
an equivalence for all definable homeomorphisms. It already fails
when $Y$ is one point. There is an exact corrected equivalence if
the target retains a mapping-cylinder neighborhood and its full
product ruling, and morphisms preserve that ruling and the
boundary bundle map. This correction holds for any specified
locally trivial definable bundle $B\to Y$.
\end{saresult}
\begin{proof}
For $Y$ a point the boundary map is automatically a locally trivial
bundle and the singularity is constant along $Y$. The functor is
precisely the boundary-collapse map refuted in A.104. Thus neither
equisingularity along the base nor (A.2.1) removes the obstruction.

For the corrected category, an object on the resolved side is a
bordant pair $(E,B)$, a space $Y$, and a bundle $\varphi:B\to Y$.
Attach $M_\varphi$ to $E$ along its outer face. Retain this tube,
its map to $Y$, and its full product rigidification as part of the target
object. Conversely remove the tube except its outer face. Strictness
gives a bordant pair. A morphism of the resolved data extends uniquely
on the tube by $(b,t)\mapsto(h_B(b),t)$ and on $Y$ by $h_Y$; the
condition $h_Y\varphi=\varphi' h_B$ makes it well-defined at radius
zero. A product-ruling preserving morphism in the target restricts
uniquely to these data. These constructions and their canonical
identifications commute with all definable parameter families.
They are inverse enriched equivalences. Retaining this information
is a stated change of category, not a proof of the unrigidified claim.
Preserving the scalar radius alone would allow a varying family
$h_t$ of base maps and would not give the claimed unique extension.
\end{proof}
