## 1. Exact scalar statement

Fix a real number `b>1`. Let

\[
 u_n=\sum_{i=1}^s m_i\alpha_i^n,
 \qquad m_i\in\mathbf Z_{>0},\quad
 \alpha_i\in\mathbf C^\times,
 \qquad N=\sum_i m_i>0.
 \tag{T1}
\]

Repeated roots may be listed separately; grouping them preserves positive integral coefficients. Suppose there is a nonzero polynomial

\[
 P(v,Y)\in\mathbf C[v,Y]
 \tag{T2}
\]

of positive `Y`-degree such that

\[
 P(b^n,u_n)=0
 \tag{T3}
\]

for every sufficiently large integer `n`.

**Theorem T4.** There are a common integer `L>0` and integers `a_i` with

\[
 \alpha_i^L=b^{a_i}\qquad\text{for every }i.
 \tag{T4}
\]

Thus every base root has a rational power of `b` as its modulus and a root of unity as its phase. No purity, integral-weight, matrix characteristic-polynomial comparison, or continuity of an algebraic sheet is assumed.

The relation (T3) is implied when `u_n` is a chosen regular value of one algebraic function `f` over `C(v)` at `v=b^n`. In that setting its minimal polynomial supplies (T2). The theorem is only a necessary root classification; it does not assert that every arbitrary collection satisfying (T4) can be evaluations of one fixed irreducible algebraic function on compatible sheets.

## 2. Group algebra and finite-power uniqueness

Let `Gamma` be the multiplicative subgroup of `C^times` generated by `b` and all `alpha_i`. It is a finitely generated abelian group. Its torsion subgroup is finite; choose a positive integer `m` annihilating it. The power-image group

\[
 \Lambda=\Gamma^m=\{g^m:g\in\Gamma\}
 \tag{T5}
\]

is torsion free. Indeed, if `g^m` has finite order, then `g` has finite order, and the choice of `m` gives `g^m=1`. Consequently `Lambda` is free abelian of some finite rank `r`, and

\[
 \mathbf C[\Lambda]
   \simeq\mathbf C[z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_r^{\pm1}]
 \tag{T6}
\]

is an integral domain. Write `[lambda]` for the group-basis monomial associated with `lambda in Lambda`, and put

\[
 B=[b^m],\qquad U=\sum_i m_i[\alpha_i^m].
 \tag{T7}
\]

The element `B` is transcendental over `C`: its integral powers are distinct group-basis elements because `b^m` has infinite order.

For a group-algebra element `W=sum_lambda c_lambda[lambda]`, evaluation on the integer sequence index `r` means

\[
 W(r)=\sum_\lambda c_\lambda\lambda^r.
 \tag{T8}
\]

This is compatible with addition and multiplication. It is injective even if equality of sequences is required only for sufficiently large integers. To see this, group distinct nonzero complex roots `lambda_1,...,lambda_d`. Vanishing at `r=r0,...,r0+d-1` gives the usual Vandermonde matrix multiplied by the invertible diagonal matrix with entries `lambda_j^{r0}`. Hence all coefficients are zero.

Applying this to (T3) on indices `n=mr` yields the exact group-algebra identity

\[
 P(B,U)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbf C[\Lambda].
 \tag{T9}
\]

The polynomial `P(B,Y)` is nonzero because `B` is transcendental. Thus `U`, viewed in the fraction field of the Laurent polynomial ring, is algebraic over `C(B)`.

## 3. The elementary derivation proof

Choose a lattice basis for `Lambda` so that

\[
 B=z_1^d\qquad(d>0).
 \tag{T10}
\]

This is ordinary integer linear algebra: the nonzero integer vector of `b^m` has a positive gcd `d`, and the extended Euclidean algorithm, or its successive coordinate operations, sends it by a unimodular basis change to `(d,0,...,0)`.

For `j>=2`, define the Euler derivation

\[
 D_j=z_j\frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}
 \tag{T11}
\]

on the Laurent polynomial ring and extend it to its fraction field by the quotient rule. It kills `C(B)`. Let `Q(Y)` be the monic minimal polynomial of `U` over `C(B)`. Characteristic zero makes it separable, so `Q'(U) != 0`. Differentiating its relation gives

\[
 0=D_jQ(U)=Q'(U)D_jU,
 \qquad\text{hence }D_jU=0.
 \tag{T12}
\]

Write the positive Laurent polynomial `U` after grouping equal monomials as

\[
 U=\sum_{a\in S} M_a z_1^{a_1}\cdots z_r^{a_r},
 \qquad M_a\in\mathbf Z_{>0}.
 \tag{T13}
\]

In (T12), the coefficient of that monomial is `M_a a_j`. Distinct monomials are linearly independent, and `M_a` is nonzero in characteristic zero. Thus `a_j=0` for every `a in S` and every `j>=2`. All support lies on the first lattice axis.

No original summand disappears on passing from (T7) to (T13), because all its coefficients are positive. Therefore, for each original root, there is an integer `c_i` with

\[
 [\alpha_i^m]=z_1^{c_i}.
 \tag{T14}
\]

Raising to the `d`th power and using (T10) gives

\[
 [\alpha_i^{md}]=[b^{mc_i}].
\]

Equality of group-basis elements is equality in the original subgroup of `C^times`. Hence (T4) follows with the common exponent `L=md` and `a_i=mc_i`. If the group has rank one, there are no derivations `D_j` to apply and the same conclusion already follows from its one-dimensional lattice. This proves Theorem T4.

The positive coefficients are used exactly to prevent cancellation of a root after torsion-killing and grouping. The proof does not recover virtual eigenvalues that cancel from the sequence or from the chosen powered progression.

### The relative-algebraic-closure fact, if desired

For completeness, the same elementary method proves the auxiliary field statement suggested in the parent task. If `h in C(z1,...,zr)` is algebraic over `C(z1)`, the derivations `D_j` for `j>=2` give `D_jh=0` by separability. The kernel of ordinary differentiation on a rational function field `M(z)` of characteristic zero is `M`. For `h=0` this is immediate. Otherwise write `h=p/q` in coprime polynomials; the identity `p'q-pq'=0` implies `p` divides `p'` unless `p` is constant; degree then forces `p'=0`, and similarly `q'=0`. Thus both are constant. Iterating gives `h in C(z1)`. In particular `C(z1)` is algebraically closed in this purely transcendental extension. Its intersection with the Laurent polynomial ring is `C[z1^{±1}]`, either by the same monomial derivative argument or by Laurent coefficient uniqueness. The main proof above establishes exactly the needed support conclusion directly.

## 4. One common radius forces one eigenvalue

Now assume the finite power sum is the ordinary trace of a regular base value of an algebraic matrix family. Precisely, let `F` be an invertible finite matrix over an algebraic extension of `C(v)`, with a regular invertible value `F_b`. Let

\[
 f(v)=\operatorname{Tr}F(v),\qquad
 f_b=\operatorname{Tr}F_b.
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, choose a regular evaluation of the same family above `v=b^n` and assume only

\[
 f_{b^n}=\operatorname{Tr}(F_b^n).
 \tag{T15}
\]

All finitely many entries lie in a finite algebraic extension, so the trace is one algebraic element with one minimal relation. Different allowed sheets at different extension parameters are permitted.

**Theorem T16.** Suppose all eigenvalues of `F_b` have one common absolute value `R>0`. Then all eigenvalues of `F_b` are equal to a single number `lambda`. If its full centralizer is semisimple, then

\[
 F_b=\lambda I,\qquad
 \operatorname{End}_{F_b}(\mathbf C^N)=M_N(\mathbf C).
 \tag{T16}
\]

This uses neither an integral spectral weight nor regularity at `v=1`. It requires only the ordinary trace comparison (T15), not full spectral retuning.

**Proof.** Apply Theorem T4 to the genuine eigenvalue trace of `F_b`, with algebraic multiplicities. There is a common `L>0` and integers `a_i` with `alpha_i^L=b^{a_i}`. Equality of the moduli forces all `a_i` to be one integer `a`, and

\[
 R^L=b^a.
 \tag{T17}
\]

At degrees `n=Lr`, the trace is exactly

\[
 u_{Lr}=N b^{ar}.
 \tag{T18}
\]

Consequently the algebraic function

\[
 g(v)=f(v)^L-N^L v^a
 \tag{T19}
\]

has a zero at the selected regular place over each sufficiently large distinct parameter `v=b^{Lr}`. A nonzero algebraic function cannot do this. Indeed, its monic minimal polynomial over `C(v)` has a nonzero rational constant term; away from finitely many coefficient poles, a zero of the function forces a zero of that nonzero rational function. Thus (T19) vanishes identically. Negative `a` causes no issue in `C(v)` and at the nonzero parameter `b`.

Evaluate the identity at the original regular place over `b`, where `f_b=Tr F_b`. It gives

\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}F_b|=N b^{a/L}=NR.
 \tag{T20}
\]

This is equality in the triangle inequality for the `N` actual eigenvalues, each of modulus `R`. Their positive multiplicities force them all to have the same phase. Hence every eigenvalue equals one `lambda`, with `|lambda|=R` and `lambda^L=b^a`.

The matrix `F_b-lambda I` is nilpotent, without assuming diagonalizability. It is central in the full centralizer. If that algebra is semisimple, its center is reduced, so the nilpotent is zero. The full centralizer of the resulting scalar matrix is `M_N(C)`, as claimed. QED.

The common eigenvalue can have a root-of-unity phase in the unrestricted rational-radius setting. For example `F(v)=sqrt(v) I` can be evaluated on the negative sheet at `b` and on sheets `(-1)^n b^{n/2}` at `b^n`. This satisfies the trace comparison with base eigenvalue `-sqrt(b)`. It does not contradict F51: its spectral weight relative to `Q=b^2` is `1/2`, not an integral weight. The present centralizer conclusion needs only equality of the eigenvalues, not the removal of this common phase.

## 5. Actual algebra consequence and exact limits

For the actual generic rank-three algebra at a positive real parameter `b>1`,

\[
 A_b\simeq\mathbf C\times\mathbf C\times
                 M_2(\mathbf C)\times M_2(\mathbf C)
 \tag{T21}
\]

is semisimple and nonsimple. Theorem T16 excludes its realization as the **full** centralizer of a regular base value satisfying (T15) and one common spectral radius, at any auxiliary rank. This strengthens the F51 single-integral-weight gate to an arbitrary common spectral radius. An all-conjugate pure spectrum satisfies the common-radius hypothesis at each chosen complex embedding, so one embedding already supplies the stated contradiction under the extra comparison assumptions.

Theorem T4 concerns base eigenvalues. From identity traces alone it does not identify the family characteristic polynomial at other parameters, its eigenvalue functions, or their collision contact orders. The mixed-radius trace-only full-order construction in F54 Part 2 must therefore be retained as a separate escape, not contradicted by this theorem. The F52 whole-order contact proof still requires its stronger spectral comparison, unless additional inserted-trace hypotheses are supplied.

The fixed-parameter F46 constructions, the faithful F48 tensor functors and the F53 convolution realizations remain valid. They need not impose this retuning law on a family whose full target centralizer is the specified source algebra. An action by a specified subalgebra is distinct from full centralizer equality. Arbitrary inserted traces and virtual Euler traces do not automatically have the positive multiplicities of (T1).
