## 2. The actual generic graph

Let `K=Q(v)`, and let `A_r=K check H_(r,2)` be the actual nonstandard Temperley–Lieb subalgebra of `TL_r⊗TL_r`, generated by

\[
P_i=C'_i\otimes C'_i+C_i\otimes C_i.
\]

The tower inclusions are the ones generated by the first `r−2` generators. For a two-row partition `lambda` write `f_lambda=dim M_lambda=|SYT(lambda)|`. The simple labels at rank `r` are

\[
\{\lambda,\mu\}\ (\lambda\ne\mu),\qquad +\lambda,\qquad-\lambda,
\qquad\epsilon_+.
\]

The first labels are unordered pairs of two-row partitions of `r`; the signs `±lambda` are included only when `lambda` is neither a row nor a column. Their modules and dimensions are

| Label | Module | Dimension |
|---|---|---:|
| `{lambda,mu}` | `M_lambda⊗M_mu` restricted to `A_r` | `f_lambda f_mu` |
| `+lambda` | trace-free symmetric square of `M_lambda` | `f_lambda(f_lambda+1)/2−1` |
| `−lambda` | exterior square of `M_lambda` | `f_lambda(f_lambda−1)/2` |
| `epsilon_+` | scalar module `P_i=D` | `1` |

Zero modules at small ranks are omitted. In particular, at rank two the label `{(2),(1,1)}` supplies the nonstandard sign scalar; it is not an extra signed label attached to a forbidden one-dimensional partition.

For an exact branch test, let `Rem(lambda)` be the partitions obtained by deleting a removable corner. Use the following rules, directly encoding Proposition 5.2:

- From `{lambda,mu}`, take each pair `(lambda',mu')∈Rem(lambda)×Rem(mu)`. If they differ, output `{lambda',mu'}`. If they agree at `nu`, replace that diagonal tensor product by `+nu`, `−nu` and `epsilon_+`, deleting the zero signed modules.
- From `+lambda`, output each unordered distinct pair from `Rem(lambda)`, each nonzero `+lambda'`, and `epsilon_+` with multiplicity `|Rem(lambda)|−1`.
- From `−lambda`, output each unordered distinct pair from `Rem(lambda)` and each nonzero `−lambda'`.
- From `epsilon_+`, output `epsilon_+`.

For two-row partitions there are at most two removable corners. The displayed scalar multiplicity is therefore zero or one. No unordered-pair duplication occurs in the first rule: two distinct partitions cannot share two different immediate predecessors, since the union of those predecessors would recover each partition. The other labels belong to distinct types, and within each type the corners give distinct partitions. Thus every nonzero edge has multiplicity one, in agreement with the source theorem.

Every path from rank one to a fixed simple label determines a one-dimensional seminormal line. Induction on restriction gives

\[
\dim \check M_\alpha=\#\{\text{paths from rank one to }\alpha\}.
\]

This genuinely instantiates the abstract multiplicity-free-chain mechanism with an actual nonstandard algebra chain. A path has `r` stages, each carrying at most two two-row partitions and a type, and its edges can be checked by deletion of a box. Its encoding length and verification time are polynomial in the expanded size `r`. This is not automatically polynomial in `log r` when sizes are given in binary; nor does it count classical Kronecker multiplicities. It counts generic simple dimensions and fixes lines only up to independent nonzero scalars.

## 3. The actual r=4 test module

Consider

\[
M_K=M_{(3,1),K}\otimes M_{(2,2),K}.
\]

By the source theorem, it is the six-dimensional **simple** `A_4`-module with label `{(3,1),(2,2)}`. Its penultimate restriction is

\[
\operatorname{Res}_{A_3}M_K
\cong N(\delta)\oplus N(D-2)\oplus\epsilon_+\oplus\epsilon_-.
\tag{T30-1}
\]

This follows either from the branch rule or from ordinary restrictions:

\[
M_{(3,1)}|_{H_3}=M_{(3)}\oplus M_{(2,1)},
\qquad M_{(2,2)}|_{H_3}=M_{(2,1)},
\]

then the actual rank-three tensor decomposition already proved in the earlier reports. The `N(delta)` plane is exactly the first ordinary tensor factor's trivial `H_3` line times the second factor's standard `H_3` module. It has two generic tower path lines, since its restriction to `A_2` is the sum of the two scalar simples.

At `v=1`, the module is the diagonal symmetric-group tensor product

\[
M_1=[(3,1)]\otimes[(2,2)]
\cong[(3,1)]\oplus[(2,1,1)].
\tag{T30-2}
\]

For a direct check, on the class order `(1^4),(2,1,1),(2,2),(3,1),(4)`, the first standard character is `(3,1,−1,0,−1)` and the pairing-standard character is `(2,0,2,−1,0)`. Their product is `(6,0,−2,0,0)`, the sum of `(3,1,−1,0,−1)` and its sign twist `(3,−1,−1,0,1)`. The latter is the irreducible of shape `(2,1,1)`. This proves (T30-2) by characteristic-zero character theory.

## 4. Fixing a regular, saturated penultimate plane

The integral form matters. Work over

\[
R=\mathbb Q[v,v^{-1}]_{(v-1)}
\]

with the fixed ordinary Specht tensor lattice

\[
M_R=\mathsf S_{(3,1),R}\otimes_R\mathsf S_{(2,2),R}.
\]

These are the usual free Specht forms specializing to the characteristic-zero Specht modules. Let `H_3⊂H_4` be the parabolic generated by the first two simple reflections. Its trivial projector is regular over `R`:

\[
p_+=\frac{\sum_{w\in S_3}v^{\ell(w)}T_w}
{\sum_{w\in S_3}v^{2\ell(w)}}.
\tag{T30-3}
\]

The denominator specializes to six and is a unit. The numerator is the usual Hecke symmetrizer: multiplying by a simple generator gives `v` times the numerator, and squaring multiplies it by the displayed denominator. Thus `p_+²=p_+`. At `v=1` it is the ordinary average `(1/6)sum_(w∈S3)w`.

Define

\[
E_R=(\mathsf S_{(3,1),R}p_+)\otimes_R\mathsf S_{(2,2),R}
\subset M_R.
\tag{T30-4}
\]

It is the image of the idempotent `p_+⊗1`, hence a direct summand, free of rank two and **saturated** in `M_R`. It is stable under `A_3`, because the first-factor projector is central in the parabolic ordinary Hecke algebra and commutes with its two generators. Its generic fiber is the unique `N(delta)` summand of (T30-1).

In particular,

\[
E_R=E_K\cap M_R.
\tag{T30-5}
\]

Indeed, if `x∈E_K∩M_R`, the regular projector fixes `x`, so `x` lies in its `R`-image. The converse is immediate.

Every generic tower path line through `N(delta)` is contained in `E_K`. A vector on such a line that has been diagonally rescaled to be regular in `M_R` and to have nonzero reduction must, by (T30-5), reduce to a nonzero vector of `E_1`. This conclusion does not assume that an arbitrary meromorphic seminormal basis already specializes to a basis. It applies to every attempted regular nonzero diagonal normalization of those particular lines in the fixed lattice.

The two lines can themselves be regularly separated inside `E_R`: the rank-two step projectors are `P_1/D` and `1−P_1/D`, whose eigenvalues are the distinct scalars one and zero and whose denominators are units at `v=1`. This observation does not solve the rank-four decomposition problem below.

## 5. Exact compression calculation at v=1

Use the real orthogonal models

\[
U=\{(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\in\mathbb R^4:\sum_i x_i=0\}
\cong[(3,1)],
\]

and `W`, the sum-zero subspace of the permutation representation on the three pairings of four letters, so `W≅[(2,2)]`. Let `S_3⊂S_4` fix the fourth letter. Its invariant line in `U` is spanned by

\[
w=(1,1,1,-3),\qquad\langle w,w\rangle=12.
\]

Therefore

\[
E_1=\mathbb Rw\otimes W.
\]

Let `p` be the orthogonal projector onto `Rw`, and `Pi=p⊗1_W` the projector onto `E_1`. Let

\[
Z=\sum_{1\le i<j\le4}(ij)
\]

act diagonally on `U⊗W`. For an internal transposition `(ij)` with `i,j≤3`, it fixes `w`, so

\[
p\rho_U((ij))p=p.
\]

For an external transposition `(i4)`, the inner product is `−4`: for example `(14)w=(−3,1,1,1)`, whose dot product with `w` is `−4`. Consequently

\[
p\rho_U((i4))p=-\frac13p.
\tag{T30-6}
\]

The action of `S_4` on the three pairings has quotient `S_3` and kernel the Klein four group. The three internal transpositions map bijectively to the three transpositions of this quotient; the three external ones do so as well (opposite transpositions in `S_4` have the same image). In the three-point permutation representation, the sum of the three quotient-transposition matrices is the all-ones matrix. On its sum-zero subspace `W`, that sum is zero. Thus

\[
\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}\rho_W((ij))=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^3\rho_W((i4))=0.
\tag{T30-7}
\]

Equations (T30-6)–(T30-7) give the exact compression

\[
\Pi Z\Pi
=p\otimes\left(0-\frac13\,0\right)=0.
\tag{T30-8}
\]

On the two irreducible summands in (T30-2), the central element `Z` has eigenvalues `+2` and `−2`. This follows from the trace formula for a central conjugacy-class sum: its six transpositions have character values `+1` and `−1`, respectively, in dimension three, so its scalar is `6(±1)/3=±2`.

If a nonzero `x∈E_1` belonged to either irreducible summand, then `Zx=±2x`, whence `Pi Z Pi x=±2x`, contradicting (T30-8). Therefore

\[
E_1\cap[(3,1)]=0,
\qquad E_1\cap[(2,1,1)]=0.
\tag{T30-9}
\]

The equality is inside the specific representation (T30-2), not just a statement about abstract module types. Every nonzero vector in this penultimate plane mixes the two final classical constituents.

One can quantify the mixing: the two final central projectors are `(1±Z/2)/2`, and their compression to `E_1` is `1/2` times the identity. Since `Z` is self-adjoint and `Z²=4` on this module, each nonzero vector of `E_1` has nonzero projections of equal squared norm onto the two classical summands. This refinement is not needed for the obstruction.

## 6. Precise conclusion and remaining freedom

**Claim T30.** The actual two-row nonstandard tower has multiplicity-free generic restriction and explicit path labels. Nevertheless, on the fixed ordinary Specht tensor lattice for `{(3,1),(2,2)}`, no regular nonzero diagonal renormalization of its two path lines through the penultimate label `{(3),(2,1)}` can make those specialized vectors belong to either of the final `S_4` irreducible summands. In particular diagonal renormalization of this tower's seminormal lines cannot produce a basis partitioned by the two classical summands.

**Proof.** Generic line containment is (T30-1); regular saturated containment after specialization is (T30-4)–(T30-5); the two zero intersections are (T30-9). A nonzero scalar rescaling cannot move a vector out of that plane. ∎

This is not a prohibition on nontrivial mixing of generic path lines, a different integral lattice, a different canonical-basis construction, or an alternative way to compute Kronecker coefficients. It establishes that the already available generic path labels do not automatically become decomposition-compatible labels under diagonal normalization in this natural fixed lattice. Some nontrivial comparison operation is required even in this six-dimensional actual example.

The established tower therefore narrows one part of the problem: generic nonstandard branching and effective expanded-size path labels already exist in the two-row quotient. The remaining specialization gate is real and explicit, rather than merely an absent theorem about an unspecified geometry. The calculation does not attribute this new r=4 obstruction to Blasiak; the source supplies the modules and branch structure, and the compression argument is given here in full.
