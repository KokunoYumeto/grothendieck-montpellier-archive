## 0. Scope and present gate

Let (E/\overline{\mathbb Q}\), let
\(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega _1+\mathbb Z\omega _2\), and put
\(\tau=\omega _2/\omega _1\in\mathfrak H\). The base embedding is the given
inclusion into \(\mathbb C\). We work in the category of rational motives for
absolute Hodge cycles generated by the two elliptic curves (E,\overline E\);
the same period calculations apply in the motivated category.

The specific remaining assertion is
\[
 E\not\sim\overline E
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname {trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(\tau,\overline\tau)=2.
 \tag{TG}
\]
The explicit test object is the curve A.3.4 of the report:
\[
 j_0=(2+i)^{-1},\qquad c=j_0/(j_0-1728),\qquad
 E_8:\ y^2=4x^3-3cx-c.
\]
Its nonisogeny to its conjugate was proved there using the distinct valuations
of (j_0,\overline{j_0}\) at the Gaussian prime (2+i\). Nothing in this file
replaces that proof by a numerical test.

The results below narrow the form of any remaining relation, identify it as a
specific higher-weight period relation, and test several published theorems
against that relation. They do not prove or refute (TG). In particular, the
compatibility examples below are explicitly constructed complex numbers, not
periods of an asserted arithmetic elliptic curve.

## TG-1. Every rational-function relation is already an isogeny relation

**Proved.** Suppose (E/\overline{\mathbb Q}\) is non-CM. Then
\[
 \overline\tau\in\mathbb Q(\tau)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\sim\overline E.
\]
Consequently, for (E\not\sim\overline E\), no nonzero polynomial relation
over \(\mathbb Q\) can have degree at most one in either variable.

**Proof.** Schneider's theorem makes \(\tau\) transcendental: an algebraic
\(\tau\in\mathfrak H\) with algebraic (j(\tau)\) is imaginary quadratic.
Write \(\overline\tau=R(\tau)\), with (R\in\mathbb Q(X)\). Conjugation
gives \(\tau=R(\overline\tau)=R(R(\tau))\). Because evaluation at the
transcendental number \(\tau\) is injective on \(\mathbb Q(X)\), this is the
identity (R\circ R=X\). The degree of a composite of nonconstant rational
maps \(\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1\) is the product of the degrees. Thus
\((\deg R)^2=1\), and (R\) has degree one.

Write (R(X)=(aX+b)/(cX+d)\), with (a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Q\) and
\(ad-bc\ne0\). Clearing denominators shows that the lattices
\(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\tau\) and
\(\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\overline\tau\) are commensurable after multiplication
by (c\tau+d\). This gives an analytic isogeny. Its finite kernel consists
of torsion points of (E\), so the quotient and an isomorphism to the target
are defined over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\). Conversely, the pullback on the
two homology lattices of an isogeny is an invertible rational matrix and
directly gives a rational fractional-linear relation, as in A.3.2.

A relation linear in (Y\) writes \(\overline\tau=R(\tau)\), since a
nonzero denominator polynomial cannot vanish at the transcendental \(\tau\).
Conjugation treats a relation linear in (X\). This proves the final claim.

**Base-field qualification.** The coefficients in this statement are rational.
For (R\in\overline{\mathbb Q}(X)\), the same degree argument gives
\(\overline R\circ R=X\), hence a fractional-linear map with algebraic
coefficients. The lattice commensurability proof requires rational matrix
entries; no unproved passage from algebraic entries to rational entries is
being made here.

## TG-2. Symmetric equation and the fixed field of conjugation

**Proved.** For (E\not\sim\overline E\), algebraic dependence of
\(\tau,\overline\tau\) is equivalent to a nonzero irreducible equation
\[
 P(\tau,\overline\tau)=0,\qquad
 P\in\mathbb Q[X,Y],\quad P(Y,X)=P(X,Y),
 \quad \deg_XP=\deg_YP=d\ge2.
 \tag{1}
\]
Furthermore, for (F=\mathbb Q(\tau,\overline\tau)\),
\[
 F^{\langle c\rangle}
 =\mathbb Q(\tau+\overline\tau,\tau\overline\tau),\qquad
 [F:F^{\langle c\rangle}]=2.
 \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** In the dependent case the evaluation map
\(\mathbb Q[X,Y]\to\mathbb C\) has a prime ideal of height one. This UFD
therefore has kernel \((P)\) for an irreducible polynomial (P\). Complex
conjugation fixes \(\mathbb Q\) and exchanges the evaluations of (X,Y\),
so (P(Y,X)=aP(X,Y)\) for (a\in\mathbb Q^\times\). Swapping again gives
(a^2=1\). If (a=-1\), the polynomial is divisible by (X-Y\), so
irreducibility would force (P\) to be proportional to (X-Y\). That is
impossible for \(\tau\in\mathfrak H\). Hence (P\) is symmetric, and its
two separate degrees agree. TG-1 excludes degree one; degree zero is excluded
by transcendence of each coordinate.

For (2), write (r=\tau+\overline\tau\), (s=\tau\overline\tau\). Both
are fixed by conjugation, and \(\tau\) satisfies (T^2-rT+s=0\), while
\(\overline\tau=r-\tau\). Thus \([F:\mathbb Q(r,s)]\le2\). Equality
holds because the subfield \(\mathbb Q(r,s)\) is real and \(\tau\) is not.
The nontrivial automorphism is conjugation, proving (2).

In particular, (TG) is exactly algebraic independence of the real numbers
\(r,s\). The rational change of expressions is explicitly given in (2);
it does not prove that independence.

## TG-3. The exact flag quotient of the product period torsor

**Proved reduction to the named period-conjecture instance.** Put
\(A=E\times\overline E\), where the two factors are nonisogenous. The
motivic group in the indicated category is
\[
 G=\Delta _2
 =\{(g_1,g_2)\in\mathrm{GL}_2^2:\det g_1=\det g_2\},
 \qquad \dim G=7.
\]
The equality follows from the product Mumford--Tate group, or the elliptic
group calculation already used in A.4.9: each factor is non-CM, their derived
groups are independent \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)'s, and the common determinant is
the one Tate character.

Let (B=H_B^1(A)\), (D=H_{\mathrm{dR}}^1(A)\), and
\(P=\operatorname {Isom}^{\otimes}(B_{\overline{\mathbb Q}},D)\).
The two algebraic holomorphic lines in (D\) define the algebraic morphism
\[
 q:P\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1,
 \qquad
 p\longmapsto
 \bigl(p^{-1}F^1D_E,p^{-1}F^1D_{\overline E}\bigr).
 \tag{3}
\]
The derived group \(\mathrm{SL}_2\times\mathrm{SL}_2\) acts transitively
on the target, so (q\) is surjective. Every fibre has dimension five.
With the oriented basis \((\overline{\omega_1},-\overline{\omega_2})\)
on the second curve, its value at the analytic comparison is
\[
 q(p_{\mathrm{an}})=([1:\tau],[1:-\overline\tau]).
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus the period-conjecture assertion of Bost--Charles, Conjecture 2.12,
for the **named motive (h^1(E_8)\oplus h^1(\overline E_8)\)** implies
that (4) is generic on the target: the pullback of every nonzero regular
function on a target chart is nonzero on the surjective source, and a generic
point does not annihilate it. This proves the requested logical implication
from that named conjecture, without asserting the conjecture itself.

The exact smaller gate is injectivity of
\[
 \overline{\mathbb Q}[X,Y]\longrightarrow\mathbb C,
 \qquad X\longmapsto\tau,\quad Y\longmapsto-\overline\tau.
 \tag{5}
\]
Algebraic independence over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) and over
\(\mathbb Q\) are equivalent: a relation over a number field can be
multiplied by all of its field conjugates to give a nonzero rational relation.

## TG-4. Full subgroup generation permits a proper flag curve

**Proved algebraic construction.** The subgroup-generation conclusion proved
in A.4.9 has no implication to flag genericity by group generation alone.
Here is an exact model of the two assertions.

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Put
\(G'=\mathrm{SL}_2\times\mathrm{SL}_2\), let (B'\) be the product of
the two upper-triangular Borels, and let
\(q':G'\to G'/B'=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). Take a proper
irreducible algebraic curve (C\) in the target, dominant over each factor.
After a group translation it contains the base point. Its inverse image
(S=(q')^{-1}(C)\) contains (B'\) and is proper in (G'\).

Let (H\) be the closed subgroup generated by (S\). Because (C\) projects
dominantly to the first factor, some (g=(g_1,g_2)\in S\) has (g_1\notin B\).
Now (H\) contains both (B\times1\) and
\((g_1Bg_1^{-1})\times1\). These generate \(\mathrm{SL}_2\times1\):
their Lie algebras are two distinct two-dimensional Borel subalgebras of the
three-dimensional \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), and hence span all of it. A closed
subgroup with full Lie algebra contains the connected \(\mathrm{SL}_2\).
The second projection gives (1\times\mathrm{SL}_2\) in exactly the same
way. Therefore (H=G'\), although (q'(S)=C\) is a proper curve.

This construction concerns specified algebraic subvarieties of a group. It
does not identify (S\) with an arithmetic period locus. It proves exactly
that such a locus being a group generator does not, as a purely group-theoretic
inference, exclude the possible flag relation (1).

## TG-5. Homogenization turns the remaining equation into a specific period

**Proved.** For a polynomial (P(X,Y)=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}X^iY^j\) of
bidegree ((m,n)\), the relation (P(\tau,\overline\tau)=0\) is equivalent
to
\[
 \sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\,
 \omega_1^{m-i}\omega_2^i
 \overline{\omega_1}^{\,n-j}\overline{\omega_2}^{\,j}=0.
 \tag{6}
\]
This follows on multiplying by the nonzero denominator
\(\omega_1^m\overline{\omega_1}^{\,n}\).

The Künneth map identifies each product in (6) with the integral of
\(\omega^{\boxtimes m}\boxtimes\overline\omega^{\boxtimes n}\) over the
corresponding product of cycles on (E^m\times\overline E^n\). Applying the
rational symmetric projectors identifies (6) with one linear period relation
of
\[
 T_{m,n}=\operatorname {Sym}^m h^1(E)\otimes
          \operatorname {Sym}^n h^1(\overline E).
 \tag{7}
\]
The Koszul sign in a realization of motives is handled by the ordinary
symmetric power in the tensor category of (H^1\) Hodge structures; the
corresponding algebraic projector on cohomology uses the signed permutation
action. Equivalently one can stay in the full Künneth tensor product, where
the displayed product integrals require no convention about that projector.

The representation of the derived group of (G\) on (7) is
\(\operatorname {Sym}^m\mathbb Q^2\boxtimes
\operatorname {Sym}^n\mathbb Q^2\), irreducible for (m,n>0\). Its extreme
Hodge types are ((m+n,0)\) and ((0,m+n)\). A Tate twist shifts both indices
equally, so the Hodge level remains (m+n\). A pure constituent of a
1-motive has Hodge level at most one. Thus there is no isomorphism of (7),
even after Tate twist, with a pure constituent of a 1-motive when (m+n>1\):
such an isomorphism would preserve the Hodge types and their differences.

The opened Huber--Wüstholz linear-period theorems, Theorems 9.7 and 9.10,
apply to 1-periods. Formula (6) gives an exact map to a period of (7), but no
map to a 1-period is supplied by those theorems. The just-proved Hodge-level
obstruction rules out an isomorphism of the simplest proposed sort. It does
not rule out every possible use or extension of their methods. For the
symmetric residual equation in TG-2, (m=n=d\ge2\), and the displayed tensor
has Hodge level (2d\ge4\).

## TG-6. A compatibility model for all individual period fields and linear bounds

**Proved construction, not an arithmetic elliptic example.** Choose positive
real (t\) and real (r,s,p,h\) such that these five numbers are algebraically
independent over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(\pi)\). Set
\[
 z=t+it^2,\qquad u=r+is,\qquad v=p+ih,\qquad c_0=2\pi i,
\]
and define the four symbols
\[
 \omega_1=u,\quad\omega_2=uz,\quad
 \eta_1=v,\quad\eta_2=zv-c_0/u.
 \tag{8}
\]
The second tuple is their actual complex conjugate. These numbers have the
following exact properties:

1. \(\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=c_0\), and its conjugate also holds.
2. \(4(z-\overline z)^2+(z+\overline z)^4=0\), while
   \(\operatorname {trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(z,\overline z)=1\).
3. Each individual four-entry field over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) has
   transcendence degree four.
4. The combined two-tuple field has transcendence degree six.
5. The ten numbers (1,c_0\) and the eight entries of the two tuples are
   linearly independent over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).

**Proof.** The first two identities follow by direct substitution. Since
\(z+\overline z=2t\), the second field has degree of transcendence one.
The first tuple generates exactly
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}(u,z,v,\pi)\): division recovers (z\), and its
determinant recovers \(\pi\). Algebraic independence of (u,z,v\) over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}(\pi)\) follows from that of (r,s,t,p,h\); (t\)
is algebraic over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(z)\), and independent pairs
((r,s)\), ((p,h)\) supply the independent linear combinations (u,v\).
Thus the transcendence degree is four, and conjugation gives the same result
for the second tuple. Together the tuples recover (u,\overline u,z,
\overline z,v,\overline v,\pi\), hence (r,s,t,p,h,\pi\). Their combined
field therefore has transcendence degree six.

For linear independence, a proposed relation's coefficients of the
independent variables (p,h\) force both coefficients of (v\) and
\(\overline v\) to vanish separately. They are of the form (a+bz\) and
(a'+b'\overline z\), with algebraic coefficients. Transcendence of (z\)
and \(\overline z\) forces all four coefficients to be zero. The remaining
coefficients of (r,s\) similarly reduce the holomorphic part to
(a_0+b_0\pi=0\). Transcendence of \(\pi\) forces the last coefficients
to vanish.

Thus the individual four-period dimension, the ten-dimensional linear period
space for two nonisogenous non-CM curves, the determinant relation, and actual
complex conjugation are compatible with a dependent pair of ratios. No
algebraic values of (g_2,g_3\), or of (j(z)\), have been asserted for (8).
Those arithmetic conditions are the remaining structure that a proof would
have to exploit. This construction is stronger than merely observing that a
linear-independence theorem has a different wording from (TG).

## TG-7. Exact test of Pila--Tsimerman's modular Ax--Schanuel theorem

The primary source opened was Pila--Tsimerman, *Ax--Schanuel for the
(j\)-function*, arXiv:1412.8255, Theorems 1.1--1.3, pp.1--3. Theorem 1.1
controls components of the intersection of the graph of (j^n\) with an
algebraic variety, assuming their moduli projection is not contained in a
proper weakly special variety.

For a proposed relation (P\) of (1), the exact candidate is
\[
 V=\{P(z_1,-z_2)=0\}\times\{(j_0,\overline{j_0})\}
 \subset \mathbb C^2\times Y(1)^2.
\]
Its intersection with the graph of (j^2\) has an isolated point at
\((\tau,-\overline\tau,j_0,\overline{j_0})\), since the fibres of (j\)
are discrete. The moduli projection is a point. Points with arbitrary
constant coordinates are weakly special under the paper's definition;
nonisogeny does not remove this constant-coordinate case. Therefore this
application fails the stated hypothesis, and gives no contradiction.

For Theorem 1.3's differential form, the equations include
(D(j(z))=j'(z)D(z)\), and the (j\)-coordinates must be nonconstant. A
\(\mathbb Q\)-derivation kills the algebraic (j_0\). Since
(j_0\ne0,1728\), its analytic inverse has nonzero derivative; the imposed
chain rule would force (D(\tau)=0\). Thus a derivation chosen to detect
transcendence of \(\tau\) cannot simply be declared compatible with analytic
evaluation of (j\). The missing assertion is precisely that compatibility,
and the displayed equations show why this attempted input cannot have it.

## TG-8. Functional period theorems and specialization

Three primary results were checked through their exact maps.

**Habegger--Pila, 2012, Proposition 3.1, p.8.** Their modular-logarithm result
requires a neighborhood in the inverse image of an algebraic moduli curve to
lie in an algebraic hypersurface. Equation (1) is at one point; the source's
conclusion would apply after proving that equation on such a neighborhood.
The constant moduli point is not an algebraic moduli curve. The exact extra
step would be to extend (1) as an identity of inverse-(j\) germs along a
positive-dimensional algebraic curve.

**Bakker--Tsimerman, 2025, Theorems 1.1 (=4.25), 1.3, and 5.1.** The
geometric period-conjecture theorem measures transcendence over the function
field of a complex base and uses the relative motivic group. For the constant
object (E_8\times\overline E_8\), the relative group is trivial and the
conclusion is the tautological lower bound zero. Their period-leaf
intersection theorem allows a weak Mumford--Tate subvariety as its exceptional
conclusion; over an isolated base point monodromy is trivial. In Theorem 5.1,
an isolated component also has an isotrivial restriction. These are the exact
specializations at issue, not a claim that no argument can ever connect the
methods with arithmetic periods.

**Bakker--Pila--Tsimerman, arXiv:2410.01248, Theorem 1.1, p.3, and §4.2,
pp.14--15.** Their theorem identifies algebraic closures of fields of germs
of period functions and derivatives of Hodge-period coordinates under the
stated absence of an infinite unitary factor; the elliptic example expresses
the relations using \(\tau,j',j''\). The passage from such germs to values
at a fixed point has a nonzero kernel: a local parameter (s-s_0\) is nonzero
as a germ but evaluates to zero. Moreover evaluation is not a differential
map, since evaluation of (d(s-s_0)/ds\) is one whereas the derivative of its
constant evaluated value is zero. Thus the field-of-germs identity alone
does not supply an injective arithmetic specialization. Establishing an
appropriate specialization statement is the precise remaining step in this
route.

## TG-9. Elliptic logarithms and the current arithmetic value bounds

The main input theorem of Senthil Kumar, *Algebraic independence of values of
Weierstrass elliptic and zeta functions* (2026), Theorem 1, was opened. It
requires \(u_1,u_2,\omega\) to be \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent and
\((\mathbb Zu_1+\mathbb Zu_2)\cap\Lambda=0\), and gives an at-least-two
lower bound among the listed invariants, periods, logarithms, and their
\(\wp,\zeta\)-values.

Taking the extra logarithms to be lattice periods violates the lattice-
intersection condition. Taking them to be the ratios \(\tau,\overline\tau\)
does not make their \(\wp,\zeta\)-values algebraic or place those values in
\(\mathbb Q(\tau,\overline\tau)\). The exact additions to the candidate
field are \(\wp(\tau),\wp(\overline\tau),\zeta(\tau),
\zeta(\overline\tau)\), together with the original periods. No field
containment eliminating these quantities follows from (1). Thus neither
substitution proves (TG).

Brownawell--Kubota, *The algebraic independence of Weierstrass functions and
some related numbers*, Acta Arithmetica XXXIII (1977), Appendix B, Theorem 8,
printed p.143, does contain the expression
\(\eta_2=\overline\tau\eta_1+a\omega_1\). Its explicit hypothesis is
that \(\tau\) is imaginary quadratic. The proof uses a nonreal lattice
endomorphism; it cannot be substituted for a non-CM relation. The functional
independence theorem in the same paper concerns functions of a variable.
McCulloch's 2024/2026 paper, Theorem 2.7 and §4, makes the specialization
particularly explicit: the lower bound is over \(\mathbb C\) for
nonconstant analytic (z_i\)'s, so constant period ratios are already in its
base field and violate its independence-of-functions hypothesis.

## TG-10. The G-function route and its additional arithmetic input

André's primary survey *G-functions, motives, and unlikely intersections—old
and new*, Rend. Lincei Mat. Appl.36 (2025), 381--392, published in 2026, was
opened at §§2.2, 2.3, 3.1, 3.2.3, 3.3.2. For a pencil and a nonsingular base
point it gives the precise matrix relation
\[
 G(s)=P(s)P(s_0)^{-1}.
 \tag{9}
\]
A relation among the entries of (P(s_1)\) therefore becomes a relation
among (G(s_1)P(s_0)\), generally with arithmetic period coefficients from
(P(s_0)\). Obtaining a relation with algebraic coefficients among the
G-values requires eliminating these constants. For the two-ratio relation,
that elimination has not been established.

Bombieri's principle in §3.1 requires a global relation: the same polynomial
must vanish at all places where the indicated G-values converge. A complex
relation (1) does not provide its (p\)-adic counterparts. Even once that
input is supplied, the stated output bounds heights or the number of points
of bounded degree; a single fixed algebraic parameter is not excluded by
finiteness. Both omitted steps are explicit here: elimination in (9), and
globality at all the prescribed places. The survey's §2.3 records the
G-function proof of the existing lower bound two for an elliptic period
matrix; that bound is compatible with TG-6.

## 14. Schwarz reflection and the exact single-point obstruction (06:16 UTC)

This checks the proposed analytic-arc route rather than assuming that an
arithmetic relation at one point persists with algebraic modular values.
Suppose P(tau,bar(tau))=0 as in TG-2. Pass to the unique absolutely
irreducible component C over Qbar through this point. It is unique because
two distinct components meet in a finite algebraic set, whereas tau is
transcendental. For the same reason, the point is smooth and neither
projection is ramified there: singular points and finite ramification loci
on this algebraic curve are algebraic points. The involution
kappa(z,w)=(bar(w),bar(z)) fixes the point and preserves C. Consequently
there is a nonconstant real analytic fixed arc through it, and a holomorphic
algebraic Schwarz germ w=S(z) near tau satisfying S(tau)=bar(tau) and
bar(S)(S(z))=z. On that arc S(z)=bar(z).

The corresponding modular map is exactly
  z |-> (j(z), j(-S(z))).
It is holomorphic near tau, and on the fixed arc its values are
(j(z),bar(j(z))). The original arithmetic input says that ONE value,
(j(tau),j(-S(tau)))=(j0,bar(j0)), belongs to Qbar^2. It does not say that
the image arc is algebraic or that it contains any second algebraic pair.
The equality of the two coordinates by conjugation on the real arc is an
identity of real analytic maps and supplies no algebraic equation on their
values.

There is a sharper direct Ax--Schanuel test. Put C'={(z,-w):(z,w) in C}.
For a small complex arc U0 in C' through (tau,-bar(tau)), set
V=C' x A^2 and let U be its intersection with the graph of j^2 near the
chosen point. Then dim(V)=3 and dim(U)=1. Neither j-coordinate is constant.
A modular relation Phi_N(j(z),j(-S(z)))=0 identically would, by evaluation,
make E isogenous to its conjugate, already excluded. Thus Pila--Tsimerman
Theorem1.1 applies and gives the equality 3=1+2. It proves no contradiction.
Alternatively fixing the two modular values gives a one-dimensional V but
an isolated U whose modular projection is a weakly special point, excluded
by that theorem's hypothesis. These are the two exact possible inputs;
neither is a single-point arithmetic theorem.

For the version with derivatives, choose source jets z'=1,z''=0 and

## 15. Algebraic fractional-linear relations and elliptic determinant theorems

Suppose that the residual relation has the particularly restrictive form

  bar(tau)=(a tau+b)/(c tau+d),  M=((a,b),(c,d)) in GL2(Qbar).

Write tau=omega2/omega1 for the differential-normalized periods of E,
and use their actual complex conjugates for bar(E). Put

  u1=d omega1+c omega2,  u2=b omega1+a omega2,
  r=bar(omega1)/u1.

The denominator u1 is nonzero: c tau+d=0 would make tau algebraic,
unless c=d=0, which contradicts invertibility of M. The displayed
fractional-linear relation is exactly

  bar(omega1)=r u1,  bar(omega2)=r u2.

Thus the two Qbar-spans of the holomorphic periods are proportional by
the specific scalar r. For E nonisogenous to bar(E), r is necessarily
transcendental. Indeed both curves are non-CM (a CM elliptic curve is
isogenous to its conjugate); the period dimension formula for their
product gives independence of its eight ordinary and second-kind
period entries. In particular its four holomorphic entries are
Qbar-linearly independent. If r were algebraic, the first of the two
displayed equations would contradict that independence.
