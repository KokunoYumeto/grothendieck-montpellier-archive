## FG-1. Exact representability statement

Let R be noetherian. Let C be a small rigid symmetric R-linear exact
tensor category, and let omega_1,omega_2:C -> Proj_f(R) be faithful
exact tensor functors. The exact structure on the target consists of
short exact sequences of finite projective modules; these split as
R-modules and remain exact after every R-algebra extension.

Assume one object M generates every object up to isomorphism using
finite sums, tensor products, duals, and admissible subobjects and
quotients. Assume omega_1(M),omega_2(M) are finite free of the same rank.

Then the functor Isom^tensor(omega_1,omega_2) is a closed subfunctor of
Isom_R(omega_1(M),omega_2(M)), represented by a closed subscheme.
There is a finite diagram of ORIGINAL objects, maps and admissible
sequences such that a comparison on this diagram extends uniquely
to a tensor comparison on all of C.

After bases, the ambient scheme has coordinate ring

    B=R[u_ij,det(u)^(-1)],

with universal invertible matrix u and inverse v. Direct sums, tensor
products and duals give matrices u_P,v_P for the corresponding
expressions in M and its dual; their entries belong to B.
For a later finite projective module that need not be free, choose a
realization as the image of an idempotent on a finite free R-module
and write the equations there. Equivalently, a module-valued equation
is imposed by applying every element of the dual of its finite
projective Hom module; the resulting coefficient ideal is defined
globally over R. Thus the formulas below do not presume a global
basis on every omega_a(N). Over Z the finite projectives are free.

For an admissible monomorphism i:N -> P with P already constructed,
write i_a=omega_a(i), and choose R-linear retractions r_a with
r_a i_a=1. These exist because its cokernel is projective. Impose

    (1-i_2 r_2) u_P i_1=0,
    (1-i_1 r_1) v_P i_2=0.

Define

    u_N=r_2 u_P i_1,   v_N=r_1 v_P i_2.

The displayed equations say u_P maps image(i_1) into image(i_2) and
v_P maps image(i_2) into image(i_1). Over every R-algebra S, the split
identities persist, so direct substitution gives u_N v_N=1 and
v_N u_N=1. The construction is therefore an isomorphism on the
admissible subobject after every base change, not merely on R-points.

For an admissible quotient q:P -> N, preserve its kernel by the same
two equations. Choose R-linear sections s_a of q_a=omega_a(q).
Then

    u_N=q_2 u_P s_1,   v_N=q_1 v_P s_2

are the induced inverse maps on the quotients. Their independence of
the sections follows because the preserved kernels contain the
differences of any two sections. Again this is valid over every
R-algebra, since all the sequences split.

Proceed through one chosen admissible expression for every object
of C. Impose every original naturality equation

    u_Y omega_1(f)=omega_2(f) u_X

and all tensor, unit, symmetry and dual compatibility equations.
Expressions for the same object are identified by their original
isomorphisms, so these equations also ensure independence of the
chosen presentations. Each equation has coefficients in B. Conversely
a solution to all these equations gives the specified inverse maps
on every object and is a tensor natural isomorphism. Its component
on M determines all other components, proving the closed embedding
and uniqueness.

Let I be the ideal generated by all coefficients of these equations.
The ring B is noetherian, so I is finitely generated. More precisely
I is generated by a finite subset of the ORIGINAL equations: choose
finite ideal generators; each is a finite linear combination of
original equation coefficients; take the union of those finite sets.
Enlarge the finite set of involved objects and maps by the finite
admissible presentations used to construct their matrices. This
produces the asserted finite original diagram.

Prescribing a component on an already included Artin or CM object
adds ordinary matrix equations, so the same conclusion holds with
such prescribed finite data.

## FG-2. Application to the quartic comparison, with lattice scope

Let sigma be the E.2.6 element in Gal(Qbar/Fcm) that exchanges the
two real roots of x^4-x-1, and let v,w be the corresponding embeddings
of k=Q(alpha). Consider an exact rigid Z-linear category C as in FG-1
with two actual integral Betti functors omega_v,omega_w. Its rational
objects and maps are abelian motives and absolute Hodge morphisms.

The integral hypothesis needed for the geometric application is
explicit: the generator M has a specified admissible expression in
tensor constructions, sums and duals of finitely many actual abelian
and Artin generators, and its integral Betti lattices are the lattices
given by that expression. All other objects of C have the stipulated
admissible expressions from M. Thus an admissible subobject has its
saturated image lattice and an admissible quotient has its free
quotient lattice. Arbitrary unrelated lattices on the same rational
motive are not supplied by this hypothesis.

The finite-diagram Shimura construction in
work/agent_real_abelian_lift.md Section2, BEFORE its real Hodge
correction, gives an integral tensor comparison T_0 on every finite
such diagram, with the sigma-selected Artin permutation. The indexed
absolute Hodge tensors include each requested rational morphism;
its actual Betti matrix is integral because it is a morphism of C.
Preservation of the ambient lattices and of the rational image
subspaces gives preservation of their saturated intersection
lattices. The quotient maps give the corresponding integral maps
on free quotients. Hence T_0 applies to the finite diagram furnished
by FG-1.

Its component on M satisfies the finite set of original equations
generating I and therefore all equations. FG-1 extends it uniquely
to a global integral tensor comparison on C. In particular this
specified single-generator exact integral model cannot separate
the two quartic places.

This assertion includes all morphisms between objects already
admissibly generated, whether or not each individual morphism is
admissible. It does not identify C with the full geometric
correspondence diagram. For example an isogeny q:E -> E/H induces
an injection on H1 whose cokernel is finite torsion. That injection
is not an admissible subobject construction in the exact category
of finite free Z-modules. If both E and E/H already have independent
admissible presentations, q remains a legitimate ordinary morphism
and its naturality equation is included. The proof makes no claim
that every isogenous quotient object lacks such a presentation.
Rather, it identifies exactly the additional presentation required
before the noetherian argument can be applied to that object.

The full pure integral question E.2.3(d), including every geometric
lattice and all finite-extension quotient diagrams of E.2.9, remains
outside the proved conclusion until these hypotheses are verified
for that entire diagram. No local/adelic comparison is promoted to
an integral one; the integral T_0 is the actual path transport on
the explicitly defined finite diagram.
