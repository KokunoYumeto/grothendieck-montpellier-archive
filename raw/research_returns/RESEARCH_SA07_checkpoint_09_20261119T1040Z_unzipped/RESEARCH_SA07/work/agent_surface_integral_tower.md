# Integral comparison for all abelian surfaces: exact finite-family proof

Author/checker: period_generation, 2026-11-19. Canonical result E.2.25. Root and elliptic_splitting independently
passed the full proof and opened source maps; integrated after authorization. This extends the exact construction of

## AS-1. The exact algebraic groups and actual adelic openness

Let A_i/Qbar be finitely many pairwise geometrically nonisogenous, absolutely
simple, non-CM abelian varieties of dimension one or two. Put
V_i=H_1(A_i(C),Q), Lambda_i=H_1(A_i(C),Z), and choose labelled algebraic
polarizations psi_i. Enlarge a finite field of definition so that all geometric
endomorphisms and the required finite set of labelled Hodge tensors are defined.
The geometric endomorphism algebras of the simple surfaces are precisely Q,
a real quadratic field F, or an indefinite quaternion division algebra B;
the remaining simple-surface case is CM and is already covered by E.2.6.

Here are the groups, as actual subgroups of GL(V_i), with their actual actions.

* End^0(A_i)=Q: H_i=Sp(V_i,psi_i), and M_i=GSp(V_i,psi_i).
* End^0(A_i)=F real quadratic: V_i is two-dimensional over F. Rosati fixes F.
  There is a unique F-bilinear alternating form Psi_i with
  Tr_F/Q(a Psi_i(v,w))=psi_i(av,w) for all a in F. Consequently
  H_i=Res_F/Q SL_F(V_i), and M_i consists of F-linear transformations with
  F-determinant in Q^*. The determinant is the common rational multiplier.
* End^0(A_i)=B quaternionic: V_i is a free left B-module of rank one.
  Its endomorphism centralizer is B^op acting on the right. The multiplier
  of right multiplication by b is Nrd(b), so
  H_i=SL_1(B^op), and M_i=(B^op)^*.

These identifications have a direct primary-source verification that avoids an
unproved maximality assertion. Lombardo, Surfaces, Theorem1.3 p2,
Corollary1.5 p3, and Theorem1.7 p3 give the corresponding full large-prime
images. In the quaternionic case Lemmas5.3--5.4 p19 identify the commuting
right and left matrix actions explicitly; Lemma5.6 pp19--20 identifies the
right determinant with the cyclotomic character. The Mumford--Tate equality
for each A_i follows from Lombardo, Nonsimple, Corollary4.5 p12. The inclusion
MT(A_i) in the displayed endomorphism/polarization stabilizer is immediate
from the labelled tensors. At any one sufficiently large prime the inclusion
becomes equality by the cited full-image theorem and Mumford--Tate equality.
Faithful scalar extension Q->Q_l then gives equality over Q. This also proves
the quaternionic reduced-norm multiplier identity over Q from the actual
right-matrix identity at such a prime.

For the RM form, positivity of Rosati makes its restriction to the totally
real field the identity: a nontrivial involution on a real quadratic field
sends a nonzero trace-zero a to -a and makes Tr(a a^*) negative. The trace
pairing determines Psi_i uniquely; self-adjointness gives F-bilinearity,
alternation, and nondegeneracy. A two-dimensional alternating form has
similitude equal to determinant, which proves the stated rational multiplier.

For the product A=prod A_i, Lombardo, Nonsimple, Corollary4.4 p12 gives

    H(A)=prod H_i = H,
    MT(A) = M = G_m H,
    M^{der}=H.

The product assertion is an equality in the actual block representation,
not merely an abstract isomorphism: the projections are the given factor
maps, and the cited theorem proves the inclusion into the product is onto.
The passage from its l-adic assertion to the Hodge assertion uses the same
corollary's Mumford--Tate equality. All H_i are simply connected and semisimple.
Each contains -1 in the actual representation. The common multiplier gives
the exact sequence

    1 -> H -> M --nu--> G_m -> 1.                         (AS1.1)

Indeed M is the image of G_m x H by (t,h)->t h, with kernel the diagonal
mu_2 generated by (-1,-I); nu(t h)=t^2. Thus the multiplier-one kernel
is precisely H, including its scheme structure. There is no disconnected
extra kernel in (AS1.1).

The algebraic fundamental group pi_1(M) is Z via nu: the coroot lattice
is the full cocharacter lattice of a maximal torus of the simply connected
H, and the quotient cocharacter lattice is that of G_m. The Hodge
cocharacter has multiplier z or z^{-1}, according to homology convention,
so its class generates this Z. Cadoret--Moonen Sections2.2--2.3 p4 therefore
give Hodge maximality. Their Theorem A(ii) p2 now applies to the already
proved Mumford--Tate equality for the actual finite product A. After one
finite extension K, the actual adelic image

    rho(Gamma_K) is compact open in M(A_f).               (AS1.2)

This is a conclusion for the full restricted-product topology. It is not
deduced by merely multiplying individually open l-adic images. Enlarge K
to a finite Galois field as needed: a finite-index subgroup of a compact
group has closed finite-index image, hence open image, so (AS1.2) persists.

The Tate module and source convention is H_1. Cadoret--Moonen formulate
their representation on H^1; taking inverse dual carries their actual
integral comparison and algebraic group isomorphically to this convention.
In particular the absolute value of the multiplier exponent is one, which
is the precise Hodge-maximality calculation used above.

## AS-2. Open finite-family image over Gamma_Fcm

Enlarge the finite set of geometric classes to its finite Gamma_Q-orbit.
This is finite because each model is over a number field. Select one A_i
in each class. Actual complex conjugation c permutes the classes by pi,
with pi^2=1. Choose actual isogenies phi_i:c A_i -> A_{pi(i)} and take
K/Q finite Galois defining the models, endomorphisms, polarizations,
the phi_i, and the field needed in AS-1. Define

    C_i=(phi_i)_* c_i : V_i -> V_{pi(i)},

where c_i is the actual anti-complex-linear map on Betti homology.
Functoriality of the integral Betti--etale comparison gives, for gamma in
Gamma_K, the exact identity

    C_i rho_i(c gamma c) C_i^{-1} = rho_{pi(i)}(gamma).    (AS2.1)

The maps C_i identify the Hodge groups H_i with H_{pi(i)}. To see this
without presuming that phi_i preserves a selected polarization, use that
phi_i is a rational Hodge isomorphism and that c_i changes h(z) into
h(bar z). The rational algebraic group generated by those two Hodge
homomorphisms is the same group. Thus their derived groups are identified.
Equivalently, the change of polarization lies in the labelled Rosati
endomorphism space, centralized by H_i.

Let G=rho(Gamma_K), and let theta on G be induced by c gamma c through
(AS2.1). Set

    N=rho(Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Qcm).

This is closed and normal in G and is contained in H(A_f), since
Q^ab is contained in Qcm and the common multiplier is cyclotomic.
Actual c is central on Qcm. Hence for each gamma in Gamma_K,

    [gamma,c] in Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Qcm,
    g theta(g)^{-1} in N,  g=rho(gamma).                  (AS2.2)

By (AS1.2), G contains independently supported open subgroups in every
local factor of H, and full standard integral factors at all but finitely
many primes. All commutators below are formed from these supported
elements; consequently they prove an actual product inclusion.

### AS-2a. Swapped classes or swapped local factors

For a theta-orbit of length two, take an element g supported on its first
factor. Then g theta(g)^{-1} has support on the two factors. Its commutator
with h supported on the first factor is [g,h] on the first factor and 1
on the other. Normality of N therefore supplies the supported derived
subgroup of each open local factor. A semisimple local group has an open
derived subgroup of every sufficiently small open subgroup, by the explicit
analytic commutator argument in AS-2c below. At a good prime the full
SL_2 or Sp_4 integral group is perfect, by the root identities in AS-2d.

### AS-2b. Fixed classes: nontriviality on every local simple factor

If End^0=Q, H_i=Sp_4 (or SL_2 for an elliptic factor). If conjugation by
C_i were trivial, C_i would lie in its scalar centralizer. A nonzero
scalar is complex-linear, whereas C_i is anti-complex-linear, a
contradiction. The noncentrality of a rational matrix remains true at
every completion and modulo every sufficiently large prime.

For RM, C_i induces an automorphism kappa of the labelled real quadratic
field F. If kappa is nontrivial, then at split primes it swaps the two
SL_2 factors and AS-2a applies. At an inert or ramified prime it acts
nontrivially on the centroid F_v of the Q_l-Lie algebra sl_2(F_v), so
its automorphism of that Lie algebra is not the identity. This Lie algebra
is Q_l-simple: over an algebraic closure its two simple summands are
transitively permuted by Gal(Q_lbar/Q_l), and an ideal is a stable sum
of such summands.

If kappa is the identity, C_i is an F-linear 2x2 matrix. On each real
F-embedding its action is anti-complex-linear on a real plane. In such a
plane a nonzero anti-complex-linear map has trace zero and square a
positive scalar. Thus Tr_F(C_i)=0 and C_i^2=d_i with d_i totally positive.
In particular it is not an F-scalar. Every embedding F->F_v is injective,
so C_i is nonscalar in every F_v matrix factor; for all but finitely many
primes its reduction is nonscalar in every residue-field factor as well.
This last claim follows by choosing one nonzero off-diagonal entry or
diagonal difference in F and excluding the divisors of its field norm.

For QM, let B be the labelled left quaternion algebra. The automorphism
of B induced by C_i is inner, by Skolem--Noether; choose u in B^* realizing
it. The map D_i=u^{-1}C_i centralizes B, so is right multiplication by a
nonzero b_i in B^op. Left multiplication by u commutes with H_i, hence
Ad(C_i)|H_i=Ad(D_i)|H_i. Multiplication by u is a Hodge endomorphism,
so D_i remains anti-complex-linear. Under B_R=M_2(R), the right action
of b_i is anti-complex-linear on the standard real plane; its trace is
zero and its determinant is negative. In particular b_i is noncentral.
It stays noncentral in every completion of B and outside finitely many
primes in the split matrix reductions. The local Lie algebras of
SL_1(B^op) are forms of sl_2 and are simple in characteristic zero.

### AS-2c. Actual bad-prime openness by analytic commutators

Consider one fixed local simple factor J over Q_l and the supported
closed subgroup R=N intersect J(Q_l), normal under an open subgroup U.
The preceding step proves that theta acts nontrivially on Lie J.
All required small analytic curves in J are explicit. For X in its Lie
algebra use the Cayley transform

    q_X(t)=(1+tX)(1-tX)^{-1},  q_X'(0)=2X.               (AS2.3)

For Sp_4, X^t psi+psi X=0 makes the two matrices 1+tX and 1-tX have
the same pulled-back alternating form, so q_X(t) is symplectic.
For SL_2 over F_v, trace X=0 makes det(1+tX)=det(1-tX).
For a quaternion division factor, reduced trace X=0 gives the identical
reduced-norm equality. Thus (AS2.3) is an actual J-valued rational
analytic curve, also at division primes, on a sufficiently small Q_l-ball.
It lies in U after shrinking that ball.

The curve q_X(t) theta(q_X(t))^{-1} belongs to R and has derivative
2(X-theta X). Its conjugates by U have derivatives spanning Lie J:
their span is stable under Ad U, hence under the Lie algebra (differentiate
Ad q_Y(t)); it is a nonzero ideal in a simple Lie algebra. Choose finitely
many such curves whose derivatives form a basis. Their product is an
analytic map with invertible Jacobian at the origin. In local coordinates
it has the form Jx+P(x), with P of order at least two. On a small ball,
J^{-1}P is strictly contracting. Iterating x -> y-J^{-1}P(x) solves
Jx+P(x)=Jy for each sufficiently small y. The image therefore contains
an open neighborhood of 1 in J(Q_l), entirely inside R.

The same argument proves openness of the closed derived subgroup of
any open local subgroup U of H: derivatives of

    g q_X(t) g^{-1} q_X(t)^{-1}

are 2(Ad_g-1)X. As g varies in U these vectors span the whole semisimple
Lie algebra. Indeed a linear functional annihilating all of them also
annihilates [Y,X], by differentiating g=q_Y(s), and [Lie H,Lie H]=Lie H.
Choose finitely many independent derivatives and use the same contraction.
For a product of local simple factors use their individually supported
curves. This is a proof about actual group neighborhoods, not an assertion
that a Zariski-dense subgroup is automatically open.

### AS-2d. Full groups at all sufficiently large primes

Exclude a finite set containing 2,3, the field/quaternion discriminants,
denominators of all fixed maps, failures of the noncentral reductions,
and the finite set in (AS1.2). The supported integral factors are
Sp_4(Z_l), SL_2(Z_l), or SL_2(O_v) for an unramified quadratic extension.

For Sp_4, the reduction of R is normal in Sp_4(F_l). Some commutator
g theta(g)^{-1} is noncentral. Otherwise theta would differ from the
identity by a central character of the perfect group Sp_4(F_l), so theta
would be the identity; the centralizer of the standard symplectic action
is scalar, contradicting the chosen noncentral C_i reduction. Mayle--Wang,
Lemma2.3(4) p6, gives that a normal noncentral subgroup is the full group.
Lombardo, Surfaces, Lemma2.4 p6 (Serre's symplectic lifting lemma), then
gives R=Sp_4(Z_l).

For SL_2 over the residue field k_v, the proof of PSL_2 simplicity in
E.2.24 applies over every finite field of characteristic at least five:
the projective-line action is faithful and doubly transitive; the normal
abelian root group in a point stabilizer and perfectness force every
nontrivial normal subgroup to be the whole projective group. Perfectness
then rules out a remaining quotient of order two. A nontrivial semilinear
field automorphism cannot act trivially on this group: traces of matrices
in SL_2(k_v) cover k_v, so identity would force it to fix every field element;
the remaining matrix centralizer is scalar. Thus the normal subgroup
generated by the theta-commutators has full reduction. Swapped residue
factors are isolated by AS-2a.

For completeness, lifting full SL_2(k_v) to SL_2(O_v), where O_v/Z_l is
unramified, is the same explicit all-layer argument as in E.2.24.
Lift U(a) for each a in k_v to g in R, and write
g=U(a_tilde)(1+lB) modulo l^2. In g^l the first error is the sum of
Ad(U(-j a_tilde))B over 0<=j<l, a polynomial of degree at most two in j.
The sums of 1,j,j^2 vanish in k_v, so

    g^l = 1+l a E_12 mod l^2.

All a, lower roots, and conjugates span sl_2(k_v) as an F_l-space.
The identity (1+l^n X)^l=1+l^{n+1}X modulo l^{n+2} supplies every
successive layer. Closedness gives the full integral group.

These integral groups are perfect. For SL_2 use
[diag(2,1/2),U(t)]=U(3t), and its lower-root counterpart; 3 is a unit.
For Sp_4 take its standard root subgroups. On a short root choose a
diagonal torus element with root value 2, and on a long root one with
root value 4. The same commutator formula has coefficient 1 or 3.
The root subgroups generate Sp_4 over a local ring by symplectic Gaussian
elimination: move a unit coordinate of a primitive first column into its
first position, clear the other coordinates with symplectic elementary
operations, use the paired column to clear its dual, and reduce to Sp_2.
This also proves the finite-field perfectness used above.

Since every local factor was supported independently, N contains a
product that is open in H(A_f): use open compact subgroups at the finite
exceptional primes and the full integral factors elsewhere, and take
the closure of the finite supported products. N is closed, so it contains
this full product.

Finally set U_0=Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Qcm. Its closed derived subgroup
is contained in Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Fcm: for every CM field L,
L^ab Qcm/Qcm is abelian, and U_0 maps to this abelian Galois group.
Compactness gives

    rho(overline([U_0,U_0]))=overline([N,N]).

The right side is open in H(A_f), by AS-2c at the exceptional primes and
perfectness at all other primes, with the same supported-product argument.
We have therefore proved

    rho(Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Fcm) is open in H(A_f).   (AS2.4)

Further finite extensions of K preserve this openness. Notice the direction
of the group inclusion: only the derived subgroup inclusion in Gamma_Fcm
was used; no equality between these infinite Galois subgroups is claimed.

## AS-3. Exact rational and integral approximation in each frozen target

All H_i satisfy the proved strong-approximation criterion D.4.1.
They are semisimple and simply connected, with noncompact real points:
Sp_4(R); SL_2(R)^2 for RM; and SL_2(R) for the indefinite quaternion case.
Restriction of scalars and products are the exact maps in D.4.1. Thus

    H_i(Q) is dense in H_i(A_f).                         (AS3.1)

Fix sigma in Gamma_Fcm and one A_i with its conjugate target fixed.
E.2.11 supplies a rational tensor lift of the prescribed all-CM/Artin
comparison. Its block selected by the finite-model Artin idempotent gives

    T_0: V_i -> V_{sigma A_i}

over Q, intertwining each labelled endomorphism and polarization and
fixing the one Tate line. The actual completed transport rho_i(sigma)
has exactly the same tensor, endomorphism and Tate properties. Therefore

    a=T_0^{-1}rho_i(sigma) belongs to H_i(A_f).           (AS3.2)

One can check this directly from the centralizers in AS-1: the difference
centralizes the labelled endomorphism algebra and has multiplier one.
It also follows from the group map: the kernel D of the all-CM restriction
has connected semisimple image in the finite geometric group MT(A_i);
its quotient is a quotient of the geometric CM protorus, so this image
contains MT(A_i)^{der}; semisimplicity gives the reverse inclusion.
Thus the actual finite image of D is precisely H_i.

Let U be any open subgroup of H_i(A_f) contained in the integral lattice
stabilizer. By (AS3.1) choose h in H_i(Q) intersect aU. Then

    T=T_0 h lies in rho_i(sigma)U.                       (AS3.3)

It is rational and its completion is an integral isomorphism from
Lambda_i tensor Zhat to Lambda_{sigma A_i} tensor Zhat. Matrix entries
and inverse entries therefore lie in Q intersect Zhat=Z. Consequently
T is an actual integral unimodular isomorphism of the two Betti lattices.
This proves the required integral coset intersection, including the target
lattice and all labelled endomorphism constraints. No adelic torsor point
was promoted to a rational point: T_0 is the independently proved rational
point from E.2.11, and (AS3.1) is applied to its actual H_i-torsor.

## AS-4. One global quartic swap for all surface and CM factors

Let A_{<=2,CM} consist of all abelian varieties over Qbar geometrically
isogenous to products of simple factors of dimension at most two and
arbitrary CM abelian varieties. Use the full geometric integral diagram
of E.2.16, with all isogeny lattices, tensor maps, products, specified
projectors, algebraic correspondences, finite-model Artin modules and
all Artin objects. The polarization/Tate conventions are the same.

Enumerate one non-CM simple A_n in every geometric isogeny class of
dimension at most two using algebraic model data. Choose the first algebraic
basis of H^0(A_n,Omega^1) in that data ordering, also for each conjugate.
Its period map identifies H_1(A_n,Z) with a full lattice in C^dim(A_n).
Among ordered integral bases minimize the sum of squared Euclidean norms
of their period vectors, then their real-coordinate lexicographic tuple.
Any first available basis supplies a finite norm bound, and there are only
finitely many ordered lattice bases under that bound, so the unique choice
exists. Root independently endorsed this direct existence proof. No
computational decidability is asserted.

Start with the nonempty compact fibre

    C_0={sigma in Gamma_Fcm : sigma|P=(12)(34)}

from E.2.6, for the fixed splitting field and root labels of x^4-x-1.
At stage n choose a finite Galois K_n containing P, the first n model
data and finite class orbits, their endomorphisms and isogeny comparison
maps needed in AS-2, and the nth field in the fixed Galois exhaustion.
The fields are increasing and exhaust Qbar. Within the previous compact
fibre, choose the first nonempty K_n-restriction cylinder, C'_n. This
freezes all relevant conjugate models and their integral bases.

For any sigma_n in C'_n the stabilizer is exactly

    H_n=Gamma_Fcm intersect Gamma_Kn
        intersect all_{i<n} ker(rho_{A_i}),
    C'_n=sigma_n H_n.                                  (AS4.1)

The composition formula rho_{A_i}(sigma_n k)=
rho_{A_i}(sigma_n)rho_{A_i}(k), valid because k fixes K_n, proves (AS4.1).
Apply (AS2.4) to the finite family A_1,...,A_n. Its image is open in the
product of the actual H_i(A_f), hence the kernel of the first n-1 full
representations has open image U_n in H_n(A_f). Here H_n(A_f) denotes
the algebraic factor attached to A_n, not the Galois subgroup in (AS4.1).
To avoid that notational collision in the TeX version, call this factor
J_n and the Galois stabilizer Kappa_n.

Thus the possible n-th matrices form the open coset

    rho_{A_n}(sigma_n) U_n.                             (AS4.2)

AS-3 proves this coset contains an actual integral lattice isomorphism.
Choose the first such integer matrix in the fixed entry-height/lexicographic
enumeration; impose it to obtain a nonempty compact C_n. The C_n are nested.
Their intersection is nonempty, and because the K_n exhaust Qbar, it
contains exactly one sigma_*. This is a specified element, with no
assertion that the first-extendible-branch rule is computationally decidable.
It has the prescribed quartic action and transports every A_n lattice
integrally by its actual completed Galois map.

Every CM factor has the prescribed integral algebraic comparison of
E.2.6 for this same sigma_*. For a product B of representatives and CM
factors, use the direct sum map. For any geometric isogeny q:B->A,

    L_A=q_*^{-1}Lambda_A,
    Lambda_B subset L_A subset (1/m)Lambda_B,
    L_A/Lambda_B=ker(q) in B[m](C).

The actual completed transport sends this finite subgroup to ker(sigma q).
Its integral source map therefore sends L_A onto L_{sigma A}. This gives
the actual integral isomorphism

    T_A=(sigma q)_* T_B q_*^{-1}:Lambda_A->Lambda_{sigma A}.

The same completion injectivity used in E.2.16 makes this independent of
q and every decomposition, and proves all algebraic-correspondence,
tensor, exterior-power and projector equations. H^1 uses inverse dual;
the common Tate map is 1. The finite-model Artin idempotents give the
maps e_u->e_{sigma_*u} and hence the exact descent of E.2.3. Thus this
is one integral tensor comparison for the whole stated diagram, including
the two specified quartic real places. Labelled real Frobenius remains
subject to the explicit obstruction E.2.7.

The comparison scope now includes every abelian surface over Qbar. It
does not assert the all-abelian comparison or the full pure comparison:
the actual remaining abelian factors include simple non-CM varieties of
dimension at least three. The curve-cover criterion E.2.22 remains the
exact test for extending the same sigma_* to the entire abelian diagram.
