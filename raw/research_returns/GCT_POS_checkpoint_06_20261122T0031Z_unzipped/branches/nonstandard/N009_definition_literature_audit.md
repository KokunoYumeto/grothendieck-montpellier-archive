## The exact integral object

The order in N-009 is exactly the unital A-subalgebra generated by P_s=C'_s⊗C'_s+C_s⊗C_s inside the full ambient Hecke tensor product, with A=Z[v,v⁻¹]. This is GCT IV, Definition11.1, equation(93), printed p.59, after renaming the paper's q to v. The ground ring A=Z[q,q⁻¹] is fixed in §2.1, printed p.16. The full rank-four generic dimension114 and nine irreducibles are in §11.8, printed p.67. They are not in §11.7, which concerns the rank-three canonical basis.

The ambient Hecke algebra convention is GCT IV equation(22), printed p.20: (T_s−q)(T_s+q⁻¹)=0. The canonical generators are fixed in §3.1, printed p.21: C'_s=T_s+q⁻¹ and C_s=T_s−q. These become exactly the arithmetic used in every certificate when q_IV=v. The counting variable in the present rank-three discussion is q_count=v²; it must not be confused with q_IV.

No localization or saturation is part of Definition11.1. The neighboring A′=A[1/[2]] is used to identify normalized primitive projectors and later the rank-two Hopf structure (Proposition11.3). It does not change the definition of the P-generated order. Remark11.9, p.61, explicitly distinguishes abstract specialization from its ambient image and observes freeness only after tensoring with Q. Theorem11.16, pp.65–66, separately proves A-freeness for rank three. It does not assert rank-four A-freeness.

The nonstandard Temperley–Lieb quotient in Blasiak (2012) is a different object: its rank-four generic dimension is89, by Corollary7.1, whereas the full algebra here has dimension114. Neither a quotient nor a saturation of the full order is substituted into N-009.

## A normalization typo in one paragraph

The introductory paragraph of §11.1, printed p.58, writes the two q-powers in C'_s and C_s in the opposite positions. This conflicts with the established convention in equation(22) and §3.1, and with its own assertions of bar invariance, primitive projectors, and the later quadratic relation P_s²=[2]²P_s.

The certificates use the consistent canonical definitions from §3.1. This is independently corroborated by Blasiak (2015), arXiv:1010.0421v2, §§2.1–2.3, printed pp.3–4: ground ring Z[u,u⁻¹], the same Hecke quadratic relation, C'_s=T_s+u⁻¹, C_s=T_s−u, and Definition2.2 for the P-generated subalgebra. Thus this is not a choice to reinterpret the order after seeing the computation.

A direct consistency check is decisive: if one instead used the reversed paragraph formulas with the unchanged Hecke relation, their value on the trivial⊗trivial character would be 5v²+v⁻²−2 rather than D=v²+2+v⁻². They would not obey the specified P_s²=DP_s relation generically. The supplied code, prompt, and cited rank-three presentation all use the consistent convention.

## Localization boundary

The obstruction point is (2,v−1), where [2]=v+v⁻¹ lies in the maximal ideal. There is no extension of the certificate map A→R=(Z/1024)[η]/η⁸, v↦1+η, that inverts2 or [2]. Consequently N-009 says nothing by itself about freeness after either localization. It also does not address a different lattice such as the saturation (Q(v)B4)∩(H4⊗H4). The theorem disproves a basis for the full P-generated integral order asked for in item7.

A positive Q(v)-basis and a positive Q[v±]-basis remain possible and are established in N-003/N-004. They do not contradict nonfreeness of the specified A-order.
