\subsection*{Established tensor constraints beyond the formal model}
The actual tensor coefficients obey additional identities that are
not among the assumptions of PNEW-014. For $s\ge1$,
\[
 k_n(s)=\dim\bigl(\Lambda^{ns}((\mathbb C^s)^{\otimes3})
                         \bigr)^{SL_s^3}
       \le {s^3\choose ns},
\]
and exterior complementation gives
\[
 k_n(s)=k_{s^2-n}(s)\quad(0\le n\le s^2),\qquad
 k_{s^2}(s)=1,\qquad k_n(s)=0\quad(n>s^2).
\]
The empty-rectangle coefficient at $n=0$ is interpreted as one.
These are established rectangular-symmetry and exterior-power
identities, not new theorems of this continuation: Briand, E.,
Orellana, R., and Rosas, M. (2015), \emph{Rectangular symmetries for
coefficients of symmetric functions}, Electronic Journal of
Combinatorics22(3), P3.15, Corollary8 and Remark9, printed p.~10;
the relevant preceding argument is Theorem6, pp.~8--9,
doi:10.37236/4808. The identities and their precise scope were checked
against the primary journal PDF.

For example, the formal PNEW-014 model has
$f_{16}(4)=66{,}386{,}566{,}128$, whereas the actual coefficient
$k_{16}(4)$ is one. Thus that model does not satisfy this stronger
actual tensor constraint. The identity compares different heights
$n$ as $s$ varies, and bounds the values on their actual residue
classes. It does not itself bound every constituent polynomial at
all positive arguments, as required by the strict indices. The
exact calculation and source-scope note are retained in
\nolinkurl{branches/plethysm/explorations/tensor_exterior_constraints.md}
and its accompanying certificate. No strict-index conclusion is
drawn from these identities alone.
