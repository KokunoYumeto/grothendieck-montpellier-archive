The following parts of the argument were checked in full.

1. **Bounds before removing the central extension.** A nontrivial χ self-twist makes the spectrum invariant under a nontrivial two-power rotation, hence partitions it into antipodal pairs. With at most one pair this gives dimension at most two. With no self-twist, the K-012 Parseval estimate allows at most one unpaired eigenvalue, and therefore dimension at most three. Only one constituent can contain the unique pair. Every other irreducible constituent is a line. Since the whole factor has dimension at least five, a line character is available in each factor.

2. **Untwisting all three projective actions.** Twisting a factor by one of its line characters cancels the scalar character of Z on that whole factor, so the representation genuinely descends to H. The product of the three chosen line characters is trivial on Z, because the original three scalar actions multiply to one. It therefore descends to H and is a power of χ. Consequently the combined twist preserves the tensor's invariance under K=ker χ. Individual twists need not preserve all H-invariance; the proof uses only K-invariance at this point, as required.

3. **Locating the residual action.** For any nonlinear irreducible H-representation, column orthogonality at g gives trace zero. Since g has two-power order M, the half-range powers of a primitive M-th root form a rational basis. Zero trace therefore gives equality of every antipodal eigenvalue multiplicity. Such a constituent is contained in the sole paired two-plane. Every remaining line carries a linear character of H, hence is trivial on K. The two-plane is H-invariant whether it is nonlinear or a sum of two lines. The determinant of the whole twisted factor is a linear character of H, so K acts with determinant one on that plane.

4. **The nonzero projection.** A nonzero tensor projection with exactly one paired factor is a nonzero element of P_A⊗U_B⊗U_C, and K acts trivially on the latter two factors. Expanding in a basis there produces a nonzero K-fixed vector in P_A. Every finite-order element of SL₂ fixing a nonzero vector has both eigenvalues one and is diagonalizable; it is therefore the identity. Thus K acts trivially on that full twisted factor and projectively trivially on the original one. The already checked endomorphism-algebra unit-group argument from K-012 then gives K=1.
