\subsection{The actual extreme-corner reduction at nineteen arrows}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: the extreme-corner cut at nineteen]
\label{CP2:A-extreme-minimal-group-nineteen}
A nineteen-arrow category of type $\CP^2$ cannot have a nongroup source
corner with one minimal row, or a nongroup sink corner with one minimal
column.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{CP2:A-extreme-minimal-group-reduction} replaces such a source
corner $M$ by its minimal group $G=eMe$ and each outgoing stalk by its
subset of arrows fixed by $e$. The resulting category has the same
actual homotopy type and at most $19-(|M|-|G|)\le18$ arrows, because
$M$ is not a group. This contradicts
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-global-lower-nineteen}. The sink case is dual.
\end{proof}

The separately frozen numerical derivative tests source and sink status
from the complete hom-cardinality matrix. For each corner table it
computes a minimal idempotent $e$, the sets $Me,eM,eMe$, and whether the
whole monoid is a group. The source predicate is
$|Me|=|eMe|<|M|$; the sink predicate is its left--right dual. Each
excluded complete corner choice is counted in
\texttt{proved\_corner\_choices}. All action enumeration, associativity
propagation, canonicalization, literal certificate construction, and
independent certificate checking remain unchanged. This is a table
predicate and does not exclude an entire cardinality profile unless
every corner choice passes a proved obstruction.
