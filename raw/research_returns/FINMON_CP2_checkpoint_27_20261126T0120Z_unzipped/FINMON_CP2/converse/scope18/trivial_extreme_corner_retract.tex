\subsection{Removing an extreme corner with trivial crossing actions}

\begin{theorem}[Proved: an actual retraction at a trivially acting extreme corner]
\label{CP2:A-trivial-extreme-corner-retract}
Let $p$ be a source or sink object of a finite category $C$,
with at least one nonempty strict hom-set incident to $p$.
Here ``source'' means that no strict arrow enters $p$, and
``sink'' is the opposite condition. Put $M=C(p,p)$.
Suppose every action of $M$ on an incident strict hom-set
is trivial. Replace $M$ by its identity, keeping all other
arrows and composition values, to obtain $C'$. There are
actual functors $i:C'\hookrightarrow C$ and $R:C\to C'$
with $Ri=\operatorname{id}_{C'}$, and their pullbacks are
isomorphisms on rational second cohomology. If
$BC\simeq\CP^2$, then $Bi$ is a homotopy equivalence.
The new category has exactly $|\operatorname{Mor}C|-(|M|-1)$
arrows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Treat a source object; the sink case follows by opposition.
The proposed functor $R$ fixes all objects and every arrow
outside $M$, and sends $M$ to $1_p$. This preserves the
corner multiplication and every cross-arrow product with
$M$, precisely by the trivial-action hypothesis. Products
in the full deletion $D=C\setminus\{p\}$ and its actions
on the connecting hom-sets are unchanged. There are no
arrows from $D$ back to $p$. These observations prove
functoriality, show that the retained arrows form a
subcategory, and give the literal identity $Ri=1$.

The category is the collage of its source corner and the
full block $D$. The connecting bar is nonempty by hypothesis.
Apply the opposite form of Lemma~\ref{CP2:A-trivial-cut-H2}
to this cut. It gives the stated rational second-cohomology
isomorphism without requiring $D$ to be connected.

Assume $BC\simeq\CP^2$. The actual retraction makes every
integral homology group of $BC'$ a direct summand of that
of $BC$. It also makes $BC'$ connected and simply connected.
Its only possible positive integral homology groups are
$0$ or $\mathbb Z$ in degrees two and four. The rational
second isomorphism forces the second group to be $\mathbb Z$,
so the split pullback $R^*:H^2(BC';\mathbb Z)\to
H^2(BC;\mathbb Z)$ is an isomorphism. Choose $v$ mapping
to the integral generator $u$. Naturality gives
$R^*(v^2)=u^2$, the primitive fourth generator. The split
fourth map is therefore also an isomorphism. It follows
that $Bi$ is an integral homology equivalence between
simply connected CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence
by \cite[Corollary~4.33]{CP2-Hatcher2002}. Exactly the
$|M|-1$ nonidentity corner arrows were removed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: singleton crossings at eighteen arrows]
\label{CP2:A-singleton-extreme-eighteen}
An eighteen-arrow category of type $\CP^2$ cannot have a
source or sink object with nontrivial corner, at least one
strict incident hom-set, and every nonempty strict incident
hom-set a singleton.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All crossing actions are then forced to be trivial. The
theorem would give an actual projective-plane model with
at most seventeen arrows, contrary to
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-global-lower-eighteen}.
\end{proof}
