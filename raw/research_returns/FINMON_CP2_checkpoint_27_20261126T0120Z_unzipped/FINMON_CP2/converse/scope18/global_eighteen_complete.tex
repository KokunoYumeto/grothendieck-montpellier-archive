\subsection{The complete exclusion of eighteen arrows}

\begin{theorem}[Proved: the unrestricted lower bound is nineteen arrows]
\label{CP2:A-global-lower-nineteen}
Every finite category whose classifying space is homotopy equivalent
to $\mathbb CP^2$ has at least nineteen arrows, counting identities.
Consequently the least arrow number satisfies
\[
 \boxed{19\leq n\leq22}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-global-lower-eighteen} already excludes every
budget at most seventeen.  Suppose a category $\mathcal C$ with
exactly eighteen arrows has $B\mathcal C\simeq\mathbb CP^2$,
and let $k$ be its number of objects.  It is nonempty and connected.

For $k=1$, the minimum-monoid classification
Theorem~\ref{A:monoid-minimum23-classification} requires at least
twenty-three arrows, a contradiction.

For $k=2$, Proposition~\ref{CP2:A-strong-two-eighteen} excludes
cyclic support.  In the remaining collage case, first choose the
positive-source orientation of
Lemma~\ref{CP2:A-two-object-positive-orientation}.
Corollary~\ref{CP2:A-two-object-eighteen-oriented-twenty-two}
then gives the complete twenty-two-budget scope.
Corollary~\ref{CP2:A-all-two-object-eighteen-budgets} excludes
all twenty-two.  This is the proved joint orientation argument;
it does not require both ordered corner profiles to satisfy the
positive-source hypothesis simultaneously.

For $k=3$, Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-strong-three-eighteen} excludes
cyclic support.  A connected acyclic support on three objects
is a source star, a sink star, or a full linear order: a directed
two-step path forces its third hom-set to be nonempty by composition.
The two star orientations are excluded by
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-star-eighteen-complete}, and the full linear
case by Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-linear-eighteen-complete}.

For $k=4$ and $k=5$, respectively, use
Theorems~\ref{CP2:A-four-eighteen-complete} and
\ref{CP2:A-five-eighteen-complete}.  Each includes its separate
cyclic-support reduction and its complete acyclic-support scope.
Finally, if $k\geq6$, then $18\leq2k+7$, so
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-high-all-eight} excludes the category for
arbitrary support.  These cases exhaust all finite categories
with eighteen arrows.

The independently checked twenty-two-arrow construction of
Theorem~\ref{A:category-upper22} supplies the stated upper bound.
\end{proof}
