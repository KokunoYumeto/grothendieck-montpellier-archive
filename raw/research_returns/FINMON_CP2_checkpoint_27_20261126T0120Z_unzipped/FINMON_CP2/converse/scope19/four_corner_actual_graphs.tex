\subsection{Actual graph comparisons for the small nonnilpotent corners}

\begin{lemma}[Proved: natural actual graph maps through order four]
\label{CP2:A-four-corner-actual-graph}
Let $N$ have order at most four and not be isomorphic to the
nilpotent monoid of Proposition~\ref{CP2:A-nilpotent-four-corner-four-states}.
For every finite left $N$-set $T$ there is an actual natural
rational homology equivalence from $B(N,T)$ to a finite graph.
For any commuting monoid action, its graph action sends an
oriented edge to an oriented edge with the same parameter or
collapses it to a vertex. Empty $T$ is allowed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the trivial right rational $N$ module is projective, the
actual orbit functor to the discrete set $N\backslash T$ has
the asserted properties. The minimal-ideal classification in
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-six-versus-four-semisimple-quotient} leaves
the following cases: the identity-adjoined right-zero bands
on two or three elements, and the four monoids
\[
 e_i e_j=e_j,\quad ae_i=e_i,\quad e_i a=e_{\tau(i)},
 \qquad (\tau,a^2)=(\mathrm{id},1),(\mathrm{id},a),
 ((01),1),(\mathrm{const}_0,a).
\]
Only the classification is used here, not that theorem's
order-six bound on the other corner.

Write $p_i$ for the actors of $e_i$, $A$ for that of $a$,
and $U$ for their common image. The $p_i$ fix $U$ pointwise;
when present, $A$ also fixes $U$ pointwise. Construct a graph
category with core vertices $U$ and additional midpoint
vertices as follows. From each midpoint draw one leg to
each specified endpoint. All legs are oriented from the
midpoint to the core; even when endpoints coincide their
legs remain distinct.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
N\text{ type}&\text{midpoints}&\text{endpoints}\\\hline
R_{c+1}&t\in T\setminus U&p_i t\quad(0\leq i<c)\\
(\mathrm{id},1)&A\text{-orbits in }T\setminus U&p_0t,p_1t\\
(\mathrm{id},a)&t\in AT\setminus U&p_0t,p_1t\\
((01),1)&\{t,At\}\text{ of size two}&p_0t,p_1t\\
(\mathrm{const}_0,a)&t\in T\setminus AT&p_0t,p_1t.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
For the involution with exchanged columns, choose one
representative of each two-point orbit just to name its legs.
This choice does not enter the underlying graph or its action.

Here is an actual functor from the left action category to
this graph category. Core states go to their core vertices.
In the five lines of (1), other states go respectively to
their own midpoint; their $A$-orbit; the point indexed by
$At$ (a core vertex when $At\in U$); their two-point orbit
or, if $At=t$, the vertex $p_0t=p_1t$; and their own midpoint
when $t\notin AT$, or the vertex $p_0t$ otherwise.
Every band arrow is sent to its corresponding leg if its
source maps to a midpoint and to an identity otherwise.
In the exchanged-column case, replacing a representative
by $At$ exchanges the two leg labels. The arrow $a$ is
sent to an identity in the first three extra-actor cases.
In the last case it is sent to the zero leg at a midpoint
and to an identity otherwise. Identity arrows go to identities.

These prescriptions respect all products. In particular,
in the exchanged-column case the relation $e_i a=e_{1-i}$
is exactly the leg exchange. In the last case the relations
$e_i a=e_0$ and $a^2=a$ say that a leg after the $a$ arrow
collapses to its zero leg. The relations $ae_i=e_i$ and
$e_i e_j=e_j$ hold because subsequent arrows at a core
vertex are identities. Thus the construction is a literal
category functor, not merely a chain comparison.

Every commuting map of $N$-sets induces the evident graph
map. On core vertices it is the given state map. On a
midpoint indexed by a state or orbit, take the image state's
assigned vertex or midpoint; each leg is sent to the band
leg at that image state, or collapsed when it is a core
vertex. This commutes strictly with the preceding functor.
It can permute the two legs of an orbit midpoint, but their
orientations still run from midpoint to core. It never
reverses an oriented leg.

We verify rational homology for this actual functor. The
right-projective resolutions in
Lemma~\ref{CP2:A-four-corner-graph-chain} give zero action-space
homology above degree one. Contract the zero leg at every
midpoint in the graph; these legs form a forest. The
resulting graph has vertex set $U$, with exactly the
degree-one bases and boundary maps of that lemma. Thus
the source and target have the same rational zeroth and
first Betti numbers. The functor is onto components, since
every component of the graph contains a core vertex.

It is also onto first homology. After the forest contraction,
every graph cycle is a linear combination of differences
of legs at the same midpoint, with zero endpoint boundary.
For a midpoint represented by $t$, lift such a difference
to the actual normalized action one-chain
\[
 [t\xrightarrow{e_i}p_i t]
       -[t\xrightarrow{e_0}p_0t].                            \tag{2}
\]
Its boundary is exactly $p_it-p_0t$, and its image is the
specified difference of graph legs. Hence a cycle lifts
to a cycle. The equal finite Betti numbers prove isomorphisms
in degrees zero and one. Both sides have zero higher
rational homology. This proves the asserted natural actual
rational equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: an order-eight center and nonnilpotent four-corners]
\label{CP2:A-star-eight-nonnilpotent-four-leaves}
A source star whose center has order at most eight, and
whose leaf corners have order at most four and avoid the
nilpotent four-element monoid, has zero rational degree-two
products whenever its rational homology is bounded.
There is no bound on its nonempty stalk sizes. The opposite
statement holds too.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The leaf block is rationally acyclic in positive degrees.
Collapse it in the actual mapping-cylinder model. As in
Proposition~\ref{CP2:A-star-eight-three-leaves}, this passes
boundedness and surjects onto the star's second cohomology.
The preceding functors give an actual equivariant rational
equivalence of its connecting action space to a disjoint
union of finite graphs. The nonnegative monoid bar preserves
this equivalence, as does the induced map of actual cofibers.
Apply Lemma~\ref{CP2:A-directed-graph-collapses-eight} and
pull back the zero products. Opposition gives the other case.
\end{proof}
