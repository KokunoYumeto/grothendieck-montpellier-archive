\subsection{Strict reductions in the nineteen-arrow three-object engines}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: trivial extreme actions at nineteen arrows]
\label{CP2:A-three-object-trivial-extreme-actions-nineteen}
A nineteen-arrow category with connected three-object acyclic support
cannot have type $\CP^2$ if a nontrivial source corner acts trivially on
every outgoing strict hom-set, or a nontrivial sink corner acts trivially
on every incoming strict hom-set.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The extreme corner has a nonempty incident strict hom-set because the
support is connected. Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-trivial-extreme-corner-retract}
then gives an actual retract of type $\CP^2$ with exactly
$19-(|M|-1)\le18$ arrows. This contradicts
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-global-lower-nineteen}. The argument applies equally
to a source or a sink and uses no further bound on the corner order.
\end{proof}

The separate linear engine also uses the actual minimal-group
reduction at an extreme nongroup corner, exactly as stated in
Corollary~\ref{CP2:A-extreme-minimal-group-nineteen}. These two tests are
strict-arrow reductions to the completed eighteen-arrow exclusion.
The adjacent-edge, fixed-arrow, singleton-long, semisimple-pair, and
projective-singleton tests retain their original general statements.
Their hypotheses concern rational homology, module projectivity,
specified corner orders, or literal actions; none is extended by
replacing an eighteen-arrow hypothesis with a nineteen-arrow one.

The source-star engine uses only the general exact-orbit and relative
product tests already recorded for that engine. Its corner orders
remain at most seven. This observation is a scope justification for
the separately frozen producers and is not a claim that their new
finite enumerations have completed.
