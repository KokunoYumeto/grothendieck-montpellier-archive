\subsection{A finite actor-image cover for three-by-two sources}

\begin{lemma}[Proved: two- and three-valued balanced row functionals]
\label{CP2:A-three-by-two-balanced-actor-cover}
Let $M$ have a $3\times2$ rectangular-band minimal ideal and
$H_2(BM;\mathbb Q)\ne0$.  Its faithful actor image is, up to
relabeling the minimal rows and columns, a submonoid of the
following $24$-element transformation-pair monoid, or the
eight-element simultaneous-reflection extension of the pure
$3\times2$ band monoid.

For the first alternative, put $t=(0,0,1)$ on the three rows.
The $24$ pairs are the transformations
$\sigma:\{0,1,2\}\to\{0,1,2\}$ and
$\tau:\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}$ satisfying
\[
 t_{\sigma(i)}-t_{\sigma(0)}
     =\bigl(\tau(1)-\tau(0)\bigr)t_i
       \qquad(0\leq i\leq2).                                  \tag{1}
\]
Their multiplication is
\[
       (\sigma,\tau)(\sigma',\tau')
          =(\sigma\circ\sigma',\tau'\circ\tau).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Every image contains the six pairs with both transformations
constant and the identity pair.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $V$ be the two-dimensional row-augmentation module and
$W$ the one-dimensional column-augmentation module.
The natural formula
\[
             H_2(BM;\mathbb Q)
                    \cong W\otimes_{\mathbb QM}V
\]
gives a nonzero balanced linear functional $\lambda$ on $V$.
Write $\sigma_m,\tau_m$ for the two actors of $m$.
The action on $W$ is the scalar
$\chi(m)=\tau_m(1)-\tau_m(0)\in\{0,1,-1\}$, so
\[
       \lambda(mv)=\chi(m)\lambda(v).
\]
Choose row values $t_0=0$,
$t_i=\lambda(e_{i0}-e_{00})$.  They are not all equal, and
\[
 t_{\sigma_m(i)}-t_{\sigma_m(0)}=\chi(m)t_i.                    \tag{3}
\]
Equivalently every row actor descends to an affine self-map
of the finite set of row values, with slope $\chi(m)$.

If there are two distinct values, one occurs twice.
Relabel the rows and apply an affine change of the values
to make them $(0,0,1)$.  Equation (3) is then exactly (1).
For $\tau$ the identity, $\sigma$ preserves each of the
two value classes, giving four possibilities.
For $\tau$ the transposition, $\sigma$ exchanges the
value classes: both rows in the double class map to the
singleton and its one row maps to either member of the
double class.  This gives two possibilities.
For either constant $\tau$, the row actor has image
inside one value class.  There are $2^3+1=9$ such maps.
The total is $4+2+2\cdot9=24$.
Equation (3), or direct affine composition, proves closure
under (2).

If all three row values are distinct, a slope-one map
of this finite subset of $\mathbb Q$ is its identity,
and a slope-zero map is constant.  A slope-minus-one map,
if present, is the unique reflection exchanging the
extreme values and fixing the middle value.
Because the values distinguish the rows, the corresponding
row transformations are respectively the identity,
a constant, and that reflection.  The column
transformations have the same slopes.  Thus the image
contains only the six band pairs, the identity, and
possibly the simultaneous row reflection and column
transposition.  The seven-element image already appears
in the first alternative; adjoining the reflection gives
exactly the stated eight-element monoid.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Proved: the complete faithful cover through order eleven]
\label{CP2:A-three-by-two-faithful-hulls-through11}
Up to ordinary monoid isomorphism, there are respectively
\[
                  1,\quad5,\quad16,\quad29,\quad50
\]
faithful actor-image monoids of orders $7,8,9,10,11$
having minimal band $3\times2$ and nonzero rational
second homology.  The resulting $101$ tables cover
the faithful images of every such source monoid of order
at most eleven.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The base consisting of the six constant pairs and the
identity has seven elements.  There are seventeen
remaining actors in the first monoid of the lemma.
An image of order at most eleven therefore uses at most
four of those seventeen actors.  Enumerating all
\[
       \sum_{j=0}^{4}\binom{17}{j}
             =1+17+136+680+2380=3214
\]
subsets and checking closure under every product is
complete.  The numbers of closed subsets of the five
sizes are $1,11,47,105,165$.  Add the single
three-valued reflection image from the other alternative.

Canonicalization uses all twelve simultaneous relabelings
from $\mathfrak S_3\times\mathfrak S_2$, and compares the
entire set of actor pairs.  This is exactly ordinary
monoid isomorphism for faithful images.  Indeed every
isomorphism preserves the characteristic minimal band
and hence permutes its rows and columns.  Conjugating
those actors determines the image of every element,
because the actor representation is faithful.
Conversely every such conjugacy of complete actor sets
is a monoid isomorphism by formula (2).

The separately authored program
\path{monoid/code/enumerate_three_by_two_faithful_hulls11.py}
performs this literal enumeration.  It reconstructs the
full multiplication table of each canonical set, checks
all $10615$ product entries and $110117$ associativity
equations, and re-extracts the original row and column
actors from the resulting tables.  The balanced-functional
equations check that every image has positive rational
second homology.

As an exact control, all fifty-one canonical actor sets
through order ten agree with the faithful classes extracted
independently from the earlier complete positive source
catalogs.  Those counts are $1,5,16,29$.
The fresh order-eleven computation gives fifty further
classes.  Full actor sets, tables, source-catalog
identifications and counts are recorded in
\path{monoid/certificates/three_by_two_faithful_hulls11_scope.json}.
This is a census of faithful actor images; it does not
enumerate the original order-eleven monoids.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: the finite square question sufficient for this family]
\label{CP2:A-three-by-two-eleven-finite-square-cover}
To exclude the minimal-$3\times2$ source family in the
nineteen-arrow budget $(11,3,5)$, it is sufficient to
prove that each collage with source one of the $101$
faithful hulls above, opposite corner $R_3$, and at most
five states has zero degree-two square over $\mathbb F_2$
whenever its first Betti number is zero and its second
Betti number is one.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The actual faithful actor quotient is bijective on the
minimal band.  Apply
Theorem~\ref{CP2:A-band-quotient-integral-low-degree}.
The induced state set has at most five elements, and
the target collage has the same first and second
integral homology as a hypothetical projective-plane
source.  Its mod-two first and second Betti numbers
therefore satisfy the stated conditions.  Every
mod-two degree-two class of the source is pulled back
from the target, so the claimed square obstruction
would exclude the source.
\end{proof}

The sufficient finite square question in this corollary
is not decided by the actor-image enumeration.
In particular no restriction on the target third or
fourth homology is part of that question.  A positive
target third Betti number, or an actual proper retract
known only not to be a projective plane, supplies no
exclusion for this comparison.
