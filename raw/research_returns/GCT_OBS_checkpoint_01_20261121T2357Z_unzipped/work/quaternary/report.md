## Running the checkers

Use Python 3.8 or later. The code requires only the standard library. The files `check_border18.py` and `check_endpoint.py` use the bundled modular-elimination helper in `check_projection.py`; they do not import any original-run program. Run from this directory:

```sh
python scripts/check_projection.py 11 12 13 14 15 16 17 --workers 6 --output results/rebuilt_projection.json
python scripts/check_border18.py --workers 6 --output results/rebuilt_border18.json
python scripts/check_ordinary.py --output results/rebuilt_ordinary.json
python scripts/check_endpoint.py --insertions 4 --output results/rebuilt_endpoint.json
```

The projection computation took approximately 0.9,1.9,3.8,7.7,17.5,41.9,103.2 seconds for sizes 11 through 17 in this environment. The dense ternary certificate took 47.2 seconds. Timings are descriptive and machine dependent. Workers may be reduced to one without changing arithmetic.

The elimination routine packs field elements into independent integer lanes. Its correctness is proved in QC-01: a reciprocal approximation to division underestimates the quotient by at most one, one correction suffices, and lanes are wide enough to prevent carries/borrows. It is ordinary exact Gaussian elimination, not a probabilistic rank test. The scalar 8×8 eliminator used for the ordinary cores is separate.

## Mathematical consequences actually established

QC-01 gives c_m=binomial(m+3,3) at m=11 through 16 and c_17=1124. QC-02 proves the bordered induction and recomputes its dense m=17 antecedent, yielding c_18=1262. QC-03 proves the uniform antecedent by the finite core and boundary arguments, hence c_m=4m²−2m+2 for all m≥17. The source's separate exceptional-character proof is not needed: the preserved rank-84 boundary block plus eight-column ordinary blocks outside it already gives the full base rank 8m−4. This makes the dependency chain shorter and easier to audit.

QC-04 proves the degree-m Tor corollary using the full-variable linear-syzygy theorem of Efremenko–Landsberg–Schenck–Weyman. It does not assert full Tor independence, vanishing in all degrees, or uniform positive-characteristic validity.

For (17,23), the padded four-variable image therefore has affine dimension 1127, against determinant dimension 1060. For (8,9), the inherited c8=165 gives 168 against164. These dimension results do not identify a degree, a partition, an equation, or a complete isotypic rank comparison.

## Targeted direction audit

The qualitative arguments in CUE_GCT G-17/G-18 were read: the enhanced cone extracts a single highest-weight ray using the Cartan flag algebra; a common stable padded Fermat point gives the asserted quotient dimensions; a generic padding line removes the finite projective symmetry, so the common invariant degree semigroup has gcd1. These support the qualitative eventual sign in each strict direction. The source's enormous effective generator and degree bounds were not independently verified here and are not used.

In particular, for n>sqrt(2)m, eventual determinant dominance along a fixed strict four-row direction is not a proof that every finite degree has no gap. Item6's finite early degrees remain a separate question. R-22 and R-28 through R-33 were not used in the present dimension proof; no blanket verification is recorded for them.
