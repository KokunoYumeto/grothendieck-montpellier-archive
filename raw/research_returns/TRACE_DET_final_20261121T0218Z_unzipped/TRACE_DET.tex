\begin{abstract}
The determinant and positivity identifications must be separated. Cote 1 proves an abstract Hilbert-space trace theorem; it does not construct an arithmetic operator for $\zeta$. We give concrete kernels for the normal, zero-based operator $B$ of the SA23 intake and check $\det(1-wB)=\Xi(\sqrt w)/\Xi(0)$ and $B\ge0\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{RH}$. The stipulated Alp\"oge theorem therefore makes this designated operator positive. The actual finite source determinants have a different sign mechanism: their nonsingular Gram forms have balanced inertia. The rank-zero signed scalar is not a positive Hilbert kernel or positive-type group coefficient. Nevertheless it has a positive determinant-line interpretation; an explicit Berezin transform and the actual normalized period maps give positive frame operators. More strongly, actual source Bessel convolutions supply infinitely many orthogonal rank-one projections on one fixed Hilbert space. Their summable weighted sums have explicit kernels, exact trace-norm tails and ordinary Fredholm determinants. Choosing the reciprocal-square zero weights realizes $B\oplus0$ in that model without RH. This closes the common-space construction. The scalar prime-sum endpoint also determines the weights and integer multiplicities through an explicit cosine kernel; its positivity is RH-equivalent. The historical finite determinant correspondence and direct sign transfer remain unestablished. For the identified scalar endpoint we derive exact ordinary Fredholm-resolvent and two-indicator Weil-form formulas, including gamma and pole corrections and a complete global one-sided Landau implication.

Conditional only on Masullo's full spectral-jet description, we construct a trace-class Green transform on the actual even zeta quotient whose ordinary determinant is $\Xi(\sqrt w)/\Xi(0)$. This construction uses neither RH nor the draft's nuclearity theorem; the resulting operator is not boundedly similar to a selfadjoint operator. The character-sector extension separates functional-equation partners, removes central-zero blocks explicitly, and proves the same obstruction to selfadjoint similarity in the remaining quotient. Masullo's Theorem 10.3.2.1 separately asserts trace-class test operators, while the arithmetic formula is Theorem 12.5.1.1. The latter's test class excludes every nonzero convolution square. We also prove the boundary multiplicity correction forced by its weighted norm. The toy bound holds over finite fields for $k\ge2$ and needs gcd factors over composite cyclic rings; its Fourier/moment identification is false. The six RH programmes are classified through their exact criteria, with a positive paired trace formula for Li coefficients. For finite-field curves, the correct analogue is purity of Frobenius, not positivity of Frobenius. The archive repairs are proved and separated from unverified manuscript attribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{The main question: which determinant, and which positivity?}
\subsection{B-01: the actual operator inherited from SA23}
\begin{proposition}
Choose one representative $\gamma_j$ of each pair $\{\gamma,-\gamma\}$ of zeros of $\Xi$, retaining its multiplicity. On
\[
 H=\ell^2(J,\text{counting measure}),\qquad
 B e_j=\gamma_j^{-2}e_j,
\]
the operator $B$ is normal and trace class, with kernel
\[
 K_B(j,k)=\delta_{jk}\gamma_j^{-2}.
\]
Unconditionally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bdet}
 \det_H(1-wB)=G(w).
\end{equation}
A unitarily equivalent operator on $L^2(0,1)$ has the continuous kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinekernel}
 K_B(x,y)=2\sum_{j\ge1}\gamma_j^{-2}\sin(\pi jx)\sin(\pi jy).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The order-one and symmetry statements for $\Xi$, and $\Xi(0)\ne0$, are classical \cite[Theorem 2.12 and (2.12.4), pp.~29--30]{titch}. The zero count implies $\sum_j|\gamma_j|^{-2}<\infty$: in the dyadic annulus $2^m\le|\gamma|<2^{m+1}$ the sum is $O(m2^{-m})$. Thus the singular values of the diagonal $B$ are summable. Since
$M_G(R)=M_\Xi(\sqrt R)/|\Xi(0)|$, $G$ has order $1/2$. Hadamard factorization \cite[Theorem 3.16, p.~92]{mcmullen} at order less than one gives
\[
 G(w)=\prod_j(1-w/\gamma_j^2),
\]
with constant one from $G(0)=1$ and no nonconstant exponential factor. This is exactly the diagonal Fredholm determinant. This special change of variable is also explicitly discussed after Theorem 2.12 in \cite[p.~30]{titch}; it is the construction used in SA23 \S4.1(2).

The functions $\sqrt2\sin(\pi jx)$ form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(0,1)$. The summands in \eqref{eq:sinekernel} have modulus at most $2|\gamma_j|^{-2}$, so the series converges uniformly and absolutely on $[0,1]^2$. Termwise integration on finite linear combinations of basis functions gives the diagonal action. Continuity and density extend it to the asserted integral operator.
\end{proof}
The zero set is an input to both kernels. This is a concrete spectral realization, not a kernel extracted from the primes or from an algebro-geometric correspondence. The supplied cote 1 sheets contain neither $\Xi$ nor this $B$. Their contribution is the abstract trace/determinant mechanism, especially scan 79, (18)--(23), scan 80, and the genus-zero lemma on scans 111--112 \cite{archive}. Calling it ``Grothendieck's operator for $\Xi$'' would misattribute the intake's application to the archive itself.

\subsection{B-01 and DET-04: positivity of this realization, not every realization}
\begin{proposition}
For the designated diagonal $B$, $B\ge0$ if and only if RH. Under the stipulated Alp\"oge theorem, $B$ is positive and
\[
 \Tr B^n=\sum_j\gamma_j^{-2n}>0\quad(n\ge1).
\]
For any $T\in\Sone$ with $\det(1-wT)=G(w)$, the same power traces hold. Such a $T$ need not be positive, even under RH.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A diagonal operator is positive exactly when its entries are nonnegative real. Here each entry is nonzero, and $\gamma^{-2}>0$ is equivalent to $\gamma$ being nonzero real. This is precisely RH under $\rho=1/2+i\gamma$. It gives the displayed positive traces. For arbitrary $T$ the Fredholm zero theorem gives the same nonzero eigenvalues with multiplicities; the Hilbert trace theorem applied to $T^n$ gives the trace equality. For exact published zero-theorem locators see \cite[ch.~II, \S4, Theorems 2 and 4, pp.~354 and 359]{groth56}; the trace-class Hilbert conclusion is the cote 1 result \cite{archive}.

To refute operator positivity, write $b_j=\gamma_j^{-2}>0$ under RH and replace the first two diagonal entries by
\[
 T_0=\begin{pmatrix}b_1&1\\0&b_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=T_0\oplus\diag(b_3,b_4,\ldots).
\]
This is a rank-one perturbation of $B$, hence trace class. Its determinant is still $\prod_j(1-wb_j)$, and the diagonal of $T_0^n$ is $(b_1^n,b_2^n)$, so every power trace agrees. Yet $T\ne T^*$, and therefore $T\not\ge0$. No simplicity of zeros is needed.
\end{proof}
The spectrum of the compact $B$ includes $0$. Its quadratic form is strictly positive on nonzero vectors under RH, but it has no positive uniform lower bound. Cote 1's inequality compares $u$ with $|u|=\sqrt{u^*u}$; it does not infer $u\ge0$ from its determinant. In geometric language, a characteristic polynomial supplies spectral data; a positive Hermitian realization or a polarization is additional structure.

\subsection{B-02: why the unsquared variable is impossible in trace class}
\begin{proposition}
There are no $T\in\Sone$, $c\ne0$, and $a\in\C$ such that
$\Xi(t)=c\det(1+(t-a)T)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The nonzero eigenvalues of a trace-class operator are absolutely summable. The Fredholm zero theorem would give
$\sum_{\Xi(\gamma)=0}|\gamma-a|^{-1}<\infty$.
But $|\Im\gamma|<1/2$, and the Riemann--von Mangoldt count above implies divergence of this sum for every fixed $a$. For example, a dyadic height annulus contains a number of zeros of order $2^m m$, and each shifted reciprocal modulus there is bounded below by a positive multiple of $2^{-m}$ for large $m$. The annulus contributions therefore do not even tend to zero. This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}
This rechecks SA23 \S4.1(1); it excludes this ordinary Fredholm determinant, not regularized determinants or an unbounded spectral generator.

\subsection{DET-01--03: the toy does not supply the missing operator}
\paragraph{DET-02 (open paths are matrix coefficients).}
Let $q$ be a prime power, $\psi$ a nontrivial additive character of $\F_q$, $h\in\F_q^\times$ and $t:\F_q\to[0,\infty)$. Put $K_h(x,y)=\psi(hxy)$, $D=\diag(t)$, $a_x=\sqrt{t(x)}$ and $A=D^{1/2}K_hD^{1/2}$. For $k\ge1$, define the open-path sum
$S_k(h)=\sum_{x_1,\ldots,x_k}\prod_i t(x_i)\psi(h\sum_{i<k}x_ix_{i+1})$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathcycle}
 S_k(h)=a^*A^{k-1}a,
 \qquad
 \Tr A^k=\sum_{x_1,\ldots,x_k}\prod_i t(x_i)
 \psi\!\left(h\left(\sum_{i<k}x_ix_{i+1}+x_kx_1\right)\right).
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Each edge of $A$ supplies a square-root weight at either endpoint. The endpoint factors in $a^*A^{k-1}a$ complete exactly one weight at each vertex of an open path. Taking a diagonal entry of $A^k$ instead identifies the last vertex with the first and adds the closing edge. Thus the two sums are different. Their generating functions are respectively
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}S_k(h)z^{k-1}=a^*(1-zA)^{-1}a,
 \qquad
 -\frac{d}{dz}\log\det(1-zA)=\sum_{n\ge1}\Tr(A^n)z^{n-1},
\]
by the Neumann series and finite determinant identity near zero.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{DET-01 (the Fourier operator is not positive).}
The literal positivity proposal is refuted already by $q=3$, $h=1$, $t\equiv1$. With $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$,
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&\omega&\omega^2\\1&\omega^2&\omega\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K(0,1,-1)^T=i\sqrt3(0,1,-1)^T.
\]
Thus $K$ and $K/\sqrt3$ are not selfadjoint. Moreover $K^2=3J$, where $Jf(x)=f(-x)$, gives $S_3=9$ but $\Tr K^2=3$ and $\Tr K^3=-3i\sqrt3$. The Fourier matrix also has eigenvalues $\sqrt3,-\sqrt3$, so a nonzero scalar multiple cannot make all eigenvalues positive. These exact calculations disprove the two literal identifications in Fable suggestion 3, not the existence of some different future operator.

\paragraph{DET-03 (a fixed finite determinant is a polynomial).}
Any fixed $r\times r$ characteristic determinant $\det(1-wA)$ is a polynomial of degree at most $r$, whereas $G$ has infinitely many zeros. They therefore cannot be identical. This obstruction also covers fixed matrices with polynomial entries. It does not cover a convergent sequence of determinants or a matrix into whose entries $G$ has already been inserted.

\subsection{DET-05: a precise bridge that would suffice}
A finite determinant is a Fredholm determinant after extending its finite matrix by zero. That formal observation does not make it a compression of \fid{B-01}. The finite analogue with actual spectral meaning is
\[
 \det(1-wA)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-w)^j\Tr(\Lambda^jA).
\]
Triangularization proves this identity by elementary symmetric functions. Its geometric formalism is the exterior-power $\lambda$-structure: SGA 6, V, Definition 2.1, Example 2.2(b), Lemma 2.2.1, and the Adams logarithmic identity (7.1.1) \cite{sga6}. The analytic version on trace ideals is cote 1, scans 78--79.

\begin{proposition}
If positive finite-rank operators $A_N$ on a common Hilbert space converge in trace norm to $T$, then $T\ge0$, and $\det(1-wA_N)\to\det(1-wT)$ locally uniformly. A separately proved identity of the limit with $G$ would give a direct positive realization.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Trace-norm convergence implies operator-norm convergence, so $T=T^*$ and $\langle v,Tv\rangle=\lim_N\langle v,A_Nv\rangle\ge0$. For determinant convergence one can use the mixed-coefficient inequality of cote 1, scan 78, (15), with nuclear norms. Writing $\alpha_n$ for the symmetric polarized Fredholm coefficient, telescope its $n$ arguments. If $M=\max(\|A\|_1,\|T\|_1)$, this gives
\[
 |\alpha_n(A)-\alpha_n(T)|\le
 \frac{\|A-T\|_1M^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Summing the determinant series on $|w|\le R$ bounds the difference by
$R\|A-T\|_1e^{RM}$, which tends to zero for $A=A_N$.
\end{proof}
This is a sufficient bridge, not a necessary form of every possible proof. A unitary intertwiner or an arithmetic positive kernel with independently proved determinant would be alternative bridges. Merely knowing scalar signs does not provide them.

\paragraph{DET-06 (the actual signed Gram sign is not matrix positivity).}
In the source's linear-first convention, the definitions use an indefinite Hermitian form $h_H(v,w)=w^*Hv$, with $H=H_{k,r}$ of size $2r+k+1$, a negative-vector quantity $T(z)=-\Re h_H(z,z)$, and
\[
 G_f=|D_{r+1}|^2-T(z)T(z')|D_r|^2.
\]
The proposition \texttt{GGram} identifies this with $(-1)^{r+1}\det(\mathrm{gramM})$, while \texttt{GNonneg} asserts its nonnegativity under its negative-vector hypotheses. Those hypotheses do not assert that the Gram matrix is positive.
\begin{proof}[Complete counterexample in the stated domains]
Take $k=2,r=0$, $H=\diag(1,1,-1)$, $g=I_3$, $z=(0,0,1)^T$ and $z'=(\sqrt3,0,2)^T$. The type has three coordinates. The source's middle-block condition \texttt{inWb} is vacuous at $r=0$, and $g^*Hg=H$, so $g$ belongs to \texttt{UH}. Direct calculation gives $T(z)=T(z')=1>0$. These are all the hypotheses of the two named propositions. The single-entry pairing matrix is $\mathrm{Zhat}=[-2]$, hence $D_0=1$, $D_1=-2$, and $G_f=4-1=3>0$. But
\[
 \mathrm{gramM}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]
has eigenvalues $1$ and $-3$; on $(1,1)^T$ its quadratic form is $-6$. Its determinant is $-3$, exactly consistent with the asserted signed determinant identity. Thus the positive sign is compatible with an indefinite matrix.
\end{proof}
This does not refute the source's scalar sign. It refutes reading that particular sign as positivity of its Gram matrix, much less as positivity of \fid{B-01}.

\paragraph{DET-10 (the actual sign is a balanced-inertia statement).}
Under the exact \texttt{GNonneg} hypotheses, if $M=\mathrm{gramM}$ is nonsingular, it has exactly $r+1$ positive and $r+1$ negative eigenvalues. Using the cited \texttt{GGram} identity,
\[
 G_f=(-1)^{r+1}\det M=\prod_j|\lambda_j(M)|>0
\]
in this case, and $G_f=0$ in the singular case. Thus this actual sign does not merely tolerate an occasional negative direction: the nonsingular Gram form has balanced inertia.
\begin{proof}
Put $n=2r+k+1$. Reordering the standard basis pairs coordinate $a$ with $2r+k-a$ for $0\le a<r$; the remaining coordinates are $r,\ldots,r+k$. The explicit $H_{k,r}$ is the orthogonal sum of $r$ matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\diag(1^k,-1)$. Each paired block has eigenvalues $1,-1$. Hence $H_{k,r}$ has positive and negative indices $r+k,r+1$.

Let $X$ have the $2r+2$ source Gram vectors as columns:
\[e_0,\ldots,e_{r-1},z,ge_0,\ldots,ge_{r-1},gz'.\]
Then $M=X^*H_{k,r}X$. If $M$ is singular, the cited identity gives $G_f=0$. Otherwise $X$ is injective, since $Xc=0$ implies $Mc=0$. The image $V$ has dimension $2r+2$, and the restricted Hermitian form on $V$ is nondegenerate with matrix $M$ in these columns.

The first $r+1$ columns span a subspace $L\subset V$ of dimension $r+1$. The isotropic coordinate vectors pair to zero with one another. The support condition on $z$ makes their pairings with $z$ zero, and $h_H(z,z)=-T(z)<0$. Thus the restricted form on $L$ is $\diag(0^r,-T(z))$, so it is nonpositive. Every negative definite subspace of $V$ has dimension at most the ambient negative index $r+1$. Every positive definite subspace of $V$ intersects $L$ trivially, so its dimension is at most $\dim V-\dim L=r+1$. Since $M$ is nondegenerate, its two indices sum to $2r+2$ and therefore both equal $r+1$. The product of its real eigenvalues now has sign $(-1)^{r+1}$, giving the asserted absolute-value product and strict positivity.

When $k=0$, the $2r+2$ columns lie in dimension $2r+1$, so the singular case necessarily applies. When $r=0$, $L$ is the negative line through $z$. No minor was divided by. The source additionally assumes $T(z')>0$ and $g^*Hg=H$; the latter and both support conditions enter the cited \texttt{GGram} identity, while the inertia step only needs $T(z)>0$. This agrees with the unused \texttt{\_hT'} argument in the displayed source proof.
\end{proof}
The negative index in this finite Hermitian argument is not a statement that $B$ has negative eigenvalues. It is a proof about a different, explicitly identified matrix; the extra operator identification still has to be supplied.

\subsection{STEP7-01--02: interlacing selectors and genuine period positivity}
The period neighborhood also contains more positive structure than the indefinite Gram sign alone. The following distinctions retain the exact source hypotheses.

\paragraph{STEP7-01 (a determinant encoding the actual interlacing predicate).}
\path{Formalism/SharpRestriction/CuspSpanNormBounds/RelevantZeroGramTerms.lean}, lines 256--259, defines \texttt{InterlaceS} by
\[
 \lambda_i<u_i\quad(0\le i<k),\qquad
 u_{i+1}<\lambda_i\quad(0\le i<k-1).
\]
For strictly decreasing real lists $u_0>\cdots>u_{k-1}$ and $\lambda_0>\cdots>\lambda_{n-1}$, $n\ge k$, with no cross equality among the first $k$ entries, define the matrix
\[
 C(u,\lambda)_{ij}=\mathbf1_{\{u_i>\lambda_j\}},\qquad 0\le i,j<k.
\]
This matrix is constructed here from the inspected predicate; it has not been located as the historical source determinant. Its determinant belongs to $\{0,1\}$ and is one exactly when \texttt{InterlaceS} holds.
\begin{proof}
Let $t_i$ count the $j<k$ for which $\lambda_j\ge u_i$. Row $i$ consists of $t_i$ zeros followed by $k-t_i$ ones, and $0\le t_0\le\cdots\le t_{k-1}\le k$. If some $t_i=k$, a row is zero; if two thresholds agree, two rows agree. Either case gives determinant zero. Otherwise the $k$ thresholds are distinct elements of $\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$ in increasing order, so $t_i=i$. The matrix is upper triangular with all diagonal entries one, hence has determinant one. The equalities $t_i=i$ are equivalent, using the excluded cross equalities, to $\lambda_i<u_i$ and $u_i<\lambda_{i-1}$ for $i>0$. These are exactly the displayed predicate. For $k=0$ the empty determinant equals one and the predicate is vacuous. For $k\ge2$, the nonzero-selector matrix has entry $(0,1)$ equal to one and entry $(1,0)$ equal to zero, so it is not selfadjoint despite having every eigenvalue equal to one.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{DET-08 (the strongest direct determinant link for the toy).}
There \emph{is} a precise finite determinant relation for the path sums. With $P=aa^*$,
\[
 S_k(h)=\Tr(A^{k-1}P),\qquad
 \sum_{k\ge1}S_k(h)z^{k-1}
 =-\left.\partial_u\log\det(I-zA-uP)\right|_{u=0}.
\]
\begin{proof}
Expanding the finite trace gives $\Tr(A^{k-1}aa^*)=a^*A^{k-1}a$. The Neumann series sums it to $a^*(I-zA)^{-1}a$. For invertible $M$, multilinearity of columns gives

\end{proof}
This constructive bridge uses an insertion $P$ and a second parameter. It neither replaces path sums by $\Tr A^k$ nor makes $A$ positive, and its finite-dimensional generating function is rational.

\paragraph{DET-12(a): no direct positive Gram kernel.}
Set \(r=0\), \(k=2\), and \(H=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)\).
On the negative unit vectors define
\[
 K(z,z')=|h(z,z')|^2-1=G_f(2,0,z,z',I).
\]
This is not a positive semidefinite kernel, hence is not a Hilbert Gram
kernel or a pointwise limit of positive semidefinite kernels.
The same conclusion holds for the direct normalized scalar
\((|h(z,z')|^2-T(z)T(z'))/(T(z)T(z'))\).
\begin{proof}
For \(t\in\mathbb R\), put \(z(t)=(\sinh t,0,\cosh t)^T\).
The support condition is vacuous for \(r=0\),
\(h(z(t),z(t))=-1\), and \(I^*HI=H\). Thus these are actual
source-admissible data. Direct calculation gives
\[
 h(z(s),z(t))=-\cosh(t-s),\qquad
 K(z(s),z(t))=\sinh^2(t-s).
\]
For \(t\ne0\), the two-point matrix at \(z(0),z(t)\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&c\\c&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad c=\sinh^2t>0.
\]
Its quadratic form at \((1,-1)\) is \(-2c<0\).
A Hilbert Gram kernel has nonnegative quadratic forms, so no such
realization exists. Equivalently, the zero diagonal would force every
feature vector to be zero while the off-diagonal is nonzero.
For a pointwise limit of positive kernels, each fixed finite quadratic
form is the limit of nonnegative numbers and is nonnegative.
The displayed matrix rules out that possibility, for sequences or nets.
The normalized scalar restricts to this same kernel on the negative
unit vectors, proving its assertion as well.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The group and positive trace-coefficient versions.}
Let \(z_0=(0,0,1)^T\) and
\[
 F(g)=G_f(2,0,z_0,z_0,g)=|h(z_0,gz_0)|^2-1,\qquad g^*Hg=H.
\]
Then \(F\) is not of positive type, is not a pointwise limit of
positive-type functions, and cannot be \(\Tr(A\pi(g))\) for a unitary
representation \(\pi\) and positive trace-class operator \(A\).
\begin{proof}
The matrices
\[
 g(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
 \cosh t&0&\sinh t\\0&1&0\\\sinh t&0&\cosh t
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfy \(g(t)^*Hg(t)=H\), \(g(t)z_0=z(t)\), and
\(g(s)g(t)=g(s+t)\), by matrix multiplication and the hyperbolic
addition identities. Hence \(F(e)=0\) and \(F(g(t))=\sinh^2t\).
The positive-type matrix \(F(g_i^{-1}g_j)\) for \(g_1=e,g_2=g(t)\)
is the indefinite matrix above, since \(g(t)^{-1}=g(-t)\).
The pointwise-limit argument is unchanged.

Every proposed positive trace coefficient would be of positive type:
for \(X=\sum_j c_j\pi(g_j)\),
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_j\Tr(A\pi(g_i^{-1}g_j))
 =\Tr(AX^*X)=\|XA^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2\ge0.
\]
Here \(A^{1/2}\) is Hilbert--Schmidt and the cyclic trace identity
applies to the bounded and Hilbert--Schmidt factors.
Alternatively, \(F(e)=\Tr A=0\) gives
\(\|A^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}=0\), hence \(A=0\), contradicting
\(F(g(t))>0\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The direct scalar is not a squared Hilbert distance.}
For \(a>0\), take \(t=(-a,0,a)\) and real coefficients \(c=(1,-2,1)\).
Their sum is zero, but
\[
 \sum_{i,j}c_ic_jK(z(t_i),z(t_j))
 =2\sinh^2(2a)-8\sinh^2a=8\sinh^4a>0.
\]
The last equality follows from \(\sinh(2a)=2\sinh a\cosh a\) and
\(\cosh^2a-1=\sinh^2a\). Thus conditional negative semidefiniteness
fails. Indeed, a squared Hilbert distance would satisfy, by expansion
and \(\sum_i c_i=0\),
\[
 \sum_{i,j}c_ic_j\|\Phi_i-\Phi_j\|^2
 =-2\left\|\sum_i c_i\Phi_i\right\|^2\le0.
\]
This proves the stated distance obstruction completely. These
refutations concern the direct scalar and the specified normalization,
not an inverse, exponential, integral transform, added or subtracted
term, or another modified family.

\paragraph{DET-12(b): positive determinant lines, indefinite ambient exterior form.}
Write \(q=r+1\), and let \(X\) contain the \(2q\) source Gram vectors.
The induced exterior Hermitian form is specified by
\[
 h^{[2q]}(x_1\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{2q},
          y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{2q})
 =\det[h(x_i,y_j)]_{i,j}.
\]
For the column wedge \(\omega\) of \(X\), the source Gram identity gives
\[
 G_f=(-1)^q h^{[2q]}(\omega,\omega).
\]
If \(X^*HX\) is nonsingular, the signed form on the determinant line
\(\Lambda^{2q}(\operatorname{im}X)\) is positive definite. For \(k\ge2\),
however, that signed form is indefinite on the full ambient exterior
power.
\begin{proof}
By \fid{DET-10}, \(V=\operatorname{im}X\) has signature \((q,q)\).
In an \(h\)-orthonormal basis of \(V\), the exterior norm of its top
wedge has sign \((-1)^q\); multiplication by the same sign gives
value \(1\). Every other nonzero element of this one-dimensional line
is a nonzero scalar multiple, so the signed form is positive definite.

For the ambient signature \((p,q)=(r+k,r+1)\), a basis wedge
containing \(q\) positive and \(q\) negative directions has signed
value \(+1\). When \(k\ge2\), there are also \(q+1\) positive directions,
so a wedge containing \(q+1\) positive and \(q-1\) negative directions
has signed value \(-1\). This proves ambient indefiniteness.
When \(k=1\), the ambient dimension is \(2q\), so its top exterior
space is the positive line just described. When \(k=0\), the exterior
degree exceeds the ambient dimension, and that exterior space is zero.
For a degenerate restricted form, the displayed wedge has value zero,
so it does not define a positive definite metric on that line.
\end{proof}
The source compound matrices and wedge coordinates are
\path{Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Jets/SumIoPrAlt.lean},
lines 104--110. Its \texttt{TensH}, lines 88--94, still carries

\paragraph{DET-12(c): explicit Lorentz-projection factorization.}
Let \(E=[e_0,\ldots,e_{r-1}]\), \(F=gE\), \(y=gz'\), and
\[
 C=E^*HF,\quad b=E^*Hy,\quad c=z^*HF,\quad d=z^*Hy.
\]
Then \(\widehat Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}C&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
and \(D_r=\det C\). On the chart \(\det C\ne0\), define
\[
 U=\operatorname{im}E+\operatorname{im}F,\quad
 x=z-E(C^*)^{-1}c^*,\quad y_0=y-FC^{-1}b,\quad
 s=d-cC^{-1}b.
\]
With \(T=T(z)>0\), the space
\(V_+=U^\perp\cap x^\perp\) is positive definite of dimension \(k\),
the vector \(p=y_0+(s/T)x\) belongs to it, and
\[
 \boxed{\quad G_f=|\det C|^2T\,h(p,p)
 =\bigl\|\sqrt T\,(\det C)p\bigr\|_{V_+}^{\,2}.\quad}
\]
\begin{proof}
The source support, isotropy and unitary conditions give
\begin{align*}
 E^*HE&=F^*HF=0,\qquad E^*Hz=0,\qquad F^*Hy=0,\\
 z^*Hz&=-T,\qquad y^*Hy=-T',\qquad T'=T(z')>0.
\end{align*}
The Gram matrix of \([E,F]\) is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&C\\C^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
It is invertible, so these columns are independent.
Replacing \(F\) by \(FC^{-1}\) gives the form
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
of signature \((r,r)\). Thus \(U\) is nondegenerate and \(U^\perp\)
has signature \((k,1)\).

Substitution gives
\[
 E^*Hx=F^*Hx=E^*Hy_0=F^*Hy_0=0.
\]
For example,
\(F^*Hx=c^*-C^*(C^*)^{-1}c^*=0\) and
\(E^*Hy_0=b-CC^{-1}b=0\).
The other two equations use the displayed isotropy and support
relations. Hence \(x,y_0\in U^\perp\).
Each removed vector is isotropic and orthogonal to the original
vector, giving \(h(x,x)=-T\) and \(h(y_0,y_0)=-T'\).
Since \(((C^*)^{-1}c^*)^*=cC^{-1}\), expansion gives
\[
 h(x,y_0)=x^*Hy_0
 =d-cC^{-1}b-cC^{-1}b+cC^{-1}b=s.
\]
In the global conjugate-first convention,
\(h(x,p)=s+(s/T)(-T)=0\). Splitting off the negative line
\(\mathbb Cx\) from the space of signature \((k,1)\) proves that
\(V_+\) has signature \((k,0)\). Expanding the norm gives
\[
 h(p,p)=-T'+|s|^2/T.
\]
Subtracting \(cC^{-1}\) times the first \(r\) rows from the last
row of \(\widehat Z\) yields
\(D_{r+1}=(\det C)s\). Substitution in the actual definition of
\(G_f\) proves the boxed formula.
\end{proof}
For \(r=0\), the empty matrix is invertible with determinant \(1\),
\(U=0\), and this is the usual Lorentz projection.
For \(k=0\), \(V_+=0\) and the scalar vanishes on this chart;
globally the \(2r+2\) Gram vectors lie in dimension \(2r+1\), so
the source Gram identity gives \(G_f=0\) everywhere.
If \(D_r=0\), no inverse or limiting formula is used:
the definition gives \(G_f=|D_{r+1}|^2\ge0\) directly.
The assumption \(T>0\) is retained, as required by the source.

The determinant line and the positive space in this factorization
depend on the input configuration. Fixing the first vector at \(r=0\)
fixes a positive space in which the scalar is the norm of a projected
second vector; it does not make the two-variable scalar a Gram
inner product. Part (a) proves that such a reinterpretation of the
cross-values is impossible. Thus this calculation gives valid positive
geometry and a precise direct-kernel obstruction, without a historical
identification or a Xi approximation.

\subsection{DET-13: an explicit positive transform and its trace-class family}

The preceding obstruction is specific to the signed scalar itself.  There is
an affirmative transformed construction, using a known Berezin kernel.  The
new comparison here is its exact expression in the source's $r=0$ determinant
and the resulting ordinary Fredholm family; positivity of the Berezin kernel
is an existing theorem \cite[Theorem 3.8, \S4.1 (4.1), \S4.4 (4.7)--(4.9)]{neretin}.

\begin{proposition}[DET-13]
Let $h(z,z')=z^*\operatorname{diag}(I_k,-1)z'$, $k\ge1$, and
$T(z)=-h(z,z)>0$.  For the actual $r=0$, $g=1$ signed Gram expression put
\[
 S(z,z')=|h(z,z')|^2-T(z)T(z').
\]
For every $a>0$ the function on negative projective lines
\begin{equation}
 F_a([z],[z'])=
 \left(1+\frac{S(z,z')}{T(z)T(z')}\right)^{-a}
 =\left(\frac{T(z)T(z')}{|h(z,z')|^2}\right)^a
 \label{eq:positive-gram-transform}
\end{equation}
is a continuous positive-definite kernel with diagonal one.  It is invariant
under $U(k,1)$ and is the Gram kernel of an explicit continuous family of unit
vectors $v_x$ in a separable Hilbert space $\mathcal H_a$.

For every finite positive Radon measure $\nu$ on this ball, the operator
\begin{equation}
 T_{a,\nu}=\int v_x\langle v_x,\,\cdot\,\rangle\,d\nu(x)
 \quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad
 T_{a,\nu}\ge0,\qquad T_{a,\nu}\in S_1,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}T_{a,\nu}=\nu(\mathbb B^k).
 \label{eq:positive-frame}
\end{equation}
It is a trace-norm limit of positive finite-rank sums on this same Hilbert
space.  Its ordinary determinant is
\begin{equation}
 \det(1-wT_{a,\nu})=
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-w)^n}{n!}
 \int_{(\mathbb B^k)^n}
 \det\big[F_a(x_i,x_j)\big]_{i,j=1}^n\,d\nu^n,
 \label{eq:positive-frame-det}
\end{equation}
with absolute locally uniform convergence.  For a finite atomic measure
$\nu=\sum_{j=1}^N b_j\delta_{x_j}$ with $b_j\ge0$, its determinant is exactly
\[
 \det\left[\delta_{ij}-w\sqrt{b_i b_j}F_a(x_i,x_j)\right]_{i,j=1}^N.
\]
No RH or zero input is used in this construction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every negative line has a unique representative $(x,1)$ with
$x\in\mathbb B^k=\{x:\|x\|<1\}$.  Scaling either original vector cancels
from the quotient in \eqref{eq:positive-gram-transform}, and preservation of
$h$ proves its simultaneous $U(k,1)$ invariance.  In these coordinates,
\[
 F_a(x,y)=
 \frac{(1-\|x\|^2)^a(1-\|y\|^2)^a}
 {|1-\langle x,y\rangle|^{2a}}.
\]
The denominator is nonzero.  To specify the Hilbert realization, let
$E=\ell^2(\mathbb N^k)$, $c_\alpha=(a)_{|\alpha|}/\alpha!>0$, where
$(a)_n=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)$ and $(a)_0=1$, and set
\[
 u_x=(1-\|x\|^2)^{a/2}
       (\sqrt{c_\alpha}\,x^\alpha)_\alpha,
 \qquad v_x=u_x\otimes\overline{u_x}\in E\otimes\overline E.
\]
The binomial and multinomial expansions give
$\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\overline{x}^\alpha y^\alpha
=(1-\langle x,y\rangle)^{-a}$; they converge absolutely since
$\sum_j|x_jy_j|\le\|x\|\|y\|<1$.  Thus $\|u_x\|=\|v_x\|=1$ and
$\langle v_x,v_y\rangle=F_a(x,y)$.  This is the rank-one-ball version of
the cited Berezin construction, with the normalization needed here.
Take $\mathcal H_a$ to be the closed span of the $v_x$.  Continuity of the
kernel and its unit diagonal give norm continuity of $v_x$, since
$\|v_x-v_y\|^2=2-2F_a(x,y)$.  Kernel invariance defines a well-defined
unitary action $U_gv_x=v_{gx}$ on this span and then its completion: every
finite linear relation has the same squared norm after the assignment, and
$U_{g^{-1}}$ supplies the inverse.  In particular the transformed group
function is a genuine unitary matrix coefficient.

Write $P_x=v_x\langle v_x,\,\cdot\,\rangle$.  It is a positive rank-one
projection with trace norm one, and
$\|P_x-P_y\|_1\le2\|v_x-v_y\|$.  Hence its integral in $S_1$ exists for
finite $\nu$, is positive, and has trace $\int1\,d\nu$.  More concretely,
choose compact sets with remaining measure tending to zero.  On each compact
set, a finite Borel partition and representative points give sums
$\sum_j\nu(E_j)P_{x_j}$; their trace-norm error is bounded by the omitted
mass plus $\sum_j\nu(E_j)\sup_{x\in E_j}\|P_x-P_{x_j}\|_1$.  Uniform
continuity makes the latter tend to zero.  This both constructs the integral
and proves the asserted common-space approximation.  No norm compactness of
the whole ball is assumed.

For the determinant formula, first compress to the span of the first $m$
vectors of an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal H_a$.  The finite-dimensional
determinant expansion and the Leibniz formula under the integrals give its
$n$th exterior coefficient as
\[
 \frac1{n!}\int
 \|Q_mv_{x_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge Q_mv_{x_n}\|^2\,d\nu^n,
\]
where $Q_m$ is the orthogonal projection.  Indeed, for each coordinate minor,
expand both alternating determinants: Fubini factors the $n$ integrals, and
permuting the $n$ variables gives the factor $n!$.  Summing coordinate minors
is the finite Cauchy--Binet identity.  As $m\to\infty$, these integrands
converge to the full Gram determinant and are bounded by one, by the Gram
inequality.  The compressed operators converge in trace norm: this follows
from the same rank-one bound and dominated convergence.  Therefore DET-05
and dominated convergence yield \eqref{eq:positive-frame-det}.  The absolute
value of its $n$th term is at most
$|w|^n\nu(\mathbb B^k)^n/n!$, which justifies the entire-series limit.

For atomic $\nu$, define $V:\mathbb C^N\to\mathcal H_a$ by
$Ve_j=\sqrt{b_j}v_{x_j}$.  Then $T_{a,\nu}=VV^*$ and
$(V^*V)_{ij}=\sqrt{b_i b_j}F_a(x_i,x_j)$.  Restricting to their finite
ranges, or comparing their nonzero eigenvalues, proves the finite determinant
identity, including zero weights and repeated points.
\end{proof}

This gives an explicit family satisfying the positivity and approximation
parts of DET-05, after a nonlinear transformation of the actual signed
determinant.  It is not a claimed historical construction.  The choice of
finite measure and the Xi spectral identity remain substantive arithmetic
inputs.  In particular a putative equality with $G$ forces at least
$\nu(\mathbb B^k)=\operatorname{Tr}B$ and
$\iint F_a(x,y)^2\,d\nu(x)d\nu(y)=\operatorname{Tr}B^2$; all the higher
exterior coefficients must also agree.  These first two necessities follow
from the trace above and
$\operatorname{Tr}(P_xP_y)=|\langle v_x,v_y\rangle|^2$ by absolute Fubini.
Thus the bare signed kernel is excluded by DET-12, whereas transformed
positive families really exist.  The remaining Xi identification cannot be
replaced by either an impossibility assertion or positivity alone.

In Grothendieck's language this is the ordinary analytic trace/determinant
mechanism of cote~1/78--80 and its finite exterior-power approximants
(SGA~6, V, Definition~2.1, Example~2.2(b), Lemma~2.2.1).  The homogeneous
Hermitian domain and its unitary kernel are not, by this argument, an
arithmetic cohomology, an $\ell$-adic realization, or a crystal over
$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$.

\(v_\nu\in(R_\nu).\mathrm{sm}\cap(R_\nu).\mathrm{TK}\), and
real \(t_\nu\ge0\). Define
\[
 a_\nu=\sqrt{t_\nu d_\nu}\,l_\nu(v_\nu),\qquad
 A_I=\sum_{\nu\in I}t_\nu d_\nu
                \operatorname{be}_\nu(v_\nu,v_\nu).
\]
Then \(A_I\) is positive and finite rank on the common target \(W_p\),
with
\[
 A_I=\sum_{\nu\in I}a_\nu a_\nu^*,\qquad
 \Tr A_I=\sum_{\nu\in I}t_\nu d_\nu\|l_\nu(v_\nu)\|^2.
\]
For the positive Gram matrix
\(M_I=(\langle a_\nu,a_\mu\rangle)_{\nu,\mu\in I}\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \det_{W_p}(I-wA_I)=\det_{\mathbb C^I}(I-wM_I)
 =\sum_{J\subset I}(-w)^{|J|}
       \left\|\bigwedge_{\nu\in J}a_\nu\right\|^2.\quad}
\]
In particular, the alternating Taylor coefficients are nonnegative.
These are explicit frame determinants constructed from the source
maps; they have not thereby been identified with the historical
determinants of the Remark.
\begin{proof}
Both vector-domain requirements are imposed, so the source
factorization with \(x=v=v_\nu\) gives
\[
 t_\nu d_\nu\operatorname{be}_\nu(v_\nu,v_\nu)
 =a_\nu a_\nu^*,\qquad
 (aa^*)\eta=\langle a,\eta\rangle a.
\]
Each summand is bounded, selfadjoint and of rank at most one.
Moreover
\[
 \langle\eta,A_I\eta\rangle
 =\sum_{\nu\in I}|\langle a_\nu,\eta\rangle|^2\ge0.
\]
If \(a_\nu\ne0\), its rank-one summand has sole nonzero eigenvalue
\(\|a_\nu\|^2\); if \(a_\nu=0\), it contributes zero. Finite
additivity of the trace proves the stated trace identity.

Let \(S:\mathbb C^I\to W_p\) have columns \(a_\nu\).
Then \(A_I=SS^*\) and \(M_I=S^*S\).
For each nonzero eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(S^*S\), \(S\) maps its
eigenspace injectively onto the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(SS^*\),
with inverse \(\lambda^{-1}S^*\). The converse follows in the
same way. These are positive selfadjoint finite-rank operators,
so their nonzero eigenvalues agree with multiplicities and there
are no nontrivial Jordan blocks. Extra zero eigenvalues contribute
only factors \(1\), proving the first determinant equality.

In the column expansion of \(\det(I-wM_I)\), choose the
\(-wM_I\) column exactly at positions \(J\).
The identity columns outside \(J\) force the corresponding rows
in the permutation expansion; the result is
\((-w)^{|J|}\det((M_I)_J)\), with no extra sign.
This principal minor is the Gram determinant of the vectors
\(a_\nu\), \(\nu\in J\). If they are dependent, both the determinant
and their wedge vanish. Otherwise Gram--Schmidt gives their
Gram determinant as the product of the squared lengths of successive
orthogonal residuals, which is precisely the squared Hilbert
exterior norm. For \(J=\varnothing\), both quantities are \(1\).
Summing over \(J\) proves the boxed expansion.
\end{proof}
The normalization \(\sqrt{d_\nu}\,l_\nu\) is also explicitly used in
\path{Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FlagMeasures/BesselExteriorPowerConstantBoundLargeGap.lean},
lines 80--85. The additional weights \(t_\nu\) and vectors \(v_\nu\)
are choices in our frame construction, not a source-supplied Xi
spectral choice. Zero weights and zero period vectors are permitted.

\paragraph{DET-14(b): necessary and sufficient trace-class condition.}
Suppose, after specified compatible isometric embeddings where needed,
the vectors above form a countable family \(a_1,a_2,\ldots\) in a
single Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\). Set
\(A_N=\sum_{j\le N}a_ja_j^*\). Then
\[
 \sum_j\|a_j\|^2<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sup_N\Tr A_N<\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_N\ \hbox{converges in trace norm}.
\]
The limit \(A\) is positive and
\[
 \Tr A=\sum_j\|a_j\|^2,\qquad
 \|A-A_N\|_1=\sum_{j>N}\|a_j\|^2.
\]
\begin{proof}
The trace norm of \(a_ja_j^*\) is its nonnegative sole eigenvalue
\(\|a_j\|^2\). Thus summability makes the series absolutely
convergent in the Banach space \(S_1\), with sum \(A\).
Trace-norm convergence implies operator-norm convergence.
Limits of nonnegative quadratic forms are nonnegative, so \(A\)
and each tail \(A-A_N\) are positive.
The trace is continuous in trace norm, since
\(|\Tr T|\le\|T\|_1\). Therefore their traces are the corresponding
scalar sums. For a positive trace-class operator the trace norm
equals the trace, proving the exact tail formula.
The finite identity \(\Tr A_N=\sum_{j\le N}\|a_j\|^2\)
shows equivalence of summability and bounded partial traces.
Conversely, trace-norm convergence makes these continuous traces
bounded, proving the remaining implication.
\end{proof}
For these actual period vectors the condition is exactly
\[
 \sum_j t_jd_j\|l_j(v_j)\|^2<\infty,
\]
after the common-space compatibility has been supplied.
The positive rank-one construction makes this a trace-ideal
criterion rather than an inference from summable eigenvalues of a
nonnormal operator. By \fid{DET-05}, these conditions imply local
uniform convergence of the ordinary Fredholm determinants.
An identification with \(G\) still requires a separately proved
spectral or coefficient identity independent of selecting the
vectors or weights from Xi's zeros.

\paragraph{DET-15(b): the fixed model has infinitely many orthogonal source projections.}
The retained \texttt{Std} hypotheses imply that \(H_\sigma\)
is infinite dimensional. There exists an orthonormal sequence
\(u_1,u_2,\ldots\) in \(D\) such that, with the same side data,
measure, representation, decay exponent, formal degree and map \(l\),
each actual convolution \(\operatorname{rconv}(e_{u_j})\)
extends to \(P_j=\eta_{u_j}\eta_{u_j}^*\).
The vectors \(\eta_{u_j}\) are orthonormal in \(\mathcal B\).
\begin{proof}
First, \(\mu_G(G)=\infty\); this can be proved without an
unstated Radon or local-finiteness assumption. Since \(k\ge1\),
the middle block of \(H_{k,r}\) has the positive coordinate \(r\)
and negative coordinate \(r+k\). Let \(g(t)\) act as the
real Lorentz boost on these two coordinates and as the identity
on all others. Then \(g(t)\in G\). The nonzero row covector
\(\ell=e_r^T+e_{r+k}^T\) satisfies \(\ell g(t)=e^t\ell\).
Every \(x\in G\) is invertible, since \(H_{k,r}\) is invertible,
so \(Q(x)=\|\ell x\|>0\). The measurable sets
\[
 E_n=\{x\in G:e^n\le Q(x)<e^{n+1}\},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
\]
partition \(G\), and \(g(1)E_n=E_{n+1}\).
Left invariance gives the same measure to all these sets.
If \(\mu_G(G)\) were finite, their common measure would be zero,
and countable additivity would force \(\mu_G(G)=0\).
That contradicts the nonzero measure supported on \(G\).

If \(H_\sigma\) had finite dimension \(D_0\), then \(D_0\ge1\)
because \(\|w_0\|=1\). For an orthonormal basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_{D_0}\),
unitarity gives pointwise on \(G\)
\[
 \sum_{i,j=1}^{D_0}
 |\langle e_i,\sigma(g)e_j\rangle|^2=D_0.
\]
The Schur relation integrates every summand to \(1/d\).
Its finite sum would therefore give
\(D_0\mu_G(G)=D_0^2/d<\infty\), a contradiction.
Thus \(H_\sigma\) is infinite dimensional.

The closure of \(D\) is nonzero and invariant under every bounded
\(\sigma(g)\). Irreducibility therefore makes it all of \(H_\sigma\).
Recursively choose a vector in \(D\) outside the span of the previously
chosen orthonormal vectors, subtract its finite orthogonal projection
onto that span, and normalize. Each result still lies in the
linear space \(D\), and the recursion never stops since \(D\)
is dense in the infinite-dimensional \(H_\sigma\).
This produces the asserted sequence. The isometry in part (a)
makes the corresponding \(\eta_{u_j}\) orthonormal.

It remains to check that each projection is the stated source
convolution, rather than only an abstract projection. For any unit
\(u\in D\), the orbit span \(D_u=\mathrm{DD}(\sigma,u)\) is
contained in \(D\) by invariance. All clauses of \texttt{Std}
remain unchanged except the unit vector and its orbit span;
the unit norm holds, and the decay bound restricts from \(D\)
to \(D_u\). Thus \texttt{Std}\((\sigma,u,a,\mu_G,d)\) holds.
The \texttt{IsEll} factorization restricts to \(D_u\).
Its nonvanishing clause also holds: part (a) gives
\(\int\|l(\sigma(g)u)\|^2\,d\nu(g)=1/d>0\).
As \(\nu\) is supported on \(G\), some \(g\in G\) has
\(l(\sigma(g)u)\ne0\), and \(\sigma(g)u\in D_u\).
The Bessel-map clause itself is unchanged.
All hypotheses of the source projector theorem therefore hold
with \(w_0\) replaced by \(u\), proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{DET-15(c): explicit trace-class convolution sums.}
Let \((\beta_j)\) be any complex sequence with
\(\sum_j|\beta_j|<\infty\). On this one fixed Hilbert space,
\[
 T_N=\sum_{j\le N}\beta_jP_j,\qquad
 T_\beta=\sum_{j\ge1}\beta_jP_j
\]
are the finite and limiting operators. For each finite \(N\),
\(T_N\) is the bounded extension of the actual source convolution
with \(e_N=\sum_{j\le N}\beta_j e_{u_j}\) on \(\mathcal D\).
Moreover,
\[
 \|T_\beta-T_N\|_1=\sum_{j>N}|\beta_j|,\qquad
 \Tr T_\beta=\sum_j\beta_j,\qquad
 \det(I-wT_\beta)=\prod_j(1-w\beta_j).
\]
The operator is normal; it is positive exactly when every
\(\beta_j\) is real and nonnegative.
\begin{proof}
The orthonormal vectors \(\eta_{u_j}\) diagonalize these operators
on their closed span, and the orthogonal complement is killed.
Consequently the singular values are \(|\beta_j|\), including
multiplicity, and the stated trace-norm, trace and determinant
formulas follow from the absolutely convergent diagonal series
and product. Normality follows by the same diagonal computation.
Positivity is sufficient for nonnegative real \(\beta_j\), and
necessary by testing the quadratic form on each \(\eta_{u_j}\).
For finite \(N\), linearity of the finitely many integrable
convolution expressions on \(\mathcal D\), followed by part (a),
identifies \(T_N\) with the stated convolution.
\end{proof}
For nonnegative weights, the exact trace tail is \(\sum_{j>N}\beta_j\).
The unweighted partial sums instead have trace \(N\), so they
cannot converge in trace norm. Thus the common-space problem for
these actual projections is solved; the trace-class passage
requires a specified summable weighting.
For example, \(\beta_j=2^{-j}\) gives a positive trace-class
construction independent of all zeros, with trace \(1\) and
exact trace-norm tail \(2^{-N}\). This example does not claim
the Xi determinant.

\paragraph{An actual measurable integral kernel.}
Write \(\eta_j(x)=\sqrt d\,f_{u_j}(x)\) using the measurable
representatives constructed above. Then
\[
 K_\beta(x,y)=\sum_j\beta_j\eta_j(x)\eta_j(y)^*
\]
converges absolutely in operator norm for almost every \((x,y)\),
belongs to \(L^2(\nu\times\nu;\mathrm{HS}(W_p))\), and is an
integral kernel for \(T_\beta\) on \(\mathcal B\).
\begin{proof}
Put \(a(x)=\sum_j|\beta_j|\|\eta_j(x)\|^2\).
Tonelli and \(\|\eta_j\|_2=1\) give
\(\int a\,d\nu=\sum_j|\beta_j|<\infty\), so \(a(x)<\infty\)
almost everywhere. Cauchy--Schwarz for the series gives
\[
 \sum_j|\beta_j|\|\eta_j(x)\|\,\|\eta_j(y)\|
 \le\sqrt{a(x)a(y)}.
\]
The rank-one matrix norm and Hilbert--Schmidt norm are both
\(\|\eta_j(x)\|\,\|\eta_j(y)\|\).
This proves absolute convergence and measurability, and bounds
the squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm of the sum by \(a(x)a(y)\),
which is integrable on the product. Applying the same bound
to tails proves convergence in product \(L^2\).

For almost every fixed \(x\) and any \(W\in L^2(\nu;W_p)\),
\[
 \int\|K_\beta(x,y)W(y)\|\,d\nu(y)
 \le\sqrt{a(x)}\left(\sum_j|\beta_j|\right)^{1/2}\|W\|_2,
\]
again by Cauchy--Schwarz. Thus the kernel integral is defined.
For an \(L^2\) matrix kernel, pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz and
integration show that the operator norm is at most its
product \(L^2\) Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Therefore the finite
kernel sums converge as operators to this integral operator.
Their operators are precisely \(T_N\), so the limit is
\(T_\beta\), including on \(\mathcal B\).

\subsection{DET-17: prime weights give an arithmetic trace, but a different determinant}

The source does supply useful nonnegative summable arithmetic

\begin{proposition}[DET-17]
Retain one local source model and the actual orthogonal rank-one
convolution projections \(P_n=\eta_n\eta_n^*\) of \fid{DET-15},
indexed by \(n\ge2\).  For \(\Re s>1\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bessel-prime-operator}
 A(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}P_n,\qquad
 A_N(s)=\sum_{2\le n\le N}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}P_n .
\end{equation}
Then \(A(s)\) is normal and trace class, with
\begin{align}
 \|A(s)-A_N(s)\|_1
 &=\sum_{n>N}\Lambda(n)n^{-\Re s},
 \label{eq:bessel-prime-tail}\\
 \Tr A(s)&=-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)},\\
 D_s(w):=\det(I-wA(s))
 &=\prod_{n\ge2}\bigl(1-w\Lambda(n)n^{-s}\bigr).
 \label{eq:bessel-prime-det}
\end{align}
For real \(\sigma>1\), \(A(\sigma)\) and all its finite
approximants are positive.  Each finite approximant is the
bounded extension of an actual source convolution.

At \(\sigma=2\), \(D_2\) is not \(G(w)=\Xi(\sqrt w)/\Xi(0)\),
even after any fixed nonzero rescaling of \(w\) or multiplication
by a zero-free entire factor.  This obstruction requires neither
RH nor the prime number theorem.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The source prime coefficients and their normalization are exact.
\path{Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/ExplicitFormula/PerronTruncOf.lean},
lines 45--52, constructs their summable positive real majorant,
using the general von Mangoldt Dirichlet-series theorem; lines
53--60 bound complex terms by that majorant.
\path{Formalism/ZeroFreeRegion/ZetaLogDerivativeBounds/NormLogDerivLeLamF.lean},
lines 10--31, gives the termwise identification with
\(\Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}\), positivity, absolute summability,
and the log-derivative identity.  The latter is also used
explicitly in
\path{Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/mellinGchiModOne.lean},
lines 168--193, after the absolute-convergence argument for the
actual prime-ramp Mellin transform.

For completeness, summability also follows from
\(0\le\Lambda(n)\le\log n\), used explicitly in
\path{Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/ExplicitFormula/PerronSumBound.lean},
lines 173 and 190--195.  If \(\sigma>1\), choose
\(\epsilon=(\sigma-1)/2>0\).  The elementary bound
\(\log x\le x^\epsilon/\epsilon\), \(x\ge1\), gives
\[
 \Lambda(n)n^{-\sigma}
 \le\epsilon^{-1}n^{-(\sigma+1)/2},
\]
whose sum converges.  For complex \(s\), the absolute value of
the coefficient in \eqref{eq:bessel-prime-operator} is exactly
\(\Lambda(n)n^{-\Re s}\).

On the orthonormal family \(\eta_n\), the operator in
\eqref{eq:bessel-prime-operator} is diagonal with those
coefficients, and is zero on the orthogonal complement of
their span.  Its singular values are their absolute values.
This proves trace class, normality, and
\eqref{eq:bessel-prime-tail}.  The continuous trace on the
trace ideal gives
\[
 \Tr A(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}
 =-\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s),\qquad \Re s>1,
\]
with the sign and normalization of the cited source identity.
For real \(\sigma>1\) the diagonal coefficients are
nonnegative, proving positivity.

The finite operators are actual source convolutions with
\[
 e_{N,s}(g)=
 \sum_{2\le n\le N}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}
 \,\mathrm{eFun}(\mu_G,\sigma_{\rm rep},u_n)(g).
\]
There is no further formal-degree factor: each source
\(\mathrm{eFun}\) already gives \(P_n\), whose trace is one.
The infinite operator also has the actual integral kernel
\[
 K_s(x,y)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}
                 \eta_n(x)\eta_n(y)^*
\]
with the almost-everywhere absolute convergence and product
\(L^2\) convergence proved in \fid{DET-15}.
No unproved infinite group-convolution kernel is asserted.
The ordinary trace-class determinant formula gives
\eqref{eq:bessel-prime-det}.  Absolute summability makes this
product locally uniformly convergent in \(w\).
Away from the zeros of its individual nonzero factors,
the tail has an absolutely convergent logarithm series;
therefore these factors give all its zeros, with their full
coincident-factor multiplicities.

At \(s=2\), its zeros are consequently
\[
 w_n=\frac{n^2}{\Lambda(n)}
 \quad\text{for }\Lambda(n)>0.
\]
Let \(N_2(R)\) count them in \(|w|\le R\), including
multiplicity.  For each counted \(n\ge2\),
\[
 n^2\le R\Lambda(n)\le R\log n<Rn,
\]
so \(n<R\), and then \(n^2\le R\log R\).  Counting all integers
rather than just prime powers gives, for sufficiently large \(R\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bessel-prime-zerocount}
 N_2(R)\le\lfloor\sqrt{R\log R}\rfloor.
\end{equation}
This elementary upper bound needs no information about the
distribution of primes.

In contrast, the classical zero count cited in \fid{B-01},
\cite[Theorem 9.4, (9.4.3), p.~214]{titch}, is
\[
 N_\zeta(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}
            -\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T).
\]
Write \(\gamma=-i(\rho-\tfrac12)\).  The functional equation
pairs \(\rho\) with \(1-\rho\), with equal multiplicity, so each
nonreal pair contributes once to the zeros \(w=\gamma^2\) of
\(G\).  The critical-strip bound gives
\[
 (\Im\rho)^2\le|\rho-\tfrac12|^2
             \le(\Im\rho)^2+\tfrac14.
\]
Allowing an irrelevant bounded number of possible real zeros,
the number \(N_G(R)\) of zeros of \(G\) in \(|w|\le R\) satisfies
\[
 N_\zeta(\sqrt{R-\tfrac14})\le N_G(R)
 \le N_\zeta(\sqrt R)+O(1).
\]
The two sides have the same leading term; hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bessel-Xi-zerocount}
 N_G(R)\sim\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt R\log R.
\end{equation}
The upper bound \eqref{eq:bessel-prime-zerocount} is
\(o(\sqrt R\log R)\), so the two determinants cannot agree.
For fixed \(a\ne0\), the zero count of \(D_2(aw)\) is
\(N_2(|a|R)=O_a(\sqrt{R\log R})\), giving the same contradiction.
A zero-free entire multiplier does not change the zero count.
\end{proof}

\subsection{DET-11: an exact resolvent bridge for the scalar prime-sum endpoint}
There is a stronger affirmative comparison than the common residue denominator. It concerns the scalar endpoint itself, rather than an identification of the upstream finite matrices. Let $G_\zeta$ be exactly the function in \fid{DET-09}, and put
\[
 \Phi_\zeta(s)=\int_0^\infty G_\zeta(e^u)e^{-su}\,du,
 \qquad v=s(1-s),\qquad C=B(I+B/4)^{-1}.
\]
Write $\psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma$ and
\[
 A_\infty(s)=\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}-\frac12\log\pi+
 \frac12\psi(s/2).
\]
The following identities are unconditional, with the zero-based $B$ of \fid{B-01}.
\begin{proposition}
The trace-class determinant $D_C(v)=\det(I-vC)$ satisfies, for every $s\in\C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cdetcompletion}
 D_C(s(1-s))=2\xi(s).
\end{equation}
Where $\xi(s)\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cresolvent}
 \frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}=(2s-1)\Tr\bigl(C(I-s(1-s)C)^{-1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, for $\Re s>1$ the actual scalar transform is
\begin{align}\label{eq:Phiresolvent}
 \Phi_\zeta(s)
 &=\frac{A_\infty(s)-(2s-1)\Tr\bigl(C(I-vC)^{-1}\bigr)}{s(s-1)}\notag\\
 &\hspace{7mm}-\frac1{(s-1)^2}+\frac{1+\gamma_E}{s-1}.
\end{align}
Thus its nontrivial-zero contribution is an ordinary trace of a resolvent of a fixed trace-class operator after explicit completion terms. This representation uses the zeros as input.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The diagonal entries of $I+B/4$ are nonzero: a zero would give $\gamma^2=-1/4$, hence a zero of $\xi$ at $0$ or $1$, whereas $\xi(0)=\xi(1)=1/2$. They tend to one, so the inverse is bounded. The entries of $C$ are therefore
\[
 c_j=(\gamma_j^2+1/4)^{-1}=[\rho_j(1-\rho_j)]^{-1},
 \qquad \sum_j|c_j|<\infty.
\]
Absolute locally uniform convergence of the products gives
\[
 D_C(v)=\frac{\prod_j(1-(v-1/4)\gamma_j^{-2})}
 {\prod_j(1+\gamma_j^{-2}/4)}
 =\frac{G(v-1/4)}{G(-1/4)}.
\]
Since $v-1/4=-(s-1/2)^2$, evenness of $\Xi$ gives
$G(v-1/4)=\xi(s)/\xi(1/2)$ and
$G(-1/4)=\xi(0)/\xi(1/2)$. This proves \eqref{eq:Cdetcompletion}, including the normalization factor two and with no square-root branch choice.

On any compact set avoiding the zeros of $D_C$, the sum

 \frac{D_C'(v)}{D_C(v)}=-\sum_j\frac{c_j}{1-vc_j}
 =-\Tr\bigl(C(I-vC)^{-1}\bigr).
\]
The inverse is bounded by the same diagonal argument, and its product with $C$ is trace class. The chain rule and $v'(s)=1-2s$ prove \eqref{eq:Cresolvent}. At $s=1/2$ no division by $2s-1$ is made; both sides of that identity vanish.

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Completions and normalization.}
For a nontrivial primitive character of conductor $q$, put
\[
 \kappa_\chi=\frac{1-\chi(-1)}2,\qquad
 F_\chi(s)=
 (q/\pi)^{(s+\kappa_\chi)/2}
 \Gamma((s+\kappa_\chi)/2)L(s,\chi),
 \qquad \chi^\vee=\chi^{-1}.
\]
For the modulus-one character put
\[
 F_\zeta(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)=2\xi(s).
\]
These are precisely the Dirichlet completion conventions of
\fid{MAS-11}. The classical completed functional equation and
entire-order-one assertion are used as background:
\cite[(25.15.5)--(25.15.6)]{dlmf2515},
\cite[\S6.1; (6.2.2)]{kedlayaFE}, and
\cite[\S1, p.~282]{rajan1998}.
In particular,
\[
 F_\chi(s)=\epsilon_\chi F_{\chi^\vee}(1-s),
 \qquad \epsilon_\chi\epsilon_{\chi^\vee}=1,\qquad
 F_\zeta(s)=F_\zeta(1-s).
\]
The last root-number identity follows by applying the functional equation
twice to the nonzero function $F_\chi$.
The nonprincipal completions are entire; the two simple poles of the
principal completion are cancelled by $s(s-1)$, without adding zeros.
Nonvanishing at $s=1$ and the functional equation give
$F_\chi(0)F_\chi(1)\ne0$; also
$F_\zeta(0)=F_\zeta(1)=1$.
For the source's nonvanishing input see
\path{Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/CharFamilyLFunctionNeZeroOfOneLeRe.lean},
lines 9--20. Together with the functional equation this places the
completed zeros in $0<\Re\rho<1$.

Choose any positive integer $M$ such that
$b_i=M\alpha_i/2$ is an integer for every $i$.
The uniform choice
\[
 M=2d_{\mathcal L},\qquad
 b_i\in\{d_{\mathcal L},1\}
\]
always works. It is a sufficient choice, not a claim of minimality.
Define the unambiguously entire function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DET18completedproduct}
 \mathscr P_M(s)=
 \left(\frac{F_\zeta(s)}{F_\zeta(0)}\right)^M
 \prod_{i\in I}
 \left(
 \frac{F_{\chi_i}(s)F_{\chi_i^\vee}(s)}
 {F_{\chi_i}(0)F_{\chi_i^\vee}(0)}
 \right)^{b_i}.
\end{equation}
No fractional powers occur in this definition. Define the meromorphic
weighted logarithmic derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DET18weightedlog}
 \mathscr L_{\mathcal L}(s)=
 \frac{F_\zeta'(s)}{F_\zeta(s)}
 +\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\alpha_i}{2}
 \left(
 \frac{F_{\chi_i}'(s)}{F_{\chi_i}(s)}
 +\frac{F_{\chi_i^\vee}'(s)}{F_{\chi_i^\vee}(s)}
 \right).
\end{equation}
If $m_\chi(\rho)=\operatorname{ord}_\rho F_\chi$, with value zero
when $F_\chi(\rho)\ne0$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DET18weightedmultiplicity}
 S_\rho=m_\zeta(\rho)+
 \sum_{i\in I}\frac{\alpha_i}{2}
 \bigl(m_{\chi_i}(\rho)+m_{\chi_i^\vee}(\rho)\bigr).
\end{equation}
At every zero in this union, $S_\rho>0$ and
$\operatorname{ord}_\rho\mathscr P_M=MS_\rho$.
Coincident zeros of different characters contribute additively.

\begin{proposition}
There is a diagonal normal operator $C_{\mathcal L,M}\in\mathcal S_1$
such that, for every $s\in\C$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DET18det}
 \det(I-s(1-s)C_{\mathcal L,M})=\mathscr P_M(s).
\end{equation}
For each unordered noncentral pair $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$ its diagonal
contains
$[\rho(1-\rho)]^{-1}$ with multiplicity $MS_\rho$.
At the central point it contains $4$ with multiplicity $MS_{1/2}/2$,
understood as zero if there is no central zero.
These multiplicities are nonnegative integers.
Away from the completed zero set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DET18resolvent}
 \mathscr L_{\mathcal L}(s)
 =\frac1M\frac{\mathscr P_M'(s)}{\mathscr P_M(s)}
 =\frac{2s-1}{M}
 \Tr\!\left(C_{\mathcal L,M}
 (I-s(1-s)C_{\mathcal L,M})^{-1}\right).
\end{equation}
The determinant and trace identities require no GRH. Under GRH for the
retained family, $0\le C_{\mathcal L,M}\le4I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every paired factor in \eqref{eq:DET18completedproduct} is invariant
under $s\mapsto1-s$: its two functional-equation constants have product
one. Thus $\mathscr P_M(s)=\mathscr P_M(1-s)$,
$\mathscr P_M(0)=\mathscr P_M(1)=1$, and $\mathscr P_M$ is entire of
order at most one. The functional equation also gives
$m_{\chi}(1/2)=m_{\chi^\vee}(1/2)$. Each paired factor consequently
has even central order. The zeta factor has even central order by its
own symmetry. This proves $MS_{1/2}\in2\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ without a
central nonvanishing assumption.

Set $E(z)=\mathscr P_M(1/2+z)$. Its entire power series has only even
powers. Consequently there is a unique entire function $\mathscr D_M$
with
\[
 E(z)=\mathscr D_M(1/4-z^2),\qquad \mathscr D_M(0)=1.
\]
This definition involves no choice of a square-root branch. If
$\mathfrak M_E(r)=\max_{|z|\le r}|E(z)|$, then
\[
 \max_{|v|\le r}|\mathscr D_M(v)|
 \le \mathfrak M_E\bigl(\sqrt{r+1/4}\bigr).
\]
Hence $\mathscr D_M$ has order at most $1/2$.

The map $s\mapsto v=s(1-s)$ is locally invertible at
$\rho\ne1/2$ and has local expression $v=1/4-(s-1/2)^2$ at the
central point. It follows that the distinct zeros of $\mathscr D_M$
are $v_\rho=\rho(1-\rho)$, with multiplicity $MS_\rho$ for each
unordered noncentral pair and multiplicity $MS_{1/2}/2$ at $v=1/4$.
There is no zero at $v=0$. Write the resulting multiplicity at a
distinct $v$ as $q_v$.

For completeness, the order bound gives the summability needed for an
ordinary determinant. Choose $1/2<\theta<1$. For large $R$ the
logarithm of the maximum modulus of $\mathscr D_M$ on $|v|\le2R$
is at most $C_\theta R^\theta$. Jensen's formula, using
$\mathscr D_M(0)=1$, implies
$\sum_{|v|\le R}q_v=O_\theta(R^\theta)$.
Dyadic summation now gives
\[
 \sum_{\mathscr D_M(v)=0}\frac{q_v}{|v|}<\infty.
\]
Hadamard factorization for an entire function of order less than one
has genus zero and no nonconstant exponential factor. Its constant
is fixed by $\mathscr D_M(0)=1$, so
\[
 \mathscr D_M(v)=\prod_{\mathscr D_M(a)=0}
 (1-v/a)^{q_a}.
\]
On a Hilbert space with one orthonormal coordinate for each repeated
zero $a$, let $C_{\mathcal L,M}$ be diagonal with entry $a^{-1}$.
The displayed summability proves membership in $\mathcal S_1$.
Its ordinary Fredholm product is the displayed product for
$\mathscr D_M$, proving \eqref{eq:DET18det}. This argument does not
infer trace class from eigenvalue summability for an unspecified
non-normal operator; the operator has been explicitly defined as
diagonal.

On a compact set disjoint from the zero set of $\mathscr D_M$,
the series
\[
 \sum_a \frac{q_a/a}{1-v/a}
\]
converges absolutely and uniformly. The tail denominators are
uniformly bounded away from zero since $a^{-1}\to0$; the remaining
denominators form a finite nonvanishing set. The same reasoning
gives a bounded diagonal inverse of $I-vC_{\mathcal L,M}$.
Thus termwise logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{\mathscr D_M'(v)}{\mathscr D_M(v)}
 =-\Tr\!\left(C_{\mathcal L,M}(I-vC_{\mathcal L,M})^{-1}\right).
\]
The chain rule, $v'(s)=1-2s$, and the integer exponents in
\eqref{eq:DET18completedproduct} prove
\eqref{eq:DET18resolvent}.

If $S_{1/2}=0$, both sides of \eqref{eq:DET18resolvent} vanish at
$s=1/2$. If $S_{1/2}>0$, put $z=s-1/2$ and
$q=MS_{1/2}/2$. The central block contributes $q/z^2$ to the
resolvent trace and therefore $S_{1/2}/z$ to the right side of
\eqref{eq:DET18resolvent}. This is exactly the logarithmic
derivative's central pole. No division by $2s-1$, and no removal
of central zeros, is used.

Under GRH, $\rho=1/2+it$ and
$[\rho(1-\rho)]^{-1}=(1/4+t^2)^{-1}\in(0,4]$.
This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The precise positivity condition.}
In fact, for this diagonal realization, GRH for the retained family
is equivalent to $0\le C_{\mathcal L,M}\le4I$.
To see the converse, write $\rho=\beta+it$ with $0<\beta<1$.
Then
\[
 \rho(1-\rho)=\beta(1-\beta)+t^2+i\,t(1-2\beta).
\]
Its real part is positive. If its reciprocal is real and positive,
either $\beta=1/2$ or $t=0$. In the latter case
$(\beta(1-\beta))^{-1}\ge4$, with equality only at $\beta=1/2$.
The upper bound $C_{\mathcal L,M}\le4I$ therefore forces the
critical line also in this case. Positivity alone would permit a
real off-center zero with eigenvalue greater than four; no such zero
is asserted to exist here.

\paragraph{When the integer multiplication can be omitted.}
The following is an exact condition, not an assertion that any
actual retained zero has a fractional weight.
\begin{proposition}
There exists $T\in\mathcal S_1$ with ordinary determinant
$D(v)=\det(I-vT)$ such that
\[
 \mathscr L_{\mathcal L}(s)
 =\frac{d}{ds}\log D(s(1-s))
\]
as meromorphic functions if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DET18integrality}
 S_\rho\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\quad(\rho\ne1/2),\qquad
 S_{1/2}/2\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\end{equation}
If these conditions hold, $T$ can be chosen diagonal with the same
eigenvalues as above and these reduced multiplicities. It is positive
under the stipulated GRH.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every zero of an ordinary entire determinant has an integer
multiplicity. At a noncentral preimage the map $s\mapsto s(1-s)$
preserves it, whereas at $s=1/2$ it doubles it. Logarithmic
derivatives have precisely these zero multiplicities as residues.
This proves necessity.

For sufficiency, divide every multiplicity $q_a$ in the product for
$\mathscr D_M$ by $M$. Conditions
\eqref{eq:DET18integrality} say exactly that the results are
nonnegative integers. The reciprocal-eigenvalue sum remains finite,
so the resulting diagonal operator $T$ is trace class. Its
normalized determinant $D$ satisfies $D(v)^M=\mathscr D_M(v)$ by
the absolutely convergent products. Pull back along $v=s(1-s)$
and logarithmically differentiate to obtain the asserted identity.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, the normalized analytic germ
$\mathscr D_M(v)^{1/M}$ at $v=0$ extends to an entire ordinary
determinant precisely when \eqref{eq:DET18integrality} holds.
Positive rational weights alone do not establish these
divisor-integrality conditions. For the actual level, denominators
of $S_\rho$ and $S_{1/2}/2$ divide $2d_{\mathcal L}$. Thus the least
integer multiplication needed at the divisor level is
\[
 \operatorname{lcm}\left(
 \{\operatorname{den}(S_\rho):\rho\ne1/2\}
 \cup\{\operatorname{den}(S_{1/2}/2)\}\right),
\]
where $\operatorname{den}(0)=1$. This least common multiple is
finite because all its entries divide the single integer
$2d_{\mathcal L}$. Its numerical value is not inferred from
positivity.

There is additional structure in the actual source array.
\texttt{KSet} is inverse-closed and its exceptional character is
real, by \path{PrimePairSizeCondition.lean}, lines 39--45.
A real Dirichlet character equals its inverse. Consequently
$I$ is inverse-closed and $\alpha_{\chi^\vee}=\alpha_\chi$:
the exceptional character is fixed by inversion, and all other

Reindexing the finite sum then gives
\[
 S_\rho=m_\zeta(\rho)+\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i m_{\chi_i}(\rho).
\]
In particular, the half in the general family formula is not an
additional denominator at noncentral zeros for this actual array.
This observation still does not evaluate the multiplicities needed
to omit all integer multiplication.

The factor $1/M$ in \eqref{eq:DET18resolvent} also cannot in general
be absorbed by replacing $C_{\mathcal L,M}$ by

the coefficients of $v$ in
\[
 \frac1M\Tr(C_{\mathcal L,M}(I-vC_{\mathcal L,M})^{-1})
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad

are respectively $\Tr(C_{\mathcal L,M}^2)/M$ and

Integer multiplicities, trace scaling, and eigenvalue scaling are

\subsection{STEP6-01--03: an exact candidate for the two-indicator Weil test}
The timeline does not define its $W$. We can nevertheless give a precise candidate normalization, identify its arithmetic cross term, and prove the stated half-plane shape. None of these calculations attributes this normalization to the historical determinant construction.

Put $g=\mathbf1_{(0,1]}$, $g_X=\mathbf1_{(0,X^{-1}]}$ and $f^\pm_{X,\eta}=g\pm X^\eta g_X$, for $X\ge1$ and $\eta\in\R$. On these real functions define
\[
 \M f(s)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^s\,\frac{dx}{x}\quad(\Re s>0),
 \qquad W_{\rm std}(f)=\sum_\rho\M f(\rho)\M f(1-\rho),
\]
with all nontrivial zeros and their multiplicities. This is a quadratic form in $f$. If $W$ instead denotes the linear zero functional, this expression applies it to $f*_\times f^\sharp$, where $f^\sharp(x)=x^{-1}\overline{f(x^{-1})}$: its Mellin transform is $\M f(s)\overline{\M f(1-\bar s)}$. Fubini is valid for these functions on $0<\Re s<1$. For example, $g*_\times g^\sharp(x)=\min(1,x^{-1})$, not $g(x)$.

\paragraph{STEP6-01: the trace and its positive product.}
Use the paired-zero Hilbert space of \fid{B-01}, put
\[
 Ae_j=\gamma_je_j,\qquad
 D(A)=\{v:\sum_j|\gamma_j|^2|v_j|^2<\infty\},\qquad
 C=(A^2+I/4)^{-1}=B(I+B/4)^{-1}.
\]
Here $A$ is the maximal closed normal diagonal operator. For $L=\log X$ and $a=X^{\eta-1/2}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WeilIndicatorTrace}
 W_{\rm std}(f^\pm_{X,\eta})
 =2\Tr\!\left[C\bigl((1+a^2)I\pm2a\cos(AL)\bigr)\right],
 \qquad W_{\rm std}(g)=2\Tr C=2\lambda_1.
\end{equation}
Under RH, $A$ is selfadjoint, $U=e^{iAL}$ is unitary and, writing $F_\pm=I\pm aU$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WeilIndicatorPositive}
 W_{\rm std}(f^\pm_{X,\eta})
 =2\Tr(CF_\pm^*F_\pm)
 =2\|F_\pm C^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal S_2}^{2}\ge0.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Direct absolutely convergent integration gives $\M f^\pm(s)=(1\pm X^{\eta-s})/s$ for $\Re s>0$. If $\rho=\beta+it$ is a nontrivial zero, the numerator product is bounded by $(1+X^\eta)^2$ and $|\rho(1-\rho)|\ge t^2$. The zero count $N(T)=O(T\log T)$ gives
$\sum_\rho|\rho(1-\rho)|^{-1}<\infty$: the dyadic annulus $2^m\le|t|<2^{m+1}$ contributes $O((m+1)2^{-m})$, and the finitely many low zeros have nonzero denominators. Thus the quadratic sum is absolutely convergent, locally uniformly in $X,\eta$, and can be paired or conjugated.

The entries $c_j=(\gamma_j^2+1/4)^{-1}=[\rho_j(1-\rho_j)]^{-1}$ define a trace-class $C$. The sequences $c_j$ and $\gamma_j^2c_j$ are bounded: the denominators never vanish and $|\gamma_j|\to\infty$. Hence $C$ maps the Hilbert space into $D(A^2)$ and is the two-sided inverse of $A^2+I/4$ on the appropriate domains. Since $|\Im\gamma_j|<1/2$, the diagonal operators $e^{\pm iAL}$ and $\cos(AL)$ are bounded, with entry bounds $X^{1/2}$ and $\cosh(L/2)$. Their products with $C$ are trace class.

For either sign, expansion gives
\[
 \M f^\pm(\rho)\M f^\pm(1-\rho)
 =\frac{1+X^{2\eta-1}\pm X^{\eta-\rho}\pm X^{\eta-1+\rho}}
 {\rho(1-\rho)}
 =\frac{1+a^2\pm2a\cos(\gamma L)}{\gamma^2+1/4}.
\]
This is even in $\gamma$, so every reflection pair contributes twice the representative's diagonal entry. It proves the first identity in \eqref{eq:WeilIndicatorTrace}. The base test gives $2\Tr C$, and \fid{B-03} gives $\Tr C=\lambda_1$.

Under RH, $C\ge0$ and all these diagonal operators commute. Therefore
$F_\pm^*F_\pm=(1+a^2)I\pm a(U+U^*)=(1+a^2)I\pm2a\cos(AL)$.
The operator $C^{1/2}$ is Hilbert--Schmidt. Taking the trace of
$(F_\pm C^{1/2})^*(F_\pm C^{1/2})$ proves \eqref{eq:WeilIndicatorPositive}. Off RH, the exact trace remains valid, but $U^{-1}$ need not be $U^*$ and $C$ need not be positive; the positive-product step is not asserted there.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{STEP6-02: the actual smoothed sum is a weighted cosine trace.}
Let $G_\zeta(X)$ be the exact source scalar of \fid{DET-09}, namely
$\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)(X/n-1)-X\log X+(1+\gamma_E)X$. Set
\[
 R(X)=\sum_\rho\frac{X^\rho}{\rho(1-\rho)},\qquad
 b(X)=\log(2\pi)-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{X^{-2n}}{2n(2n+1)}.
\]
Unconditionally, for every $X\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GzCosine}
 G_\zeta(X)=b(X)+R(X)
 =b(X)+2\sqrt X\,\Tr\bigl(C\cos(A\log X)\bigr).
\end{equation}
All sums converge absolutely and locally uniformly. The correction satisfies
\[
 b(1)=\log(4\pi)-1,\qquad
 \log(4\pi)-1\le b(X)<\log(2\pi).
\]
Under RH, \eqref{eq:GzCosine} gives
$|G_\zeta(X)-b(X)|\le2\lambda_1\sqrt X$.
\begin{proof}
The primary explicit-formula input is the \emph{corrected} Lemma 2.1 of Ramar\'e's corrigendum \cite[pp.~1--2]{ramare}; the original article's Lemma 2.2 has sign errors. With $\psi(X)=\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)$ and $\widetilde\psi(X)=\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)/n$, the corrected formula is
\[
 \widetilde\psi(X)=\log X-\gamma_E+\frac{\psi(X)-X}{X}
 -\sum_\rho\frac{X^{\rho-1}}{\rho(\rho-1)}+\frac{b(X)}X.
\]
Multiply by $X$, subtract $\psi(X)$ and then subtract
$X\log X-(1+\gamma_E)X$. This proves $G_\zeta=R+b$, because the source ramp is $X\widetilde\psi-\psi$. The zero sum is absolutely convergent by $|X^\rho|\le X$ and the reciprocal-denominator estimate above. Pairing $\rho$ with $1-\rho$ gives
$X^\rho+X^{1-\rho}=2\sqrt X\cos(\gamma\log X)$ and proves the trace identity.

The corrigendum also gives
\[
 b(X)=X\int_X^\infty
 \left(\log(2\pi)+\tfrac12\log(1-t^{-2})\right)\frac{dt}{t^2}.
\]
Expand $-\log(1-t^{-2})=\sum_{n\ge1}t^{-2n}/n$ and apply Tonelli to the nonnegative series. This gives exactly the displayed series for $b$, including $X=1$, where the logarithmic singularity is integrable. The identity
$1/[2n(2n+1)]=1/(2n)-1/(2n+1)$ gives the sum $1-\log2$ at $X=1$. Positivity and monotonicity of its terms prove the bounds and continuity. For $X>1$ the equivalent elementary expression is
\[
 b(X)=\log(2\pi)+\tfrac12\log(1-X^{-2})
 +\tfrac12\left(X\log\frac{X+1}{X-1}-2\right).
\]
At prime-power endpoints the jumps of $X\widetilde\psi-\psi$ cancel, so no half-weight convention remains in $G_\zeta$. Setting $X=1$ checks
$2\lambda_1=G_\zeta(1)-b(1)=2+\gamma_E-\log(4\pi)$.
Under RH, $|\cos(\gamma_j\log X)|\le1$ and $c_j>0$, giving the asserted bound.
\end{proof}
This derivation cites the classical analytic explicit formula; it independently proves its algebraic translation, convergence, endpoint handling and trace representation. It does not construct $A$ arithmetically from a period determinant.

\paragraph{STEP6-03: exact one-sided bounds and the half-plane implication.}
The two reflected cross sums coincide, so
\begin{align}
 W_{\rm std}(f^\pm_{X,\eta})
 &=2\lambda_1(1+X^{2\eta-1})
   \pm2X^{\eta-1}\bigl(G_\zeta(X)-b(X)\bigr),\label{eq:WeilGz}\\
 W_{\rm std}(f^+_{X,\eta})\ge0
 &\Longleftrightarrow G_\zeta(X)\ge b(X)-\lambda_1(X^{1-\eta}+X^\eta),\label{eq:WeilLower}\\
 W_{\rm std}(f^-_{X,\eta})\ge0
 &\Longleftrightarrow G_\zeta(X)\le b(X)+\lambda_1(X^{1-\eta}+X^\eta).\label{eq:WeilUpper}
\end{align}
Thus the plus sign gives a lower bound, while the minus sign gives an upper bound. At $X=1$ the values are $8\lambda_1$ and $0$, consistent with $f^+=2g$ and $f^-=0$.

\begin{lemma}
If $0<\theta<1$ and either $G_\zeta(X)\ge-KX^\theta$ or $G_\zeta(X)\le KX^\theta$ for some $K\ge0$ and all sufficiently large $X$, then $\zeta$ has no zero with real part greater than $\theta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The scalar Mellin identity established in \fid{DET-11} is
\[
 \Phi_\zeta(s)=-\frac{\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)}{s(s-1)}
 -\frac1{(s-1)^2}+\frac{1+\gamma_E}{s-1}\qquad(\Re s>1).
\]
It continues meromorphically to $\Re s>0$. At $s=1$, putting $h=s-1$, the expansion
$-\zeta'/\zeta=h^{-1}-\gamma_E+O(h)$ makes the first term
$h^{-2}-(1+\gamma_E)h^{-1}+O(1)$, so the polar terms cancel. At a nontrivial zero $\rho$ of multiplicity $m$, its residue is $m/[\rho(1-\rho)]\ne0$. There are no real zeros on $(0,1)$, since the positive alternating eta series equals $(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)$ there; for $s>1$ use the Euler product. Thus $\Phi_\zeta$ is analytic near every positive real point.

For a lower bound choose $u_0\ge0$ beyond its threshold and define $h(u)=0$ before $u_0$ and $h(u)=G_\zeta(e^u)+Ke^{\theta u}\ge0$ afterwards. The crude bound $G_\zeta(X)=O(X(1+\log X)^2)$ follows from $\Lambda(n)\le\log n$. Hence its Laplace transform $H$ converges for $\Re s>1$, where
\[
 H(s)=\Phi_\zeta(s)-\int_0^{u_0}G_\zeta(e^u)e^{-su}\,du
       +\frac{Ke^{-(s-\theta)u_0}}{s-\theta}.
\]
The compact integral is entire. This gives meromorphic continuation of $H$ to $\Re s>\theta$, analytic near every real point there, with nonzero poles at any zeta zeros there.

For clarity, the required positive-Laplace fact has a short proof. If a nonnegative locally integrable function has finite abscissa of convergence $\sigma_c$, its Laplace transform is singular at the real point $\sigma_c$. Otherwise let a disc of analytic continuation there have radius $\delta>0$. Set $\sigma_0=\sigma_c+\delta/4$ and $r=\delta/2$. The Taylor disc about $\sigma_0$ has radius at least $3\delta/4$, whereas $\sigma_0-r<\sigma_c$. Differentiation under the integral at $\sigma_0$ is justified by convergence at a slightly smaller point still above $\sigma_c$. Its Taylor series towards the left has nonnegative terms, and Tonelli gives
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{r^n}{n!}
  \int_0^\infty u^nh(u)e^{-\sigma_0u}\,du
 =\int_0^\infty h(u)e^{-(\sigma_0-r)u}\,du<\infty,
\]
contradicting the definition of $\sigma_c$. The case $\sigma_c=-\infty$ already converges everywhere.

Here $\sigma_c\le1$. If $\sigma_c>\theta$, the displayed continuation is analytic through that real point, contradicting this fact. Thus $\sigma_c\le\theta$, so $H$ is holomorphic on $\Re s>\theta$. Meromorphic uniqueness forces $\Phi_\zeta$ to have no pole there, excluding all the indicated zeros. For an upper bound use $h(u)=Ke^{\theta u}-G_\zeta(e^u)$ beyond $u_0$; the same proof applies to $-\Phi_\zeta$ and its nonzero residues.
\end{proof}

For $0<\eta<1$, eventual nonnegativity of either specified test therefore gives zero-freeness in $\Re s>\max(\eta,1-\eta)$, by \eqref{eq:WeilLower}--\eqref{eq:WeilUpper} and boundedness of $b$. In particular $0<\eta\le1/2$ gives exactly $\Re s>1-\eta$. At $\eta=1/2$ eventual positivity of the plus-test family is RH-equivalent: the forward direction uses the lemma and reflection, and the reverse is \eqref{eq:WeilIndicatorPositive}. This proves an equivalence for the defined form, not the unprovided historical identification.

The approximate amplitude is also transparent. For any real $q(X)>0$, the same expansion gives
$W_{\rm std}(g+qg_X)=2\lambda_1(1+q^2/X)+2qR(X)/X$.
Its positivity is exactly $G_\zeta(X)\ge b(X)-\lambda_1(X/q+q)$.
If $c_1X^\eta\le q(X)\le c_2X^\eta$ eventually, for positive constants $c_1,c_2$, the same exponent follows, using $|\lambda_1|$ in the coarse constant.

The remaining source gate is concrete: specify the linear or quadratic convention for $W$, the test correspondence and normalization, and the passage from the finite period/determinant construction to this zero form. The source scalar is exactly \path{Formalism/StatementOC.lean}, lines 11--13. The actual final path in \fid{DET-09} bounds a character sum with positive weights \emph{from above on power windows}; the present lemma proves a zeta-only criterion for a global eventual bound. The source also has lower bounds on differences $G_\zeta-G_\chi$, which are a different assertion. Character averaging and window quantifiers remain additional parts of any correspondence with that path. The positive product here uses the stipulated RH; the converse lemma does not prove its own positivity premise. \fid{DET-16} below constructs the operator directly from this arithmetic scalar kernel under RH, including its full multiplicities. \subsection{DET-16: the arithmetic cosine kernel and full-multiplicity reconstruction}
The scalar prime-sum endpoint supplies a precise positive-kernel route under
RH. Its cyclic Hilbert space alone loses repeated-zero multiplicities.
The atom masses retain those integers, however, and determine a
full-multiplicity positive realization of \(B\). This uses the standard
cyclic construction for a positive-definite kernel; the specific atomic
model and every normalization needed here are proved below. No novelty
claim for that construction is intended.

\paragraph{The arithmetic kernel and its exact positivity criterion.}
Use the actual \(G_\zeta\) and explicit correction \(b\) of
\fid{STEP6-02}, and define, without a zero list,
\[
 k(t)=e^{-|t|/2}\bigl(G_\zeta(e^{|t|})-b(e^{|t|})\bigr),
 \qquad t\in\R,\qquad K(u,v)=k(v-u).
\]
This is a continuous real even function, with
\[
 k(0)=2+\gamma_E-\log(4\pi)=2\lambda_1.
\]
The exact completed-zero expansion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arithkernelzero}
 k(t)=\sum_\rho
 \frac{N_\rho e^{(\rho-1/2)t}}{\rho(1-\rho)}
 =2\Tr\bigl(C\cos(tA)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here the first sum is over distinct nontrivial zeros and \(N_\rho\)
is their multiplicity; the second uses the one-per-reflection-pair
operators of \fid{STEP6-01}. Before RH, the associated \(\gamma\)'s
may be complex. The series converges absolutely locally uniformly.

\begin{proposition}
The following conditions are equivalent:
\[
 \mathrm{RH};\qquad k\text{ is bounded on }\R;\qquad
 K\text{ is positive definite}.
\]
Positive definite means
\(\sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_jk(t_j-t_i)\ge0\)
for every finite list of real \(t_i\) and complex \(c_i\).
Under these conditions there is a finite positive even measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arithkernelmeasure}
 \mu_K=\sum_{\gamma>0}
 \frac{m_\gamma}{\gamma^2+1/4}
       (\delta_\gamma+\delta_{-\gamma}),\qquad
 k(t)=\int_\R e^{ixt}\,d\mu_K(x),
\end{equation}
where this displayed identification uses the distinct positive
Xi-zero ordinates and their multiplicities. Its total mass is
\(k(0)\), and it has no atom at zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The arithmetic identity and all endpoint conventions are those proved in
\fid{STEP6-02} from the corrected explicit formula
\cite[corrected Lemma 2.1, pp.~1--2]{ramare}.
For \(t\ge0\), divide that formula by \(e^{t/2}\).
The functional equation pairs \(\rho\) with \(1-\rho\), so the
zero sum is even in \(t\); conjugation makes it real.
On a bounded \(t\)-interval each exponential is bounded by
\(e^{|t|/2}\), and
\(\sum_\rho N_\rho/|\rho(1-\rho)|<\infty\).
This proves \eqref{eq:arithkernelzero}, its convergence and continuity.

Under RH, \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\) and
\(\rho(1-\rho)=\gamma^2+1/4\).
Pairing the positive and negative ordinates gives
\eqref{eq:arithkernelmeasure}. The same summability proves finite
mass and uniform absolute convergence on all real \(t\).
For a finite list,
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_jk(t_j-t_i)
 =\int_\R\left|\sum_jc_je^{ixt_j}\right|^2d\mu_K(x)\ge0.
\]
Also \(|k(t)|\le\mu_K(\R)=k(0)\).
The classical \(\Xi(0)\ne0\) excludes the zero atom.

Conversely, positivity of the two-point Gram matrices at \(0,t\)
gives \(|k(t)|\le k(0)\), so positive type implies boundedness.
If merely \(|k(t)|\le M\), then, for every \(X\ge1\),
\[
 |G_\zeta(X)-b(X)|\le M\sqrt X.
\]
Since \(b\) is bounded, this supplies a global eventual lower bound
\(G_\zeta(X)\ge-K\sqrt X\) with finite \(K\).
The proved Landau lemma in \fid{STEP6-03}, with \(\theta=1/2\),
excludes zeros with real part greater than \(1/2\).
The functional equation excludes those with real part less than \(1/2\).
Thus boundedness implies RH. This proves all equivalences.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The exact Weil test space.}
For \(t\in\R\), put
\[
 q_t(x)=e^{-t/2}\mathbf1_{(0,e^t]}(x),\qquad
 \mathcal Mq_t(s)=\frac{e^{(s-1/2)t}}s\quad(\Re s>0).
\]
On the finite complex span of these functions define the
conjugate-linear-first form
\[
 \mathcal W(f,h)=
 \sum_\rho N_\rho\,
 \overline{\mathcal Mf(1-\overline\rho)}\,\mathcal Mh(\rho).
\]
This sum is absolutely convergent, and directly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arithkernelweilgram}
 \mathcal W(q_u,q_v)=k(v-u).
\end{equation}
Indeed the summand is
\(e^{(\rho-1/2)(v-u)}/[\rho(1-\rho)]\).
The reflection-conjugation symmetry of the zeros makes
\(\mathcal W\) Hermitian. On real functions its quadratic value is
the \(W_{\rm std}\) normalization of \fid{STEP6-01}; under RH it is
\(\sum_\rho N_\rho|\mathcal Mf(\rho)|^2\).
Thus positivity of this specific dilation-generated Weil form is
equivalent to RH by the preceding proposition.

For \(X=e^t\ge1\) and \(a=X^{\eta-1/2}\), the source test in
\fid{STEP6-01} is \(q_0\pm a q_{-t}\). Its quadratic value is exactly

 k(0)(1+a^2)\pm2a\,k(t)
 =2\lambda_1(1+X^{2\eta-1})
 \pm2X^{\eta-1}(G_\zeta(X)-b(X)).
\]
This checks the sign, both factors of two, and the half-center.
It specifies a candidate Weil form; it does not identify the

\paragraph{Recovering the atoms from the arithmetic function.}
Assume RH, or equivalently the positive-type condition just proved.
For every real \(\lambda\), define its coefficient by the actual limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arithkernelatom}
 a_K(\lambda)=
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{2T}
       \int_{-T}^{T}k(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\,dt.
\end{equation}
The limit exists and equals \(\mu_K(\{\lambda\})\). To prove this,
insert the finite measure in \eqref{eq:arithkernelmeasure} and use
Fubini on the finite time interval. The inner average is
\(\sin(T(x-\lambda))/(T(x-\lambda))\), with value one when
\(x=\lambda\). Its modulus is at most one, and it tends to zero
at every other \(x\). Dominated convergence proves the assertion.
The same argument applies to any finite positive measure with Fourier
transform \(k\). Such a measure must have these same atoms; their
masses already sum to \(k(0)\), its entire mass, so it has no
remaining part. Thus the positive measure is uniquely determined.

Write
\[
 \Gamma_K=\{\lambda>0:a_K(\lambda)>0\},\qquad
 m_K(\lambda)=(\lambda^2+1/4)a_K(\lambda)
 \quad(\lambda\in\Gamma_K).
\]
The exact explicit formula proves that \(m_K(\lambda)\) is a
positive integer and is the full zero multiplicity.
Integrality is a consequence of this arithmetic identity, not a
property of an arbitrary positive-definite kernel.
Both the frequencies and these integers have now been specified
from \(G_\zeta\) and \(b\), rather than by an input enumeration of zeros.
For \(\lambda>0\), the same coefficient has the entirely arithmetic form
\[
 a_K(\lambda)=
 \lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac1{\log Y}
 \int_1^Y
 \frac{G_\zeta(X)-b(X)}{X^{3/2}}
       \cos(\lambda\log X)\,dX.
\]
The change of variables is \(X=e^t\), using evenness of \(k\).
The recovered set is discrete, has no finite accumulation point,
and stays a positive distance from zero, by its identification with
the nonzero Xi ordinates.

\paragraph{The cyclic construction and its multiplicity limit.}
Complete the finite span of symbols \(e_t\), with
\(\langle e_u,e_v\rangle=k(v-u)\), after dividing by its null space.
Call the resulting Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_{\rm cyc}\).
Translations \(U_se_t=e_{t+s}\) are unitary, and \(e_0\) is cyclic.
This is the usual cyclic construction of a positive-definite function.
Here it has the explicit unitary model
\[
 \mathcal H_{\rm cyc}\simeq L^2(\mu_K),\qquad
 e_t(x)=e^{ixt},\quad U_sh(x)=e^{ixs}h(x),\quad e_0=1.
\]
\begin{proof}
The displayed map is isometric by the Fourier formula.
Its range is dense: for any atom \(\lambda\), the strong vector average
\[
 \frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{-i\lambda t}e_t\,dt
\]
lies in the closed span of the \(e_t\)'s and converges in
\(L^2(\mu_K)\) to \(\mathbf1_{\{\lambda\}}\).
This is the same sinc calculation, now with its square bounded
by one and dominated convergence for the finite measure.
Finite linear combinations of the atomic indicators are dense.
The vector integrals exist because \(t\mapsto e_t\) is norm
continuous, as follows by dominated convergence.
The same argument gives strong continuity of \(U_s\).
\end{proof}
Its selfadjoint generator, with \(U_s=e^{isA_{\rm cyc}}\), is
\[
 A_{\rm cyc}h(x)=xh(x),\qquad
 D(A_{\rm cyc})=\{h:\int x^2|h(x)|^2d\mu_K(x)<\infty\}.
\]
Selfadjointness follows directly by testing the adjoint on the
orthonormal atomic basis
\(\mathbf1_{\{\lambda\}}/\sqrt{a_K(\lambda)}\).
No differentiability of the cyclic vector is assumed.

There is exactly one dimension at each distinct signed atom,
regardless of its mass. On the positive-frequency subspace let
\(B_{\rm red}=A_{\rm cyc}^{-2}\). It is positive and trace class, but
\[
 \det(I-wB_{\rm red})
 =\prod_{\lambda\in\Gamma_K}(1-w/\lambda^2).
\]
This equals \(G(w)\) exactly when all the zeros are simple.
The reflection-even half of the full cyclic space gives the same
reduced determinant; the full signed cyclic space gives its square.
Thus the cyclic construction alone does not restore multiplicity.

In particular, atom mass is not an ordinary trace multiplicity.
On the full cyclic space, the diagonal operator
\(D_Kh(\lambda)=a_K(\lambda)h(\lambda)\) is trace class and
\[
 k(t)=\langle e_0,U_te_0\rangle
     =\Tr(D_KU_t).
\]
By contrast, the ordinary trace of a trace-class \(f(A_{\rm cyc})\)
is \(\sum_{\lambda}f(\lambda)\), with one term per distinct atom,
not the integral of \(f\) against \(\mu_K\).

\paragraph{The ordinary determinant with all multiplicities.}
The recovered integers give the explicit Hilbert space and operators
\[
 \mathcal H_K=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Gamma_K}\C^{m_K(\lambda)},
 \qquad A_K|_\lambda=\lambda I,\quad
 C_K|_\lambda=(\lambda^2+1/4)^{-1}I,\quad
 B_K|_\lambda=\lambda^{-2}I.
\]
Take the maximal diagonal domain for \(A_K\). The spectrum with its
fiber dimensions is determined by the arithmetic kernel; its Hilbert
realization is canonical up to unitary choices inside these fibers.
This is a multiplicity reconstruction from the masses, not the cyclic
GNS space itself.

\begin{proposition}
Under RH, \(B_K\) and \(C_K\) are positive trace-class operators and
\[
 \Tr C_K=\frac{k(0)}2=\lambda_1,\qquad
 k(t)=2\Tr(C_K\cos(tA_K)),\qquad
 B_K=C_K(I-C_K/4)^{-1}.

Their ordinary determinants satisfy
\[
 \det(I-wB_K)=\frac{\Xi(\sqrt w)}{\Xi(0)}=G(w),
 \qquad

\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\lambda_0=\inf\Gamma_K>0\). The ordinary eigenvalue sums are
\[
 \sum_{\lambda>0}\frac{m_K(\lambda)}{\lambda^2+1/4}
 =\sum_{\lambda>0}a_K(\lambda)=\frac{k(0)}2,
\]
\[
 \sum_{\lambda>0}\frac{m_K(\lambda)}{\lambda^2}
 =\sum_{\lambda>0}a_K(\lambda)
       \left(1+\frac1{4\lambda^2}\right)
 \le\left(1+\frac1{4\lambda_0^2}\right)\frac{k(0)}2.
\]
For these positive block-diagonal operators these are exactly their
trace norms, proving trace class. Absolute trace convergence gives
the cosine identity. Also
\(\|C_K\|\le(\lambda_0^2+1/4)^{-1}<4\), so the indicated inverse

in the definition of \(B_K\).
For \(v\in\C\), the scalar spectral transform gives

 \det(I-vC_K)=\frac{G(v-1/4)}{G(-1/4)}.
\]
Setting \(v=s(1-s)\), evenness of \(\Xi\) and
\(\xi(0)=1/2\) give \(2\xi(s)\), exactly as in \fid{DET-11}.
Thus the normalization of its resolvent trace is unchanged.
\end{proof}

One may also retain both signs, putting a copy of

vectors or trace-class \(T\). The same conclusion holds for
\(2\Tr(T(V_t+V_{-t})/2)\).
\item Under RH, every bounded intertwiner from the cyclic
arithmetic translation model of \fid{DET-16}, or its restored
full-multiplicity model, into \((H_\sigma,V_t)\) is zero.
\end{enumerate}
All these conclusions transfer to the isometric Bessel submodel
of \(H_\sigma\). Only that submodel's right-translation action
is asserted here.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed definition of \(H_{k,r}\) in
\path{Formalism/FormalDegreeBessel/StatementG.lean}, lines 20--24,
makes the indicated two-plane orthogonal to the other coordinates
and gives it matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\).
The identity \(\cosh^2t-\sinh^2t=1\) therefore gives
\(g_t^*H_{k,r}g_t=H_{k,r}\). Thus \(g_t\in G\), and the
hyperbolic addition formulas give \(g_tg_s=g_{t+s}\).
This is a continuous group map. The source representation
conditions make \(V_t\) a strongly continuous unitary group.
On the Euclidean orthonormal vectors
\((e_r+e_{r+k})/\sqrt2\) and
\((e_r-e_{r+k})/\sqrt2\), the matrix \(g_t\) has eigenvalues
\(e^t,e^{-t}\); it is the identity on their orthogonal
complement. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{opn}(g_t)=e^{|t|}.
\]
For \(u,v\in D\), \eqref{eq:boost-source-decay} now bounds the
coefficient by \(C_{u,v}e^{-a|t|}\), proving its vanishing.

The space \(D\) is dense. Its closure is a nonzero closed
invariant subspace, because \(w_0\in D\), \(\|w_0\|=1\),
and every \(\sigma(g)\) is bounded and preserves the orbit
span. Source irreducibility makes the closure \(H_\sigma\).
For arbitrary \(u,v\in H_\sigma\), choose \(u_0,v_0\in D\).
Unitarity gives the uniform estimate
\[
 |\langle v,V_tu\rangle-\langle v_0,V_tu_0\rangle|
 \le\|v-v_0\|\|u\|+\|v_0\|\|u-u_0\|.
\]
Make its right side arbitrarily small, then let \(|t|\) tend
to infinity in the coefficient on \(D\). This proves vanishing
for all vectors, without claiming their exponential rates.

If \(R\) is finite rank, write
\(Rx=\sum_{j=1}^q\langle v_j,x\rangle u_j\).
The conjugate-linear-first convention gives
\[
 \Tr(RV_t)=\sum_{j=1}^q\langle v_j,V_tu_j\rangle,
\]
so this continuous function vanishes at infinity.
For \(T\in\mathcal S_1\), choose finite-rank \(R\) with
\(\|T-R\|_1<\epsilon\). Uniformly in \(t\),
\[
 |\Tr((T-R)V_t)|\le\|T-R\|_1\|V_t\|<\epsilon.
\]
The uniform limit is continuous and vanishes at infinity.
This proves the first assertion.

A nonzero eigenvector would give
\(|\langle u,V_tu\rangle|=\|u\|^2>0\), contradicting the
coefficient limit. More generally, if a nonzero finite-dimensional
space \(E\) were invariant under every \(V_t\), invariance under
\(V_{-t}\) would make \(V_t|_E\) unitary. For an orthonormal
basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\) of \(E\),
\[
 \sum_{i,j=1}^m|\langle e_i,V_te_j\rangle|^2=m.
\]
The left side tends to zero by the first assertion, a
contradiction. If a nonzero compact \(Q\) commuted with all
\(V_t\), its adjoint would also commute, since \(V_t^*=V_{-t}\).
Then \(Q^*Q\) would be nonzero positive compact and commute
with the group. Its largest positive eigenvalue has a nonzero
finite-dimensional eigenspace, invariant under every \(V_t\).
This is impossible, proving the compact-commutant assertion.

For the arithmetic assertion, a bounded continuous function
\(h(t)\) tending to zero at infinity has zero Cesaro coefficient
at every real frequency: for any \(L>0\),
\[
 \left|\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}h(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\,dt\right|
 \le\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-L}^{L}|h(t)|\,dt
      +\sup_{|t|>L}|h(t)|\qquad(T>L).
\]
First let \(T\to\infty\), then \(L\to\infty\).
Suppose \(k\) tended to zero. It would be bounded, so
\fid{DET-16}, using the complete global Landau proof of
\fid{STEP6-03}, would imply RH. Under RH, the exact explicit
formula and Cesaro calculation in \fid{DET-16} give, at every
positive Xi ordinate \(\lambda\),
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1{2T}
       \int_{-T}^{T}k(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\,dt
 =\frac{m_\lambda}{\lambda^2+1/4}>0.
\]
There are such zeros by the classical zero count in \fid{B-01}.
This contradicts the preceding estimate. Thus \(k\) does not
tend to zero, without an RH assumption in this conclusion.
All the proposed source trace coefficients, including their
displayed cosine combination, do tend to zero and therefore
cannot equal \(k\).

Under RH, the atomic model in \fid{DET-16} has a complete
orthonormal basis \(e_\lambda\) satisfying
\(U_te_\lambda=e^{i\lambda t}e_\lambda\); in the restored
model there are exactly \(m_K(\lambda)\) such basis vectors
at each retained frequency. If a bounded map \(J\) satisfies
\(JU_t=V_tJ\), every \(Je_\lambda\) is a boost eigenvector,
hence zero. Density of the basis and boundedness give \(J=0\).
This excludes, in particular, an injective bounded intertwiner
or a unitary identification of these flows.

Finally, let
\(\mathcal J:H_\sigma\longrightarrow\mathcal B_\sigma
\subset\mathcal B\)
be the isometry from \fid{DET-15}, defined on \(D\) by
\(\mathcal Ju=\eta_u=\sqrt d\,[x\mapsto l(\sigma(x)u)]_\nu\).
Its image \(\mathcal B_\sigma\) is closed. For \(u\in D\)
and \(g,x\in G\), the source representation law gives
\[
 \eta_u(xg)=\sqrt d\,l(\sigma(xg)u)
          =\sqrt d\,l(\sigma(x)\sigma(g)u)
          =\eta_{\sigma(g)u}(x).
\]
This identity concerns the canonical coefficient representatives
on \(G\), which supports \(\nu\). The source orbit span is
invariant, so its right side is again a coefficient of a vector
in \(D\). It defines an isometric right-translation action on
these classes and extends by the isometry to
\(\mathcal J\sigma(g)\mathcal J^{-1}\) on
\(\mathcal B_\sigma\).
Consequently the boost action there is unitarily \(V_t\), and
all the preceding conclusions transfer. We use no assertion
that right translation is unitary or even defined on arbitrary
classes of the ambient \(L^2(\nu;W_p)\).
\end{proof}

In particular, the nonzero positive trace-class operator obtained
in \fid{DET-16} on this Bessel image cannot commute with the
natural boost. Its determinant remains \(G\), and its finite
source convolution approximants remain valid. The obstruction
concerns a proposed identification of flows, not those operator
and determinant constructions. It leaves open a separately
specified historical matrix correspondence and other choices of
flow; it is not an impossibility theorem for arithmetic
realizations of \(B\).

\section{TOY-01--02: the estimate, its domain and the invalid sign inference}
\begin{theorem}
Let $q$ be a prime power, $t:\F_q\to[0,\infty)$, and $k\ge2$. In counting measure put
\[
 Q_k(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_ix_{i+1},\quad
 N_k=\sum_{Q_k(x)=0}\prod_{i=1}^kt(x_i),\quad M=\sum_x t(x).
\]
Then $qN_k=M^k+R_k$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:toybound}
 |R_k|\le(q-1)q^{(k-1)/2}\|t\|_2^2\|t\|_\infty^{k-2}.
\end{equation}
The constant is attained for $t\equiv1$ and odd $k\ge3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nontrivial additive character $\psi$. Orthogonality gives
$q1_{Q=0}=\sum_{h\in\F_q}\psi(hQ)$. The zero mode contributes $M^k$. For $h\ne0$ the other mode is
\[
 S_k(h)=\langle t,K_hD_tK_h\cdots D_tK_ht\rangle,
\]
with $k-1$ kernels and $k-2$ multipliers. Since
$(K_hK_h^*)_{x,x'}=\sum_y\psi(h(x-x')y)=q\delta_{x,x'}$,
$\|K_h\|=\sqrt q$, and $\|D_t\|=\|t\|_\infty$. Cauchy--Schwarz and the operator-norm inequality give the summand bound in \eqref{eq:toybound}; summing over $q-1$ modes proves it. At $k=2$ the multiplier product is empty. The zero weight is handled directly.

For $t\equiv1$, $K_h^2=qJ$ with $J1=1$. If $k=2m+1$, then $S_k=1^TK_h^{2m}1=q^{m+1}$, independent of $h\ne0$. This is equality in the bound, including its final triangle inequality.
\end{proof}
The original Fable statement specifies $q$ prime, so it is correct with the added $k\ge2$ convention. The prompt's shorter phrase ``on $\Z/q$'' needs the following distinction.

\begin{proposition}
For arbitrary integer $q\ge2$ on the cyclic ring $\Z/q$, a valid replacement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composite}
 |R_k|\le q^{(k-1)/2}\|t\|_2^2\|t\|_\infty^{k-2}
 \sum_{h=1}^{q-1}\gcd(h,q)^{(k-1)/2}.
\end{equation}
The prime-form bound fails at $q=4$, $t\equiv1$, $k=3$. The displayed bound also fails at $k=1$, even for prime $q$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $d_h=\gcd(h,q)$. The entries of $K_hK_h^*$ equal $q$ when $x-x'$ is divisible by $q/d_h$ and zero otherwise. After a permutation this is a sum of $d_h\times d_h$ all-ones blocks multiplied by $q$. Hence $\|K_h\|=\sqrt{qd_h}$, and the preceding proof gives \eqref{eq:composite}.

For $q=4$, $Q_3=x_2(x_1+x_3)$. The values $x_2=0,1,2,3$ allow respectively $16,4,8,4$ choices, so $N_3=32$, $R_3=4\cdot32-4^3=64$. The claimed prime-form right side is $3\cdot4\cdot4=48$. The corrected bound is $16(1+2+1)=64$. At $k=1$, $N_1=M$, $R_1=(q-1)M$. For $q=2$, $t=(2,1)$ this is $3$, but the claimed expression is $5/2$.
\end{proof}
The exact computation script checks 144 finite prime-field cases and the two counterexamples. It corroborates these proofs and is not a proof of their universal statements.

\paragraph{TOY-02 (scalar signs do not imply spectral positivity).}
Even the stronger condition $\Tr T^n>0$ for every $n\ge1$ fails to imply $T\ge0$: take $T=\diag(1,-1/2)$. Then $\Tr T^n=1+(-1/2)^n>0$, but $\det(1-wT)=(1-w)(1+w/2)$ has a negative zero. Thus the positive-axis singularity statement for a power series with nonnegative coefficients (often called Pringsheim's theorem in this setting) does not supply Fable's proposed conclusion. In a cohomological no-cancellation argument, the degrees, weight bounds and factorization are substantive extra hypotheses.

\paragraph{Geometric location.}
For $t\equiv1$, $N_k$ counts points of the affine $\F_q$-scheme $Q_k=0$. If $t$ is realized as the trace function of a constructible $\ell$-adic sheaf $\mathcal T$, the twisted sums have the usual interpretation using $\mathcal T^{\boxtimes k}$ and the Artin--Schreier character sheaf pulled back by $hQ_k$. The trace formalism is SGA 5, III, Corollaries 4.7--4.8; its curve specialization is III B, Corollary 1.3.1 \cite{sga5}. An arbitrary real nonnegative function $t$ is not automatically such an arithmetic sheaf. The proof above is finite Fourier analysis and does not construct a motive over $\Spec\Z$, a crystal, or divided powers. Exterior powers in the determinant formula must not be confused with divided-power multiplicity in the other prompts.

\paragraph{MAS-06 (a concrete obstruction to the uncut convolution trace).}
The distinction already occurs on the radial part of the full weighted space. Put
$w(u)=e^{2\alpha|u|}(1+u^2)^{s/2}$ and choose a nonzero $h\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with $h(0)=0$. Convolution by $h$ on $L^2(\R,w(u)du)$ is not compact, hence is not trace class.
\begin{proof}
The weight ratios $w(u+v)/w(u)$ are uniformly bounded for $v$ in a fixed compact set, so the compactly supported convolution is bounded by the weighted version of Young's inequality (conjugate by $f\mapsto\sqrt w f$ and dominate by a compactly supported convolution kernel).
Choose $f(u)=\overline{h(-u)}$. Then $k=h*f$ is compactly supported and continuous, with $k(0)=\int|h|^2>0$. For $R\to+\infty$, put $f_R(u)=f(u-R)/\|f(\cdot-R)\|_{L^2(w)}$. Uniformly for $v$ in any fixed compact set,
\[
 \frac{w(R+v)}{w(R)}\longrightarrow e^{2\alpha v}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \|h*f_R\|_{L^2(w)}^2\longrightarrow
 \frac{\int|k(v)|^2e^{2\alpha v}dv}{\int|f(v)|^2e^{2\alpha v}dv}>0.
\]
Take $R_j\to\infty$ far enough apart that the translates of $\operatorname{supp}k$ are disjoint. The images $h*f_{R_j}$ are pairwise orthogonal and have norms bounded below, so they have no convergent subsequence, while each $f_{R_j}$ has norm one. This disproves compactness.
\end{proof}
Already for $F=\Q$, one has $C_\Q\simeq\R_{>0}\times\widehat\Z^\times$, with norm the first coordinate. Indeed a unique rational factor clears the finitely many nonzero finite valuations and makes the real coordinate positive; a rational number preserving all finite units and that sign must be one. This normalization and its inverse are continuous on the open unit charts, proving the topological decomposition. The compact factor can be given Haar mass one, so functions constant along it give exactly the closed radial subspace above, with $u=\log|g|$. The same decomposition is recorded in the draft, \S A.2.2.1, (A.2.2.3), p.~546; the Haar product convention is \S A.3.3.1, p.~561. The test $\phi(g)=h(\log|g|_\Q)$ is compactly supported on $C_\Q$ and satisfies $\phi|_{C_\Q^1}=0$. Thus even an admissible test can give a nonnuclear convolution on the full weighted space. This does not contradict nuclearity on the theta quotient: the quotient is a different operator realization. It also does not by itself identify the precise uncut geometric projection with this radial restriction; that identification is not needed for the obstruction to replacing cutoff traces by a full convolution trace.

\subsection{MAS-03--05: what positivity means here}
For centered multiplicative convolution write $\psi^*(g)=\overline{\psi(g^{-1})}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squareone}
 (\psi*\psi^*)(1)=\int_{C_F}|\psi(g)|^2d\mu(g).
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Insert $g=1$ in the convolution integral. Since $C_F$ is abelian, Haar measure is invariant under the inversion used in the involution. The integrand is exactly $|\psi|^2$. Compact support makes the integral finite; if it is zero, continuity makes $\psi$ identically zero.
\end{proof}
Since $1\in C_F^1$, the class admitted in \eqref{eq:masmain} contains no nonzero convolution square. This proves \fid{MAS-03}: the theorem cannot be directly evaluated on the Weil positivity cone. The draft itself makes this distinction in \S1.4.6, p.~30, saying that positivity is not used and returns only after the proposed GRH deduction.

Under GRH, $\eta_c=c|\cdot|^{-1/2}$ is unitary. In a fixed smooth character component the usual convergent Weil zero functional therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weilsquares}
 W(\psi*\psi^*)=\sum_cN(c)|\M\psi(\eta_c)|^2\ge0.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Fubini gives multiplicativity of the Mellin transform on convolution. Inversion gives
$\M(\psi^*)(\eta)=\overline{\M\psi(\overline\eta^{-1})}$, which is $\overline{\M\psi(\eta)}$ for unitary $\eta$. The Mellin transform of a smooth compactly supported function decays faster than every power in vertical strips, and the standard fixed-$L$-function zero count is $O(T\log T)$, so the zero sum converges absolutely. Summing the nonnegative terms proves the formula.
\end{proof}
This is \fid{MAS-04}. For $\zeta$ and Dirichlet $L$-functions it is an Alp\"oge consequence. For all Hecke $L$-functions of all number fields it is conditional on the supplied Masullo claim. It is scalar quadratic positivity. Masullo's weighted translation action is explicitly nonunitary (Remark 10.4.1.4, p.~457); thus $A(\psi^*)=A(\psi)^*$ is not automatic. The counterexample \fid{DET-04}, or already $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, proves that nonnegative spectral values and power traces do not establish positive selfadjointness in that quotient metric (\fid{MAS-05}).

\subsection{MAS-BOUND: the boundary Sobolev cap needs a strict inequality}
The norm \eqref{eq:masnorm} permits an independent endpoint check. In the coordinate $u=\log|g|_F$, a Mellin-derivative functional with kernel $\chi(g)|g|_F^{b+it}u^m$ has squared dual norm, up to the finite measure of $C_F^1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundaryintegral}
 \int_\R e^{2bu-2\alpha|u|}|u|^{2m}(1+u^2)^{-s/2}\,du.
\end{equation}
It is finite for all integers $m\ge0$ if $|b|<\alpha$, and, at $b=\pm\alpha$, is finite exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strictcap}
 2m<s-1.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
The dual of weighted $L^2(w\,du)$ is represented by kernels in $L^2(w^{-1}\,du)$. This gives \eqref{eq:boundaryintegral}. On each tail a negative exponential dominates all polynomial factors. At a boundary one tail has zero exponential exponent; its integrand is comparable to $|u|^{2m-s}$ and is integrable exactly when $2m-s<-1$. Near zero there is no singularity for $m\ge0$. For $|b|>\alpha$ one tail grows exponentially and diverges. This proves necessity as well as sufficiency.
\end{proof}
Consequently the number of permitted derivative orders, capped by a zero's multiplicity $N$, is
\[
 \min\{N,\max(0,\lceil(s-1)/2\rceil)\}.
\]
The printed (10.3.2.4) uses $\min\{N,\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor+1\}$. At odd integral $s$ it includes the divergent endpoint; for example $s=3,m=1$ gives a logarithmic divergence. The difference is activated only if $N$ reaches the extra claimed order; this calculation does not assert that a particular zero does so. Theorem 10.3.2.1 allows real $s>1$ without excluding odd integers. For the present comparison one can avoid the issue by choosing strip edges away from the relevant zeros, or nonexceptional $s$. This records an actual correction forced by the displayed norm while leaving the main trace-class comparison explicitly conditional.

\subsection{MAS-10: an ordinary Xi determinant on the actual weighted quotient}
The trace-ideal question for the reciprocal-square multiplier has an affirmative answer. The extra ingredient is a sector estimate in the actual weighted Hilbert norm. It avoids any assumption that the zero modes form a Riesz basis or have uniformly bounded biorthogonal vectors.

Specialize to $F=\mathbf Q$, the trivial character of $C_{\mathbf Q}^1$, center $a=1/2$, width $\alpha=1/2$, and $s>1$. Normalize the compact Haar factor to mass one and write $u=\log|g|$. Thus
\[
 H=L^2(\R,w(u)\,du),\qquad
 w(u)=e^{|u|}(1+u^2)^{s/2},\qquad E=H/V,
\]
where $V$ is the closed centered theta-image. This is the actual Hilbert quotient of \cite[(10.2.2.1)--(10.2.2.2), p.~445]{masullo}, in the indicated character sector.

\textbf{Exact conditional input.} Use \cite[Theorem 10.2.2.1, (10.2.2.4)--(10.2.2.6), p.~446]{masullo} only for its full spectral-annihilator description. Let $\rho$ range over distinct nontrivial zeta zeros, with multiplicity $N_\rho$, and put $z_\rho=\rho-1/2$. Classically
\[
 0<\Re\rho<1,\qquad z_\rho\ne0,
\]
by the Euler product, the functional equation, nonvanishing on $\Re s=1$, and $\xi(1/2)\ne0$; see \cite[\S2.12; \S3.2, pp.~46--47]{titch}. These facts require no RH. Every zero is therefore interior to the chosen strip, so there is no boundary truncation. The cited draft claim says that
\[
 \ell_{\rho,m}(f)=\int_{\R}f(u)e^{z_\rho u}u^m\,du,
 \qquad 0\le m<N_\rho,
\]
annihilate $V$ and have norm-dense linear span in the Hilbert dual $E'$. This Hilbert-dual density is also explained in \cite[Remark 10.2.1.2, pp.~440--441]{masullo}. No nuclearity assertion from Theorem 10.3.2.1 is used. The conclusions below are conditional on the spectral-annihilator claim alone, together with the stated classical zeta facts; \emph{RH is not an input}.

Put $B_0=1/2+s/4$ and choose an auxiliary real parameter $\mu>B_0$, distinct from the center $a=1/2$. On the ambient space define
\[
 g_\mu(v)=\frac{e^{-\mu|v|}}{2\mu},\qquad
 K_\mu f=\int_{\R}g_\mu(v)f(\,\cdot-v)\,dv.
\]
The integral is a strong, vector-valued integral for each $f$, as justified below. Let $K$ be its induced operator on $E$.

\begin{proposition}
The operator $K$ is trace class and $I-\mu^2K$ has a bounded inverse. The operator
\[
 R=K(I-\mu^2K)^{-1}
\]
is trace class, is independent of $\mu$, and has the full jet multiplier $-1/z^2$ at $z=z_\rho$. Reflection $Jf(u)=f(-u)$ descends to a unitary involution of $E$ and commutes with $R$. For $E_+=\ker(J-I)$ and $R_+=R|_{E_+}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:masactualXi}
 \det_{E_+}(I-wR_+)=\frac{\Xi(\sqrt w)}{\Xi(0)}.
\end{equation}
Each pair $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$ contributes its full multiplicity $N_\rho$, exactly once. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:masGreennorm}
 \|K\|_1\le\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{\mu^2-B_0^2}}
 \sum_\rho N_\rho\Re\frac1{\mu^2-z_\rho^2},
\end{equation}
where the sum includes all distinct nontrivial zeros, and every real part in it is positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Translations and the quotient.}
The function $\log\sqrt w$ is Lipschitz, with almost-everywhere derivative
\[
 b(u)=\tfrac12\operatorname{sgn}(u)+\frac{s u}{2(1+u^2)},
 \qquad |b(u)|\le B_0.
\]
Consequently translations $T_vf(u)=f(u-v)$ satisfy
$\|T_v\|\le e^{B_0|v|}$. They are strongly continuous: first check this on compactly supported smooth functions, and then use their density and the locally uniform bound. The functionals $\ell_{\rho,m}$ are continuous because
$\int_{\R}e^{2\Re(z_\rho)u}|u|^{2m}w(u)^{-1}\,du<\infty$. Substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:masjettranslation}
 \ell_{\rho,m}(T_vf)=e^{z_\rho v}
 \sum_{j=0}^m\binom{m}{j}v^{m-j}\ell_{\rho,j}(f).
\end{equation}
The complete annihilator description therefore implies $T_vV\subset V$: every jet vanishes on $T_vf$ when $f\in V$, and $V$ is the annihilator of its annihilator. Since
$g_\mu\in L^1(\R,e^{B_0|v|}\,dv)$, the strong integral defining $K_\mu$ is bounded and preserves $V$. Under the isometric identification $E=V^\perp$, its quotient operator is the compression
\[
 K=P_{V^\perp}K_\mu|_{V^\perp}.
\]
No operator-norm Bochner integrability of $v\mapsto T_v$ is asserted or required.

\emph{The weak domain and the sector estimate.}
Both $g_\mu$ and $g_\mu'$ belong to the same exponentially weighted $L^1$ space, and
$g_\mu''=\mu^2g_\mu-\delta_0$ in distributions. Hence $y=K_\mu f$ satisfies
\[
 y,y',y''\in H,\qquad y''=\mu^2y-f.
\]
These statements follow first for compactly supported smooth $f$ by convolution, then for arbitrary $f\in H$ by the weighted convolution bounds and convergence in distributions. Let $U:H\longrightarrow L^2(\R)$ be multiplication by $\sqrt w$ and put $h=Uy$. Then
\[
 h\in H^1(\R),\quad D h:=h'-bh=Uy'\in H^1(\R),
 \quad D^2h=Uy''.
\]
Indeed $h'=\sqrt w\,y'+bh$, and the same calculation applies to $y'$. The weak derivative of $b$ is never used, so the cusp of the weight at zero causes no omitted boundary term. Integration by parts in $H^1$ gives
\begin{align*}
 \langle(\mu^2-D^2)h,h\rangle
 &=\|h'\|_2^2+\mu^2\|h\|_2^2-\|bh\|_2^2\notag\\
 &\quad+\langle h',bh\rangle-\langle bh,h'\rangle.
\end{align*}
For example, $D^*=-\partial_u-b$ on $H^1$, and the identity follows from
$-\langle Dh,D^*h\rangle$; this also justifies it on the stated weak domain. With
$\delta=\mu^2-B_0^2>0$ and $c=B_0/\sqrt\delta$, it follows that
\[
 \Re\langle(\mu^2-D^2)h,h\rangle
 \ge\|h'\|_2^2+\delta\|h\|_2^2,
\]
\[
 |\Im\langle(\mu^2-D^2)h,h\rangle|
 \le2B_0\|h'\|_2\|h\|_2
 \le c\bigl(\|h'\|_2^2+\delta\|h\|_2^2\bigr).
\]
The last inequality is $2XY\le X^2+Y^2$. Since
$(\mu^2-d^2/du^2)y=f$, the number $\langle K_\mu f,f\rangle$ is the conjugate of
$\langle(\mu^2-d^2/du^2)y,y\rangle$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:masGreensector}
 \Re\langle K_\mu f,f\rangle\ge0,
 \qquad |\Im\langle K_\mu f,f\rangle|
 \le c\Re\langle K_\mu f,f\rangle.
\end{equation}
Compression preserves this numerical-range inequality because
$\langle PK_\mu x,x\rangle=\langle K_\mu x,x\rangle$ for $x\in V^\perp$.
Therefore both $K$ and $K^*$ satisfy the same symmetric sector bound.

\emph{A trace-ideal lemma.}
If a bounded operator $T$ satisfies the sector bound in \eqref{eq:masGreensector} and an orthonormal basis $(e_n)$ satisfies
$\sum_n\Re\langle Te_n,e_n\rangle<\infty$, then $T$ is trace class. To prove this, set
\[
 A=(T+T^*)/2\ge0,\qquad Q=(T-T^*)/(2i).
\]
The diagonal sum says
$\sum_n\|A^{1/2}e_n\|^2<\infty$, so $A^{1/2}$ is Hilbert--Schmidt and $A$ is trace class. The sector bound says $-cA\le Q\le cA$ in quadratic forms. It yields
\[
 Q=A^{1/2}CA^{1/2}
\]
for a bounded selfadjoint $C$ with $\|C\|\le c$. Here is the factorization argument. Define on $\operatorname{Ran}A^{1/2}$ the form
$\beta(A^{1/2}x,A^{1/2}y)=\langle Qx,y\rangle$.
It is well defined: if $x\in\ker A$, the positive operators $cA\pm Q$ have zero quadratic value at $x$ and therefore annihilate $x$, implying $Qx=0$. Cauchy--Schwarz for these two positive forms makes $\beta$ bounded. Its diagonal bound
$|\beta(u,u)|\le c\|u\|^2$ extends to the closure and represents a bounded selfadjoint operator $C$ of norm at most $c$. Extend it by zero on the orthogonal complement. Consequently
\[
 T=A^{1/2}(I+iC)A^{1/2},\qquad
 \|T\|_1\le\|I+iC\|\|A^{1/2}\|_2^2
 \le\sqrt{1+c^2}\,\Tr A.
\]
The trace-class estimate follows either from the Hilbert--Schmidt product inequality or directly by expanding the product in an orthonormal basis and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the sum of the rank-one norms. This proves the lemma without a prior spectral trace identity.

\emph{Full jets and an orthonormal triangular basis.}
For $|\Re z|<\mu$,
\[
 F_\mu(z):=\int_{\R}g_\mu(v)e^{zv}\,dv
 =\frac1{\mu^2-z^2}.
\]
Integrating \eqref{eq:masjettranslation}, with absolutely convergent exponential-polynomial moments, gives
\[
 K'\ell_{\rho,m}
 =\sum_{j=0}^m\binom{m}{j}
 F_\mu^{(m-j)}(z_\rho)\ell_{\rho,j},
 \qquad \lambda_\rho:=F_\mu(z_\rho)=\frac1{\mu^2-z_\rho^2}.
\]
These eigenvalues need not be real. If $z_\rho=b+it$, set $A_\rho=\mu^2-b^2+t^2>0$. Then
\[
 \Re\lambda_\rho=\frac{A_\rho}{A_\rho^2+4b^2t^2}>0,
 \qquad |\lambda_\rho|\le\frac1{\mu^2-1/4+t^2}.
\]
The classical zero count used in \fid{B-01} consequently gives
$\sum_\rho N_\rho|\lambda_\rho|<\infty$.

Enumerate the distinct zeros and list each finite jet block in increasing jet order. Their Riesz representatives form a total linearly independent sequence in $E$, and every finite prefix spans a $K^*$-invariant space. Applying Gram--Schmidt preserves these finite-prefix spaces and gives an orthonormal basis $(e_n)$ such that, in the paper's conjugate-linear-first convention,
\[
 K^*e_n-\overline{\lambda_n}e_n\in\operatorname{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_{n-1}),
 \qquad \langle e_n,K^*e_n\rangle=\overline{\lambda_n}.
\]
The conjugation is required by the anti-linear Riesz identification. Each $\lambda_\rho$ occurs $N_\rho$ times. No bounds on the original basis vectors or their biorthogonal partners enter this argument. Hence
\[
 \sum_n\Re\langle K^*e_n,e_n\rangle
 =\sum_\rho N_\rho\Re\lambda_\rho<\infty.
\]
The trace-ideal lemma and the sector bound prove $K\in\Sone(E)$ and
\eqref{eq:masGreennorm}. This establishes trace class before using compactness or a trace-equals-eigenvalue theorem; summable eigenvalues of a general nonnormal operator have not been substituted for singular-value control.

\emph{Exact spectrum, inverse and the reciprocal-square multiplier.}
Since $K^*$ is now compact, if $P_n$ projects onto the first $n$ basis vectors, then
$\|(I-P_n)K^*\|\to0$. To see this, approximate $K^*$ in norm by a finite-rank operator and use uniform convergence of $P_n$ on its finite-dimensional range. The invariant flags give a block matrix
\[
 K^*=\begin{pmatrix}T_n&B_n\\0&D_n\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \|D_n\|\longrightarrow0,
\]
with $T_n$ finite triangular of diagonal $\overline{\lambda_1},\ldots,\overline{\lambda_n}$.
If $q\ne0$ differs from all these diagonal values, choose $n$ with $\|D_n\|<|q|$. Both $T_n-qI$ and $D_n-qI$ are invertible, the latter by a Neumann series, and the explicit upper-triangular block inverse makes $K^*-qI$ invertible. Conversely each diagonal value is an eigenvalue in a finite invariant prefix. For a fixed nonzero eigenvalue, take $n$ past all its occurrences with $\|D_n\|$ less than its modulus. Every generalized eigenvector then lies in that prefix, since the tail block minus the eigenvalue is invertible. Its algebraic multiplicity is exactly the finite triangular multiplicity. There are only finitely many occurrences: equality $\lambda_\rho=\lambda_{\rho'}$ is equivalent to $z_\rho^2=z_{\rho'}^2$, hence to $\rho'=\rho$ or $1-\rho$. Thus the nonzero spectrum of $K$, with algebraic multiplicities, is precisely the $\lambda_\rho$; no additional nonzero spectral part remains.

Since $z_\rho\ne0$, no $\lambda_\rho$ equals $1/\mu^2$, so $I-\mu^2K$ is boundedly invertible. The ideal property now proves
$R=K(I-\mu^2K)^{-1}\in\Sone(E)$. On every finite jet block, rational composition gives the multiplier
\[
 \frac{F_\mu(z)}{1-\mu^2F_\mu(z)}=-\frac1{z^2}.
\]
The identity holds with all jets at $z=z_\rho$, since the denominator is nonzero there; equivalently it is an identity in the corresponding truncated Taylor algebra. Its diagonal is $-z_\rho^{-2}$ with full multiplicity. The same compact triangular argument gives exactly these nonzero spectral values and their algebraic multiplicities. Two choices of $\mu$ act identically on every dual jet, so their difference is annihilated by a dense subspace of $E'$ and is zero. This proves independence of the auxiliary parameter.

\emph{Parity and the determinant.}
Reflection $Jf(u)=f(-u)$ is a unitary involution on $H$, since $w$ is even, and
\[
 \ell_{\rho,m}(Jf)=(-1)^m\ell_{1-\rho,m}(f).
\]
The functional equation gives $N_{1-\rho}=N_\rho$ and $z_{1-\rho}=-z_\rho$. The complete annihilator description therefore yields $JV=V$, so $J$ descends to a unitary involution of $E$. The even Green kernel commutes with reflection, as do $K$ and $R$. Hence $R_+=R|_{E_+}$ is trace class on the orthogonal even summand.

For each pair $\{\rho,1-\rho\}$, reflection exchanges two independent jet spaces of dimension $N_\rho$ with the indicated signs. Its $+1$ eigenspace has dimension $N_\rho$. Applying $(I+J')/2$ to the dense full jet span proves density in the even dual. Thus $R_+$ has precisely one eigenvalue $-z_\rho^{-2}$ per pair, with algebraic multiplicity $N_\rho$. If $\gamma_\rho=-iz_\rho$, then $\gamma_\rho$ is a possibly complex zero of $\Xi$ and $\gamma_\rho^{-2}=-z_\rho^{-2}$. The ordinary trace-class determinant theorem of cote~1 and \fid{B-01} give
\[
 \det_{E_+}(I-wR_+)
 =\prod_{\{\rho,1-\rho\}}
 \left(1+\frac{w}{z_\rho^2}\right)^{N_\rho}
 =\frac{\Xi(\sqrt w)}{\Xi(0)}.
\]
The product is absolutely locally uniformly convergent. This proves every assertion without RH.
\end{proof}

The construction in \fid{MAS-10} is an ordinary Fredholm determinant on the stated arithmetic theta quotient, conditional on its full spectral-annihilator description. It does not assert that $R_+$ is selfadjoint or positive in that quotient metric, and equality of its determinant with the determinant of $B$ does not supply a unitary intertwiner. If, under RH, the heat construction of \fid{MAS-08} is made on this same quotient, its reciprocal-square operator equals $R$ because its full action on the separating dual jets is the same. No unresolved small-time trace-norm condition remains for the ordinary determinant in \eqref{eq:masactualXi}.

\subsection{MAS-07--09: smooth-test obstruction, heat realization and the actual metric}
Retain the fixed setup of \fid{MAS-10}: center $1/2$, strip half-width $\alpha=1/2$, trivial compact character, compact Haar mass one, and
\[
 H=L^2(\R,w(u)\,du),\qquad
 w(u)=e^{|u|}(1+u^2)^{s/2},\quad s>1,\qquad E=H/V.
\]
The full interior jets are indexed by $z_\rho=\rho-1/2$ and their orders below the zero multiplicity. The source input remains the conditional full spectral-annihilator claim of \cite[Theorem 10.2.2.1, (10.2.2.4)--(10.2.2.6), p.~446]{masullo}. The preceding Green construction has already proved $R_+\in\mathcal S_1(E_+)$ and $\det(I-wR_+)=G(w)$ from that claim, without RH or the draft's separate nuclearity theorem. The heat construction below additionally uses the latter theorem and the stipulated RH. The final no-similarity conclusion will again require only the spectral-annihilator input.

\paragraph{MAS-07: no single compact smooth test has the required multiplier.}
No compact smooth test $\phi$ has centered Mellin values $-1/z_\rho^2$ at every nontrivial zero. More generally, the same is true of $1/(c^2-z_\rho^2)$ for any fixed $c\ge0$, including the multiplier of $C$ when $c=1/2$.
\begin{proof}
Compact-character averaging and the fixed centering send $\phi$ to some $h\in C_c^\infty(\R)$, with centered Mellin transform
$F(z)=\int h(u)e^{zu}\,du$. Compact Haar mass and compact radial support justify differentiation under this average. Three integrations by parts, with vanishing boundary terms, give
\[
 |z|^3|F(z)|\le
 \sup_{|\sigma|\le1/2}\int_\R |h'''(u)|e^{\sigma u}\,du<\infty
 \qquad(z\ne0,\ |\Re z|\le1/2).
\]
Classically $|\Re z_\rho|<1/2$, and there are zeros with $|z_\rho|\to\infty$. But
$|z_\rho|^3/|c^2-z_\rho^2|\sim|z_\rho|\to\infty$, a contradiction. The denominators do not vanish at a nontrivial zero, since such a zero is nonreal. This proof needs no RH. Under RH it is exactly the obstruction to the prescribed values $1/(\gamma^2+c^2)$ at $z_\rho=i\gamma$.
\end{proof}
The assertion concerns the designated zero-by-zero multiplier, not an unordered eigenvalue multiset after an unspecified permutation. A different kernel or subsequent functional calculus is not excluded.

\paragraph{MAS-08: trace-class heat operators and the same inverse square.}
For this paragraph invoke the stipulated RH and write $\rho=1/2+i\gamma$, $\gamma\in\R\setminus\{0\}$, with multiplicity $N_\gamma$. Every zero is strictly interior to the fixed strip, so all jets
$\ell_{\gamma,m}(f)=\int f(u)e^{i\gamma u}u^m\,du$, $0\le m<N_\gamma$, are retained. In addition to their finite independence and norm-dense span in $E'$, assume the draft's continuous map
\[
 \mathcal A:C_c^\infty(\R)\longrightarrow\mathcal S_1(E)
\]
obtained from the centered radial sector of \cite[Theorem 10.3.2.1(2), (10.3.2.2), pp.~453--454]{masullo}. Its continuity is in trace norm, not just operator norm.

Let $T_vf(u)=f(u-v)$. The proof in \fid{MAS-10} gives a strongly continuous quotient group with
$\|T_v\|\le e^{B_0|v|}$, $B_0=1/2+s/4$. For any $h$ satisfying
$\int|h(v)|e^{B_0|v|}\,dv<\infty$, define
\[
 \mathcal A(h)f=\int_\R h(v)T_vf\,dv,\qquad
 \|\mathcal A(h)\|\le\int_\R|h(v)|e^{B_0|v|}\,dv.
\]
This is a Hilbert-space Bochner integral for each fixed vector $f$, hence a strong operator integral. No Bochner measurability of $v\mapsto T_v$ in $\mathcal B(E)$ is asserted.
Put
\[
 k_t(v)=(4\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(4t)},\qquad H_t=\mathcal A(k_t)\quad(t>0).
\]
Then $H_t\in\mathcal S_1(E)$, with trace-norm continuity on compact subintervals of $t>0$, and
\[
 H_tH_r=H_{t+r},\qquad H_t\longrightarrow I\ \hbox{strongly as }t\downarrow0,
 \qquad \sup_{0<t\le1}\|H_t\|<\infty.
\]
\begin{proof}
Choose $\chi\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))$ with
$\sum_{n\in\Z}\chi(v-n)=1$. One construction divides a nonnegative bump positive on $[-1/2,1/2]$ by the positive periodic sum of its translates. Put
$q_{n,t}(v)=\chi(v)k_t(v+n)$. Then
$k_t(v)=\sum_n q_{n,t}(v-n)$, pointwise and in $L^1(e^{B_0|v|}\,dv)$.

Continuity of the linear map $\mathcal A$ on the fixed-support Fr\'echet space with support in $[-1,1]$ supplies $C,M$ such that
\[
 \|\mathcal A(q)\|_1\le C\sum_{j=0}^{M}\|q^{(j)}\|_\infty.
\]
For $t$ in any fixed interval $[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)$, Gaussian differentiation gives
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{M}\|q_{n,t}^{(j)}\|_\infty
 \le C_{a,b}(1+|n|)^M
       \exp\!\left(-\frac{((|n|-1)_+)^2}{4b}\right).
\]
Each derivative is a polynomial times the Gaussian; on $|v|\le1$ this is the displayed bound. Since
$\mathcal A(q(\,\cdot-n))=T_n\mathcal A(q)$, multiplication by $e^{B_0|n|}$ still leaves a summable bound. Thus
$\sum_nT_n\mathcal A(q_{n,t})$ converges in trace norm, uniformly on $[a,b]$. The weighted $L^1$ estimate identifies its sum with the strong integral $H_t$. Each summand is trace-norm continuous in $t$, by the conditional test-map continuity, so their sum is too.

For each fixed $f$, vector-valued Fubini and the group law give
\[
 H_tH_rf=\iint k_t(v)k_r(y)T_{v+y}f\,dy\,dv=H_{t+r}f.
\]
The integrable majorant is
$k_t(v)k_r(y)e^{B_0(|v|+|y|)}\|f\|$; Gaussian convolution gives the last equality. This is vectorwise Fubini, not an operator-valued Bochner assertion about the translation group.
Finally substitute $v=\sqrt t\,y$ in the norm bound. For $0<t\le1$ the integrand is dominated by a fixed Gaussian times $e^{B_0|y|}$. This proves uniform boundedness. Applying the same substitution to
$H_tf-f$ and using strong continuity of $T_v$ proves strong convergence by dominated convergence.
\end{proof}

The complete spectral action, including jets, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasHeatJets}
 H_t'\ell_{\gamma,m}
 =\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj
 F_t^{(m-j)}(i\gamma)\ell_{\gamma,j},
 \qquad F_t(z)=e^{tz^2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the nonzero spectrum of $H_t$ consists precisely of $e^{-t\gamma^2}$, with algebraic multiplicity $2N_\gamma$ for each $\gamma>0$.
\begin{proof}
Apply a jet to the strong integral and expand $(u+v)^m$ in the translation formula. Gaussian decay dominates every polynomial moment and the exponential factor. The resulting moments are derivatives of the Gaussian Mellin transform $F_t$, proving \eqref{eq:MasHeatJets}. These finite jet actions obey the product rule $M_FM_G=M_{FG}$ by Leibniz's identity.

If a nonzero eigenvalue $\lambda$ of the compact $H_t$ differs from all displayed values, evaluation on an eigenvector by jets of order zero and then successively higher orders makes every evaluation zero. Their totality makes that vector zero, a contradiction. Conversely an order-zero eigenfunctional at a displayed value makes $H_t-\lambda I$ noninvertible, so that value is in the spectrum.

For a displayed isolated value $\lambda$, take its finite-rank Riesz projection. Its transpose acts on a finite jet block as identity when the block's diagonal value is $\lambda$ and as zero otherwise: restrict the resolvent to that invariant block and integrate its finite triangular matrix. Exactly the two blocks at $\gamma$ and $-\gamma$ match, with total dimension $2N_\gamma$. The image of the dense jet span is their finite-dimensional span, which is closed; continuity makes the entire projection range equal that span. This gives the full algebraic multiplicity. These are generalized eigenspaces, or Riesz subspaces; no diagonalizability is presumed at a multiple zero.
\end{proof}

The even kernels commute with the unitary involution $Jf(u)=f(-u)$ from \fid{MAS-10}. Hence $H_{t,+}=H_t|_{E_+}$ is trace class. On each paired Riesz subspace the dual even part has the independent basis
\[
 \ell_{\gamma,m}+(-1)^m\ell_{-\gamma,m},
 \qquad 0\le m<N_\gamma.
\]
Indeed $J'\ell_{\gamma,m}=(-1)^m\ell_{-\gamma,m}$ interchanges the two blocks, and applying $(I+J')/2$ to the dense jet span proves totality on the even part. Thus the algebraic multiplicity of $e^{-t\gamma^2}$ on $E_+$ is $N_\gamma$.

The heat construction gives the exact identity with the Green operator:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasHeatInverse}
 Q=\int_0^1H_t\,dt,\qquad
 R=Q(I-H_1)^{-1},\qquad
 R_+=Q|_{E_+}(I-H_{1,+})^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proof}
Here $Q$ really is a $\mathcal B(E)$-valued Bochner integral: $t\mapsto H_t$ is norm continuous for $t>0$, has separable norm range there, and is bounded near zero. Assigning any value at $0$ does not change the integral. Its truncations $\int_\varepsilon^1H_tdt$ are trace class, and the omitted interval has operator norm at most a constant times $\varepsilon$, so $Q$ is compact. Since $\gamma\ne0$, the just-proved spectrum excludes $1$ from $\operatorname{Spec}H_1$; hence $I-H_1$ has a bounded inverse.

On every finite jet block the operator $Q(I-H_1)^{-1}$ has analytic multiplier
\[
 \frac{\int_0^1e^{tz^2}\,dt}{1-e^{z^2}}=-\frac1{z^2}
 \quad\hbox{near }z=i\gamma.
\]
The denominator is nonzero there. Integration and this finite jet algebra identity include every derivative up to the full zero multiplicity. This is exactly the multiplier of the bounded Green operator $R$ in \fid{MAS-10}. Their transposes therefore agree on all jets, whose span is dense in $E'$, so the bounded operators agree. Restricting their commuting even parts proves the last identity.
\end{proof}
The trace-class property and ordinary determinant of this $R_+$ were already established by \fid{MAS-10}; the identity also gives $Q=R(I-H_1)\in\mathcal S_1(E)$. No estimate of $\int_0^1\|H_{t,+}\|_1dt$ is needed for these conclusions. For comparison, the scalar trace integral is finite:
\[
 \int_0^1\Tr H_{t,+}\,dt
 =\sum_{\gamma>0}N_\gamma\frac{1-e^{-\gamma^2}}{\gamma^2}<\infty.
\]
At each positive time the Hilbert trace theorem and the proved multiplicities give the trace; Tonelli and the zero count give the integral. Scalar trace integrability alone would not justify a trace-norm integral for these nonpositive operators.

\paragraph{MAS-09: the actual quotient metric rules out selfadjointness.}
Under stipulated RH define
\[
 K(x)=\int_\R\frac{e^{ixu}}{w(u)}\,du.
\]
This is a continuous real even function, tends to zero as $|x|\to\infty$, and satisfies $K(x)>0$ for every real $x$.
\begin{proof}
The reciprocal weight is integrable, giving continuity and decay by the $L^1$ Fourier theorem. Put $\beta=s/2>0$. The Gamma integral gives
\[
 (1+u^2)^{-\beta}
 =\frac1{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_0^\infty r^{\beta-1}e^{-r}e^{-ru^2}\,dr.
\]
For $r>0$, use the Cauchy Fourier identity
$e^{-|u|}=\pi^{-1}\int_\R e^{iyu}(1+y^2)^{-1}dy$. For $u\ne0$ it follows by a single residue at $i$ or $-i$ in the decaying half-plane; at $u=0$ it is the arctangent integral. The Gaussian Fourier integral now gives
\[
 \int_\R e^{ixu}e^{-|u|}e^{-ru^2}\,du
 =\frac1\pi\sqrt{\frac\pi r}
   \int_\R\frac{e^{-(x+y)^2/(4r)}}{1+y^2}\,dy>0.
\]
Fubini here is justified by the integrable Cauchy density and Gaussian. Integrate this strictly positive expression against the positive Gamma measure. Changing the original $r,u$ order is justified by the absolute integrable majorant
$e^{-|u|}(1+u^2)^{-\beta}$. This proves strict positivity. Reality and evenness also follow directly from the even reciprocal weight.
\end{proof}

Use the paper's conjugate-linear-first inner-product convention, so that the actual Riesz representation is $\ell_{\gamma,0}(f)=\langle v_\gamma,f\rangle$, where
$v_\gamma(u)=e^{-i\gamma u}/w(u)$. Since the functional annihilates $V$, this vector belongs to $V^\perp$ and represents it isometrically on the Hilbert quotient. In $E_+$ the even representative is
$c_\gamma(u)=\cos(\gamma u)/w(u)$, and direct integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasActualCorrelation}
 \langle c_\gamma,c_\delta\rangle
 =\tfrac12\bigl(K(\gamma-\delta)+K(\gamma+\delta)\bigr)>0.
\end{equation}
But
\[
 R_+^*c_\gamma=\gamma^{-2}c_\gamma,\qquad
 H_{t,+}^*c_\gamma=e^{-t\gamma^2}c_\gamma.
\]
Two distinct positive ordinates give distinct real eigenvalues and nonorthogonal eigenvectors. A selfadjoint operator has orthogonal eigenvectors at distinct real eigenvalues, so neither $R_+$ nor any $H_{t,+}$ is selfadjoint or positive in this metric. More generally, this same argument applies to any bounded multiplier whose real values differ at two positive ordinates. This is a calculation in the specified quotient norm, not a replacement model.

\paragraph{MAS-09: no bounded similarity can repair $R_+$.}
Conditional only on the full spectral-annihilator input of \fid{MAS-10}, $R_+$ is not boundedly similar to a selfadjoint operator.
\begin{proof}
First, if RH fails, choose an off-line nontrivial zero. Its centered parameter $z=\rho-1/2$ has nonzero real part and nonzero imaginary part, since zeta has no real nontrivial zero. Therefore $z^2$, and hence $-1/z^2$, is nonreal. By \fid{MAS-10} this is an eigenvalue of $R_+$. Similarity preserves spectrum, whereas a selfadjoint operator has real spectrum, which is already a contradiction.

It remains to consider RH. If some zero has $N_\gamma\ge2$, the independent even jets
$e_0=\ell_{\gamma,0}+\ell_{-\gamma,0}$ and
$e_1=\ell_{\gamma,1}-\ell_{-\gamma,1}$ satisfy, with $F(z)=-1/z^2$,
\[
 R_+'e_0=\gamma^{-2}e_0,\qquad
 R_+'e_1=\gamma^{-2}e_1+F'(i\gamma)e_0,\qquad
 F'(i\gamma)=\frac{2}{(i\gamma)^3}\ne0.
\]
The full interior jets are essential here. Through the Riesz identification this gives a nontrivial generalized eigenvector for $R_+^*$ at a real eigenvalue. A selfadjoint operator $A$ has none: if
$(A-\lambda)y=x$ and $(A-\lambda)x=0$ with $\lambda\in\R$, then
$\|x\|^2=\langle x,(A-\lambda)y\rangle=\langle(A-\lambda)x,y\rangle=0$.
Bounded similarity preserves this obstruction.

If all zeros are simple, the positive zero count $N(T)\sim T\log T/(2\pi)$ forces distinct positive pairs $\gamma_n,\delta_n\to\infty$ with $|\gamma_n-\delta_n|\to0$. Otherwise a positive eventual lower gap would give $N(T)=O(T)$; equivalently, apply the pigeonhole principle to the successive gaps below $T$. Discreteness ensures pairs with gaps tending to zero escape each fixed compact interval.
By \eqref{eq:MasActualCorrelation},
\[
 \|c_\gamma\|^2=\tfrac12(K(0)+K(2\gamma))\longrightarrow K(0)/2,
 \qquad
 \langle c_{\gamma_n},c_{\delta_n}\rangle\longrightarrow K(0)/2.
\]
Thus their normalized vectors $u_n,v_n$ satisfy
$\langle u_n,v_n\rangle\to1$ and $\|u_n-v_n\|\to0$, while remaining eigenvectors of $R_+^*$ for distinct eigenvalues.

If $R_+^*=SAS^{-1}$ with $A$ selfadjoint and $S$ boundedly invertible, then
$S^{-1}u_n,S^{-1}v_n$ are orthogonal, each of norm at least $1/\|S\|$. Their difference has norm at least $\sqrt2/\|S\|$, contradicting its upper bound
$\|S^{-1}\|\|u_n-v_n\|\to0$. Finally, bounded similarity of $R_+$ to a selfadjoint operator would give one for $R_+^*$ by taking adjoints. This proves the assertion in all cases, without a simplicity assumption.
\end{proof}

Consequently the actual ordinary Fredholm determinant constructed in \fid{MAS-10} can equal that of the designated positive diagonal $B$ while its quotient operator has no bounded similarity to a selfadjoint operator. The obstruction distinguishes the determinant and full algebraic spectrum from the Hilbert metric. A change to an unbounded or nonequivalent completion would be another construction; it is not supplied by this argument. Every Masullo-dependent conclusion remains conditional on the exact draft inputs stated above, with nuclearity needed for the Gaussian trace-class family but not for \fid{MAS-10} or the no-similarity conclusion.

\subsection{MAS-11: fixed characters, central zeros and the paired determinant}
The Green construction extends to a fixed primitive Dirichlet character, and more generally to a fixed unitary Hecke character of a number field, conditional only on the same spectral-annihilator theorem. Two distinctions matter: central zeros require a finite-dimensional removal, and a character which is not self-dual gives the normalized product of its completed $L$-function and that of its functional-equation partner.

\paragraph{Completion conventions.}
Let $\chi$ be a unitary Hecke character of $C_F$, let $\chi^\vee=\chi^{-1}$, and put $Q_\chi=|d_F|N\mathfrak f_\chi$. At a real place write $\chi_v(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)^{\kappa_v}|x|^{it_v}$, with $\kappa_v\in\{0,1\}$ and $t_v\in\R$. At a complex place write $\chi_v(z)=(z/|z|)^{m_v}|z|_v^{it_v}$, with $m_v\in\Z$, $t_v\in\R$, and $|z|_v=|z|^2$. Define
\[
 \Gamma_{\R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),\qquad
 \Gamma_{\C}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s),
\]
\[
 \Lambda_\chi(s)=Q_\chi^{s/2}
 \prod_{v\text{ real}}\Gamma_{\R}(s+\kappa_v+it_v)
 \prod_{v\text{ complex}}\Gamma_{\C}(s+|m_v|/2+it_v)
 L_f(s,\chi).
\]
Here $L_f$ uses the primitive finite Euler factors: $(1-\chi_v(\varpi_v)q_v^{-s})^{-1}$ when unramified and $1$ when ramified. The completed functional equation is
\[
 \Lambda_\chi(s)=\epsilon_\chi\Lambda_{\chi^\vee}(1-s),
 \qquad |\epsilon_\chi|=1.
\]
These local factors and the global functional equation are the Tate conventions in \cite[\S4.7, pp.~19--22; Theorem 5.22, pp.~38--39]{poonen786}; the conductor normalization is also recorded in \cite[\S1, pp.~281--282]{rajan1998}. The gamma factor without the $2$ at each complex place differs only by a constant, which will cancel below.

If $\chi=|\cdot|^{i\tau}$, set $p_\chi(s)=(s+i\tau)(s-1+i\tau)$; otherwise set $p_\chi(s)=1$. Then
\[
 \mathcal F_\chi(s)=p_\chi(s)\Lambda_\chi(s)
\]
is entire of order one. The pole statement follows from the same Tate theorem. The entire-order-one assertion is stated in \cite[\S1, p.~282]{rajan1998} for normalized unitary characters; an arbitrary unitary character is an imaginary norm twist of a normalized one, so its completion is a vertical translate times a nonzero constant. The asserted order is therefore unchanged. Also $p_\chi(s)=p_{\chi^\vee}(1-s)$, so $\mathcal F_\chi$ satisfies the same completed functional equation. Pole cancellation introduces no zero at either simple pole. Its zeros are the zeros of the completed Hecke $L$-function, and the Euler product and functional equation place them in $0\le\Re s\le1$.

For a primitive Dirichlet character modulo $q$, one may instead use the customary constant multiple
\[
 \Lambda_\chi(s)=(q/\pi)^{(s+\kappa)/2}
 \Gamma((s+\kappa)/2)L(s,\chi),\qquad
 \kappa=(1-\chi(-1))/2,
\]
\[
 \epsilon_\chi=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{i^\kappa\sqrt q},\qquad
 \tau(\chi)=\sum_{a\bmod q}\chi(a)e^{2\pi ia/q}.
\]
See \cite[(25.15.5)--(25.15.6)]{dlmf2515} and the derivation in \cite[\S6.1; (6.2.2)]{kedlayaFE}. For $q>1$ the primitive character is nonprincipal and $p_\chi=1$; for $q=1$ use $p_\chi=s(s-1)$. Constants such as the usual factor $1/2$ in $\xi$ cancel in every normalization below.

\paragraph{The actual quotient and the central removal.}
Fix $a=1/2$, a strip half-width $\alpha>1/2$, and a weight parameter $\nu>1$. The slight enlargement of the strip ensures that all completed zeros are interior using only the closed-strip fact just established. No boundary nonvanishing theorem is needed. Put
\[
 H_\alpha=L^2(\R,e^{2\alpha|u|}(1+u^2)^{\nu/2}\,du).
\]
Choose the unitary base point $\chi$ in its compact-character component, as allowed in \cite[Notation 10.2.2.1, p.~445]{masullo}, and normalize compact Haar measure to mass one. Label by $E_\chi$ the quotient sector whose dual has base point $\chi$. In the already centered ambient realization, the unitary radial gauge
\[
 f(g)=\overline{\chi(g)}\,h(\log|g|)
\]
identifies its ambient space with $H_\alpha$ and its closed centered theta-image with some $V_\chi\subset H_\alpha$. The centering by $a=1/2$ is already incorporated in this theta-image, as in (10.2.2.1)--(10.2.2.2) of the source. This convention labels the primal sector by its dual character, eliminating any convention about conjugate isotype notation.

The sole draft input is \cite[Theorem 10.2.2.1, (10.2.2.4)--(10.2.2.6), p.~446]{masullo}, with the Hilbert-dual density described in \cite[Remark 10.2.1.2, pp.~440--441]{masullo}: on $E_\chi=H_\alpha/V_\chi$, the independent continuous jets
\[
 \ell_{\rho,j}(h)=\int_\R h(u)e^{z_\rho u}u^j\,du,
 \quad z_\rho=\rho-\tfrac12,\quad 0\le j<N_\rho,
\]
have norm-dense span in $E_\chi'$. Throughout all sums and products, $\rho$ runs over the distinct completed zeros and $N_\rho$ denotes its multiplicity. Restriction to the fixed sector follows by bounded compact-character averaging from the source's full dual density. In these coordinates pure translations are the original radial translations multiplied by the inverse unitary scalar supplied by $\chi$ on the chosen radial section. This gauge is essential for an arbitrary imaginary norm twist.

The source's $L(s,\chi)$ is already completed: \cite[Definition 3.2.1.2 and (3.2.1.2)--(3.2.1.5), p.~123]{masullo} include the archimedean factors, while Definition 3.2.1.4 separately names the finite $L_f$. Away from the polar case, our completion differs by the nowhere-zero factor $2^{r_2}Q_\chi^{s/2}$. In the polar case, Proposition 3.2.1.3(2) gives the same two simple poles, and the ideal in \cite[Theorem 7.1.4.2(1), p.~326]{masullo} uses $(s+i\tau)(1-s-i\tau)L(s,\chi)$, equal to $-p_\chi(s)L(s,\chi)$. Hence its zero set with multiplicities is exactly that of $\mathcal F_\chi$; clearing the poles contributes no extra spectral jets. The product formula (7.1.4.9) from that draft is not used here.

Put $B_0=\alpha+\nu/4$ and choose $\mu>B_0$. The convolution kernel $g_\mu(v)=e^{-\mu|v|}/(2\mu)$ induces $K_\chi$ on $E_\chi$. The proof of \fid{MAS-10} applies with precisely the following parameter replacements:
\[
 |(\log\sqrt{w_\alpha})'|\le B_0,\qquad
 F_\mu(z)=\frac1{\mu^2-z^2},\qquad
 \lambda_\rho=\frac1{\mu^2-z_\rho^2}.
\]
Indeed the complete annihilator and the translation formula preserve $V_\chi$; the weighted weak-domain calculation gives the same numerical sector with constant $B_0/\sqrt{\mu^2-B_0^2}$. Since $|\Re z_\rho|\le1/2$,
\[
 \Re\lambda_\rho>0,\qquad
 |\lambda_\rho|\le\frac1{\mu^2-1/4+(\Im\rho)^2}.
\]
Entire order one gives $\sum_{\rho\ne1/2}N_\rho|z_\rho|^{-2}<\infty$, and hence $\sum_\rho N_\rho|\lambda_\rho|<\infty$. For example, Jensen's formula gives $n(R)=O_\eta(R^{1+\eta})$ for every $\eta>0$, and dyadic summation with $\eta<1$ proves the asserted reciprocal-square sum. Gram--Schmidt on the invariant finite jet flags therefore gives the finite diagonal sum of $\Re K_\chi^*$ used in \fid{MAS-10}. Its sector-factorization lemma proves $K_\chi\in\mathcal S_1$, before any use of eigenvalue trace formulas. The same compact triangular argument identifies its exact nonzero spectrum and algebraic multiplicities. In particular,
\[
 \|K_\chi\|_1\le\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{\mu^2-B_0^2}}
 \sum_\rho N_\rho\Re\frac1{\mu^2-z_\rho^2}.
\]
Neither GRH nor the draft's nuclearity theorem is used.

Let $m=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}\mathcal F_\chi(s)$, and let $P_{\chi,0}$ be the Riesz projection of $K_\chi$ for $\mu^{-2}$, or zero if $m=0$. Its rank is exactly $m$: its transpose is identity on the full central jet block and zero on every other jet block. This also proves independence of $P_{\chi,0}$ from $\mu$, by dual density. Remove its entire generalized eigenspace and set
\[
 E_\chi^\circ=(I-P_{\chi,0})E_\chi,\qquad
 K_\chi^\circ=K_\chi|_{E_\chi^\circ},\qquad
 R_\chi=K_\chi^\circ(I-\mu^2K_\chi^\circ)^{-1}.
\]
The complement need not be orthogonal. It is closed and invariant, restriction preserves trace class, and the compact spectral description makes the indicated inverse bounded. Consequently $R_\chi\in\mathcal S_1(E_\chi^\circ)$. Its complete noncentral jet multiplier is $-1/z^2$, independent of $\mu$, and its nonzero eigenvalues are $-z_\rho^{-2}$ with their full algebraic multiplicities. Coincident values are counted additively. Removing only eigenvectors need not suffice at a multiple central zero: $F_\mu$ is even at zero and can have nontrivial higher central jets.

\paragraph{The normalized determinant and the partner involution.}
Write
\[
 C_\chi(z)=\mathcal F_\chi(\tfrac12+z),\qquad
 f_\chi(z)=z^{-m}C_\chi(z),\qquad
 a_\chi=f_\chi(0)=\mathcal F_\chi^{(m)}(1/2)/m!\ne0.
\]
The functional equation gives the same central order $m$ for $\chi^\vee$, and
\[
 \frac{f_\chi(-z)}{a_\chi}
 =\frac{f_{\chi^\vee}(z)}{a_{\chi^\vee}}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \mathcal H_\chi(z)=
 \frac{f_\chi(z)f_{\chi^\vee}(z)}{a_\chi a_{\chi^\vee}}
\]
is even, entire of order at most one, and equal to $1$ at zero. Define $G_\chi(w)=\mathcal H_\chi(i\sqrt w)$ by its even power series; this is an entire function of order at most $1/2$, with no square-root branch choice. The usual conjugation identity also gives $a_{\chi^\vee}=\overline{a_\chi}$ in the displayed completion conventions.

\begin{proposition}
Under the exact spectral-annihilator input above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasCharacterFull}
 \det_{E_\chi^\circ}(I-wR_\chi)=G_\chi(w).
\end{equation}
On the labeled direct sum $E_\chi^\circ\oplus E_{\chi^\vee}^\circ$, inversion induces a unitary exchange involution. The determinant on its even summand is also $G_\chi(w)$, whereas the determinant on the whole direct sum is $G_\chi(w)^2$.

If $\chi=\chi^\vee$ as a global character, inversion acts within $E_\chi^\circ$. On its even summand,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasCharacterSelfdual}
 \det_{E_{\chi,+}^\circ}(I-wR_{\chi,+})
 =\frac{f_\chi(i\sqrt w)}{a_\chi},
\end{equation}
and the full single-sector determinant is the square of this function.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Hadamard factorization of the central-zero-removed entire function gives
\[
 \frac{f_\chi(z)}{a_\chi}
 =e^{b_\chi z}\prod_{\rho\ne1/2}
 \left[(1-z/z_\rho)e^{z/z_\rho}\right]^{N_\rho}.
\]
Multiply by the same identity at $-z$. Both the linear exponential and every genus-one convergence factor cancel. The absolutely locally uniformly convergent result is
\[
 \frac{f_\chi(z)f_\chi(-z)}{a_\chi^2}
 =\prod_{\rho\ne1/2}(1-z^2/z_\rho^2)^{N_\rho}.
\]
The functional equation identifies the left side with $\mathcal H_\chi(z)$. The trace-class determinant theorem, with the algebraic multiplicities already proved, now gives
\[
 \det(I-wR_\chi)
 =\prod_{\rho\ne1/2}(1+w/z_\rho^2)^{N_\rho}
 =\mathcal H_\chi(i\sqrt w).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:MasCharacterFull}, including its normalization and the absence of a residual exponential factor.

In the radial gauges, $J h(u)=h(-u)$ is a unitary map from the $\chi$ space to the $\chi^\vee$ space. The functional equation pairs the zeros $z$ and $-z$ with their multiplicities, and
\[
 \ell_{\chi^\vee,\,1-\rho,j}(Jh)=(-1)^j\ell_{\chi,\rho,j}(h).
\]
The full annihilator descriptions imply $JV_\chi=V_{\chi^\vee}$. Evenness of $g_\mu$ gives $JK_\chi=K_{\chi^\vee}J$, hence the same intertwining for the central Riesz projections and for $R_\chi$. This proves that $J$ restricts to a unitary map of the central complements. The exchange involution is
\[
 \mathcal J(x,y)=(Jy,Jx).
\]
Its even summand is the graph $\{(x,Jx)/\sqrt2:x\in E_\chi^\circ\}$, unitarily identified with $E_\chi^\circ$. The restricted direct-sum operator is therefore unitarily equivalent to $R_\chi$. The partner determinant is identical by the functional equation, proving both direct-sum assertions. Labeled copies are retained even if the compact restrictions of $\chi$ and $\chi^\vee$ coincide; their chosen unitary base points and radial gauges can differ.

When $\chi=\chi^\vee$, the functional equation gives $\epsilon_\chi=(-1)^m$ and $f_\chi(-z)=f_\chi(z)$. Reflection exchanges the two independent noncentral jet blocks at $z$ and $-z$; its even part has dimension $N_\rho$ for that pair. Applying the bounded even projector to the dense jet span proves completeness on the even quotient. The resulting determinant has precisely one factor $1+w/z_\rho^2$ per unordered pair, with full multiplicity $N_\rho$. Its square is \eqref{eq:MasCharacterFull}, while $(f_\chi(i\sqrt w)/a_\chi)^2=G_\chi(w)$. Both candidate square roots are entire and equal to $1$ at zero, so they agree near zero and hence everywhere. This proves \eqref{eq:MasCharacterSelfdual}. The central jets, before removal, have reflection signs $(-1)^j$ for $0\le j<m$; their even and odd dimensions are $\lceil m/2\rceil$ and $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. No assumption of central nonvanishing or simple zeros enters the argument.
\end{proof}

The half-width $\alpha=1/2$ is also admissible when the classical nonvanishing theorem on $\Re s=1$ is included, because the functional equation then puts all completed zeros in the open strip. The formulation above deliberately needs only the weaker closed-strip consequence of the Euler product and functional equation. It concerns fixed characters, with no asserted bound uniform in conductor or infinity type. For a non-self-dual character, the single-sector inverse square naturally yields the paired product; an even completed factor belonging to that character alone is not supplied by its functional equation. These determinant identities establish neither positivity nor selfadjointness in the actual quotient metric.

\paragraph{Grothendieck framework.}
The conclusion is an analytic trace-class realization of completed rank-one Hecke $L$-functions on the stated adelic Hilbert quotients. Cote~1 supplies the ordinary Fredholm trace/determinant mechanism after the sector argument has proved membership in $\mathcal S_1$; its exterior-power coefficients and trace logarithm apply to $R_\chi$ and its parity restrictions. Rank one here describes the local character representations in the Euler factors. The argument supplies no constructible $\ell$-adic complex on $\Spec\mathcal O_F$, no arithmetic cohomology with a Frobenius or correspondence whose determinant is this analytic determinant, and no identification of the quotient with a motive. The geometric trace-formula comparison therefore retains the same boundary as in the preceding Grothendieck-framework discussion: the new result closes the analytic character determinant step, conditional on the spectral-annihilator theorem, without constructing such a geometric identification.


\subsection{MAS-12: the character quotient metric still prevents selfadjoint similarity}
Retain the fixed character, centered quotient, weight and central removal of \fid{MAS-11}. In particular,
\[
 w_\alpha(u)=e^{2\alpha|u|}(1+u^2)^{\nu/2},\quad
 \alpha>\tfrac12,\quad\nu>1,\quad
 E_\chi=H_\alpha/V_\chi,
\]
and $R_\chi$ acts on $E_\chi^\circ=(I-P_{\chi,0})E_\chi$. All zeros below are distinct, with their multiplicities denoted by $N_\rho$. The same conclusions hold at $\alpha=1/2$ under the additional boundary nonvanishing input described in \fid{MAS-11}.

\begin{proposition}
Conditional on the exact spectral-annihilator input of \fid{MAS-11}, $R_\chi$ is not boundedly similar to a selfadjoint operator. The same is true of the operator on the even exchange summand of the labeled $\chi,\chi^{-1}$ pair. If $\chi=\chi^{-1}$ globally, the internal even operator $R_{\chi,+}$ also is not boundedly similar to a selfadjoint operator.

These assertions require neither GRH nor a simplicity hypothesis. Under GRH all retained nonzero eigenvalues are positive, but these actual quotient operators are not selfadjoint or positive, and no equivalent Hilbert norm makes them selfadjoint.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{The central removal in the actual metric.}
Identify $E_\chi$ isometrically with $V_\chi^\perp\subset H_\alpha$. In the paper's conjugate-linear-first convention, the Riesz representatives of the central jets are
\[
 p_j(u)=\frac{u^j}{w_\alpha(u)},\qquad 0\le j<m,
 \quad m=\operatorname{ord}_{s=1/2}\mathcal F_\chi(s).
\]
Let $C_0$ be their span, with $C_0=0$ if $m=0$. The transpose of the Riesz spectral projection $P_0=P_{\chi,0}$ is identity on these jets and zero on all noncentral jets. Their norm-dense full span therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}P_0^*=C_0,\qquad
 E_\chi^\circ=\ker P_0=C_0^\perp\cap E_\chi.
\]
Thus if $\Pi_0$ is the \emph{orthogonal} projection onto $C_0$ in $E_\chi$, then $Q=I-\Pi_0$ is the orthogonal projection onto $E_\chi^\circ$. It is generally different from $I-P_0$. In particular the orthogonal complement removed here is $\operatorname{Ran}P_0^*$, not $\ker P_0^*$.

For a retained critical-line zero $\rho=1/2+i\gamma$, its evaluation representative before and after the removal is
\[
 v_\gamma(u)=\frac{e^{-i\gamma u}}{w_\alpha(u)},\qquad
 q_\gamma=Qv_\gamma.
\]
The latter represents the evaluation restricted to $E_\chi^\circ$ and satisfies
\[
 R_\chi^*q_\gamma=\gamma^{-2}q_\gamma.
\]
Restrictions of finite collections of noncentral jets remain independent: a combination vanishing on $\ker P_0$ would equal its composition with $P_0$, whereas the transpose of $P_0$ kills that combination.

Set
\[
 K_\alpha(t)=\int_\R\frac{e^{itu}}{w_\alpha(u)}\,du.
\]
This is real, even and continuous, $K_\alpha(0)>0$, and $K_\alpha(t)\to0$ as $|t|\to\infty$. Every polynomial times $1/w_\alpha$ belongs to $L^1$. The Fourier decay theorem consequently gives $\|\Pi_0v_\gamma\|\to0$ as $|\gamma|\to\infty$: compute its finitely many coordinates in an orthonormal basis of $C_0$, whose elements are polynomials divided by $w_\alpha$. Direct integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasCharacterCorrelation}
 \langle q_\gamma,q_\delta\rangle
 =K_\alpha(\gamma-\delta)
  -\langle\Pi_0v_\gamma,\Pi_0v_\delta\rangle,
 \qquad
 \|q_\gamma\|^2=K_\alpha(0)-\|\Pi_0v_\gamma\|^2.
\end{equation}
In fact $K_\alpha(t)>0$ for every real $t$, by the Gamma/Gaussian proof in \fid{MAS-09} with $e^{-|u|}$ replaced by $e^{-2\alpha|u|}$. After a nonzero central removal, the subtraction in \eqref{eq:MasCharacterCorrelation} must still be retained; it need not preserve strict positivity for every individual pair.

\emph{A growth argument forcing nearby distinct ordinates in the simple case.}
Let $n_F=[F:\mathbf Q]$. The completion fixed in \fid{MAS-11}, the identity $L_f(s,\chi)\to1$ as real $s\to+\infty$, and Stirling's formula \cite[(5.11.1)]{dlmf511} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MasCharacterRealGrowth}
 \log\mathcal H_\chi(x)=n_F x\log x+O_\chi(x)
 \qquad(x\to+\infty).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal H_\chi(x)=|f_\chi(x)|^2/|a_\chi|^2>0$ for $x>1/2$. To verify the leading coefficient, each real-place gamma factor contributes $(x/2)\log x+O_\chi(x)$ to $\log|\Lambda_\chi(1/2+x)|$, and each complex-place factor contributes $x\log x+O_\chi(x)$. Their sum is $(n_F/2)x\log x+O_\chi(x)$; taking the squared modulus doubles it. The conductor adds only a linear term, and pole clearing and central-zero removal add $O_\chi(\log x)$. The limit of the finite Euler product follows by dominated convergence in its absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for real $s\ge2$.

Suppose temporarily that every noncentral zero is simple and that each centered zero lies on the real or imaginary axis. There are only finitely many noncentral real-axis zeros, since they lie in the compact interval $[-1/2,1/2]$ and the completed entire function is nonzero. The product proved in \fid{MAS-11} yields, for large real $x$,
\[
 \log\mathcal H_\chi(x)
 =O_\chi(\log x)
  +\sum_{\gamma}\log(1+x^2/\gamma^2),
\]
where the sum ranges over its nonzero imaginary-axis zeros $i\gamma$. The finite real-axis factors contribute their logarithmic absolute values; they do not change this equality.

Let $N_{\rm im}(T)=\#\{\gamma:0<|\gamma|\le T\}$. If $N_{\rm im}(T)=O(T)$, enlarge the constant so that $N_{\rm im}(t)\le Ct$ for all $t>0$, using the zero-free neighborhood of the removed central point. Tonelli applied to
\[
 \log(1+x^2/\gamma^2)
 =\int_{|\gamma|}^{\infty}\frac{2x^2}{t(t^2+x^2)}\,dt
\]
would give
\[
 \sum_\gamma\log(1+x^2/\gamma^2)
 \le2Cx^2\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t^2+x^2}=\pi Cx,
\]
contradicting \eqref{eq:MasCharacterRealGrowth}. Thus $N_{\rm im}(T)$ is not $O(T)$. Since these zeros are simple, there exist distinct ordinates $\gamma_j,\delta_j$ of the same sign such that
\[
 |\gamma_j|,|\delta_j|\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad |\gamma_j-\delta_j|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed, an eventual positive lower gap on both half-lines would give $N_{\rm im}(T)=O(T)$; discreteness permits the chosen pairs to escape each compact interval. Their squares are distinct. This step is used only after multiplicities have been disposed of below.

\emph{Exhausting the spectral and multiplicity cases.}
If some centered zero $z_\rho$ has both nonzero real and imaginary parts, then $-z_\rho^{-2}$ is a nonreal eigenvalue of $R_\chi$. Similarity preserves spectrum, so similarity to a selfadjoint operator is impossible.

Otherwise every centered zero lies on one of the two axes. If a noncentral zero has multiplicity at least two, its first two restricted jets satisfy
\[
 R_\chi'\ell_{\rho,0}=F(z_\rho)\ell_{\rho,0},\qquad
 R_\chi'\ell_{\rho,1}=F(z_\rho)\ell_{\rho,1}
                          +F'(z_\rho)\ell_{\rho,0},
 \quad F(z)=-z^{-2},\quad F'(z)=2z^{-3}\ne0.
\]
The independent jets therefore give a genuine Jordan chain at the real eigenvalue $F(z_\rho)$. Through the Riesz identification, this is a Jordan chain for the adjoint. A selfadjoint operator has no such chain: if $(A-\lambda)y=x$ and $(A-\lambda)x=0$ with real $\lambda$, then $\|x\|^2=\langle x,(A-\lambda)y\rangle=0$. Bounded similarity preserves this obstruction.

In the remaining case, all noncentral zeros are simple and lie on the axes, so the preceding growth argument gives the nearby pairs. By \eqref{eq:MasCharacterCorrelation},
\[
 \|q_{\gamma_j}\|,\|q_{\delta_j}\|\to\sqrt{K_\alpha(0)},
 \qquad \langle q_{\gamma_j},q_{\delta_j}\rangle\to K_\alpha(0).
\]
The normalized vectors $a_j,b_j$ therefore satisfy $\|a_j-b_j\|\to0$, while being eigenvectors of $R_\chi^*$ for distinct real eigenvalues.
If $R_\chi^*=SAS^{-1}$ with $A$ selfadjoint and $S$ boundedly invertible, then $S^{-1}a_j$ and $S^{-1}b_j$ are orthogonal, each of norm at least $1/\|S\|$. Their difference has norm at least $\sqrt2/\|S\|$, but also at most $\|S^{-1}\|\|a_j-b_j\|\to0$, a contradiction. Taking adjoints transfers the no-similarity conclusion from $R_\chi^*$ to $R_\chi$.

\emph{Exchange parity and internal self-dual parity.}
The even summand of the labeled pair is unitarily identified with $E_\chi^\circ$ by $x\mapsto(x,Jx)/\sqrt2$, as proved in \fid{MAS-11}. Its operator and its adjoint are therefore unitarily conjugate to $R_\chi$ and $R_\chi^*$, so the conclusion transfers with the actual metrics.

If $\chi=\chi^{-1}$ globally, reflection acts internally. Let $E_{\chi,+}$ be the original even summand, and let $C_{0,+}$ be the span of the even central vectors $u^{2j}/w_\alpha$ for $2j<m$. The orthogonal projection $Q_+$ from $E_{\chi,+}$ onto $E_{\chi,+}^\circ$ is identity minus projection onto $C_{0,+}$. For a positive critical ordinate, put
\[
 c_\gamma(u)=\frac{\cos(\gamma u)}{w_\alpha(u)},\qquad
 q_{\gamma,+}=Q_+c_\gamma.
\]
These are adjoint eigenvectors for $\gamma^{-2}$, and
\[
 \langle c_\gamma,c_\delta\rangle
 =\tfrac12\bigl(K_\alpha(\gamma-\delta)+K_\alpha(\gamma+\delta)\bigr).
\]
Their finite central projections again tend to zero as the ordinate grows. The nonreal-eigenvalue case persists on the internal even summand, because each noncentral $\{z,-z\}$ pair retains its full multiplicity there. In the multiple-zero case the independent even jets
$\ell_{z,0}+\ell_{-z,0}$ and $\ell_{z,1}-\ell_{-z,1}$ give the same nonzero Jordan entry $F'(z)$; central removal does not affect their independence.

In the remaining simple-axis case, self-duality makes the imaginary ordinates symmetric. If the positive ordinates had an eventual positive lower gap, their total two-sided count would be $O(T)$, contrary to the growth argument. Choose nearby positive pairs tending to infinity. For these pairs the displayed cosine formula and Fourier decay give
\[
 \|q_{\gamma,+}\|^2\longrightarrow K_\alpha(0)/2,\qquad
 \langle q_{\gamma,+},q_{\delta,+}\rangle\longrightarrow K_\alpha(0)/2.
\]
Normalized eigenvectors again coalesce at distinct eigenvalues, giving exactly the same bounded-similarity contradiction.

This exhausts all cases without GRH. Under GRH the exact eigenvalues from \fid{MAS-11} are positive, but selfadjointness is already excluded. A positive bounded operator is selfadjoint. Finally an equivalent Hilbert inner product is represented by a bounded positive invertible metric operator; its square root would give a bounded similarity to a selfadjoint operator if the original operator were selfadjoint in that metric. Thus an equivalent Hilbert norm cannot remove the obstruction.
\end{proof}

The ordinary character determinants of \fid{MAS-11} consequently coexist with this metric obstruction. Whenever the relevant zero-location statement is available---the stipulated theorem in the zeta/Dirichlet setting, and GRH as an additional assumption for a general Hecke character---a positive diagonal realization or the positively weighted convolution construction of \fid{DET-15} cannot be related to these weighted theta-quotient operators by a boundedly invertible intertwiner. Their determinant agreement concerns the complete algebraic spectrum and trace-class products, and does not identify the Hilbert metrics.

\section{The six programmes after the stipulated RH theorem}
The following are consequences through existing criteria, not assertions that those criteria supplied Alp\"oge's proof. A structural construction ``does not follow'' means that the cited implication gives no such construction; no formal logical independence claim is intended. \subsection{PRG-WEIL: the quadratic form, with its sign specified}
Use $\widetilde g(s)=\int_0^\infty g(x)x^{s-1}dx$ and $g^\sharp(x)=x^{-1}\overline{g(x^{-1})}$. For multiplicative convolution,
\[
 \widetilde{g*g^\sharp}(s)=\widetilde g(s)\overline{\widetilde g(1-\bar s)}.
\]
The explicit formula in the attached Connes--Consani normalization reads
\[
 \widetilde f(0)-\sum_\rho\widetilde f(\rho)+\widetilde f(1)=\sum_v\mathcal W_v(f).
\]
Therefore, for smooth compactly supported $g$ with $\widetilde g(0)=\widetilde g(1)=0$, the Alp\"oge consequence is
\[
 Q(g)=\sum_\rho|\widetilde g(\rho)|^2\ge0,
 \qquad \sum_v\mathcal W_v(g*g^\sharp)=-Q(g)\le0.
\]
This follows by the same Mellin calculation as \fid{MAS-04}; rapid decay and the zero count justify the sum. The converse is Weil's criterion, in the precise version \cite[Appendix C, Proposition C.1, label \texttt{mainprop}]{cc}. It is positivity of a global quadratic form, not positivity of each local term or of a scaling generator. The analogous character-sector statement follows from stipulated Dirichlet GRH with the corresponding completed local factors.

The source's local archimedean result, \fid{PRG-CC-LOCAL}, is already unconditional: \cite[Introduction, Theorem 1, label \texttt{mainthmintro}]{cc} proves
$W_\infty(g*g^*)\ge\Tr(\vartheta(g)\mathbf S\vartheta(g)^*)\ge0$
for its specified support $[2^{-1/2},2^{1/2}]$ and transform vanishings at $i/2$ and $0$. Here $\mathbf S$ is the Sonin-space projection and the notation is the paper's centered normalization. That local compression is real operator structure, but is not an identity with $B$. A geometric realization of the global form by arithmetic correspondences or intersection theory remains an additional target. The curve comparison has exact home in SGA 5, III B, 1.3.1; no EGA construction of the number-field realization is obtained by the inequality alone.

\subsection{PRG-LI / B-03: a positive sequence and an actual paired trace}
Li's criterion is \cite[\S1, Theorem 1]{li}. Its coefficients are
\[
 \lambda_n=\frac1{(n-1)!}\left.\frac{d^n}{ds^n}
 \bigl(s^{n-1}\log\xi(s)\bigr)\right|_{s=1}
 =\sum_\rho\left[1-\left(1-\frac1\rho\right)^n\right],\qquad n\ge1,
\]
where the latter is the symmetric zero sum. All $\lambda_n$ are positive under the stipulated RH. The following checked special form connects this criterion to the designated $B$ and refines SA23 \S4.1(4).

\begin{proposition}
Unconditionally, put $C=B(1+B/4)^{-1}$ and $P_n(x)=2-2T_n(1-x/2)$, where $T_n$ is the Chebyshev polynomial. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:litrace}
 \lambda_n=\Tr P_n(C)=\Tr\bigl[2I-2T_n(I-C/2)\bigr]\quad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
Under RH, $P_n(C)\ge0$ and $\lambda_n>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The operator $1+B/4$ is invertible: a diagonal zero would require $\gamma_j^2=-1/4$, corresponding to $\rho=0$ or $1$, neither a zero of $\xi$; the diagonal entries tend to one, so their inverses are uniformly bounded. Thus $C$ is trace class, with entries
$x_j=(\gamma_j^2+1/4)^{-1}=[\rho_j(1-\rho_j)]^{-1}$.
For $a=1-1/\rho$, the reflected zero $1-\rho$ gives $a^{-1}$ and
$a+a^{-1}=2-x$. The Chebyshev recurrence gives
$a^n+a^{-n}=2T_n(1-x/2)$. The two Li terms therefore sum to $P_n(x)$.

Since $P_n(0)=0$, $P_n(C)$ is trace class, and the pair sum converges absolutely because $P_n(x)=O_n(x)$ near zero. Pairing preserves the symmetric height cutoff. This proves \eqref{eq:litrace}; the displayed bracket is one polynomial with canceled constant term, not a subtraction of two separately defined infinite traces. Under RH each $x_j$ lies in $(0,4)$, so $T_n(1-x_j/2)\le1$ and $P_n(C)\ge0$. There are arbitrarily small positive $x_j$, and $P_n'(0)=n^2>0$, giving strict positivity of the trace.
\end{proof}
No trace-class operator can have the unpaired spectrum $\{1/\rho\}$, by reciprocal-zero divergence. That narrow intake statement remains correct. A broader assertion that Li coefficients have no trace-class expressions is refuted by \eqref{eq:litrace}. Their positivity supplies no identification of the finite automorphic determinants with this $B$.

\subsection{PRG-NB: the precise closure assertion is now true}
In $L^2((0,\infty),dx)$ put $\rho_a(x)=\{1/(ax)\}$ and $\chi=1_{(0,1]}$. B\'aez-Duarte's strengthened Nyman--Beurling criterion, \cite[Theorem 1.1]{baez}, gives the Alp\"oge consequence

 \chi\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\rho_a:a\in\mathbb N\}}^{L^2}.
\]
This is membership of the indicated function, not density of that span in all of $L^2(0,\infty)$. There is no remaining RH gate in this equivalent statement. Prescribed approximants, sharper rates or an arithmetic geometric explanation require their own results; the cited paper already provides constructive approximation information. Cote 1 supplies no identification of these dilations with its determinant operator, and the closure statement itself is functional analysis rather than an EGA/FGA assertion.

\subsection{PRG-DB-FAMILY: de Branges, with the entire shift family}
For every $\varepsilon>0$ set
\[
 E_\varepsilon(z)=\xi(1/2+\varepsilon-iz)=\Xi(z+i\varepsilon),\qquad
 E_\varepsilon^\#(z)=\overline{E_\varepsilon(\bar z)}=\Xi(z-i\varepsilon).
\]
The Alp\"oge consequence is the Hermite--Biehler property
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HB}
 |E_\varepsilon(z)|>|E_\varepsilon^\#(z)|\quad(\Im z>0)
\end{equation}
for \emph{every} $\varepsilon>0$. A numbered family criterion in this convention is \cite[Proposition 1.2; (1.3), (1.7), (1.11)]{suzuki}.
\begin{proof}[Proof of this shifted-$\Xi$ form]
Under RH, $\Xi(z)=\Xi(0)\prod_{\gamma>0}(1-z^2/\gamma^2)$. If $z=x+iy$, $y>0$, each real zero satisfies
$|z+i\varepsilon-\gamma|^2-|z-i\varepsilon-\gamma|^2=4y\varepsilon>0$, and likewise for $-\gamma$. Each paired factor has strictly larger modulus at the upper argument. If the lower-argument product vanishes, the inequality is immediate since the upper argument cannot be a real zero. Otherwise the ratios of partial paired products converge, and fixing one factor bounds every later ratio below a number strictly greater than one. This proves \eqref{eq:HB}. There are no real zeros of $E_\varepsilon$.

Conversely, if all shifts satisfy \eqref{eq:HB} and $\Xi(z_0)=0$ with $\Im z_0>0$, choose $0<\varepsilon<\Im z_0$. Then $E_\varepsilon(z_0-i\varepsilon)=0$ in the upper half-plane, contradicting \eqref{eq:HB}. Conjugation symmetry excludes lower-half-plane zeros as well.
\end{proof}
Each shift consequently defines a de Branges space and a positive reproducing kernel
\[
 K_\varepsilon(w,z)=
 \frac{E_\varepsilon(z)\overline{E_\varepsilon(w)}-
 E_\varepsilon^\#(z)\overline{E_\varepsilon^\#(w)}}{2\pi i(\bar w-z)}.
\]
Positivity here is positivity of all finite kernel Gram matrices. Abstract canonical-system existence follows from de Branges reconstruction; the separate target is a prescribed arithmetic Hamiltonian or kernel, not bare existence. Suzuki's particular arithmetic construction has an explicit parameter range (Theorem 2.3 and \S5). A single large shift is insufficient for RH.

Older sufficient shift-inner-product positivity proposals must be distinguished from this family criterion: Conrey--Li \cite[\S3.1, (3.1)--(3.4)]{conreyli} refutes the proposed $\xi$ choices. Those hypotheses are false, not surviving open consequences of RH. Neither the correct kernel criterion nor those false conditions construct an arithmetic motive or crystal.

\section{PRG-FF: the finite-field dictionary, checked on a curve}
Let $C/\F_q$ be smooth, proper and geometrically connected of genus $g$, with $\ell\nmid q$. Write $V=H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(C_{\overline{\F}_q},\Q_\ell)$ and choose the Frobenius convention with eigenvalues $1$ on $H^0$ and $q$ on $H^2$. Then
\begin{align}
 \#C(\F_{q^n})&=1+q^n-\Tr(F^n\mid V),\label{eq:curve_trace}\\
 Z(C,T)&=\frac{\det(1-TF\mid V)}{(1-T)(1-qT)},\label{eq:curve_det}\\
 -\log\det(1-TF\mid V)&=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Tr(F^n\mid V)}{n}T^n.\label{eq:curve_log}
\end{align}
Exact source locators are SGA 5, III B, Corollary 1.3.1, printed p.~143, and Deligne, Weil I, (1.5.1), (1.5.3), (1.5.4), pp.~275--276 \cite{sga5,weil1}. Equation \eqref{eq:curve_log} is finite linear algebra, so no infinite-dimensional trace theorem is needed. The exterior-power coefficients match cote 1 and SGA 6, V, \S2 and (7.1.1).

Purity, \cite[Theorem (1.6)]{weil1}, says $|\alpha_j|=q^{1/2}$ under each complex embedding. Thus in a complex diagonal realization of the semisimplified eigenvalue multiset, $U=q^{-1/2}F$ is unitary and
\[
 P_1(T)=\det(1-\sqrt q\,T U).
\]
This numerical scaling is not a canonical half Tate twist in the ordinary $\ell$-adic category. Nor is an arbitrary identification of an $\ell$-adic vector space with a complex Hilbert space being asserted. The correct analogue is fixed modulus of eigenvalues, not positive selfadjointness of Frobenius.

\begin{proposition}
For $E/\F_3:y^2=x^3-x$, the curve is smooth, $P_1(T)=1+3T^2$, and $U$ has eigenvalues $i,-i$. No nonzero scalar multiple of its Frobenius can be positive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The projective cubic is $Y^2Z-X^3+XZ^2=0$. Its $X$ derivative is $Z^2$ in characteristic three, nonzero on the affine chart; at the sole point at infinity $[0:1:0]$, its $Z$ derivative is one. It is therefore a smooth genus-one curve. For each $x=0,1,2$, $x^3-x=0$, giving three affine points plus infinity. Hence $\#E(\F_3)=4$, the Frobenius trace is $3+1-4=0$, and its determinant on $H^1$ is $3$, since $\Lambda^2H^1\simeq\Q_\ell(-1)$. Thus $P_1(T)=1+3T^2$. The two eigenvalues are $\pm i\sqrt3$. Any nonzero scalar multiple still has opposite nonzero eigenvalues, which cannot both be nonnegative real. Similarity cannot change this obstruction.
\end{proof}
In particular $\det(1-wU)=1+w^2$ and $\Tr U^2=-2$, despite purity. The cup pairing on $H^1$ is alternating with target $\Q_\ell(-1)$; Frobenius is a $q$-similitude. Even that relation alone does not imply purity: $D=\diag(1,q)$ obeys $D^{\mathsf T}JD=qJ$ for $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, but its eigenvalue moduli are $1$ and $q$. Direct multiplication proves the claimed identity. Polarization positivity, weight-one purity and positivity of an operator are different statements.

\section{ARC-01--05: repairing the archive argument mathematically}

\subsection{ARC-01: the complete corrected genus-zero lemma}
\begin{lemma}
Let $f(z)=\sum a_nz^n$ be a nonzero entire function and $h(z)=\sum b_nz^n$ an entire function of genus zero with $b_n\ge0$ and $|a_n|\le b_n$. If the nonzero zeros $z_j$ of $f$, counted with multiplicity, satisfy $\sum_j|z_j|^{-1}<\infty$, then $f$ has genus zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A genus-zero product $Cz^m\prod_j(1-z/u_j)$ with $\sum|u_j|^{-1}<\infty$ has minimal exponential type. Indeed, for any $\varepsilon>0$, choose a tail with reciprocal sum less than $\varepsilon/2$. Its modulus is at most $e^{\varepsilon|z|/2}$, while the remaining polynomial is at most $C_\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon|z|/2}$. Thus
\[
 |f(z)|\le h(|z|)\le C_\varepsilon e^{\varepsilon|z|}.
\]
Hadamard factorization \cite[Theorem 3.16, p.~92]{mcmullen} at order at most one and the assumed reciprocal summability give
$f(z)=Ce^{bz}g(z)$ with $C\ne0$ and $g(z)=z^m\prod_j(1-z/z_j)$ of genus zero. If factorization is initially written with genus-one factors, the absolutely convergent sum $\sum1/z_j$ is absorbed into $b$. This standard factorization step is the background theorem invoked on scan 111, line 13.

If $b\ne0$, replace $z$ by $(\bar b/|b|)z$ so that the new exponent is $|b|z$. Choose $\pi/3<\theta<\pi/2$. On $|\arg z|\le\theta$ one has $\Re z\ge\cos\theta\,|z|$. Applying the preceding bound for $f$ with $\varepsilon=|b|\cos\theta/2$ yields $|g(z)|\le A e^{-a|z|}$ on that sector, where $a>0$. The genus-zero bound for $g$ gives $|g(z)|\le B e^{a|z|/3}$ everywhere.

Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$ and $G_0(z)=g(z)g(\omega z)g(\omega^2z)$. For every direction one of the three rotated arguments has argument in $[-\pi/3,\pi/3]$. All have equal modulus. Therefore $|G_0(z)|\le AB^2e^{-a|z|/3}$ everywhere. Liouville's theorem \cite[Theorem 1.18, p.~19]{mcmullen} makes this entire function constant, and the decay makes the constant zero. A product of three nonzero entire functions is not identically zero. This contradiction forces $b=0$ and proves the lemma.
\end{proof}
If the initial genus-zero majorant has arbitrary complex coefficients, coefficientwise absolute values can be majorized by
$|C|(1+z)^m\prod_j(1+z/|u_j|)$; this justifies the positive-coefficient reduction. The required majorization is radial, $h(|z|)$, not $|h(z)|$. For instance $f=1-z$, $h=1+z$, $z=-1$ disproves the latter inequality. The result applies to arbitrary trace-class Hilbert operators via singular-value determinant domination, not through a positivity hypothesis on the original operator.

\subsection{ARC-02: the order in scan 110}
For $\alpha>1$, $P_\alpha(z)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-z/n^\alpha)$ has order $1/\alpha$, not $1$.
\begin{proof}
Reciprocal summability gives genus zero. Let $N=\lfloor r^{1/\alpha}\rfloor$. At $z=-r$ the first $N$ factors are at least two, hence $\log M(r)\ge N\log2$. Conversely
\[
 \log M(r)\le \sum_{n\le N}\log(1+r/n^\alpha)+r\sum_{n>N}n^{-\alpha}.
\]
The first sum is at most $N\log(2r)-\alpha\log(N!)=O_\alpha(N)$, using $\log(N!)\ge N\log N-N$ and $r<(N+1)^\alpha$. The integral estimate for the tail also gives $O_\alpha(N)$. Thus $\log M(r)$ is bounded above and below by positive multiples of $r^{1/\alpha}$, proving the order.
\end{proof}
The source says ``sauf erreur,'' so an authorial tentative error is a real possibility; mathematical certainty does not identify the mistaken glyph.

\subsection{ARC-03--04: scan 113 requires a liminf and a prefactor}
For a positive nonincreasing sequence $a_n\to0$ define
\[
 r=\inf\{s>0:a_n=O(n^{-1/s})\},\qquad
 \sigma=\inf\{s>0:\sum_na_n^s<\infty\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:liminf}
 \frac1r=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(1/a_n)}{\log n},\qquad r=\sigma,
\end{equation}
with extended reciprocal conventions.
\begin{proof}
If $a_n\le Cn^{-1/s}$, the liminf in \eqref{eq:liminf} is at least $1/s$. Conversely if $1/s$ is strictly less than that liminf, the required bound holds for all sufficiently large $n$ and extends to the finite prefix by a constant. Taking thresholds proves the first assertion. If $a_n=O(n^{-1/s})$, then $\sum a_n^t$ converges for every $t>s$, so $\sigma\le r$. If $\sum a_n^s<\infty$, monotonicity gives
\[
 \lfloor n/2\rfloor a_n^s\le\sum_{j=\lceil n/2\rceil}^n a_j^s\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $a_n=o(n^{-1/s})$, proving $r\le\sigma$ and the little-$o$ assertion of the manuscript.
\end{proof}
A counterexample to the printed limsup is explicit. Let $N_k=2^{2^k}$ for $k\ge0$, $a_1=1$, and $a_n=N_k^{-1}$ for $N_k\le n<N_{k+1}=N_k^2$. The sequence decreases to zero. The logarithmic ratio is one at $N_k$ and tends to $1/2$ at the right ends of the blocks, so its limsup is one and liminf is $1/2$. Also $a_n\le n^{-1/2}$, while $a_nn^{1/s}$ is unbounded along block ends for $s<2$. Thus $r=2$, refuting the asserted limsup formula.

For the manuscript's alternative proof that $\sum_n a_n<\infty$ implies $na_n\to0$, suppose $a_{n_i}\ge1/n_i$ after a harmless rescaling and $\varepsilon_i=(n_i-n_{i-1})/n_i$, then for $i\ge2$ summability gives $\sum\varepsilon_i<\infty$, and the correct recurrence is
\[
 n_i=\frac{n_1}{\prod_{j=2}^i(1-\varepsilon_j)}
 \le\frac{n_1}{\prod_{j=2}^\infty(1-\varepsilon_j)}<\infty.
\]
The infinite product is positive because eventually $-\log(1-\varepsilon_j)\le2\varepsilon_j$. This contradicts strict increase of the integers. The prefactor is necessary unless an initial value one is explicitly fixed; an initial zero would instead make the first factor vanish. To prove little-$o$, the subsequence threshold is an arbitrary positive constant, reduced to one by rescaling.

\subsection{ARC-05: norm and domain readings that the formulas force}
For a compact Hilbert operator with singular values $s_j$, the nonzero singular values of $\Lambda^n u$ are the products $s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_n}$ over strictly increasing indices. This follows first for finite-rank singular-value decompositions and then by finite-rank approximation. For $u$ trace class in the first identity and $u$ Hilbert--Schmidt in the second, this gives the finite identities
\[
 \|\Lambda^n u\|_1=\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_n}s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_n}
 =\alpha_n((u^*u)^{1/2}),\qquad
 \|\Lambda^n u\|_2^2=\alpha_n(u^*u).
\]
This proves the readings $\sqrt{u^*u}$ in scan 78, (16), and subscript $2$ in (17). At $n=1$, $u=I_2$, the latter square-root expression is $\sqrt2$, whereas $\|I_2\|_1=2$, so reading subscript $1$ is concretely false.
