## DP25: induced CM type and the balanced-product attempt

**Result: PASS.** The Frobenius-orbit argument uses precisely the full induced CM type. At an unramified prime inert in the quadratic subfield, restriction alternates between its two embeddings, forcing even orbit length and half of each orbit selected. At a split prime the restriction is constant on an orbit, so the count is either zero or the whole orbit. The cited full-CM height/type formula therefore gives the asserted slopes and one-dimensional heights. There is no unsupported assumption that an inert orbit is stable under complex conjugation.

The source premise was checked directly. `HostRaw.K` is the field generated by the square root, and `HostRaw.cmType` selects its specified complex value. `HasType` specifies the two embedding multiplicities; `CMObj.typeP/typeM` impose their multiplicity-one induced versions. `IsTen` is a Serre-tensor datum, not itself an existence theorem. The proof correctly does not infer these CM types from definiteness of a unitary group.

For the explicit Jacobian, the calculations `S+T=-1`, `ST=2`, and `(S-T)^2=-7` correctly identify the quadratic embedding classes. Multiplication by a Galois element preserves or exchanges the two classes, so it cannot change the unequal type counts into an induced type. At 11 the quadratic field splits. In `primeClass`, an earlier exceptional branch may assign a ramified class rather than `splitK`; nevertheless no such branch produces either of the two classes in `InertK`. The main proof claims only exclusion from `InertK`, so it remains correct.

The multiplicity lists for the specified action on `J^a x bar(J)^b` are exactly `(a,a,b)` and `(a,b,b)`. They are constant on both classes only when `a=b`. For `a=b>0`, `CMObj.onto` is surjectivity of the product of the prescribed CM-linear maps on complex points. The maps are algebraic homomorphisms. Properness makes their image closed; surjectivity on complex points gives a surjective homomorphism, and the type dimensions make source and target equidimensional. It is therefore an isogeny. The source blocks are ordinary at 11, while the target has only slopes 1/3 and 2/3, so the contradiction is valid. The maps in the source are defined over the integral base, and the good-reduction isogeny comparison is legitimate.

**Scope retained:** this checks the fixed CM action and the stated powers/conjugate-product route. It does not rule out combining differently relabeled factors or constructing a different mixed-type theta correspondence. The final sentence of DP25 preserves that distinction.

## DP30: the actual Cartier dual

**Result: PASS, including p=2 and h=2 boundary checks.** Teichmuller averaging is a strict integral coordinate change; the total-degree support condition gives an additive law below `q=p^h`. The duality orientation is correct:

`H[F]^D = G[p]/image(V|G^(p)[p]) = G[p]/G[F]`.

The kernel of V lies in the p-torsion, so the restricted image has order p. The relative-Frobenius coordinate map `z -> x^p` is faithfully flat of rank p and identifies the quotient with the `(h-1)`st Frobenius kernel of the twist. Equality `G[p]=G[F^h]` uses equality of defining ideals; it does **not** assert equality of the p-series and Frobenius as maps. Nor does the proof assert `V=F^(h-1)`.

Every monomial surviving in `k[X,Y]/(X^r,Y^r)` has total degree at most `2r-2<q`, also at p=2. Thus the quotient is genuinely `alpha_(p^(h-1))`. The distribution Hopf algebra, its complete primitive basis, and the Frobenius-semilinear restricted operation follow as stated. Perfectness of k justifies calling its image a k-space of dimension h-2. For h=2 the image dimension is zero and the single basis vector is killed, as required.

The formal connected component has the whole tangent space of H. A nonzero special-fibre tangent vector lifts primitively to its free integral tangent lattice; an existing coordinate differential detects its nonzero restricted p-power. Reduction of the iterated invariant derivation then gives a unit p-th derivative. This establishes a real dual direction, without assuming a toy logarithm or that an actual theta entry uses that direction. The integrated scope paragraph is accurate.

## DP31: normalized splitting, inverse limit and torus

**Result: PASS under the stated unramified scalar hypotheses.** The overall paper assumes k perfect, and the scalar subsection fixes odd p. With `R=W(k)` and `R_n=R/p^n`, the base rings lie within the directly read Zink Theorem 9/103 scope: p is nilpotent and `R_n/pR_n=k` is of finite type over itself. The published Proposition 121 and equation (243) provide the needed injection. Only injectivity is used; surjectivity from Theorem 122 is unnecessary. The identification of the dual display with the connected Cartier dual and the disjoint-slope Hom vanishing are cited background. The parent and formal_bound are refining the latter's exact primary locator.

The integral partial multiplication factors define the biextension without assuming B integral. Their associativity, symmetry and interchange identities hold over K because they are coboundaries there; all entries of those identities are integral, so they descend to R. The chosen orientation of a splitting gives exactly the two displayed equations for I. A trivial biextension class yields a morphism respecting the canonical identity sections, hence a normalized series in `1+ST R_n[[S,T]]`.

The Hom-reduction argument is valid. Once the coordinates of f are divisible by `p^j`, the target p-series sends them into `p^(j+1)` because its linear part is p and every nonlinear term has degree at least two. Division followed by reduction is legitimate for `j<n` in `W(k)/p^n`. Composition with the nonzero height-h p-series is injective on `k[[T]]`, so induction proves zero Hom at every level. This does not improperly infer integral vanishing from rational slopes alone.

For uniqueness, normalized splittings differ by a bihomomorphism J. Its least positive T-degree modulo p cannot satisfy `J([p]S,T)=J(S,T)^p`: substitution preserves a nonzero coefficient on the left, whereas Frobenius raises the least positive T-degree on the right. The higher p-adic steps use the same injective substitution. Thus the normalized splitting at each level is unique, and reductions of successive splittings agree. The coefficientwise inverse limit lies in the claimed integral power-series ring; each coefficient of a product or composition uses only finitely many coefficients, so the defining identities pass to that limit.

In additive coordinates the remaining difference is separately additive and hence `aXY`. The torus rescales both first-variable arguments together and the second-variable arguments inversely; equivariance of the Honda logarithm/formal group makes the pulled-back I another splitting of the **same** integral factors. Uniqueness therefore makes I invariant. Since the character of XY is `zeta^(1-p) != 1`, the coefficient a is zero. No arbitrary choice of trivialization is being averaged or silently assumed invariant.

Finally `sigma/2` is p-integral because p is odd. Its binomial power preserves the integral unit series, and the inverse-logarithm weight bound supplies the full coefficient slope after Legendre's formula. The explicit DP29 example proves optimality of the asymptotic slope, not an unsupported optimal constant claim. The actual dual-pair case has target `End(G)` rather than zero Hom; the paper correctly leaves that multivariate kernel and its geometric realization separate.
