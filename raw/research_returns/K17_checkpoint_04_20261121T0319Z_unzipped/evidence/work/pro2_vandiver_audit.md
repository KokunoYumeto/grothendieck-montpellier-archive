## 1. Arithmetic group and compact orbit basis

### 1.1 The exact arithmetic hypothesis holds

Gras--Jaulent, *Note on 2-rational fields*, arXiv:0805.1168v1, Theorem and Definition 0, pp.1--2, uses

    V_K={x in K^times: x is an l-th power in every K_v for v|l,
                         and v_q(x)=0 mod l for finite q not dividing l}.

For K=Q and l=2, every x in V_Q is ±2^a times a rational square. Its being square in Q_2 forces a even, and -1 is not square in Q_2, so the sign is positive. Hence V_Q=Q^{times2}. The 2-primary root-of-unity groups of Q and Q_2 are both {±1}; their 2-ranks are both1. Thus condition(iv) holds, so Q is2-rational. Theorem2, p.3, explicitly treats the maximal extension unramified at finite primes away from2, permitting real places to complexify, and gives C_2 * Z_2. The proof also explicitly identifies the index-two subgroup over Q(i) as free pro2 of rank2.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.1168 ; web ref `turn772465151view0`. I read the theorem, definitions, and its proof in full; I did not independently reprove the external structural results cited in that proof.

The candidate's basis changes are valid continuous free-product/Nielsen automorphisms. Replacing gamma by c gamma is its own inverse when c is fixed. ### 1.2 The inverse-limit free group has the required universal property

Let X be compact profinite and let F=lim_j F_p(X_j), where X_j ranges over finite continuous quotients and each transition sends a free generator to its image generator. The canonical X->F is continuous. Given a continuous map X->Q to a finite p-group, it factors through some X_j, hence extends continuously to F by projection. For a general pro-p Q, apply this to every finite quotient; uniqueness at finite levels makes the extensions compatible. This gives existence and uniqueness of F->Q. The generators X topologically generate F: every finite quotient of F factors through an appropriate stage and is generated by the image of X. The stage projections separate points by the definition of inverse limit.

For X=Z_2, translation acts continuously through the cofinal finite quotients Z/2^n. The semidirect product F(X) rtimes Z_2 has the universal property of the rank-two free pro2 group: arbitrary h',gamma' in a pro2 group Q give the continuous orbit map x->(gamma')^x h'(gamma')^{-x}. Its free extension is equivariant because equivariance holds on every generator. Consequently it extends to the required semidirect-product homomorphism, uniquely because h_0 and gamma topologically generate. This proves the identification of H with F(X), not just its abelianization. There is no incorrect assumption that an uncountable discrete basis converges to1.

## 2. Magnus construction and separation

The finite-rank mod-p Magnus embedding is explicitly stated in Minac--Pasini--Quadrelli--Tan, *Koszul algebras and quadratic duals in Galois cohomology*, arXiv:1808.01695v3, section5.1, p.21, equation(5.1):

    S -> 1+I(X) in F_p<<X_1,...,X_d>>, x_i ->1+X_i,

is a monomorphism of profinite groups, and F_p[[S]] is the indicated power-series algebra. The text cites Koch sections4.2,7.6 for the standard result. Primary URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.01695 ; refs `turn729991455view0`, `turn729991455view1`. The integral Magnus map exists by the free-group universal property into the pro2 unit group; reduction mod2 is this injection, so the integral map is injective as well.

For compact X, the compatible integral maps at finite partitions yield theta. The coefficient of variable degree r is exactly

    lim_j Z_2[X_j^r] = Z_2[[X^r]].

Indeed finite clopen product partitions are cofinal on compact X^r, and finitely many coordinate partitions have a common refinement on X. Inverse limits commute with the degree product. Thus the target is the stated product of measure modules, with its topology given by finitely many degrees, finite clopen partitions, and coefficient reductions. The (2,I) topology at finite rank is cofinal with these conditions, so no topology was replaced by an inequivalent one.

If theta(g)=1, its image at each finite partition has trivial finite-rank Magnus expansion. Every stage image of g is then1, and stage separation gives g=1. Accordingly a nontrivial element has some nonzero finite degree; choosing the least such degree is legitimate. There is no element of a product module which is nonzero while every component is zero. Large 2-adic divisibility changes coefficients within degrees, and does not invalidate this argument.

## 4. Complete finite-weight construction of substitution

Fix a weight cutoff N. Let R_N be the finite-dimensional quotient of the free associative target by all words of weight>N. Let J_N be its positive-weight ideal. Then J_N^{d}=0 for d>N/3. The continuous map x->Y_x=rho(h_x)-1 takes values in J_N.

For d<=N/3 and each target word w of weight<=N, the coefficient of w in Y_{x_1}...Y_{x_d} is a continuous Q_2-valued function f_{w,d} on compact X^d. It is bounded. For mu_d in Z_2[[X^d]], define that coefficient of Eval_N(mu_d) to be integral f_{w,d} dmu_d. This integral exists and depends continuously on mu_d: uniformly approximate f_{w,d} by locally constant functions, whose integrals are finite sums of the clopen-set masses. All integral measures have norm at most1, so the approximation error is uniform over all mu_d. There are only finitely many target words and finitely many d at this cutoff.

Summing these finitely many maps defines a continuous linear Eval_N on the entire Magnus algebra. It is multiplicative. For external products of measures, the needed Fubini/product identity holds first for locally constant functions by finite sums, then for continuous functions by uniform approximation. Concatenation matches the target word order, so it is the required algebra homomorphism. The Eval_N are compatible as N increases.

The map Eval_N composed with theta and the actual rho_N are continuous homomorphisms from H to a Hausdorff target and agree on every h_x. Since the h_x topologically generate H, the homomorphisms agree everywhere. This is the clean uniqueness argument; it does not apply the free-pro2 universal property directly to all of U(Q_2), which need not be compact.

Now let r be the first nonzero Magnus degree of g. Lower degrees vanish. Each Y_x has positive generator length, so terms of Magnus degree greater than r cannot contribute to a target word of generator length r. For degree exactly r, only the length-one part of each Y_x contributes. Thus the coefficient of the ordered word s_{m_1}...s_{m_r} is exactly

    (product_j a_{m_j}) integral product_j u^{m_j x_j} dmu_r.

Choose N at least sum_j m_j to read this coefficient. All preceding continuity and finiteness arguments apply separately for that N. Growing denominators in other weights do not enter. The argument retains ordered variables and does not symmetrize the tensor.

## 5. Analytic separation

For integral mu_r, its multivariate Amice transform has coefficients in Z_2, hence converges on the entire open unit polydisc. For u a topological generator of1+4Z_2, the distinct points t_{1+2^j}=u^{1+2^j}-1 converge to u-1 inside4Z_2. For any fixed grid values in all but one variable, the resulting one-variable power series has bounded coefficients and vanishes on an infinite set with that interior accumulation point. On the closed radius1/2 disc it is a restricted analytic function, so the identity theorem gives zero. Equivalently, translate about u-1 and apply the first-nonzero-coefficient argument. Repeating coefficient by coefficient in the remaining variables gives the zero formal multivariate power series.

The Amice transform is injective by the Mahler basis (or the completed group-algebra identification for Z_2^r). Thus mu_r=0, contradicting its selection. This proves injectivity on H. Cyclotomic detection on geometric abelianization then proves injectivity on P. The convention that real places may complexify is essential to identify P correctly, and was retained throughout.

## 6. Soulé index reconciliation requested by the parent

The degreewise equality in `work/vandiver_k.md`, section6, is **only for odd p and odd m>=3**:

    [Z_p(m):kappa_m(G_Q(mu_p^infinity))]
       = #H^2(Z[1/p],Z_p(m))
       = #K_{2m-2}(Z){p}.

It is fully consistent with Ishii Theorem1.1:

1. H^2 is finite, so the index is finite and kappa_m is nonzero.
2. If m=1 mod(p-1), the character component A_p^{[1-m]} is trivial and zero. Hence H^2=0 and kappa_m is surjective.
3. As m runs over odd integers>=3, the exponents1-m run over all even character classes. Thus all full-domain kappa_m are surjective iff A_p^+=0, exactly Vandiver.

The standard product is over all a in (Z/p^n)^times and uses (1-zeta_n^a)^{a^{m-1}}. Kurihara's corestriction definition restricts to precisely this product; no factorial occurs. Choosing different p^n-th radicals changes them by roots of unity fixed by the full cyclotomic-domain group and does not change its Kummer character. Replacing (1-zeta) by (zeta-1) multiplies by powers of -1, whose odd-p Kummer class is zero because -1 is its own p^n-th root. Changing a compatible generator zeta to zeta^b, b in Z_p^times, rescales the scalar-valued character by b^{-m}; this is a unit, and the corresponding tensor-basis change cancels it in Z_p(m).
