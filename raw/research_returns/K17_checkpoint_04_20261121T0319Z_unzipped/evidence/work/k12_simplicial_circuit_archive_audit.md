## Exact statement checked

Let the minimal vectors of a lattice of rank N span its real vector space. Let r be the dimension of the span of the rank-one matrices vv^t associated to those vectors. If r−N≤4, one matrix from each minimal-vector pair is a linearly independent collection. No primitiveness or index-one hypothesis on a set of minimal vectors is needed.

## Independent reconstruction

Assume dependence and choose an inclusion-minimal dependent collection p(v_1),…,p(v_k). Minimality means its linear rank is k−1, the relation space is one-dimensional, and every coefficient of a nonzero relation is nonzero. The relation must have both signs: the trace against the positive-definite norm defining the lattice is strictly positive on every p(v_i), so a nontrivial relation with coefficients of one sign is impossible.

Write the relation as an equality of two positive combinations and let W be the span of all the v_i, of dimension m. For a positive combination Σ a_i v_i v_i^t, its kernel is the common orthogonal complement of its supporting vectors; its image is their span. Equality of the two matrices therefore says that each signed support spans the same space W. Each side has at least m members, giving k≥2m. This step is valid for an arbitrary positive-definite scalar product; no coordinate integrality is used here.

Let σ_W be the cone generated by every minimal-vector ray lying in W and let r_W be its dimension. Projection P onto W-perpendicular gives a nonnegative functional Q↦tr(PQ) on the ambient perfect cone, vanishing exactly on the rays with v∈W. Hence σ_W is a face. Also W is rational, since the circuit consists of lattice vectors, and L∩W is a lattice of rank m. Its restricted norm has the same minimum: it contains the circuit vectors at that minimum, and cannot have a smaller norm than L. Its minimal vectors are exactly the minimal vectors of L in W. Thus σ_W is a genuine perfect cone for L∩W, independently of the face argument. In particular, the cited theorem applies to it even when it lies on the rank boundary of the ambient cone. The circuit implies r_W≥k−1.

Since all minimal vectors span the ambient vector space of dimension N, select N−m of them whose images form a basis modulo W. The induced map Sym²(V)→Sym²(V/W) kills the span of σ_W and sends their rank-one matrices to those of a vector-space basis. Those N−m image matrices are independent (they become the diagonal elementary matrices in that basis). Thus r≥r_W+N−m. This remains true even if the selected vectors have nonzero W components.

Put d=r−N. The last two inequalities give d≥k−1−m≥m−1, so m≤d+1. They also give r_W≤d+m≤2d+1≤9. The low-dimensional perfect-cone theorem makes σ_W simplicial. Finally every distinct minimal-vector pair gives a distinct extremal ray: a positive sum of rank-one positive-semidefinite matrices has rank one only when all summands have the same image; equal minimal lengths then identify their vectors up to sign. The circuit is therefore a dependence among distinct extremal rays of a simplicial cone, a contradiction.

## Consequence and limitations

A projective cell of dimension twelve has cone dimension thirteen. For ambient lattice ranks nine and ten, the respective excesses are four and three, so it has exactly thirteen minimal-vector pairs. This deduction is unaffected by the separate DEKM cone/projective indexing concern, because the two dimensions have been explicitly distinguished here.

This audit does not audit or promote the subsequent glue/index orientation arguments. In particular, simpliciality is not basicness; nontrivial lattice glue can remain. No K12 vanishing follows from this proposition alone.
