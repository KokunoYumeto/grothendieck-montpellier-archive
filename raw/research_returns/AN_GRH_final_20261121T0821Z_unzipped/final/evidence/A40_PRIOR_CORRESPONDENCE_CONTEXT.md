Read the introductory scope, §2 pp.4–5, and §3 pp.5–7. Section2 explicitly works over the complex numbers. Its last paragraph on p.5 describes representing a Jacobian endomorphism by an effective curve correspondence using the Picard functor and a degree-g twist. Section3 assumes number-field data and uses analytic Jacobians; its worked discriminant-five example is a genus-six curve with two degree-two maps. These are useful background/credit for the correspondence construction. They do not establish A40's ordinary characteristic-two irreducibility, generic smoothness, or arbitrary prescribed finite-field image assertion. No claim of having read the full paper or surveyed all prior literature is made.
