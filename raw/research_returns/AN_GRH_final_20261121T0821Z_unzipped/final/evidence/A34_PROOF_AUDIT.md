## Criterion and scope

For a genus-two curve in characteristic two, a geometrically connected constant cyclic etale pointed cover of odd degree n has whole Prym B. Let M be the exponent of B(k)[2-infinity] over the stated finite field. The inequality n > (2M-1)^2 separates the prescribed rational point section from every distinct rational point. There is no p-rank restriction and no bielliptic exception. The exponent must be measured again after any constant extension.

The final corollary realizes M = 2 and n = 19, so this sufficient test strictly extends the rational-unit test for the *same specified cover and field*. The statement does not claim that all other covers of that pointed curve fail the unit test or that a uniform supply of qualifying covers exists.

## Complete proof check

1. A hypothetical collision produces a rational two-primary divisor class delta on the chosen cover. The connected image of sigma-1 is exactly B: the integral group-ring identity expressing n minus the norm as a multiple of sigma-1, and surjectivity of multiplication by n, prove the reverse inclusion. If t = (sigma-1)delta has exact order m, then m divides M and a principal divisor m(sigma Q' + P' - Q' - sigma P') gives a degree-2m function. Exactness of m rules out a square; for m = 1 the simple poles do so. The function is therefore separable.
2. The field generated by its deck translates satisfies the cited controlled-field bound e divides 2m and genus at most (2m/e - 1)^2. The cover genus n+1 and the strict numerical assumption exclude e=1. Every intermediate extension is separable. The published induction is cited precisely rather than duplicated.
3. The descended odd deck action on the intermediate curve is faithful because its kernel divides both e and n. It is free because an odd prime stabilizer would partition a fibre divisor of the power-of-two degree e into equally weighted odd-prime orbits. The genus bound and Riemann--Hurwitz force a genus-one quotient curve. Its degree-e map from the original cover has total different 2n.
4. A zero fibre of the descended function contains at most the two positive support points, each with equal f-multiplicity m. If e>2, every local ramification index in that fibre is even and at least two. The local derivative calculation gives different exponent at least that index. Freeness gives n distinct translated fibres, forcing different degree at least ne>2n. Thus e=2.
5. If the two positive points have different images, both ramify and their two disjoint deck orbits contribute at least 4n to the different. Hence they form one unramified fibre. Equivariance makes the two negative points unramified too, and they form one fibre. The resulting identities x=sigma y and y=sigma x contradict the free odd deck action. This is the cyclic four-support argument that removes the elliptic exception in this genus-two situation.

## Exact exponent and limits

When p is primitive modulo the prime degree r, the cyclotomic local ring O = Z_p[zeta_r] is unramified of degree r-1 and acts on the whole Prym. A finite rational p-primary O-module has at most one nonzero cyclic O-summand: each summand contributes r-1 to its killed-by-p dimension, while geometric p-rank is at most the Prym dimension r-1. The norm of the faithful character L-value determines that summand's length. Thus the rational group is O/p^e, where e is the valuation of that L-value, and its exponent is exactly p^e. This uses finite-module structure and p-rank, not an assumed crystalline rank-one comparison.

The integral norm argument proves the uniform Weil bound. The characteristic-two sharpening keeps its exceptional c=-1 case and the Q=2,4 exceptions explicit. In the exceptional case the inverse-character identity forces the canonical detector to vanish. The occupancy calculation then gives r at most (N+1)/2, or (N+1)/3 when N is even.

Substitution of only the coarse Weil exponent bound gives a threshold stronger than the recovered B.3 size criterion whenever the quotient exists. The exact genus-two numerator identity and a2 <= 6Q establish this comparison. The small-field and exceptional cases cannot pass the coarse criterion. The possible improvement therefore comes from the actual exponent; Artin/Hooley alone supplies neither the pointed constant quotient nor that exponent or its field of definition.
