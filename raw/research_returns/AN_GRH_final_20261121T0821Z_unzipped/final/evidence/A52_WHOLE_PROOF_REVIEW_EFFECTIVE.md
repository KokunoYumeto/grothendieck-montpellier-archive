## Integral operator and geometry

The minimal positive coefficient-norm annihilator exists because the given A provides one. Dividing a common prime different from p preserves the annihilation relation on the actual p-primary class, so the coefficient gcd is a power of p. The free and disjoint deck orbits above the two distinct base points make the function degree exactly the coefficient norm, with no cancellation. If that principal function were a pth power, its root would give the strictly smaller integral annihilator B/p; this proves separability without making the invalid converse assertion that every p-divisible principal divisor has a pth-root function.

The field generated by the deck translates is stable and lies in a separable function-field extension. The cited controlled-field theorem gives precisely e dividing d and genus at most (d/e−1)^2. The numerical hypothesis rules out e=1. At an image-point stabilizer, constant local ramification index on the original deck orbit forces coefficient periodicity for every coefficient, including zero coefficients. A nonzero faithful evaluation rules out any such nontrivial period. Consequently both image orbits have full size n, and the deck action on the intermediate curve is faithful.

If the two image orbits met, comparing the integral zero and pole orders would give a negative proportional shift of the coefficient vector. Iteration through the odd order of that shift yields (r_Q^u+r_P^u)B=0 in a torsion-free integral group ring. This is impossible independently of the characteristic. The two image orbits are therefore disjoint.

A fibre through a nonzero coefficient is a zero or pole fibre of the descended function. Every point in it must lie in the original divisor support. The full, disjoint image orbits leave exactly one such point, so it is totally ramified. Equivariance extends this to all 2n points, including positions where a coefficient is zero. Thus e divides every coefficient. Together with minimality this makes e a nontrivial p-power. The freeness argument on arbitrary intermediate fibres is valid with multiplicities: a nontrivial prime-order stabilizer would partition their degree-e fibre divisors into equally weighted orbits and make that prime divide e. Hence the odd prime-to-p deck group is free on the intermediate curve.

The genus congruence for that free action, together with genus less than n, forces both intermediate quotient curves to have genus one. At each totally ramified point, the different exponent is at least e because p divides e and the first nonzero derivative term occurs after the leading term. Riemann–Hurwitz gives e≤g−1. Finally, faithfulness gives K=F K^G inside K/K^G, so the asserted fibre product and degree-e map descend at the function-field level. The etale base change preserves the claimed total ramification. The obstruction and its exclusion when 2≤g≤p follow exactly.

## Fixed-field lattice construction

The order-growth computation gives f_r=t ell^(r−s), and the subgroup generated by the deck multiplier contains the full high-level reduction kernel. Thus the fixed number field is the same E at every r≥s. Integral fixed rings equal the rings of integers of these fixed fields, and the flat tensor product with Z_p preserves the kernel defining invariants. This verifies the identification R=O_E tensor Z_p, including product rings and even deck-field degrees.

The actual rational primitive torsion group is stable under this invariant ring. Its annihilator is open. Density of O_E in its p-adic completion gives a surjection to the finite quotient R/Ann_R(N_r), so the intersection ideal has exactly index W_r. The (L+1)^h lattice points with L=floor(W_r^(1/h)) are more numerous than that quotient, including the case W_r=1. Two points in one coset give a nonzero algebraic integer c with coefficient norm at most C_E L after the fixed low-level polynomial substitution. The norm can only decrease when reducing the resulting group-ring representative. Because E is a field, a nonzero c has nonzero faithful complex evaluation.

The factor 1−sigma^(ell^(r−1)) vanishes on every proper conductor. Its product with c kills the actual primitive component by the annihilator choice and kills every other component by that conductor factor. Therefore it kills the entire actual collision class. It retains nonzero faithful evaluation. The alternative scalar M_r gives the stated minimum and the exact sufficient inequality. No arbitrary isogenous representative or freely chosen module is substituted for the actual p-primary group.

For R a product of unramified local rings, the component annihilator ideals are p^(e_v), so W_r=p^(sum h_v e_v) and M_r=p^(max e_v), as stated. The fully transitive case E=Q reduces exactly to the earlier bound.
