\paragraph{Original Lidskii endnote: the primary-source gate is closed.}
The original Russian article has now been read in its 1959 issue,
not merely through the later English translation.
On printed p.~487 Lidskii attributes the genus-zero property under
analogous operator hypotheses to H.~Weyl, by a different method based
on the results of his reference~11 (Weyl, 1949).
The attached footnote points to reference~12, explicitly
Grothendieck's \emph{Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society}
no.~16 (1955); in translation, it says that an indication of this occurs
there. Lidskii says he learned these facts after preparing his work
for press. The same page records receipt on 23~November~1958.
\cite[p.~487, final discussion, footnote and references 11--12]{algLidskii1959}
This is contemporary evidence that the memoir contained the indicated
claim before the inspected 1966 printing. It neither states that
Grothendieck printed a proof nor establishes the correctness of the
attribution to Weyl. The exact text of the first printing remains
uncollated; that narrower printing question must be kept separate.

\paragraph{The metric \emph{R\'esum\'e}: a distinct source checked.}
The official 1996 facsimile reprint retains the original pagination.
In \S3, no.~6, p.~53, the numbers $\rho_i(u)$ are the eigenvalues
of $(u^*u)^{1/2}$. Theorem~5 on p.~54 gives the eigenvalue
summability criterion for \emph{Hermitian} $u$, and treats general
$u$ by passing to $(u^*u)^{1/2}$. Its displayed sum is the nuclear
norm. The following Hilbert--Schmidt identities and the final
note~8~bis, p.~77, concern singular values. That note refers to
Weyl's convexity method and an unwritten S\~ao Paulo seminar of 1954.
These passages provide no genus-zero determinant or trace-eigenvalue
proof for arbitrary nonnormal trace-class operators.
\cite[\S3, no.~6, pp.~53--56; final note 8~bis, p.~77]{algMetricResumeFacsimile}
The complete modern transcription was searched and its theorem
architecture checked; no determinant/genus-zero passage was found.
The original relevant pages, introduction, contents and final notes
were then visually collated. This records the actual scope of the
inspection, without claiming that every facsimile page was read line by line.
