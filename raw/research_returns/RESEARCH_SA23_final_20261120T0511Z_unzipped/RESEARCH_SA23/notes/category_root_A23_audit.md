## A.3: cotangent calculation and unbounded complexes

Reviewed `sections/root_A3.tex`, Lemmas SA23-A-14, A-15, A-18, A-19 and Theorem A-16 in full.

1. **Projectivity on each jet.** A derivation into a module killed by J^n kills J^(n+1), so the square-zero test map is continuous. The lifted first coordinate is forced by the map of trivial extensions. Thus the projectivity of P_n is for arbitrary B_n-modules and is not a finite-generation argument.
2. **Localization and completions.** For q containing J, a maximal-adically continuous map on B_q restricts to a J-adically continuous map on B. A lift sends denominators to units and extends to B_q. Its continuity follows from squaring the ideal killed by the reduction. Completing the local source and target is permitted by Stacks15.38.4, opened directly at https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07ED . Stacks15.50.2, opened at https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07PK , is explicitly for complete noetherian local rings and equates the required topological formal smoothness with regularity. It has no finite-residue or finite-type restriction.
3. **Relative completion has zero residue fibre.** Noetherian completion is flat. Base change from T→T-hat along T→κ gives κ→κ, so L_(T-hat/T) has zero derived residue fibre. Applying transitivity to source and target completions therefore gives precisely the fibre identification displayed in A-14. This avoids asserting that an arbitrary uncompleted local map is regular.
4. **The regular-map input has the claimed scope.** Read the downloaded Blickle–Fink LaTeX source around Theorem2.10 (`sources/blickle_fink_latex/maindocument.tex`, lines309ff). The statement is for arbitrary maps of noetherian rings; the F-finite restriction begins later. Its cotangent complex is the ordinary differential module in degree zero and is flat.
5. **No unbounded Nakayama substitution.** Because P_n is projective, the lift P_n[0]→E_n exists and induces the identity on H^0. Derived tensor with a residue field has H^0 equal to P_n⊗κ, so the map is an isomorphism on that fibre. Read Neeman's actual scan, printedpp526–527: Theorem2.8 and Lemma2.12 apply to D(R) for a commutative noetherian ring, including unbounded complexes. They justify the vanishing of the cone without a pseudo-coherence assumption.
6. **Complete coefficients.** N is finite over the complete noetherian adic ring, so N=lim N/J^nN and the surjective tower computes its derived limit. Derived Hom preserves these derived limits. The Hom tower is surjective because a derivation at level n is a B_(n+1)-linear map from P_(n+1) into the quotient and P_(n+1) is projective. Thus the Mittag–Leffler step is valid for arbitrary rank P_n.

## A.3: the lift and the two module conditions

7. **Flatness is established in the correct order.** Completeness places J in the Jacobson radical; the local formal-smoothness/flatness result applies at every maximal ideal, and this implies A_0-flatness of B_0. I is finite over A_0 because A is noetherian. Hence N=I⊗B_0 is finite over B_0 before the obstruction calculation. Stacks92.16.1, opened at https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08SM , has exactly the prescribed square-zero base extension and prescribed map I→N used here.
8. **Noetherianity and completeness are not circular.** The constructed extension has kernel N=IB and the natural I⊗_A B→B is injective. Together with B_0-flatness this gives A-flatness by the low-degree Tor sequence. Ideals of B are finitely generated using their images in B_0 and their intersections with N, so B is noetherian before Artin–Rees and exact completion are invoked. Completing its finite-module extension sequence has the already complete endpoints N and B_0, and therefore identifies B with its completion.
9. **Formal smoothness of the result.** Flat base change identifies L_(B/A)⊗^L B_0 with L_(B_0/A_0). A-14 therefore annihilates the relevant Ext^1 for IM and M/IM, and then for every module M killed by a power of the lifted ideal. A continuous square-zero test map produces exactly such a kernel. Vanishing splits the pullback extension, and the lifted map kills the square of the previously killed ideal.
10. **Weak topological formal projectivity.** The colimit Ext criterion is valid because filtered colimits of module groups are exact and injective coefficient modules efface higher Ext. The Tor term is (IB∩Q^n)/(IQ^n); Artin–Rees makes this inverse system pro-zero with uniform shifts. The change-of-rings exact sequence then proves the required colimit Ext^1 vanishing first for A_0-modules and subsequently for arbitrary A-modules.
11. **Strong projective-quotient condition.** The nested quotients split, giving the topological product of discrete projective D_(0,n). Each such projective lifts across I by a column-finite idempotent matrix, even at infinite rank: δ=u²−u has square zero and e=u+(1−2u)δ is idempotent. Finite generation of I is exactly what ensures I∏D_n=∏ID_n. The resulting product-to-B map is an algebraic isomorphism by flatness of B and nilpotence, without invoking finite Nakayama. The separate Artin–Rees argument indeed proves inverse continuity; it does not assume an open mapping theorem.
12. **Uniqueness.** A-15 annihilates the extension-class torsor, while automorphisms remain the stated derivation group. Any flat lift with specified reduction canonically identifies its kernel with I⊗B_0, so the classification applies to every lift in the theorem. Continuity follows from preserving the lifted ideal or the displayed derivation estimate.

## A.2: infinite absolute rank with finite relative rank

Reviewed `sections/root_A12.tex`, Lemmas SA23-A-06 and A-07, Theorem A-08, Corollary A-09, focusing on the branch singled out by root.

13. The K^p-linear span of k is the field kK^p: it is a subring, and a nonzero element a has inverse a^(p−1)/a^p with denominator in K^p. Infinite [kK^p:K^p] therefore supplies a countable K^p-linearly independent sequence lying in k itself.
14. For F=K((t)), F^p=K^p((t^p)). The compositum KF^p is the union of E_0((t^p)) over finite intermediate E_0/K^p: each compositum expression uses finitely many constants, and a finite coefficient-field basis expands every Laurent series into finitely many F^p-series. No infinite coefficient expansion is used.
15. Hence f=Σa_i t^(pi) cannot belong to KF^p and therefore cannot belong to kF^p. The proof of ker(d:F→Ω_(F/k))=kF^p by an exponent-one p-basis is valid at arbitrary cardinality. Thus df is nonzero, while the finite tail identities put it in every t-power of Ω. This correctly obstructs projectivity.
16. If Ω_(K/k) has finite dimension, the relative exponent-one p-basis is finite, so [K:kK^p] is finite. Infinite absolute [K:K^p] then forces infinite [kK^p:K^p], exactly the preceding obstruction. The alternative infinite-relative branch's coordinate argument also checks: its derivations are continuous into the separated target A because they kill t, which is sufficient for the displayed series calculation.
17. The positive direction uses finite absolute p-rank at its essential point. Separability preserves a p-basis of k and allows its finite extension to one of K. Only finitely many Frobenius monomial components are assembled into power series. The tailored lift A^p→C is well defined because the pth power of a square-zero element is zero, and the resulting finite p-basis presentation gives the required lift. The universal differential calculation is then valid.

The conclusion is exactly the stated discrete criterion: K/k separable and finite absolute p-rank. The same-field imperfect infinite-p-rank counterexample is within that scope and does not confuse discrete and adic formal smoothness.
