## A.3: labels SA23-A-14 through SA23-A-19

The complete written proof in sections/root_A3.tex is valid, with the
scope condition about the absent manuscript definition of P retained.

- **A14.** Continuous lifting into square-zero algebras over B/J^n
  proves projectivity of the differential module after this reduction.
  The local completed maps are formally smooth, and the opened Stacks
  tag 07PK, Proposition 15.50.2, applies to these complete local rings.
  Cotangent complexes of the completion maps vanish after tensoring
  with the residue field by flat base change. The resulting residue
  field calculation, splitting of projective H^0, and the cited Neeman
  residue-field detection lemma give the asserted projective complex.
- **A15.** A finite module over a complete Noetherian adic ring is
  complete. Derived Hom preserves the derived inverse limit. The
  degree-zero Hom transitions are surjective because the projective
  differential module at level n+1 maps onto the coefficient quotient
  at level n. Thus no lim^1 term remains.
- **A16.** The obstruction module I tensor B0 is finite. Stacks 08SM,
  Lemma 92.16.1, is precisely the square-zero lifting obstruction.
  The vanishing proved before applies. The identification of IB with
  that tensor module gives the Tor1 vanishing needed to lift flatness.
  Filtering arbitrary A-modules by IM and M/IM completes the flatness
  argument. Noetherianity follows from the finite square-zero ideal.
  Completion and Artin--Rees give the exact comparison with the
  completed extension. The formal smoothness argument uses discrete
  J-torsion coefficients; its square-zero pullback kernel is such a
  coefficient module. A splitting is continuous after squaring an
  open ideal, as the proof says.
- **A16, uniqueness paragraph.** The same Ext1 vanishing yields uniqueness with the chosen
  reduction. A-linear isomorphisms inducing that reduction automatically
  induce the specified map on IB. Continuity follows from the adic
  topologies. Automorphisms are the indicated derivations.
- **A17.** The distinction between the complete-local and general adic
  hypotheses is correct. The Perez Rodriguez source was opened by root;
  this audit does not claim a separate reading of that article.
- **A18.** The continuous Hom criterion is equivalent to the vanishing
  of colim Ext1, by embedding a discrete module in an injective module.
  The displayed change-of-rings edge sequence has the required
  direction. Tor1 is (IB cap Q^n)/(I Q^n); Artin--Rees makes it a
  pro-zero system. Filtering coefficients by IM and M/IM proves
  ascent of weak formal projectivity.
- **A19.** A countable complete strictly formally projective module is
  a product of the split projective successive kernels. Choose V0=B0;
  this makes the tail indexing exact. Arbitrary projectives lift
  through a square-zero ideal using the idempotent matrix correction,
  which remains meaningful for column-finite matrices. Summation
  converges, finite generation of I commutes with products, and the
  reduced map is an isomorphism. Nilpotence gives surjectivity and
  flatness gives vanishing of its kernel. The Artin--Rees argument
  establishing inverse continuity was checked term by term and is
  valid.

## A.4: labels SA23-A-10 through SA23-A-12

The explicit cusp construction in sections/root_A4.tex is valid.

- For D=k[z_i^2,z_i^3], each nonzero element belongs to only finitely
  many coordinate cusp primes. Choosing a fresh coordinate proves
  the prime-avoidance identity used in the localization argument.
  The described complement is multiplicatively closed.
- Each retained coordinate prime becomes maximal. The localized
  local ring is a one-dimensional cusp over the fraction field
  generated by the other coordinates.
- Finite character and the finite generation of ideals in those
  local rings prove global Noetherianity by the finite patching
  argument in the text. The proof does not merely assume
  Noetherianity from local Noetherianity.
- The regular/formally smooth fiber locus consists of the generic
  point and is not open. The field and local cusp assertions have
  the required characteristic and topology distinctions.
- For Frobenius, every original syzygy of (z^2,z^3) has coefficients
  in the maximal ideal. After Frobenius its coefficients still lie
  there, whereas (z^p,-1) is a new syzygy of (z^{2p},z^{3p}).
  Its unit coefficient proves nonflatness, including p=2.
