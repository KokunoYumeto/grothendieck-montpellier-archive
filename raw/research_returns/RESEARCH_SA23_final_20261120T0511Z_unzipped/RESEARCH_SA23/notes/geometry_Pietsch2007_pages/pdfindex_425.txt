An entire function d(ζ,T) is called a Fredholm denominator or Fredholm
divisor if its zeros are just the poles of the Fredholm resolvent. Furthermore, the
multiplicity of every zero ζ0 is supposed to coincide with the algebraic multiplicity of
the eigenvalue λ0 =1/ζ0. In this way, d(ζ,T) is uniquely determined up to a factor
e f(ζ), where f denotes an entire function.
The corresponding function D(ζ,T) is said to be a Fredholm numerator or a
Fredholm minor (of ﬁrst order).
