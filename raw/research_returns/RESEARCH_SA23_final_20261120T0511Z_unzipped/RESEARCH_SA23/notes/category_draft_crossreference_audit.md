## The concrete textual match

At physical 64, near the top, the manuscript says that the coefficients k_n
occurring in **Chapter I, §2, no 2, Proposition 1** equal 1 for a Hilbert space.
Its preceding argument uses the Hilbert determinant/exterior-power estimate;
the following argument treats the norm of mixed exterior products of bounded
operators. This is a specific mathematical and notational match with the
published Fredholm construction.

In the published paper, k_n is defined by a determinant supremum in **Chapter
II, §2, Proposition 1**, p346, equation (1 bis). Its Hilbert value k_n=1 is
stated on **p348**, immediately after the coefficient convergence estimates.
Thus the manuscript's reference does not preserve the published chapter and
subdivision numbering. The common k_n and mathematical role identify a
counterpart in the publication; they do not identify a dated physical draft
or prove that this copy was the 1952 first draft or 1953 accepted manuscript.

## What the dated publication notes say

- Fredholm p319 footnote 1 dates the first drafting of **that exposition** to
  1952 and reports acceptance of the definitive manuscript by *Summa
  Brasiliensis Mathematicae* in 1953. It explains its later withdrawal and
  publication in BSMF. The introduction describes the eventual three-chapter
  organization: algebra, Banach-space Fredholm theory, integral-operator
  applications. Neither the footnote nor that outline identifies an enclosure,
  manuscript leaf, Montpellier dossier, or a particular discarded Hilbert
  chapter.
- Memoir I Appendix pp194–195 identifies the article *La théorie de Fredholm*
  as its reference [11], then still announced for *Summa*. It explains that
  certain fine Hilbert-space compact-operator eigenvalue results were
  originally intended for that article but removed after Grothendieck found
  them essentially in Weyl's 1949 paper. It explicitly acknowledges that the
  earlier references consequently became inadequate. The note names no
  chapter or proposition of the removed material and does not describe a
  physical enclosure or manuscript that can be uniquely matched to cote 1.

## Exact historical consequence and remaining witness

These primary passages jointly support a **contextual inference** that the
cote 1 Hilbert draft belongs to the same mathematical project as the article
whose drafting Grothendieck dated to 1952 and submission to 1953. The explicit
k_n cross-reference makes that inference more specific than a topic match.
It still supplies **no proved upper date for the particular genus-zero/trace
sheets**, including physical pages 79–80. An older numbering scheme can be
retained in a later copy, and the publication notes do not identify this copy.
Nor does the omission note assert that every result on these leaves formed
part of the accepted 1953 manuscript.

The smallest remaining historical gate is a dated witness identifying these
particular leaves or their specific genus-zero argument: for example an
explicitly identified dated enclosure, a dated citation to this exact draft,
or an archival description with an evidenced leaf-level identification. No
such witness is supplied by the inspected passages. This does not refute the
prompt's cautious possibility that the manuscript may predate later
published proofs, and it is separate from the proved mathematical
reconstruction from the 1956 published foundations.
