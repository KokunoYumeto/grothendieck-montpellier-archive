# SGA6: exact mathematical identification and historical limit

Primary sources opened:

1. Original EGA IV-4, 1967, 19.1.8, printed p.189, physical PDF page188,
   https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_1967__32__5_0.pdf.
2. Original SGA6, 1971, Exposé VII by Pierre Berthelot, printed
   pp.416–423, physical PDF pages423–430,
   https://library.slmath.org/nonmsri/sga/sga/pdf/sga6.pdf.
   Chapter DOI 10.1007/BFb0066291; volume DOI 10.1007/BFb0066283.
3. Original https://grothendieck.umontpellier.fr/23.pdf, physical
   PDF pp.53–54, archive footer52–53, visually checked. Neither PS
   page has a date. Physical52 is a dossier cover listing Deligne
   letters of17 and29October1966; that is not a date for the PS.

The mathematical correspondence is exact: VII1.1 calls a finite
projective linear map regular when its Koszul complex is acyclic in
positive degrees; VII1.4 defines the corresponding ideal and immersion
locally. VII1.3 is the quasi-regularity conclusion, VII1.4.2 is invariance
under minimal generators, VII1.5 is flat base change and fpqc descent,
and VII1.7(i)–(ii) gives composition and the split-conormal converse.
VII1.7(iii) explicitly retains Noetherianity for the other converse.
These are the precise statements proved or refuted in C2–C6.

EGA IV19.1.8 is present in the original final printing, exactly as the
PS proposes. It names the positive-homology condition and refers the
reader to Grothendieck's 1966 algebraic-geometry seminar. Thus the
proposed explanatory remark was incorporated into the final EGA text.

The stronger lead in the supplied reading, “This is the origin of the
Koszul-regular immersions of SGA6 VII,” has two distinct readings.
If it means the first instant at which the notion and results were
invented, the original PS contradicts that reading: it describes
ongoing Riemann–Roch seminar work and says Berthelot had already
verified one transitivity. The PS is a record of that development.
It also explicitly proposes the EGA19.1.8 note and refers to SGA6
as a planned publication for late1967 or early1968; these are direct
positive links to the eventual texts.

If the lead instead asserts that this document caused adoption of
the eventual VII definitions, those retrospective words neither prove
nor disprove it. The original VII opening has the same motivation
but does not identify this PS as its source, and it has no dated
lecture header. The June1971 Preface says the volume largely
reproduces the earlier IHES multigraph edition; it does not give
a chronology of the invention of the regular-immersion definition.

**Smallest remaining documentary gate:** a dated source explicitly
connecting the drafting or adoption of VII1.1/1.4 to this PS, if a
causal-document claim is wanted. No gate is needed for the mathematical
correspondence or the refutation of a literal first-invention moment.
The safe historical description is: the PS records the development
of the Koszul-regular notion used in SGA6VII and proposes the EGA
explanatory remark that subsequently appears in print.
