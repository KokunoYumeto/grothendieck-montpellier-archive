## Split coefficients, weights and the Boolean structure

For a general G(R)-semimodule, the sign-fixed part is the disjoint union of the2-torsion in its amplitude fibres. It equals the Boolean skeleton only when those fibres have no2-torsion. The fixed scheme of sign on affine d-space is Spec Z[x_i]/(2x_i), with the full affine space in characteristic two. The rank-one phenomenon is already in the corrected SGA3 I Remark4.7.1.2, page29; the application to split coefficients is the new point here.

Additive linearization kills an idempotent support monoid. Inclusion–exclusion valuations form a different construction, with a free group on the join-irreducibles of a finite distributive lattice. Keeping those operations distinct repairs the proposed support linearizations and their K0 interpretations. SGA6 IV equation(1.5.2) and Definition2.2 provide the exact Grothendieck-group framework; the supplied cote2 scan17 transcription records the tensor/restriction operations, and cote43 scan37 records Witt operations.

Arbitrarily different bounded distributive support lattices can share the same classical Dedekind zeta sheet. Its RH therefore cannot reconstruct that support geometry or produce a satisfiability algorithm. Likewise, a Frobenius action on the Boolean skeleton does not fix the amplitude's Tate weight. The literal infinite analytic zero sheaf and the product/direct-sum tail do not meet the finite constructible Weil-object requirements.

The representation cone and finite-set cone are different. For C2, the effective finite-set cone maps to a+b epsilon with a≥b≥0; the effective representation cone only requires a,b≥0. The regular representation1+epsilon is not itself idempotent. It maps to the supported zero under a quotient of the effective representation semiring; no unital map from the whole representation ring to G(Z) is asserted. These distinctions are relevant to cote133 scan19 and to its known subset/exterior-power correction. They prevent an effective signed lift from silently becoming a literal point-count formula.
