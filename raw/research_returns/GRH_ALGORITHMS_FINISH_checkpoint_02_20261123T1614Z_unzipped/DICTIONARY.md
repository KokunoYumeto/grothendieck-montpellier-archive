# Algebraic-geometric dictionary and proof of each translation

This dictionary translates the results rather than replacing their proofs by analogies. The baseline records report reading the following SGA locators; this finishing run does not assert a new inspection of those archive editions: SGA1, Exposé V, Theorem4.1 and §7; SGA4, Exposé IX, Theorems3.2–3.3; SGA6, Exposé0, Appendix (Grothendieck1957 report), ChapterI, §2, Example1, printedpp24–25. No archive cote is asserted without the corresponding sheet being inspected. The older paper cites cotes28 and98, but that citation alone is not a new inspection of their sheets.

## Finite fields, components, and algorithms

A squarefree polynomial over a finite field gives a finite étale scheme. SGA1 V4.1 and V§7 identify finite étale covers with finite sets carrying the fundamental-group action; over a finite field the action is generated by Frobenius. Connectedness is therefore transitivity of Frobenius. This carries the Gaussian-period proof exactly: primitive ell-th roots form the set G=(Z/ell)^*, taking H-invariants gives functions on G/H, and multiplication by p is an r-cycle precisely when p is not in H. Its invariant algebra is consequently a field of degree r. Linear equations compute the invariants, so the geometric translation preserves the stated complexity.

For A=Fq[X]/g, the absolute-Frobenius fixed algebra B=ker(Frob_p−1) is Fp^t, one scalar for each connected component. Indeed CRT decomposes A into t finite fields, and the fixed elements of pth powering in each are precisely Fp. Idempotents in B and A agree. Selecting a nontrivial idempotent is exactly selecting a proper nonempty open-and-closed subscheme, which computes a factor by gcd. This identifies Tang's reduction and the earlier quasipolynomial route with a concrete component computation; it does not turn an existence theorem for components into an efficient algorithm. The number of oracle calls is bounded by internal vertices of the splitting tree, at most n−1.

The zero-divisor proposition starts from an automorphism of a constant finite étale scheme. Its moved-point ideal isolates the moved subset. When every orbit has prime size r, the eigenprojection has nonzero image on each orbit. Patching the identities of the image ideals produces a globally invertible eigenvector. The final annihilator idempotent is invariant under the cyclotomic group, so its open-and-closed subset descends to the original scheme. This is the proof of the descent statement; it does not require averaging by r−1 in the ground field.

## Kummer torsors and the missing unit

For X=Spec(D) and r invertible on X, SGA4 IX3.2 gives the étale Kummer sequence 1→mu_r→G_m→G_m→1. Since D is a product of fields, Pic(X)=0: an invertible module is free of rank1 on every component, and their chosen bases assemble to a global basis. SGA4 IX3.3 and the cohomology sequence therefore identify H¹_et(X,mu_r) with D*/(D*)^r. The equation Y^r=eta represents its torsor. A maximal r-primary unit is a nontrivial class on every component, so the torsor gives a degree-r field extension there. The Teichmuller equivariance extends the cyclotomic action to the torsor; its quotient has r geometric points and is connected, giving the desired degree-r field. Conversely a presented field provides the unit by its Frobenius eigenvector and multiplicative projection. Thus the torsor language proves precisely the same computational equivalence; it does not produce the class from the order of the desired field.

For a presented extension and r dividing p−1, the nonresidue theorem constructs such a nontrivial Kummer class by an explicit Frobenius equation. The quadratic even-degree case constructs it without A, including the p=3 mod4 case via the norm-one group of the quadratic subfield. The norm calculation certifies the failure of the torsor to have a section. In characteristic r, the field-construction tower instead uses the additive Artin–Schreier equation; its linear map has a one-dimensional kernel and codimension-one image. These are different torsors and the characteristic restriction is essential.

For a factor-count witness, equal-degree factorization gives a finite étale scheme with n components of common degree d. The component count prime to r is exactly what prevents the relative norm of the unit eigenvector from annihilating its nontrivial Kummer class. The resulting norm character is a primitive rth root. This explains why an explicit selected cyclotomic factor with suitable multiplicity suffices, while the complete cyclotomic polynomial can fail the multiplicity condition.

The unit group of a product of fields also agrees with its K1: determinants identify each field's stable general linear group modulo elementary matrices with its multiplicative group, and products commute with this construction. This is a K1 statement, not the K0 lambda-ring assertion in SGA6. No SGA6 theorem about K1 is claimed.

## Arithmetic covers, Artin representations, and lambda operations

After removing the primes p and r from Spec(Z), the normal closure of X^r−p gives a finite étale cover with group G=F_r semidirect F_r*. The pure field and cyclotomic field correspond to the permutation representations P and Q0 used in the paper. Equality reg_G=(r−1)(P−1)+Q0 in the representation Grothendieck group gives equality of every unramified Euler factor. Equality after taking inertia invariants gives the ramified factors too. This carries the zeta identity into the language of zero-dimensional Artin motives. The induced order-r character over the cyclotomic field is the representation P−1 over Q: its rank-one origin over a different base does not make it a rational Dirichlet character.

SGA6 Exposé0 Appendix, ChapterI §2 Example1, defines tensor products and exterior powers on representation classes. In that lambda-ring, the subring generated by one-dimensional characters of G factors through G/[G,G]. Tensor products, exterior powers, duals, sums and differences preserve trivial action of the commutator subgroup. Here [G,G] is the translation group. Every class in that subring has the same character value at the identity and at a nonzero translation. Therefore it cannot give the auxiliary-prime indicator. This is a rigorous restriction on these operations, not a proof that all possible geometric constructions or all consequences of A are insufficient.

The virtual class Q0+1−P has dimension zero and character r on nonzero translations and zero elsewhere. It is a virtual class, not a nonzero effective representation of dimension zero. Its local logarithmic-derivative coefficients are nonnegative because they are averages of that character over inertia. The smoothed window criterion thus has an exact Artin-representation interpretation. Its missing fixed gap below2 is an analytic estimate on closed points at a prescribed finite scale, and is not supplied by the formal representation identity.

## Cyclotomic restrictions and symmetry

Primitive mth roots give a finite étale scheme whose Frobenius orbits have length ord_m(p). Counting its components yields phi(m)/ord_m(p). The r-adic valuation proof in the paper shows that a complete product with the required factor count already contains a conductor with a good auxiliary prime. This is a statement about the actual orbit sizes, not merely about the total degree of a cover.

For r-power cyclotomy the geometric points form a regular cyclic Delta-set. A Delta-stable subalgebra corresponds to a translation-invariant partition of that set. The block through1 is a subgroup H, hence the algebra is the algebra of functions on Delta/H. The r-adic order calculation then proves the dimension restriction and the component-multiplicity obstruction. A selected proper factor corresponds to a Frobenius-stable subset that breaks the full Delta symmetry, so it is not covered by this restriction.

## Class groups, solution points, and weights

For Spec(O_K), the ideal class group is Pic: invertible ideals correspond to rank-one projective modules, and principal ideals correspond to free modules. Bounds for prime-ideal class-group generators therefore bound divisor representatives for Pic, while class-character Hecke functions detect its nontrivial characters. This translation does not supply relations or a runtime for a whole class-group algorithm. The elliptic isogeny applications use the analogous ideal-class actions on CM objects; the table deliberately audits the named mixing and split-prime stages.

A single algebraic solution of a polynomial system is a closed point with residue field K. After inverting its denominators and discriminant, degree-one primes of K give points in reductions. Counting those prime ideals needs zeta_K, without a normal closure. This carries the exact Koiran input to arithmetic geometry and explains why a nonnormal cubic solution field is a valid obstruction to replacing every input by an abelian field.

For a zero-dimensional finite étale scheme over a finite field, Frobenius on H⁰ permutes a finite basis; its eigenvalues are roots of unity and have absolute value1. This is the elementary weight-zero statement. It does not prove the location of complex zeros of a number-field zeta function: even the trivial Artin representation has Riemann zeta as its global L-function. No finite-field purity theorem is being used as a proof of number-field GRH.

## Elliptic torsion and CM periods

For an elliptic curve over Fp², E[r] is a rank-two finite étale group scheme. The discriminant valuation one forces its Frobenius matrix to be a non-scalar Jordan matrix modulo r: a scalar lift would have characteristic discriminant divisible by r². On nonzero torsion modulo sign, its eigenline orbits have length d and its remaining orbits length rd. The corresponding finite étale x-coordinate scheme therefore has exactly N components in each degree, with r not dividing N. The factor-count norm transfer constructs the extension; its Fp-linear Frobenius fixed space descends the degree-2r presentation to degree r. This is an exact proof in the language of finite étale schemes. Waterhouse's existence of an isogeny class does not construct a curve in polynomial time.

For a quadratic order O, proper ideal classes act simply transitively on the CM j-values. The ring class field makes this a finite étale torsor away from the discriminant. At a split good prime p, ordinary CM reduction preserves distinctness, and Frobenius translates the class group by the prime ideal class g. An index-r quotient A/H on which g is nontrivial is consequently a connected degree-r Frobenius set. To construct its coordinate algebra over Q, take power sums of the CM coordinates over each coset and multiply their orbit linear factors. Translations and complex conjugation preserve that polynomial, so its algebraic-integer coefficients lie in Z. The Vandermonde matrix on the distinct reduced CM coordinates proves that one power sum separates the adjacent cosets, even if p divides their size. This constructs the quotient field without choosing one CM root. The missing input is a polynomially bounded order whose g is nonzero in A/rA; the torsor interpretation does not supply it.

For the ramified conductor r, the exact Picard-group kernel is (O/rO)* / Fr*. Since O/rO is the dual-number algebra Fr[epsilon]/epsilon², this kernel is additive Fr and has order r. A principal generator (t+v sqrt(D))/2 maps nontrivially into it precisely when r does not divide v. Thus the class-polynomial factor count measures this infinitesimal residue class. It is distinct from quadratic splitting in the base field. The exceptional extra units for D=-3 remove this kernel when r=3.

## Restrictions on candidates and analytic positivity

A fixed finite list of cyclotomic elements defines a finite collection of Kummer covers. In their common Galois closure, complex conjugation has square one. Primes with that Frobenius therefore split every relative Kummer cover after the quadratic residue-field extension. Compatibility with the next cyclotomic layer allows every prescribed hard valuation a. Thus the corresponding fixed torsor sections cannot uniformly provide a nontrivial Kummer class; the statement does not apply to an adaptive family growing with p. The analogous finite polynomial-menu proof uses the same Frobenius involution and the exponent of the r-primary part of its finite permutation group.

For global r-units u in Q(zeta_r), the quotient u/conjugate(u) has no finite divisor and absolute value one at all archimedean embeddings, hence is a root of unity. Conjugation acts as minus one in the Teichmuller character, so its projection has order at most r. This translates the unit restriction into the anti-invariant part of K1, rather than a missing class in K0. When the residue degree is even, conjugation is the middle Frobenius power and the same restriction applies to the entire r-primary local unit. No such latter assertion is made for odd residue degree.

In the cubic case, unique factorization in the Eisenstein integers and cubic reciprocity carry a small-height nontrivial Kummer class at p to a small split prime whose Frobenius is nontrivial in the pure cubic cover. Its norm is at most three times the coefficient height squared. This proves equivalence of two quantitative searches; it is not a new consequence of finite-field weights.

The analytic positive-test calculation concerns the global L-function of a dimension-zero virtual Artin motive. Positivity of its local detector coefficients is distinct from positivity of a genuine cohomology representation. The conductor term has the sign opposite to the pole response. Turan's bound on compactly supported positive-definite functions shows that discarding the unknown nonabelian zero sum loses the polylogarithmic prime scale. This is a theorem about that explicit-formula strategy, not a general obstruction to geometric constructions or to Dirichlet GRH implications.

The exact positive detector is the logarithmic derivative of the dimension-zero Artin quotient zeta_K/L. ## Weighted character averages and their scope (GRH-P056–P059, P068–P071)

For a finite set of auxiliary primes, the product of their order-r residue-class torsors is a finite abelian cover of an open subscheme of Spec Z. The indicator of identity Frobenius in every cyclic quotient is the average of all its characters. Its character coefficients have absolute sum one. The weighted explicit formula therefore gives an error proportional to the logarithm of the product modulus, rather than the degree r^m. This is exactly the displayed orthogonality calculation, not an additional geometric estimate. It produces an average statement over the closed points p of Spec Z; it does not select a point above a prescribed exceptional p.

The positive log-tent is a test function on prime powers, not a weight in the cohomological sense. The almost-all bounds give short finite-field presentations for most characteristics, and the simultaneous 12r table gives the two cited algebraic algorithmic interfaces. The cubic Tate menu assigns a distinct ramified conductor prime to every character; restriction to that inertia factor proves character independence. This is an explicit independence argument in the character group of a finite étale cover. It does not assert independence of motives or zero ordinates. No archive cote is added because no stronger Grothendieck-programme implication is used.

## The tensor cone and rational twists (GRH-P060–P063)

In the representation ring of the affine Galois group, every tensor power of the degree-(r−1) summand belongs to the span of that summand and the abelian regular representation. Tensor products are the multiplication in this representation ring; the two supported idempotent class functions are the indicator of identity and of nonidentity translations. Positivity of all squared characters is exactly positivity of their two masses. The Gram determinant proves the complete interval −Psi_K/(r−1)≤Psi_L≤Psi_K. This explicit finite representation calculation does not assert positivity of every virtual K-theory class.

Tensoring by a rank-one rational character is the usual twist of a finite-monodromy representation on an open subscheme of Spec Z. The local commutator proof shows that its ramification contribution at p cannot disappear. Induction and tensor products give the complete compositum zeta identity, including inertia invariants at ramified primes. The quoted analytic zero-density theorem still contains the original conductor; no purity or general correspondence theorem converts it into a uniform bound at logarithmic scale.

## Rational families and residue classes (GRH-P066–P067)

For C in F_(p²)(T), the norm-one cubic cover W³=conjugate(C)/C descends to F_p by W↦1/W. A noncube value makes its fibre have no F_p-point. If the divisor of this rational function is not divisible by three, its smooth compactification is geometrically connected and has bounded genus. Hasse–Weil then forbids the assumed absence of rational points outside finitely many fibres. The remaining divisor condition gives C=c b g³, with b over F_p and c the noncube leading-coefficient ratio. This is a finite-field curve calculation; it uses the already proved weight-one eigenvalue bound and does not derive an effective section from an abstract descent assertion.

For fixed p, CRT and Dirichlet's theorem give both split and connected cubic fibres among prime parameters in every reduced class of modulus coprime to p. The geometric fibre types are distinguished by Frobenius, and a rational congruence condition that omits p does not fix that Frobenius class. This qualitative theorem has no short-parameter conclusion.

## The Möbius family and the excluded domain

Normalize the prime-field starting curve to

    E_K: y^2+Kxy+y=x^3.

Its first descending kernel can be written without an extension-field
cube root. After translating that generator to the origin, the
quotient's remaining cubic has Kummer radicand

    b_0(t)=(zeta-zeta^2)zeta/(t-zeta),  t in F_p,  t!=1.

This is an explicit family of degree-three finite étale Kummer
covers over `k`. For a scheme-theoretic parameter domain one can
use the open set `T^3-1!=0`; it contains precisely the needed
prime-field parameters. No assertion that a cube root of the
parameter-normalizing function is a rational function is needed:
at an actual input `t in F_p`, its unique prime-field cube root
is obtained by exponentiation.

The quotient `k^*/F_p^*` is the set of projective classes
`[A:B]` of nonzero elements `A+B zeta`. The Möbius formulas in
the proof identify this projective line with the parameter line,
apart from explicitly listed coordinate exceptions. Every scalar
is a cube, so this passage preserves the cubic/noncubic property.
Multiplication by the *known cube* `(1-zeta)^3` removes the two
coordinate exceptions and has an explicit root correction.

There is a different, substantive restriction: the resulting
starting curve must be ordinary for the restricted oracle to
apply. Its j-invariant is

    27 t^3 (t^3+8)^3 / (t^3-1)^3.

If this value belongs to the supersingular locus, the ordinary
lattice argument and the oracle domain do not apply. The proof
handles the easy j=0 inputs directly but does not eliminate all
other supersingular instances. Thus the explicit Möbius map is
not advertised as an unconditional one-call reduction from every
promised cube to the promised ordinary selector.

## Scope of the equivalence

The full oracle equivalence avoids this last issue by fixing the
supplied ordinary starting curve and walking its valid chain.
One obtains a single maximal unit that then handles *all* cube
inputs, irrespective of their individual Möbius-associated
curves. Constructing that ordinary starting curve from p alone,
or implementing the restricted selector without the cube-root
oracle, remains a separate operation. The dictionary introduces
neither an effective-descent theorem nor a new analytic hypothesis.

## Ordinary quartic starts and smoothed Fourier transfer (GRH-P072–P073)

The quartic construction begins with the finite étale quadratic tower F_p⊂F_(p²)⊂F_(p⁴). The nontrivial relative automorphism sends U to−U. The difference of the two rational j-maps is explicitly calculated, and the nonsquare class of U² forces one of U or2U to have a j-value outside the quadratic fixed field. Multiplication by p on a supersingular elliptic curve is purely inseparable of degree p², so its factorization through relative Frobenius squared forces its j-value into F_(p²). The selected curve is therefore ordinary. This is an explicit two-point avoidance argument on the moduli line; it constructs no section of the remaining cubic torsor and supplies no prime-field parent.

The smooth Mellin transform bounds each complex Dirichlet prime sum by square-root scale times log conductor. Fourier expansion of the finite-field coefficient indicator then incurs its proven l1 norm of order sqrt(p). This improves the elementary method's guarantee to numerical p log²p. It does not turn finite-field weight bounds into an estimate polynomial in logp or show that the actual combined error is that large.

## Supersingular torsion and Kummer classes (GRH-P096–P097)

## Projective specialisation and arithmetic over a residue algebra (GRH-P105–P110)

For a projective point over a number field, the coordinate ideal is an invertible module at every prime abovep. Its quotient by the radical is a line bundle on the reduced finite special fibre. Annihilator-idempotent patching trivializes this line bundle without choosing a connected component. This removes different common valuation factors simultaneously and gives the correct projective specialisation. Arithmetic algorithms can then run over the whole finite etale algebra: a zero test partitions its support by idempotents, and each component follows its field algorithm. Pila's fixed-shape abelian-variety theorem applies when radical chart ideals, identity and a complete addition atlas are already present. This provides no CM moduli model by itself. These deductions use ordinary finite algebra and projective geometry; no further archive cote or new lambda-ring, K-theoretic, or weight assertion is claimed.
