## CC-AG01 — The abelian category with algebraically closed coefficients

Let \(q=p^a\), let \(\ell\ne p\), set \(E=\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\), and fix an embedding \(\iota:\overline{\mathbf Q}\hookrightarrow E\). Let \(\mathcal A_q\) be the rational numerical category generated by abelian varieties over \(\mathbf F_q\), finite étale schemes, and Tate motives under finite sums, tensor products, duals, and direct summands, exactly as in AQ-ABELIAN-RECOGNITION. Use the parity-corrected symmetry, so its ordinary direct-sum realization is symmetric monoidal. Define

\[
 \mathcal A_{q,E}
 =\operatorname{Kar}(\mathcal A_q\otimes_{\mathbf Q}E),
\]

where the tensor product first extends every Hom space and \(\operatorname{Kar}\) then adds images of idempotents. Thus coefficient extension adds summands; it does not add motives of arbitrary varieties as generators.

Define the multiplicative abelian group

\[
 W(q)=\left\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbf Q}^{\,\times}\ \middle|\
 \begin{array}{l}
 \alpha\text{ is a unit at every finite prime away from }p,\\
 \exists w\in\mathbf Z\ \forall\sigma:\ |\sigma(\alpha)|=q^{w/2}
 \end{array}\right\}.
\]

The integer \(w=w_q(\alpha)\) is unique. Let \(\operatorname{Gr}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{W(q)}(E)\) be the category of \(W(q)\)-graded \(E\)-vector spaces with finite total dimension. Its tensor grading is multiplication in \(W(q)\), and its symmetry is the ordinary interchange of vector factors.

**Theorem.** Geometric Frobenius gives a symmetric monoidal equivalence

\[
 \mathcal A_{q,E}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\
 \operatorname{Gr}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{W(q)}(E),\qquad
 M\longmapsto
 \bigoplus_{\alpha\in W(q)}
 R_E(M)_{F=\iota(\alpha)}.                         \tag{CC-AG.1}
\]

In particular, every \(\alpha\in W(q)\) labels an invertible rank-one object \(L_\alpha\), unique up to isomorphism, and every object is a finite direct sum of these, with arbitrary nonnegative multiplicities. Tensor product satisfies \(L_\alpha\otimes L_\beta\simeq L_{\alpha\beta}\). The weight is \(w_q(\alpha)\), and \(\mathbf Q(1)\) corresponds to the label \(q^{-1}\). Also

\[
 K_0(\mathcal A_{q,E})\simeq\mathbf Z[W(q)]
\]

as rings, with the classes of actual objects corresponding to the finitely supported nonnegative sums of group elements.

**Complete proof.** AQ-ABELIAN-RECOGNITION proves, for objects \(M,N\) of the rational category,

\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)
          \otimes_{\mathbf Q}\mathbf Q_\ell
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf Q_\ell[F]}
       (R_\ell M,R_\ell N),                       \tag{CC-AG.2}
\]

and proves semisimplicity of Frobenius. Extend (CC-AG.2) to \(E\). The commutant on the right is the kernel of the finite linear system \(TF_M=F_NT\); field extension commutes with this kernel. The left side becomes \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)\otimes_{\mathbf Q}E\). Thus realization remains fully faithful on the category with extended Hom spaces. For idempotents \(e\in\operatorname{End}(M)\otimes E\) and \(f\in\operatorname{End}(N)\otimes E\), its Hom space is

\[
 f\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)\otimes E\bigr)e.
\]

The identical formula holds for maps between their realized images. Hence realization is fully faithful on the entire idempotent completion. Frobenius remains semisimple after extension and on every summand.

Its eigenvalues lie in \(W(q)\). Indeed, eigenvalues \(\pi\) on \(H^1\) of an abelian variety are integral Weil numbers of weight one. Choose an identification of \(\overline{\mathbf Q}\) with the algebraic numbers in \(\mathbf C\) when using complex conjugation. Then \(\pi\overline{\pi}=q\), so integrality of both factors forces every non-\(p\) finite valuation to vanish. They are therefore \(p\)-units. The eigenvalues on exterior powers are products of such numbers; Tate twists multiply them by integral powers of \(q\); finite étale factors supply roots of unity. These operations, duals and summands preserve the indicated group of numbers and their integral weights. This proves that the displayed functor lands in the target category. Since a map between semisimple Frobenius modules commutes with Frobenius exactly when it preserves its eigenspaces, the functor is fully faithful into that category.

It remains to prove that every label occurs and can be split off with multiplicity one. Let \(\alpha\in W(q)\), of weight \(w\). Choose \(n\ge0\) large enough that \(\beta=q^n\alpha\) is integral and \(m=w+2n>0\). Such \(n\) exists: outside \(p\) all valuations are already zero, and multiplication by \(q^n\) clears the finitely many negative valuations above \(p\) in a number field containing \(\alpha\).

Choose \(\gamma\in\overline{\mathbf Q}\) with \(\gamma^m=\beta\). It is integral by transitivity of integrality, and every conjugate has absolute value \(q^{1/2}\). Honda's Main Theorem (1968, p. 83) therefore supplies an abelian variety \(B/\mathbf F_q\) for which \(\gamma\) is a Frobenius eigenvalue on \(H^1\). This is exactly the occurrence argument already proved in AQ-WEIL-EXIST. The object

\[
 M_\alpha=h^1(B)^{\otimes m}(n)\in\mathcal A_q
\]

has eigenvalue \(\gamma^m q^{-n}=\alpha\), with weight \(m-2n=w\).

Its realized \(\alpha\)-eigenspace is nonzero and Frobenius is scalar there. Choose a line in that eigenspace and a vector-space complement to it, including all other eigenspaces. Projection onto the line commutes with Frobenius. Full faithfulness over \(E\) lifts this rank-one projector to an idempotent in \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A_q}(M_\alpha)\otimes E\). The idempotent completion supplies its image \(L_\alpha\). This is why occurrence alone would not have sufficed in the rational category: the projector is now allowed to have extended coefficients.

Every finite-dimensional \(W(q)\)-graded vector space is a finite sum of these one-dimensional realizations. This proves essential surjectivity. The tensor-product eigenspace decomposition is compatible with multiplication of labels, and the corrected symmetry realizes as ordinary symmetry, so the equivalence is symmetric monoidal. Full faithfulness and realization give \(L_\alpha\otimes L_\beta\simeq L_{\alpha\beta}\), in particular \(L_{\alpha^{-1}}\) is the inverse. The claims about weights and Tate twists follow from the eigenvalue convention. Finally the target is semisimple with these simple objects; its Grothendieck ring is freely generated additively by their classes, with the multiplication just computed. This proves the ring assertion and the whole theorem. \(\square\)

**Comparison with the primary literature.** Milne's Definition 2.5, author-manuscript pp. 14–15, uses the condition that some \(q^n\alpha\) is integral. Under the common archimedean absolute-value condition, this is equivalent to the \(p\)-unit condition above: complex conjugation gives \(\alpha\overline\alpha=q^w\); away from \(p\), integrality of both conjugate factors and this identity force both valuations to be zero. The converse is the denominator-clearing argument in the proof. Milne's Proposition 2.6, p. 15, contains the occurrence argument, while his Proposition 3.7, p. 36, gives the broader coefficient-extended classification under the full Tate conjecture imposed in that part of the paper. Here (CC-AG.2) is already proved for \(\mathcal A_q\) from the cited all-abelian theorem; no full Tate conjecture for arbitrary varieties is used.

## CC-AG02 — The split group, its weight factor, and its Frobenius point

Write \(\omega_E\) for the underlying-vector-space functor in (CC-AG.1). For any abelian group \(M\), let

\[
 D_E(M)=\operatorname{Spec}E[M],
\]

with group algebra Hopf structure \(\Delta([\alpha])=[\alpha]\otimes[\alpha]\).

**Theorem.** There is a canonical identification, relative to the chosen realization and \(\iota\),

\[
 \operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega_E)
       \simeq D_E(W(q)).                            \tag{CC-AG.3}
\]

Let

\[
 W_0(p)=\left\{\alpha\in\overline{\mathbf Q}^{\,\times}\ \middle|\
 \begin{array}{l}
 \alpha\text{ is a }p\text{-unit},\\
 |\sigma(\alpha)|=1\text{ for every }\sigma
 \end{array}\right\}.
\]

Choosing a square root \(u=\sqrt q\) gives

\[
 W(q)\simeq W_0(p)\times\mathbf Z,\qquad
 \alpha\longmapsto(\alpha u^{-w_q(\alpha)},w_q(\alpha)),
\]

and consequently

\[
 \operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega_E)
       \simeq D_E(W_0(p))\times\mathbf G_{m,E}.       \tag{CC-AG.4}
\]

The weight cocharacter is the inclusion of the displayed \(\mathbf G_m\) factor. The Tate object has character \((1,-2)\). Frobenius is the distinguished point \(f\) with character values \(\chi_\alpha(f)=\iota(\alpha)\), and its nonnegative powers are Zariski dense in this affine group.

**Complete proof.** Let \(R\) be any commutative \(E\)-algebra. A tensor automorphism of \(\omega_E\otimes_E R\) acts on each \(L_\alpha\) by a scalar \(g_\alpha\in R^\times\). Naturality with respect to every matrix endomorphism of \(L_\alpha^{\oplus r}\), and to the inclusions and projections of summands, forces its action on every object to be this scalar on its entire \(\alpha\)-graded summand. Compatibility with tensor product and the unit says

\[
 g_{\alpha\beta}=g_\alpha g_\beta,\qquad g_1=1.
\]

Conversely any group homomorphism \(W(q)\to R^\times\) defines such a natural tensor automorphism by those scalars. This correspondence is functorial in \(R\), and the group algebra represents precisely this functor. It proves (CC-AG.3).

The function \(w_q:W(q)\to\mathbf Z\) is a group homomorphism with kernel \(W_0(p)\). The number \(u\) is a \(p\)-unit of weight one, so \(n\mapsto u^n\) splits this homomorphism. Explicitly, the displayed map and \((a,n)\mapsto au^n\) are inverse group homomorphisms. Taking group algebras gives (CC-AG.4). A scalar \(z\) in the weight factor acts on label \(\alpha\) by \(z^{w_q(\alpha)}\), which proves the cocharacter statement. Since \(q^{-1}=u^{-2}\), the Tate character is \((1,-2)\).

The map \(\alpha\mapsto\iota(\alpha)\) is multiplicative, so it defines \(f\in D_E(W(q))(E)\). To prove density, let a regular function

\[
 h=\sum_{j=1}^s c_j[\alpha_j]\in E[W(q)]
\]

with distinct \(\alpha_j\) vanish at every \(f^r\), \(r\ge0\). For \(r=0,\ldots,s-1\), this gives

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^s c_j\,\iota(\alpha_j)^r=0.
\]

The Vandermonde determinant is nonzero because \(\iota\) is injective and the labels are distinct. Thus every \(c_j\) is zero. No nonzero regular function vanishes on these points; their Zariski closure is the whole affine group. \(\square\)

## CC-AG03 — Fixed-field torsion, coefficients, and the limits of the description

The character group \(W(q)\) contains all roots of unity \(\mu_\infty\), precisely its torsion subgroup. Thus \(D_E(W(q))\) is a diagonalizable affine group of multiplicative type, generally of infinite type; it is **not a protorus**. For example, the inclusion \(\mu_n\subset W(q)\) gives a quotient \(D_E(W(q))\twoheadrightarrow D_E(\mu_n)\), a nontrivial finite étale group when \(n>1\). This also shows why the weight-zero factor \(D_E(W_0(p))\) must not be confused with an identity component: it retains the same root-of-unity characters. These are the finite-field Artin contributions.

**Justification of the quotient assertion.** For a subgroup \(H\subset M\), choose representatives for the cosets of \(H\) in \(M\). The group algebra \(E[M]\) is then a free \(E[H]\)-module, with those representatives as a basis. Its rank is nonzero, so the induced map \(D_E(M)\to D_E(H)\) is faithfully flat. For \(H=\mu_n\), the abstract character group is cyclic of order \(n\), whose group algebra is \(E[t]/(t^n-1)\). In characteristic zero it is finite étale and has \(n\) geometric points. A protorus, being an inverse limit of tori, is connected and cannot have this nontrivial finite étale quotient. \(\square\)

This description is compatible with AQ-RATIONAL-FIBRE. Over \(\mathbf F_9\), the quaternionic elliptic-curve motive of AQ-RATIONAL-FIBRE has eigenvalue \(-3\) with multiplicity two, and there is no rational numerical object having only one copy. After coefficient extension, (CC-AG.1) supplies the corresponding rank-one summand. Thus (CC-AG.3) does not assert a neutral \(\mathbf Q\)-valued fibre functor or remove rational multiplicity restrictions.

There is also a separate base-field operation: passing from a fixed \(\mathbf F_q\) to \(\overline{\mathbf F}_p\) uses power transitions on Frobenius characters and their germs. Milne treats this distinction in **Proposition 3.2**, p. 34. We have held the finite base field fixed throughout. The category of arbitrary numerical motives, integral lattices, mixed extensions, and the archive's uncertain \(Q'\), \(M_0\), or quotient conventions remain outside (CC-AG.1)–(CC-AG.4).
