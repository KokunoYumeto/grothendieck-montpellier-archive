# Archive questions and rigorous RH bridges

## Results and source status

The supplied RH and Dirichlet GRH declarations are adopted as research theorems in this module. The strongest new effective bridge proved here is a finite recognition theorem for semisimple abelian Artin–Tate motives of bounded conductor: all good-prime Frobenius polynomials below the explicit bound `(φ(lcm(N,4)) log(lcm(N,4)))²` recover every finite-character and Tate-weight multiplicity. The proof uses the corrected Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan Corollary1.2 and finite character orthogonality. A second concrete bridge gives the effective distribution of trace-zero primes for the hexagonal CM curve `y²=x³+1` through its quadratic splitting character. A third gives exact arithmetic zeta factors for rational line-arrangement complements, while preserving the distinction from the topological pseudoline surface in cote154.

The current literature substantially strengthens the answer to cote15. The October–November2026 CM-Hodge result of van Hoften and Sempliner, combined with Milne's precise earlier implications, clears the full Tate and standard conjectures for abelian varieties in the stated settings. Using those cited inputs, this module proves full faithfulness after extension of Hom spaces to Qℓ, and recognition by the complete Frobenius polynomial or signed Euler factor, for rational numerical motives generated by abelian varieties, Artin motives and Tate twists over a finite field. The companion CC-AG01–03 goes further: after extension to Qbar_l and idempotent completion, the abelian category is equivalent to finite Weil-number-graded vector spaces and its tensor group is the diagonalizable D_E(W(q)). The further CC-AG04–06 completes the actual passage to Fbar_p: the category becomes finite Frobenius-germ-graded E-vector spaces, with character group Z⊕(W0(p)⊗Q) and a protorus as tensor group. It proves finite-model, numerical-cycle, full-Hom and idempotent descent; within this sector, the categorical germ transition is settled. A separate explicit quaternion example over F9 proves that a Q-valued fibre functor and arbitrary rank-one Weil objects still cannot be presumed. CC-AG07–08 further gives the exact rational-object descent test: Galois-invariant multiplicities must be divisible by the explicit orbitwise Schur indices. Finite base-field power maps have kernel μ_m and zero cokernel; their essential surjectivity requires extended coefficients, as a rank-one sign Artin counterexample over F_(q²) demonstrates. CC-AG11–12 further makes descent effective: the integral-slope monoid is explicitly cyclic, and in every slope sector a specified target rank gives one finite polynomial test that decides descent and lists all source isomorphism classes over a fixed finite field. From the prime field, CC-AG13 further gives the entire all-slope geometric multiplicity monoid explicitly as (hξ/dξ)N, with a finite exponent test and one source simple attaining its generator. For other finite source fields the rank-dependent result does not claim a uniform cutoff for all multiplicities. These deductions are fully written and independently read; the complete new external CM-Hodge proof is a cited input, not a proof independently certified in this run.

The original cote14 definition has now been recovered from the official complete PDF. It agrees with the generated Albanese quotient used below, for the projective-parameter formulation indicated by its bracketed word *projectif*. The full forward proof addresses the original rational ℓ-adic conclusion over an algebraically closed base in every characteristic; a Walker-based construction refutes its converse even rationally. AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE proves integral equality with the relation using all smooth quasiprojective parameters in characteristic zero. The further AQ-14-NONPROPER proves the rational cohomological implication for that broader relation in every characteristic, using Suslin–Roitman, uniform integral generic base change, and the torus/odd-cohomology weight distinction. The free-zero-cycle and abelian-versus-semiabelian Albanese conventions are explicit; AQ-14-ALTERATION first proves that their discrepancy in characteristic p is p-primary torsion. The checked companion CC-ALB01–02 then eliminates it integrally: the Suslin ordinary-Albanese kernel is divisible, and one fixed alteration degree kills the entire fixed-correspondence image. Thus the projective and smooth quasiprojective relations are equal over every algebraically closed field, for both Albanese conventions. Definition1.1 does not explicitly say algebraically closed, and no such convention was found in the relevant preceding handwritten pages inspected; a separate F5 example disproves a reading that tests the premise only on rational points over an arbitrary field.

The original cote136 admissibility and both uniqueness questions are now recovered from the official scans, with physical and footer numbering distinguished. Complete proofs give an admissible integral divided-power simplex theory and refute strict equalizing sources over every nonzero base ring. Common sources are proved over every Q-algebra, every Z_(p)-algebra, finite products of these coefficient rings, and hence every ring of positive characteristic. Arithmetic path gluing now proves the ordinary terminal-groupoid assertion more broadly over every coefficient ring in which all but finitely many rational primes are units, including mixed semilocal rings such as Z_(2,3). The proof constructs one compatible cone for each finite diagram after specified natural path replacements; it does not claim strict common sources over these mixed semilocal rings. The coefficient-preserving simplicial cotensor is admissible over any ring; the two remaining questions over Z are source existence and terminality. A separate explicit integral model fails horizontal admissibility at every sufficiently large prime weight. The standard theory's actual tensor square also fails over every non-rational coefficient ring: its zero-form row has horizontal homology k/pk in every prime-power weight p^r, despite simplexwise freeness and exact vertical comparison. That tensor obstruction was reported in the earlier SA08 intake and is completely re-proved here. This separates a genuine counterexample from the still unresolved integral existence and localization questions.

BR-14-SEP further removes quasiprojectivity from the cote14 parameter convention: over an algebraically closed field, projective, affine, quasiprojective and all smooth connected separated finite-type parameters generate the same integral relation. The arbitrary-base-field k-point reading remains false.

## Reading conventions

An archive locator such as `14/038` follows the supplied transcription's scan number. For cote14 this is the physical one-based PDF page, including its cover; physical38 has printed archive footer37 and handwritten manuscript37. Newly recovered definitions are therefore cited with both physical and footer numbers. The images originally generated as `c14_scanNNN.png` use zero-based PDF indices; their filenames are not historical locators.

Original French text is preserved in the curated question register, including deleted passages and the transcription's `unsure` markers. A modern mathematical reading is provided in a separate column. In particular, “modulo torsion” on14/038 concerns the cohomological conclusion: the premise used in the proof is integral. Rationalizing that premise produces an explicit counterexample over an algebraic closure of a finite field.

The original projective Albanese relation is introduced in Definition1.1, official14.pdf physical24/footer23: a correspondence sends a degree-zero zero-cycle with vanishing Albanese sum to the cycle being declared equivalent to zero. Proposition1.2(i) gives the subgroup and Chow quotient;1.2(v), physical25/footer24, places its kernel inside algebraic equivalence. Corollary2.3, physical34/footer33, identifies P as the algebraically trivial part of that quotient. Theorem2.1, physical31/footer30, supplies the induced map from the parameter Albanese. This is sufficient to identify the quotient below without assuming that it is a universal finite-dimensional algebraic representative. The equality of relations is proved in characteristic zero by AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE; AQ-14-NONPROPER proves the forward implication for all smooth quasiprojective parameters in every characteristic; AQ-14-ALTERATION supplies the intermediate away-from-characteristic comparison; CC-ALB01–02 then proves integral equality in every characteristic by divisibility and the uniform alteration-degree argument.

The bridges use exactly the adopted analytic statements. They do not upgrade a finite-order Dirichlet character to a nonzero-infinity-type CM Hecke character, do not recover extension classes from semisimple Frobenius polynomials, and do not identify a cellular surface with an arithmetic scheme. Where an implication is not established, the status is a remaining gate rather than a claim of formal logical nonimplication. Explicit counterexamples are proved in full where available.
