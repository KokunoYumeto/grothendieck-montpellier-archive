## Reading conventions

An archive locator such as `14/038` follows the supplied transcription's scan number. For cote14 this is the physical one-based PDF page, including its cover; physical38 has printed archive footer37 and handwritten manuscript37. Newly recovered definitions are therefore cited with both physical and footer numbers. The images originally generated as `c14_scanNNN.png` use zero-based PDF indices; their filenames are not historical locators.

Original French text is preserved in the curated question register, including deleted passages and the transcription's `unsure` markers. A modern mathematical reading is provided in a separate column. In particular, “modulo torsion” on14/038 concerns the cohomological conclusion: the premise used in the proof is integral. Rationalizing that premise produces an explicit counterexample over an algebraic closure of a finite field.

The original projective Albanese relation is introduced in Definition1.1, official14.pdf physical24/footer23: a correspondence sends a degree-zero zero-cycle with vanishing Albanese sum to the cycle being declared equivalent to zero. Proposition1.2(i) gives the subgroup and Chow quotient;1.2(v), physical25/footer24, places its kernel inside algebraic equivalence. Corollary2.3, physical34/footer33, identifies P as the algebraically trivial part of that quotient. Theorem2.1, physical31/footer30, supplies the induced map from the parameter Albanese. This is sufficient to identify the quotient below without assuming that it is a universal finite-dimensional algebraic representative. The equality of relations is proved in characteristic zero by AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE; AQ-14-NONPROPER proves the forward implication for all smooth quasiprojective parameters in every characteristic; AQ-14-ALTERATION supplies the intermediate away-from-characteristic comparison; CC-ALB01–02 then proves integral equality in every characteristic by divisibility and the uniform alteration-degree argument.

The bridges use exactly the adopted analytic statements. They do not upgrade a finite-order Dirichlet character to a nonzero-infinity-type CM Hecke character, do not recover extension classes from semisimple Frobenius polynomials, and do not identify a cellular surface with an arithmetic scheme. Where an implication is not established, the status is a remaining gate rather than a claim of formal logical nonimplication. Explicit counterexamples are proved in full where available.

## AQ-FINITE: finite Frobenius recognition of abelian Artin–Tate motives

#### Statement.

Adopt the Dirichlet GRH theorem supplied in `ALPOGE_Main.lean`,
`LFunction_eq_zero_re`. Let $N\geq1$, put
$$Q=\operatorname{lcm}(N,4),\qquad B_Q=(\varphi(Q)\log Q)^2,$$ and let
$M,M'$ be finite direct sums of Tate twists of abelian Artin motives
over $\mathbb Q$, whose Artin representations factor through
$G_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$. Work initially with a coefficient
number field $E\subset\mathbb C$ splitting $G_N$. Write
$$M=\bigoplus_{\chi,n}(\chi\otimes E(n))^{m_{\chi,n}},
 \qquad
 M'=\bigoplus_{\chi,n}(\chi\otimes E(n))^{m'_{\chi,n}}.$$ Then
$M\simeq M'$ if and only if $$\det(1-TF_p\mid M)=\det(1-TF_p\mid M')
 \quad\text{for every prime }p\leq B_Q,\ p\nmid Q.                 \tag{AQ.1}$$
Here $E(n)$ has Frobenius eigenvalue $p^{-n}$. These finitely many
polynomials recover every multiplicity $m_{\chi,n}$. The same
isomorphism criterion holds with rational coefficients. The characters
are labelled by their Euler-factor values $\chi(p)$; changing the
Artin-reciprocity convention inverts all character labels and leaves the
criterion unchanged. One may use a single auxiliary $\ell>B_Q$ for all
the displayed Frobenius factors, avoiding the prime $\ell$ among the
tested primes.

#### Known input and exact locator.

Lamzouri, Li, and Soundararajan, *Conditional bounds for the least
quadratic non-residue and related problems*, arXiv:1309.3595v3,
Corollary 1.2, p. 3, proves under Dirichlet GRH that every reduced
residue class $a\bmod Q$, $Q>3$, contains a prime
$p_a\leq(\varphi(Q)\log Q)^2$. This result is cited, not reproved.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The forward direction is functoriality. Inflate all characters
to $G_Q$. For each $a\in G_Q$ choose such a prime $p_a$. Every
eigenvalue at $p_a$ is $\chi(a)p_a^{-n}$, of complex absolute value
$p_a^{-n}$. Since $p_a>1$, different integers $n$ give different
absolute values. Equality in (AQ.1) therefore gives equality, for every
$a$ and $n$, of the sums of eigenvalues of absolute value $p_a^{-n}$.
Multiplying this sum by $p_a^n$ gives
$$t_n(a)=\sum_\chi m_{\chi,n}\chi(a).$$ Finite character orthogonality
yields the reconstruction $$m_{\chi,n}=\frac1{|G_Q|}\sum_{a\in G_Q}
             t_n(a)\chi(a)^{-1}.                                \tag{AQ.2}$$
Indeed, translating $\sum_a\theta(a)$ by an element $b$ with
$\theta(b)\ne1$ makes that sum zero for every nontrivial character
$\theta$; it is $|G_Q|$ for the trivial character. Thus (AQ.2) gives the
same multiplicities for $M'$. For rational coefficients, the semisimple
commutative algebra $\mathbb Q[G_Q]$ is a product of cyclotomic fields.
The multiplicity of each simple rational module is recovered from the
common multiplicity of its characters over a splitting field. The
rational representations, weight by weight, are therefore isomorphic as
well. ◻

</div>

This is a finite stopping criterion for recognition within the abelian
Artin–Tate part of the programme. It needs every residue class, or
equivalent full character information: values on an arbitrary generating
set do not determine a general character. It does not reconstruct
extension classes, integral lattices, period comparisons, or nonabelian
fundamental groups.

## AQ-14-FORWARD: the cohomological implication at cote 14, scan 38

#### Archive question.

The sheet labels *Théorème 3.1*, marks its proof *dém. pas faite*, and
asks whether $u_Z=0$ implies, modulo torsion, the vanishing of
$$a({}^tZ):H^{2(d-i)+1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(d-i))
                     \longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathbb Q_\ell).
                                                               \tag{AQ.3}$$
Here $d=\dim X$ and $A$ is an abelian parameter variety. Its margin asks
*A-t-on une réciproque*. The lower display $H^{(n-i)+1}$ is inconsistent
with the degree in the preceding paragraph; (AQ.3) uses the latter.

#### Precise modern formulation.

For a smooth projective geometrically connected $X$ over an
algebraically closed field $k$, let $N^i(X)\subset CH^i(X)_{\rm alg}$ be
generated by $$\Gamma_*z,\quad
 z\in\ker\bigl(CH_0(T)^0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alb}_T(k)\bigr),
 \quad\Gamma\in CH^i(T\times X),                                \tag{AQ.4}$$
where $T$ is smooth projective geometrically connected. Set
$P^i(X)=CH^i(X)_{\rm alg}/N^i(X)$, the generated Albanese quotient. For
$Z\in CH^i(A\times X)$ the map on degree-zero cycles descends to
$u_Z:A(k)\to P^i(X)$, since two lifts of the same point of
$\operatorname{Alb}_A=A$ differ by an Albanese-kernel class. If $u_Z=0$
integrally, then (AQ.3) is zero for every $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$.
The sole initially supplied sheet 14/038 does not define
$A^i_{\rm alb}(X)$, but the official full cote now resolves the
definition. In its physical PDF page 24 (footer and manuscript page 23),
Definition 1.1 declares a cycle Albanese-equivalent to zero when it has
the form $Z(v)$ for a smooth connected projective parameter $T$, a
degree-zero zero-cycle $v$ with $S_T(v)=0$, and a correspondence $Z$ for
which the intersection is defined. The word *projectif* is bracketed in
the sheet, between *un schéma* and *lisse connexe*. We first prove the
projective-parameter formulation. The subsequent AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE
and CC-ALB01–02 establish equality with the quasiprojective relation in
every characteristic. BR-14-SEP in
`research/root_separated_parameters.tex` further proves
$N_{\rm proj}=N_{\rm aff}=N_{\rm qp}=N_{\rm sep}$ for all smooth
connected separated finite-type parameters over the same algebraically
closed base. Thus the bracketed projectivity convention does not change
this quotient. The nonproper Albanese and free-zero-cycle conventions
remain explicit. Here $S_T$ is the Albanese summation map. Proposition
1.2(i), on the same page, says these cycles form a subgroup containing
rational equivalences and defines the quotient of the Chow group by its
image. Proposition 1.2(v), physical PDF page 25 (footer 24), says
Albanese equivalence implies algebraic equivalence. Finally Corollary
2.3, physical PDF page 34 (footer 33), defines $P^i(X)$ itself as the
algebraically trivial subgroup of $A^i_{\rm alb}(X)$. Thus this is the
original quotient in the projective formulation, not a quotient defined
by regular maps to abelian varieties. The Chow moving lemma makes the
finitely many intersections in (AQ.4) defined; since rational
equivalences are already killed, the cycle-level and Chow formulations
agree. Theorem 2.1, physical PDF page 31 (footer 30), defines $u_Z$
through the Albanese summation map. Its image lies in $P^i(X)$, so
vanishing in this subgroup and in the original total quotient are
equivalent. The supplied `scan038` uses physical PDF numbering: it is
footer/manuscript page 37, after the cover. Definition 1.1 itself says
only that $k$ is a field. The preceding handwritten sheets inspected in
the full cote concern other motive conventions and preparatory cycle
calculations; no explicit algebraic-closure convention was found there.
Accordingly the algebraically closed hypothesis in our theorem is
retained as an explicit mathematical scope condition, rather than
attributed to an unseen original convention.

#### Primary inputs.

Bloch (1979), *Torsion algebraic cycles and a theorem of Roitman*,
Propositions 3.5 (correspondences), 3.8 (specialization), and Theorem
4.2 (prime-to-characteristic Roitman), provide the inputs. They are also
explicitly recorded in Achter, Casalaina-Martin, and Vial, *On the image
of the second $\ell$-adic Bloch map*, arXiv:2007.07180v2, Propositions
A.23, A.24, A.26, and A.29. These appendix statements apply in every
codimension.

#### Torsion lemma.

For Bloch’s map $$\lambda_X^i:CH^i(X)[\ell^\infty]\longrightarrow
                    H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(i)),$$ one
has
$$N^i(X)\cap CH^i(X)[\ell^\infty]\subseteq\ker\lambda_X^i.          \tag{AQ.5}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Choose $x$ in the intersection, a finite expression
$x=\sum_j\Gamma_{j*}z_j$ of the form (AQ.4), and $r$ with $\ell^r x=0$.
Spread out this entire expression, its rational-equivalence
certificates, its varieties and correspondences, their Albanese maps,
and the equalities $\operatorname{alb}(z_j)=0$, over an integral
finitely generated ring over the prime ring. Shrink so that the
varieties are smooth projective, the chosen generic Albanese varieties
extend to abelian schemes $B_j$, and the chosen maps $T_j\to B_j$
extend. It is unnecessary to assert that these are universal relative
Albanese maps. Here the base can be chosen regular: the finitely
generated field of definition has a separating transcendence basis over
its prime field, and a model becomes smooth over $\mathbb F_p$, or over
$\mathbb Z[1/M]$ in characteristic zero, after a further shrinking. All
the finitely many conditions just listed survive that shrinking. In
characteristic zero invert $\ell$ as well. Choose a closed point with
finite residue field of characteristic $p\ne\ell$. A regular system of
parameters in its local ring gives a flag of prime ideals with regular
successive quotients. Localizing at consecutive primes gives discrete
valuation rings. Specialization along this flag, after choosing
algebraic closures of the residue fields, gives geometric special fibres
over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, denoted by subscripts $0$.

Every degree-zero zero-cycle on a smooth projective connected variety
over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ is torsion: join its finite support by
finitely many projective curves and lift the differences of points to
their normalizations. Every degree-zero divisor class on those curves is
a point of a Jacobian defined over a finite extension of a finite field,
and has finite order.

The specialized map $T_{j,0}\to B_{j,0}$ factors through
$h_j:\operatorname{Alb}(T_{j,0})\to B_{j,0}$. This map is an isomorphism
on $\ell$-power torsion. Indeed Kummer theory gives
$H^1(T,\mathbb Z_\ell(1))=T_\ell\operatorname{Pic}(T)
=T_\ell\operatorname{Pic}^0(T)_{\rm red}$: the Néron–Severi group is
finitely generated and has zero Tate module. Duality of
$\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\rm red}$ and Albanese therefore gives
$H^1(T,\mathbb Z_\ell)=\operatorname{Hom}(T_\ell
\operatorname{Alb}_T,\mathbb Z_\ell)$, compatibly with maps. This is
also the codimension-one calculation in Achter–Casalaina-Martin–Vial,
Propositions A.25 and A.28. The generic Albanese map thus induces an
isomorphism on $H^1(-,\mathbb Z_\ell)$, and smooth proper base change
preserves this isomorphism. The latter is obtained by taking inverse
limits of the finite-coefficient base-change isomorphisms for each
variety; no assertion that the Albanese map is an isomorphism on every
finite-coefficient $H^1$ is needed. Equivalently $T_\ell h_j$ is an
isomorphism. It follows that $h_j$ is an isogeny with no $\ell$-primary
kernel, and hence an isomorphism on $\ell$-power torsion. Since the
specialization of the equality $\operatorname{alb}(z_j)=0$ says that
$z_{j,0}$ maps to zero in $B_{j,0}$, the $\ell$-primary part of its
actual Albanese class is zero. Prime-to-$p$ Roitman implies that the
$\ell$-primary part of $z_{j,0}$ itself is zero. Thus every $z_{j,0}$,
and hence $x_0$, has order prime to $\ell$. The equality $\ell^r x_0=0$
also specializes, so $x_0=0$.

Bloch’s compatibility with specialization sends $\lambda_X^i(x)$ to
$\lambda_{X_0}^i(x_0)=0$. The cohomological specialization map is an
isomorphism by smooth proper base change, first for finite coefficients
and then for their direct limit $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(i)$.
Consequently $\lambda_X^i(x)=0$. Only finitely many data are spread for
each $x$; no simultaneous model for all of $N^i(X)$ has been assumed. ◻

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof of AQ-14-FORWARD.* Put $g=\dim A$. Roitman identifies
$CH_0(A)[\ell^\infty]$ with $A[\ell^\infty](k)$. Use the torsion
zero-cycle lift of a torsion point under this isomorphism; it is
unnecessary to identify it with the literal class $[a]-[0]$. For every
such torsion cycle $z$, the premise gives
$Z_*z\in N^i(X)\cap CH^i(X)[\ell^\infty]$. Thus (AQ.5) gives
$\lambda_X^i(Z_*z)=0$. Correspondence compatibility, followed by taking
Tate modules and tensoring with $\mathbb Q_\ell$, makes
$$Z_*:H^{2g-1}(A,\mathbb Q_\ell(g))
             \longrightarrow H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$$ zero: the
degree-zero Bloch map identifies the source with $V_\ell A$. Under the
perfect Poincaré pairings its transpose is (AQ.3), so (AQ.3) is zero. ◻

</div>

This proves the stated generated-Albanese-quotient formulation in every
characteristic, independently of RH.

#### AQ-14-RATIONAL-PREMISE.

The integral premise cannot be replaced by $u_Z\otimes\mathbb Q=0$. Take
an elliptic curve $E/\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ and $X=A=E$,
$Z=\Delta_E-E\times\{0\}$. Then $P^1(E)=\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$ and
$u_Z(a)=[a]-[0]$. The group $E(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$ is torsion, so
the rationalized premise holds. The normalized diagonal acts as the
identity on the nonzero two-dimensional $H^1(E,\mathbb Q_\ell)$,
refuting the weakened assertion. The phrase *modulo torsion* in the
original sheet qualifies its cohomological conclusion. For the asserted
$P^1$ identification, a divisor correspondence on $T\times E$ defines a
morphism $T\to\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$ after choosing a base point, and
hence factors through $\operatorname{Alb}_T$. Every generator of
$N^1(E)$ is therefore zero.

#### AQ-14-FIELD-SCOPE.

The algebraically closed base is also substantive. If the premise is
tested only on $k$-rational points over an arbitrary field, it can fail
to detect the cohomological map. Take $k=\mathbb F_5$ and $E:y^2=x^3+1$.
The affine fibres over $x=0,1,2,3,4$ have respectively $2,0,2,0,1$
points, so $\#E(\mathbb F_5)=6$, including infinity. For
$Z=6(\Delta_E-E\times\{0\})$, the map $u_Z$ on $E(\mathbb F_5)$ is zero
by Lagrange’s theorem, while the induced rational $\ell$-adic map is
$6\operatorname{id}\ne0$ for every $\ell\ne5$. The original context’s
use of divisibility of $A_Y(k)$ in physical PDF page 34 (footer 33) is
consistent with the algebraically closed convention used here. This
counterexample prevents an unintended arbitrary-field reading.

## AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE: removing the parameter qualification in characteristic zero

Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X/k$ be smooth projective. For
a smooth connected parameter variety $T$, write $Z_0(T)^0$ for the group
of finite formal sums $v=\sum_jn_j[t_j]$ of $k$-points with
$\sum_jn_j=0$. In this paragraph the Albanese of a nonproper $T$ means
its *abelian Albanese*: the universal pointed morphism
$a_T:T\to\operatorname{Alb}_{\mathrm{ab}}(T)$ to an abelian variety. Put
$a_T(v)=\sum_jn_ja_T(t_j)$; this does not depend on the base point used
for the pointed morphism.

For $\Gamma\in\mathrm{CH}^i(X\times T)$ define
$$\Gamma(v)=\sum_j n_j\,\iota_{t_j}^{!}\Gamma
                         \in\mathrm{CH}^i(X),$$ where
$\iota_t:X\times\{t\}\hookrightarrow X\times T$ is the regular immersion
of the fibre. Thus this is a sum of fibre restrictions; no nonproper
pushforward $X\times T\to X$ is being used. Let $N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$
be generated by all such $\Gamma(v)$ with $T$ smooth connected
quasiprojective and $a_T(v)=0$. Let $N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}(X)$ use smooth
connected projective $T$ instead.

If every permitted $T$ has a smooth projective compactification, then
$$N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)=N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}(X).                 \tag{AQ.14.1}$$
In particular this equality holds in characteristic zero. It also holds
if the convention for a nonproper parameter is the universal semiabelian
Albanese, with the kernel imposed on $Z_0(T)^0$. This last convention
must be formulated on zero-cycles, not automatically on the ordinary
Chow group of the nonproper variety.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The inclusion from right to left is immediate, since a smooth
projective parameter is quasiprojective. For the other inclusion take a
generator $\Gamma(v)$ as above. Choose a smooth projective
compactification $j:T\hookrightarrow\overline T$. Represent $\Gamma$ by
a finite integral linear combination of integral codimension-$i$
subvarieties of $X\times T$, and take their closures in
$X\times\overline T$. This gives a class
$\overline\Gamma\in\mathrm{CH}^i(X\times\overline T)$ whose restriction
is $\Gamma$.

The morphism $a_{\overline T}\circ j:T\to
\operatorname{Alb}(\overline T)$, normalized to send the chosen base
point to zero, factors by universality through $a_T$. Therefore
$a_{\overline T}(j_*v)=0$. Here $j_*v$ denotes the same finite formal
zero-cycle regarded on $\overline T$; it does not assert the existence
of a pushforward $j_*:\mathrm{CH}_0(T)\to\mathrm{CH}_0(\overline T)$.
For each point in the support of $v$, refined fibre restriction commutes
with restriction to the open subset $X\times T$; equivalently, both
restrictions are computed in the same neighbourhood of $X\times\{t_j\}$.
Hence $$\overline\Gamma(j_*v)=\Gamma(v).$$ The left side is a generator
allowed in $N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}(X)$, proving the reverse inclusion. The
same proof works for the semiabelian convention, because the target
$\operatorname{Alb}(\overline T)$ is also semiabelian, and because the
semiabelian Albanese of a smooth projective parameter is its ordinary
abelian Albanese.

In characteristic zero, embed the quasiprojective $T$ in a projective
variety and resolve that projective closure by a projective resolution
that is an isomorphism over its smooth locus, in particular over $T$.
The resulting $\overline T$ is smooth projective. This is the strong
compactification consequence of Hironaka’s resolution theorem, stated in
precisely this form in Kahn and Sujatha, *Birational geometry and
localisation of categories*, May 22, 2008 author version, Theorem
1.6.3(a), p. 11. ◻

</div>

Thus AQ-14-FORWARD applies in characteristic zero to the quotient
generated using all smooth quasiprojective parameters, and
AQ-14-CONVERSE over $\mathbb C$ remains a counterexample for that larger
parameter class as well. The algebraically closed base-field
qualification remains. In positive characteristic this comparison
applies to parameters admitting a smooth projective compactification; no
existence of such compactifications in all dimensions is asserted here.
The next theorem proves the rational cohomological forward implication
without needing that positive-characteristic equality of relations.
AQ-14-ALTERATION gives the intermediate $p$-primary torsion bound; the
subsequently checked companion theorems CC-ALB01–02 remove that
discrepancy integrally in every characteristic.

## AQ-14-NONPROPER: the rational cohomological implication for all smooth quasiprojective parameters

Retain the algebraically closed base $k$ and smooth projective $X$. Let
$N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$ be the relation defined in AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE,
using the *abelian* Albanese of a smooth connected quasiprojective
parameter and free degree-zero zero-cycles. Put
$P^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)=\mathrm{CH}^i(X)_{\mathrm{alg}}/
N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$. For every prime $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$,
one has $$N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)\cap\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]
       \subseteq\ker\lambda_X^i.                            \tag{AQ.14.2}$$
Consequently the implication of original 14/038 holds for this
quasiprojective-parameter quotient in every characteristic: if $A$ is
abelian, $Z\in\mathrm{CH}^i(A\times X)$, and
$u_Z:A(k)\to P^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$ is zero integrally, then
$$a({}^tZ):H^{2(\dim X-i)+1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(\dim X-i))
                       \longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathbb Q_\ell)$$ is zero.
The same implication holds for the smaller relation using the
semiabelian Albanese kernel. No equality
$N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}=N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}$ in arbitrary positive
characteristic is asserted.

#### Inputs and coefficient conventions.

Write $H_j^S(T,R)$ for Suslin homology: it is the homology of the
complex generated in degree $n$ by integral closed subvarieties of
$T\times\Delta^n$ finite and surjective over $\Delta^n$, tensored with
$R$. Its degree-zero cycles are finite formal sums of closed points.
Write $G_T$ for the pointed Serre semiabelian Albanese and
$$0\longrightarrow S_T\longrightarrow G_T\longrightarrow B_T
       \longrightarrow0$$ for its torus and abelian quotient.
Universality shows that $B_T$ is the abelian Albanese of $T$: a
homomorphism from a torus to an abelian variety is zero. The notation
$[\ell^\infty]$ means the subgroup annihilated by some power of $\ell$.

Geisser, *Rojtman’s theorem for normal schemes*, arXiv:1402.1831v2,
Theorem 1.1, p. 2, gives, for normal $T$,
$$H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)[\ell^\infty]
       \xrightarrow{\sim}G_T(k)[\ell^\infty],\qquad
 H_1^S(T,\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell=0.
                                                               \tag{AQ.14.3}$$
The degree-zero convention on the left is automatic for torsion. This
prime-to-characteristic theorem does not require a smooth
compactification. The finite-coefficient perfect pairing used below is
Geisser–Schmidt (2016), Theorem 1.1, journal p. 92; their Theorem 7.1,
p. 118, identifies it with the Suslin–Voevodsky comparison. We invoke
its prime-to-characteristic case, where tame and ordinary abelian
$\ell$-primary covers agree. Kahn (2014), *Algebraic tori as Nisnevich
sheaves with transfers*, Lemma 4.1(b), journal p. 707 (July 4, 2013
author version, Lemma 3(b), p. 8), states
$\operatorname{Alb}_{\mathrm{sa}}(G)=G$ for a semiabelian $G$.

The integral generic-base-change input is Sun, arXiv:1511.01393v1,
Definition 2.2, p. 3, Lemma 2.3(iii), pp. 4–5, and Theorem 2.5(i), p. 7,
with $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$. In Lemma 2.3(iii)’s proof, one dense open
works for *all* finite coefficient levels, by the finite set of
Jordan–Hölder constituents. The constant $\mathbb Z_\ell$ system has the
single simple constituent $\mathbb F_\ell$. Constructibility and
preservation of stratifiability are part of the stated setup and Remark
2.5.1(ii). We use this only over a finite-type $\mathbb F_p$-base,
$p\ne\ell$, which satisfies the finite cohomological-dimension condition
(LO).

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Characteristic zero follows already from AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE
and AQ-14-FORWARD. Thus assume $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$ and
$\ell\ne p$. We first give the three facts needed for specialization.

*1. A correspondence with proper target descends to Suslin
zero-homology.* For $\Gamma\in\mathrm{CH}^i(X\times T)$, the
fibre-evaluation map $Z_0(T)\to\mathrm{CH}^i(X)$ kills every Suslin
boundary. Indeed, a generator of a Suslin one-chain is an integral
$W\subset\mathbb A^1\times T$ finite and surjective over $\mathbb A^1$.
Its normalization $C$ is a smooth curve, finite over $W$ and over
$\mathbb A^1$. Let $\overline C$ be its smooth projective completion.
Pull $\Gamma$ back along the morphism $X\times C\to X\times T$; pullback
is well defined between smooth varieties by the graph/refined Gysin
construction. Extend a representing cycle by closure to a class on
$X\times\overline C$.

The function $t/(t-1)$ on $\overline C$ has divisor equal to the fibre
over zero minus the fibre over one, including multiplicities, and no
contribution over infinity. The normalization projection formula
identifies their images in $Z_0(T)$ with the two faces of $W$. Applying
the extended correspondence to this principal divisor gives zero in
$\mathrm{CH}^i(X)$. Its evaluations at points of $C$ agree with the
original fibre evaluations, since refined pullback is local around those
fibres. This proves the claim and produces a homomorphism
$$\Gamma_*:H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^i(X).
                                                               \tag{AQ.14.4}$$
The construction is compatible with extension of the base field and with
automorphisms of that field. No pushforward along the nonproper
projection $X\times T\to X$ was used.

*2. The integral $H^1$ of the semiabelian Albanese.* The homology
coefficient sequence and (AQ.14.3) give a natural isomorphism
$$H_1^S(T,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)
             \simeq G_T(k)[\ell^\infty].                    \tag{AQ.14.5}$$
For clarity, the coefficient sequence has left term
$H_1^S(T,\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ and right term
$\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z),
\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)=H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)[\ell^\infty]$; the
left term is zero by (AQ.14.3).

The prime-to-$p$ finite pairings of Geisser–Schmidt are natural and
compatible with the coefficient homomorphisms. Dualizing the direct
system $H_1^S(T,\mathbb Z/\ell^n)$, whose transition is induced by
$\mathbb Z/\ell^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z/\ell^{n+1}$, identifies its
dual with the inverse system $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(T,\mathbb Z/\ell^n)$
under reduction. The pairings are viewed in
$\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ via
$1\bmod\ell^n\mapsto\ell^{-n}\bmod\mathbb Z_\ell$; this makes the
transition compatibility explicit. Thus
$$H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(T,\mathbb Z_\ell)
  \simeq\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(
       G_T(k)[\ell^\infty],\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\bigr)
  \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}
       (T_\ell G_T,\mathbb Z_\ell).                         \tag{AQ.14.6}$$
The left side is the inverse limit of the finite groups; there is no
$\varprojlim^1 H^0$ obstruction, because the constant $H^0$ transition
maps are surjective. All isomorphisms are functorial. Since $G_T$ is its
own Serre Albanese, the Albanese morphism $a_T:T\to G_T$ induces an
isomorphism on $H^1(-,\mathbb Z_\ell)$. This conclusion is deliberately
integral-adic, not an assertion about $H^1(-,\mathbb Z/\ell^n)$ for each
$n$.

*3. The finite-field vanishing.* Suppose for the moment that
$k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. Every class in $H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)^0$ is
torsion. To see this, join the finitely many points in a representative
by finitely many integral curves in the connected smooth quasiprojective
variety $T$, and pass to their normalizations. Such curves can be chosen
through any prescribed finite set by hyperplane sections of sufficiently
high degree; only the resulting curves and their normalizations are
used. Differences of points lift to degree-zero classes on these smooth
open curves. For a smooth curve $C$, $H_0^S(C,\mathbb Z)^0$ is the group
of points of its Rosenlicht generalized Jacobian, equivalently the curve
case of Geisser, Theorem 1.3, p. 3. Each such Jacobian and each of its
points are defined over some finite extension of a finite field, and its
rational points over that finite field form a finite group. Each lifted
class, and hence each original degree-zero class, is torsion.

Let $v\in H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)^0$ have zero image in $B_T(k)$, and let
$v_\ell$ denote its $\ell$-primary component. Under (AQ.14.3), the class
$v_\ell$ corresponds to an element of $S_T(k)[\ell^\infty]$. Consider
the homomorphism $$S_T(k)[\ell^\infty]\longrightarrow
 H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)[\ell^\infty]
 \xrightarrow{\Gamma_*}\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]
 \xrightarrow{\lambda_X^i}
 H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(i)).                \tag{AQ.14.7}$$
All varieties, maps and $\Gamma$ descend to a common finite field
$\mathbb F_q$, after enlarging it if needed. Thus (AQ.14.7) is
Frobenius-equivariant. On $V_\ell S_T=T_\ell S_T\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$
geometric Frobenius has eigenvalues $q^{-1}$ times roots of unity: a
torus splits over a finite extension of $\mathbb F_q$. On
$H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$ its eigenvalues have complex absolute
value $q^{-1/2}$, by Deligne (1974), Theorem (1.6), printed p. 276,
after the indicated Tate twist. These spectra are disjoint. A linear map
commuting with Frobenius between these two spaces is zero: apply
relatively prime characteristic polynomials and Bézout’s identity, or
use generalized eigenspaces after extending scalars. Semisimplicity is
not required for this step.

The rational Tate module of the last group in (AQ.14.7) is exactly
$H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$. More explicitly, that group has maximal
divisible subgroup $H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Z_\ell(i))_{\mathrm{free}}
 \otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ and finite quotient contained in
$H^{2i}(X,\mathbb Z_\ell(i))[\ell^\infty]$. The domain of (AQ.14.7) is
divisible. Its image is therefore in the maximal divisible subgroup, and
a homomorphism between these finite-corank divisible $\ell$-groups is
zero if its map of rational Tate modules is zero. Consequently (AQ.14.7)
is zero, and $\lambda_X^i(\Gamma_*v_\ell)=0$.

It follows that over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ the conclusion (AQ.14.2)
holds: in a finite sum of generators each $H_0^S$ class is torsion; its
prime-to-$\ell$ component maps to prime-to-$\ell$ torsion, whereas the
$\ell$-primary component has zero Bloch image by the preceding argument.
Taking the $\ell$-primary component of an expression for
$x\in N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)\cap\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]$ gives the
claimed vanishing.

*4. Spreading to the finite-field situation.* Now take arbitrary
algebraically closed $k$ of characteristic $p>0$ and a fixed
$x\in N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)\cap\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]$. Choose a
finite expression $x=\sum_j\Gamma_{j*}v_j$, with $v_j\in Z_0(T_j)^0$
mapping to zero in $B_{T_j}(k)$, and choose $r$ with $\ell^r x=0$.
Spread all varieties, finite zero-cycles, correspondences,
rational-equivalence witnesses for these equalities, and the pointed
maps $T_j\to G_{T_j}\to B_{T_j}$ over an integral finite-type
$\mathbb F_p$-scheme $S$. After shrinking, $S$ is regular, $X/S$ is
smooth projective, the $T_j/S$ are smooth with geometrically connected
fibres, and the $G_j/S$ are semiabelian schemes in exact sequences
$0\to S_j\to G_j\to B_j\to0$ with tori $S_j$ and abelian schemes $B_j$.
The sections representing the zero-cycles remain inside $T_j$, and the
relations $B_j(v_j)=0$ persist. The spread maps need not be relative
universal Albanese morphisms.

Apply Sun’s integral generic base change simultaneously to the finitely
many $T_j\to S$ and $G_j\to S$. Their $R^1(-)_*\mathbb Z_\ell$ are
constructible adic sheaves, and become lisse on a further dense open. By
(AQ.14.6) the map induced by $T_j\to G_j$ is an isomorphism at a
geometric generic point. Indeed its base extension to $k$ is the chosen
Albanese map, and prime-to-$p$ étale cohomology is unchanged by
extension of algebraically closed fields. Its kernel and cokernel vanish
on a further dense open, so this is an isomorphism of integral adic
sheaves there. Generic base change now identifies its stalk at *every*
geometric point of that open with the actual map on
$H^1(-,\mathbb Z_\ell)$ of the fibre. This uses one open for the full
integral system, not separate opens for infinitely many coefficient
levels.

Choose a closed point $s$ in this open; its residue field is finite.
Over $\overline{k(s)}$, the map $T_{j,s}\to G_{j,s}$ factors by
universality as $$T_{j,s}\longrightarrow G_{T_{j,s}}
                  \xrightarrow{h_j}G_{j,s}.$$ By (AQ.14.6), $T_\ell h_j$
is an isomorphism. A homomorphism of semiabelian varieties whose Tate
module is an isomorphism is an isogeny with no $\ell$-primary kernel: a
positive-dimensional connected kernel or quotient has a nonzero Tate
module, and a nonzero finite $\ell$-primary kernel makes the Tate-module
image a proper sublattice. The induced homomorphism of abelian quotients
$$b_j:B_{T_{j,s}}\longrightarrow B_{j,s}$$ is also an isogeny with no
$\ell$-primary kernel. Indeed an isogeny of semiabelian varieties maps
the torus onto the torus; the kernel of its map of abelian quotients is
a quotient of its finite kernel. The first assertion follows since the
resulting quotient of the target by the image torus is a quotient of the
proper source abelian variety, and hence has zero toric part.

The equality $B_{j,s}(v_{j,s})=0$ and injectivity of $b_j$ on
$\ell$-primary torsion imply that the $\ell$-primary component of the
actual abelian Albanese class of $v_{j,s}$ is zero. Although the whole
class need not vanish, this is precisely what step 3 needs: the
$\ell$-primary Suslin class of $v_{j,s}$ corresponds to the actual torus
$S_{T_{j,s}}$. Thus the argument in step 3 gives
$\lambda_{X_s}^i(x_s)=0$.

Finally specialize from a geometric generic point to $s$ along a regular
flag through $s$, as in the complete flag construction in AQ-14-FORWARD.
The finite cycle and rational-equivalence data make $x_s$ the
specialization of $x$, preserve $\ell^r x_s=0$, and identify each
specialized fibre evaluation with $\Gamma_{j,s*}v_{j,s}$. Refined Gysin
functoriality provides this compatibility locally at the sections.
Bloch’s specialization theorem sends $\lambda_X^i(x)$ to
$\lambda_{X_s}^i(x_s)$, and smooth proper base change for $X/S$ makes
the cohomological specialization an isomorphism. Cohomological
invariance under algebraically closed extension also identifies the
original $k$-fibre with the geometric generic fibre for this
calculation. Therefore $\lambda_X^i(x)=0$, proving (AQ.14.2).

For the final cohomological implication, lift each $\ell$-power torsion
point of $A$ to its torsion zero-cycle class by prime-to-$p$ Roitman.
The integral premise puts its image under $Z$ in the left side of
(AQ.14.2). Bloch correspondence compatibility, followed by Tate modules
and tensoring with $\mathbb Q_\ell$, therefore kills
$Z_*:H^{2\dim A-1}(A,\mathbb Q_\ell(\dim A))
\to H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$. Poincaré duality gives exactly the
displayed transpose. This is the same final argument as AQ-14-FORWARD,
now with the broader relation. For the semiabelian-kernel convention,
its relation is contained in the abelian-kernel relation, so the torsion
vanishing and the implication follow as well. ◻

</div>

## AQ-14-ALTERATION: the two parameter relations agree away from the characteristic

#### Statement.

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $p>0$, and let $X/k$
be smooth projective. Define
$N^i_{\rm proj}(X)\subset N^i_{\rm qp}(X)\subset CH^i(X)$ using the
projective and smooth quasiprojective parameters, respectively, and the
ordinary abelian Albanese, exactly as in AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE. Then
$$N^i_{\rm qp}(X)/N^i_{\rm proj}(X)
 \quad\hbox{is a $p$-primary torsion group.}               \tag{AQ.ALT.1}$$
In particular the relations agree after tensoring with $\mathbb Z[1/p]$,
hence also with $\mathbb Q$. The natural map between the two cycle
quotients has $p$-primary kernel and induces an isomorphism on
$\ell$-primary torsion for every $\ell\ne p$. The same assertions hold
if the nonproper relation uses zero cycles annihilated by the
semiabelian Albanese. Together with AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE this gives
equality with rational coefficients in every characteristic. This is
retained as an intermediate theorem. The companion theorems CC-ALB01–02
subsequently prove that this kernel is zero, by divisibility and the
fixed-alteration-degree quantifier made explicit below.

#### Exact external inputs.

The separability refinement is needed here: Illusie and Temkin (2014),
Exposé X, Theorem 3.5(iii), printed p. 202, gives a *separable*
projective prime-to-$\ell$ alteration with regular source over a perfect
field. Their Theorem 2.1 alone is not the separability statement; the
distinction is explicit on p. 167 and in §3.5.4, p. 207. The other input
is the extension of a rational map from a smooth variety to an abelian
variety to a morphism on the whole source: Milne, *Abelian Varieties*,
version 2.0 (March 16, 2008), Chapter I, Theorem 3.2, printed p. 17. We
use these as cited classical inputs. The moving and trace arguments
needed for their application are proved below.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* *1. Moving zero cycles inside a dense open using Suslin
relations.* For a smooth connected quasiprojective $T$ and a nonempty
open $U\subset T$, every class in $H^S_0(T,\mathbb Z)$ is represented by
a zero cycle supported in $U$. The replacement can be chosen with the
same degree. We prove this directly on point generators. There is
nothing to prove for a point already in $U$, or if $\dim T=0$. Fix
$x\in T\setminus U$. An affine neighborhood of $x$ embeds into affine
space. Successive general hyperplanes through $x$ cut its dimension down
to one and cut $T\setminus U$ down to dimension zero in that
neighborhood. At each step one avoids finitely many positive-dimensional
components; this is possible because $k$ is infinite and the linear
system has only the point $x$ as its base locus. An irreducible
one-dimensional component through $x$ therefore meets $U$. Its closure
in $T$ is an integral curve $C_0$ through $x$, still meeting $U$. Let
$C\to C_0$ be its normalization and choose $x'\in C$ above $x$. The
normalization is finite, and $C$ is a smooth curve because $k$ is
perfect. Let $\overline C$ be its smooth projective completion,
$D=\overline C\setminus C$, and $F=C\setminus(C\times_T U)$. Both $D$
and $F$ are finite sets. Choose $y\in C\times_T U$.

For sufficiently large $n$, Riemann–Roch gives a rational function $f$
on $\overline C$, with poles only at $y$, whose first-order jet at $x'$
is a uniformizer and whose value at every point of
$D\cup(F\setminus\{x'\})$ is $1$. Explicitly the required evaluation map
is surjective because the first cohomology of $$\mathcal O_{\overline C}
 \left(ny-2x'-\sum_{z\in D\cup(F\setminus\{x'\})}z\right)$$ vanishes
once its degree exceeds $2g(\overline C)-2$. Thus $f$ has a simple zero
at $x'$ and no zero or pole at the other points of $D\cup F$. Its
divisor is of the form $$\operatorname{div}(f)=[x']+z-a[y],
 \qquad \operatorname{supp}(z)\subset C\times_T U,$$ where $z$ is
effective and $a>0$. This divisor is a Suslin boundary on $C$: the
nonconstant morphism $g=f/(f-1):\overline C\to\mathbb P^1$ is finite,
and $g^{-1}(\mathbb A^1)\subset C$ since $f=1$ on $D$. Its graph gives a
finite correspondence from $\mathbb A^1$ to $C$; its fibres at $0$ and
$1$ differ by $\operatorname{div}(f)$, up to the harmless choice of
boundary sign. Push this correspondence to $T$. It shows that $[x]$ has
a same-degree representative supported in $U$. Linearity proves the
moving assertion.

Both maps used in our relation respect these Suslin boundaries. For the

## AQ-14-CONVERSE: the generated-quotient converse fails

For the generated quotient just defined, over $\mathbb C$ the converse

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The cycle class of $Z$ is zero because that of $\gamma$ is

from Walker’s Theorem 5.7(2),(4), p. 930, and Example 5.5, p. 928. If

#### AQ-ABELIAN-RECOGNITION.

Fix $k=\mathbb F_q$, $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$, and let
$\mathcal A_q$ be the rational numerical category obtained from the
motives of abelian varieties over $k$, finite étale $k$-schemes, and
Tate motives by finite sums, tensor products, duals, and direct
summands. Equivalently, its objects are direct summands of finite sums
$$h^i(A)(n)\otimes h^0(S),\qquad
 A/k\text{ abelian},\quad S/k\text{ finite \'etale},
 \quad n\in\mathbb Z.$$ We use geometric Frobenius $F$ and
$\mathbb Q_\ell(1)$ with eigenvalue $q^{-1}$. The summand above has
weight $w=i-2n$ and parity $i\bmod2=w\bmod2$. Let $R_\ell(M)$ be its
ordinary direct-sum realization, with the weight decomposition
remembered. Then
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_\ell
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q_\ell[F]}
       (R_\ell(M),R_\ell(N)).                         \tag{AQ.15}$$
Moreover, for $M,N\in\mathcal A_q$,
$$M\simeq N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P_M(t)=P_N(t),\qquad
 P_M(t)=\det(1-tF\mid R_\ell(M)).                    \tag{AQ.16}$$ The
same equivalence holds with the weight-signed Euler factor
$$Z_M(t)=\prod_w
 \det(1-tF\mid R_\ell(M)_w)^{(-1)^{w+1}}            \tag{AQ.17}$$ in
place of $P_M$. These statements are in the rational numerical category,
and concern actual objects with their full multiplicities.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* First we explain the scalar extension in (AQ.15). For an
abelian variety $X/k$, numerical and $\ell$-adic homological equivalence
agree by the cited all-abelian theorem. Let
$A^r_{\rm num}(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ denote the space of rational cycles
modulo numerical equivalence. The numerical intersection pairing with
$A^{\dim X-r}_{\rm num}(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is nondegenerate. For any finite
linearly independent collection $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s$ of numerical
classes, one can choose complementary classes $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_s$
making $(\deg(\alpha_i\beta_j))_{i,j}$ invertible over $\mathbb Q$.
Their cohomology classes are then linearly independent over
$\mathbb Q_\ell$, by pairing against the $\beta_j$. This also bounds $s$
by the finite Betti number, proving finiteness and injectivity after
extension to $\mathbb Q_\ell$. The full Tate theorem supplies
surjectivity onto the Frobenius-invariant subspace. Hence
$$A^r_{\rm num}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell
 \simeq H^{2r}(X_{\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell(r))^{F=1}.
                                                               \tag{AQ.18}$$

The cohomological projectors of an abelian variety are algebraic here
without a further conjecture. For any integer $a\geq2$, multiplication
by $a$ acts on $H^j(A)$ as $a^j$. The polynomials
$$e_i=\prod_{\substack{0\leq j\leq2\dim A\\j\ne i}}
       \frac{[a]^*-a^j}{a^i-a^j}$$ give its projectors in numerical
correspondences: their required identities hold in cohomology, hence
numerically by the established equivalence on $A\times A$. Tensor
products are realized using $A\times B$, and Poincaré duality expresses
duals as complementary cohomological degrees with Tate twists. This
proves the stated description of $\mathcal A_q$.

Consider first $M=h^i(A)(n)$ and $N=h^j(B)(m)$, and set $a=\dim A$. A
correspondence between them has codimension $a+m-n$ on $A\times B$. Its
relevant Künneth component, by Poincaré duality, is
$$H^{2a-i}(A)\otimes H^j(B)(a+m-n)
 \simeq\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(H^i(A)(n),H^j(B)(m)\bigr).$$ It lies in
the required cohomological degree precisely when $j-i=2(m-n)$,
equivalently when the weights agree. In that case (AQ.18) for
$A\times B$, followed by the algebraic projectors, gives exactly
(AQ.15). For unequal weights every Frobenius-equivariant map is zero:
the eigenvalues have complex absolute values $q^{(i-2n)/2}$ and
$q^{(j-2m)/2}$, so their spectra are disjoint. The numerical
correspondence group is also zero, since its cohomological component has
the wrong degree and homological equals numerical equivalence.

For finite étale factors $S,T$, the schemes $A\times B\times S\times T$
are disjoint unions of abelian varieties viewed over finite extensions
of $k$. A Frobenius-invariant class on a component orbit is determined
by a class invariant under the appropriate power of Frobenius on one
component. The all-abelian Tate theorem over that finite extension
supplies the cycle. Intersection pairings are multiplied by the nonzero
extension degree, which is invertible over $\mathbb Q$. Thus the same
injection and surjection apply to these factors. Passing to finite sums
and applying source and target idempotents proves (AQ.15) for every
object of $\mathcal A_q$.

Frobenius acts semisimply on all these realizations. For $H^1(A)$ this
follows from Tate (1966), Theorem 2(a), printed p. 140:
$\mathbb Q[\pi_A]$ is the centre of the semisimple algebra
$\operatorname{End}_k(A)\otimes\mathbb Q$. It is a product of number
fields, so its Frobenius element has square-free minimal polynomial;
dualizing or inverting preserves this property. The identity
$H^i(A)=\bigwedge^i H^1(A)$ shows semisimplicity in every degree. Finite
étale Frobenius has finite order, and tensor products, Tate twists and
invariant direct summands of semisimple single-operator representations
remain semisimple in characteristic zero.

Equality in (AQ.16) therefore gives an isomorphism
$R_\ell(M)\simeq R_\ell(N)$ as $\mathbb Q_\ell[F]$-modules: each
irreducible polynomial in a semisimple operator’s characteristic
polynomial records the multiplicity of its simple
$\mathbb Q_\ell[F]$-module. By (AQ.15) this is an invertible element of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$. Choose a
rational basis $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ of this Hom space. The polynomial
$$D(x_1,\ldots,x_s)=
 \det R_\ell\!\left(\sum_\nu x_\nu f_\nu\right)$$ over $\mathbb Q_\ell$
is not zero. Since $\mathbb Q$ is infinite, some rational tuple has
$D\ne0$; this elementary fact follows by induction on the number of
variables, using the finite number of roots of a nonzero polynomial in
one variable. It gives a rational morphism $f:M\to N$ with invertible
realization. By full faithfulness its realization’s inverse comes from a
morphism in
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(N,M)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$. The
equations $gf=1_M$ and $fg=1_N$ form a finite linear system over
$\mathbb Q$ in the coefficients of $g$. A rational linear system
solvable after extension to $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is solvable over
$\mathbb Q$, by row reduction. Thus $f$ has a rational inverse. The
converse in (AQ.16) is immediate.

Finally a reciprocal root contributed by weight $w$ has complex absolute
value $q^{-w/2}$. Weights of different parity cannot contribute the same
root. Hence the numerator and denominator of (AQ.17) are coprime, each
with constant term one. Equality of those rational functions recovers
their numerator and denominator separately, and their product is
$P_M(t)$. This reduces (AQ.17) to (AQ.16) and completes the proof. ◻

</div>

No essential-surjectivity claim onto all Frobenius representations is
needed here. In particular, this recognition theorem does not identify
an arbitrary smooth projective motive with an object of $\mathcal A_q$,
does not retain integral lattices, and does not classify mixed
extensions. It is a genuine strengthened answer to the abelian sector of
cote 15, supplied by the new cycle-algebraicity theorem rather than by
analytic zero location.

#### AQ-RATIONAL-FIBRE: an explicit neutralization obstruction.

Even this now-established abelian category need not admit a fibre
functor with values in rational vector spaces. This is a concrete
restriction on any reading of the archive’s motivic Galois group as a
neutral group over $\mathbb Q$. It is not a refutation of a group
defined after scalar extension or of a motivic gerbe formulation.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Consider $E:y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbb F_3$, then base-change it
to $k=\mathbb F_9$. It is smooth: the derivative of $x^3-x$ is $-1$ in
characteristic three. For each of $x=0,1,2$, the right side is zero, so
there is one affine point; with infinity, $\#E(\mathbb F_3)=4$. Thus its
$3$-Frobenius polynomial on $H^1$ is $T^2+3$. The square of Frobenius is
$-3$ on $H^1$, and injectivity of the Tate-module action of
endomorphisms implies that the $9$-Frobenius endomorphism is $[-3]$.
Tate (1966), Theorem 2(d), p. 140, identifies
$$D=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_9}(E)\otimes\mathbb Q
   =D_{3,\infty},$$ the quaternion division algebra ramified at $3$ and
infinity. Alternatively, the main theorem on p. 134 gives
$\dim_{\mathbb Q}D=4$, since Frobenius is scalar on a two-dimensional
Tate module; every nonzero endomorphism of an elliptic curve is an
isogeny, so this algebra is a division algebra.

Put $M=h^1(E)$ in $\mathcal A_9$. Pullback endomorphisms give an
injective algebra map $D^{\rm op}\to\operatorname{End}(M)$. By (AQ.15),
the target has rational dimension four, since its scalar extension is
$M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. This map is therefore an isomorphism, so
$\operatorname{End}(M)$ is a quaternion division algebra.

Use the usual parity-corrected symmetry on numerical motives, for which
the ordinary $\ell$-adic realization is a symmetric tensor functor. The
categorical trace of $1_M$ is then $2$: its image is the ordinary trace
of the identity on $H^1(E,\mathbb Q_\ell)$, and the scalar map
$\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q_\ell$ is injective. A rational fibre functor
would preserve this trace, hence send $M$ to a two-dimensional rational
vector space. Its faithfulness would embed the four-dimensional division
algebra $\operatorname{End}(M)$ into $M_2(\mathbb Q)$. Equal dimensions
force this embedding to be an isomorphism. That is impossible because
$M_2(\mathbb Q)$ has nonzero noninvertible elements. This proves the
asserted obstruction.

It also exhibits an object restriction invisible in a list of
eigenvalues alone. Here $P_M(t)=(1+3t)^2$. There cannot be an object
$L\in\mathcal A_9$ with $P_L(t)=1+3t$: otherwise (AQ.16) would give
$L\oplus L\simeq M$, but the former has a nontrivial idempotent
endomorphism and the latter’s division algebra has none. The eigenvalue
$-3$ occurs, as AQ-WEIL-EXIST predicts, but its multiplicity cannot be
discarded in a rational motivic classification. ◻

## AQ-COCYCLE: the image-density statement at 18/059

Let $P$ be an affine torsor under an algebraic group $G$ over
$F\subset\mathbb C$, and $p\in P(\mathbb C)$. The closure of the image
of its automorphism cocycle is the translated $F$-locus of $p$. Thus the
image is Zariski dense in $G_{\mathbb C}$ exactly when $p$ is generic in
$P$ over $F$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Embed $P$ in affine space over $F$, and let $I$ be the ideal
over $\mathbb C$ of the $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/F)$-orbit of $p$.
Each finite-dimensional degree-bounded part of $I$ is stable under the
coefficientwise semilinear action. Its unique reduced-row-echelon basis,
relative to an ordered monomial basis, must therefore have entries fixed
by every automorphism. The fixed field of
$\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/F)$ is $F$: any nontrivial embedding of
$F(a)$ over $F$, for $a\notin F$, extends first across a transcendence
basis and then an algebraic closure to an automorphism of $\mathbb C$.
Consequently every degree-bounded part has a basis over $F$, and
$I=(I\cap F[\mathbf x])\mathbb C[\mathbf x]$. An $F$-polynomial vanishes
on the orbit exactly when it vanishes at $p$. Thus $I$ is precisely the
ideal of $Z_F(p)_{\mathbb C}$, proving the density assertion. The torsor
difference map $D_p:P_{\mathbb C}\to G_{\mathbb C}$ is an algebraic
isomorphism and the cocycle is $D_p(\sigma p)$, up to inversion
according to convention. Its closure is consequently
$D_p(Z_F(p)_{\mathbb C})$. ◻

</div>

The generated-subgroup version is false for general torsors. In
$G=\mathbb G_m^2$ take $F=\mathbb Q$ and $p=(t,t+1)$ with $t$
transcendental. Its locus is a curve. The translated cocycle curve is
$u\mapsto(u/t,(u+1)/(t+1))$. A character $x^a y^b$ identically one there
would have orders zero at $u=0$ and $u=-1$, giving $a=b=0$. Thus this
proper curve generates the whole torus. This refutes an abstract torsor
inference, without claiming an arithmetic-motivic counterexample to the
period conjecture.

## AQ-CM: the hexagonal elliptic curve and a genuine GRH consequence

The hexagonal real-elliptic classification recorded in SA22, §2(A.7),
concerns the $j=0$ period lattice and its real forms. It is not the
Grothendieck–Teichmüller hexagon. For example, the latter is the
identity $$f(z,x)z^m f(y,z)y^m f(x,y)x^m=1,\qquad
 z=(xy)^{-1},\quad m=(\lambda-1)/2,$$ in a profinite free group, as in
Boavida de Brito, Horel, and Robertson (2019), Definition 7.2(II). The
two uses of *hexagonal* name different mathematical data.

#### Hodge obstruction.

For $E:y^2=x^3+1$, the motive $h^1(E)$ is not Artin–Tate, even after
adjoining its CM field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* An Artin motive has Hodge type $(0,0)$, and $\mathbb Q(n)$ has
type $(-n,-n)$ in our convention. Tensor products, sums, subobjects, and
quotients of such Hodge structures have only diagonal types. But
$H^1(E)$ has nonzero types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Base extension to an
algebraic number field does not change these complex Hodge types. This
proves the claim. ◻

</div>

Thus the fact that the CM field is abelian does not turn its weight-one
Hecke character into a finite-order Dirichlet character with an integral
Tate twist. No conclusion about the zeros of $L(h^1(E),s)$ is obtained
merely by substituting that field in the supplied Dirichlet theorem.

#### Effective distribution of trace-zero primes.

Let $a_p=p+1-\#E(\mathbb F_p)$, for $p>3$, and put
$N_0(x)=\#\{3<p\leq x:a_p=0\}$. Then
$$a_p=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ p\equiv2\pmod3,\qquad
 N_0(x)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Li}(x)+O(\sqrt x\log x)            \tag{AQ.7}$$
under the adopted Dirichlet GRH. The implied constant is effective.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* With the quadratic character extended by $\chi_p(0)=0$,
$$a_p=-\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi_p(x^3+1).$$ If $p\equiv2\bmod3$,
cubing permutes $\mathbb F_p$, so the sum is $\sum_u\chi_p(u+1)=0$. If
$p\equiv1\bmod3$, set $m=(p-1)/2$. Euler’s criterion and the binomial
theorem show $$a_p\equiv-\sum_{j=0}^m {m\choose j}
                 \sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}x^{3j}
       ={m\choose (p-1)/3}\pmod p.$$ The $j=0$ sum is $p=0$ modulo $p$.
Among the positive exponents $3j\leq3(p-1)/2$, the only multiple of
$p-1$ is $p-1$, and its power sum is $-1$ modulo $p$. The displayed
binomial coefficient is nonzero modulo $p$ because all its factorial
arguments are less than $p$. This proves the equivalence.

Now $\zeta_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})}(s)=
\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{-3})$, so the adopted theorem gives GRH for this
quadratic Dedekind zeta function. Lagarias–Odlyzko (1977), Theorem 1.1,
equation (1.3), applied to its nontrivial Frobenius class, gives
$$\left|\pi(x;3,2)-\tfrac12\operatorname{Li}(x)\right|
 \leq c_1\left(\tfrac12\sqrt x\log(3x^2)+\log3\right),
 \qquad x>2.$$ Here $c_1$ is their effective absolute constant. For
$x\geq3$, $N_0(x)=\pi(x;3,2)-1$ because $p=2$ is a bad prime of $E$.
This proves (AQ.7). ◻

</div>

This gives an effective arithmetic statement about the hexagonal curve
using the quadratic splitting character. It does not replace that
curve’s weight-one Hecke $L$-function by a Dirichlet $L$-function.

## AQ-67-RANGE: the global isomorphism in 67/078 is false

The literal question is *Ces flèches sont elles des iso — et si oui,
comment expliciter un iso en sens inverse ?* In the stable nodal $(0,4)$
case, normalize both three-pointed rational components with the node at
$0$ and the other two marked points at $1,\infty$. In the tangent-tensor
normalization of the capping construction, a tensor $q$ in the image has
absolute value equal to the product of the two node-cap conformal radii.
Consequently
$$|q|\leq16,\qquad q=17\ \hbox{has no preimage}.                  \tag{AQ.8}$$
If the target is written with dual tangent tensors, the equivalent
statement uses the inverse tensor. This normalization is fixed by the
original sheets: 67/075 calls the propagation datum a nonzero basis of
$T_{x'}\otimes T_{x''}$; 67/078 writes its inverse as a dual pairing and
asks for mutually polar disks in the two tangent lines. In coordinates,
polarity is $|q^{-1}|r'r''=1$, hence $r'r''=|q|$. The target in
67/076–077 imposes no bound on this nonzero tensor. We use the map
without additional marked-point disks in 67/077.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* For completeness, the Koebe bound needed here follows from the
area argument. If $F(z)=z+b_0+\sum_{n\geq1}b_nz^{-n}$ is univalent for
$|z|>1$, Green’s formula gives the area inside $F(|z|=r)$ as
$\pi(r^2-\sum_{n\geq1}n|b_n|^2r^{-2n})\geq0$. Letting $r\downarrow1$
proves $\sum_{n\geq1}n|b_n|^2\leq1$. For a univalent
$f(z)=z+a_2z^2+\cdots$ in the unit disk,
$g(z)=\sqrt{f(z^2)}=z+(a_2/2)z^3+\cdots$ is an odd univalent function
there. The branch exists because $f(z^2)/z^2$ never vanishes in the
disk. Equality of two $g$-values forces equality of the squared
arguments under $f$, and oddness then proves injectivity. Its reciprocal
$1/g(1/z)$ is univalent outside the disk, so the area inequality gives
$|a_2|\leq2$. If $w\ne0$ is omitted by $f$, the normalized univalent
function $f/(1-f/w)$ has second coefficient $a_2+1/w$. Thus
$|1/w|\leq|a_2|+|a_2+1/w|\leq4$. This is the Koebe quarter theorem
(Duren, 1983, Theorem 2.3, p. 31).

A node cap is the image of a univalent map $h$ from the unit disk with
$h(0)=0$ and conformal radius $r=|h'(0)|$. It omits $1$ and $\infty$.
Apply the preceding conclusion to $h/h'(0)$ and the omitted value
$1/h'(0)$ to get $r\leq4$. Both caps therefore have radii at most four.
The tensor is the product of the two derivative vectors, up to the
unit-modulus boundary identification, so its norm is at most sixteen.
The nonzero tensor $17$ belongs to the stated unrestricted target,
establishing failure of surjectivity. ◻

</div>

The intake SA22 reports the sharper exact image $0<|q|<16$, tangent
invertibility, and a $\pi_1$-isomorphism. Its full prior proofs are not
in the supplied packet. The next result independently supplies complete
proofs for the labelled geodesic-cut $(0,4)$ sector, while AQ-67-NONHOLO
separates the real and complex tangent claims. The elementary
obstruction (AQ.8) already answers 67/078 negatively and is independent
of RH.

## AQ-67-EXACT: the canonical radius and the exact labelled $(0,4)$ image

#### Scope and normalization.

Consider the geodesic-cut interpretation of the map without marked-point
disks in cote 67/077. Fix a partition of four labelled punctures into
two pairs and remember the separating geodesic. Its length is $\ell>0$.
Cutting produces two hyperbolic pairs of pants of boundary lengths
$(\ell,0,0)$, where zero denotes a cusp. Cap the geodesic boundary by
the unit disk using its normalized hyperbolic arclength, as prescribed
in 67/074. On each resulting three-pointed sphere use the unique
coordinate with cap centre $0$ and the two labelled cusps $1,\infty$.
Let $r(\ell)>0$ be the conformal radius of the cap at zero in that
coordinate. A cap coordinate $w$ has $|w|=1$ at the seam and
$z=r(\ell)w+O(w^2)$ after a rotation of $w$. The tensor coordinate $q$
of 67/075 then has modulus $r(\ell)^2$.

Put $$x=\frac{\ell}{4\pi},\qquad
 \phi(x)=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\log\Gamma(1+ix)
                       -\log\Gamma(\tfrac12+ix)\bigr),$$ where the
analytic logarithm of $\Gamma$ on the right half-plane is real on the
positive real axis. Then
$$\boxed{\quad r(\ell)=4\exp\!\left(\frac{\phi(x)-\pi/4}{x}\right).\quad}
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.1}$$
The function $r:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,4)$ is a real-analytic
diffeomorphism. With the orientation of the normalized twist angle
$\theta\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$ chosen so that its increase
multiplies the tangent tensor by $e^{i\theta}$, the capping map is
$$(\ell,\theta)\longmapsto r(\ell)^2e^{i\theta}.
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.2}$$
It is a real-analytic diffeomorphism onto $0<|q|<16$. Its real tangent
map is invertible everywhere, and its composite with the inclusion
$\{0<|q|<16\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^\times$ induces an isomorphism of
fundamental groups. These are statements about this labelled
geodesic-cut sector. They do not assert complex holomorphicity or
algebraic *étaleness* of the map in 67/077–082, nor do they settle the
full multi-node question.

#### Geometric inputs and source locators.

The hyperbolic pair of pants with specified nonnegative boundary lengths
is unique, with zero lengths interpreted as cusps. Its Fenchel–Nielsen
length and twist parametrization is recalled in Mondello (2011), §2.2,
p. 640. The canonical attachment here is the infinite flat-cylinder
grafting of Mondello, §5.4, Theorem 5.4, p. 660; a punctured flat
cylinder is conformally the punctured disk with the arclength
parametrization used above. For an explicit independent source for the
projective-connection calculation below, see Fırat (2021), equations
(3.11), (3.13), (3.14a), (3.23), and (3.30)–(3.34), arXiv:2102.03936v2,
pp. 14–17 and 24–25. We specialize the two other ends to cusps and
determine the branch directly below; we do not infer the branch from
that paper’s integer-valued numerical prescription (3.27).

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Write $\lambda=\ell/(2\pi)$, so $x=\lambda/2$. The developing
map $D$ of the hyperbolic metric is a local isometry to a half-plane.
Near a geodesic of length $2\pi\lambda$, diagonalize its hyperbolic
monodromy, with fixed points $0,\infty$. In the arclength cap coordinate
its analytic continuation is, up to a nonzero constant, $w^{i\lambda}$.
Indeed, increasing $\arg w$ by $2\pi$ multiplies this function by
$e^{-2\pi\lambda}$, and along $|w|=1$ its hyperbolic arclength is
$\lambda\,d\arg w$. The same expression supplies the analytic
continuation across the seam. Its Schwarzian derivative therefore has a
double pole at the cap centre with leading coefficient
$(1+\lambda^2)/2$. At a cusp its leading coefficient is $1/2$, as
follows from the local developing coordinate $\log(z-z_0)$. There are no
other poles. On the sphere with centres and cusps $0,1,\infty$, the
resulting Schwarzian is consequently $$T(z)=\frac{1+\lambda^2}{2z^2}
       +\frac1{2(z-1)^2}
       -\frac{1+\lambda^2}{2z(z-1)}.                           \tag{AQ.67.3}$$
To see uniqueness, any two such projective connections differ by a
quadratic differential with at most simple poles at three points; this
space is $H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-1))=0$.

Set $a=(1+i\lambda)/2$. Direct substitution in $y''+\tfrac12T(z)y=0$
gives the two solutions $$y_\pm(z)=z^{(1\pm i\lambda)/2}(1-z)^{1/2}
     {}_2F_1\!\left(\frac{1\pm i\lambda}{2},
                    \frac{1\pm i\lambda}{2};
                    1\pm i\lambda;z\right).$$ The ratio, normalized by
its leading term at zero, is $$D(z)=\frac{y_+(z)}{y_-(z)}
     =z^{i\lambda}
       \frac{{}_2F_1(a,a;2a;z)}
            {{}_2F_1(\bar a,\bar a;2\bar a;z)}.                \tag{AQ.67.4}$$
All developing maps with the prescribed Schwarzian differ by a Möbius
transformation. The ratio in (AQ.67.4) has the same diagonal monodromy
at zero; hence it is a valid developing map after changing the target
half-plane by that transformation. Its boundary still contains the two
fixed points $0,\infty$; thus it is a straight half-plane bounded by a
line through zero, rather than a disk with a different boundary circle.

We now fix the phase without an unspecified multiple of $\pi$. On the
real interval $0<z<1$, $y_- =\overline{y_+}$. Their Wronskian, computed
at zero, is $y_+'y_- -y_+y_-'=i\lambda$. In particular neither solution
vanishes there, and the continuously lifted argument $\Theta(z)$ of
$D(z)$, normalized by $\Theta(z)-\lambda\log z\longrightarrow0$ as
$z\downarrow0$, satisfies $$\Theta'(z)=\frac{\lambda}{|y_+(z)|^2}>0.
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.5}$$
The zero-balanced hypergeometric asymptotic $${}_2F_1(a,a;2a;z)
   \sim\frac{\Gamma(2a)}{\Gamma(a)^2}[-\log(1-z)]
       \qquad (z\uparrow1)$$ is DLMF (15.4.21). Thus
$$\lim_{z\uparrow1}\Theta(z)=\vartheta(\lambda),\qquad
 \vartheta(\lambda)=2\operatorname{Im}
       \left(\log\Gamma(1+i\lambda)
                    -2\log\Gamma(\tfrac12+i\lambda/2)\right).
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.6}$$
This is an equality of lifted arguments. For clarity, the asymptotic is
locally uniform in $\lambda\geq0$. The hypergeometric factor on $0<z<1$
has no zeros when $\lambda>0$ by the Wronskian, and at $\lambda=0$ it is
positive. Its continuous logarithm, zero at $z=0$, therefore has
limiting phase equal to the indicated continuous gamma phase, rather
than that phase plus a nonzero multiple of $2\pi$. Equivalently that
possible integer is locally constant in $\lambda$ and is zero at
$\lambda=0$.

The pair of pants has its reflection fixing the perpendicular seams.
After capping, this antiholomorphic involution fixes $0,1,\infty$, so it
is complex conjugation. The perpendicular seam from the cusp at $1$ to
the geodesic boundary is the terminal segment of the real interval
$(0,1)$. Its developing image lies on the unit circle, by (AQ.67.4). The
cusp endpoint has phase $\vartheta$. By (AQ.67.5), approaching that cusp
from the core uses phases less than $\vartheta$. Hence the developing
half-plane of the core is the half-plane with argument interval
$(\vartheta-\pi,\vartheta)$. The axis of the boundary monodromy, whose
fixed points are $0,\infty$, is its ray of argument $\vartheta-\pi/2$.
The seam meets that axis perpendicularly. Starting from the cusp, this
is the first intersection of the seam with the axis; the further formal
intersections obtained after crossing the boundary line of that
half-plane are not in the hyperbolic core. This selects the lifted phase
$\vartheta-\pi/2$, with no integer ambiguity.

Choose $w=1$ at this perpendicular foot. On a collar of the geodesic,
and thence by analytic continuation into its cap,
$$w=\exp\left(\frac{\log D-i(\vartheta-\pi/2)}{i\lambda}\right).$$ Here
the logarithm has the lifted phase just specified. Its boundary modulus
is one and its boundary argument has the normalized arclength, so this
is precisely the prescribed cap coordinate. At zero, (AQ.67.4) gives
$$w=\exp\left(-\frac{\vartheta-\pi/2}{\lambda}\right)z+O(z^2),
 \qquad
 r(\ell)=\exp\left(\frac{\vartheta-\pi/2}{\lambda}\right).
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.7}$$
The duplication formula, DLMF (5.5.5), yields
$\vartheta=2\lambda\log2+2\phi(\lambda/2)$, proving (AQ.67.1).

It remains to establish the exact range and the nonvanishing derivative,
rather than only an upper bound. The digamma integral DLMF (5.9.16)
gives, for $x\geq0$, $$\phi'(x)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\psi(1+ix)
                            -\psi(\tfrac12+ix)\bigr)
           =\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(xt)}{e^{t/2}+1}\,dt.
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.8}$$
Let $f(t)=(e^{t/2}+1)^{-1}$. Then
$$f''(t)=\frac{e^{t/2}(e^{t/2}-1)}{4(e^{t/2}+1)^3}>0
               \quad(t>0).$$ The function and its derivatives decay
exponentially. Two integrations by parts, with their endpoint terms
included, give
$$\phi'(x)=\frac1{x^2}\int_0^\infty f''(t)(1-\cos xt)\,dt>0
                  \quad(x>0).                                \tag{AQ.67.9}$$
Stirling’s expansion for the same analytic logarithms, DLMF (5.11.1), or
the corresponding gamma-ratio expansion (5.11.13), gives
$\phi(x)\longrightarrow\pi/4$ as $x\to\infty$. Since $\phi(0)=0$, it
follows that $0<\phi(x)<\pi/4$ for $x>0$. Therefore
$$\frac{d}{dx}\log r(4\pi x)
       =\frac{x\phi'(x)+\pi/4-\phi(x)}{x^2}>0.                 \tag{AQ.67.10}$$
At zero, $\phi'(0)=2\log2$, so
$$r(\ell)=16\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{\ell}+O(\ell^2)\right)
              \quad(\ell\downarrow0),
 \qquad r(\ell)\longrightarrow4\quad(\ell\to\infty).$$ Real analyticity
and (AQ.67.10) prove that $r$ is the asserted diffeomorphism.

Finally Fenchel–Nielsen gluing of the two labelled pairs of pants has
exactly one length $\ell>0$ and one twist modulo $\ell$, equivalently an
angle $\theta$ modulo $2\pi$. The two derivative vectors each have norm
$r(\ell)$, and the twist contributes their tensor’s unit phase. This
proves (AQ.67.2). Its inverse is $\ell=r^{-1}(\sqrt{|q|})$,
$\theta=\arg q$ with the latter viewed as a circle-valued coordinate. In
logarithmic polar coordinates the real Jacobian is diagonal, with
entries $2r'(\ell)/r(\ell)>0$ and $1$. Both the punctured disk and
$\mathbb C^\times$ deformation retract to the same circle $|q|=1$; the
inclusion therefore induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$, taking one full
twist to the positive generator. ◻

</div>

## AQ-67-NONHOLO: the natural complex-analytic formulation fails

Give the labelled geodesic-cut $(0,4)$ space its natural complex
structure, namely the covering
$$\mathcal C=\mathcal T(S_{0,4})/\langle D_\alpha\rangle$$ of the moduli
space of four labelled punctures, where $\alpha$ is the chosen
separating curve and $D_\alpha$ is its Dehn twist. Equivalently, this is
the component of the cover that remembers the isotopy class of the cut.
The two sides and all punctures remain labelled. Fenchel–Nielsen
coordinates upstairs are $(\ell,\tau)\in\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb R$,
with $D_\alpha(\ell,\tau)=(\ell,\tau+\ell)$; downstairs the normalized
angle is $\theta=2\pi\tau/\ell$ modulo $2\pi$. The canonical capping map
of AQ-67-EXACT is not holomorphic from this complex manifold to
$\mathbb C^\times$. Thus its everywhere invertible real tangent map does
not make it a complex-analytic or $\mathbb C$-algebraic *étale* map.
This refutes that stronger interpretation of 67/077–082 for the natural
Teichmüller complex structure already in the labelled $(0,4)$ sector.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Suppose it were holomorphic. By AQ-67-EXACT it is a bijection
with nonzero real Jacobian onto the punctured disk
$\mathcal D=\{0<|q|<16\}$. The holomorphic inverse function theorem then
makes it a biholomorphism with $\mathcal D$. Conjugating the rotations
$q\mapsto e^{is}q$ by it gives holomorphic automorphisms of
$\mathcal C$. Their lifts to $\mathcal T(S_{0,4})$, continuously chosen
to equal the identity at $s=0$, are the explicit global diffeomorphisms
$$H_s(\ell,\tau)=
       \left(\ell,\tau+\frac{s\ell}{2\pi}\right),
                      \qquad s\in\mathbb R.                  \tag{AQ.67.11}$$
This follows directly from the tensor’s unit twist phase; any choice of
zero-angle section changes $\theta$ by a function of $\ell$ and leaves
(AQ.67.11) unchanged. Since the quotient projection is a holomorphic
covering, every $H_s$ is holomorphic as well.

Let $\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}$ and $g_{\mathrm{WP}}$ be the Weil–Petersson
Kähler form and metric. Wolpert (1983), Lemma 4.1, p. 229, says that the
arclength twist vector field $t_\alpha=\partial/\partial\tau$ has
Hamiltonian potential $-\ell$, in his normalization:
$$\iota_{t_\alpha}\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=-d\ell.$$ The vector field of
(AQ.67.11) is $V=(\ell/2\pi)t_\alpha$, so $$\iota_V\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}
   =-\frac{\ell}{2\pi}d\ell
   =-d\!\left(\frac{\ell^2}{4\pi}\right).$$ Cartan’s formula and
$d\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=0$ imply $\mathcal L_V\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=0$;
hence every global $H_s$ preserves $\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}$. A different
fixed normalization of the Weil–Petersson form only multiplies these
identities by a nonzero constant and gives the same conclusion. A
holomorphic map preserving the Kähler form preserves the metric, since
that metric is recovered from the form and the complex structure. Thus
every $H_s$ would be a Weil–Petersson isometry of $\mathcal T(S_{0,4})$.

Brock and Margalit, *Weil–Petersson isometries via the pants complex*,
Main Theorem, p. 2 of arXiv:math/0412499v1, prove that every such
isometry is induced by an element of the extended mapping class group;
their theorem explicitly includes $S_{0,4}$. That group is countable. On
the other hand (AQ.67.11) gives distinct maps for every two distinct
real numbers $s$, because $\ell>0$ and the upstairs twist coordinate is
real, not taken modulo $\ell$. This is an uncountable family of
isometries, a contradiction. ◻

</div>

## AQ-67-INVOLUTION: the proposition holds; its corollary fails

The proposition of 67/091 gives an equivalence between two-regular
modules with involution and triples $(P,Q,m)$, where $P,Q$ are
two-regular and $m\subset P/2P\oplus Q/2Q$ meets each summand trivially.
This proposition is correct, with morphisms of triples preserving $m$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* For an involution on $M$, let $P=M_+$ and $Q=M_-$.
Two-regularity gives $P\cap Q=0$ and $2M\subset P\oplus Q\subset M$. Let
$m=2M/(2P\oplus2Q)$. If an element of $2M$ has zero $Q$-coordinate, say
$2x\in P$, then $2(\sigma x-x)=0$, so $x\in P$ and $2x\in2P$. This
proves $m\cap(P/2P)=0$, and the other summand is identical.

Conversely take the inverse image $L\subset P\oplus Q$ of $m$, with
involution $(p,q)\mapsto(p,-q)$. It is stable because modulo two this
involution is the identity. It is two-regular, and the intersection
conditions give $L_+=2P\oplus0$, $L_-=0\oplus2Q$. Multiplication by two
canonically identifies these eigenspaces with $P,Q$ and recovers the
original $m$ from $2L/(4P\oplus4Q)$. Starting with $M$, the
reconstructed $L$ is $2M$, canonically isomorphic to $M$ by
multiplication by two. Maps restrict to the eigenspaces, and maps of
triples preserve the indicated inverse images. These constructions are
inverse on maps as well as objects, proving the equivalence. ◻

</div>

The corollary claiming invertible rank-one eigenspaces for a nontrivial
involution on a rank-two projective module over a commutative
two-regular ring is false even when the ring is an integral domain. Set
$$R=\mathbb Z[x,y,z]/(x^2+yz-1),\quad M=R^2,\qquad
 \sigma=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\z&-x\end{pmatrix}.                   \tag{AQ.9}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The polynomial $f=x^2+yz-1$, viewed in $\mathbb Z[x,y][z]$, is
primitive and linear over the fraction field; hence it is irreducible in
the unique factorization domain $\mathbb Z[x,y,z]$. Thus $R$ is a
domain, in particular two-regular. Clearly $\sigma^2=1$ and
$\sigma\ne\pm1$.

In $S=\mathbb Z[x,y,z]$ set $A=\sigma-I$ and $B=\sigma+I$. Then
$AB=BA=fI$. If $\bar A\bar v=0$ over $R$, lift $v$ to $S^2$ and write
$Av=fw$. Multiplication by $B$ gives $fv=fBw$; since $f$ is a
non-zero-divisor, $v=Bw$. Therefore
$\ker\bar A=\operatorname{im}\bar B$. Interchanging $A,B$ proves
$\ker\bar B=\operatorname{im}\bar A$, and hence
$$P=\ker(\sigma-I)\simeq\operatorname{coker}(\sigma-I).$$ At the maximal
ideal $\mathfrak m=(2,x-1,y,z)$ every entry of $A$ vanishes, so
$P/\mathfrak mP\simeq\mathbb F_2^2$. Generically $A$ has rank one,
giving $P$ rank one. An invertible module would have a one-dimensional
fibre at $\mathfrak m$, a contradiction. The same argument applies to
$Q$. ◻

</div>

Connectedness of $\operatorname{Spec}R$ alone cannot repair the
corollary. This algebraic counterexample does not involve RH, and cannot
be excluded by it.

## AQ-154-COUNT: what can actually be counted over finite fields

The supplied 154/120 says $$\chi(\mathcal X)=s_0-s_1+s_2=1-g,\qquad
 2s_1=4s_0,\qquad \chi(\mathcal X)=s_2-s_0.$$ Its $\mathcal X_2$ is a
cellular surface of relative positions of pseudolines. Neither that
sheet nor SA11 supplies it with a scheme over a finite field. The
original Euler-characteristic calculation is therefore not an assertion
about $\#\mathcal X_2(\mathbb F_q)$.

There is a genuine arithmetic consequence for a different, explicitly
specified object. Let $L_1,\dots,L_n$ be distinct rational projective
lines in $\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb Q}$, let $U$ be their complement, and let
$S$ contain the finitely many primes where lines coincide or their
incidence lattice changes. If $v$ runs through their intersection points
and $m_v$ lines meet at $v$, set $$b_2=\sum_v(m_v-1)-n+1.$$ For every
$p\notin S$ and $r\geq1$,
$$\#U(\mathbb F_{p^r})=p^{2r}-(n-1)p^r+b_2,\qquad
 Z(U_{\mathbb F_p},t)
  =\frac{(1-pt)^{n-1}}{(1-p^2t)(1-t)^{b_2}}.                  \tag{AQ.10}$$
Consequently its incomplete arithmetic zeta function is $$\zeta^S(U,s)=
 \frac{\zeta^S(s-2)\zeta^S(s)^{b_2}}
      {\zeta^S(s-1)^{n-1}},\qquad
 \zeta^S(s)=\prod_{p\notin S}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.                 \tag{AQ.11}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Every intersection point is rational because the lines are
rational. Over $\mathbb F_{p^r}$ the sum of the $n$ line counts is
$n(p^r+1)$. It overcounts an intersection $v$ exactly $m_v-1$ times.
Subtracting the corrected union count from $p^{2r}+p^r+1$ proves the
first formula. Insert it in $Z(U_{\mathbb F_p},t)=
\exp(\sum_{r\geq1}\#U(\mathbb F_{p^r})t^r/r)$ and use
$-\log(1-z)=\sum_{r\geq1}z^r/r$ to get the second. Multiplying its Euler
factors gives (AQ.11), initially for $\operatorname{Re}s>3$ and then by
meromorphic continuation. ◻

</div>

Under the adopted RH, the nontrivial-zero contribution to the divisor of
(AQ.11) is on $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52$: the
middle line gives poles when $n>1$, while the other two give zeros when
their exponents are positive. Trivial zeros, the zeta pole, and the
explicit omitted Euler factors remain separately specified by (AQ.11).
These shifted divisors do not give a formula for the pseudoline counts
or the topology of $\mathcal X_2$.

## AQ-136: recovered admissibility, strict failure, and coefficient-local comparisons

#### Original pages and conventions.

 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{136.3}$$
The final lines distinguish an initially simplex-dependent $\lambda_n$
from the desired bound independent of $n$. On physical 119, let $\pi(i)$
be the smallest $p$ for which the simplicial module $C_*^{i,p}$ is not

$$\pi(i)\longrightarrow+\infty.
                                                               \tag{136.4}$$
Physical 121 asks for a common admissible source
$$C''\longrightarrow C,\qquad C''\longrightarrow C',              \tag{136.a}$$
and, for parallel morphisms $f,g:C\rightrightarrows C'$, an admissible

#### Precise category used in the proofs.

Write $S=\Gamma_k(T)=\bigoplus_{w\geq0}kT^{[w]}$, with
$T^{[a]}T^{[b]}=\binom{a+b}{a}T^{[a+b]}$. The coefficient weight is $w$,
the cochain degree is $p$, and $d$ has bidegree $(0,1)$. Let
$\mathcal A_k$ consist of simplicial, nonnegatively bigraded, strictly
commutative PD differential $S$-algebras $C_n^{w,p}$, with morphisms
preserving $S$, both gradings, the differential and divided powers.
Their PD ideal contains all positive-weight even elements; odd elements
square to zero. Require (136.3) with bounds uniform in $n$, and (136.4).
Here flasque means that the normalized simplicial chain complex is
acyclic, including in degree zero. The associated functor on arbitrary
simplicial sets is obtained by taking compatible sections over all
simplices, separately in each bidegree. This construction preserves the
algebra and PD operations and is the colimit-to-limit extension on
physical 111. These conventions specify the category rather than
presuming unretrieved prior-run definitions. The strict counterexample
below needs only the eventual degree-zero case of (136.4), so it also
applies to a larger interpretation with weaker bounds in (136.3). The
rational assertions concern the explicit category $\mathcal A_k$ just
defined.

#### AQ-136-COMPARISON: the explicit tests give the cohomological comparison.

For completeness, the numerical tests used here do imply the weight-tail
comparison on physical 113–115. More precisely, for every fixed $d$
there is $W_d$ such that, for every simplicial set $X$ and $w\geq W_d$,
the two natural comparison maps give
$$H^j(C^{w,*}(X))\simeq H^j(X;k)\otimes_k S^w
                  \qquad(0\leq j\leq d).                  \tag{136.2}$$
This assertion uses the uniform-in-simplex form of (136.3).

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Let $\mathcal P$ be the abelian category of simplicial
$k$-modules. Sections over $X$ are
$\Gamma_X(F)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal P}(k[X],F)$. The normalized
chain complex $K=N(k[X])$ has free $k$-modules, with basis the
nondegenerate simplices. We first check that if $F$ has acyclic
normalization, then it is acyclic for the right-derived functors of
$\Gamma_X$.

Under Dold–Kan this is the assertion
$\operatorname{Ext}^r_{\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(k)}
 (K,N(F))=0$ for $r>0$. Choose a resolution of $K$ by projective chain
complexes, using sums of projective disks and degree-zero projectives.
Every short exact sequence in this resolution is split in each chain
degree: the first quotient has projective terms, and its kernel then has
projective terms, inductively. For any nonnegative chain complex $J$ of
projective modules, the cochain complex
$\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(J,N(F))$ is acyclic. Indeed a closed
homogeneous map is null-homotopic by induction on the source degree:
subtract the already chosen homotopy on the boundary, obtain a map into
cycles, and lift it to the next term of the acyclic target using
projectivity of that source term. This begins in degree zero and works
for every homogeneous degree.

Now a chain map from a kernel in the projective resolution to $N(F)$ has
a graded extension to the preceding projective complex. Its defect of
commuting with the differential factors through the quotient and is a
closed degree-one Hom element. The just-proved Hom acyclicity corrects
this defect. Thus every such chain map extends as a chain map; applying
Hom to the resolution is exact in positive degrees. This proves the
required derived-section acyclicity.

The same projective-disk resolution gives
$R^j\Gamma_X(\underline{k})=H^j(X;k)$. One can verify this without
identifying a topological site: on complexes with projective terms, the
cohomology of $\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(K,k[0])$ has degree-zero
functor $\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Ch}(K,k[0])$ and vanishes in positive
degrees on projective disks and degree-zero projectives. Degreewise
split exact sequences give its long exact sequence, so the projective
resolution computes exactly these groups. The resulting cochain complex
is the usual normalized simplicial cochain complex of $X$.

Choose $w$ so large that (136.3) holds through cochain degree $d+1$, and
that all rows $C_*^{w,p}$ for $0\leq p\leq d+1$ are acyclic as
simplicial modules. There is one such bound for every $d$, by
(136.3)–(136.4). The first-quadrant hypercohomology spectral sequence
for $R\Gamma_X(C^{w,*})$, applied first to vertical cohomology,
identifies its groups through degree $d$ with
$R^j\Gamma_X(\underline{S^w})$. Applied instead to the derived sections
of each term, it identifies those same groups with
$H^j(\Gamma_X(C^{w,*}))$, by the acyclicity just proved. The coefficient
augmentation and the map from ordinary to derived sections are the
comparison maps in these two identifications. Since $S^w$ is a rank-one
free $k$-module, they give (136.2). Both spectral sequences converge in
these degrees: all indices are nonnegative and each total degree uses
only finitely many bidegrees. This proves the claim for arbitrary $X$,
without interchanging a weight-tail limit with an infinite product. ◻

</div>

#### Simplicial algebra fact used below.

Let $L_n=k^{n+1}$ have basis $x_0,\ldots,x_n$. For $\theta:[m]\to[n]$,
put $$\theta^*x_j=\sum_{\theta(a)=j}x_a.$$ Then $t_n=\sum_jx_j$ defines
an inclusion of the constant simplicial module $k$ in $L$, and put
$Q=L/kt$. The normalized chain complex of $L$ is the two-term disk
$k\xrightarrow{\pm1}k$ in degrees one and zero: in the normalization
using $\bigcap_{i<n}\ker d_i$, its degree-one term is $kx_0$, and in
degrees at least two the simultaneous kernels of $d_0$ and $d_1$ are
zero. Thus $L$ is simplicially homotopy equivalent to zero. An exact
known input for the last step is the chain-to-simplicial homotopy
theorem, Stacks Project, Lemma 14.29.3, Tag `01A4`.

For every $r>0$, $\Gamma^r(L)$ and $\operatorname{Sym}^r(L)$ are
consequently simplicially contractible: apply the functor to a homotopy
between the identity and zero. Functorial preservation of simplicial
homotopies is Stacks Project, Remark 14.26.4, Tag `019M`; these
positive-degree functors send the zero map to zero. If $V$ is a
simplicially contractible module and $W$ any simplicial module, then
$V\otimes_k W$ is also contractible. Indeed the homotopy maps
$h_{n,\alpha}:V_n\to V_n$, indexed by $\alpha:[n]\to[1]$, give homotopy
maps $h_{n,\alpha}\otimes1$ on $V_n\otimes W_n$, satisfying the same
face and degeneracy identities. No flatness or characteristic-zero
hypothesis is used in this observation.

#### AQ-136-DP-MODEL: an explicit admissible integral theory.

For every commutative ring $k$, define $$B_n=\Gamma_k(x_0,\ldots,x_n)
       \otimes_k\Lambda_k(e_0,\ldots,e_n)/(e_0+\cdots+e_n),
 \quad dx_j=e_j,\quad de_j=0,\quad T\mapsto\sum_jx_j .        \tag{136.5}$$
Each $x_j$ has bidegree $(1,0)$ and $e_j$ has bidegree $(1,1)$.
Simplicial operators use the displayed summation formula on both $x_j$
and $e_j$. Divided powers of the coefficient $T$ are sent to divided
powers of its sum. Then $B\in\mathcal A_k$; (136.3) holds in every
weight, and its row $B^{w,p}_*$ is contractible whenever $w>p$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* First, all the displayed algebraic structures exist integrally.
The ordinary divided-power polynomial algebra has basis
$\prod_jx_j^{[a_j]}$ and the multiplication above; its precise universal
property is Stacks Project, Lemma 23.5.1, Tag `07H5`. Tensoring it with
an exterior algebra gives the usual strictly graded-commutative PD
algebra. One direct verification is to work first over $\mathbb Z$. It
is a free abelian group inside its rationalization, where
$\gamma_r(z)=z^r/r!$ for positive-weight even $z$. For an even monomial
containing an exterior generator, its square is zero, so its divided
powers of order at least two vanish. For a positive polynomial
divided-power monomial, $r!$ divides the factorial coefficient in its
$r$th power: choose one variable occurring positively and count
partitions of its $ra$ occurrences into $r$ unordered blocks of size
$a$. The addition formula for divided powers therefore preserves the
integral lattice. The identities hold in the rationalization and hence
over $\mathbb Z$; the resulting integer polynomial identities give the
construction over every $k$, including rings with torsion. Eliminating
$e_0$ gives an exterior algebra on $e_1,\ldots,e_n$, so the displayed
relation is compatible with this structure.

The differential is explicitly $d(x_j^{[a]})=x_j^{[a-1]}e_j$ for $a>0$
and extends by the signed Leibniz rule. Its square is zero. It satisfies
$d\gamma_r(z)=\gamma_{r-1}(z)dz$: this follows over $\mathbb Q$ by
differentiation and integrally by the same free-lattice argument. The
simplicial maps preserve it. The relation $\sum e_j=0$ says precisely
that $dT=0$. All simplicial identities follow from composition of the
sums over the fibres of order-preserving maps.

At a fixed simplex make the invertible linear change of variables
$(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\mapsto(T,x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. The PD universal property
gives $$B_n\simeq S\otimes_k\bigotimes_{j=1}^n
            \bigl(\Gamma_k(x_j)\otimes\Lambda_k(e_j)\bigr).
                                                               \tag{136.6}$$
For one factor a contraction onto $k$ is $h(x^{[a]}e)=x^{[a+1]}$ and
$h(x^{[a]})=0$. The identity $dh+hd=1-\epsilon$ follows on each basis
monomial; $\epsilon$ keeps only the constant term. The tensor
contraction is obtained successively by $h\otimes1+\epsilon\otimes h$,
with the usual sign in the tensor action of a degree-minus-one map. Thus
the inclusion $S\to B_n$ is a cochain homotopy equivalence. The
contractions preserve weight, so $H^{w,0}(B_n)=S^w$ and all
$H^{w,p}(B_n)$ for $p>0$ vanish, uniformly in $n$.

Finally there is a natural identification of simplicial modules
$$B_*^{w,p}=\Gamma^{w-p}(L)\otimes_k\Lambda^p(Q),             \tag{136.7}$$
with zero understood for $w<p$. If $w>p$, the first factor is
contractible by the preceding simplicial fact, and so is the tensor
product. Hence (136.4) holds with $\pi(w)\geq w$. This proves every
assertion. ◻

</div>

#### AQ-136-STRICT: the original strict equalizing-source conjecture is false.

If $k\ne0$, there are two parallel morphisms in $\mathcal A_k$ which
admit no equalizing morphism from any admissible source. In particular
this refutes the strict question on physical 121 over $\mathbb Z$,
without a rationalization or a proposed lifting lemma. The supplied
intake had reported a strict-form refutation under the earlier
identifier C07; the following is a complete independent proof from the
recovered original conditions, not a claim to have recovered that absent
proof.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Use the theory $B$ of (136.5). Define a differential $B$-module
$M$ by adjoining symbols $\alpha_j$ of bidegree $(1,0)$ and $\beta_j$ of
bidegree $(1,1)$, imposing $\sum\alpha_j=\sum\beta_j=0$, and putting
$d\alpha_j=\beta_j$, $d\beta_j=0$. In invariant form
$$M=B\otimes_k\bigl(Q\{0\}\xrightarrow{1}Q\{1\}\bigr),
                                                               \tag{136.8}$$
where the two copies have coefficient weight one and the braces indicate
their cochain degrees. There is a weight-preserving degree-zero PD
derivation $$\delta:B\longrightarrow M,\qquad
 \delta x_j=\alpha_j,\quad\delta e_j=\beta_j,\quad
 \delta(x_j^{[a]})=x_j^{[a-1]}\alpha_j .                    \tag{136.9}$$
Extend it by the degree-zero Leibniz rule. The two sum relations make
$\delta T=0$ and $\delta\sum e_j=0$. The divided-power addition formula
shows that it annihilates all of $S$ and respects the PD identities. It
commutes with $d$, as is checked on $x_j,e_j$ and their divided powers
and then by the Leibniz rule. Everything is natural in the simplex.

Form the square-zero extension $D=B\ltimes M$, with $M^2=0$, its induced
differential, and divided powers
$$\gamma_r(b+m)=\gamma_r(b)+\gamma_{r-1}(b)m
 \quad(r\geq1)                                             \tag{136.10}$$
for even elements in the PD ideal. Give $M$ zero higher divided powers.
Formula (136.10), the addition formula, and $M^2=0$ verify all the PD
axioms; alternatively these are the terms of degree zero and one in an
extra square-zero variable in the integral construction just proved.
Differential compatibility follows because $$d(\gamma_{r-1}(b)m)
  =\gamma_{r-2}(b)(db)m+\gamma_{r-1}(b)dm,$$ with the first term absent
for $r=1$, agreeing with $\gamma_{r-1}(b+m)d(b+m)$ after using $M^2=0$.
Thus $$j_0,j_1:B\rightrightarrows D,
 \qquad j_0(b)=(b,0),\quad j_1(b)=(b,\delta b)              \tag{136.11}$$
are strict morphisms of differential PD $S$-algebras.

We verify that $D$ is admissible. The summand $M$ is vertically
contractible: on homogeneous $b\in B$ put
$h(b\beta_j)=(-1)^{\deg b}b\alpha_j$ and $h(b\alpha_j)=0$. The relations
are preserved, and $dh+hd=1$ follows by cancelling the two terms
involving $db$. Consequently $S\to D_n$ has exactly the same cohomology
as $S\to B_n$ in every weight and degree. Horizontally, $$M_*^{w,p}=
  (B_*^{w-1,p}\otimes Q)\ \oplus\
  (B_*^{w-1,p-1}\otimes Q).                                \tag{136.12}$$
The second term is zero if $p=0$. Both terms are contractible for
$w>p+1$, by (136.7) and the tensor homotopy fact. Hence
$\pi_D(w)\geq\max(w-1,0)$. No coefficient tail has been discarded: both
$B$ and $D$ retain every $T^{[w]}$ over the original ring $k$.

At every simplex the equalizer of $j_0,j_1$ is exactly $S$. To check
this, eliminate the zeroth variable as in (136.6); $M_n$ is free over
$B_n$ on $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n$. Expand an
element of $B_n$ uniquely as
$$\sum_{a,I}s_{a,I}x_1^{[a_1]}\cdots x_n^{[a_n]}e_I,
 \qquad s_{a,I}\in S.$$ The coefficient of $\beta_j$ in its $\delta$ is
the signed exterior partial derivative with respect to $e_j$. The basis
monomials containing $e_j$ map injectively to distinct monomials after
removing it. Thus $\delta=0$ forces every nonempty $I$ coefficient to
vanish. Then the coefficient of $\alpha_j$ is the divided-power partial
derivative, sending $x_j^{[a_j]}$ to $x_j^{[a_j-1]}$ with coefficient
one. It forces all $a_j>0$ coefficients to vanish. This argument works
over arbitrary $k$; it does not divide by an integer. The remaining
elements are exactly $S$.

Suppose an admissible $E$ and an $S$-morphism $u:E\to B$ equalized
(136.11). By the computed equalizer, $u$ factors through the constant
simplicial algebra $S$. Since it is an $S$-morphism, this factorization
retracts the structure map $S\to E$. In each weight $w$ it makes the
nonzero constant simplicial module $kT^{[w]}$ a direct summand of
$E_*^{w,0}$. Therefore $H_0^{\rm simp}(E_*^{w,0})$ contains $k$ for
every $w$. This contradicts (136.4), which makes that row acyclic for
all sufficiently large $w$. The contradiction proves the strict
assertion false. ◻

</div>

#### AQ-136-RATIONAL: common sources and terminal localization over a $\mathbb Q$-algebra.

If $k$ is a $\mathbb Q$-algebra, every finite family of objects of
$\mathcal A_k$ has a common source in $\mathcal A_k$. The ordinary
category obtained from $\mathcal A_k$ by inverting every morphism is
equivalent to the terminal groupoid. This is a statement about that
groupoid, not about all higher homotopy groups of an
infinity-categorical localization.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Identify $S=\Gamma_k(T)$ with $k[t]$ by $T^{[w]}=t^w/w!$. For
an integer $N\geq1$, define $$B_{N,n}=
 \frac{k[t,x_0,\ldots,x_n]\otimes\Lambda(e_0,\ldots,e_n)}
      {(\sum x_j-t^N,\ \sum e_j)},
 \quad dx_j=e_j,\quad dt=de_j=0.                           \tag{136.13}$$
Put $\operatorname{wt}(t)=1$ and
$\operatorname{wt}(x_j)=\operatorname{wt}(e_j)=N$. Use the same simplex
operators as before, fixing $t$. Over a $\mathbb Q$-algebra the PD
operations on even positive-weight elements are uniquely $z^r/r!$. Thus
(136.13) is a differential PD $S$-algebra.

Eliminating $x_0,e_0$ gives the polynomial de Rham complex over $S$ in
$n$ variables of weight $N$. In one variable the homotopy
$h(x^ae)=x^{a+1}/(a+1)$ contracts onto the constants. Its tensor product
contraction proves (136.3) in every weight, again preserving weight. For
the horizontal calculation let $A$ be the same construction with
coefficient $U=\sum x_j$ and all $x_j,e_j,U$ of weight one. As a
simplicial module
$A_*^{m,p}=\operatorname{Sym}^{m-p}(L)\otimes\Lambda^p(Q)$, so it is
contractible if $m>p$. Before extension of coefficients regrade these
weights by $N$. We have
$$B_N=k[t]\otimes_{k[U]}A,\qquad U\longmapsto t^N,
 \qquad k[t]=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{N-1}t^r k[U].                \tag{136.14}$$
The last equality is an actual free-module decomposition, valid over
every $k$. The component of weight $w$ therefore identifies with
$A^{m,p}$, where $w=Nm+r$ and $0\leq r<N$. It is contractible for $m>p$.
In particular $\pi_{B_N}(w)\geq\lfloor w/N\rfloor$, which tends to
infinity. This proves $B_N\in\mathcal A_k$.

Now fix finitely many admissible theories $C_a$. Choose $N\geq1$ large
enough that every simplicial module $C_a^{N,0}$ is acyclic, using
(136.4). The coefficient $t^N$ determines a map from the constant
simplicial module $k$ to $C_a^{N,0}$. It extends along the inclusion
$k\to L$ to a simplicial linear map
$$L\longrightarrow C_a^{N,0}.       \tag{136.15}$$ Here is the precise
lifting reason. Normalization sends $k\to L$ to the inclusion of the
degree-zero term into the two-term disk. Vanishing of $H_0$ says that
the coefficient element $t^N$ at simplex zero is the boundary of an
element in normalized degree one. Choosing that element gives a chain
map from the disk. The Dold–Kan equivalence, Stacks Project Theorem
14.24.3, Tag `019G`, lifts it to (136.15), retaining the given constant
map. No lift of an arbitrary cochain complex through a surjection is
being asserted.

Send $x_j$ to the image of its basis vector in (136.15), and $e_j$ to
the vertical differential of that image. Their sums are respectively
$t^N$ and zero. The polynomial/exterior universal property defines a
simplicial differential algebra map $B_N\to C_a$, and it preserves
divided powers because $k$ contains $\mathbb Q$. It preserves weights
and $S$ by construction. These maps give the claimed simultaneous common
source.

It remains to prove terminality after inversion; strict equalizers
cannot be used, by the preceding counterexample. For any admissible $D$,
let $$P(D)_n=D_n\otimes_k k[s,ds],\quad
 \operatorname{wt}(s)=\operatorname{wt}(ds)=0,
 \quad d s=ds,\quad d(ds)=0.                              \tag{136.16}$$
It has cochain-degree-zero $s$ and degree-one $ds$. This is an object of
$\mathcal A_k$. Integration of polynomials contracts $k[s,ds]$ onto $k$,
naturally in $D$, and so leaves every vertical cohomology unchanged. Its
row of bidegree $(w,p)$ is the sum of copies of $D^{w,p}$ and
$D^{w,p-1}$ indexed by powers of $s$. Normalization and homology commute
with these direct sums; hence (136.4) is retained. All PD operations
exist canonically over $\mathbb Q$. The maps $j:D\to P(D)$ and
$e_0,e_1:P(D)\to D$ are admissible morphisms and $e_0j=e_1j=1$.
Consequently $e_0=e_1=j^{-1}$ in the all-arrow localization.

For any parallel $f,g:C\rightrightarrows D$, choose a common-source map
$a:B_N\to C$ as above. There is an explicit PD differential map
$H:B_N\to P(D)$ given on each generator by
$$H(x_j)=(1-s)fa(x_j)+sga(x_j),\qquad H(e_j)=dH(x_j),
 \qquad H(t)=t.                                           \tag{136.17}$$
Its sums are $t^N$ and zero, it is natural in the simplex and preserves
weights. Thus the relations and all structures are respected. Its two
evaluations are $fa$ and $ga$. The equality of evaluations in the
localization, followed by inversion of $a$, gives $f=g$ there.

Finally any finite zigzag in the localization involves finitely many
objects $C_0,\ldots,C_r$. Choose a common source $B_M$ and arrows
$a_i:B_M\to C_i$. For every original arrow $f:C_i\to C_j$ in the zigzag,
the two parallel arrows $fa_i$ and $a_j$ have the same image in the
localization, by the preceding paragraph. Hence the image of $f$ is
$a_j a_i^{-1}$, and its inverse is $a_i a_j^{-1}$. The whole zigzag
telescopes to $a_r a_0^{-1}$. Applying the same argument to the union of
any two finite zigzags shows that they agree. Existence of common
sources makes the groupoid connected; $B_1$ makes it nonempty. It
therefore has exactly one morphism between each two objects, proving the
assertion. ◻

</div>

#### AQ-136-INTEGRAL-TAIL: the direct one-generator integral analogue fails.

The strict counterexample retained every coefficient $T^{[w]}$ and works
over $\mathbb Z$. There is also a precise obstruction to extending the
rational common-source construction by replacing $t^N$ with $T^{[N]}$.
For $N\geq2$, form the analogous divided-power theory $B_N^{\rm PD}$
over $S=\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}(T)$ by $$B_{N,n}^{\rm PD}
  =S\langle x_0,\ldots,x_n\rangle
       \otimes\Lambda(e_0,\ldots,e_n)
          /(\sum x_j-T^{[N]},\ \sum e_j),                   \tag{136.18}$$
where the first relation means the PD quotient, each $x_j,e_j$ has
weight $N$, and $dx_j=e_j$. Eliminating the zeroth variables gives a
free PD polynomial de Rham complex over $S$, so vertical acyclicity is
retained. Nevertheless (136.4) fails: its degree-zero simplicial
homology is nonzero in arbitrarily large weights.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* In simplex zero the zero-form algebra is $S$; in simplex one it
is $S\langle x_1\rangle$, with the two faces evaluating $x_1$ at
$T^{[N]}$ and at zero. Therefore its simplicial degree-zero homology is
$$H_0^{\rm simp}((B_N^{\rm PD})^{*,0})=S/J_N,
 \qquad J_N=(\gamma_m(T^{[N]}):m\geq1).                    \tag{136.19}$$
This is a direct calculation of the cokernel of $d_0-d_1$, using the
$S$-basis $x_1^{[m]}$; it does not presume flat base change of a
simplicial resolution. The ideal $J_N$ is the full PD ideal generated by
the chosen coefficient, not merely its ordinary principal ideal: the
iterated-PD and addition identities show that the displayed ordinary
ideal is PD-stable and is the smallest such ideal containing $T^{[N]}$.

Let $p>N$ be prime. Only $mN\leq p$ contributes in weight $p$. The
corresponding product is $$T^{[p-mN]}\gamma_m(T^{[N]})
   =\frac{p!}{m!(N!)^m(p-mN)!}\,T^{[p]}.                   \tag{136.20}$$
The coefficient is an integer by the PD identities. Its numerator has
exactly one factor $p$, and none of the factorials in its denominator
has one: $N<p$, $m\leq p/N<p$, and $p-mN<p$. Thus it is divisible by
$p$. Every weight-$p$ coefficient of $J_N$ is consequently a multiple of
$p$, so $(S/J_N)^p$ surjects onto $\mathbb Z/p$ and is nonzero. Primes
$p>N$ are arbitrarily large, proving the failure of (136.4). For $N=1$
the PD ideal is the entire positive coefficient ideal; the
counterargument does not apply. That value would require a target’s
weight-one row to be acyclic, which general admissibility does not
guarantee. ◻

</div>

The rational theorem thus does not extend by the single-coefficient
substitution (136.18), even though its vertical Poincare lemma is valid.
This is a counterexample to that particular construction, not to the
integral common-source or integral terminal-localization assertions.
Those two integral assertions remain distinct open gates in this module.
Their original defining sheets are now recovered, so the gate is no
longer the absence of the original question.

#### AQ-136-PLOCAL: common sources over every $\mathbb Z_{(p_0)}$-algebra.

Fix a prime $p_0$. If $k$ is any commutative
$\mathbb Z_{(p_0)}$-algebra, every finite family of admissible strict PD
theories in $\mathcal A_k$ has a common admissible source. In particular
this covers $\mathbb Z_{p_0}$, $\mathbb F_{p_0}$ and $\mathbb Z/p_0^r$
with their original coefficients. Terminality of the $p_0$-local
all-arrow groupoid is proved separately in AQ-136-PLOCAL-LOCALIZATION
below.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Put $S=\Gamma_k(T)$ and choose $N=p_0^e$, $e\geq0$. The PD
homomorphism $$\rho_N:\Gamma_k(U)\longrightarrow\Gamma_k(T),
                  \qquad U\longmapsto T^{[N]},              \tag{136.21}$$
becomes a homogeneous map if $U$ is assigned weight $N$. For every
$m\geq0$ it sends the basis element $U^{[m]}$ to
$$c_mT^{[mN]},\qquad c_m=\frac{(mN)!}{m!(N!)^m}.
                                                               \tag{136.22}$$
The integer $c_m$ is a $p_0$-adic unit. Indeed counting the multiples of
successive powers of $p_0$ in a factorial gives $$v_{p_0}((mp_0^e)!)
  =m\frac{p_0^e-1}{p_0-1}+v_{p_0}(m!),
 \qquad
 v_{p_0}((p_0^e)!)=\frac{p_0^e-1}{p_0-1};$$ the difference in (136.22)
is zero. For $0\leq a<N$, the integer $\binom{mN+a}{a}$ is also a
$p_0$-adic unit. For powers $p_0^j$ with $j\leq e$, divisibility of $mN$
by $p_0^j$ makes the corresponding three factorial floors cancel. For
$j>e$, $a<p_0^e$ implies $$\left\lfloor\frac{mN+a}{p_0^j}\right\rfloor
  =\left\lfloor\frac{m}{p_0^{j-e}}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \left\lfloor\frac a{p_0^j}\right\rfloor=0,$$ so those floors
also cancel. These integers are therefore units in every
$\mathbb Z_{(p_0)}$-algebra $k$.

The products $$T^{[a]}\rho_N(U^{[m]})
    =c_m\binom{mN+a}{a}T^{[mN+a]}$$ are consequently unit multiples of
all the distinct basis elements of $S$. This proves the actual
graded-module isomorphism
$$S=\bigoplus_{a=0}^{N-1}T^{[a]}\Gamma_k(U)                 \tag{136.23}$$
via $\rho_N$. In particular $S$ is free, not merely flat, over the
indicated divided-power coefficient algebra. The claim holds even if $k$
has $p_0$-torsion or nilpotents.

Let $A$ be the admissible integral DP simplex theory (136.5), with its
coefficient named $U$, and regrade all its weights by $N$. Form
$$B_N^{\rm PD}=S\otimes_{\Gamma_k(U)}A .                    \tag{136.24}$$
In each simplex this is exactly the PD quotient with $\sum x_j=T^{[N]}$
and $\sum e_j=0$ from (136.18). Eliminating $x_0,e_0$ identifies it with
the free PD polynomial de Rham complex over $S$ in the remaining
variables. The integral contractions in (136.6) prove vertical
cohomology $S$ in degree zero and zero otherwise, in every weight and
every simplex.

For the horizontal calculation, (136.23) is a natural direct sum
decomposition of the whole simplicial module, since its coefficients are
constant simplicially. The component of weight $w=Nm+a$, $0\leq a<N$,
and cochain degree $r$ is identified, as a simplicial $k$-module, with
$A_*^{m,r}$. The latter is contractible for $m>r$, by (136.7). Thus
$\pi_{B_N^{\rm PD}}(w)\geq\lfloor w/N\rfloor\to\infty$, and
$B_N^{\rm PD}$ is admissible. The coefficient tail is complete: every
$T^{[w]}$ occurs in the free basis calculation, with no rationalization.

Given finitely many admissible targets $C_b$, choose $e$ so large that
every row $C_b^{N,0}$ is acyclic. Condition (136.4) permits this along
the unbounded sequence $N=p_0^e$. Lift the constant coefficient
$T^{[N]}$ along $k\to L$ to a simplicial linear map $L\to C_b^{N,0}$,
using exactly the normalized-disk argument of (136.15). Its coordinate
images have sum $T^{[N]}$. Send $x_j$ to those images and $e_j$ to their
vertical differentials. The PD polynomial universal property, together
with $d\gamma_m(x)=\gamma_{m-1}(x)dx$, gives a strict simplicial
differential PD map $B_N^{\rm PD}\to C_b$ over $S$. It preserves both
gradings and the two sum relations. These are the required simultaneous
source maps. ◻

</div>

#### Finite-product and positive-characteristic consequence.

Common-source existence also holds for a finite product
$k=\prod_{i=1}^r k_i$ of rings covered by AQ-136-PLOCAL. Indeed the
coefficient idempotents split every $k$-theory, its divided powers and
its morphisms into $r$ component theories over $k_i$. Both vertical
cohomology and normalized simplicial homology respect this finite
direct-product decomposition. Thus admissibility is equivalent to
admissibility of all components, using the maximum of their finitely
many cutoffs. Take componentwise common sources and their finite
product; this is the required admissible source over $k$. Zero component
rings cause no exception.

In particular common-source existence holds for every commutative ring
of positive characteristic. If its characteristic is
$m=\prod_i p_i^{a_i}>1$, the Chinese remainder idempotents of
$\mathbb Z/m$ give $k=\prod_i k_i$, each a $\mathbb Z/p_i^{a_i}$-algebra
and hence a $\mathbb Z_{(p_i)}$-algebra. The preceding argument applies.
The zero ring is harmless as well. This finite idempotent decomposition
is not available for $\mathbb Z$; no prime-varying integral coefficient
object is substituted for the single ring in that remaining question.

#### AQ-136-PATH-OBSTRUCTION: the unweighted rational interval has no characteristic-$p$ replacement of the same kind.

Let $k\ne0$ be a commutative ring of characteristic $p_0>0$. There is no
nonnegative commutative differential $k$-algebra $R$ with coefficient
isomorphism $k\simeq H^0(R)$, two $k$-algebra augmentations
$\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1:R\to k$, and an element $s\in R^0$ such that
$\epsilon_0(s)=0$ and $\epsilon_1(s)=1$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Since $R$ is nonnegative, $H^0(R)=\ker(d:R^0\to R^1)$ without a
quotient by boundaries. The Leibniz rule and characteristic $p_0$ give
$d(s^{p_0})=p_0s^{p_0-1}ds=0$. The coefficient isomorphism therefore
forces $s^{p_0}$ to be the image of some $c\in k$. Applying the two
augmentations gives both $c=0$ and $c=1$, contradicting $k\ne0$. ◻

</div>

This last obstruction rules out only such an unweighted strict interval.
Weighted comparisons and other localization arguments are not excluded.
In particular it is consistent with AQ-136-PLOCAL and the terminality
proved in AQ-136-PLOCAL-LOCALIZATION below. It supplies no
counterexample to integral terminal localization.

#### AQ-136-COTENSOR: a simplicial path theory over any coefficient ring.

For any $C\in\mathcal A_k$ over any commutative ring $k$, put
$$P_s(C)_n^{w,r}=C^{w,r}(\Delta[n]\times\Delta[1]).             \tag{136.25}$$
Here $C(X)$ is the compatible-section extension from simplices used in
AQ-136-COMPARISON. The operations are taken pointwise. Then $P_s(C)$
belongs to $\mathcal A_k$. Its constant-path inclusion $j:C\to P_s(C)$
and two evaluations $e_0,e_1:P_s(C)\to C$ are strict PD morphisms over
$S$, and $e_0j=e_1j=1$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The construction is a limit of the given bigraded differential
PD algebras, so inherits every operation and the coefficient map.
Functoriality in $\Delta[n]$ gives its simplicial structure; projection
and the two vertex inclusions give the displayed maps and their
identities.

Fix a cochain bound $d$. AQ-136-COMPARISON gives a weight bound $W_d$
independent of the simplicial set. Each $\Delta[n]\times\Delta[1]$ is
contractible: it is the nerve of the product ordered set $[n]\times[1]$,
which has a terminal element, or use the usual monotone contraction of
each simplex. Therefore, for $w\geq W_d$,
$$H^0(P_s(C)_n^{w,*})=S^w,\qquad
 H^r(P_s(C)_n^{w,*})=0\quad(1\leq r\leq d),                \tag{136.26}$$
with the first isomorphism induced by the coefficient map. The bound is
uniform in $n$, as required by (136.3). There is no interchange of
infinitely many separate simplex-dependent bounds.

For fixed $(w,r)$, write $F=C_*^{w,r}$. The corresponding simplicial
module in (136.25) is its cotensor $F^{\Delta[1]}$. Evaluation at the
zero vertex and the constant map exhibit a simplicial linear homotopy
equivalence $$F^{\Delta[1]}\simeq F.              \tag{136.27}$$
Explicitly the order-preserving minimum map $[1]\times[1]\to[1]$ induces
a simplicial map $\mu:\Delta[1]\times\Delta[1]\to\Delta[1]$ with
$\mu(s,0)=0$ and $\mu(s,1)=s$. Precomposition by this map gives a
homotopy between the constant-path evaluation and the identity on the
cotensor. All its maps are linear, since operations on functions to $F$
are pointwise. Thus it induces the same normalized simplicial homology
as $F$, without a flatness hypothesis. Condition (136.4) for $C$
consequently gives that condition for $P_s(C)$, with the same cutoff
function. This proves admissibility and all assertions. ◻

</div>

#### AQ-136-PLOCAL-LOCALIZATION: the $p_0$-local groupoid is terminal.

For every commutative $\mathbb Z_{(p_0)}$-algebra $k$, the ordinary
localization of $\mathcal A_k$ at all its morphisms is equivalent to the
terminal groupoid. The same holds for a finite product of such
coefficient rings and hence for every ring of positive characteristic.
Together with AQ-136-PLOCAL this proves both non-strict original
uniqueness assertions in these coefficient ranges.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* We first show that any two parallel morphisms
$f,g:C\rightrightarrows D$ have the same image in the all-arrow
localization. Choose $N=p_0^e$ so large that both $C_*^{N,0}$ and
$D_*^{N,0}$ are acyclic. The proof of AQ-136-PLOCAL provides an
admissible source and map $$a:B_N^{\rm PD}\longrightarrow C.$$ Set
$F=D_*^{N,0}$. The coordinate images under $fa$ and $ga$ define
simplicial linear maps $\phi_0,\phi_1:L\to F$. On the constant submodule
$kt\subset L$ both send $t=\sum_jx_j$ to the coefficient $T^{[N]}$.
Their difference thus factors through
$$Q=L/kt,\qquad \psi:Q\longrightarrow F.        \tag{136.28}$$
Normalization is exact, and the disk calculation for $L$ shows that
$N(Q)=k[1]$. The normalized map $N(\psi)$ is consequently a cycle in
$N(F)_1$. Since $H_1(N(F))=0$, that cycle is the boundary of an element
of $N(F)_2$. Mapping the generator of $k[1]$ to this element gives a
chain homotopy between $N(\psi)$ and zero. The chain-to-simplicial
homotopy statement, Stacks Project Lemma14.29.3/Tag`01A4`, gives a
simplicial linear homotopy from zero to $\psi$. Equivalently it gives a
linear map $Q\to F^{\Delta[1]}$ with those endpoints.

Compose this map with $L\to Q$ and add the constant-path map associated
with $\phi_0$. The result is a simplicial linear map
$$\Phi:L\longrightarrow F^{\Delta[1]},\qquad
 e_0\Phi=\phi_0,\quad e_1\Phi=\phi_1,\quad
 \Phi(t)=T^{[N]}\text{ as a constant path}.                 \tag{136.29}$$
The last equality is exact: the homotopy term factors through $Q$ and is
zero on $kt$, throughout the homotopy. This relative condition is what
preserves the original coefficient relation.

Apply the free PD polynomial universal property in each simplex, sending
$x_j$ to $\Phi(x_j)$, $e_j$ to $d\Phi(x_j)$, and every coefficient of
$S$ to itself. Equation (136.29) gives the two relations
$\sum x_j=T^{[N]}$ and $\sum e_j=0$; all operators are natural in the
simplex. We obtain a strict differential PD map over $S$
$$H:B_N^{\rm PD}\longrightarrow P_s(D),\qquad
                         e_0H=fa,\quad e_1H=ga.            \tag{136.30}$$
It preserves both gradings, because the coordinate homotopy was carried
out within weight $N$, cochain degree zero. AQ-136-COTENSOR proves that
its target is admissible over the same coefficient ring.

Since $e_0j=e_1j=1$, the two evaluations both equal $j^{-1}$ after
inversion of all morphisms. Thus $fa=ga$ in the localization, and
inversion of $a$ gives $f=g$ there. Finite common sources exist by
AQ-136-PLOCAL. For any two finite zigzags with the same endpoints,
choose one common source for all their objects. For an original arrow
$u:C_i\to C_j$, its composite with the chosen source arrow to $C_i$ and
the chosen source arrow to $C_j$ are parallel; they have the same
localized image by the argument just given. Every edge is therefore the
corresponding ratio of these source arrows, and both zigzags telescope
to the same ratio. The localization is nonempty and connected, and has
exactly one arrow between any two objects.

For a finite product of coefficient rings, choose the sources
$B_{N_i}^{\rm PD}$ and the relative coordinate homotopies separately in
each factor. Their finite products are admissible and form the same
diagrams over the product ring: finite idempotent decomposition commutes
with compatible sections and hence with $P_s$. The identical
parallel-arrow and telescope argument applies. The Chinese-remainder
decomposition proved in AQ-136-PLOCAL covers every
positive-characteristic ring. The zero ring again causes no exception. ◻

</div>

The simplicial cotensor varies the argument $X$ of a theory. It does not
adjoin a weight-zero element with endpoints zero and one to one cochain
algebra; the hypotheses of AQ-136-PATH-OBSTRUCTION are therefore not
being asserted. All coefficient divided powers remain fixed in
(136.29)–(136.30). The two non-strict questions over $\mathbb Z$ remain
unresolved by this argument, since its admissible source $B_N^{\rm PD}$
uses the one-prime free coefficient decomposition (136.23).

#### AQ-136-WEIGHT-ONE: admissibility does not force the lowest row to be acyclic.

For $N\geq2$ and $k\ne0$, there is an admissible theory $C$ to which the
standard source $B=B_1$ has no $S$-morphism. Define, inside (136.5),
$$C^{w,r}_n=
 \begin{cases}
 S^w,&w<N,\ r=0,\\
 0,&w<N,\ r>0,\\
 B^{w,r}_n,&w\geq N.
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{136.31}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* These submodules are preserved by $d$ and simplicial operators.
Products involving a term of weight at least $N$ remain in that range;
products of lower-weight coefficients are coefficients. Divided powers
likewise preserve the subalgebra: they preserve the coefficient algebra,
and multiply a positive weight at least $N$ by a positive integer. Thus
$C$ is a strict differential PD $S$-subalgebra of $B$. Its vertical
cohomology is $S$ in degree zero and zero otherwise in every weight. All
sufficiently large weight rows are the rows of $B$, so (136.4) holds.
Consequently $C$ is admissible.

Its weight-one zero-form row is the nonzero constant simplicial module
$kT$. A hypothetical $S$-map $B\to C$ would restrict on the coordinate
module to a simplicial linear map $L\to\underline{kT}$ taking $t$ to
$T$. On normalization, a chain map from the disk $k\xrightarrow{\pm1}k$
to $k[0]$ must have zero degree-zero component, because its composite
with the disk differential is zero. It cannot take $t$ to nonzero $T$.
This is the required contradiction. ◻

</div>

#### AQ-136-MULTIWEIGHT: independent coefficient disks in every large weight are still not admissible.

Fix $N_0\geq1$. One might try to remove the coefficient-tail obstruction
of AQ-136-INTEGRAL-TAIL by imposing all relations
$\sum_jx_{N,j}=T^{[N]}$ for every $N\geq N_0$, using a separate
divided-power simplex disk for each relation. This construction has the
required vertical cohomology, but its zero-form row has nonzero
horizontal $H_1$ in arbitrarily large weights, already after extension
to $\mathbb Q$. It therefore fails condition (136.4), both over
$\mathbb Q$ and over $\mathbb Z$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* First define the integral construction precisely. Put
$$R=\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}(U_N:N\geq N_0),\qquad
 S=\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}(T),\qquad
 U_N\longmapsto T^{[N]},\quad \operatorname{wt}(U_N)=N.
                                                               \tag{136.32}$$
In simplicial degree $n$, let
$$A_n=\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}(x_{N,j}:N\geq N_0,\ 0\leq j\leq n)
       \otimes\Lambda(e_{N,j}:N\geq N_0,\ 0\leq j\leq n)
       /(\textstyle\sum_j e_{N,j}:N\geq N_0),                \tag{136.33}$$
where $d x_{N,j}=e_{N,j}$, $d e_{N,j}=0$, both generators have weight
$N$, and their cochain degrees are zero and one, respectively. The
coefficient map sends $U_N$ to $\sum_jx_{N,j}$. Simplicial operators act
on each $N$-family by the sum-over-fibres rule of (136.5). All divided
powers and differential identities are those of the integral model
AQ-136-DP-MODEL; tensoring the models and taking the filtered union over
finite sets of indices gives the displayed strict PD algebra. Define
$$B^{\rm multi}=S\otimes_R A.        \tag{136.34}$$ Equivalently this is
the strict PD quotient with $\sum_jx_{N,j}=T^{[N]}$ and
$\sum_j e_{N,j}=0$ for every $N\geq N_0$. Eliminating $x_{N,0}$ and
$e_{N,0}$ identifies each simplex with the free divided-power polynomial
de Rham algebra over $S$ on the remaining variables. In a fixed weight
only finitely many $N$ can occur, and in a fixed simplex there are
finitely many variables for each such $N$. The weight-preserving
integral disk contractions from (136.6) therefore apply in each weight,
proving vertical cohomology $S$ in degree zero and zero in every
positive degree.

We compute the zero-form horizontal homology. An invertible linear
change of divided-power variables replaces $x_{N,0}$ by
$U_N=\sum_jx_{N,j}$, independently for every $N$. Hence $A_n^0$ is a
free $R$-module, with divided-power monomials in $x_{N,j}$, $j\geq1$, as
basis. Its augmentation to $\mathbb Z$ sends every variable to zero. As
a simplicial abelian group, its weight-$w$ part is the direct sum over
the finitely many tuples $(a_N)$ satisfying $\sum_N N a_N=w$ of
$$\bigotimes_N\Gamma^{a_N}(L).
                                                               \tag{136.35}$$
For $w>0$, at least one $a_N$ is positive. That factor is linearly
simplicially contractible by the homotopy calculation preceding (136.5),
and tensoring its homotopy with the identities contracts the displayed
tensor product. Weight zero is the constant module $\mathbb Z$. Thus the
augmented normalized complex $N(A_*^0)\to\mathbb Z$ is exact. Each
normalized term is an $R$-linear direct summand of a free term $A_n^0$
by the standard degeneracy decomposition. It is therefore a projective
$R$-module, so this is a projective resolution of the augmentation
module $\mathbb Z$ over $R$.

Normalization commutes with arbitrary extension of scalars here: its
degeneracy decomposition is split and functorial, so tensoring carries
the projector onto the normalized summand to the corresponding projector
after tensoring. Consequently $$H_i\bigl(N((B^{\rm multi})_*^0)\bigr)
                  =\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(S,\mathbb Z).
                                                               \tag{136.36}$$
Rationalization is flat. In characteristic zero the divided-power
polynomial algebra is the polynomial algebra, with $T^{[N]}=t^N/N!$.
Thus (136.36) after tensoring with $\mathbb Q$ is computed over
$$R_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q[u_N:N\geq N_0],\qquad
 S_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q[t],\qquad
                     u_N\longmapsto a_N=t^N/N!.
                                                               \tag{136.37}$$
The Koszul complex on the countable polynomial variables $u_N$ is a free
resolution of the augmentation module $\mathbb Q$. For completeness, the
Koszul complex on a finite initial set is exact in positive degrees
because adjoining a new variable tensors the previous resolution with
the two-term complex of multiplication by that variable, which is
injective on the preceding polynomial quotient. Its degree-zero homology
is the quotient by the variables in that finite set. Filtered unions are
exact for modules, so passage through all finite initial sets gives
positive homology zero and degree-zero quotient $\mathbb Q$.

After tensoring with $\mathbb Q[t]$, we therefore obtain the Koszul
complex $K(a_N:N\geq N_0)$. Write its degree-one generator as $\xi_N$,
with weight $N$ and $d\xi_N=t^N/N!$. For $N>N_0$ put $$\eta_N=\xi_N-
              \frac{N_0!}{N!}t^{N-N_0}\xi_{N_0}.
                                                               \tag{136.38}$$
This is a homogeneous invertible change of basis of the free direct sum
on the generators: both it and its inverse use only two terms for each
generator. Moreover $d\eta_N=0$. It follows that $$K(a_N:N\geq N_0)
  \simeq K(t^{N_0}/N_0!)\otimes_{\mathbb Q[t]}
                 \Lambda_{\mathbb Q[t]}(\eta_N:N>N_0),
                  \qquad d\eta_N=0.                       \tag{136.39}$$
Multiplication by $t^{N_0}/N_0!$ is injective in $\mathbb Q[t]$. The
homology of its two-term complex is therefore $\mathbb Q[t]/(t^{N_0})$
in degree zero. The second factor in (136.39) is free in every degree
with zero differential, whence $$H_*\bigl(K(a_N:N\geq N_0)\bigr)
  =\mathbb Q[t]/(t^{N_0})\otimes_{\mathbb Q}
                         \Lambda_{\mathbb Q}(\eta_N:N>N_0).
                                                               \tag{136.40}$$
In particular $1\otimes\eta_N$ is a nonzero horizontal $H_1$ class of
weight $N$ for every $N>N_0$. The zero-form row is not horizontally
acyclic in any eventual range of weights, in contradiction to condition
(136.4). Since flat rationalization preserves homology, integral
eventual acyclicity would imply rational eventual acyclicity. The
integral construction fails too. ◻

</div>

The argument applies even when $N_0=1$. The obstruction consists of the
higher homology of the redundant, independent coefficient relations; it
is distinct from the prime-weight $H_0$ obstruction in
AQ-136-INTEGRAL-TAIL. It refutes this free independent-disk construction
only. Adding coherent higher relations or constructing a different
admissible source remains a separate problem; the integral original
conjecture (a) and integral terminal localization are not refuted by
(136.40).

#### AQ-136-DERHAM-CELLS: coherent high-weight zero-form cells still cannot give an integral full de Rham source.

Let $k$ be a commutative ring and put $S=\Gamma_k(T)$, with
$\operatorname{wt}(T)=1$. Suppose $P_*$ is a simplicial graded PD
$S$-algebra such that in each simplicial degree it has a homogeneous
presentation $$P_n=S\langle X_n\rangle_{\rm PD},
           \qquad \operatorname{wt}(x)\geq2
                      \quad(x\in X_n).                    \tag{136.41}$$
The sets of variables may be infinite and may differ with $n$.
Simplicial operators need not preserve a chosen generating set: they are
arbitrary homogeneous PD $S$-algebra morphisms. In particular they can
encode coherent higher relations among the cells.

Write $\Omega^1_{P/S,\mathrm{sp}}$ for the module of *special* relative
PD differentials: it represents derivations satisfying
$$d\gamma_m(a)=\gamma_{m-1}(a)\,da.
                                                               \tag{136.42}$$
Thus, in (136.41), it is the free $P_n$-module with basis $dx$ for
$x\in X_n$, of the same weights as the variables. This is precisely the
one-form row of the full relative PD polynomial de Rham algebra. It is
not an identification with unrestricted Beck derivations in the
algebraic theory of all PD operations.

For any prime $p$ with $k/pk\ne0$, the following implication holds:
$$[T^{[p]}]=0\ \hbox{in }H_0(N(P_*^p))
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H_1\bigl(N(\Omega^1_{P_*/S,\mathrm{sp}}{}^{\!p})\bigr)
                                  \ne0.                    \tag{136.43}$$
Here the superscript $p$ denotes weight, and $N$ denotes simplicial
normalization, not a weight bound. Consequently, if infinitely many
rational primes are nonunits in $k$, no full relative PD de Rham theory
built from (136.41) can satisfy original condition (136.4). In
particular this rules out that entire class of constructions over
$\mathbb Z$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Fix $p$ with $\bar k=k/pk\ne0$ and reduce the simplicial
algebra modulo $p$. All its elements have nonnegative weight, and its
weight-zero part is $\bar k$. Let $\bar P_+$ denote the ideal of
positive-weight elements and define the weight-$p$ ordinary
indecomposables $$Q_*=(\bar P_+/(\bar P_+)^2)^p.
                                                               \tag{136.44}$$
This is a simplicial $\bar k$-module. We emphasize *ordinary*
indecomposables: divided powers of positive elements are not added to
the denominator. Simplicial maps preserve the ordinary product ideal, so
no invariance of the displayed free variables under faces is needed.

The divided-power monomial basis gives, in every simplicial degree, the
following basis of $Q_n$: $$z=T^{[p]},\qquad x\in X_n\text{ with }
                                      \operatorname{wt}(x)=p.
                                                               \tag{136.45}$$
To check this assertion completely, a monomial involving two distinct
factors among the coefficient variable $T$ and the free variables is a
product of two positive-weight monomials, with coefficient one, and
hence is zero in (136.44). A monomial involving only one free variable
has the form $x^{[m]}$ with $m\operatorname{wt}(x)=p$. Since $p$ is
prime and $\operatorname{wt}(x)\geq2$, this forces $m=1$ and
$\operatorname{wt}(x)=p$. The only remaining coefficient monomial is
$T^{[p]}$. Any product of two positive pure coefficient monomials of
total weight $p$ is $$T^{[a]}T^{[p-a]}=\binom pa T^{[p]}=0
                    \quad(0<a<p)                         \tag{136.46}$$
in characteristic $p$. Products involving a free variable cannot
contribute to this coefficient basis vector or to a single free
generator. Thus none of the vectors in (136.45) is killed or related by
the product ideal. This proves the asserted free $\bar k$-basis, without
assuming that $\bar k$ is a field or reduced.

There is a canonical simplicial augmentation $\bar P_*\to\bar k$ sending
every positive-weight element to zero. Form the simplicial module $$F_*=
 \left(\bar k\otimes_{\bar P_*}
              \Omega^1_{\bar P_*/\bar S,\mathrm{sp}}\right)^p.
                                                               \tag{136.47}$$
Its degree-$n$ term is free on the symbols $dx$ with $x\in X_n$ of
weight $p$. Composing the relative derivation with the augmentation
kills products of positive-weight elements by the Leibniz rule. It
therefore induces a simplicial linear map $Q_*\to F_*$, taking $x$ to
$dx$ and $z$ to zero, because coefficients in $S$ have zero relative
differential. The bases just computed give an exact sequence of
simplicial $\bar k$-modules $$0\longrightarrow\underline{\bar k z}
       \longrightarrow Q_*
       \longrightarrow F_*\longrightarrow0.              \tag{136.48}$$
The first module is constant: all simplicial operators fix the original
coefficient $T^{[p]}$.

The premise of (136.43) supplies a normalized one-chain
$u\in N_1(P_*^p)$ such that $$\partial u=T^{[p]}.
                                                               \tag{136.49}$$
Apply the relative PD derivation to $u$. It commutes with all simplicial
maps, so $$du\in N_1(\Omega^1_{P_*/S,\mathrm{sp}}{}^{\!p}),
                 \qquad\partial(du)=dT^{[p]}=0.           \tag{136.50}$$
Reduce $u$ modulo $p$ and project it to $Q_*$; write $v$ for its
normalized image. Then $\partial v=z$. Its image $a$ in $N_1(F_*)$ is a
cycle. The connecting map for (136.48) sends its homology class to
$$[z]\in H_0(\underline{\bar k z})
                                      =\bar k z,
                                                               \tag{136.51}$$
which is nonzero. Hence $[a]\ne0$. Equivalently, this last step can be
checked without the long exact sequence. If $a=\partial b$, exactness of
normalization permits lifting $b\in N_2(F_*)$ to
$\widetilde b\in N_2(Q_*)$. Then $v-\partial\widetilde b$ belongs to
$N_1(\underline{\bar k z})=0$, whereas its boundary is $z\ne0$, a
contradiction.

The class $a$ is exactly the image of $du$ under the chain map obtained
by reduction modulo $p$ and augmentation of the differential module. If
$du$ were a boundary integrally over $k$, its image would be a boundary
in $F_*$. The nonzero class in (136.51) therefore proves that (136.50)
is itself a nonzero $H_1$ class. No flat-base-change assertion for
homology or identification with an abstract cotangent complex has been
used.

Finally, condition (136.4) in cochain degree zero would make $P_*^w$
acyclic for every sufficiently large weight $w$, so the premise of
(136.43) would hold at every sufficiently large prime. If infinitely
many primes are nonunits in $k$, there are arbitrarily large such
primes, and (136.43) contradicts eventual horizontal acyclicity of the
one-form row. This proves the consequence. ◻

</div>

For a ring, $k/pk\ne0$ is equivalent to $p$ not being a unit. Thus this
construction obstruction is consistent with the established $p$-local
and positive-characteristic results: only finitely many rational primes
can remain nonunits in those cases. It is also consistent with the
standard integral theory (136.5), whose simplex presentations have new
variables of weight one.

In particular, a relative free simplicial PD resolution of the truncated
coefficient algebra $S/(T^{[w]}:w\geq N)$, $N\geq2$, that is built using
only new variables of weights at least $N$, cannot become admissible
merely by taking its full relative special-PD de Rham algebra. Higher
simplicial cells may repair every zero-form relation; (136.43) then
forces the one-form obstruction. This rules out that coherent repair,
not all possible coherent repairs: weight-one variables, additional form
relations, or other non-free theories are outside the theorem. The
original integral common-source and localization questions remain
separate.

#### AQ-136-TENSOR: the flat tensor target already fails.

The supplied SA08 intake already reports the corresponding prime-power
obstruction in its statement A-S09-26, at
`sections/A_hadamard_image.tex:25`. That prior full proof is not present
among the supplied sources. The proof here rederives the tensor
assertion completely and makes no novelty claim; it does not certify the
intake’s additional assertion about a full PD closure in a rational
Hadamard algebra.

Let $k$ be a commutative ring, $S=\Gamma_k(T)$, and let $B$ be the
admissible divided-power simplex theory in AQ-136-DP-MODEL. Form the
actual simplicial PD differential $S$-algebra $$E=B\otimes_S B.$$ Both
inclusions $B\to E$ preserve the coefficient algebra. Every simplex of
$E$ is free over $S$ in each cochain degree, and has vertical cohomology
$S$ in degree zero and zero in positive degrees, in every weight.
Nevertheless, for every rational prime $p$ and integer $r\geq1$,
$$H_1\bigl(N(E^{p^r,0}_*)\bigr)\simeq k/pk.                 \tag{136.T1}$$
Consequently $E$ fails the horizontal admissibility condition over every
nonzero ring which is not a $\mathbb Q$-algebra. In particular, this
failure occurs even over the $p$-local and positive-characteristic rings
for which the original common-source and terminal-groupoid questions are
already answered affirmatively above.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* In simplex degree $n$, eliminate $x_0$ and $y_0$ using
$\sum x_j=\sum y_j=T$, and eliminate $e_0,f_0$ using
$\sum e_j=\sum f_j=0$. The ordinary tensor has the explicit PD
presentation $$E_n=S\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n\rangle
       \otimes_k\Lambda_k(e_1,\ldots,e_n,f_1,\ldots,f_n),
 \qquad dx_j=e_j,\quad dy_j=f_j.$$ Here angle brackets mean free
divided-power variables and all the displayed variables have weight one.
Their cochain degrees are zero for $x_j,y_j$ and one for $e_j,f_j$. This
is a tensor of $2n$ divided-power de Rham disks over $S$. Each such disk
has augmentation to $S$, and its positive part contracts by
$$h(z^{[a]}\,dz)=z^{[a+1]}.$$ This is an $S$-linear, weight-preserving
contraction. Tensoring the finite number of contractions proves the
asserted vertical cohomology and simplexwise freeness, without a
flatness hypothesis on $k$.

Put $P_*=B_*^{*,0}$, regarded now as a simplicial graded $S$-module, and
augment it to $k=S/(T^{[1]},T^{[2]},\ldots)$ by killing all positive
weights. In simplex degree $n$, $$P_n=\Gamma_k(x_0,\ldots,x_n)
       \simeq S\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\rangle,$$ so it is a free graded
$S$-module. In weight $w>0$ its underlying simplicial $k$-module is
$\Gamma^w_k(L)$, with $L_n=k^{n+1}$ and the summation simplicial maps
already specified in AQ-136-DP-MODEL. The explicit linear simplicial
contraction of $L$ induces a contraction of $\Gamma^w(L)$. Weight zero
is the constant $k$. Therefore $N(P_*)\to k$ is a resolution, and its
terms are projective graded $S$-modules: normalization is the image of a
degreewise splitting idempotent on $P_n$.

The zero-form row of $E$ is the diagonal tensor $P_*\otimes_S P_*$. The
Eilenberg–Zilber comparison for flat simplicial modules identifies its
normalized complex, up to quasi-isomorphism, with
$$N(P_*)\otimes_S N(P_*).$$ An exact primary formulation of this input
is Stacks, Remark 21.39.11, Tag `08QD`, following Lemma 21.39.10. The
statement there is first made for the associated unnormalized complexes;
the split degenerate summands give the normalized formulation used here.
The comparison respects the internal weight grading. Both factors are
nonnegative projective resolutions of $k$, so their tensor computes
$k\otimes_S^{\mathbf L}k$. One may check the last assertion by the
first-quadrant double-complex filtration: tensoring one resolution with
each projective term of the other preserves its homology, and each total
degree has finitely many bidegrees. Thus $$H_i\bigl(N(E^{*,0}_*)\bigr)
                         \simeq\operatorname{Tor}_i^S(k,k).
                                                               \tag{136.T2}$$

Let $I=\bigoplus_{w>0}S^w$. Apply $-\otimes_S k$ to
$0\to I\to S\to k\to0$. Since $S$ is projective over itself and the map
$S\otimes_S k\to k\otimes_S k$ is the identity of $k$, its exact Tor
sequence gives $$\operatorname{Tor}_1^S(k,k)
                           \simeq I/I^2.$$ For $m>1$, the weight-$m$
component of $I^2$ is generated inside $kT^{[m]}$ by
$$\binom ma T^{[m]},\qquad 1\leq a<m.$$ If $m=p^r$, every displayed
binomial coefficient is divisible by $p$, because
$(1+X)^{p^r}=1+X^{p^r}$ in $\mathbb F_p[X]$. Their integer greatest
common divisor divides $\binom{p^r}{1}=p^r$. Moreover
$$\binom{p^r}{p^{r-1}}
   =p\binom{p^r-1}{p^{r-1}-1}$$ has $p$-adic valuation exactly one:
modulo $p$, all the coefficients of $(1+X)^{p^r-1}$ are alternately
$1,-1$, as follows by dividing $1+X^{p^r}$ by $1+X$. The greatest common
divisor is therefore exactly $p$. Bezout’s identity shows that these
coefficients generate the ideal $pk$ after any base change to $k$. Hence
$(I/I^2)^{p^r}=k/pk$, proving (136.T1).

If $k$ is a nonzero ring without a $\mathbb Q$-algebra structure, some
rational prime $p$ is not a unit in $k$; otherwise the map from
$\mathbb Z$ would extend to its localization $\mathbb Q$. For that
prime, $k/pk\ne0$. Equation (136.T1) gives nonzero first simplicial
homology in arbitrarily high weights of the zero-form row. This
contradicts (136.4). ◻

</div>

This is a failure of the displayed common-target construction, including
in a simplexwise free and vertically acyclic case. Replacing a module
tensor by its derived tensor does not establish horizontal
admissibility: (136.T2) already is the derived-module calculation. No
model structure on strict PD cochain algebras, or claim that every
possible PD derived pushout presentation must have this zero-form row,
is assumed. In particular the result neither refutes the original
common-source conjecture nor the integral terminal-groupoid assertion.
Horizontal admissibility is additional information about an actual
simplicial presentation, not a consequence of its simplexwise vertical
derived isomorphism class.

#### AQ-136-SEMILOCAL: arithmetic path gluing for the ordinary localization.

Let $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_s\}$ be a finite nonempty set of rational primes,
and put $$R_P=\mathbb Z[\ell^{-1}:\ell\text{ prime},\ \ell\notin P].$$
For every commutative $R_P$-algebra $k$, the ordinary groupoid obtained
from $\mathcal A_k$ by inverting every morphism is equivalent to the
terminal groupoid. Together with the rational case, this proves the
assertion for every commutative ring in which all but finitely many
rational primes are units.

This is a theorem about the original localization question. It does not
assert a strict common admissible source for two given theories over
$R_P$ when $|P|>1$. Its proof constructs common sources after specified
natural arrows to arithmetic path replacements. The integral ring
$\mathbb Z$, and rings with infinitely many nonunit rational primes, are
not included. The zero ring is harmless: its unital algebra category has
only the zero algebra. Assume henceforth that $k\ne0$.

#### 1. An exact arithmetic sequence for arbitrary modules.

Set $$k_i=k\otimes_{R_P}\mathbb Z_{(p_i)},\qquad
 K=k\otimes_{R_P}\mathbb Q.$$ These are localizations of $k$: to obtain
$k_i$, invert the product of the primes in $P\setminus\{p_i\}$, and to
obtain $K$, invert the product of all primes in $P$. For every
$k$-module $M$, write $M_i=M\otimes_k k_i$ and $M_0=M\otimes_k K$. There
is an exact sequence $$\begin{aligned}
 0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow
       \left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^sM_i\right)\oplus M_0
   &\xrightarrow{\delta} M_0^{\oplus s}\longrightarrow0,\\
 \delta((m_i),m_0)&=(m_i-m_0)_i.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{136.S1}$$

Here is a direct proof, including the base-change assertion. For
$M=R_P$, the kernel statement is $\bigcap_i\mathbb Z_{(p_i)}=R_P$ inside
$\mathbb Q$. For surjectivity, given finitely many $a_i\in\mathbb Q$,
choose a common denominator $D=d\prod_i p_i^{e_i}$ with $d$ prime to
every $p_i$. Write $a_i=b_i/D$. The Chinese remainder theorem gives an
integer $b$ such that $b+b_i$ is divisible by $p_i^{e_i}$ for every $i$.
With $a_0=b/D$ we have $a_i+a_0\in\mathbb Z_{(p_i)}$, as required. The
rightmost module $\mathbb Q^{\oplus s}$ is flat over $R_P$, so tensoring
this short exact sequence with any $R_P$-module remains exact. Apply
this to the underlying $R_P$-module of $M$. This proves (136.S1) for
arbitrary $M$, with no flatness assumption on $k$ or on $M$.

All localizations used below are flat. Thus vertical and normalized
horizontal homology commute with them, and an admissible theory
localizes to an admissible theory with the same weight cutoffs.
Normalization commutes with these base changes, either by exactness or
by its splitting idempotents. The coefficient algebra localizes as
$\Gamma_k(T)\otimes_k k_i=\Gamma_{k_i}(T)$.

#### 2. A rational tensor lemma with a uniform weight bound.

Let $K$ be any $\mathbb Q$-algebra, possibly zero, and let
$N_1,\ldots,N_s$ be positive integers. Over $K$, let $B_{N_i}$ be the
weighted simplex theory whose zero-form coordinates have weight $N_i$
and whose coefficient relation is $\sum_jx_{i,j}=T^{[N_i]}$. It is the
rationalization of the basic source in AQ-136-PLOCAL. Set
$$F=\bigotimes_{i=1}^s{}_{\Gamma_K(T)}B_{N_i}.$$ Then $F$ is admissible.
Its vertical cohomology is exactly $\Gamma_K(T)$ in degree zero in every
weight; for fixed form degree $q$, its horizontal homology vanishes in
all weights $$w\ge q\max_iN_i+\sum_iN_i.           \tag{136.S2}$$

We give the module calculation rather than infer this claim from a
vertical Künneth argument. Identify $\Gamma_K(T)$ with $S_K=K[t]$, by
$T^{[a]}=t^a/a!$. Rescaling the coordinates by $N_i!$ writes the $i$th
coefficient relation as $\sum_jx_{i,j}=t^{N_i}$. Before this base
change, use the coefficient algebra $K[U_i]$ and relation
$U_i=\sum_jx_{i,j}$, where $U_i$ and the coordinates have weight $N_i$.
Its form-$q_i$ row is $$\bigoplus_{m\ge0}\operatorname{Sym}^m_K(L)
                         \otimes_K\Lambda^{q_i}_K(Q),
 \qquad Q=L/K\!\left(\sum_jx_j\right),                 \tag{136.S3}$$
with weight $N_i(m+q_i)$ in the $m$th summand. The positive-$m$ summands
are simplicially contractible: apply $\operatorname{Sym}^m$ to the
explicit linear contraction of $L$, and tensor the resulting simplicial
homotopy with the identity on $\Lambda^{q_i}Q$. Hence the augmentation
of this row onto $\Lambda^{q_i}Q$, on which $U_i$ acts as zero, is a
horizontal quasi-isomorphism. Each simplex of the row is a free
$K[U_i]$-module: eliminate $x_{i,0}$ using $U_i$, and use the exterior
basis on the remaining differentials.

For a fixed tuple $(q_1,\ldots,q_s)$ with sum $q$, take the tensor of
the rows (136.S3), using the independent coefficient ring
$A=K[U_1,\ldots,U_s]$. The same contraction, on any factor with positive
$m$, shows that it maps by a horizontal quasi-isomorphism to
$$V_* =\bigotimes_i\Lambda^{q_i}Q_*.$$ All of $V_*$ has the single
internal weight $b=\sum_iq_iN_i$, and every normalized term of $V_*$ is
projective over $K$, being a direct summand of a finite free module. The
normalized source is a nonnegative complex of projective $A$-modules. It
therefore computes the derived base change
$S_K\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}N(V_*)$. Here ordinary base change of the
normalized source is the normalized row after base change; the split
normalization idempotents justify this even though $A\to S_K$ need not
be flat.

Resolve $K=A/(U_1,\ldots,U_s)$ by the Koszul complex on the variables
$U_i$. It is exact over arbitrary $K$: successive multiplication by a
polynomial variable is injective. Tensoring this resolution with the
nonnegative projective $K$-complex $N(V_*)$ is a projective
$A$-resolution of that complex, by the first-quadrant filtration. Thus
the required derived base change is computed by
$$K(t^{N_1},\ldots,t^{N_s};S_K)
                               \otimes_K N(V_*).         \tag{136.S4}$$
Reorder the indices so $N_1=\min_iN_i$, and let $\xi_i$ be the Koszul
generator of weight $N_i$ and homological degree one. The invertible
basis change $$\eta_i=\xi_i-t^{N_i-N_1}\xi_1\quad(i>1)$$ has
$d\eta_i=0$. Consequently (136.S4) is quasi-isomorphic to
$$S_K/(t^{N_1})\otimes_K
       \Lambda_K(\eta_2,\ldots,\eta_s)\otimes_K N(V_*).
                                                               \tag{136.S5}$$
The passage to the quotient is valid after tensoring: $N(V_*)$ is
bounded below with projective $K$-terms, and the exterior factors are
free. Every term in (136.S5) has weight at most
$$b+(N_1-1)+\sum_{i>1}N_i
                    \le q\max_iN_i+\sum_iN_i-1.$$ There are finitely
many tuples $(q_i)$ for a fixed $q$, so this proves (136.S2). The
tensor-to-normalized comparisons used here are the flat Eilenberg–Zilber
comparison of Stacks, Remark 21.39.11, Tag `08QD`; equivalently one can
apply its first-quadrant total complex form at each finite tensor step.

Finally, simplexwise, eliminate one zero-form coordinate per factor
using $t^{N_i}$. The vertical complex is $S_K$ tensored with finitely
many polynomial de Rham disks. Integration over the $\mathbb Q$-algebra
$K$ contracts each disk onto its constants, preserving weight. This
proves the stated vertical comparison. Thus $F$ is admissible, with a
bound independent of the simplex dimension. This lemma includes $s=1$.

#### 3. Arithmetic path replacements.

For a differential $K$-algebra $D$, put
$$\operatorname{Path}_{\rm dR}(D)=D\otimes_K K[z,dz],
 \quad |z|=0,\quad |dz|=1,\quad\operatorname{wt}(z)
                  =\operatorname{wt}(dz)=0.$$ Evaluation at $z=0,1$,
$dz=0$ gives $e_0,e_1$. Integration is a natural contraction of this
path complex onto $D$, so both evaluations induce the same isomorphism
in vertical cohomology. This construction is used only over the
$\mathbb Q$-algebra $K$. Its positive-weight divided powers are uniquely
specified by $\gamma_m(a)=a^m/m!$ on even elements.

For $C\in\mathcal A_k$, write $C_i=C\otimes_k k_i$ and
$C_0=C\otimes_k K$. Define $\mathcal E(C)$, in every simplex degree, to
consist of tuples $$((c_i)_{i=1}^s,c_0,(\omega_i)_{i=1}^s)$$ with
$c_i\in C_i$, $c_0\in C_0$, and
$\omega_i\in\operatorname{Path}_{\rm dR}(C_0)$, subject to
$$e_0\omega_i=c_i|_K,
             \qquad    e_1\omega_i=c_0.                   \tag{136.S6}$$
This is a limit of strict bigraded PD differential algebras, so it
inherits all the required operations. It is simplicial and functorial in
$C$. The coefficient map takes a coefficient to its localizations and
constant paths. The same prescription gives a natural morphism
$$\eta_C:C\longrightarrow\mathcal E(C).
                                                               \tag{136.S7}$$

We verify admissibility explicitly. Forgetting multiplication gives a
short exact sequence of simplicial cochain modules
$$0\longrightarrow\mathcal E(C)\longrightarrow D(C)
 \longrightarrow C_0^{\oplus 2s}\longrightarrow0,          \tag{136.S8}$$
where $D(C)=(\bigoplus_i C_i)\oplus C_0
\oplus\operatorname{Path}_{\rm dR}(C_0)^{\oplus s}$, and the last map is
the endpoint difference in (136.S6). It is surjective in every cochain
and simplex degree: $(1-z)a+zb$ gives prescribed endpoints $(a,b)$. This
section is linear and simplicially natural; it need not be a cochain
map.

Fix a cochain cutoff $d$. At sufficiently large weight, uniformly in
simplex degree, every localized vertical complex has coefficient
cohomology in degree zero and zero cohomology in degrees $1$ through
$d$. The same is true of the rational path complexes. On degree-zero
cohomology, the cokernel of the map in (136.S8), after eliminating the
path constants, is exactly the cokernel of (136.S1), and its kernel is
$S_k^w$. Thus the long exact vertical cohomology sequence gives
$H^0=S_k^w$ and $H^j=0$ for $1\le j\le d$. All bounds are independent of
simplex degree. For fixed form degree $q$, the path row is a direct sum
of copies of the two rows $C_0^{w,q}$ and $C_0^{w,q-1}$. Polynomial
coefficients are free over $K$, so these rows are horizontally acyclic
at large weight. The other rows in (136.S8) are likewise acyclic, and
its horizontal long exact sequence proves the same for $\mathcal E(C)$.
This proves $\mathcal E(C)\in\mathcal A_k$.

We will also use its compatibility with the simplicial cotensor $P_s$ of
AQ-136-COTENSOR: $$\mathcal E(P_s C)\simeq P_s\mathcal E(C).
                                                               \tag{136.S9}$$
For each $n$, the simplicial set $\Delta[n]\times\Delta[1]$ has finitely
many nondegenerate simplices. Sections of a simplicial module on it are
therefore computed by a finite limit of values on simplices. Flat
localization commutes with these limits. Tensoring with $K[z]$ also
commutes with them, since it is flat, and a fixed path cochain degree is
a sum of only the two displayed form degrees. Limits commute with the
finite limits in (136.S6). These natural identifications preserve
products, the differential, divided powers and all endpoint maps,
proving (136.S9).

#### 4. One local source and homotopies for an entire finite diagram.

Let a finite directed graph be labelled by objects $C_v\in\mathcal A_k$
and actual morphisms $f_e:C_v\to C_{v'}$ on its edges. For each $i$,
choose a power $N_i=p_i^{a_i}$ so large that every zero-form row
$(C_v)_i^{N_i,0}$ is horizontally acyclic. There is one such power for
all vertices, since they are finite in number. The p-local construction
gives the basic admissible source $A_i=B_{N_i}(k_i)$ and maps
$$a_{i,v}:A_i\longrightarrow(C_v)_i
                                                               \tag{136.S10}$$
for every vertex.

For each edge, the two maps $f_ea_{i,v}$ and $a_{i,v'}$ from this *same*
$A_i$ to $(C_{v'})_i$ have a coefficient-preserving simplicial PD
homotopy $$h_{i,e}:A_i\longrightarrow P_s((C_{v'})_i).
                                                               \tag{136.S11}$$
Here is the simultaneous-choice justification. On the weight-$N_i$
coordinate module $L$, the difference of the two coordinate maps kills
the constant submodule $k_i\sum x_j$, because both maps take it to
$T^{[N_i]}$. It factors through $Q=L/k_i\sum x_j$, whose normalized
complex is $k_i[1]$. Horizontal $H_1$ of the target zero-form row
vanishes. The resulting degree-one cycle is therefore a boundary, giving
a chain nullhomotopy, and Dold–Kan gives a simplicial linear homotopy.
Adding the first coordinate map makes it relative to the coefficient
submodule. The free PD coordinate presentation extends it, with its
differential, to (136.S11). This is precisely the construction checked
in AQ-136-PLOCAL-LOCALIZATION. The same row cutoff works for every edge;
choices for the finitely many homotopies may be made separately after
the single common $A_i$ and all maps (136.S10) have been fixed. The
resulting data supply every edge of the finite graph at once.

#### 5. Gluing that finite diagram and killing every finite loop.

Form the rational admissible theory
$$F=\bigotimes_{i=1}^s{}_{S_K}(A_i\otimes_{k_i}K),$$ which is covered by
Step 2, and let $j_i:(A_i)_K\to F$ be the canonical inclusions. Define
$A$ by the same path-gluing formula as (136.S6), using local objects
$A_i$, central object $F$, and endpoint maps $j_i$:
$$A=\left\{((a_i),a_0,(\omega_i)):
     \begin{array}{l}
       e_0\omega_i=j_i(a_i|_K),\\
       e_1\omega_i=a_0\quad(1\le i\le s)
     \end{array}\right\}.                                 \tag{136.S12}$$
Here $a_i\in A_i$, $a_0\in F$, and
$\omega_i\in\operatorname{Path}_{\rm dR}(F)$. The verification in Step 3
applies verbatim to this diagram of different theories. In detail, its
endpoint difference map is still degreewise surjective; all local and
central vertical cohomology groups are exactly their coefficient rings,
and the induced maps are the coefficient localizations. Sequence
(136.S1) consequently gives vertical cohomology exactly $S_k$ in every
weight. Its horizontal rows are eventually acyclic by the finitely many
p-local source cutoffs and the uniform rational bound (136.S2). Hence
$A\in\mathcal A_k$.

For each vertex, multiplication of the rationalized maps (136.S10)
defines a strict map $$b_v:F\longrightarrow(C_v)_0,\qquad
        b_v(u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_s)
                   =\prod_i a_{i,v}(u_i).$$ It is well defined over
$S_K$, since all component maps agree on that coefficient algebra. It
preserves the differential and the gradings; over the rational algebra
it also preserves the uniquely specified divided powers. Its restriction
along $j_i$ is $(a_{i,v})_K$. Applying it to the central value and the
paths in (136.S12), with (136.S10) on the local values, gives a strict
map $$\alpha_v:A\longrightarrow\mathcal E(C_v).
                                                               \tag{136.S13}$$

Likewise multiply the rationalized maps (136.S11) to obtain
$b_e:F\to P_s((C_{v'})_0)$. Its endpoints are $f_eb_v$ and $b_{v'}$:
this follows from multiplicativity of $f_e$ and of the endpoint maps. On
local values use $h_{i,e}$, on the central value use $b_e$, and on
rational paths use its differential path extension. This gives
$$A\longrightarrow\mathcal E(P_s C_{v'})
                         \simeq P_s\mathcal E(C_{v'}),$$ whose endpoints
are $\mathcal E(f_e)\alpha_v$ and $\alpha_{v'}$. Thus the single source
$A$ supplies homotopies for every edge of the entire finite graph, with
all its vertex maps fixed.

In the all-arrow localization, the two evaluations
$P_sD\rightrightarrows D$ are equal: they are both inverse to the
constant-path arrow $D\to P_sD$. All three objects and arrows are
admissible by AQ-136-COTENSOR. Consequently
$$\mathcal E(f_e)\alpha_v=\alpha_{v'}$$ in that localization. Naturality
of (136.S7) now shows that, with $\beta_v=\eta_{C_v}^{-1}\alpha_v$,
$$f_e\beta_v=\beta_{v'}.
                                                               \tag{136.S14}$$
Every $\beta_v$ is an isomorphism there, since all original arrows are
inverted.

An arbitrary morphism in the ordinary localization is represented by a
finite word of arrows and their formal inverses. For any two such words
with the same endpoints, take the finite graph consisting of all their
objects and arrows. Equation (136.S14) rewrites either word as
$\beta_{\rm end}\beta_{\rm start}^{-1}$, so the words are equal. Taking
two isolated vertices supplies an isomorphism between any two objects.
Finally, the category is nonempty by AQ-136-DP-MODEL. It is therefore
equivalent to the terminal groupoid. No higher coherence assertion about
an $\infty$-categorical localization is required or inferred: the common
cone and its edge homotopies are used to compare arbitrary *finite
words* in the ordinary all-arrow groupoid. $\square$

#### AQ-136-TAME: finite-degree repair does not justify a full acyclic closure.

This finding records an obstruction to a prescribed construction, not a
counterexample to the original common-source or terminal-groupoid
question. It is compatible with the supplied SA08 intake’s
statement-level reports about failed delayed bar primitives; their
unavailable full proofs are not certified here.

Let $k$ be any commutative ring, fix a positive weight $m$, and put
$$D=\Lambda_k(a)\otimes_k\Gamma_k(b),\qquad
 |a|=1,\quad |b|=2,\quad \operatorname{wt}(a)=\operatorname{wt}(b)=m,
 \qquad da=b,\quad db=0.                                  \tag{136.T1}$$
Here $|\ |$ denotes form degree, and the positive even part has its
usual divided powers. Then $$H^0(D)=k,\qquad
 H^{2r-1}(D)=k[r],\qquad H^{2r}(D)=k/rk\quad(r\ge1),         \tag{136.T2}$$
where the two positive-degree groups in (136.T2) have weight $mr$ and
$k[r]=\ker(r:k\to k)$. In particular over $\mathbb Z[1/d!]$ the
augmentation ideal is acyclic through form degree $2d$. If $p$ is a
nonunit prime of a nonzero $k$, the class of $b^{[p]}$ in form degree
$2p$ is nonzero.

There is nevertheless an admissible strict integral theory
$C\in\mathcal A_k$ containing this constant simplicial algebra as a
retract in simplicial PD $k$-algebras. More precisely, let $B$ be the
standard theory of AQ-136-DP-MODEL, let both $B$ and $D$ map to $k$ by
killing their positive-weight generators, and set
$$C=B\times_k D.               \tag{136.T3}$$ Give $C$ the coefficient
map $s\mapsto(s,\epsilon_S(s))$ from $S=\Gamma_k(T)$. For $k=\mathbb Z$,
no map $A\to C$ of strict PD theories can simultaneously have an element
$x\in A_n^{m,1}$ mapping to $a$ and satisfy
$$H^{2p}(A_n^{mp,*})=0                 \tag{136.T4}$$ for a prime $p$.
Thus a fully vertically acyclic PD replacement with that prescribed lift
cannot be assumed to exist.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The monomials $b^{[r]}$ and $ab^{[r]}$ form the indicated free
graded $k$-basis of $D$. The special differential rule and the signed
Leibniz rule give $$d(b^{[r]})=0,\qquad
 d(ab^{[r-1]})=b b^{[r-1]}=r b^{[r]}.$$ Thus, apart from $k$ in degree
zero, the whole cochain complex is the direct sum of the two-term blocks
$$k ab^{[r-1]}\xrightarrow{\ r\ }k b^{[r]},
       \qquad\text{in form degrees }2r-1,2r.$$ Taking their kernels and
cokernels proves (136.T2) over arbitrary $k$, including rings with
torsion or nilpotents. The PD algebra and differential exist integrally:
they are the usual divided-power polynomial algebra tensored with one
exterior generator, and the displayed formulas satisfy the special
derivation identity. In each block with $r\le d$, $r$ is a unit over
$\mathbb Z[1/d!]$, proving the bounded-degree claim. A nonunit prime has
$k/pk\ne0$, proving the surviving class assertion.

Both augmentations in (136.T3) preserve divided powers and
differentials. The fibre product has componentwise algebra, PD and
differential operations. The constant simplicial structure on $D$ and
the given structure on $B$ make these operations simplicial. Its
underlying bigraded module is $B\oplus I_D$, where $I_D$ is the
positive-degree augmentation ideal of $D$; the unit is the common
diagonal copy of $k$. For each fixed positive form degree $q$, $I_D^q$
has only the single weight $m\lceil q/2\rceil$. Consequently beyond that
weight the simplicial row of $C$ is exactly the row of $B$. Its
horizontal homology therefore vanishes in the required weight tail.
Similarly, (136.T2) shows that in each fixed positive vertical degree
the extra cohomology has finite weight support, independently of the
simplex. In degree zero there is no extra positive-weight summand and
$H^0(C_n)=S$ exactly. These are precisely both uniform admissibility
conditions, so $C\in\mathcal A_k$. The map $D\to C$,
$d\mapsto(\epsilon_D(d),d)$, splits its projection to $D$.

Now take $k=\mathbb Z$ and a proposed map $f:A\to C$ as in the
statement. The element $dx$ is even of positive weight, so its divided
power $\gamma_p(dx)$ exists. It is closed, because the special
derivation rule gives $d\gamma_p(dx)=\gamma_{p-1}(dx)d^2x=0$. Condition
(136.T4) would make it a boundary in $A_n$. But its image under $f$ is
$b^{[p]}$, whose class in $C_n$ is the nonzero element $1\bmod p$ of the
direct summand $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ in (136.T2). A chain map carries
boundaries to boundaries, a contradiction. ◻

</div>

The example has the admissible map $B\to C$,
$b\mapsto(b,\epsilon_B(b))$. It therefore supplies no obstruction to a
common source or to terminal localization: only the specified acyclic
closure is excluded. The elementary disk calculation also pinpoints the
gap in a degreewise tame construction. Such a construction still needs
strict transition maps between whole PD algebras and, for each fixed
form degree, one eventual weight bound uniform across stages and
simplices. Moreover coefficient multiplication raises weight without
raising form degree, so leaving a defect above a cutoff at its first
weight does not control its later coefficient multiples. No existence or
nonexistence of the required coherent relative construction is inferred
from this calculation.

## AQ-SA08-LIFT: a concrete divided-power lifting obstruction

The supplied SA08 intake concerns the uniqueness problem of cote
136/117–121 and a proposed lifting input. The original cote-136 sheets
have now been recovered and analyzed in AQ-136 above. The auxiliary
lifting claim is checked separately in its primary source: Caradot–Lin
(2026), arXiv:2511.22614v4, §2.1, pp. 5–6, and Proposition 3.10,
pp. 19–20. There *free complex* means free in each degree, and
surjectivity of the PD ideals is degreewise. Under these hypotheses the
asserted lift need not exist, over any nonzero commutative ring $k$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Use cochain degrees and the square-zero PD dg algebras
$$R'=k\oplus(ku\xrightarrow{\,d\,}kv),\qquad
 |u|=-2,\quad |v|=-1,\quad du=v,
 \qquad R=k\oplus k\epsilon,\quad|\epsilon|=-2,\quad d\epsilon=0.$$
Their augmentation ideals have zero multiplication, and $\gamma_n=0$ for
$n\geq2$ defines divided powers. All divided-power identities hold:
products of two ideal elements vanish, and the differential identity
$d\gamma_n(u)=\gamma_{n-1}(u)\,du$ is zero for $n\geq2$, including
$u\,du=uv=0$ when $n=2$. Define $\pi(u)=\epsilon$, $\pi(v)=0$, and
$\pi|_k=1$. It is a PD dg map surjective on the ideals. Take the
termwise free complex $X=k\epsilon$, concentrated in degree $-2$, and
the identity chain map $X\to k\epsilon\subset R$. A PD dg algebra lift
from the free PD dg algebra on $X$ would restrict on its generator to a
chain lift $\epsilon\mapsto cu$. Compatibility with $\pi$ forces $c=1$,
whereas compatibility with the differential forces $cv=0$, hence $c=0$
in $k$. This contradicts $k\ne0$. ◻

</div>

The notation $X$ here denotes just $k$ placed in degree $-2$; no
resolution or projectivity in the category of complexes is being
assumed. The failed implication is precisely from a graded lift to a
chain lift. Theorem 3.15 of the same version, pp. 23–24, assumes a
quasi-isomorphism. Our $\pi$ is not one: $H^*(R')=k$ but
$H^*(R)=k\oplus k\epsilon$. Consequently this counterexample does not
refute that later theorem.

The unresolved SA08 statements are the actual integral common-source
assertion A-CS, terminality of the all-arrow localization A-TL, and
application of the SA09 comparison structures A-SA09. The original
common-source and groupoid questions are now identified precisely.
Common sources are proved over the rational, p-local and
positive-characteristic coefficient rings stated above. The ordinary
terminal groupoid is proved more broadly in AQ-136-SEMILOCAL for every
ring with only finitely many nonunit rational primes. Both original
assertions over $\mathbb Z$ remain separate gates. The full prior SA09
comparison structures remain an intake-level source gate; no absent
prior proof is claimed checked here. Neither a weight grading on a
divided-power algebra nor the adopted analytic zero theorem supplies a
chain lift in the displayed counterexample. That is a specific false
lifting route; it is not a disproof of the remaining existence or
localization conjectures.
