\section{K-025: a polystable odd lift at the requested dyadic order}

\begin{proposition}[K-025]
There is a critical tensor in $(\mathbb C^{26})^{\otimes3}$ whose
product-linear stabilizer contains an element with determinant-product
character of order
\[
                         3\cdot2^{25}=3\cdot2^{26-1}.
\]
Consequently a proof of an exact primitive-$2^{n-1}$ tensor pole
cannot exclude all higher odd lifts by asserting that polystable
stabilizers never have determinant order $3\cdot2^{n-1}$.
This statement alone makes no assertion about the full stabilizer,
fixed-component dimensions, or any global Hilbert residue.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first construct a critical dimension-three block with determinant
character of order three.  In the first factor put
\[
 u_0=\sqrt{2/3}\,e_0,\qquad
 u_1=-e_0/\sqrt6+e_1/\sqrt2,\qquad
 u_2=-e_0/\sqrt6-e_1/\sqrt2,
\]
and define
\[
\begin{split}
 T^{(3)}={}&u_0\otimes e_0\otimes e_0
       +u_1\otimes e_1\otimes e_2
       +u_2\otimes e_2\otimes e_1\\
       &+\frac{e_2}{\sqrt3}\otimes
          (e_0\otimes e_2+e_1\otimes e_1+e_2\otimes e_0).
\end{split}
\]
The tight-frame identity
$\sum_j u_ju_j^*=e_0e_0^*+e_1e_1^*$ and
$\|u_j\|^2=2/3$ show that all three reduced Hermitian matrices
are the identity.  Cross terms in the second and third factors
vanish because the first-factor vectors in their two summands
lie in orthogonal subspaces.  In the first factor the two
sets of supported second/third index pairs are disjoint.
Thus $T^{(3)}$ is critical.

For a primitive cube root $\omega$, the diagonal element with
factor exponents
\[
                   (0,0,1),\qquad(0,1,2),\qquad(0,1,2)
                              \pmod3
\]
fixes $T^{(3)}$.  Its determinant-product exponent is
$1+3+3=7\equiv1\pmod3$, so that character has order three.

Take the critical dimension-$23$ tensor from K-020 at $k=3$.
Its full finite determinant character has order
$D=2\cdot256^3=2^{25}$.  Form its orthogonal direct sum with
$T^{(3)}$, using direct sums of dimensions $23+3$ in each mode.
The resulting reduced matrices are again identity matrices,
so this dimension-$26$ tensor is critical and polystable.
The block-diagonal product of a primitive order-$D$ stabilizer
from K-020 and the displayed order-three element fixes this
tensor.  Its determinant character is the product of a primitive
$D$th root and a primitive cube root, hence has order $3D$.
This proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

The example locates an actual odd-lift obstruction in the tensor
representation, rather than only in formal rational-function data.
It does not establish cancellation at the lower dyadic order.
