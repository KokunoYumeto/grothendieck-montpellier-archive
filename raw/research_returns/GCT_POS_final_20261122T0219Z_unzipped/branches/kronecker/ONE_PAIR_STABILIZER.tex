\section{K-013: the single-pair criterion and all five-dimensional stabilizers}

\begin{theorem}[K-013]
Retain the hypotheses and notation of K-012, with odd $n\ge5$.
Suppose each factor spectrum of $g$ contains at most one antipodal
pair, counted as an unordered pair of eigenvalue indices: explicitly,
if $a_j$ is the multiplicity of $\zeta^j$, require
$\sum_{0\le j<M/2}a_j a_{j+M/2}\le1$.
In particular, when the pair exists its two eigenspaces are
one-dimensional.  If one factor has no antipodal pair,
K-012 applies.  Otherwise let $P_A,P_B,P_C$ be the two-dimensional
subspaces spanned by the respective paired eigenspaces and let
$U_A,U_B,U_C$ be their complementary eigenspaces.  Suppose at least one
of the three tensor projections
\[
 T_{P_AU_BU_C},\qquad T_{U_AP_BU_C},\qquad T_{U_AU_BP_C}
\]
is nonzero.  Then $L=S\langle g\rangle$ and the effective
$SL_n^3$ stabilizer is trivial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the finite central extension $\widetilde H\to H$ constructed in
K-012.  Its argument already proves $H^{\rm ab}\simeq\mu_M$ via
$\chi$, and
\[
 \rho(g^u)=0\quad\text{for every nonlinear irreducible character
 of }H\text{ and every odd }u.                              \tag{K-013a}
\]
Indeed the $M$ linear characters exhaust the column-orthogonality sum
$|C_H(g^u)|=M$.

First consider a projective irreducible constituent $V$ of one factor,
meaning an irreducible $\widetilde H$-constituent with the factor's
fixed central character.  If it has a nontrivial $\chi$ self-twist,
its $g$-spectrum is invariant under a nontrivial two-power rotation.
Its eigenvalues then occur in antipodal pairs.  Since the ambient
factor has at most one such pair, $\dim V\le2$.
If there is no self-twist, (K-012b)--(K-012d) give
$\sum_j(a_j-a_{j+M/2})^2\le1$.  Thus its spectrum has at most one
unpaired eigenvalue, besides antipodal pairs.  Again there is at most
one pair, so $\dim V\le3$.  Moreover at most one constituent of a
factor can contain that pair; every other constituent is a line by
the antipodal-free argument of K-012.  As $n\ge5$, each factor has
at least one line constituent (in fact at least two).

Choose a line character $\lambda_A$ in the first factor and twist the
entire factor by $\lambda_A^{-1}$.  The center $Z$ now acts trivially,
so the twisted factor is an honest representation of $H$.
Do the same in the other two factors, using $\lambda_B,\lambda_C$.
The product $\lambda_A\lambda_B\lambda_C$ is trivial on $Z$,
because the original central scalar actions multiply to one on the
tensor product.  It therefore descends to a character of $H$, hence
is a power of $\chi$.  Consequently these three twists preserve
invariance of $T$ under $K=\ker\chi$.

By (K-013a), every nonlinear irreducible constituent of a twisted
factor has zero trace at $g$.  For eigenvalues in $\mu_M$, zero trace
is equivalent to equality of the multiplicities at each antipodal
pair: $\zeta^j$, $0\le j<M/2$, are a rational basis of
$\mathbb Q(\zeta)$.  Such a constituent therefore lies entirely
in the unique two-dimensional paired subspace.  The unpaired
eigenlines are linear $H$-characters, so $K$ acts identically on
$U_A,U_B,U_C$.  Each paired subspace is invariant under $H$; if it
contains no nonlinear constituent it is simply a sum of two lines.
The determinant of each twisted factor is also a linear character
of $H$, hence trivial on $K$.  It follows that the remaining action
of $K$ on each $P$ is through $SL_2$.

Suppose, for example, that $T_{P_AU_BU_C}\ne0$.  Since the two $U$
factors are fixed pointwise by $K$, some nonzero coefficient vector
of this projection is a $K$-fixed vector in $P_A$.  A finite subgroup
of $SL_2(\mathbb C)$ fixing a nonzero vector is trivial: every element
has both eigenvalues equal to one and is diagonalizable because it
has finite order.  Thus $K$ acts identically on $P_A$ as well as on
$U_A$.  It acts projectively trivially on the original first factor.
The connected endomorphism-algebra unit-group argument at the end of
K-012 now gives $K=1$.  The other two projections are identical cases.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Exact five-dimensional application}
The seventy-seven types of K-010 have the following numbers of
antipodal pairs in their three factors, with factor order ignored:
\[
\begin{array}{c|r}
 (0,0,0)&2\\
 (0,0,1)&8\\
 (0,1,1)&3\\
 (1,1,1)&64
\end{array}                                                  \tag{K-013b}
\]
No factor has two antipodal pairs.  The first thirteen types are
settled by K-012.  For each of the other sixty-four types, count
support edges that meet exactly one of the three paired coordinate
sets.  The exact distribution is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 \text{number of such edges}&3&4&5&6&7\\\hline
 \text{number of types}&3&7&34&14&6
\end{array}.                                                 \tag{K-013c}
\]
In every case at least one of the three projections in the theorem
is nonzero.  Hence all seventy-seven representatives have full
stabilizer $S\times\mu_{16}$ with faithful determinant character.
In particular their effective $SL_5^3$ stabilizers are trivial and
their quotient points are smooth.  This conclusion uses no conjectural
classification of finite groups, and no identification of a
prequotient normal determinant with a quotient determinant before
the finite stabilizer has been settled.
