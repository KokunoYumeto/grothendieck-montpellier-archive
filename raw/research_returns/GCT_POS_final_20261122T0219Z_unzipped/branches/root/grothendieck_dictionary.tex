\section{Scope of the geometric translation}

This part gives a dictionary for every finding in this continuation.
It transports the proofs already given; it does not infer positivity
from a formal Grothendieck-group identity.  Integral models, geometric
fibres, and characteristic-zero representations are distinguished
throughout.  The precise historical locators below come from the
supplied GCT\_PLETH source audit, read in full in this run.  They are
carried as verified background unless a new reading is expressly
recorded in \texttt{SOURCE\_AUDIT.md}.  The original cote-133 scans
were not newly inspected; the supplied transcriptions and their

\subsection{Schemes, representations and operations}
For a finite locally free module $E$ over a base $S$, the affine
representation space is
$\mathbb V(E)=\operatorname{Spec}_S\operatorname{Sym}(E^\vee)$,
with the usual convention for its functor of points.  A group-scheme
action is equivalently a coaction on its coordinate algebra.  A
$\mathbb G_m$-coaction is a grading, and a representation of a
diagonalizable group $D(M)$ is an $M$-grading.  These are the
frameworks of SGA3, Expos\'e I, \S4.5 at original marginal p.29,
Definitions4.6.1/4.6.3, and Propositions4.7.2/4.7.3
at pp.34--36, and Expos\'e VIII,
Definition1.1, Theorem1.2 and Corollaries1.3/1.4.  The latter
expos\'e was read in the editor-maintained primary edition, PDFp.3,
with original marginal pagination.

In characteristic zero, the representation ring is the exact
$K_0$ of the tensor category of finite-dimensional representations.
For vector bundles, the operations
$\lambda^i[E]=[\Lambda^iE]$ give the special $\lambda$-ring of
SGA6, Expos\'e V, Definition2.1, Lemma2.2.1 and2.4.1, and
Expos\'e VI, Theorem3.3.  Schur operations are obtained from these
operations; symmetric powers satisfy
$\sum h_i(E)t^i=\lambda_{-t}(E)^{-1}$.
The free special $\lambda$-ring and its finite-rank quotients are
SGA6 V, Proposition4.2/Definition4.4 and Proposition4.8/Corollary4.9.
The $\gamma$-operations and filtration are V,3.2--3.4 and3.10.
No assertion that a virtual class has a positive representative
follows from its membership in that filtration.

Over $\mathbb Z$, Schur functors and Cauchy identities require
integral filtrations, rather than characteristic-zero direct-sum
decompositions.  The exact references are Akin, Buchsbaum and
Weyman (1982), Theorems II.2.16, p.232, and III.1.4, pp.245--246.
Their convention $L_{(a)}=\Lambda^a$ means that the usual Schur
functor $\mathbb S_\lambda$ is their $L_{\lambda'}$.
For the corresponding symmetric-function identities see
Macdonald (1995), I(4.2)--(4.3), pp.62--63;
I(7.3), pp.113--114; I(7.5)--(7.6), p.114;
Appendix A(5.3)--(5.6), pp.155--156, and A(7.2)/(7.4), p.160.

\subsection{Closures and parameter schemes}
The orbit map of an integral representation has a schematic image;
its formation is an algebraic operation, with the hypotheses and
base-change limitations of EGAI,9.5.1--9.5.4, pp.176--177, and
9.5.8--9.5.9, p.178.  This supplies integral orbit-closure models.
It does not prove that a chosen model is flat or that invariants
commute with every reduction modulo a prime.  The Hilbert and Quot
schemes parameterize the flat families specified in FGA,
Expos\'e221, Theorems3.1/3.2, pp.259--260; the Hilbert specialization
follows Proposition3.8, p.265, and Remark3.9, p.266, records the
base $\mathbb Z$.  Projectivization and twisting sheaves use EGAII,
2.4.1--2.4.2, p.28;2.5.2/2.5.4, p.31;
2.5.7--2.5.9, p.32.  A fixed Hilbert polynomial is part of such
a moduli problem, not a conclusion of an arbitrary orbit closure.

\section{Kronecker findings as statements about quotient schemes}

Put $V_n=(\mathbb C^n)^{\otimes3}$ and
$X_n=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[V_n]^{SL_n^3}$.
The regraded coordinate algebra has
$\dim\mathcal O(X_n)_s=k_n(s)$.
The affine GIT quotient identifies closed orbits; Proj of a
semi-invariant ring supplies the corresponding projective
semistable construction.  These characteristic-zero statements
are the quotient setting of the proofs, and are not claims about
unqualified invariant formation over $\mathbb Z$.

\subsection{Character modules and integral stabilizer models}
Let $\Sigma$ be a tensor support and let
$A:\mathbb Z^\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{3n}$ send an edge
to the sum of its three coordinate characters.  For the tensor
whose supported coefficients are one, its diagonal stabilizer
over $\mathbb Z$ is exactly
\[
                 D(\operatorname{coker}A).
\]
Indeed its equations are $x_i y_j z_k=1$ for all supported
edges, so its Hopf algebra is the group algebra of that cokernel.
The determinant-product character is the class of the all-ones
vector.  Thus all lattice indices and character orders in
K-002--K-020 have a literal integral group-scheme interpretation.
The unavoidable rank-two free summand gives the scalar torus
$S=\{(aI,bI,cI):abc=1\}$.

When $\operatorname{coker}A$ has free rank two and cyclic torsion
of order $M$, the diagonal stabilizer is $S\times\mu_M$ after
a choice of splitting.  The
finite group scheme $\mu_M$ is finite flat over $\mathbb Z$ of
degree $M$; it is smooth precisely away from primes dividing
$M$.  This is SGA3 VIII, Proposition2.1, editor PDFp.8, original
pp.6--7.  Consequently these integral equations remain meaningful
at2, but the reduced complex finite-group arguments must not be
transferred to that fibre without further work.

For a free support, a positive margin-one solution gives a complex
critical tensor by taking square roots of the masses.  This is the
moment-map/closed-orbit step proved in the Kronecker sections.
Independence of the incidence columns puts that tensor and the
coefficient-one tensor in the same diagonal orbit.  Positivity of
real masses proves complex polystability; it is not an assertion
of fibrewise polystability over every residue field.

\paragraph{K-001.}
The classical quotient $X_3$ is affine three-space with coordinate

are consequently those calculated in K-001.  The invariant-ring

\paragraph{K-002--K-008 and K-010--K-014.}

Their localized normal Euler classes add to the exact nonzero

\paragraph{K-009.}
The bound

\paragraph{K-015--K-016.}

\paragraph{K-017.}

\paragraph{K-018.}
The boundary gadget constructs polystable supports whose

\[
             2^{n-1+\lfloor(n-2)/7\rfloor}.
\]
This refutes the proposed universal order $2^{n-1}$ bound even

\paragraph{K-019--K-020.}
The additional finite-group proofs identify the full complex

\paragraph{K-021.}
Iterating the same character-module reduction collapses one
nested eigenspace block in each tensor factor.  The smooth
fixed scheme of the originally targeted subgroup has dimension
$343l^3+14l+1$ in dimensions $n=2+56l$.  This is an exact

the constant curve wave from dimensions four and five.
The global leading coefficient remains a separate sum.

\paragraph{K-022.}
The affine cone over $\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)$ is the quotient
of five ordered vectors in dimension two by $SL_2$.
The chosen positive column grading has two nonzero smooth
$\mu_M$-fixed curves. Their density-one inverse Euler classes
are nonzero and opposite; their sum vanishes. The Pfaffian
resolution gives the same cancellation in the Hilbert numerator.
Thus normality, factoriality, Gorenstein duality and rational
singularities do not imply noncancellation of fixed-component
terms. This is a direct counterexample in the same localized
$K_0$ framework, not a claim about the tensor grading itself.

\paragraph{K-023.}
In the completed representation $\lambda$-ring, the cycle-index
character of cubing permutations equals the symmetric algebra
on $W\oplus\mathbb S_{(2,1)}W$, with central weights one and
three. This specializes Scharf's higher Lie-character identity
as stated in Adin--Heged\H us--Roichman, Definition2.1 and
Theorem2.2, printed p.5. The rectangular Schur coefficient extracts
the determinant-isotypic graded character, hence the Hilbert
character of its $SL_n$ quotient coordinate algebra. The rational
$C_3$ representation ring
maps dimension and cyclic trace to the same integer modulo
three. This is an integral character congruence; it does not
identify the quotient schemes in characteristic three.

\paragraph{R-NEW-004 and K-024.}
The hook-chain vector defines a complex closed orbit in
$W\oplus\mathbb S_{(2,1)}W$. Its infinitesimal stabilizer is
zero, and its full general-linear stabilizer is the cyclic
diagonalizable group $\mu_{2^{n-1}}$, with faithful determinant.
The corresponding special-linear quotient is smooth on its
unique nonzero maximal dyadic fixed curve. The grading-stack
residual gerbe is $B\mu_{2^{n-1}}$. The conormal exterior
Euler class is invertible after evaluating at a primitive
dyadic character and inverting two. In particular its
density-normalized local term is a unit at primes over three.
The characteristic-zero Lie proof contains a coefficient
determinant three; no smooth integral model at three or
base-change theorem for invariant rings is inferred from it.

\paragraph{PNEW-013.}
The modular pole transfer is a statement about integral
Hilbert characters and their cyclotomic denominators. Under
reduction modulo three, characters of orders $M3^r$ have the
same prime-to-three order $M$. The unique auxiliary residual
gerbe and its full inertia exclude every higher colliding
auxiliary pole. The unit Euler term then survives reduction,
so the congruent tensor Hilbert character must have an actual
characteristic-zero denominator factor of order $M3^r$ for
some $r\ge0$. This proves the exponential denominator bound
and refutes numerical PH3. It is not a claim that an invariant
ring over $\mathbb Z$ commutes with reduction, and it does
not prove the exact primitive-$M$ pole ($r=0$) of the tensor
Hilbert series. SGA3 supplies the character-group dictionary and
SGA6 the exterior Euler-class framework; the cyclotomic
arithmetic and transfer proof are given in full here.

\paragraph{K-025.}
The orthogonal direct sum of two critical tensor representations
has identity moment marginals when both blocks do. Their diagonal
stabilizer elements give a block-diagonal subgroup of the larger
stabilizer, and the determinant-product character multiplies.
The coprime orders $2^{25}$ and three therefore give an actual
polystable dimension26 point with character order $3\cdot2^{25}$.
This is an existence statement for a point and its inertia subgroup;
it supplies neither its full inertia nor an actual Hilbert pole.

\subsection{The Euler class and the wave}
At a smooth fixed component the moving normal calculation is
the Koszul Euler class
\[
        \lambda_{-1}(N^*)=\sum_j(-1)^j[\Lambda^jN^*].
\]
The exact source for the regular-immersion Tor/conormal formula
is SGA6 VII, Proposition2.5; the self-intersection formula is
Proposition2.7, with its stated hypotheses, and Corollary2.8.
The complete VII \S2 transcription was read in this run.
The equivariant Hilbert-residue formula used here has its own
complete Koszul proof, ALPOGE\_GCT P-F23, including (P-F23g).
SGA6 VII is not substituted for an unqualified equivariant
localization theorem.

The point-pullback convention fixes the eigenvalue phases.
Evaluation of the inverse moving Euler class at the grading
element, multiplied by the component's graded density, gives
the local leading coefficient.  Contributions of all components
of the relevant maximal dimension must then be added.
An inverse Euler class lies in a localized equivariant $K$-group;
it need not be the character of an actual effective finite
representation.  Its finite Fourier wave need not be a motive,
and a nonzero individual local class does not prove that their
sum is nonzero.  K-008/K-014 perform that sum; K-018--K-020 do not.
This distinction agrees with Derksen's universal-denominator
theorem and its actual-denominator counterexamples, with exact
locators in the source audit.

\paragraph{R-NEW-003.}
The explicit denominator and finite character-sum numerator
give the character of $\mathcal O(X_4)$ as an exact rational
function.  This is a finite presentation of that character,
not an explicit affine equation presentation of $X_4$ and not
the computed minimal numerator.  The passage from exact
quasipolynomiality to a proper rational character is justified
by the cited Baldoni--Vergne theorem in the proof.

\section{Plethysm and positive formulas in the geometric language}

\paragraph{PNEW-001--PNEW-007.}
The inherited cubic family is the graded coordinate algebra
of an affine invariant quotient, or equivalently the specified
multiplicity algebra.  Schur operations identify each graded
piece with the requested multiplicity space.  The complete
fixed-locus and Koszul analysis in the standalone proof gives
the nonzero dyadic wave.  Its exact Fourier comparison with
the Hilbert function gives the constituent-index obstructions.
PNEW-002--PNEW-007 sharpen constants, exhibit exact witnesses,
and use a Veronese regrading of the same algebra.  Their
geometric translation is therefore exact, but the exponential
inequality itself remains the proved numerical estimate; it
is not a formal consequence of purity or of an effective
$K_0$ class.  In particular the pure Tate cohomology of the
earlier Kleinian example coexists with failure of the asserted
positive homogeneous polytope formula.

\paragraph{PNEW-008.}
Tensoring a representation by a determinant line changes its
linearization and can change its raw general-linear central kernel, while
the explicitly computed invariant/multiplicity algebra remains
the same in the stated family.  Hence the quotient grading
and its strict saturation obstruction can persist without
growth of the intrinsic or effective stabilizer.  This is a
statement about linearizations and quotient inertia; it does
not identify raw central kernels under all conventions.

\paragraph{PNEW-009.}
For a fixed representation embedding, the branching cone and
its piecewise quasipolynomial multiplicities give the finite
chamber data used in the exact binary-shell density proof.
The resulting scarcity of strict saturation exceptions is a
counting statement about integral dominant weights indexing
Schur bundles.  A special $\lambda$-ring identity alone does
not supply its uniform estimates, and varying the embedding
changes the cone, lattice and constants.  No uniform theorem
on all such moduli data is asserted.

\paragraph{PNEW-010.}
The finite cokernel in G-040 is the graded coherent module of
the displayed equivariant morphism of symmetric-power bundles.
Its minimal number of generators over the polynomial invariant
algebra $J$ is the dimension of its fibre modulo $J_+$, by
Nakayama's lemma.  The exponential lower bound is therefore
a lower bound for the rank of a free sheaf surjecting onto
that actual module in the prescribed grading.  Fitting ideals
record generator bounds and rank strata.  The precise modern
locators read here are Stacks, tag07Z6, Lemmas15.8.2,
15.8.4(3),15.8.7,15.8.8, and tag05P8, Lemma31.9.6.
No unverified EGA Fitting locator is assigned.  A lower bound
for a dense free presentation does not rule out a compressed
positive counting construction.

\paragraph{PNEW-011.}
The blow-up of $\mathbb P(h_m)$ in the simplex frame has the
known Grassmannian Cox ring of Castravet--Tevelev (2006),
Remark3.9, author p.9, published p.1487.  The finding computes
the actual multigrading and permutation action.  The scalar
line bundle $cH-k\sum E_i$ has its space of global sections
identified with the stated balanced-weight space of the
two-row Schur module.  For six points this is $\operatorname{Gr}(2,7)$
with its specified six-coordinate weight and $S_6$ action.
Integral standard monomials give a basis of the coordinate
ring; they do not by themselves give positive multiplicities
for its irreducible $S_6$ representations.

\paragraph{PNEW-012.}
The fixed polytope family counts those scalar-section
multiplicity spaces in every degree.  The marked-tree positive
formula is credited to Pak--Panova--Swanson, using the classical
KOH identity of O'Hara/Zeilberger and the GOS identity of
Goodman--O'Hara--Stanton from Kirillov--Reshetikhin.
Its bounded compilation and
binary-degree evaluator are proved here.  This supplies an
effective positive description of this particular equivariant
$H^0$, with at most45 polytopes and11 variables.  It does not
replace the flag-bundle sections required for arbitrary
Schur tails in G-015/G-016.  Those flag sections, ideal powers
and transverse-order conditions have been checked against
their full supplied proofs.

\paragraph{R-NEW-001 and R-NEW-002.}
R-NEW-001 computes the complete small-degree character table
of the outer-six Schur operations by two independent standard
character identities; it is finite verification data.
The canonical Hadamard--Howe maps in R-NEW-002 are natural
morphisms between the corresponding Schur constructions.
Over characteristic zero their kernels/images have the exact
isotypic ranks given by the cited rank and propagation
theorems.  The requested critical kernels are zero, so the
corresponding coherent kernel sheaves over the base field
vanish.  These rank statements are credited consequences,
not newly proved integral splitting theorems or guarantees
of unchanged rank after reduction modulo a prime.

\section{Nonstandard findings as modules, sheaves and realizations}

Let $A=\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$ and let $B_A$ be the actual
P-generated subalgebra of the tensor square of the Hecke
algebra.  Its associated coherent module on $\operatorname{Spec}A$
is the integral object in N-009.  The affine module/sheaf
dictionary is EGAI,1.3.7, p.86;1.3.8, p.87;
1.4.1, pp.90--92.  The meaning of a perfect complex is
SGA6 I,4.7, pp.121--122; coherent is not synonymous with
locally free, and no perfectness assertion is needed here.

\paragraph{N-001--N-004.}
The specified weighted cyclic identity for the full tensor of
structure coefficients fails an actual product-relation test.
The shortest negative word is a failure of one
chosen positive-coordinate cone in that coherent module.
The114-vector generic positive basis is a trivialization
over the fraction field and spans a proper integral lattice.
The conditional freeness-to-positive-basis construction is
a statement about that specified cone and coefficient ring.
None of these facts identifies a tensor category or an
effective motivic basis merely from signs in a coordinate
expansion.

\paragraph{N-005.}
The new dimension rings are associative rings on a chosen
basis with graded nonnegative structure constants.  Their
signed traces, absolute-value bounds and parity constraints
are checked explicitly.  They improve the previous search
scores, including a choice with unimodal graded dimensions.

monoidal category; its associators are inherited from the
ambient matrix category.  The unit column proves independence

\paragraph{N-006.}
For each fixed prime power $q>1$, the actual coefficient
object $M$ carries the explicitly diagonalizable Frobenius
and the actual commuting $P_1,P_2$ action.  Pull it back from
the point to $\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb F_q}$ and Tate-twist by1.

checked directly from its eigenvalues, and
$H_c^2=M$, with all other compact cohomology zero, including
the required algebra action.  Frobenius/weight conventions
are Deligne, Weil II,1.1.7, pp.150--151;

Proposition1.6/Definition1.7 and Corollary2.2.

This construction supplies a genuine constructible

It neither claims a constant-coefficient smooth projective
motive nor makes a new choice of the parameter after every
finite-field extension.  Under an extension the sheaf's
Frobenius is raised to that extension degree.  The actual
rank-three module action is retained at the exact specialized
parameter values; no sheaf over a collision-parameter family is
asserted.  The action is not replaced by matching scalar traces alone.

\paragraph{N-007--N-008.}
The rational function $1/D=q/(q+1)^2$ shows that a coefficient
in $\mathbb Q(D)$ need not be a finite Laurent-Tate character.
A pole at $q=-1$ excludes that particular interpretation.
At the sign specialization, N-008 computes the image of the
algebra in its ambient tensor product and its trace rank.
The abstract fibre $B_A\otimes_A k$ need not inject into that
ambient fibre: tensoring an inclusion is not left exact in
general.  Thus image dimension55 is not the dimension of

\paragraph{N-009.}

\paragraph{N-010--N-011.}
Over $\mathbb Q(D)$ the order splits into the explicitly

\paragraph{N-012--N-014.}

\paragraph{N-015--N-016.}

It defines
