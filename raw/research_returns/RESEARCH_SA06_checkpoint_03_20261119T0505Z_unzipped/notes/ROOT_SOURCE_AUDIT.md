### Milne normalization checks

Theorem1.6 in both the supplied and current PDF prints S^(2d)(X×X,ell), while its proof uses the generalized eigenvalue-one space in H^(2d)(X×X,Qell(d)). With the source's definition of S^r, the hypothesis needed is **S^d(X×X,ell)**. The corrected claim has the following elementary proof. Over a splitting field, let V_a be a generalized a-eigenspace of Frobenius on H^r(X). Poincaré duality identifies the corresponding reciprocal block with V_a^*. Their tensor is End(V_a), and Frobenius acts by conjugation. If a nontrivial unipotent Jordan factor U occurred, conjugation by U would be a nontrivial unipotent operator on End(V_a): an operator commuting with every endomorphism is scalar, and a scalar unipotent operator is the identity. The hypothesis S^d makes that conjugation the identity, a contradiction. Hence every block is semisimple. We do not treat the printed top-degree hypothesis as an unconditional proof of general Frobenius semisimplicity.

The definition of equivalence of a p^(n1)-structure pi1 and a p^(n2)-structure pi2 on printedp4 uses pi1^(n1 N)=pi2^(n2 N). Base change requires the crossed powers **pi1^(n2 N)=pi2^(n1 N)**, as the preceding Frobenius discussion on printedp2 correctly states. For example, H²(P¹) over Fp has pi1=p; over Fp² it has pi2=p². The crossed equality holds, whereas the printed uncrossed equality would require p^N=p^(4N), impossible for N>0. No power-change argument here uses the uncrossed formula.

### Debarre–Ein–Lazarsfeld–Voisin version checks

The supplied early Corollary2.2 prints 2≤k≤n. Its preceding argument and the published Corollary2.2 have the correct range **2≤k≤n−2**. At k=n−1 and k=n the claimed strict cone difference does not occur. The early proof of Theorem2.1 also phrases a claim about nonnegative combinations of CM-lattice one-forms too strongly for the subsequent rank-one forms. The published proof correctly takes the **closure** of the convex cone of rank-one forms arising from CM-lattice directions. To check the step directly, the fraction field of the CM order is dense in C; approximate each coefficient of a one-form by elements of that field, clear their common denominators, and rescale the rank-one forms by the positive square of that denominator. Their limits give every rank-one positive form. Pullback of the origin along the corresponding map E^n→E supplies the algebraic divisor; closedness gives the asserted pseudoeffective limit. Thus the published result is supported without the faulty early finite-combination wording.
