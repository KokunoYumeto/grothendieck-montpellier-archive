\section{A first missing copy beyond the binary partial-Hessian algebra}

\subsection*{A-037. The proper ternary ray is already larger at its second stretch}
Take \(n=r=3\), so the partial bracket has
\[
                    \lambda=(7,3,2),\qquad D_r=4.
\]
Here the Hessian is the three-variable Hessian of a
ternary cubic, of coefficient degree three and
variable degree three. It is not the full
nine-variable Hessian of the \(3\)-by-\(3\) determinant.
Let \(\mathcal B\) be the subalgebra generated by the
binary coefficients of \(F(x,y,0)\) and
\(H_3(F)(x,y,0)\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \dim\mathcal B_{8}^{\,U}[(14,6,4)]=3
       <a_{(14,6,4)}(8[3])=4.
 \quad}                                                        \tag{A-037.1}
\]
At the first stretch both dimensions are one.
Thus the whole ambient highest-weight ray is not
contained in this binary-pair algebra. This is an
exact first deficit, not a conclusion drawn from
agreement in finitely many degrees.
By O-007, the determinant retains all ternary
highest-weight copies, so in this example
\[
                         K_3(14,6,4)=4.                       \tag{A-037.2}
\]
The missing fourth copy therefore also survives
on the actual determinant closure.

\paragraph{The complete ambient multiplicity by exact weight extraction.}
Let \(w_d(a,b,c)\) be the weight multiplicity of
\((a,b,c)\) in \(\operatorname{Sym}^d(\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^3)\).
Since \(a+b+c=3d\), it is equivalently
\[
 w_d(a,b,c)=[t^d u^b v^c]
          \prod_{\substack{i,j\geq0\\i+j\leq3}}
                            (1-tu^iv^j)^{-1}.                 \tag{A-037.3}
\]
This counts multisets of the ten ternary cubic
monomials. With \(\rho=(2,1,0)\), multiplication
by the Weyl denominator gives the exact Schur
coefficient
\[
 a_\mu(d[3])
       =\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}
                      \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,
                             w_d(\mu+\rho-\sigma\rho).
                                                               \tag{A-037.4}
\]
The following are all six terms, in one fixed order:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr|r}
 (d,\mu)&+&-&-&+&+&-&a_\mu\\ \hline
 (4,(7,3,2))&12&8&8&4&4&3&1\\
 (8,(14,6,4))&107&82&78&46&48&37&4.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{A-037.5}
\]
For the second line, the corresponding weights are
\[
 \begin{split}
 &(14,6,4),\ (14,7,3),\ (15,5,4),\\
 &(15,7,2),\ (16,5,3),\ (16,6,2).
 \end{split}
\]
For the first line they are
\((7,3,2),(7,4,1),(8,2,2),(8,4,0),(9,2,1),(9,3,0)\).
Equation (A-037.3), or the recurrence obtained by
adjoining its ten geometric-series factors in
order, is a finite exact specification of every
entry. The accompanying script also independently
enumerates all \(715\) weak compositions of four
and all \(24310\) weak compositions of eight into
ten parts; it obtains these same twelve weight
counts. No floating-point rank test is used.

\paragraph{The entire pair-algebra slice has at most three copies.}
Write the two normalized binary cubics as
\[
 f(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^3\binom3i F_i x^{3-i}y^i,\qquad
 h(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^3\binom3i H_i x^{3-i}y^i.
\]
Assign formal bidegree \((1,0)\) to each \(F_i\)
and \((0,1)\) to each \(H_i\).
Their coefficient degrees as functions of the
ternary cubic are one and three, respectively.
An \(H_i\) also has the determinant-square factor
from three-variable Hessian covariance.

Consequently coefficient degree eight and third
diagonal weight four force
\[
                       a+3b=8,\qquad 2b=4,
             \qquad\text{hence }a=b=2.                        \tag{A-037.6}
\]
Thus the complete pair-algebra highest-weight
space in (A-037.1) is a quotient of the corresponding
space in
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}^2(\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^2)
          \otimes
 \operatorname{Sym}^2(\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^2).
 \]
Removing the \(H\)-factor determinant character
\((4,4,4)\) leaves the binary highest weight
\((10,2)\).
In either factor the weight counts at binary
second weights \(0,1,2\) are \(1,1,2\).
In their product the counts at second weights
one and two are \(2\) and \(5\).
The \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) highest-weight
multiplicity is therefore \(5-2=3\).
Characteristic-zero semisimplicity makes this
an upper bound for every quotient, including
the actual pair image.

More generally the analogous free-pair upper count
at bidegree \((s,s)\) and core weight \((5s,s)\) is
\[
 C_s=[z^s]\frac{1-z}{\prod_{i=1}^3(1-z^i)^2}.
 \]
It is only a free-pair bound unless the relations
of the actual pair image have been accounted for.
No dominance onto the eight independent binary
coefficients is assumed here.

\paragraph{The bound three is attained.}
The three usual highest-weight functions are
\[
 \begin{split}
 W_1&=(F_0F_2-F_1^2)H_0^2,\\
 W_2&=F_0^2(H_0H_2-H_1^2),\\
 W_3&=(F_0H_1-F_1H_0)^2.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{A-037.7}
\]
Their characters give weight \((14,6,4)\) and
coefficient degree eight.
Use the ternary family
\[
 F=x^3+3xy^2+\alpha y^2z+(\delta x+\epsilon y)z^2.
 \]
Here \(F_0=1,F_1=0,F_2=1\).
A direct three-by-three Hessian determinant along
\(z=0\) gives
\[
 H_0=72\delta,\qquad H_1=24\epsilon,\qquad
                     H_2=-24\delta-8\alpha^2.                \tag{A-037.8}
\]
These three quantities range dominantly over
\(\mathbb A^3\) over \(\mathbb C\).
The functions (A-037.7) become
\[
                     H_0^2,\qquad H_0H_2-H_1^2,\qquad H_1^2,
 \]
which are linearly independent. This proves the
equality three on the left side of (A-037.1).
At the first stretch the bracket is nonzero by
A-035 and the ambient multiplicity is one by
F-024 or (A-037.5), proving that the displayed
second stretch is the first deficit.

\paragraph{The omitted coefficient and an explicit missing Aronhold copy.}
For a general ternary cubic, write
\[
                   F=f(x,y)+zq_2(x,y)+z^2\ell_1(x,y)+c z^3.
 \]
Its binary Hessian restriction is
\[
 H_3(F)(x,y,0)
   =2\ell_1(f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2)
       -\bigl(f_{yy}q_x^2-2f_{xy}q_xq_y+f_{xx}q_y^2\bigr).
                                                               \tag{A-037.9}
\]
In particular every function in \(\mathcal B\)
is independent of the coefficient \(c\).

Let \(S(F)\) be the classical degree-four
Aronhold invariant, normalized as in Isaev,
\emph{On the image of the associated form morphism},
Saitama Math.\ J.\ \textbf{31} (2017), 89--101,
printed p.~98, equation (4.1).
That source supplies the known invariant and its
explicit polynomial formula; no new invariant
or historical priority is claimed.
Its \(\operatorname{SL}_3\)-invariance and degree
four give
\[
                         S(F\circ g)=\det(g)^4S(F).
 \]
The function
\[
                            V=S(F)F_0^2 I_2,
       \qquad I_2=F_0F_2-F_1^2,                               \tag{A-037.10}
\]
has coefficient degree \(4+2+2=8\), and weight
\[
                         (4,4,4)+(10,2,0)=(14,6,4).
 \]
It is outside the entire binary-pair algebra,
as witnessed by the explicit family
\[
                  F_c=2x^3+6xy^2+6(x^2-y^2)z+c z^3.
 \]
Its pair \((f,h)\) is independent of \(c\), whereas
the cited Aronhold formula and the normalized
binary coefficients give
\[
        S(F_c)=16(c-1),\qquad F_0=2,\qquad I_2=4,
                    \qquad V(F_c)=256(c-1).                  \tag{A-037.11}
\]
For clarity, in Isaev's normalized coefficient
notation this substitutes
\(a=2,b=d=h=i=j=0,e=f=2,g=-2\), with \(c\)
remaining free. The only surviving terms of his
formula are \(-cafg+cef^2-e^2g^2=16c-16\).
This is an exact symbolic witness of the missing
dependence, not a numerical dimension inference.

Thus \(V,W_1,W_2,W_3\) are four independent
highest-weight functions. By the ambient count,
they form the complete multiplicity basis at
this one degree and type.
No description of the whole stretched ambient
ray algebra is inferred from this basis.
