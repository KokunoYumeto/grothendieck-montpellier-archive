\section{A rank barrier for a diagonal binary base}

\subsection*{A-032. Binary tangent directions do not remove the pair deficits}
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(m\geq2\), \(s\geq1\), and write
\[
 A(x,y,z)=\operatorname{diag}(\ell_1(x,y),\ldots,\ell_m(x,y))+B(z),
                                                               \tag{A-032.1}
\]
where the binary linear forms \(\ell_i\) are nonzero and pairwise
nonproportional, and the entries \(B_{ij}\) are linear forms in
\(s\) suffix variables. Every coefficient of every entry is
allowed to vary in the full permanent coefficient map, including
binary offdiagonal coefficients that vanish at this chosen base.
Let \(J_A\) be its differential after retaining suffix degrees
zero, one and two.

Put
\[
 L_i=\prod_{h\ne i}\ell_h,\qquad
 L_{ij}=\prod_{h\notin\{i,j\}}\ell_h.
\]
Define the linear map on suffix forms
\[
 Q_A((C_{ij})_{i\ne j})
      =\sum_{i<j}L_{ij}(B_{ij}C_{ji}+B_{ji}C_{ij}).              \tag{A-032.2}
\]
Then the complete Jacobian, not only its suffix submatrix, has rank
\[
                 \operatorname{rank}J_A
                         =(m+1)+ms+\operatorname{rank}Q_A
                         \leq(m+1)+ms+\binom m2(2s-1).          \tag{A-032.3}
\]
The full target dimension is
\[
                  (m+1)+ms+(m-1)\binom{s+1}{2}.
\]
In particular, at the LMR-sufficient variable count
\(r=s+2=2m+1\), the rank deficit is at least \(\binom m2\).
At the maximal possible count \(r=2m+2\), it is at least
\(3\binom m2\). Therefore no diagonal binary base of the form
(A-032.1) can prove either desired dominance statement, regardless
of its suffix labels or of which tangent columns are retained.
This does not rule out a non-diagonal binary base.

\paragraph{An exact row-correction identity.}
Write \(\mathrm d\operatorname{per}_A(H)\) for the derivative
in the matrix-pencil direction \(H\). For \(i\ne j\), let
\[
                      H^{ij}=E_{ij}A-2A_{ji}E_{ii}.             \tag{A-032.4}
\]
Expansion of the permanent with row \(i\) replaced by row \(j\)
gives the polynomial identity
\[
 \mathrm d\operatorname{per}_A(H^{ij})
  =2\!\!\sum_{\substack{a<b\\a,b\ne i}}
       A_{ja}A_{jb}\,
       \operatorname{per}A_{\widehat i,\widehat j;\widehat a,\widehat b}.
                                                               \tag{A-032.5}
\]
Indeed the duplicated rows assign distinct columns \(a,b\) in
two orders. The subtraction in (A-032.4) cancels exactly the
terms for which one of those columns is \(i\), by expanding
the \(ii\)-cofactor along row \(j\).

At (A-032.1), the right side of (A-032.5) has no suffix-degree
zero or one term, and its quadratic part is
\[
                         2\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}
                                  L_{ik} B_{jk}B_{ki}.          \tag{A-032.6}
\]
To see this, a surviving term through degree two must use
columns \(j,k\). Its binary factor is \(\ell_j\); its remaining
minor contributes \(B_{ki}\prod_{h\notin\{i,j,k\}}\ell_h\).
The equality
\(\ell_j\prod_{h\notin\{i,j,k\}}\ell_h=L_{ik}\)
gives (A-032.6). Terms with neither column equal to \(j\)
already require at least four suffix entries.

\begin{proof}[The full rank identity]
Let \(W_s\) be the differential image of all suffix-only
entry variations. An offdiagonal suffix variation \(C_{ik}\)
has quadratic image \(L_{ik}B_{ki}C_{ik}\) and has zero
binary and suffix-linear images. Thus (A-032.6) belongs to
\(W_s\), by taking \(C_{ik}=2B_{jk}\).
But \(H^{ij}-\ell_jE_{ij}\) is a suffix-only pencil.
It follows that the image of the binary variation
\(\ell_jE_{ij}\) belongs to \(W_s\).
The transposed identity gives the same conclusion for
\(\ell_iE_{ij}\). Since \(\ell_i,\ell_j\) span the binary
linear forms, every binary offdiagonal variation is absorbed
by \(W_s\).

For binary diagonal variations, the binary output is the map
\[
          (\dot\ell_i)_i\longmapsto\sum_i\dot\ell_i L_i.
                                                               \tag{A-032.7}
\]
Evaluate at the root of \(\ell_i\). Pairwise nonproportionality
shows that a vector in the kernel has
\(\dot\ell_i=c_i\ell_i\) for each \(i\). Substitution in
(A-032.7) then gives \(\sum_i c_i=0\).
The kernel has dimension \(m-1\), and the map has rank \(m+1\).
Such a kernel direction also has its full truncated derivative
in \(W_s\): for \(C=\operatorname{diag}(c_i)\),
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(c_i\ell_i)=CA-CB,\qquad
        \mathrm d\operatorname{per}_A(CA)
                          =(\sum_i c_i)\operatorname{per}A=0.
 \]
The remaining term is a suffix-only direction.
Consequently the full image adds exactly \(m+1\) dimensions
to \(W_s\).

Next, suffix diagonal variations have suffix-linear output
\(\sum_i C_{ii}L_i\). The \(m\) forms \(L_i\) are linearly
independent, again by evaluation at the distinct roots.
This output map therefore has rank \(ms\) and has no kernel
on the suffix diagonal parameters. The suffix offdiagonal
variations have only the quadratic output (A-032.2).
Hence \(\dim W_s=ms+\operatorname{rank}Q_A\), proving the
equality in (A-032.3).

For each pair \(i<j\), the map
\[
             (C_{ij},C_{ji})\longmapsto
                         B_{ij}C_{ji}+B_{ji}C_{ij}
\]
has rank at most \(2s-1\). If the two \(B\)-forms are
independent, it has the nonzero scaling kernel
\((C_{ij},C_{ji})=(B_{ij},-B_{ji})\).
If they are dependent its image has dimension at most \(s\),
and if both vanish its image is zero.
Summing the pair bounds proves the inequality in (A-032.3).
Subtracting from the target dimension gives respectively
\(\binom m2\) and \(3\binom m2\) at \(s=2m-1\) and \(s=2m\).
\end{proof}

As separate finite checks, generic integer suffix labels at
\(\ell_i=x+i y\) gave ranks \(46,55\) for \(m=3\),
\(s=5,6\), and ranks \(111,127\) for \(m=4\),
\(s=7,8\). These attain the bounds in (A-032.3); the target
dimensions are respectively \(49,64,117,145\).
The general proof does not depend on those checks.
F-027 gives a distinct local obstruction requiring only
one isolated binary vertex at either of these variable counts.

\paragraph{Geometric scope.}
This is a statement about the tangent map at the specified
pencils. It does not imply a rank defect at generic non-diagonal
pencils, a polynomial identity on the full permanent orbit
closure, or a Schur multiplicity comparison.
The proof uses entrywise polynomial derivatives and an explicit
quotient by the image of suffix directions. Equivalently it
uses the matrix pencil over the dual numbers and retains its
suffix-order-two coordinate jet. It isolates a failed
construction strategy while leaving the general gate QA033 open.
The polynomial coordinate maps and their torus weights use
the comodule dictionary of SGA 3, I, Proposition 4.7.2,
pp.~34--35, and Proposition 4.7.3, pp.~35--36.
The dual-number derivative and the transverse jet quotient
are actual maps of these coefficient algebras. If one
subsequently passes to the characteristic-zero Schur
decomposition, the exact functorial references are
SGA 6, VI, Theorem 3.3, pp.~392--393, and Macdonald,
I, Appendix A(5.3)--(5.6), pp.~155--156.
No such irreducible multiplicity conclusion follows
from the single-base rank defect proved here.
