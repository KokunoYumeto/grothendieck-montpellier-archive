\section{Exact Hessian shifts of every stable two-row core}

\subsection*{O-030. A three-parameter family with exact determinant multiplicities}
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(n\geq3\), and put
\[
 R=n^2,\qquad q=R(n-2),\qquad
 \eta=(q+2,2^{R-1}),\qquad
 h_n(k)=[z^k]\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1-z^i)^{-1}.
\]
For integers \(u\geq0\) and \(a\geq k\geq0\), set
\[
 \begin{split}
 d&=a+uR,\\
 \Lambda(u,a,k)
   &=\bigl(na+u(q+2)-k,\ 2u+k,\ (2u)^{R-2}\bigr).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{O-030.1}
\]
Trailing zero parts are omitted; when \(u=a=k=0\), this
is the empty partition and the constant representation.
Then
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 K_n(\Lambda(u,a,k))
  &=\operatorname{sk}\bigl(\Lambda(u,a,k),(d^n)\bigr)\\
  &=g\bigl(\Lambda(u,a,k),(d^n),(d^n)\bigr)=h_n(k).
 \end{aligned}}                                                \tag{O-030.2}
\]
Thus this entire flat-tail family has an explicit positive
partition formula for the actual determinant-closure
multiplicity, including every copy.

For the unpadded permanent in the same \(R\) variables,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 q^n_{\Lambda(u,a,k)}(d[n])
       \ \geq\ \sum_{j=0}^{\min(u,k)}h_n(k-j).}                 \tag{O-030.3}
\]
In particular the lower bound is strictly larger than
(O-030.2) in each of the following ranges:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{binary tail }k&\text{Hessian power }u\\ \hline
 1&u\geq1\\
 2&u\geq2\\
 k\geq3&u\geq1 .
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{O-030.4}
\]
These are sufficient ranges for a strict gap; no assertion
of equality on the permanent side outside these ranges
is implicit. If \(u=0\), the common two-row formula does
give \(q^n_{\Lambda}=K_n(\Lambda)=h_n(k)\).

\paragraph{Partition, degree, and endpoint checks.}
The difference between the first and second parts in
(O-030.1) is
\[
                 na+uq-2k\geq(n-2)k+uq\geq0.
\]
The remaining parts are weakly decreasing. Its size is
\[
 na+u(q+2)+2u(R-1)=na+unR=nd.
\]
For \(k=0\), the determinant value is \(h_n(0)=1\).
If \(a=0\), then \(k=0\), and the highest-weight function
used below is simply a power of the Hessian section.
The case \(u=0\) is the stable two-row formula, and the
case \(d=0\) is the constant function. These conventions
also remove any need to interpret zero-length rectangles
as nontrivial symmetric-group representations.

\paragraph{The nonzero Hessian section and its weight.}
For a degree-\(n\) form \(F\) in \(R\) variables, let
\[
 H(F)=\det\operatorname{Hess}_{R}(F),
 \qquad H_0(F)=H(F)(e_1).
\]
The function \(H_0\) has coefficient degree \(R\).
The chain rule gives
\[
 H(F\circ g)=\det(g)^2 H(F)\circ g.
\]
For an upper triangular \(g\) with \(ge_1=t_1e_1\), this
makes \(H_0\) a highest-weight function of character
\[
              \det(g)^2t_1^q,
\]
which is the partition \(\eta\).

This section is nonzero on the determinant orbit closure.
For example, at the identity matrix the Hessian of the
determinant is the bilinear form
\[
                  (A,B)\longmapsto
                       \operatorname{tr}(A)\operatorname{tr}(B)
                                      -\operatorname{tr}(AB).
\]
It is nondegenerate for \(n\geq2\): on scalar matrices
its value is a nonzero scalar multiple of the product
of their scalar coefficients, and on trace-zero matrices
it is the negative trace pairing. An invertible change
of variables can take the first coordinate vector to
the identity matrix, so \(H_0\) is nonzero as a function
on the determinant orbit. This is also the full-variable
case of O-011.

\paragraph{The lower bound on every determinant copy.}
Let
\[
                         \beta=(na-k,k).
\]
The hypothesis \(a\geq k\) makes the width constraint
inactive in the two-row formula of F-002. It gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 K_n(\beta)
 &=a_\beta(a[n])
   =g\bigl(\beta,(a^n),(a^n)\bigr)\\
 &=[z^k](1-z)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z^i)^{-1}
   =h_n(k). 
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{O-030.5}
\]
Here \(K_n=a_\beta\) is the all-copy binary restriction
result O-006: every binary degree-\(n\) form is a
diagonal determinant, and a nonzero complete Schur
multiplicity subspace cannot vanish on all binary forms.

The coordinate ring of the determinant orbit closure
is a domain, since this closure is irreducible.
Multiplication by the actual nonzero function \(H_0^u\)
is therefore injective on the entire highest-weight space
in (O-030.5). The product has coefficient degree
\(a+uR=d\) and highest weight
\[
                           \beta+u\eta=\Lambda(u,a,k).
\]
Consequently \(K_n(\Lambda(u,a,k))\geq h_n(k)\).
No choice of a single nonzero copy substitutes for this
injection of the full multiplicity space.

\paragraph{The matching ordinary Kronecker upper bound.}
Remove \(2u\) from all \(R=n^2\) parts in (O-030.1).
The remaining partition and rectangle width are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sigma&=(na+uq-k,k),\\
 b&=d-2un=a+un(n-2).
 \end{split}
\]
They satisfy \(|\sigma|=nb\) and \(b\geq a\geq k\).
The determinant-twist identity
\[
 S_{\sigma+(2u)^R}(\mathbb C^n\otimes\mathbb C^n)
   \simeq
 (\det_{\mathbb C^n})^{2un}\boxtimes
 (\det_{\mathbb C^n})^{2un}
        \otimes S_\sigma(\mathbb C^n\otimes\mathbb C^n)
\]
gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 g\bigl(\Lambda(u,a,k),(d^n),(d^n)\bigr)
   &=g\bigl(\sigma,(b^n),(b^n)\bigr)\\
   &=h_n(k).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{O-030.6}
\]
The second equality is the same two-row partition
difference as in (O-030.5), now with width \(b\).
The general coordinate-ring inequalities
\(K_n\leq\operatorname{sk}\leq g\), together with the
preceding lower bound, prove every equality in
(O-030.2). In particular no separate transpose-sign
calculation is needed for the symmetric coefficient.

\paragraph{A full-space permanent construction.}
Use the normalized binary coefficients and polynomials
\(F_0,I_2,\ldots,I_n,Z\) of A-034. Their coefficient
degrees and highest weights are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{function}&\text{coefficient degree}&\text{highest weight}\\ \hline
 F_0&1&(n)\\
 I_i&i&(i(n-1),i)\\
 H_0&R&\eta\\
 Z&R+1&\eta+(n-1,1).
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{O-030.7}
\]
For completeness, if
\(F(xe_1+ye_2)=\sum_i\binom niF_i x^{n-i}y^i\), then
\[
 I_i=(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)F_1^i+
       \sum_{\ell=2}^{i}(-1)^{i-\ell}\binom i\ell
                  F_0^{\ell-1}F_1^{i-\ell}F_\ell.
\]
Writing
\(H(F)(xe_1+ye_2)=\sum_i\binom qiH_i x^{q-i}y^i\),
one has \(Z=F_0H_1-F_1H_0\).
These are actual polynomial functions; the displayed
normalizations only introduce nonzero rational scalars.

For \(0\leq j\leq\min(u,k)\), let \(T\) range over the
monomials in \(I_2,\ldots,I_n\) of weighted degree \(k-j\),
where \(I_i\) has weight \(i\). Consider all functions
\[
        Z^j H_0^{\,u-j}F_0^{\,a-k}T(I_2,\ldots,I_n).
                                                               \tag{O-030.8}
\]
Their coefficient degree is
\[
 j(R+1)+(u-j)R+(a-k)+(k-j)=a+uR=d.
\]
Their highest weight is, independently of \(j\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 j\bigl(\eta+(n-1,1)\bigr)
  +(u-j)\eta+n(a-k)e_1
  +\bigl((n-1)(k-j),k-j\bigr)
   =u\eta+(na-k,k).
 \end{split}
\]
Thus every function in (O-030.8) has the required weight
\(\Lambda(u,a,k)\).

A-034 supplies a prime-divisor test that proves
independence of this entire collection in the actual
permanent coordinate ring. Namely, set \(P=\operatorname{per}_n\)
and take \(A_*\) to be all ones except for its \(11\) entry
\(3-n\). Then
\[
                P(A_*)=2(n-1)!\ne0,\qquad H(P)(A_*)=0.
\]
There is an irreducible factor \(h(A)\) of \(H(P)(A)\),
of multiplicity \(e\geq1\), with \(h(A_*)=0\). For the
prime valuation on \(\mathbb C[A,B]\), the complete
fixed-\(A_*\) binary coefficient argument in A-034 proves
\[
 \operatorname{ord}_h H_0=e,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_h Z=e-1,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_h F_0=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_h T(I_2,\ldots,I_n)=0
                                    \quad(T\ne0).             \tag{O-030.9}
\]
The last assertion holds for every nonzero polynomial
\(T\), not only for each individual monomial.
Indeed the \(I_i\) are algebraically independent after
specializing \(A=A_*\); divisibility by \(h\) would make
that entire specialization zero.

Suppose a linear combination of the functions in
(O-030.8) vanished on the permanent orbit closure.
Group its terms by \(j\), obtaining nonzero polynomials
\(T_j\) of weighted degree \(k-j\) for the nonempty
blocks. Pullback to binary frames is a polynomial
identity in \(A,B\): independent frames extend to
\(\operatorname{SL}_R\), as proved in A-034.
By (O-030.9), the nonzero \(j\)-th block has valuation
\[
                         j(e-1)+(u-j)e=ue-j.
\]
These valuations are distinct. The block with largest
\(j\) has uniquely smallest valuation and cannot cancel.
This contradiction proves that all functions (O-030.8)
are independent. There are \(h_n(k-j)\) of them in the
\(j\)-th block, proving (O-030.3).

When \(u=0\), the basis \(F_0^{a-k}T(I)\) is the full
stable binary highest-weight space: it is independent
and has dimension \(h_n(k)\), which equals the ambient
multiplicity in (O-030.5). All binary forms are also
diagonal permanents, so equality holds on the permanent
side in this endpoint.

\paragraph{Strictness and an expanded family of saturation holes.}
For \(n\geq3\), \(h_n(0)=1\), \(h_n(1)=0\), and
\(h_n(t)>0\) for every \(t\geq2\), because every such
integer is a nonnegative combination of \(2\) and \(3\).
The positive additional term is \(j=1\) when \(k=1\)
or \(k\geq3\), and \(j=2\) when \(k=2\).
This proves the sufficient ranges (O-030.4).
If \(u\geq k\), the lower bound becomes
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{k}h_n(k-j)
     =[z^k]\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z^i)^{-1}=p_n(k),
                                                               \tag{O-030.10}
\]
so the gap is at least \(p_n(k-1)>0\) whenever \(k\geq1\).

In particular fix any \(u,a\geq1\) and put
\(\mu=\Lambda(u,a,1)\). For every integer \(s\geq1\),
\[
                 s\mu=\Lambda(su,sa,s),
\]
and \(su\geq s\). Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 q^n_{s\mu}\bigl(s(a+uR)[n]\bigr)
       \geq p_n(s)>h_n(s)=K_n(s\mu).}                          \tag{O-030.11}
\]
At \(s=1\), \(K_n(\mu)=0\), while \(2\mu\) and \(3\mu\)
both occur on the determinant closure. Therefore
\(\mu=3\mu-2\mu\) belongs to the group generated by its
actual support, and \(\mu\) is a genuine relative-saturation
hole. At every \(s\geq2\) the two compared actual
multiplicities are positive. The special case \(u=a=1\)
is A-034's ray. These are exact conclusions at all
integer dilations, without an invariant-period threshold.

\paragraph{Geometric dictionary, provenance, and limitations.}
The proof multiplies actual regular highest-weight
functions in the coordinate rings of irreducible orbit
closures. Its injectivity is the domain property, and
its exact upper bound is the determinant-line tensor
identity, followed by the established stabilizer bound.
The coordinate comodule and torus-weight constructions
are SGA 3, I, Propositions 4.7.2--4.7.3, pp.~34--36.
The Schur and determinant operations belong to the
special \(\lambda\)-structure of SGA 6, VI, Theorem 3.3,
pp.~392--393; their characteristic-zero functorial
decomposition is Macdonald, I, Appendix A(5.3)--(5.6),
pp.~155--156. The complete binary restriction argument
is O-006, and the exact two-row partition difference is
F-002. A-034 proves the polynomial centered invariants,
the fixed-Hessian-zero binary dominance, and the
prime-divisor valuation identities used here.

The Hessian and determinant-line identities admit
integral polynomial models. The normalized binary
coefficients, characteristic-zero irreducible
decompositions, and the derivative and valuation
arguments in (O-030.9) are used over \(\mathbb Q\) and
\(\mathbb C\); no assertion about every special fiber
or exact modular multiplicity follows. There is no
inference from virtual positivity, normality alone,
or support saturation to the actual multiplicities.

For \(u>0\), the partition has length \(n^2\).
An internally padded permanent of size \(m<n\) has
only \(m^2\) essential variables and hence multiplicity
zero at this weight. The strict comparisons above
are unpadded \(n=m\) statements and do not resolve
the IP/BIP large-padding comparison. No historical
priority claim is made.
