\section*{Structured-residue boundary reduction: proved local identities and remaining gap}
This note uses the exact residue tensor defined in
\path{agents/jet_review/structured_residue_tensor.py}.
It does not prove the required general-size Jacobian rank.

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_{m-1}\) be distinct nonzero scalars and
let \(a_m=\varepsilon\). Write \(K_{ij}\) for the first-jet
kernel vector
\[
 K_{ij}=E_{ij}
       +\frac{a_i}{a_j-a_i}E_{ii}
       +\frac{a_j}{a_i-a_j}E_{jj}\qquad(i\ne j).
\]
The residue bilinear form \(S_m\) is obtained by restricting
the raw second-permanent-cofactor tensor
\[
 R_m(E_{ij},E_{kl})=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&i=k\text{ or }j=l\text{ or }m\notin U,\\
 \displaystyle\frac{(-\varepsilon)^{|U|-2}}
                   {\prod_{v\in U-\{m\}}(a_v-\varepsilon)},
       &\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad U=\{i,j,k,l\}.
\]
The coordinates of \(K\) are its free off-diagonal entries.
Separate \(K=K_{\rm old}\oplus K_{\rm star}\), where the
star consists of the \(2(m-1)\) edges incident with \(m\).

\paragraph{Boundary blocks.}
For every \(\varepsilon\),
\[
                  S_m|_{K_{\rm old}\times K_{\rm old}}=0.
\]
This follows directly because both kernel basis vectors
are supported on old matrix entries. At \(\varepsilon=0\)
the star block is the nondegenerate hyperbolic form
\[
 J(X,Y;X',Y')=
     \sum_{i<m}\frac{X_iY_i'+Y_iX_i'}{a_i},
 \quad X_i=b_{im},\quad Y_i=b_{mi}.
\]
All old--star terms vanish at \(\varepsilon=0\).

For an old edge \(a\to b\), put \(A=a_a,B=a_b\) and
\(D=(A-\varepsilon)(B-\varepsilon)\). Direct substitution
of its three nonzero kernel coordinates gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 S_m(K_{ab},K_{am})&=\frac{B\varepsilon}{(B-A)D},&
 S_m(K_{ab},K_{ma})&=\frac{A\varepsilon}{(B-A)D},\\
 S_m(K_{ab},K_{bm})&=-\frac{B\varepsilon}{(B-A)D},&
 S_m(K_{ab},K_{mb})&=-\frac{A\varepsilon}{(B-A)D}.
 \end{split}
\]
Every other old--star entry is zero. For example the
three surviving terms for \(K_{ab},K_{am}\) reduce to
\[
 \frac{A\varepsilon}{(B-A)(A-\varepsilon)^2}
       -\frac{\varepsilon^2}{(A-\varepsilon)^2(B-\varepsilon)}
 =\frac{B\varepsilon}{(B-A)(A-\varepsilon)(B-\varepsilon)}.
\]
The other identities follow in the same way, or from
transposition and interchange of \(a,b\). A fresh
symbolic three-index expansion also checks all four.

Consequently the derivative cross block \(C=S_m'(0)\)
acts on an old free-edge vector \(b\) by the two
linear forms at each old vertex
\[
 L_i(b)=\sum_{j\ne i}
        \frac{b_{ij}/a_i+b_{ji}/a_j}{a_j-a_i},
 \qquad
 M_i(b)=\sum_{j\ne i}
        \frac{b_{ij}/a_j+b_{ji}/a_i}{a_j-a_i}.
\]
Both \(\sum_iL_i\) and \(\sum_iM_i\) vanish.
The star Schur complement therefore has leading
quadratic term
\[
        -2\varepsilon^2\sum_{i<m}a_iL_i(b)M_i(b).
\]
This is an exact formal-power-series statement about
the bilinear-form block, since \(J^{-1}\) exchanges
the two star channels with factors \(a_i\).

\paragraph{The universal boundary cokernel and the second-order test.}
Use the displayed two-channel embedding from the code,
with upper edge label \(u_i+2u_j\), lower edge label
\(v_i+2v_j\), and \(v_m=0\). The vector
\[
                 w=(-2,\ldots,-2,1;\,0,\ldots,0)
\]
is killed by the star projection of the embedding \(B\).
Hence \(S_mB(w)=0\) at \(\varepsilon=0\).
In the dual of the complete quadratic Jacobian, the
tuple with \(Q_m=w^2\) and all other \(Q_i=0\) is a
cokernel vector, modulo the common tuple arising from
\(\sum_iS_i=0\). This proves at least one-dimensional
cokernel for every \(m\).

The following second-order computation is conditional
on a chosen boundary right-kernel vector \(\dot B\).
Because the star projection \(B_{\rm star}\) is
surjective with kernel \(\mathbb Cw\), the equation
\(d(S_m\circ B)=0\) forces
\[
 \dot B_{\rm star}=A B_{\rm star},\qquad
                         A^TJ+JA=0.
\]
In particular \(\dot B_{\rm star}(w)=0\). Set
\(P=B_{\rm old}(w)\) and \(\dot P=\dot B_{\rm old}(w)\).
The first-order missing left/right pairing is zero,
because the old--old residue block vanishes identically
for every \(\varepsilon\).

If one corrects \(\dot B\) to first order so that all
mixed \((w,z)\) target coefficients remain zero, the
star correction at \(w\) satisfies
\[
 \dot B_{\rm star}^{(1)}(w)
       =-J^{-1}\bigl(C^T\dot P+A^TC^TP\bigr).
\]
This follows by applying the first derivative of
\(S_m(Bw,\dot Bz)+S_m(Bz,\dot Bw)=0\) to every \(z\).
In the missing \((w,w)\) coefficient, the \(A\)-term
contributes zero, since
\(J^{-1}A^T\) is skew-symmetric. The second-order
pairing is thus
\[
 -2\sum_{i<m}a_i
       \bigl(L_i(P)M_i(\dot P)+M_i(P)L_i(\dot P)\bigr).
\]
The computation is valid for any first-order correction
satisfying the mixed equations. To use it as a complete
Jacobian nonvanishing proof, one still needs the
boundary cokernel to be exactly one-dimensional and
the displayed pairing to be nonzero for a boundary
right kernel.

\paragraph{Exact finite checks and the unresolved step.}
The separate rational computation
\path{inspect_structured_boundary_kernel.py} gives a
one-dimensional right kernel at \(m=3,4\), for
\(a_i=i\) on old vertices. With its saved primitive
integer normalization, the second-order pairings are
\[
             -432\quad(m=3),\qquad
             -13657995712277177364\quad(m=4).
\]
The saved JSON contains every coordinate of both
rational kernel vectors, not only ranks.
Other workers' modular tests give boundary corank one
through \(m=6\) and full rank at generic nonzero
parameters in those sizes.
These finite results do not prove the corresponding
claim for arbitrary \(m\). A uniform uniqueness proof
for the boundary cokernel and a uniform nonvanishing
argument for the second-order pairing remain open.
