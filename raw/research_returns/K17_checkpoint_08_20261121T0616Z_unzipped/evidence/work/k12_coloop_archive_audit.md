## 1. The join operator

**Convention clarification after the first version.** The revised source explicitly uses DEKM's cone-span orientation, obtained by appending the new quadratic ray to the old span basis. Under that convention the incidence along a joined facet is the negative of the original incidence: the old missing ray must cross the new ray. Hence its operator satisfies $\partial J=-J\partial$. Replacing it in degree $d$ by $\widetilde J_d=(-1)^dJ_d$ gives the commuting right-cone convention used below. This revision was read in full and checked; it changes no boundary or homology conclusion. In the DEKM convention a filling $\partial b=\tau$ gives $\partial(-Jb)=J\tau$. The original reviewed hash above refers to the first convention, not the revised wording.

For a full-rank minimum cone, the orthogonal extension of its witness form by a unit coordinate has precisely the old minimum pairs and the new coordinate pair. Mixed nonzero components have norm at least two. Thus the proposed join is an actual cell and has cellular degree one greater.

On oriented cellular chains, append the new vertex to an oriented triangulation. The ordinary cone formula is

$$
\partial J(c)=J(\partial c)+(-1)^{d+1}c
$$

for a degree-$d$ cell. The last term is rank deficient in the larger ambient lattice, and any formerly rank-deficient facet stays rank deficient after the join. Consequently the relative chain operators satisfy $\partial J=J\partial$. This sign convention is equivalently a map from the conventional suspended chain complex after multiplying degree $d$ by $(-1)^d$.

The map is equivariant for $g\mapsto g\perp1$, so it passes to the actual oriented chain coinvariants. This construction precedes orbit representatives; therefore identifications of facets cause their signed incidences to be summed, not divided or multiplied by a target stabilizer index. An odd source stabilizer extends to an odd target stabilizer. These statements also cover nonsimplicial cells by subdivision.

Two successive extensions admit the integral swap of the new axes. On the cone's quadratic span this fixes the original subspace and transposes the two added one-dimensional summands. Thus $J^2=-J^2$, and $J^2=0$ when 2 is invertible. This is not a contracting homotopy. In particular the author correctly retains the possibility that a lifted boundary lies in $\ker J$.

For a rational coloop whose complement splits integrally, restriction of the witness form to the complement and orthogonal addition of a unit form produce the same full minimum set. If all minima generate the lattice, collecting their coefficients gives precisely this integral splitting. Without that generation assumption a rational splitting need not be integral.

## 2. Excess five

A minimal dependence of $k$ distinct rank-one rays, spanning vector space $W$ of dimension $m$, has quadratic rank $k-1$. In its signed relation both positive-semidefinite sums have the same kernel. Each side therefore spans $W$, and each has at least $m$ vectors; hence $k\ge2m$.

The full set of minima lying in $W$ is the complete minimum set of the restricted lattice, and is exposed by a positive-semidefinite functional with kernel $W$. It is a genuine nonsimplicial face of quadratic rank $r_W\ge k-1$. The selected circuit rays stay extremal because distinct rank-one positive-semidefinite rays are extremal in any cone generated by such rays.

Choose $N-m$ minima independent modulo $W$. Their projected rank-one forms are independent, giving

$$
N+5=r\ge r_W+N-m,\qquad r_W\ge2m-1.
$$

Thus $m\le6$ and $r_W\le m+5\le11$. The complete primary statement and printed proof of Dutour Sikirić–Hulek–Schürmann, Theorem 1.1(ii), were freshly read (printed pages 644 and 646). It says that the sole nonsimplicial cone of dimension at most ten is the $D_4$ cone, of vector rank four and quadratic dimension ten. That case would force $10\le4+5$, impossible here. Therefore $r_W=11$, $m=6$, and $k=12$. The use of the source's external classification is explicit; that enumeration was not rerun.

There is equality in the dimension bound. Choose minima $v_1,\ldots,v_{N-6}$ independent modulo $W$. Their quadratic classes are a basis of the quotient of the full quadratic span by its $W$-part. For any further minimum $v$ outside $W$, compare the $U\times U$ block in a splitting $W\oplus U$: its rank-one matrix must be diagonal in that basis, so only one $U$ coordinate is nonzero. Comparing the $W\times U$ block then forces $v=a v_j$. Equal minimum norm gives $a=\pm1$. Thus the stated outside pairs are all of them, and each is a rational coloop.

If all minima generate $L$, the linear independence modulo $W$ shows

$$
L=(L\cap W)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N-6}\mathbb Z v_i.
$$

For $N\ge8$, swapping two of these summands is integral, fixes the entire eleven-dimensional core quadratic span, and transposes two outside quadratic rays. It reverses orientation. The associated oriented coinvariant therefore vanishes after inverting two.
