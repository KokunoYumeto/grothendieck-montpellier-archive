## Hilbert p-class persistence

The proof in the fragment is valid. For a cyclic degree-p extension L/K ramified at one finite prime, totally ramified there, let E be the Hilbert p-class field of L. Since the maximal unramified abelian p-extension is characteristic under automorphisms of L, E/K is Galois. Its group G is a finite p-group. Inertia at the unique ramified base prime has order p because E/L is unramified.

Let N be the normal closure of that inertia subgroup. The quotient extension E^N/K is everywhere unramified: all inertia at the given prime is conjugate into N, and no other prime can ramify. A nontrivial finite p-group quotient G/N would have a quotient of order p, producing an unramified cyclic p-extension of K, contrary to the p-class vanishing. Thus N=G.

The inertia group is cyclic of order p, so G is normally generated by one element. Its elementary abelian Frattini quotient is generated by one element, and the finite p-group generator theorem makes G cyclic. Then the inertia subgroup is itself normal, hence N=I=G. Its order p forces E=L, proving the p-class vanishing at L. Odd degree prevents ramification at real places.
