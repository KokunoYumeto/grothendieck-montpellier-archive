## Theorem and exact scope

Let L be a rank-eight Euclidean lattice whose minimum vectors span L over
Q. Suppose that the cone generated by all its minimum quadratic rays has
dimension thirteen and is nonsimplicial. Then the cone has an integral
orientation-reversing stabilizer. Consequently every such projective
degree-twelve cell is zero in oriented Voronoi coinvariants over any
coefficient ring in which two is invertible.

There is **no assumption that the minimum vectors generate L over Z**.
Thus, in the outstanding rank-eight degree-twelve chain group relevant to
H_5(GL_8(Z);St_8), only simplicial cells can contribute. This theorem does
not eliminate those simplicial cells, compute their boundaries, or assert
the vanishing of that homology group or of K_12(Z).

The proof uses the complete excess-five argument already recorded in
`k12_coloop_rank8_reduction.md`, together with the short-perfection-relation
classification of Berge--Martinet and the minimum-basis index bounds of
Martinet. We give the structural argument again to fix every hypothesis.

## 1. A six-dimensional circuit and two rational coloops

Normalize the minimum of L to one. Choose a minimal linear dependence of
k distinct minimum quadratic rays, and let W be the span of the underlying
vectors, of dimension m. The circuit has quadratic rank k-1. Split its
relation into its strictly positive and strictly negative parts. Equality
of the two positive semidefinite sums implies equality of their kernels;
therefore each sign support spans W. Each has at least m vectors, so
k>=2m.

Let sigma_W be the cone of every minimum ray lying in W and let r_W be its
dimension. It is the actual perfect cone of the lattice L intersect W:
the restricted form has minimum one, its full minimum set is precisely
the old minimum set in W, and the circuit spans W. It is a face of the
ambient cone, exposed by a positive semidefinite functional with kernel
W. Distinct rank-one positive semidefinite rays are extremal in any cone
generated by such rays; the circuit thus makes sigma_W nonsimplicial.

Choose 8-m minimum vectors independent modulo W. Projecting their
quadratic rays to Sym^2(L_Q/W) proves

    13 >= r_W+8-m,       r_W >= k-1 >= 2m-1.

Thus m<=6 and r_W<=m+5<=11. Dutour Sikiric--Hulek--Schuermann,
Theorem 1.1(ii), says that the only nonsimplicial perfect cone of dimension
at most ten is D_4. That cone has vector rank four and quadratic rank ten,
incompatible with r_W<=m+5. Hence m=6, r_W=11, and k=12. The two sign
supports of the circuit each contain exactly six independent vectors.

Choose minimum vectors v,w independent modulo W. The two classes v^2,w^2
are a basis of the quotient of the thirteen-dimensional quadratic span
by the eleven-dimensional core span. In a splitting L_Q=W direct_sum U
using the images of v,w as a basis of U, write another outside minimum x
with U-coordinates (a,b). Comparing its U-by-U quadratic block with a
linear combination of v^2,w^2 forces ab=0. If a is nonzero, comparison of
the W-by-U block then gives x=a v; if b is nonzero, it gives x=b w. Equal
minimum norms force a or b to be plus or minus one. Thus v and w are the
only outside minimum pairs, and both are rational coloops.

## 2. The core is Watson's twelve-ray configuration

Let M_0 be the sublattice generated by the twelve circuit vectors. Its
minimum is one, because it is contained in L and contains those minimum
vectors. The two six-element sign supports are independent systems of
minimum vectors; their relation has strictly positive coefficients and
their union generates M_0. A decomposition into two perfection relations
would contradict the minimality of the circuit, so the relation is
perf-irreducible in Berge--Martinet's sense (Definition 2.4 and section 3).

Apply Berge--Martinet, Corollary 6.9. In rank six, the possibilities are
D_6 or the Watson configuration of their Theorem 5.1. The D_6 alternative
is impossible: all thirty minimum pairs of M_0 would also be minimum
pairs of L. Their quadratic rays span Sym^2(W), of dimension twenty-one,
contradicting r_W=11. For completeness, in the standard D_6 realization
the roots e_i+e_j and e_i-e_j span all off-diagonal quadratic coordinates
by subtraction and all diagonal coordinates by their sums.

After relabeling and changing signs, there are independent minima
f_1,...,f_6 such that

    e=(f_1+...+f_6)/3,
    M_0 = sum_i Z f_i + Z e,
    circuit pairs = {f_i, e-f_i : 1<=i<=6}.

The frame lattice F_0=sum Z f_i has index three in M_0. Let M=L intersect W.
Martinet's Theorem 5.2 bounds the index in a six-dimensional lattice of
any independent minimum frame by four. Hence

    [M:F_0] = 3 [M:M_0] <= 4,

and M=M_0. No saturation of the circuit lattice was assumed in applying
the short-relation classification; it is proved by this index argument.

## 3. There are no additional core rays

Here is a direct linear-algebra check that no extra minimum pairs can
occur in the core; it avoids any additional finite cone enumeration.
Use f_1,...,f_6 as rational coordinates. Let S be the span of f_i f_i^t and
(e-f_i)(e-f_i)^t. The circuit shows dim S=11, so S is already the entire
quadratic span of sigma_W.

If a nonzero vector x has x x^t in S, its off-diagonal coordinates have
the form

    x_j x_k = c-d_j-d_k  (j!=k),       c=(sum_i d_i)/3.       (R8.1)

Indeed, if the coefficients of (e-f_i)^2 are b_i, put d_i=b_i/3; their
off-diagonal contribution is (sum b_i)/9-(b_j+b_k)/3. The f_i^2 contribute
only to the diagonal.

For four distinct indices j,k,l,m, alternating (R8.1) gives

    (x_j-x_m)(x_k-x_l)=0.

If x_a differs from x_b, all four coordinates outside {a,b} must coincide.
If both x_a and x_b differed from that common value, using two of those
outside coordinates in the displayed identity would give a contradiction.
Thus all but at most one coordinate of x are equal. Write x=a*1+b*f_i.
Equation (R8.1) forces d_j=d for j!=i and d_i=d-a b. Consequently
c=2d-a b/3 and

    a^2=c-2d=-a b/3.

Either a=0, giving the line of f_i, or b=-3a, giving the line of e-f_i.
The all-equal case b=0 yields only x=0. Thus the only nonzero rank-one
lines in S are the twelve stated lines. Since all minimum vectors have
the same norm, the complete core minimum set is exactly their signed
representatives.

## 4. Explicit core automorphisms and their orientations

Any permutation pi of the six f_i fixes e, preserves M and permutes the
twelve minimum pairs. The two six-element sets are permuted by the same
pi. On the twelve-dimensional free ray space the determinant is therefore
sgn(pi)^2=1. The unique relation

    sum_i f_i^2 - sum_i (e-f_i)^2 = 0                     (R8.2)

is fixed. The determinant on its eleven-dimensional quotient S is +1.

There is also the involution F defined by F(f_i)=e-f_i. One has F(e)=e,
so F preserves M and F^2=1. It exchanges the two six-element ray systems.
The resulting six transpositions have determinant +1 on the free ray
space, while F negates (R8.2). Therefore F has determinant -1 on S and
reverses the core orientation. Every pi F does so as well.

The outside quadratic rays are independent modulo S. Extending a core
automorphism by fixing v,w keeps its orientation sign. Swapping v and w
while fixing M has orientation sign -1 whenever it preserves L. These
are statements about the cone automorphisms; no assumption that the
original witnessing form is an orthogonal sum is required.

## 5. Ambient glue has order at most two

The lattice generated by all the minima is

    L_min = M direct_sum Z v direct_sum Z w.

Let g=[L:L_min]. The independent minimum frame f_1,...,f_6,v,w has index
3g in L. Martinet's introductory theorem for dimension eight bounds any
such index by eight unless L is similar to E_8. The E_8 possibility is
excluded because its complete minimum quadratic span has dimension
thirty-six, whereas our cone has dimension thirteen. Hence

    3g <= 8,       so g=1 or 2.

The cited index bound is not being inferred from incomplete low-rank
Voronoi data. It is the minimum-basis classification theorem in the
original index paper. Martinet's 2014 appendix supplies the previously
omitted bound <=9 outside E_8; original Theorem 10.1 says index nine occurs
only for E_8. Together these also recover the stated <=8 bound. See the
exact source-reading scope in section 7 below.

If g=1, the involution F extended by the identity on v,w is the required
integral odd stabilizer. It remains to consider g=2.

## 6. Every admissible binary glue has an odd stabilizer

Write the index-two extension as

    L = L_min + Z*(u+a v+b w)/2,
    u in M,       a,b in {0,1}.

The two outside bits cannot both vanish: otherwise the new element would
belong to L intersect W=M, contrary to index two. If a=b=1, interchange
v and w and fix M. This preserves the glue generator modulo L_min and
reverses orientation.

It remains to suppose exactly one outside bit is one, say a=1,b=0. Since
[M:F_0]=3 is odd, the six f_i form a basis of M/2M. Represent u by a binary
word in that basis and let k be its Hamming weight. Modulo 2M,

    e = sum_i f_i,
    F(u)=u+(sum of the six bits)*sum_i f_i.

For even k, F fixes the glue class; its extension is an odd stabilizer.
For k=3, F complements the six bits, and a permutation pi of the f_i can
send the complementary three-element support to the original support.
Then pi F fixes the glue class, with orientation sign -1.

The remaining possibilities k=1 and k=5 cannot occur for a lattice of
minimum one. A weight-one class is represented by a minimum vector f_i;
a weight-five class is represented by e-f_i, since e equals sum f_i
modulo 2M. Thus in either case u is congruent to some core minimum m
modulo 2M. The extension contains both (m+v)/2 and (m-v)/2. They are nonzero,
because v does not lie in W, and the parallelogram identity gives

    Q[(m+v)/2] + Q[(m-v)/2] = (Q[m]+Q[v])/2 = 1.

Both terms would have to be at least one, a contradiction. The case
a=0,b=1 is identical. This exhausts every possible index-two extension
and completes the proof.

## 8. Exact corroborating certificate and limitations

`k12_rank8_watson_certificate.py` checks the twelve-ray Watson model in
integral coordinates, its frame index three, rank eleven and unique
relation, the integral involution, and all 192 binary glue classes with
nonzero outside bit pair. It labels the 24 impossible one-coloop classes
by a minimum representative and supplies an orientation-reversing map
for each of the other 168 classes. The symbolic no-extra-rank-one proof
and the lattice-index bounds remain part of the written argument, not
conclusions inferred from sampling.

As separate corroboration, the official EVGS rank-six codimension-ten
file has 329 records: 328 have eleven minimum pairs, and exactly one has
twelve, the final record `[2082963,4]`. The signed minima of P_6^4 give
exactly the Watson model used here. The archived checksum and all 329
bit counts are checked by the script when the archived file is present.
This does not rerun EVGS coverage, and the theorem above does not require
that finite database: its core classification comes from the stated
Berge--Martinet theorem and the direct rank-one calculation.

No full rank-eight cone enumeration, incoming boundary computation,
homology vanishing or K_12 vanishing is asserted.
