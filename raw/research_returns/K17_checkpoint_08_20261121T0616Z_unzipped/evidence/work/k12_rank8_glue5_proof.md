# K17-K12-R8-GLUE5: proper index-five glue is impossible

## Proposition

Let L be a rank-eight lattice with a simplicial complete minimum cone
of dimension thirteen. The sublattice L_0 generated by its minima cannot
have index five in L.

## Proof

The audited index-five refinement gives the following facts. There are
exactly thirteen minimum pairs. Every independent eight-element minimum
frame has index at most five in L. If its index is five, its cyclic
quotient can be generated, after signs and permutation of the frame,
by the numerator `(1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2)` divided by five.

Suppose [L:L_0]=5. Every minimum-frame index in L is a positive multiple
of five and at most five, so every such frame is a Z-basis of L_0. Fix
one frame and use it as coordinates, and let V be the 8-by-13 matrix of
the minimum-pair representatives. It contains the identity matrix and
all its full-rank minors are zero or plus/minus one. Every smaller square
minor can be padded by suitable identity columns, so V is totally
unimodular. In particular its rational column matroid agrees with its
column matroid over F_2: every nonzero minor has value plus/minus one.
Its rank is eight and its binary nullity is thirteen minus eight, or five.
The matroid has no loops or parallel elements because the thirteen
minimum pairs are nonzero and distinct up to sign.

Fix a nonzero generator of L/L_0, and write its class as u/5 with
u in L_0/5L_0. Relative to *every* minimum basis B of L_0, all eight
coordinates of u are nonzero. Their product is a nonsquare in F_5.
Indeed the displayed normal form has product 2^3, a nonsquare. Signs
are squares in F_5, permutations do not alter the product, and changing
the quotient generator multiplies the product by c^8, which is a square.
Thus this property does not require choosing a new compatible generator
separately for different bases.

Let v be a column outside a chosen basis. Its coordinates v_i in this
basis lie in {0,1,-1}, since replacing one basis column computes them as
unit-normalized maximal minors. Let S be its nonzero support. For each
i in S, replacing basis vector i by v gives another minimum basis. If
u has coordinates a_j, its new coordinates are

    a'_i = a_i/v_i,
    a'_j = a_j - v_j a_i/v_i  (j!=i).

Set r_j=a_j/v_j in F_5 for j in S. Nonvanishing of every new coordinate
forces the r_j to be pairwise distinct nonzero residues. Hence |S|<=4.
The nonsquare-product condition in the two bases also forces

    chi(product_(j in S) r_j)
      = chi(r_i * product_(j in S, j!=i) (r_j-r_i))        (G5.1)

for every pivot i, where chi is the quadratic character of F_5.
The signs v_j do not affect this equation, because -1 is a square mod5.

There are no subsets of size two or three satisfying (G5.1).
Here is a complete short check. Multiplying all residues by the same
nonzero c multiplies the two products by the same c^|S|, so it preserves
whether the two characters agree. For two-element subsets there are
two multiplicative orbits, represented by {1,4} and {1,2}. Pivoting at
1 gives old/new products 4 and 3 in the first case, and 2 and 1 in the
second; the quadratic characters differ. The four three-element subsets
form a single multiplicative orbit, represented by {1,2,3}. Pivoting at
1 gives old/new products 1 and 2, again with different characters.

Support one would make v a duplicate of a basis minimum pair. Hence every
column outside every minimum basis has support exactly four, and every
fundamental circuit has five elements. Each circuit is fundamental for
some basis, obtained by extending the circuit with one element deleted.
Consequently **every circuit of this binary matroid has size five**.

Two distinct such circuits cannot intersect. Their symmetric difference
is a nonzero binary cycle and has positive even size at most eight.
Every binary cycle is a disjoint union of circuits: choose a minimal
nonempty cycle in its support, subtract it over F_2, and repeat. If all
circuits have size five, an even nonzero union must contain at least two
of them, and therefore at least ten elements. This is a contradiction.
Thus all circuits are pairwise disjoint.

Their characteristic vectors now form a basis of the binary kernel:
they are independent by disjointness, and they span by the same cycle
decomposition. The nullity is five, so there would be five disjoint
five-element circuits, requiring at least twenty-five columns. There
are only thirteen. This contradiction proves the proposition.

## Exact scope and retained input

This rules out the actual proper ambient-glue index five case in the
remaining simplicial rank-eight endpoint. It leaves minimum-generated
cells of maximal minimum-basis index five, as well as proper glue of
other orders. It makes no assertion that those remaining cells or
their homology vanish.

The only classification input is the already audited necessary quotient
list in `k12_rank8_index5_reduction.md`: every basis index is at most five,
and an index-five frame has the `(5,3)` coefficient distribution. Its
primary source is Martinet, Table 11.1 and Proposition 9.1, with the
2014 completion of the index bound. The matroid and F_5 deductions above
are proved in full; no regular-matroid classification is invoked.
