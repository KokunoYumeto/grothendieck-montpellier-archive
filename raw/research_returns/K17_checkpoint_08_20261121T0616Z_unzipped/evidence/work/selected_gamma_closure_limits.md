## 1. Exact abelianization of the normal closure

Use the selected-free-factor setup. Write

\[
M=H^{\rm ab},\qquad T=T^-\oplus T^+,
\qquad Q=M/T,
\]

where Q has only odd Delta characters. Fix the Delta-equivariant continuous Z_p-section s:Q->M used to choose its compact basis lifts. Let T_I be the sum of the selected even character components, and let T_J^+ be the sum of the omitted even components. The selected closed free factor H_I is generated by all Q lifts and the finite bases of T_I.

Let N be the smallest closed subgroup of H which contains H_I and is normal under both H and Gamma. Put

\[
d_s(q)=\gamma s(q)-s(\gamma q)\in T^-,\qquad
C_s=\overline{\Lambda\langle d_s(q):q\in Q\rangle}\subset T^-.
\tag{GN.1}
\]

The defect is odd because s is Delta-equivariant and Gamma commutes with Delta. The image of N in M is exactly

\[
S=s(Q)+T_I+C_s.
\tag{GN.2}
\]

Indeed normal conjugation by H has no effect on M, so its image is the closed Lambda-submodule generated by s(Q)+T_I. This contains every d_s(q), hence contains the right side of GN.2. Conversely that right side is Gamma-stable: gamma*s(q)=s(gamma*q)+d_s(q), while C_s and T_I are stable. Gamma inverse stability follows by applying this equality to gamma^{-1}q, using that gamma^{-1} belongs to Lambda. All sums are compact, hence closed. This proves equality, including the closure in GN.1.

Consequently there is a canonical identification of Lambda/Delta modules

\[
(H/N)^{\rm ab}\cong M/S
\cong (T^-/C_s)\oplus T_J^+.
\tag{GN.3}
\]

The last identification uses the chosen section only to describe the quotient: inclusion of the remaining torsion modules induces the indicated equivariant isomorphism. It follows that

\[
N=H\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_s=T^-\ \text{and}\ T_J^+=0.
\tag{GN.4}
\]

For the reverse implication, any nontrivial pro-p group has a nontrivial finite p-group quotient, and that quotient has nontrivial abelianization. Thus a pro-p quotient with zero abelianization is trivial. This does not say a pro-p quotient with merely **finite** abelianization is finite.

The omitted even components are therefore an immediate, section-independent obstruction to N=H. The omitted odd components behave differently: their recovery is exactly the Gamma defect of the chosen Z_p-section, and it is not determined by the abstract torsion/free decomposition alone.

## 2. The odd recovery can change with the section

Consider the split compact module

\[
M=\Lambda X\oplus\mathbb Z_pY,
\qquad \gamma Y=\alpha Y,
\quad \alpha\in1+p\mathbb Z_p,
\]

with both summands in the same odd Delta character. Put T=gamma-1, and let [T]f denote the coefficient of T in f(T).

The section s_0(f)=fX has C_{s_0}=0. The equally valid continuous Z_p-section

\[
s_1(f)=fX+[T]f\,Y
\]

has

\[
\gamma s_1(1)-s_1(\gamma)=-Y.
\]

Thus C_{s_1}=Z_pY. This is an integral calculation, not just a rational-span statement. Both sections preserve the specified Delta character. It follows that Gamma-normal closure can recover an omitted odd torsion component even when the original module extension splits over Lambda. Such recovery alone cannot be a new arithmetic nonvanishing input.

## 3. Faithfulness does not pass to that closure

Here is the short exact group example suggested independently by the parent. Let H be free pro-p on x,y for p odd, and let Gamma=Z_p act by

\[
\phi_t(x)=x,\qquad
\phi_t(y)=x^{t/2}y x^{t/2}.
\]

The compositions satisfy phi_s phi_t=phi_{s+t}, and phi_{-t} is the inverse. The formulas are continuous. The involution c(x)=x^{-1}, c(y)=y^{-1} commutes with every phi_t: both orders send y to x^{-t/2}y^{-1}x^{-t/2}.

The abelianization map rho:H->Z_p^2 is Gamma/C2-equivariant and injective on the free factor <y>. Its Gamma-stable closed normal closure is H: after killing y, the relation phi_1(y)=1 becomes x=1. Nevertheless [x,y] is a nonidentity kernel element, as its first Magnus term XY-YX is nonzero. Thus injectivity on a free factor and on every Gamma-conjugate of that factor does not imply injectivity on their closed normal closure.

The following stronger model also retains the free prounipotent target, dense image and rank-one free Iwasawa quotient of the cubic criterion.

### A model with all the non-NV algebraic hypotheses

Fix odd p, a generator u of 1+pZ_p, an odd residue a_0 modulo p-1, and alpha in 1+pZ_p. Start with a free pro-p group P_0 on gamma and symbols x_a, one for each odd residue a. Let F be the kernel of P_0->Gamma sending gamma to its generator and all x_a to1. It is free pro-p by the pro-p Schreier theorem. Equivalently, the explicit finite-partition construction used in the moment proof identifies it with the free pro-p group on the compact orbits

\[
x_{a,t}=\gamma^t x_a\gamma^{-t},\qquad t\in\mathbb Z_p.
\]

For completeness, its abelianization is Lambda-free on the x_a: for every compact abelian Gamma-module B, any choice of b_a in B defines a homomorphism P_0->B semidirect Gamma by gamma->gamma and x_a->b_a, and hence an equivariant homomorphism F^ab->B. The converse is immediate. This is exactly the universal property of the finite direct sum of copies of Lambda.

Set H=F *_p <y>, the free pro-p product with another infinite procyclic group. Define commuting continuous actions by

\[
\gamma^s x_{a,t}=x_{a,t+s},\qquad
\gamma^s y=y^{\alpha^s},
\]

\[
\delta x_{a,t}=x_{a,t}^{\omega(\delta)^a},\qquad
\delta y=y^{\omega(\delta)^{a_0}}.
\]

The Delta action is induced on the free P_0 by fixing gamma and taking the displayed unit powers of the x_a. Its finite order and its commutation with Gamma follow directly on these generators. The action on the y factor has the same properties. Consequently

\[
H^{\rm ab}=\bigoplus_{a\ {\rm odd}}\Lambda X_a
              \oplus\mathbb Z_pY,
\qquad Q=\bigoplus_{a\ {\rm odd}}\Lambda X_a,
\qquad T^-_{a_0}=\mathbb Z_p(\alpha),\quad T^+=0.
\tag{GN.5}
\]

The exact eigenlift construction used earlier applies here as well. Its proof is purely pro-p with a coprime cyclic action: the iterated character projection of Sharifi Lemma2.1 preserves the abelian character projection, stays in any chosen stable open subgroup, and converges to an exact eigenlift. The complete primary proof was freshly read at `work/sharifi_galstr.txt`, printed pp3–4. Thus the graph section from Section2 has an actual convergent eigenbasis lift and defines an allowed closed free factor H_s. The free-basis argument is the same as before: the lifts together with y give an abelianization isomorphism; Frattini surjectivity and a section from freeness then give a free basis.

Let U be the rational free prounipotent group with one generator s_m for each odd m>=3, completed in their arithmetic weights. Let Gamma and Delta act by u^m and omega^m on s_m. Define

\[
\rho(y)=1,\qquad
\rho(x_{a,t})=
 \exp\left(\sum_{\substack{m\ge3\ {\rm odd}\\m\equiv a\ (p-1)}}
                   u^{mt}s_m\right).
\tag{GN.6}
\]

This is a continuous homomorphism. In each finite weight quotient the displayed sums are finite and continuous in t. The subgroup generated by their compact orbits lies in a bounded nilpotent p-adic matrix subgroup; a single lattice with the finitely many BCH denominators cleared contains it, and its closure is pro-p. One can therefore apply the free pro-p universal property in each finite quotient, or map P_0 to this compact group semidirect Gamma and restrict to F. Compatibility of the finite weight maps gives rho. The prescribed formulas prove Gamma/Delta equivariance exactly, and the y factor maps to1.

Its image is Zariski dense in each finite weight quotient. In each character the vectors at t=0,1,... give the Vandermonde matrix ((u^m)^t) on the finitely many retained abelian generators; its determinant is nonzero because the u^m are distinct. Thus the Lie algebra of the image closure surjects onto the target abelianization. In a finite nilpotent Lie algebra, a subalgebra with this property is the whole algebra: induct on the lower central series, expressing each commutator in terms of lifts from that subalgebra. Hence the Zariski closure is all of U at that weight.

Every allowed free factor H_s generated by the Q basis alone is faithful under rho. This is also the no-pair case of the already proved selected-factor argument, but its short proof is useful here. A nontrivial element of H_s has a nonzero first Magnus coefficient in a completed tensor power of Q. It is a finite sum of bounded multivariable power series in the orbit coordinates. The target coefficient at any ordered tuple of matching odd weights evaluates these series at T_i=u^{m_i}-1. Terms of higher source degree cannot contribute to that first target length. If rho killed the element, all such evaluations would vanish. In each residue class the permitted m_i contain infinite sequences with an interior p-adic accumulation point. Applying the one-variable analytic identity theorem successively to each variable forces the bounded series, hence that first Magnus coefficient, to be zero, a contradiction.

Choose the section s_1 of Section2 in character a_0 and the zero graph section in all other characters. Then C_s=T^- by the explicit defect -Y. Since there are no omitted even components, GN.4 says that the Gamma-normal closure of H_s is **all H**. Yet y is a nonidentity kernel element. Its actual odd companion error is

\[
\gamma(y)(y^\alpha)^{-1}=1,
\]

so its cubic numerator is identically zero. This model meets the free-group, module, exact character, equivariance and dense-free-target hypotheses; it fails the required arithmetic nonvanishing. It proves that those algebraic hypotheses, even supplemented by faithfulness of every allowed Q free factor, do not justify extending faithfulness by Gamma-normal closure. It is not a Galois realization and is not an arithmetic counterexample.

## 4. Iterating reflection gives no additional vanishing by itself

The proved reflection direction is

\[
A^{[1-a]}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A^{[a]}=0
\qquad(a\ {\rm even}),
\tag{GN.7}
\]

not its reverse. When a Bernoulli unit gives both sides, applying the same reflection again adds no character: the character involution a->1-a has order two. Passing through the exact M^+ duality changes the ambient module and twists the character; passing back returns the original class component. Coleman/WWE freeness of M^{[1-a]} makes its unramified quotient cyclic, not zero.

There is an actual finite-level arithmetic example against reversing GN.7. At p=37, Vandiver is verified, so every even class component, including A^{[32]}, vanishes. But

\[
 B_{32}=-\frac{37\cdot683\cdot305065927}{510},
\]

and Ribet's theorem gives A^{[1-32]}=A^{[5]} nonzero. The already checked primary inputs are Ribet1976 Main Theorem1.1 p151 and Hart–Harvey–Ong's verified Vandiver range; the exact Bernoulli factorization is independently certified in the positive-component calculation. Thus even-zero cannot be fed back as odd-zero in an iteration.

The second even character needed in the selected-pair theorem is 2-a, not a new output of reflection. If E is the set of even characters already proved zero, that theorem uses E intersect (2-E). It does not enlarge E. For the current fixed window, before reducing modulo p-1,

\[
E=\{2j:-12\le j\le16\},\qquad
E\cap(2-E)=\{2j:-12\le j\le13\},
\]

which is exactly the already stated selected range {-2j,2j+2:0<=j<=12}. Residue collisions at small primes are handled by taking these sets modulo p-1, not by a new reflection implication. The growing Bernoulli window genuinely deletes additional components where its Bernoulli units are proved; iterating reflection alone does not manufacture the missing units or the missing negative characters.

## Outcome

Gamma mixing has the exact integral description GN.1–GN.4 and can recover omitted odd abelian components depending on the section. This does **not** extend the known faithful restriction, as the fully compatible formal model demonstrates. The known reflection and duality operations add no characters beyond their explicit input sets. Neither proposed iteration supplies arithmetic Triple-NV or closes the remaining kernel gate.
