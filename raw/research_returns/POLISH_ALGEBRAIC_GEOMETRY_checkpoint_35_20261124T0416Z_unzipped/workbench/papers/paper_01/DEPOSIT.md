**Title:** Counterexamples to Grothendieck's cohomological-dimension conjectures
and birational invariance of the torsion Picard scheme.

**Abstract:** Grothendieck's Montpellier notes conjecture that the cohomological
 dimension of an integral affine scheme is controlled by its function field,
possibly after shrinking, and state birational invariance of the torsion
Picard scheme for a regular proper variety. We construct an excellent Dedekind
domain whose function field has two-cohomological dimension one while every
nonempty affine open has dimension two and an infinite-dimensional Picard
group modulo two. A mixed-characteristic regular local surface gives a second
failure, detected by a wild symbol. We recall Ishii–Yoshida's first-direct-image
vanishing theorem, explain the Picard assertion as a consequence of known
finite-torsor invariance, and give a finite-generation criterion when the
whole boundary has a quasi-projective neighborhood. The arithmetic proof
uses class field theory; the wild calculation uses Kato's filtration in
Suzuki's formulation; the Picard proof uses purity. The mathematics and text
were produced by GPT-6 Astra (ChatGPT Pro mode) and checked by separate
instances of the same model; no human has checked them.

**Subjects:** arithmetic geometry; étale cohomology; class field theory;
Picard schemes; ramification; algebraic fundamental groups.

**Keywords:** Dedekind domain, cohomological dimension, Picard group modulo
two, quadratic tower, wild symbol, finite flat torsor, birational morphism.
