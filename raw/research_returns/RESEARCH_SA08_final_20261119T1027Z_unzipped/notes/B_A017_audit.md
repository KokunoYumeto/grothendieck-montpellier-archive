## A-017: numerical Kähler differentials — proof audit passed

### 1. The reduction modulo a prime really is the asserted function algebra

Write $R_d=\operatorname{Num}(\mathbb Z^d)$, using the joint binomial basis $\beta_a=\prod_j\binom{x_j}{a_j}$. It is free over $\mathbb Z$. Evaluation induces an injection

\[
R_d/pR_d\longrightarrow \operatorname{Fun}(\mathbb Z^d,\mathbb F_p).
\]

Indeed, if a rational polynomial has integer values all divisible by $p$, its quotient by $p$ is again integer valued. This proves the exact kernel assertion, rather than merely pointwise equality of two polynomial expressions.

Fix $r\geq1$, and take the span of the classes $\beta_a$ with every $a_j<p^r$. For one coordinate, Vandermonde's polynomial identity and $p\mid\binom{p^r}{i}$ for $0<i<p^r$ imply

\[
\binom{x+p^r}{a}\equiv\binom xa\pmod p\qquad(a<p^r)
\]

for **all** integral $x$, including negative $x$. The evaluation matrix indexed by $0\leq x,a<p^r$ is lower triangular with diagonal 1. Taking its $d$-fold tensor product shows that the indicated span maps isomorphically onto every function on $(\mathbb Z/p^r)^d$. The evaluation injection then proves that it is a subalgebra: the product of two functions in that finite function algebra has a unique preimage in the span, and equality as functions implies equality in $R_d/pR_d$. Every joint binomial belongs to one such span. Consequently

\[
R_d/pR_d=\varinjlim_r\operatorname{Fun}((\mathbb Z/p^r)^d,\mathbb F_p)
\]

as algebras, with transition maps given by pullback along reduction. This is a filtered colimit of finite étale $\mathbb F_p$-algebras. No claim about all arbitrary functions on $\mathbb Z^d$ is needed.

### 2. Cotangent-complex conclusion, including divisible torsion

The vanishing of the cotangent complex for an étale map and its compatibility with filtered colimits give $L_{(R_d/p)/\mathbb F_p}=0$. The exact primary references were independently opened:

- [Stacks Project, Lemma 92.8.4, Tag 08R2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08R2), clauses 5 and 6.
- [Stacks Project, Lemma 92.6.2, Tag 08QQ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08QQ), derived base change.
- [Stacks Project, Proposition 92.7.4, Tag 08QX](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08QX), transitivity.

Flatness of $R_d$ over $\mathbb Z$ makes the ordinary reduction square a derived pushout, so

\[
L_{R_d/\mathbb Z}\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_p=0.
\]

Tensoring with $\mathbb F_p$ is the cone of multiplication by $p$, up to the harmless shift convention. Thus multiplication by $p$ is a quasi-isomorphism for every prime. Each homology module is uniquely divisible by every positive integer and is therefore a $\mathbb Q$-vector space. In particular the argument excludes $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ and similar divisible torsion: multiplication by $p$ on those groups is not injective. Rationalization of the complex is a quasi-isomorphism. Since $R_d\otimes\mathbb Q=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$, flat base change gives

\[
L_{R_d/\mathbb Z}\simeq \mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_d]\{dx_1,\ldots,dx_d\}[0].
\]

Its degree-zero homology is the ordinary Kähler module, so this proves the absolute assertion precisely as stated.

### 3. The relative transitivity step has no hidden Tor term

Take $R_0=\operatorname{Num}(\mathbb ZT)$ and $R=\operatorname{Num}(\mathbb Z[T,x_1,\ldots,x_n])$. The joint binomial basis gives

\[
R=R_0\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Num}(\mathbb Z^n)
\]

and exhibits $R$ as a free $R_0$-module. The first term of the transitivity triangle is therefore

\[
R\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{R_0}L_{R_0/\mathbb Z}
=R\otimes_{R_0}\mathbb Q[T]\,dT
=\mathbb Q[T,x_1,\ldots,x_n]\,dT.
\]

The map to $L_{R/\mathbb Z}$ is the ordinary inclusion of the $dT$ summand. Its cone is the direct complement freely generated by the $dx_j$, with rational polynomial coefficients, in degree zero. This verifies the relative module and its ordinary exterior powers. Exterior products over $R$ of this localized module agree with the exterior products over $R\otimes\mathbb Q$, since rational localization is idempotent. There is no unjustified passage from absolute to relative differentials.

### 4. The lattice formula gives every induced filtration piece

The induced filtration is generated by $f_0df_1\wedge\cdots\wedge df_q$ with the sum of the polynomial degrees of the $f_j$ at most $w$. Expanding all factors in the integral binomial basis proves exactly

\[
F_w\Omega^q_{R/R_0}=
\sum_{|a|+\sum_j|b_j|\leq w}
\mathbb Z\,\beta_a\,d\beta_{b_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\beta_{b_q}.
\]

This is an equality of subgroups of a known rational differential module, so it neither loses relations nor assumes a presentation is free. For $q\leq n$ and $w\geq q$, the finite ambient rational vector space has basis $T^{c_0}x_1^{c_1}\cdots x_n^{c_n}dx_I$, with $|c|\leq w-q$ and $|I|=q$. The lattice has full rank because it contains every such integral monomial differential form. Hermite normal form after denominator clearing and Smith normal form of the inclusion of the previous filtration piece therefore determine the associated graded. Differentiation, wedge product, and integral linear substitutions are finite rational matrices on these ambient spaces and preserve the defining filtration, giving the stated effective description of all structure maps.

For $n=q=1,w=2$, the generators are $dx,x\,dx,T\,dx,(x-1/2)dx$. Thus

\[
F_1=\mathbb Z\,dx,
\qquad F_2=\tfrac12\mathbb Z\,dx\oplus\mathbb Z\,x\,dx\oplus\mathbb Z\,T\,dx,
\qquad F_2/F_1=\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]

The associated-graded PD term is free of rank two, so this is a genuine discrepancy already as a graded module. The closed form $dx/2$ has no integral-valued primitive because any rational polynomial primitive has the form $x/2+g(T)$, whose values at $x=0,1$ differ by $1/2$.

## A-021: stronger obstruction survives the precise infinite-shadow quotient

The elementary graded-module discrepancy in A-021 is valid: multiplication by $T$ from weight one to weight two is 1 in $S[-1]$ and 2 in $I_1$, for $S=\Gamma_{\mathbb Z}(T)$. A graded $S$-isomorphism cannot conjugate those maps by signs.

The discrepancy also survives the quotient $Q:\mathcal G\to\mathcal H$ of D005, whose kernel is the localizing Serre closure of bounded-weight modules. Here is a proof using precisely the established structure of that quotient.

1. The quotient $S/I_1$ is bounded, so $QI_1\simeq QS$.
2. D005 proves that $S=F(\mathbb Z)$ is saturated and that $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal H}(QS)=\mathbb Z$. Its adjunction gives
   \[
   \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal H}(Q(S[-1]),QS)
   =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal G}(S[-1],S)=\mathbb Z\,T.
   \]
3. If $Q(S[-1])\simeq QS$, its isomorphism would therefore be $Q(zT)$ for an integer $z$.
4. The quotient is strong symmetric monoidal. Tensoring that isomorphism with itself gives an isomorphism
   \[
   Q(S[-2])\xrightarrow{\;2z^2T^{[2]}\;}QS.
   \]
5. In a $\mathbb Z$-linear category, if $2g:A\to U$ is invertible, multiplication by 2 on $U$ is invertible: $g(2g)^{-1}$ is a two-sided inverse, since 2 is central. Applied here, this contradicts $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal H}(QS)=\mathbb Z$.

Thus $Q(S[-1])\not\simeq QI_1$. The exact coefficient reconstruction $\overline L$ sends both objects to $\mathbb Z$, but it is not conservative on all of $\mathcal H$; that later equality does not remove the obstruction inside the shadow category. The shadows agent independently checked every type and implication of this proof on 2026-11-19.

This result concerns the precise quotient used in this paper. It does not rule out additional comparisons after a different localization or a different functor that forgets more information.
