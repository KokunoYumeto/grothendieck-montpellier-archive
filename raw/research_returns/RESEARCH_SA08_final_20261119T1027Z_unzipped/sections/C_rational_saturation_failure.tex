\subsection{A rational saturation need not admit maps to the target}

\begin{proposition}[C35: \status{Proved}; a stable-row divisibility obstruction]
\label{sa08:C35}
Let $k=\Z_{(2)}$, let $F$ be the standard PD de Rham theory,
and put $M=F^{2,1}$. Form the original odd disk
\[
 D(M)=\Gamma_k(My)\otimes_k\Lambda_k(Mx),\qquad
 |x|=1,\quad |y|=2,\quad dx=y,
\]
assigning both parameter modules weight two. Let $B(M)$ be the
smallest graded PD differential subalgebra of $D(M)\otimes_k\Q$
containing $D(M)$ and all elements $x(u)x(v)/2$, for $u,v\in M$.
This construction is functorial in linear maps and therefore defines
a simplicial PD-DGA for the indicated $M$.

The disk counit $D(M)\to F$, sending $x(u)$ to $u$ and $y(u)$
to $du$, has no extension $B(M)\to F$. The input row $F^{2,1}$
and output row $F^{4,2}$ are both horizontally acyclic, and the
standard target is vertically a coefficient resolution. Thus those
conditions alone do not justify the divisibility imposed by this
rational subalgebra construction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The rational graded algebra has its canonical divided powers on
even positive-weight elements. Intersection of all subalgebras stable
under these divided powers and the differential defines $B(M)$.
Linear maps preserve its specified generators, so preserve the
intersection-defined subalgebra; equivalently, they preserve its
iterative closure under the indicated operations. It is torsionfree,
and the inclusion of $D(M)$ is injective because that algebra is
termwise free. All generators are homogeneous in both gradings.

In simplex dimension two, use the standard presentation
\[
 F_2=S\otimes_k\Gamma_k(U,V)\otimes_k\Lambda_k(dU,dV).
\]
The closed elements
\[
 u=U\,dU=d\gamma_2(U),\qquad
 v=V\,dV=d\gamma_2(V)
\]
belong to $M_2=F_2^{2,1}$. Any extension would have to send
$x(u)x(v)/2$ to an element $z\in F_2^{4,2}$ with
\[
 2z=uv=UV\,dU\,dV.
\]
But $F_2^{4,2}$ is free over $k$ on
$T^{[a]}U^{[b]}V^{[c]}dU\,dV$ with $a+b+c=2$.
The displayed right side has coefficient one on the basis vector
with $(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)$. The equation would require $2z_{0,1,1}=1$
in $\Z_{(2)}$, which is impossible.

The acyclicity assertions concern precisely the two rows stated:
\[
 F^{2,1}=\Gamma^1(\Phi)\otimes\Lambda^1(\Psi),\qquad
 F^{4,2}=\Gamma^2(\Phi)\otimes\Lambda^2(\Psi).
\]
Their positive divided-power factors are flat and acyclic by
Statement~\ref{sa08:C05}; tensoring with the flat exterior factors
and Eilenberg--Zilber proves the assertions. The vertical statement
is the standard divided-power Poincar\'e contraction. No claim that
all rows of weight four are acyclic is needed or made.

The finite certificate \texttt{C\_rational\_saturation\_countercheck.py}
checks the entire simplex-two monomial window through weight four,
including $d^2=0$, the relevant products and the Leibniz identities.
Its weight-four, form-two coordinate vector has an odd entry, giving
the exact divisibility obstruction above.
\end{proof}

The proposition does not assert that $B(M)$ is an admissible or
acyclic closure. It isolates a failure of target-map extension for
any proposed rational-image construction that contains these forced
halves of odd products. The positive bar algebra itself imposes no
such equality between an individual word and half a shuffle product.
