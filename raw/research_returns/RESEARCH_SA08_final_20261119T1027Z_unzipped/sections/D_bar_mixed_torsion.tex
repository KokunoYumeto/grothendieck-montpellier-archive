\subsection{A mixed torsion class in the bar indecomposables}

\begin{proposition}[D032: \status{Proved}; the unary prime-power pattern fails]
\label{sa08:D032}
Let $P=\Z a\oplus\Z b$, with both parameters in form degree two,
and let $U(P)$ be the positive bar closure of
Statement~\ref{sa08:D029}. Write $U_m$ for its polynomial arity-$m$
component. Let $V_m$ be the arity-$m$ part of the PD subalgebra
generated by all components of smaller positive arity, and put
\[
 Q_m(P)=U_m(P)/V_m(P).
\]
In the basis multidegree $a^2b^4$, the complex $Q_6(P)$ has a
cohomology class of exact order two in form degree seven.
In fact its cohomology in that multidegree is $\Z/2$ in form
degree seven and zero in every other degree.
Consequently the bar indecomposables are not concentrated in
prime-power arities, and their cohomology is not always a linear
functor of $P$ in form degree $2m-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We use the integral signed Lyndon basis proved in
Statement~\ref{sa08:C24}. All bar letters have odd parity.
The free graded PD generators are the Lyndon words, together
with the concatenation squares of odd Lyndon words. Modulo
$V_m$, only generators of total arity $m$ remain. In particular,
one must retain a concatenation square of an odd Lyndon word:
it is an independent even generator, not a divided power of
the odd generator.

Choose the alphabet order induced by exponent vectors with
$a=(0,1)<b=(1,0)$, and abbreviate
$A=\gamma_2(a)$, $B=\gamma_2(b)$, and $C=ab$.
Word notation below denotes a bar word, with no coefficient
factor. In multidegree $a^2b^4$, the complete form-six basis is
\[
 u_1=aabbbb,\qquad u_2=ababbb,\qquad u_3=abbabb.
\]
Here $u_3$ is the concatenation square of the odd Lyndon word
$abb$. The complete form-seven basis, in order, is
\[
\begin{gathered}
 v_1=aabbB,\quad v_2=aabBb,\quad v_3=aaBbb,\quad
 v_4=ababB,\quad v_5=abaBb,\quad v_6=abbaB,\\
 v_7=abbbC,\quad v_8=abbCb,\quad v_9=abCbb,\quad
 v_{10}=aCbbb,\quad v_{11}=Abbbb.
\end{gathered}
\]
These lists exhaust the signed Lyndon generators of bar
length six and five and the indicated multidegree.

The first term of the bar differential has a positive
coefficient factor and is PD decomposable. The remaining
differential merges adjacent letters with the ordinary
alternating signs and the divided-power multiplication
coefficients. Reducing in the integral signed Lyndon basis
gives the following matrix from form six to form seven:
\begin{equation}
 D=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -2&0&0\\
  2&0&0\\
 -2&0&0\\
  0&-2&2\\
  0&2&-2\\
  0&0&-4\\
  0&1&1\\
  0&-1&1\\
  0&0&0\\
  1&0&0\\
 -2&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{D-Q6}
\end{equation}
In particular, for $z=v_7-2v_6$,
\[
 2z=d(u_2+u_3).
\]
The next cochain group is free over $\Z$, so $2dz=0$
implies $dz=0$. The functional
\[
 \ell\colon Q_6^7(P)^{a^2b^4}\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2,
 \qquad \ell\Bigl(\sum t_jv_j\Bigr)=t_7+t_8\pmod2,
\]
vanishes on every column of $D$ but takes the value one on
$z$. Therefore $z$ is not a boundary, and its cohomology
class has exact order two.

For completeness, the full free ranks in form degrees
$6,7,8,9,10,11$ are
\[
 3,\ 11,\ 18,\ 16,\ 7,\ 1.
\]
The successive nonzero Smith invariant factors are
\[
 (1,1,2),\qquad (1^8),\qquad (1^{10}),\qquad
 (1^6),\qquad (1).
\]
The exact integral matrices and their bases are included in
the certificate described below. Consecutive matrices multiply
to zero. Their ranks show that all rational cohomology vanishes.
For a complex of free abelian groups, the torsion of cohomology
at a term is the torsion of the cokernel of the incoming
differential: the further quotient is the free image of the
outgoing differential. The displayed Smith factors therefore
give precisely the claimed cohomology.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact computational certificate.}
The program \texttt{D\_bar\_indecomposables.py} enumerates words
of a fixed basis multidegree, expands the signed Lyndon
PD monomials, and reduces by their unitriangular integral
change of basis. It discards every decomposable monomial,
so it computes the actual integral quotient rather than its
rational saturation. The specialized program
\texttt{D\_bar\_mixed\_counterexample.py} reproduces
\textup{(D-Q6)}, checks the next differential, verifies the
cycle and its double, and checks the mod-two nonboundary
functional. Its complete output is
\texttt{D\_bar\_mixed\_counterexample.json}.
The five basis-multidegree partitions of arity four and the
seven of arity five have also been exhausted; only the
diagonal top classes $\Z/2$ and $\Z/5$, respectively, occur.
All eleven partitions of arity six were exhausted, and
rank-one arities seven through twelve were computed.
The three receipts state those finite scopes exactly;
they are not an all-arity extrapolation.

\paragraph{Limit of the counterexample.}
This is a counterexample to a proposed description of the
bar indecomposable complex. It is not an obstruction to a
particular map into an admissible theory. Such an extension
has a class in a derived Hom group; the existence of torsion
in $Q_m$ alone does not show that this class is nonzero.
