# F58 — strict Laurent positivity cannot preserve the source's changing cell structure

Author: /root/rectangular_and_theta, GPT-6 Astra Pro. Reasoning-effort setting is not exposed. Date: 2026-11-21 UTC.

This report gives an elementary obstruction to an additional strict positivity requirement on the source-prescribed cellular basis. It does not refute IV's signed/factored positivity, Conjecture 19.1 as stated, or the existence of an arbitrary positive basis of the whole tensor representation. The new proof is elementary; the source supplies the actual module, cell definitions and specialized filtration.

## 1. Exact general statement

Let k be an ordered subfield of R, let

\[
A=k[v,v^{-1}],\qquad K=k(v),\qquad
A_+=k_{\ge0}[v,v^{-1}],
\]

and let M=A^m have an ordered basis B=(b_1,\ldots,b_m). Let T_1,\ldots,T_r be matrices in Mat_m(A_+) acting on rows on the right. For a>0, write T_i(a) for their evaluations and M_a=R^m for the specialized space. The generic image algebra is the unital K-algebra generated by the T_i; the specialized image algebra is the unital R-algebra generated by the T_i(a).

**Proposition Z1.** Under these hypotheses:

1. The nonzero-entry pattern of every named generator is identical generically and at every a>0.
2. For every subset S of B, its coordinate span is invariant under all the generic generators if and only if its specialized coordinate span is invariant under all the specialized generators.
3. The cell preorder defined by all elements of the generated algebra is identical generically and at every a>0. In particular its cells, cellular coordinate submodules and their inclusion order do not change.
4. These assertions hold for every cellular coordinate subquotient with its induced quotient basis.

The primary case k=Q and entries in N[v,v^-1] is included. Allowing arbitrary nonnegative rational coefficients only makes the obstruction stronger.

### Proof of Z1

If f=Σ_e c_e v^e belongs to A_+, then

\[
f(a)=\sum_e c_e a^e.
\]

Every summand is nonnegative and every summand with c_e>0 is strictly positive. Hence

\[
f(a)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f=0.
\tag{Z1}
\]

This proves the generator-pattern assertion.

For a subset S of {1,\ldots,m}, the row-coordinate span M_S is invariant precisely when every coefficient

\[
(T_i)_{st}=0\qquad(s\in S,\ t\notin S)
\tag{Z2}
\]

vanishes. Equation (Z1) shows that these conditions hold generically exactly when they hold after a positive specialization. A generic zero is an actual Laurent-polynomial zero because A injects into K. This proves the invariant-subspace assertion without assuming semisimplicity or irreducibility.

To compare the source's all-algebra cell preorder with generator support, make a directed graph with an edge s→t whenever (T_i)_{st}≠0 for some i. The graph's reachability relation agrees with the preorder generated by all action coefficients, up to the source's choice of direction for ≤.

Indeed, every generator edge is one of the source's defining relations. Conversely, an element of the generated algebra is a finite scalar linear combination of words. If its (s,t) coefficient is nonzero, some word has nonzero (s,t) coefficient. The expansion of that coefficient is a finite sum of products indexed by state sequences. A nonzero sum has at least one nonzero product, giving a directed path from s to t. This argument does not require positivity; positivity is used only to ensure that the generator graph is unchanged by specialization. The equivalence classes, namely its strongly connected components, and the reachability order are therefore unchanged.

Finally, let S_0⊂S_1 be subsets whose coordinate spans are invariant. The induced basis on M_(S_1)/M_(S_0) consists of the classes indexed by S_1\S_0, and each induced matrix is exactly the corresponding row-and-column submatrix of T_i. It still lies in A_+. Specialization commutes with this quotient because the coordinate modules and quotients are free. The preceding arguments apply verbatim. This proves all four assertions. ∎

**Corollary Z2.** A generically simple module with strictly nonnegative Laurent generator matrices cannot acquire a nonzero proper specialized invariant subspace spanned by a subset of that same specializing basis.

More generally, a generic cell cannot split into two or more specialized cells in such a basis. This latter statement needs only the cell preorder and does not require that the generic cell module be irreducible.

The first assertion follows from Z1(2) and generic simplicity. The second follows from Z1(3), or from a proper terminal union of strongly connected components of the specialized cell graph.

The specialization must be positive and the basis must remain a basis there. No conclusion is asserted at v=0, at a negative or nonreal value, or for a singular basis change that destroys the prescribed special basis.

## 2. The exact source meaning of a cell and a cellular quotient

In **§2.4, printed pp.18–19, equation (17)**, the source defines the cell preorder using all algebra elements. A cellular submodule is explicitly a submodule spanned by a subset of the designated basis. A cellular subquotient is RΓ′/RΓ″ for coordinate submodules with Γ″⊂Γ′; its designated quotient basis is Γ′\Γ″. The right-action convention is stated on p.19.

Exact supplied locators are the labels “ss cells” and “e preorder”, lines 1567–1591. Thus Z1 is about precisely the source's definition of a cell and cellular subquotient. It is not replacing an abstract direct-sum decomposition by an unsupported coordinate claim.

The strict positivity hypothesis in Z1 is an additional hypothesis; it is not stated in these source conditions.

## 3. The actual two-row rank-four six-dimensional cell

Consider

\[
\alpha=\{(3,1),(2,2)\},\qquad
N_6=\check M_{\{(3,1),(2,2)\}}.
\]

Theorem 13.4, pp.74–75, includes N_6 as a six-dimensional generic simple of the two-row nonstandard quotient. Both partitions have at most two rows, so the module occurs in the actual tensor construction with dim V=dim W=2.

The source explicitly constructs the corresponding cell in **Example 19.3, pp.133–135, Figure 21 on p.135**. The discussion on **p.134** states that its top row spans a specialized QS_4-cellular submodule isomorphic to M_(2,1,1), while the bottom row is the designated basis of a cellular quotient isomorphic to M_(3,1). Both have dimension three. The exact supplied TeX statement is lines 8092–8094, not merely the word “quasi-cell” in isolation.

This also follows directly from the graph. Order its six basis vectors as

\[
(v_{12},v_{13},v_{23},v_3,v_2,v_1),
\tag{Z3}
\]

with the top row first. Put

\[
\delta=v+v^{-1},\quad D=\delta^2,\quad
a_2=D-2,\quad c_0=D-4=(v-v^{-1})^2.
\]

The unique arrow from the top row to the bottom row is

\[
v_{13}\xrightarrow{\,Q_2,\ c_0\,}v_2.
\tag{Z4}
\]

All three top-row vertices are mutually reachable, and all three bottom-row vertices are mutually reachable. There are nonzero bottom-to-top arrows. Generically c_0≠0, so the whole graph is strongly connected. At v=1, c_0=0; D=4 and a_2=2, so the other displayed connecting arrows remain nonzero. The top-row coordinate span becomes invariant, while the generic graph had only one cell.

The complementary generators satisfy P_i=D I−Q_i. Hence P_i and Q_i have identical off-diagonal support. They generate the same unital algebra and have the same coordinate invariant subspaces. At v=1, the ordinary diagonal symmetric-group action satisfies

\[
P_i(1)=2(1+s_i),\qquad Q_i(1)=2(1-s_i),
\tag{Z5}
\]

as follows directly from the rank-one generators specialized in IV §11.1, p.59. Thus the specialized algebra generated by the P_i(1), or by the Q_i(1), has precisely the same invariant subspaces and cell preorder as the ordinary QS_4 action. The earlier exponent-convention inconsistency is harmless for (Z5), because both conventions have the same value at v=1.

## 4. The source-compatible strict positivity obstruction

**Finding F58.** There is no basis with all of the following properties:

1. It is a free specializing basis of the actual N_6 module over a Laurent-polynomial ring, or a regular form with the same coefficient matrices.
2. Every named P_i matrix has coefficients in Q_(≥0)[v,v^-1].
3. A nonzero proper subset of the specialized basis spans the source-prescribed three-dimensional M_(2,1,1) cellular submodule at v=1.

The same statement holds with Q_i in place of P_i.

### Proof

The coordinate invariant subspace in property 3 has dimension three. By Z1(2), its lift would be a three-dimensional generic invariant subspace for all P_i. This contradicts the cited generic simplicity of N_6. The same argument applies to Q_i, since both sets generate the same algebra. ∎

There is a stronger formulation directly for the tensor basis. **No basis satisfying the actual cell and quasi-cell conditions 19.1(ii)–(iii) at rank four can additionally have all named P_i action matrices in Q_(≥0)[v,v^-1].** The same holds for the Q_i-only requirement.

To prove this, pass to the six-dimensional generic cell subquotient with α={31,22}. Positivity passes to its induced matrices by Z1(4). Condition (iii) requires this generic cell to have the two three-dimensional specialized quasi-cells just described. But Z1(3) says that its cell partition cannot change. This is a contradiction.

Equivalently, the graph argument shows that a source-compatible generic cell must contain some nonzero coefficient which vanishes at v=1. A nonzero Laurent polynomial with nonnegative coefficients cannot do so. The source's actual c_0 edge is a concrete realization of the necessary cancellation.

This application already lies in the two-row rank-four quotient. It is different from F57's basis-independent negative word trace on the extra full-algebra five-dimensional simple. F58 requires the designated cellular compatibility; F57 does not.

## 5. Why the coordinate premise is essential

Strict positive matrices can specialize from a generically simple representation to a reducible one when the special summands are not coordinate subspaces. Here is an exact two-dimensional example:

\[
A(v)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B(v)=\begin{pmatrix}1&v\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{Z6}
\]

Both matrices have coefficients in N[v]. The only invariant lines for A over an algebraic closure of Q(v) are the row lines spanned by (1,1) and (1,−1), because A has distinct eigenvalues 2 and 0. But

\[
(1,1)B=(2,v+1),\qquad
(1,-1)B=(0,v-1).
\]

Neither is in the corresponding line for v≠1. Thus the generic algebra generated by A and B acts absolutely irreducibly. At v=1, A=B, and the representation splits into the two eigenspaces above. Neither eigenspace is spanned by a subset of the standard two-vector basis.

The support graph is complete both generically and at v=1, so there is still one cell in that fixed basis. This example proves that the added coordinate/cell requirement, not ordinary reducibility at specialization alone, is doing the work in F58. It is only an illustrative elementary matrix family; no nonstandard Hecke relation is claimed for it.

Several further limitations are exact:

- The result does not prohibit an arbitrary positive basis of the whole nonstandard tensor module which fails the prescribed cell/quasi-cell compatibility.
- It does not prohibit a positive basis on N_6 for which the specialized M_(2,1,1) summand is non-coordinate.
- It does not prohibit coefficients merely nonnegative as real functions for v>0. The actual c_0=(v−v^-1)^2 is such a function, but has negative Laurent coefficient −2 and vanishes at 1.
- It does not prohibit the source's signed/factored coefficients, virtual graded coefficients, or derived Euler coefficients. Those can vanish by cancellation at v=1.
- No effectiveness claim is inferred from generic purity, an arbitrary basis, or the existence of the abstract classical decomposition.

## 6. Separate Grothendieck dictionary

A finite graded coefficient vector space is a representation of the split torus G_m. Its effective character is an element of N[v,v^-1]. Forgetting the grading induces the dimension augmentation

\[
\epsilon:\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad \epsilon(v)=1.
\]

On the effective cone, ε is zero-reflecting: a finite direct sum of positive-dimensional weight spaces has zero total dimension only when it is zero. Equation (Z1) is this elementary fact, with a positive real character evaluation in place of dimension.

The representation framework is **SGA 3, I, 4.7.2–3**, and the corresponding archive anchor is **cote 90, scan 17**. The representation and λ/σ vocabulary is **SGA 6, V, §§2–3**, with **cote 133, scan 18**. No λ-ring structure on the noncommutative word algebra and no new γ-positivity theorem are asserted.

As affine data, a coordinate basis gives the trivial bundle over Spec A, and a coordinate subset gives a direct-summand subbundle. Invariance is given by the vanishing of the corresponding cross-block matrix entries. The module/sheaf dictionary is **EGA I, Theorem 1.3.7, p.86, Corollary 1.3.8, p.87, and Theorem 1.4.1, pp.90–92**. In this specific effective-character situation those equations cannot become zero at the positive identity fiber unless they were identically zero. This is stronger than a bare specialization statement about coherent modules because it uses the effective coefficient cone.

The class c_0=v²−2+v^-2 lies in the kernel of ε but is not effective. Thus ordinary representation K0 accommodates the necessary edge cancellation, whereas an effective graded matrix kernel does not. Passing to a virtual or derived class is a possible response to this obstruction; it does not automatically preserve the designated basis, give a positive counting rule, or establish the arithmetic comparison and bounds sought elsewhere.
