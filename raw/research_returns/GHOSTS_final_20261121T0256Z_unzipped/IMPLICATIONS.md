## Hodge, Tate, and the standard conjectures

B01–B06 make the arithmetic boundedness gate precise for a fixed rational absolute Hodge class. Pointwise local algebraicity becomes global algebraicity when the total degrees of the two integral numerator cycles are bounded along infinitely many places. A separate bound on the multiplier is unnecessary: finitely many cycle-parameter components force one automatically. B04 gives a sharper arithmetic test: one bounded-degree representative at a prime outside a class-independent exceptional integer b_D lifts with its own multiplier unchanged.

For a globally algebraic class, B03 proves that the least local complexity equals its geometric characteristic-zero minimum outside finitely many primes, and never exceeds that minimum at any good prime. For a hypothetical nonalgebraic ghost, every fixed complexity threshold occurs at only finitely many primes. These statements concern cycle classes modulo homological equivalence. They do not assert algebraic equivalence or effectivity of every numerical class.
