Lemma13 proves dominance onto the corresponding full Weil-pair moduli component. Its proof is not merely an assertion that a special subfamily has algebraic cycles.

I independently checked the finite character calculation in Lemma13. For the exponent-three cover with G=(Z/3)^3 and the unramified quotient subgroup generated by (1,1,0), the two nontrivial quotient eigenspaces of H^0(C,omega_C) have character triples

    {(2,2,0),(2,2,1),(2,2,2)},
    {(0,2,2),(1,1,2),(2,0,2)}.

Each constituent has multiplicity one, and the nine pairwise sums are distinct. Multiplication of two nonzero differentials on an integral curve is nonzero, so the corresponding nine multiplication maps are nonzero into distinct eigenspaces. The tangent dual is therefore injective, exactly as required. This is a computation check of the finite arithmetic in the cited argument; Chevalley--Weil and the deformation-theoretic tangent identification remain cited background.

## R22: exact Hermitian determinant and charged isogeny

Let Lambda=H_1(A,Z) be O_K-stable and E_H its integral alternating polarization form. Since O_K is a PID, Lambda is free over O_K. Define

    h_H(x,y)=E_H(omega x,y)-bar(omega)E_H(x,y).

It is O_K-valued Hermitian, and

    E_H(x,y)=Tr_{K/Q}(h_H(x,y)/sqrt(-3)).

In an O_K basis the determinant of the Z-alternating form is the square of the Hermitian determinant. One can check this first for the identity Hermitian matrix, where the trace with the inverse different gives the unimodular alternating block, and then use change of basis; equivalently it is the norm-determinant formula for trace pairings. Thus signature(2,2) gives

    d=det h_H=chi(H)=H^4/4! in Z_{>0}.

The auxiliary product E^2 with mixed K action and H'=theta_1+d theta_2 has Hermitian determinant -d and chi(H')=d. Their product has signature(3,3), determinant -d^2, and hence determinant class -1 modulo norms. Schoen1998 §7 states precisely the rational Hermitian classification by signature and discriminant. It gives a rational isometry to the principal sixfold space, and §6 spells out its passage to an isogeny of universal families after multiplication by one lattice denominator m.

The isometry preserves the alternating forms as well as the Hermitian forms. Therefore for H0=H+H', the resulting h:A×E^2→Q satisfies

    h*theta_Q=m^2 H0,
    e=deg h=m^12 chi(H0)=m^12 d^2.

There is no uncharged variable isogeny: m is a fixed input of the chosen rational matrix and all these data are constant on the associated family. A rational isometry can also be found by enumeration of rational matrices, since its existence is proved and the equality to be tested is an exact rational matrix equation. No claim of an a priori small m or a height bound in d alone is made.

For Gamma as in R23, use the concrete transport

    tau=h_*Gamma/e.

On rational cohomology h*h_*=e and h_*h*=e, so h*tau=Gamma. If delta_t t is integral, then3delta_t Gamma is integral; proper pushforward preserves integral cohomology. Hence

    delta_tau=3delta_t e

is a valid denominator, with no inverse-matrix estimate needed. The positive middle intersection norm satisfies

    U_tau=U_Gamma/e=T/e.

For a codimension-three effective cycle Y on Q,

    deg_H0(h*Y)=(e/m^6)deg_theta(Y),
    e/m^6=m^6 d^2 in Z_{>0}.

Thus the fourfold bound after the cost-free R23 contraction is exactly the sufficient certificate

    C4=(e/m^6) C6(3delta_t e,T/e).

One may use the fully expanded sixfold constant

    C6(delta,U)=delta D6^4
       ·(51656065200+531540910908 ceil(sqrt(U))).

### A merely rational O_K action

If the original lattice is not O_K-stable, this preliminary isogeny must be charged. Choose an O_K-stable finite-index sublattice Lambda'⊂Lambda, for example f O_KLambda⊂Lambda for a conductor integer f. It defines q:A'→A of degree e0=[Lambda:Lambda']. Use H_A'=q*H and t'=q*t. Then

    chi(H_A')=e0 chi(H),    (t')^2=e0T,

and delta_t still clears t'. Apply the integral-O_K result to A'. Pushing its cycles to A preserves their degrees and changes Nt' to Ne0t. Consequently

    c_H(t)≤e0 C4(A',q*t).

This is uniform for the fixed original lattice and polarization, and does not identify complexities across arbitrary changes of polarization.
