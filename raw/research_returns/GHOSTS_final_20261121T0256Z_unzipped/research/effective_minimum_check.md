## R40: independent check of the finite-field part

The corrected parameter notation is Q_C for individual cycles and H_C=Q_C times Q_C for pairs. This distinction was repaired in the section before the final check.

Frobenius is semisimple on H1 of an abelian variety over a finite field. The relevant centrality is in End^0 over the ground finite field, not in the full geometric endomorphism algebra. Exterior powers preserve semisimplicity. Consequently the geometric Tate space in H4(2) is the sum of eigenspaces whose eigenvalues are roots of unity, with their full multiplicities. The Poincare pairing restricts nondegenerately to this subspace: a Frobenius eigenvalue can pair only with its inverse, and a root of unity cannot pair with a non-root of unity.

The proposed deliberately inefficient zeta computation is finite and correct. For a fourfold abelian variety, the even and odd total Betti numbers are both128. Thus numerator and denominator degrees of the zeta function are at most128. Its power series through degree256, including its constant term, determines that rational function uniquely: the cross-multiplied difference of two candidates has degree at most256 and vanishes to order at least257. Counts over extensions of degrees1 through256 determine the required series. The weights separate H1 from the other factors. Exact algebraic-number operations determine the fourth exterior-power polynomial and its cyclotomic factors. The stated bound m<=9800 follows from phi(m)<=70 and phi(m)^2>=m/2. For the latter inequality, each odd-prime-power factor p^a contributes p^(a-2)(p-1)^2>=1 to phi(m)^2/m; the possible 2-primary contribution is at least1/2.

Enumerating all cycles over all finite extensions and checking the ranks of whole finite-prefix intersection matrices terminates under Tate: these classes span the Tate space, whose pairing is nondegenerate. Retaining the full prefix matters because an isotropic cycle may be needed in a nonsingular Gram matrix. A nonsingular Gram matrix of rank equal to the Tate dimension gives a complete equality test for algebraic cycle classes in the chosen realization. This does not assume rational coordinates for an arbitrary l-adic vector: the target is an actual rational cycle.

The degree of an intersection is computed on a common field of definition. Extending that field does not multiply its geometric degree. The improper-intersection computation is established background: Poonen–Testa–van Luijk Lemma8.7 and Remark8.9 give the algorithm and the derived-Euler formula. R40a is now a cited background paragraph, not a new theorem or a purported new proof of that known result.

Finally, one point per geometric connected component of Q_C captures the class and degree of every effective numerator of degree<=C. Testing all ordered pairs and 1<=N<=C therefore gives the exact minimum. Under the supplied equality between numerical and homological equivalence in each additional realization, the same rational intersection tests give simultaneous equality. Without that additional input, the one chosen prime-to-characteristic realization is the precise assertion.

## R42: effective exhaustive parameter construction

The needed distinction is between the dimension of a surface and the dimension of its homogeneous coordinate ring. The latter is3. For its reduced integral homogeneous ideal I, arithmetic degree equals ordinary degree e. Hoa's theorem therefore yields

    reg I <= [e(e+1)/2]^2.

For an embedding by H^m and an original degree limit D, put E=m^2 D and R=[E(E+1)/2]^2. Every desired support satisfies reg(S/I)<=R-1. The Grothendieck–Serre relation between Hilbert function and Hilbert polynomial then gives equality in degrees R,R+1,R+2. Each of these three integer values is between0 and binom(n+R+j,n). Enumerating every such triple and interpolating is a finite exhaustive list of quadratic Hilbert polynomials. Keeping only leading coefficient e/2 with e/m^2 an integer between1 andD preserves all desired supports. Nonadmissible candidates give empty schemes; other extraneous candidates remain valid effective top-dimensional cycles of the recorded degree.

Full Hilbert schemes are essential. Taking only a reduced-support open locus would lose properness at boundary fibers. The Gotzmann construction is effective for every candidate polynomial and produces the universal family; it allows embedded or lower-dimensional boundary components, whose top-dimensional cycles remain effective and degree-labeled. The finite weighted products include every integer multiplicity and every integral support, including the empty cycle. Haiman–Sturmfels construct these schemes over an arbitrary commutative base, so the same list and equations work over the arithmetic model, not only over each field separately.

## R43: exact comparison with a supplied Betti marking

The input marking is a finite simplicial complex K0, an explicitly semialgebraic homeomorphism h0:|K0|->X(C) over the real algebraic numbers, and a rational4-cocycle tau. A basis given by explicit cocycles is equivalent. Merely computing an abstract group isomorphic to H4 would not permit comparison with an arbitrary input vector; the explicit marking resolves this issue.

For every geometric connected component of Q_C over Qbar, choose an algebraic point and compute its universal weighted top-dimensional cycle. Absolute decomposition and the incidence graph compute the connected components. A finite collection of chosen points and cycles is defined over a common finite number-field extension. Constancy of the relative cycle class on connected components, proved in B01, means that this finite list also captures cycles coming from transcendental complex parameter points.

The compact semialgebraic presentation of projective complex space uses rank-one Hermitian projection matrices. All selected supports, their singular loci, and the images of the input simplices are semialgebraic over real algebraic numbers. Basu's compatible triangulation theorem provides a common finite triangulation h:|K|->X(C).

The marking comparison has a concrete carrier construction. For each vertex v of K, find the minimal old simplex containing h0^(-1)h(v), and send v to any vertex of that simplex. Compatibility ensures that all vertices of each new simplex map into one closed old simplex, so this is a simplicial map F:K->K0. For each point, F and h0^(-1)h take values in a common closed simplex; linear interpolation gives a homotopy. Thus the computable pullback F*tau is the exact target cocycle. This addresses the comparison step rather than replacing it by an unmarked cohomology-group isomorphism.

For an integral surface support Y, the singular locus has real dimension at most2. Orient the4-simplices in its compatible subcomplex by the complex orientation on the smooth locus. Their sum is the integral fundamental cycle, since codimension-one faces lie in the smooth locus and cancel in pairs. Multiplicities are the generic lengths computed from the universal fiber, combined with the outer weights of Q_C.

There is an exact orientation algorithm, not a numerical-sign assumption. On each top simplex choose a real algebraic point at which the semialgebraic homeomorphism is differentiable with full rank and an algebraic coordinate projection gives an etale chart on the smooth support. Such points form a nonempty open semialgebraic set. Differentiability and its derivative can be expressed by first-order real formulas using the graph of the parametrization. Quantifier elimination finds a valid point and derivative, and the sign of its real Jacobian in complex-oriented coordinates fixes the simplex orientation. The same operation orients the8-dimensional fundamental cycle of X. Integer homology, rational cohomology and the cap product are finite matrix calculations thereafter.

Comparing z_i-z_j against N(F*tau cap[X]) in rational H4 gives an exact finite boundary test. Exhaustiveness of Q_C and the supplied upper bound C prove the returned minimum equals the actual geometric minimum and give equations for attaining cycles. No period zero test or general Hodge-membership oracle is assumed. R24 provides a prior finite cutoff when its lattice inputs are explicit. Alternatively, promised algebraicity alone would allow the finite bounded procedure to be run at C=1,2,... until the first success; that observation gives termination but no explicit prior cutoff.

## R44–R45: independent complete check

I read the full new section effective_exception.tex. The generic-flatness construction passes. For an affine chart R[x]/I, compute a monic Groebner basis G of I_K and record both directions of ideal-generator expressions. Clearing their finitely many coefficients by a positive rational integer makes I_R'=(G). Division by monic polynomials proves that the standard monomials span the quotient over R'. Any relation among them would give a relation over K, where G is a Groebner basis, so the coefficients vanish. This proves freeness over R', even when the basis is infinite. It avoids any need to compute the full R-torsion ideal by a nonconstructive step. No separate S-polynomial certificate is needed for this argument, although recording one is harmless. The I_K=(1) and I_K=(0) edge cases are handled explicitly. Taking the product over a finite affine cover gives a computable flattening integer.

Applied to the complete bounded parameter scheme, that integer is independent of the target class and its multiplier. B04 applies to each particular class locus and returns the same multiplier at a horizontal generization. Computing b_(C0-1) therefore excludes every local improvement outside a finite set. At the remaining places the already computed global cycle supplies the exact rational target and the upper bound C0; R40 gives the exact local values. The case C0=1 is handled separately. The specified good model and geometric-place marking prevent silently extending the output to reduction problems or descent data that were not supplied.
