## First search and its termination

Kerr–Yang's argument takes a rational bounding chain for the residue
class whose vanishing was proved in Proposition 2.13. The decomposition
of the relative complex produces W_i with exactly the support on
`p_i^{-1} V(f_{m-i})` stated in B.G1. Remark 3.4 explicitly permits the
same chains to be chosen in the rational real-proper subcomplex. These
assertions precede the explicit formulae of Section 4, so the search
does not depend on those formulae or their problematic denominator and
boundary claims.

For N=2 the graph in the proposed algorithm is precisely the graph in
that theorem: the involution t=z/(z-1) gives z=t/(t-1), and
f=1+t_1...t_{m-1}. All graph coordinates are defined over Q.

Enumerating finite rational polynomial descriptions with rational
multiplicities enumerates every such candidate. Codimension,
admissibility, support in the specified zero loci, and the finite
boundary identity are decidable algebraic tests. Real properness is a
finite semialgebraic dimension test after taking the finitely many
affine charts and writing complex coordinates as two real coordinates.
Negative-real cuts are polynomial equalities and inequalities over Q;
the deleted value 1 is an open algebraic condition. The exact standard
family uses J, k, and I={1,...,k} minus J, as now stated in the proof.

The existence theorem therefore proves that the exhaustive first
search eventually encounters an acceptable tuple. It is unnecessary
to decide whether a given residue is a boundary: the algorithm only
checks a proposed explicit bounding tuple.

## Integral closure and degeneracies

The cubical group before quotienting is free on admissible irreducible
subvarieties. Each degeneracy is the pullback of an irreducible cycle
through deletion of a coordinate; the pullback is again irreducible,
being its product with the affine line. The subgroup of degenerate
cycles is therefore generated by a subset of the basis. The quotient
is free on the nondegenerate basis elements. In particular, an
integral boundary which vanishes after tensoring with Q is already
zero integrally.

Consequently multiplying the rationally closed corrected cycle by an
actual common denominator D_m, and then by (m-1)!, produces the
integral closed cycle xi_m claimed in B.G2. The extra factorial is an
intentional scaling and not an assertion about an unknown denominator
in the correction-chain construction.

## Independent check of the regulator and index

Theorem 3.8 computes the graph regulator by an explicit iterated
integral and gives

\[
 c_{D,\mathbb Q}(\widetilde Z_m)
   =(-1)^m2^{m-1}\operatorname{Li}_m(-1).
\]

For odd m this is `(2^(m-1)-1) zeta(m)`, since
Li_m(-1)=-(1-2^(1-m))zeta(m). The correction supports give a direct
check: on W_i the corresponding t-product is -1, whereas on all
required initial negative-real cuts every t_j belongs to [0,1]. The
relevant intersections are empty, so the correction terms have zero
contribution to this regulator integral. This uses t-products, not
the misleading z-product sentence in the printed proof of Lemma 3.7.
It also avoids the inconsistent N=2 sentence after Corollary 3.9.

For odd m the complex Deligne ambiguity `(2 pi i)^m Q` is purely
imaginary. Real-part projection gives an actual real regulator value.
Flach–Morin use the same Beilinson regulator on the motivic complex,
with the target lattice `(2 pi i)^(m-1) Z`. Its covolume on a positive
primitive integral class g is therefore

\[
 R_m=\rho(g)/(2\pi)^{m-1}.
\]

There is no Chern-character-to-K-theory change of normalization in this
comparison: both maps are maps from motivic cohomology, so no additional
factorial may be inserted. The target is the real Deligne coordinate
for Spec Q, so no extra factor two appears from choosing one of a pair
of complex embeddings.

Equation (88), known for Q by Proposition 5.35, and the elementary
zeta functional equation give

\[
 \frac{|C_m|\rho(g)}{2(2\pi)^{m-1}}
 =|\zeta'(1-m)|
 =\frac{(m-1)!\zeta(m)}{2(2\pi)^{m-1}}.
\]

Here w_m=2: complex conjugation acts as -1 on the odd twist, so
Galois invariants in Q/Z(m) are killed by two, while the order-two
subgroup is invariant. It follows that

\[
 \rho(g)=(m-1)!\zeta(m)/|C_m|,
 \qquad
 [\xi_m]_{\rm free}=h_m[g],
 \quad h_m=D_m(2^{m-1}-1)|C_m|.
\]

This independently agrees with B.G3–B.G4. The index is determined by
exact integers, not by numerical recognition of a transcendental
regulator ratio.

## Localization and the saturation search

For m>=3, the localization sequence from Spec Z to Spec Q has adjacent
groups H^{-1}(Fp,Z(m-1)) and H^0(Fp,Z(m-1)), which vanish in the
positive twist m-1. Thus B.G5 is an integral isomorphism in the stated
degree. Flach–Morin explicitly give integral Zariski-motivic to etale
comparison in the required degree, citing Geisser's Theorem 1.2.

Take any integral representative gamma of the positive primitive free
class. The difference `h_m[gamma]-[xi_m]` belongs to the torsion
subgroup, whose exponent is two. By the definition of higher Chow
homology, there exists an integral chain eta with

\[
 \partial\eta=2(h_m\gamma-\xi_m).
\]

The exhaustive search among integral admissible pairs therefore
terminates. Conversely, any pair passing this finite boundary test
satisfies the equality in the free quotient, and the proved index
h_m forces gamma to be primitive. The returned eta is a certificate
of the saturation step. Gamma and eta need not be real-proper; the
search correctly takes place in the full integral cubical complex.
No integral real-proper moving lemma is required.
