# The abelian sector over the algebraic closure of a finite field

This section completes the base-field passage left separate in CC-AG01--03. It is a scoped application of the classical Frobenius-germ description, using the all-abelian Tate inputs already recorded in CC-TATE01--02 and AQ-ABELIAN-RECOGNITION. It does not assume the Tate conjecture for arbitrary smooth projective varieties and makes no claim of novelty.

Fix $\bar k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, $\ell\ne p$, $E=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell$, and $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}\hookrightarrow E$. Let $\mathcal A_{\bar k}$ be the rational numerical category generated by abelian varieties over $\bar k$, finite étale schemes, and Tate motives, under finite sums, tensor products, duals, and direct summands. Set $$\mathcal A_{\bar k,E}
 =\operatorname{Kar}(\mathcal A_{\bar k}\otimes_{\mathbb Q}E).$$ Use the parity-corrected symmetry, as in CC-AG01. Over $\bar k$, finite étale motives are sums of the unit.

The primary precedent is Milne (1994), in the corrected author version dated 2 November 2019: Theorem 1.18(a), p. 11, gives the realization into germs; Remark 2.26, p. 22, identifies the associated Weil-number protorus; and Remark 2.29, p. 24, describes the torsion-free germ group. Those statements occur with the full Tate hypothesis displayed on p. 11. The proofs below restrict the argument to the established abelian sector and explicitly check descent and the morphisms.

## CC-AG04: descent of the category and its morphisms

Write $k_a=\mathbb F_{p^a}$, with the positive integers ordered by divisibility. For $a\mid b$, the transition functor is extension of the base field $k_a\subset k_b$. There are tensor equivalences $$\underset{a}{\operatorname{2-colim}}\mathcal A_{p^a}
 \ \simeq\ \mathcal A_{\bar k},
 \qquad
 \underset{a}{\operatorname{2-colim}}\mathcal A_{p^a,E}
 \ \simeq\ \mathcal A_{\bar k,E}.                 \tag{CC-AG.5}$$ Here the filtered categorical colimit can be described explicitly: objects are objects at finite stages, and the morphisms between two such objects are the filtered colimit of their morphism spaces after passing to common finite extensions. In particular, for models $M_a,N_a$ over $k_a$, $$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_{\bar k}}(M_{\bar k},N_{\bar k})
 =
 \underset{a\mid b}{\operatorname{colim}}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_{p^b}}(M_b,N_b).   \tag{CC-AG.6}$$ The same formula holds after extending Hom spaces to $E$ and for their idempotent corners. The underlying $\ell$-adic realization therefore exists and is faithful on this abelian category over $\bar k$.

#### Complete proof.

First check numerical equivalence under extension of the base field. Let $X/k$ be smooth projective, with $k$ finite, and let $K/k$ be finite of degree $d$. Its projection $f:X_K\to X$ is finite étale. For a rational cycle $z$ on $X$ and any complementary cycle $v$ on $X_K$, the projection formula and the definition of degree give $$d\,\deg_K(f^*z\cdot v)
 =\deg_k f_*(f^*z\cdot v)
 =\deg_k(z\cdot f_*v).                           \tag{CC-AG.7}$$ Consequently $z$ numerically trivial implies $f^*z$ numerically trivial. Conversely, for a complementary cycle $u$ on $X$, $$\deg_K(f^*z\cdot f^*u)=\deg_k(z\cdot u).$$ The equality follows from compatibility of intersection with flat pullback and preservation of degree under extension of the ground field; on a closed point this is the equality of dimensions after tensoring its residue field with $K$. Thus numerical triviality is also reflected by this pullback. These cycle facts are precisely the projection and pullback formulas of Stacks, Lemmas 42.62.3--42.62.4 (Tags 0FFB--0FFC), and the degree formula 42.41.2 (Tag 0AZ2).

The same equivalence holds for $\bar k/k$: every complementary cycle over $\bar k$ descends to a finite extension, so the finite calculation applies. Cycle descent is Stacks, Lemma 45.8.2 (Tag 0FH6), or its detailed limit statement 42.67.10 (Tag 0FVQ). We have proved, in particular, that pullback on rational numerical cycle spaces is injective.

Every abelian variety over $\bar k$, together with its group law, identity, inverse, and a projective embedding, is defined over a finite subfield. Indeed these are finitely presented schemes, morphisms, and finitely many equations between their compositions; Stacks, Lemma 32.10.1 (Tag 01ZM), descends them simultaneously. Smoothness is detected after the faithfully flat extension to $\bar k$, projectivity is supplied by the descended embedding, and geometric connectedness is detected on the algebraic closure. Thus the descended object is an abelian variety. Finite tensor constructions and Tate twists descend as well.

A rational numerical correspondence is represented by a finite rational combination of cycles. All those cycles descend to one finite field. Equalities between the resulting numerical correspondences are reflected at that stage by the injectivity proved above. In particular, an idempotent over $\bar k$ descends as an actual numerical idempotent, not merely as a representative cycle that might fail its idempotent equation. Taking its image gives the descended direct summand. This proves descent of every object, and the same cycle argument proves (CC-AG.6), including injectivity and compatibility with composition and tensor product. Hence it proves the first equivalence of (CC-AG.5).

Tensoring a filtered colimit of rational vector spaces with $E$ commutes with that colimit. An $E$-coefficient correspondence has only finitely many rational cycle classes in any expression for it, so its field of definition can again be enlarged to one finite stage. The previously injective maps remain injective after tensoring with $E$. An idempotent equation over $E$ is therefore reflected there too. For idempotents $e,f$, its Hom space is the corner $f\operatorname{Hom}e$; the same argument applies to these corners. This proves the second equivalence.

Finally, numerical and $\ell$-homological equivalence agree on the finite-field abelian products by the cited all-abelian input. Their agreement over $\bar k$ follows by descending a cycle and using numerical injectivity and the unchanged geometric cohomology. The resulting realization is faithful at each finite stage, hence on the displayed colimit and its $E$-coefficient idempotent completion. $\square$

## CC-AG05: the actual category of Weil germs

Use the groups $W(p^a)$ from CC-AG01 and set $$\mathcal W_p=\underset{a\mid b}{\operatorname{colim}}
 \left(W(p^a),\ \alpha\longmapsto\alpha^{b/a}\right).
                                                        \tag{CC-AG.8}$$ Write $[\alpha,a]$ for a germ. Explicitly, $[\alpha,a]=[\beta,b]$ if and only if $\alpha^{bN}=\beta^{aN}$ for some positive integer $N$. There is a symmetric monoidal equivalence compatible with the underlying cohomological fibre functor: $$\mathcal A_{\bar k,E}\ \simeq\
 \operatorname{Gr}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\mathcal W_p}(E).
                                                        \tag{CC-AG.9}$$ The target consists of finite-dimensional vector spaces graded by $\mathcal W_p$. In particular, the germ transition is realized by the actual numerical category, including all its morphisms.

Choose $s=\sqrt p$ in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. With $W_0(p)$ as in CC-AG02, there are isomorphisms $$\mathcal W_p
 \simeq \mathbb Z\oplus\bigl(W_0(p)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\bigr)
 \simeq \mathbb Z\oplus\bigl(W_0(p)/\mu_\infty\bigr).
                                                        \tag{CC-AG.10}$$ The weight is the $\mathbb Z$-coordinate, and the Tate object has coordinates $(-2,0)$, writing the second factor additively. Consequently, for the underlying-vector-space fibre functor $\omega_E$, $$\operatorname{Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega_E)
 \simeq D_E(\mathcal W_p)
 \simeq \mathbb G_{m,E}\times
 D_E\bigl(W_0(p)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q\bigr). \tag{CC-AG.11}$$ This group is a protorus.

#### Complete proof.

An object defined over $k_a$ has the semisimple Frobenius eigenspace decomposition of CC-AG01. Give its $\alpha$-eigenspace the label $[\alpha,a]$, combining eigenspaces with the same germ. Under $k_a\subset k_b$, geometric Frobenius becomes its $b/a$-th power. The unchanged geometric cohomology therefore has exactly the relabeling prescribed in (CC-AG.8). CC-AG04 makes this construction independent of a chosen model.

We verify full faithfulness, including the maps newly available over $\bar k$. Take models of two objects over one common $k_a$. A linear map between their realized vector spaces respects germ labels precisely when each of its nonzero blocks between Frobenius eigenspaces has eigenvalues $\alpha,\beta$ with $(\alpha/\beta)^r=1$ for some positive $r$. Only finitely many eigenvalue pairs occur. Choose a common multiple of their root-of-unity orders. The linear map then commutes with that power of Frobenius. By the full faithfulness in CC-AG01 over the corresponding finite extension, it is induced by a unique correspondence with $E$-coefficients at that stage. Conversely, every correspondence over $\bar k$ descends to a finite stage by CC-AG04, so commutes with the corresponding Frobenius power and preserves the germ labels. Faithfulness was proved in CC-AG04. This proves the required isomorphism on every Hom space.

Every finite list of germs has representatives at a common finite stage. CC-AG01 realizes each representative as a rank-one object there, after extending coefficients and taking the idempotent completion. Direct sums realize every finite-dimensional graded vector space. This proves essential surjectivity. Tensor products multiply eigenvalues and therefore multiply germs. The corrected symmetry realizes as ordinary interchange, proving (CC-AG.9).

For (CC-AG.10), write an element at stage $a$ uniquely as $\alpha=s^{aw}u$ with $u\in W_0(p)$. In these coordinates the transition $a\to b$ is $$(w,u)\longmapsto(w,u^{b/a}).$$ Its colimit is $\mathbb Z\oplus(W_0(p)\otimes\mathbb Q)$: the second coordinate of the stage-$a$ element is $u\otimes a^{-1}$, in the additive tensor notation for this abelian group. These maps are compatible and give the standard localization of an abelian group by all positive integers.

The group $W_0(p)$ is divisible: any algebraic $n$-th root of a $p$-unit all of whose conjugates have absolute value one has those same two properties. Its torsion is exactly the group of all roots of unity. Thus $W_0(p)/\mu_\infty$ is divisible and torsion-free, and the quotient map identifies it with $W_0(p)\otimes\mathbb Q$. This also checks (CC-AG.10) directly. The Tate eigenvalue at stage $a$ is $p^{-a}$, giving the specified coordinates.

For a finite grading category, the tensor automorphism calculation in CC-AG02 applies verbatim: naturality forces a scalar on each label, and tensor compatibility makes the scalars a homomorphism $\mathcal W_p\to R^\times$ for every $E$-algebra $R$. The representing Hopf algebra is $E[\mathcal W_p]$, proving (CC-AG.11). The group $\mathcal W_p$ is torsion-free. Each of its finitely generated subgroups is free of finite rank, so its diagonalizable group is the inverse limit of the corresponding split tori. It is therefore a protorus, as claimed. $\square$

## CC-AG06: base extension is the identity-component inclusion

Let $P_{a,E}=D_E(W(p^a))$ be the fixed-field group of CC-AG02, and let $P_{\bar k,E}=D_E(\mathcal W_p)$. Extension from $k_a$ to $\bar k$ induces a closed immersion $$P_{\bar k,E}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow P_{a,E},$$ whose image is the identity component. There is an exact sequence of affine group schemes, with faithfully flat last map, $$1\longrightarrow P_{\bar k,E}\longrightarrow P_{a,E}
 \longrightarrow D_E(\mu_\infty)\longrightarrow1. \tag{CC-AG.12}$$ With the fixed embedding $\iota$, the last group is the constant profinite group scheme $\widehat{\mathbb Z}_E$. The extension functor $\mathcal A_{p^a,E}\to\mathcal A_{\bar k,E}$ is essentially surjective; its additional morphisms merge exactly the finite-field labels that differ by a root of unity.

#### Complete proof.

The character map $j_a:W(p^a)\to\mathcal W_p$ is $\alpha\mapsto[\alpha,a]$. Its kernel is $\mu_\infty$: the germ is the identity if and only if some positive power of $\alpha$ is one. It is surjective. Indeed, for any $[\beta,b]$, choose $\alpha$ with $\alpha^b=\beta^a$. This $\alpha$ is a $p$-unit, and if $\beta$ has weight $w$ relative to $p^b$, every conjugate of $\alpha$ has absolute value $p^{aw/2}$. Hence $\alpha\in W(p^a)$, and the displayed equality identifies the two germs. Thus $$W(p^a)/\mu_\infty\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal W_p. \tag{CC-AG.13}$$ Under (CC-AG.9), extension of fields pushes a grading forward along $j_a$, combining the vector spaces with equal images. Surjectivity and the rank-one occurrence theorem at that fixed stage prove essential surjectivity and the assertion about maps.

The contravariant group-algebra construction turns (CC-AG.13) into the displayed closed immersion. The inclusion $\mu_\infty\subset W(p^a)$ gives the last map of (CC-AG.12). It is faithfully flat because a group algebra is a nonzero free module over the group algebra of a subgroup, with any set of coset representatives as basis. Its kernel has coordinate algebra $$E[W(p^a)]\otimes_{E[\mu_\infty]}E
 =E[W(p^a)/\mu_\infty],$$ which proves exactness, equivalently exactness for the fpqc topology.

This kernel is connected. Its character group is torsion-free, so its group algebra is a filtered union of Laurent polynomial domains, with injective transition maps, and is itself a domain. Conversely, every connected subset containing the identity in $P_{a,E}$ maps to the identity in each finite étale quotient $D_E(\mu_n)$. It is therefore contained in the kernel. The kernel is precisely the connected component of the identity.

Finally, $D_E(\mu_\infty)=\varprojlim_n D_E(\mu_n)$. The embedding $\iota$ identifies $D_E(\mu_n)$ with the constant group $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ by $r:\zeta\mapsto\iota(\zeta)^r$; these identifications are compatible with divisibility and the reduction transition maps. This proves the component-group assertion. $\square$

For completeness, the distinguished fixed-field Frobenius point maps to $1\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ in (CC-AG.12), since its character on a root of unity $\zeta$ is $\iota(\zeta)$. No positive integer power of this universal point lies in the identity component: its component is that positive integer in $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, which is nonzero. On each specified finite collection of objects a sufficiently divisible power does kill the finitely many root-of-unity ratios involved. Thus the geometric category is correctly described by germs; one does not obtain a single universal finite-field Frobenius point inside its protorus.

#### Exact scope for original cote 15.

The field/germ transition and the identity-component statement are now established for actual abelian-generated rational numerical motives after the displayed coefficient extension. The historical norm-one group is $W_0(p)$, as independently recovered in AQ-WEIL-NOTATION, so (CC-AG.11) gives the precise coefficient-extended abelian-sector version of its proposed group. The assertion that *every* numerical motive lies in this sector is not proved here. The full Tate hypothesis in Milne's general theory, or the separately established cross-cycle criterion for a specified variety, remains distinct. Nor does this argument supply a fibre functor over $\mathbb Q$, lift numerical projectors to Chow projectors, or remove the rational quaternion obstruction.
