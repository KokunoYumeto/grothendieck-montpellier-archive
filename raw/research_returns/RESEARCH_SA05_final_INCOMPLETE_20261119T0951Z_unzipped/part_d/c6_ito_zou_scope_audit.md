## 2. Why the purity theorem descends to K

After a common finite extension, total Galois cohomology takes values
in its Mumford–Tate group G. The degree-two map G→G_2 is a central
isogeny whose kernel is generated by parity. Therefore H^i⊗H^i
factors through G_2 and is a direct summand of finite sums of mixed
tensors in H² with net tensor degree i. Degree-two WD purity then
makes this tensor square pure of weight 2i.

For a WD representation V, the monodromy filtration of V⊗V is the
convolution filtration. An eigenvalue alpha on gr_j(V) has alpha²
on gr_(2j)(V⊗V). Purity of the square therefore forces alpha to be
of weight i+j. This is Lemma 4.3, which proves purity of V.

Restriction to a finite extension multiplies the logarithm of
monodromy by a nonzero scalar and replaces a suitable Frobenius by
a positive power. Neither operation changes the asserted normalized
weights. Hence purity descends. This justifies applying Theorem 4.4
at the original place, unlike unramifiedness itself.

## 3. An unconditional consequence for C.6 with Chow projectors

Let Y be a finite product of hyper-Kähler varieties over a number
field and let e be a rational algebraic correspondence, defined over
that field, which is idempotent and cohomologically homogeneous.
Allow finite sums, duals and Tate twists. Let M be the resulting pure
rational Chow motive. At a fixed finite place v of residue
characteristic p, unramifiedness of M_ell for one ell≠p implies
unramifiedness for every ell≠p.

Proof: Theorem 4.4, tensor stability of pure WD representations,
and Künneth show that the relevant homogeneous ambient cohomology
has weight–monodromy at every ell. At the unramified realization
ell_0 its monodromy is zero, so Frobenius on the summand has its
single expected weight. The already proved Saito/Chow-projector
reduction in c6_chow_conditional.tex then applies **over the original
base field**: its algebraic-correspondence trace theorem supplies
common projected Weil traces; the single-weight spectrum forces
N_ell=0, and finite-inertia character traces force trivial inertia.
No descent of unramifiedness through a ramified extension is used.

This is a genuinely new special-family instance of the conditional
Chow proposition after inserting the 6 October theorem. It is not a
solution of the general-motive question. It also does not supply
common projected traces for an arbitrary absolute-Hodge or motivated
projector on a hyper-Kähler variety outside the abelian-type scope.
Mumford–Tate equality by itself does not assert that such a projector
is an algebraic correspondence.

## 4. What the strong compatibility statement does not give alone

Even common trivial inertia over a finite extension only proves
potential unramifiedness over K. A finite nontrivial inertia action
can disappear over that extension. Theorem 5.8 does not remove this
issue in its general formulation. In particular, one cannot combine
its finite-extension conclusion with a statement about one original
unramified realization and omit the remaining finite-inertia step.

The proof does control the common quadratic parity character once
its connectedness and Kuga–Satake hypotheses hold. This is stronger
than an unspecified ell-dependent finite twist, but it still does
not establish the arbitrary original-base G-valued descent that
the theorem itself does not claim.

For C.4, Theorem 4.4 gives the expected local bound
weights(ker N)≤i for every homogeneous hyper-Kähler cohomology
representation and its tensor summands. Hyper-Kähler varieties have
even dimension, so this does not settle the general threefold H³
weight–monodromy gate identified elsewhere.
