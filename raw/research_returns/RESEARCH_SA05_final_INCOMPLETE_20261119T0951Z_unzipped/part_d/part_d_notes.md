## D.1: distinction of the four groups and complete Weil-number result

The schematic quotient of Res(O_k/Z)Gm by the closure of positive units is representable by Anantharaman's Theorem 4.C, is flat, and has connected geometric fibres. The narrow-class extension is built from ideal-isomorphism torsors. We deliberately use Isom(a_c,O_k), not the inverse convention: with a_c a_d=t_cd a_{c+d}, multiplication is (u⊗v)∘[t_cd], giving Serre's generic cocycle. This establishes the integral model without confusing it with a profinite group.

A fixed finite-modulus Serre torus can differ integrally from the standard connected Serre torus. Over Q(i), quotienting by all units μ₄ gives character lattice {(a,b):a−b≡0 mod4}; this splits as the trivial plus sign lattice, while the standard permutation lattice does not. The groups are isogenous but not isomorphic. A fixed real-only modulus also excludes finite conductor data.

We first found the usual residual-reflex obstruction to lifting a simple finite-field isogeny class over its exact field, but did not stop there, because the question asks for eigenvalues and permits multiplicity. The counterexample π=2ζ₅ over F₄ obstructs a normal CM lift of the simple surface; it does not obstruct the eigenvalue. The square admits a lift using Q(ζ₅,√−3) and the CM type induced from Q(√−3). This distinction led to the general power-lifting lemma.

The proof of that lemma enlarges a maximal CM field in End⁰(A₀) as follows. At p enlarge the local field so its ramification degree is divisible by all slope denominators, and enlarge unramifiedly to split the local Galois sequence. Globalize the resulting local Galois field as a completion of a totally real Galois field using weak approximation, Hensel and Krasner. Adjoin an imaginary quadratic field in which p splits, so conjugation lies outside every relevant decomposition group. In each decomposition coset select Frobenius-subgroup orbits in the exact proportions prescribed by the slopes, and choose the complementary type on the conjugate coset. The stabilizer contains the lifted Frobenius, so the reflex residue degree is one. A right-module calculation embeds the enlarged CM field in End⁰(A₀^m). Chai–Conrad–Oort's residual-reflex theorem now gives the number-field CM lift, full CM action, exact finite residue field, and CM-linear isogeny.

The root agent independently audited these four substantive construction steps and found them sound, subject to the precise CCO theorem convention. We then verified CCO §1.4 equation (1), Theorem 1.6 and Proposition 5.4 directly: the slope is the fraction of tangent CM embeddings inducing the place, and the conclusion indeed has the full action defined over the number field and residue exactly F_q. The lemma does not invoke the 2026 CM Hodge theorem.

Every α∈Γ_q follows from this lemma: choose a≥0 with β=αq^a integral and b=w(α)+2a>0; any b-th root γ of β is an integral weight-one q-Weil number. Honda–Tate supplies A₀, the power-lifting lemma supplies B, and (H¹B)^⊗b(a) contains eigenvalue α. This works in weight zero, negative weights, odd weights and at p=2. It does not claim one fixed CM field, primitive type, modulus or number field works for all α.

The gerbe bridge remains a Q-linear reduction map from the geometric CM category to abelian motives over the algebraic closure of F_p. Its dual reverses direction. It is not reduction of the coefficient-group scheme modulo p. ## D.2 and D.3: precise category gates

For abelian-type absolute-Hodge and motivated motives, the commutative identity-component criterion is unconditional. A toric motivic identity component forces a CM Hodge structure. CM Hodge structures occur in the Hodge category generated by CM abelian varieties. Deligne/André full faithfulness lifts the isomorphism; finite descent followed by induction puts the original motive in the Artin/potentially-CM category. For arbitrary motives, the exact missing assertion is a Hodge-class comparison between that motive and a CM abelian motive. The requested equivalence with this restricted absolute-Hodge/motivated comparison assertion is proved in both directions. We do not replace it by an unproved claim that every geometric motivic group is connected, and we do not confuse the 2026 CM Hodge theorem with a theorem about classes on an arbitrary X times a CM variety. The November 2026 Kahn–Otsubo paper was checked for its analogous invertible-motive comparison condition.

The full absolute Galois group, rather than its abelianization, is the quotient in the Taniyama extension. This supplies all Artin motives, including the explicit faithful S₄ permutation motive. Thus the Taniyama category is the Artin/potentially-CM category, while its metabelian subcategory is the representation category of the maximal metabelian quotient. The general-motive inclusion in this subcategory has exactly the comparison gate from D.2(a); no stronger unconditional classification is asserted.

For D.3 the exact resolution 1→Res_F Gm→Res_K Gm×Gm→S^K→1 gives H¹(Q,S^K)=ker[Br(F)→Br(K)⊕Br(Q)]. Finite-local triviality leaves real invariants ε_v/2 with even sum. The norm-one torus used in R3 is not S^K, but the sequence 1→Gm→S^K→U→1 identifies their finite-place kernels. The form-classification assertion uses the full geometric neutral CM category with band S^K. Arithmetic Artin data require the actual arithmetic group. For F=Q(√5), K=F(i), the class (−1,−1)_F has nontrivial invariants at both real places and trivial invariants at every finite place. This produces a genuinely different rational fibre functor with all finite comparisons. ## D.4: exact image, not only its dimension

For the exponent matrix A=1_Σ, the character lattice is Z^{I_E}/ker(Aᵗ), identified with im(Aᵗ), and the cocharacter lattice is the saturation of im A. The rank determines dimension. Conjugate row-pair sums impose the norm-compatible torus; equality is equivalent to rank g+1 or to absence of any rational row relations beyond differences of conjugate pair sums. This is a necessary and sufficient effective criterion for every type, not a list of generic examples. The compact image has lattice im(Aᵗ)/Z·1 and dimension rank A−1. The weight direction meets it in μ₂, so there is no spurious integral product decomposition.

The Galois orbit of the Hodge cocharacter gives the matrix columns. Therefore the image is the smallest Q-torus containing that cocharacter, which proves its equality with the Mumford–Tate group for all types. For abelian Galois fields, character diagonalization gives an explicit odd-character Fourier criterion. The program computes full and degenerate cases exactly.
