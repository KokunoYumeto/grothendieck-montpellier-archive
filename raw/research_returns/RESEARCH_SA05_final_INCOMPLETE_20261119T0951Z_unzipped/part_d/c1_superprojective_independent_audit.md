# Independent audit: finite mixed-parity criterion under C⁺(X)

Status: the proposed argument passes, with the hypotheses and qualifications
below. This is an independent mathematical audit of Part C's proposed proof,
not a claim that C⁺(X) or the unrestricted classification has been proved.
Date: 2026-11-19, approximately 05:05–05:15 UTC.

## 1. Category and actual hypothesis

Let X/C be smooth projective connected of dimension n>0, o a rational
degree-one zero-cycle, and A=H^*(X,Q) with superdimension a|b. Retain a
separate even Tate line L. Work with G=GL(a|b)×G_m and with even morphisms
in its category R of rational superrepresentations for which
ε=(diag(1_a,−1_b),1) acts as the intrinsic parity operator. Every tensor
built from A,A∨,L,L∨ belongs to R. So do the structure-tensor space W,
its coordinate algebra, the relation ideal, and its finite submodules.

The additional assumption is C⁺(X): some rational algebraic
correspondence u on X realizes cohomological parity. This need not be
an idempotent or involution in Chow, and need not be expressible by the
point-and-diagonal tensors. The proof needs neither stronger property.
This distinction is essential: c1_product_parity_audit.md proves that
tautological parity is not forced by the requested relations.

## 2. The finite ideal is a super-scheme ideal

Write t=(μ,1,trace,o,β⁻¹) in the finite superrepresentation W. Define

       I=ker[O(W) → O(G)],       f ↦ (g ↦ f(g·t)).

This is the ideal of the *schematic super-orbit closure*. It must not
be replaced by the ideal of the orbit of ordinary rational or complex
points: that loses odd directions and need not be G-stable.

O(W)=Sym(W_even∨)⊗Λ(W_odd∨) is finite as a module over its ordinary
polynomial subalgebra, so it is noetherian. Choose finitely many ideal
generators and enlarge their span to a finite G-stable supermodule
E⊂I. This is finite because the linear G-action preserves polynomial
degree. There is no reductivity or exact-invariants assertion here.

The ideal can in principle be computed by elimination. The coordinate
algebra of GL(a|b) uses the even matrix blocks, odd off-diagonal blocks,
and inverses of the two even block determinants. The odd variables form
a finite exterior algebra, so the calculation reduces to finite-module
polynomial elimination. Only finite existence and termination are
claimed; no practical complexity bound is asserted.

## 3. Rational finite projective-injective covers

Put g=Lie(G), g_0=Lie(G_even), and B=U(g_0). If E is a finite
rational G-module, then

       P=U(g)⊗_B E|_(G_even) → E,      v⊗e ↦ v·e,

is a finite-dimensional surjection. PBW gives rank 2^(2ab) over B.
The natural G_even-action and integral torus weights make P an
algebraic G-module over Q. This integration follows from
Masuoka–Shibata, Proposition 5.4, pp. 23–24, or Theorem 5.8 and
Corollary 5.10, p. 26; the characteristic-zero identity hy(G)=U(g)
is explicitly stated on p. 22. Their Corollary 3.6, p. 9 is the
needed PBW statement. All these exact passages and the full
Proposition 5.4 proof were opened and read.

Induction is left adjoint to the exact restriction functor. Since
G_even=GL_a×GL_b×G_m is reductive in characteristic zero, its finite
rational representations are semisimple. Thus P is projective.

It is also injective. Here the usual induction/coinduction twist is
trivial:

 g_1=Hom(Q^a,Q^b)⊕Hom(Q^b,Q^a),
 det(g_1)=det(Q^a)^(−b)det(Q^b)^a
          ·det(Q^a)^b det(Q^b)^(−a)=1,
 dim(g_1)=2ab is even.

For completeness, choose an ordered odd PBW basis and let φ:U(g)→B
take its top exterior coefficient. The determinant identity makes φ
a B-bimodule map. The pairing (v,w)↦φ(vw) is nondegenerate: in PBW
bases, complementary exterior monomials pair to ±1, and all correction
terms are triangular in exterior degree. Hence U(g) identifies with
its B-linear dual as a (U(g),B)-bimodule, with no character or parity
twist. Tensoring with E gives Ind(E)≅Coind(E). Coinduction is right
adjoint to exact restriction and E is injective over G_even, so P is
injective. These constructions respect ε-parity, hence remain in R.

This argument is over Q and works also when a<b. It therefore avoids
any unspoken algebraic-closure or ordering hypothesis in a citation
to Heidersdorf's Theorem 1.1. That theorem was nevertheless separately
opened: it states that every projective in the specified category is
a mixed tensor. Its category R is defined in §2, pp. 3–4; its fullness
statement is Theorem 3.2, p. 6.

## 4. Why the projectives are actual mixed-tensor summands

The standard representation and its dual generate the rational
representations in R by subquotients of finite sums of mixed tensors.
An elementary matrix-coefficient proof must retain parity: the full
superrepresentation category splits as R⊕ΠR by the involution
intrinsic-parity·ρ(ε). Coefficient functions are generated by the
entries of the standard and inverse supermatrices. The usual
coaction embedding therefore realizes any finite representation as a
subquotient of sums of mixed tensors and their parity shifts. Taking
the exact R-summand discards the incompatible parity shifts. The
extra G_m factor is handled by the integral powers of L.

Now write a finite projective-injective P as U/V with U⊂T, where T is
a finite sum of mixed tensors. Projectivity splits U↠P, giving an
embedding P⊂T. Injectivity then retracts this embedding. Thus P is
an actual direct summand of T over Q. P∨ is automatically another
mixed-tensor summand, without needing a separate projectivity claim.

These splittings can be found by a terminating rational search:
enumerate mixed tensor sums and solve the rational intertwiner
equations for an embedding and a retraction. Existence proves
termination. Their matrices are defined over Q, as are all Lie and
torus equations. A projective object P₀≠0 may be fixed once and for
all, for example Ind(1).

## 5. Polynomial supercovariants really are diagrams

Lehrer–Zhang's Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2 give the GL(a|b) FFT;
Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.5 give its kernel: the tensor ideal
generated by the Young symmetrizer with a+1 rows and b+1 columns.
These statements are in §2 of the orthosymplectic paper, but are
explicitly theorems for the *general linear* supergroup. The article's
later orthosymplectic theorem is not used in their place.

For a fixed finite polynomial degree, the supersymmetric power of W∨
is the image of the ordinary averaging idempotent for the signed
symmetric-group action on (W∨)^⊗d. This idempotent is G-equivariant
in characteristic zero. Consequently polynomial supercovariants
reduce to mixed-tensor invariant maps by this split inclusion, and
the FFT writes them as contractions. Appendix A, Proposition A.1
also gives the explicit superpolarization statement. No exactness
of supergroup invariants is needed. The complex statements descend
to Q because all invariant spaces, diagram maps and Young
symmetrizers are defined by rational linear equations; equality can
be checked after the faithfully flat extension Q→C.

The loop value is δ=a−b. Together with the single rectangular Schur
relation S_((b+1)^(a+1))(A)=0 it gives the universal mixed-tensor
relations. This includes any identities needed for a rational
mixed-tensor projector to become an idempotent after evaluation in
Chow. Thus one must impose the rectangular relation *before* using
its corresponding Chow direct summands.

## 6. Lifting after one fixed projective tensor factor

The composite P↠E↪I is represented by the polynomial covariant
p_P in (P∨⊗O(W))^G. Embed P∨ as a split mixed-tensor summand. The
components of p_P then supply a finite list of diagram relations.

Let F in (T'⊗I)^G be any other polynomial covariant vanishing at t.
The map P⊗O(W)↠I is surjective. Tensoring with T' and P₀ gives a
surjection onto T'⊗I⊗P₀. Regard F⊗id_(P₀) as a G-map

       P₀ → T'⊗I⊗P₀.

Projectivity of P₀ lifts it through T'⊗P⊗O(W)⊗P₀. For the locally
finite infinite-dimensional coordinate algebra, choose preimages of
a finite basis of its image, place them in a finite G-submodule,
and apply projectivity to that finite-dimensional surjection. Thus
the finite-representation projectivity theorem is sufficient.

By §5 the lift is a finite diagram. Composing it with p_P expresses
F⊗id_(P₀) in the tensor ideal generated by the finite chosen
relations, modulo the rectangular universal relation. This is the
central point that replaces the invalid exact-invariants argument.

## 7. Cancellation uses C⁺, not tautological parity

Evaluate the imposed relations in Chow motives, and let
P₀(hX)=image(e:T(hX)→T(hX)), which is defined after the rectangular
relation has made e idempotent. The algebraic correspondence u
realizing parity on hX induces u_T on every mixed tensor sum and
acts as the identity on the even Tate line. Define

       u₀=e u_T e on P₀(hX).

Its Betti realization is the parity map on the nonzero superspace
P₀(A), since the even realization of e commutes with parity. Hence

       tr(u₀)=supertrace(parity on P₀(A))
             =dim_Q(P₀(A)_even)+dim_Q(P₀(A)_odd)>0.

The scalar trace in rational Chow motives equals this rational
number: realization is injective on End(1)=Q. If
F(hX)⊗id_(P₀(hX))=0, compose with id⊗u₀ and take the categorical
partial trace. The result is tr(u₀)F(hX)=0, so F(hX)=0.

Neither u²=1 in Chow nor commutation of u_T with e in Chow is
required. The sandwich e u_T e is always an endomorphism of the
summand, and its trace is determined by its Betti realization.

## 8. Necessity and scope of the finite criterion

The finite list consists of the two normalization relations
c_n(TX)=χ(X)o and Δ_*o=o⊠o, the rectangular Schur relation, and the
finitely many p_P component relations. Every one has zero
cohomology. The rectangle is a linear combination of permutation
graphs on one finite power. Once the normalizations hold, the
Frobenius graph reduction expresses each p_P component as a finite
combination of the specified pointed partial diagonals. Consequently
all requested relations imply every condition in the finite list.

Conversely, the finite list gives all diagram relations by §§6–7,
and therefore all requested point-and-diagonal relations. This is a
genuine finite necessary-and-sufficient criterion **within the class
of X satisfying C⁺(X)**. C⁺ is only a sufficiency tool and is not
claimed to follow from those finite conditions or from the complete
infinite family of narrow relations. There is no Kimura-finiteness
assumption hidden in the argument, and no claim of priority.

build W_Q and R_Q=O(W_Q) over Q, and choose bases of the realization
and its Tate line. The structure tensors give an even point t∈W(K).
Define

    I_Q=ker[R_Q → O(G_Q)⊗_Q K],  f↦(g↦f(g·t)).

This is a morphism of rational G_Q-comodules; exactness of tensoring
over Q shows that its kernel is G_Q-stable. It is a superideal in the
same noetherian Q-superalgebra, and hence has a finite G_Q-stable
generating module E. No rational model of the cup-product table is
asserted. For a rational polynomial covariant F, F(t)=0 if and only if
every coordinate of F belongs to I_Q: equivariance on all K-superalgebras
propagates vanishing at t to vanishing on the full schematic orbit;
the converse uses the identity point. This is the precise relation
statement, and is not a claim about arbitrary non-equivariant polynomials.

The projective covers, mixed splittings and universal rectangular
identities remain over Q. The external sign correspondence is assumed
for this chosen realization. Its trace on P_0 is the same positive
integer, and End(1)=Q injects into K. Thus the cancellation and the
finite necessary-and-sufficient criterion remain valid. Unless the
coefficient field and the kernel I_Q are effectively presented, only
finite existence is claimed; the rational elimination algorithm is not
silently extended to an arbitrary coefficient field.

## 10. Exact relation to Kimura finite-dimensionality

There is an exact published consequence of condition (ii). Del Padrone
and Mazza, *Schur finiteness and nilpotency*, C. R. Math. 341(5) (2005),
283–286, Theorem 3.2 on p. 286, proves for a smooth projective variety
and any chosen Weil cohomology, under C⁺(X), the equivalence of:

1. Kimura finite-dimensionality of h(X);
2. vanishing of the minimal rectangular Schur functor
   S_((b+1)^(a+1))(h(X));
3. nilpotence of the numerically trivial endomorphism ideal of h(X^m)
   for every m≥1.

The complete original Theorem 2.1 proof, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 3.2
proof were read. Proposition 3.1 supplies nilpotence from the sign
lifts and the precise row/column bound; Theorem 3.2 propagates the
minimal rectangle to tensor powers and lifts the parity projectors
through the nilpotent ideal. Thus our criterion does not assume
Kimura finite-dimensionality separately, but its rectangular condition
does imply it under the sign hypothesis. It would be incorrect to
replace the minimal rectangle by arbitrary Schur-finiteness. The
projective-cover proof above remains a direct proof and does not hide
a nilpotence or idempotent-lifting hypothesis.

The exact source is https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2005.07.010.pdf,
refs turn927673343view0, turn984306222view0 and turn468131333view0.
The later Del Padrone–Mazza arXiv:1010.3929v1, Definition 2.3 and
Theorem 2.4, pp. 3–4, confirms that sign lifts need not themselves be
idempotents and proves a larger-shape nilpotence assertion. André–Kahn
Proposition 8.3.1 and Theorem 9.2.1(c), pp. 49 and 54 in the opened
author version, have a *faithful* realization hypothesis: they apply
directly modulo homological equivalence and must not be invoked as
an unconditional Chow-motive implication. The 2005 theorem is the
exact citation for the Chow implication required here.
