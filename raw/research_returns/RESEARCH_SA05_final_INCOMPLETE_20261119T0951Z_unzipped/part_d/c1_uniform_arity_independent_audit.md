## 1. The recursive Gröbner bound

For fixed v,d, the tree of exponent sequences with |alpha_i| <= d 2^i and no earlier alpha_j <= alpha_i is finitely branching. Dickson's lemma, proved directly by successively choosing infinite nondecreasing coordinate subsequences, rules out an infinite branch. The elementary finitely-branching argument in the text therefore proves that the entire tree is finite. Breadth-first enumeration until an empty level is an actual terminating procedure computing its maximum length. This is a computability proof, not an assumed complexity estimate.

The homogeneous pair-reduction construction retains every accepted polynomial, so each new accepted leading monomial is outside the monomial ideal generated by all earlier accepted leading monomials. Homogeneous division does not raise degree. An S-polynomial's degree is bounded by the sum of the two input degrees. Sequentially processing the original generators and then the pairs therefore gives the stated bound d 2^i at the ith accepted polynomial. The leading exponent sequence belongs to the controlled tree in v+1 variables. Once the number of accepted polynomials is bounded, only finitely many pairs remain; the usual descending-leading-term proof of the pair criterion is supplied in the text itself.

The dehomogenization step is valid even if the ideal generated by the homogenized original generators is not saturated. For f in the original ideal, homogenizing one particular expression for f shows h^e f^h belongs to that ideal for some e. The chosen homogeneous order compares total degree and then the dehomogenized original monomial order. Divisibility of a leading monomial into that of h^e f^h consequently dehomogenizes to the required leading divisibility for f. No saturation theorem or hidden affine degree bound is used.

## 2. The super-module elimination

The presentation with independent even supermatrices G,H and equations GH=HG=1 represents the general linear supergroup. The counts 2(u^2+v^2) even and 4uv odd matrix entries are correct. The independent line torus adds two even variables. Parameter tensors of arities 1 through q have the stated M_0 and M_1 coordinate counts. For a rational table, a graph equation has degree at most q+1, and all group equations have degree two.

The free supercommutative algebra is free of rank J=2^O over its even-variable polynomial algebra. Multiplying each graph generator by every exterior basis element generates its ideal as a module over that polynomial algebra. Multiplication by those exterior monomials does not increase the degree in even variables. Hence the encoded module equations have ordinary degree at most q+2 after adjoining the J variables Y_j.

Adjoining all Y_iY_j makes the degree-one-in-Y component of the ordinary ideal exactly the encoded module: products of the linear module equations with even polynomials give that component, while every product involving a quadratic Y equation has Y-degree at least two. The ideal is Y-homogeneous. Homogenization, pair reduction, and dehomogenization preserve this extra grading, so the bounded Gröbner basis can be chosen Y-homogeneous.

Eliminating all even group variables and every Y_j indexed by an exterior monomial containing an odd group variable gives precisely the intersection with the parameter superalgebra. In its Y-degree-one component, quadratic-Y basis elements cannot contribute. The retained linear basis elements thus generate the super-orbit ideal as a module over the retained even parameter ring, and hence also as an ideal in the parameter superalgebra. This is an intersection calculation for the full superscheme, not merely its ordinary points.

## 3. From coefficient degree to input arity

The bounded ordinary basis has total degree at most D, so a retained linear-Y term has even coefficient degree at most D-1. Restoring its exterior basis monomial adds at most M_1 odd parameter factors. Each parameter coordinate has input arity at most q; the resulting orbit-ideal generator therefore has input arity at most q(D-1+M_1). Taking homogeneous components and finite group-stable spans preserves the input arity. The pure tensor retracts used in the finite criterion can consequently be chosen within that same bound. Combining with the already audited normalization, high-form, and rectangular conditions gives exactly the displayed maximum B_{n,a,b}.

The earlier finite-power cut-off argument was also checked in its written form. Pullback to a larger power has a left inverse given by intersection with the degree-one point classes on the added factors followed by projection. These operations preserve the narrow point-and-diagonal rings, so injectivity in one sufficiently large power implies the required finite list and hence all powers.

## 4. Coefficient qualification

The uniform bound depending only on n,a,b is asserted for a rational table. For a finitely presented coefficient algebra, its h named generators must include every coordinate of the form tuple (kappa_1,...,kappa_q), not merely the original binary multiplication constants. With that convention the orbit equations have degree at most q+2 before Y encoding, and the defining coefficient ideal contributes degree at most e+1 after encoding. The replacement max(q+3,e+1) is therefore valid.

One explicit clarification was sent to Part C: in this more general coefficient presentation the h coefficient variables must be eliminated along with the group variables, since the target ideal is the rational contraction I_Q. This adds no variables or degrees beyond the displayed count and does not change the proof. No uniform bound for unspecified arithmetic relations among arbitrary Weil-field elements is asserted.
