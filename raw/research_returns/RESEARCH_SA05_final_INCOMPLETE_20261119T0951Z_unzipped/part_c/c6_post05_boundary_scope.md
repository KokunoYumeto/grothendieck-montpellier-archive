## Tested converse via realization of an arbitrary correspondence

We considered whether an arbitrary algebraic correspondence could be encoded as the monodromy operator of a smoothable semistable degeneration, thereby reducing a named independence-of-homological-equivalence conjecture to the unramifiedness flags in C.6. No universal realization theorem was found or proved. Three separate requirements are missing: the correspondence must occur in an actual restriction/Gysin map, the normal-crossing configuration must admit a mixed-characteristic smoothing, and its generic fibre and projector must descend to a number field. These are not supplied merely by writing a cycle as a sum of subvarieties or using blowups.

Tensoring a motive M with a semistable elliptic curve gives monodromy id_M tensor N_E. That construction detects whether the realized vector space M is zero; it does not test whether an arbitrary given correspondence f realizes as zero. Part D independently confirmed this obstruction and knows no exact primary theorem completing the proposed converse.

This is a failed/uncompleted method, not an exact equivalence with van Dobben de Bruyn's finite-field conjectures. The existing sufficient implication from Fontaine's motivic Weil–Deligne compatibility conjecture remains the named conditional statement; no converse is claimed.
