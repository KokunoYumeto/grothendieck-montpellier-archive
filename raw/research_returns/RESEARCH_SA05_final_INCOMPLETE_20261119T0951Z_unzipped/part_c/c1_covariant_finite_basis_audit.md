## Exact change of method

The earlier finite criterion treated multiplication, unit, trace, point and
inverse pairing as mixed tensors. Its orbit ideal is noetherian, but its
covariant-lifting step cannot use exactness of general linear supergroup
invariants. The projective-cover argument repairs that step, at the cost of
an external algebraic parity correspondence for trace cancellation. The
positive pair consisting of an elliptic curve times a projective line
already proves that parity need not lie in the narrow diagram category.
Neither ordinary associative Specht theorems nor the opened matrix
supertrace results by themselves repaired this mixed invariant step.

The new argument first bends the requested cycles into scalar multilinear
forms and uses the actual augmentation projector `1 - eta epsilon`. It
does not split cohomological parity. The condition `Delta_*o = o x o`
makes the augmentation multiplicative, so its kernel is an algebra ideal.
The one additional identity `kappa_(2n+1) = 0` kills every higher form by
composition with the restricted multiplication. All remaining parameters
are scalar forms of arities 1 through 2n.

Their coordinate algebra is generated by pure *covariant* tensor powers
of the augmentation kernel, with a separate even Tate character. Fixing
the number of inputs and outputs gives a finite-dimensional polynomial
representation. This is the semisimple category controlled by the Schur
superalgebra. Thus the necessary lift is a Hom from a pure tensor power
between finite polynomial representations of the same degree, rather than
an invariant calculation on an arbitrary mixed module. No projective
auxiliary object or trace cancellation remains.

## Source proof check

Brundan and Kujawa, *A new proof of the Mullineux conjecture*, author version
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0210108, section 5, equations (5.1)-(5.2),
author pp. 12-13, describes polynomial representations by the finite
coefficient coalgebra. Lemma 5.1, p. 14, gives its explicit faithful
coefficient-basis action. Theorem 5.2, p. 15, proves that its image is the
entire commutant of the signed symmetric-group action: the coefficient
constraints on a commuting endomorphism are exactly the signed orbit
constraints in the coefficient basis. Both complete proofs were read by
the owner and independently by D. The source works over any field of
characteristic different from two, so the application over Q is direct.

Maschke then makes the commutant semisimple. Hemmer, Kujawa and Nakano,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0411390, Theorem 1.3.1(a)(i), author p. 3, and
the complete characteristic-zero argument in the proof of Theorem
4.2.6(a), p. 12, state this step explicitly. Even splittings are obtained
by parity averaging an ungraded module splitting. This is an internal
operation on vector superspaces and does not assert algebraicity of
parity on a Chow motive.

Lehrer and Zhang, Theorems 2.1 and 2.3, supply the pure-tensor commutant
and rectangular kernel. Every identity used to express the lifted map is
reduced, through signed symmetrizing idempotents, to equal pure tensor
degree. The rectangular Young symmetrizer therefore controls the entire
transport into Chow. D independently checked this step in the complete
written proof.

## Scope and remaining gate

The rational orbit kernel into `O(G_Q) tensor K` is a rational stable ideal
even when the Weil cup table has no rational form. This proves finite
existence over arbitrary algebraically closed base fields. An effective
construction requires the exact finite presentation of the algebra of
structure constants. The orbit is schematic and super: ordinary-point
orbit equations do not suffice.

The resulting list gives finite necessary-and-sufficient Chow conditions.
It neither decides rational equivalence nor proves those conditions for
an arbitrary pair. In particular, the general K3 determinant vanishing,
already equivalent to Kimura finite-dimensionality, is unchanged. This
is a removal of the sign hypothesis from the general finite criterion,
not a proof of the requested general K3 assertion.
