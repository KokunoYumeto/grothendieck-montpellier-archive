## Precise lemma checked

Let K be a number field, v a finite place of residue characteristic p, and M a rational absolute-Hodge or motivated motive of abelian type over K. Assume that for one prime l0 different from p the l0-adic realization of M is potentially unramified at v. There is a finite extension E/K and finitely many abelian varieties over E, all potentially of good reduction at every place of E over v, whose motives and Tate twists generate M_E. All projectors, inclusion maps, and retractions can be chosen over one further finite extension. This lemma proves potential good reduction of an alternative generator; it does not by itself prove that the original inertia action on M is trivial.

## 1. Descent of the selected weak lift

Fix Kbar inside C. Choose an abelian variety A/Kbar with M_Kbar in the category generated by h1(A) and Tate motives. Noot's Theorem 2.12 gives a Mumford–Tate-decomposed abelian variety B0/C that weakly lifts A/C. Since A is over Kbar, Proposition 3.1, applied to A and B0, implies that B0 has a Kbar-model. This invocation is within the proposition's stated scope.

The proof of Corollary 2.15 selects factors Bi in the isogeny decomposition of B0, retaining exactly those Q-simple derived factors acting nontrivially on M. These isogeny factors descend to Kbar. Indeed an isogeny factor is the image of a rational idempotent in End(B0/C) tensor Q, and Hom between abelian varieties over an algebraically closed field is unchanged by an algebraically closed extension. Multiplying the idempotent by an integer realizes its image as an abelian subvariety; the endomorphism and image therefore exist over Kbar. Consequently the selected B is over Kbar and has a central isogeny GB^der → GM^der with matching adjoint Hodge cocharacters. It and the finite number of maps subsequently used descend to a finite number field E containing K.

One may justify the Hom base-change assertion either through rigidity and the unramified Hom scheme, or by spreading a homomorphism over a connected finite-type parameter variety and applying rigidity to see it is constant; a Kbar-point of that parameter variety gives the required descent. Isogeny, rather than a chosen integral product decomposition, suffices throughout.

## 2. Generalizing Proposition 3.4 to M

Noot states Proposition 3.4 with h1(A) in place of M. Its proof is representation-theoretic and applies to the polarizable rational Hodge structure VM without change in the derived-group argument. Use the reductive group TM × TB × GB^der, acting on VM through TM × GM^der and on VB through TB × GB^der. Faithful representations and their duals generate its representation category. Each irreducible constituent of VM is obtained from a constituent of a tensor expression in VB and a representation on which the derived group acts trivially. The proof's evaluation map shows that this latter representation itself belongs to the Hodge category generated by VM and VB. It is therefore a polarizable Hodge structure with torus Mumford–Tate group, hence a CM Hodge structure.

CM Hodge structures are generated by H1 of CM abelian varieties and Tate objects. The geometric Hodge realization is fully faithful for abelian absolute-Hodge motives, and likewise for motivated abelian-type motives by André's theorem. Thus the Hodge isomorphisms and projectors lift to the appropriate motives over Kbar. Only finitely many CM objects occur in one expression for M, so a finite product C of CM abelian varieties suffices. After a further finite extension all of these objects and maps exist over E. The conclusion is M_E in the category generated by h1(B), h1(C), and Q(1).

## 3. The common-group argument, avoiding an unsupported adjoint realization claim

Let H be the Mumford–Tate group of VM direct-sum VB over Kbar. Identify GM^ad and GB^ad using the weak lift. The Hodge cocharacter of the direct sum factors through the rational closed subgroup

`P = {(gM,gB) in GM × GB : gM^ad = gB^ad}`.

By the defining minimality of a Mumford–Tate group, H is contained in P. Both projections H→GM and H→GB are surjective because VM and VB are objects of the generated category. The kernel of H→GM lies in the centre of GB and is central in H. Since H is connected reductive, its intersection with H^der is finite. It follows that

`H^der → GM^der`

is a central isogeny. The same holds for the other projection. This establishes the common adjoint group algebraically; we need not assume that an arbitrary chosen Lie-algebra Hodge isomorphism was already defined over the ground number field.

After a finite extension, the l0-adic Galois representation on M direct-sum h1(B) lies in H(Q_l0). This is the connected-field reduction in Noot §4.1: the arithmetic motivic group has geometric identity component H and finitely many components. The finite extension fixes a finite tensor presentation of the absolute-Hodge or motivated correspondences defining H. This is only containment in the Mumford–Tate group, not the Mumford–Tate conjecture.

Take a further finite local extension at a chosen place above v such that M_l0 has trivial inertia and B has semistable reduction. For an inertia element tau, its action on M direct-sum H1(B) is then

`u_tau = (1, rho_B,l0(tau)) in H(Q_l0)`.

Semistability makes this element unipotent. Every unipotent element of a connected reductive group maps to the identity in its torus quotient H/H^der. Thus u_tau lies in H^der, and its image in GM^der is the identity. The kernel of H^der→GM^der is finite. A finite-order unipotent element in characteristic zero is the identity: its minimal polynomial divides both (T−1)^r and T^m−1, whose latter polynomial is square-free. Hence u_tau=1 for every tau. Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich proves good reduction of B after this local extension.

This argument applies at every place of E above v: restriction of a potentially unramified representation is potentially unramified, and all conjugate inertia groups have the same property. Every CM abelian variety C is potentially of good reduction by the same argument with a torus Mumford–Tate group: after a finite connected-field and semistable extension, unipotent inertia in a torus is trivial. There are finitely many relevant places and varieties; finite extensions can be chosen simultaneously by taking a compositum of number-field extensions realizing the required local extensions.

## 4. Descent to the original motive, without erasing finite inertia

The restriction/corestriction maps for E/K give M as a direct summand of Ind_E^K(M_E); their composite on M is multiplication by [E:K], which is invertible in the rational category. Induction is additive and preserves split projectors. Therefore M is a direct summand of an induced finite sum of h^a(A)(t), for abelian varieties A/E with potentially good reduction above v. Geometrically these induced objects are represented by finite disjoint unions of conjugate abelian varieties, with their Hodge projectors.

This descent keeps the finite action of inertia on the set of conjugate components and on their good-reduction fibres. It must not be replaced by the false inference that trivial inertia after a ramified extension implies trivial inertia before it. The remaining proof needs the common rational trace of each such finite inertia operator on the projected motive. That is the separate rationality/trace step; the present audit does not claim it without that input.

## Audit outcome

The weak-lift construction, its descent to a number field, the common derived-group isogeny, and the potential-good-reduction implication pass the independent check under the stated abelian-type category hypotheses. The initial direct use of Proposition 3.1 for an arbitrary motive would have exceeded its scope; the B0-factor argument repairs it. The common-group elementwise argument removes any need to assume an unproved comparison of adjoint Galois representations. General motives are not covered by this lemma.
