# C.1 uniform arity-bound audit

Date: 2026-11-19. Owner: `/root/part_c`.

The full proof is in `c1_uniform_arity_bound.tex`. It strengthens the
finite cut-off to a total computable integer depending only on dimension
and the even and odd Betti dimensions when the cohomology table is
rational. The arbitrary-Weil-coefficient finite-existence theorem remains

## Why the usual closed estimate was not imported

We opened the original published article by Thomas W. Dubé,
*The structure of polynomial ideals and Gröbner bases*, SIAM Journal on
Computing 19(4) (1990), 750-773, DOI 10.1137/0219053, at
https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~r5olivei/courses/2025-spring-cs860/D90.pdf.
Lemma 8.1 is on p. 772; Theorem 8.2 and Corollary 8.3 are on p. 773.
The advertised closed bound was not used in the proof here.

The primary formalization by Alexander Maletzky, *Gröbner Bases,
Macaulay Matrices and Dubé's Degree Bounds*, Archive of Formal Proofs,
first published 15 June 2019, current document dated 26 October 2026,
explicitly discusses an error in the argument of Dubé's Lemma 8.1 and
another correction in its own auxiliary recurrence. We opened the
entry https://isa-afp.org/entries/Groebner_Macaulay.html and the complete
outline PDF https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Groebner_Macaulay/outline.pdf,
and read the discussion at section 11, printed pp. 55-56. It does not
claim that the advertised closed bound is false. We likewise make no
such claim, and do not claim to have checked the complete formal proof.
The discussion prompted a self-contained substitute for the particular
bound needed here.

## Self-contained replacement

The controlled bad-sequence tree has finitely many possible children at
every node. Dickson's lemma excludes an infinite branch; the elementary
finite-branching-tree argument makes the whole tree finite. Exhausting
its levels therefore gives a total algorithm for its height. This is an
explicit recursive function, with no efficiency claim.

For homogeneous Buchberger reduction, every accepted leading monomial
is outside the ideal of its predecessors and degrees grow by at most a
factor of two. Thus the controlled tree bounds both the number of
accepted remainders and their degrees. The degree-first extension of
an arbitrary elimination order permits dehomogenization without a
saturation assumption: some power of the homogenizing variable times
any homogenized ideal element lies in the homogenized generator ideal.

For the super orbit graph, the exterior algebra is a finite free module
over the even polynomial algebra. Multiplying every graph relation by
all exterior basis elements gives its entire ideal as a module. Encoding
module coordinates with ordinary variables Y and imposing every product
Y_i Y_j=0 preserves that module exactly in Y-degree one. Elimination of
the group variables and the Y coordinates involving odd group variables
therefore computes the desired super-orbit ideal in its linear Y part.
The bounded homogeneous algorithm preserves this additional Y grading.

## Finite exact certificate

`code/part_c/check_uniform_bound.py` and its JSON output exhaust the
controlled trees L(1,d), d=1,2,3,4, obtaining d+1, and L(2,1), obtaining 4.
They also compute the square-zero encoding for the super map
z=x, theta_1=eta, theta_2=x eta. Exact Gröbner elimination over Q gives
the expected kernel generated by theta_2-z theta_1 and its odd multiple
theta_1 theta_2. Both ideal inclusions are checked. These are checks of
the finite mechanisms, not evaluations of the enormous geometric bound.

The resulting uniform arity cut-off does not settle any new K3 Chow
vanishing. Its role is to make the finite reduction uniform in the
specified cohomological dimensions.
