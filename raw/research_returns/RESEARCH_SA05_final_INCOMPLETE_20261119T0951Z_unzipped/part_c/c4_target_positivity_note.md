# The precise extra input for the first threefold pairing

Written 19 November 2026 during the checkpoint-07 freeze, by /root/part_c.
This is a proved conditional refinement of the standard-conjecture route. It is not a proof of the remaining C4 pairing condition and is not counted as movement on its unconditional universal gate.

## 1. An image projector needs only the target algebra and a mixed pairing

We work in a rational additive category of algebraic correspondences modulo one fixed homological equivalence, with its faithful realization in finite-dimensional vector spaces over a characteristic-zero field. Let T and S be two objects. Suppose

1. A = End(S) is finite-dimensional and semisimple over Q.
2. The pairing

   $$
   \operatorname{Hom}(T,S)\times\operatorname{Hom}(S,T)
   \longrightarrow\mathbb Q,\qquad
   (f,g)\longmapsto\operatorname{Tr}_S(fg)
   $$

   is nondegenerate in its first variable.

Then every algebraic map f:T→S has an algebraic idempotent e∈A with exactly the same realized image.

To prove this, put M=Hom(T,S), N=Hom(S,T), and J=fN⊂A. The subspace J is a right ideal: for g∈N and a∈A, one has ga∈N and (fg)a=f(ga). Every right ideal of the finite-dimensional semisimple algebra A has the form eA for an idempotent e. Since e∈J, there is g₀∈N such that e=fg₀. Thus im(e)⊂im(f).

Conversely, every fg belongs to eA, so (1−e)fg=0 for every g∈N. The mixed pairing annihilates (1−e)f against every reverse map. Nondegeneracy gives (1−e)f=0. Hence f=ef and im(f)⊂im(e), proving equality.

No semisimplicity assumption on End(T) occurs. The construction is not an argument that a semisimple numerical quotient alone splits the image of an arbitrary homological map: the mixed nondegeneracy assumption is what rules out a nonzero homological residual map.

## 2. Taking the actual primitive intersection

Let V be the realization of S, let p∈A be the algebraic primitive projector, and let e be the image projector just constructed. The required space is

$$
R=\operatorname{im}(e)\cap\operatorname{im}(p).
$$

It must not be replaced without proof by p(im(e)).

The finite additive Karoubi category generated by S is semisimple, because its endomorphism algebras are corners of matrix algebras over A. Its realization is exact: all its exact sequences split, and the realization preserves sums and splittings. Consequently the kernel of

$$
(1-p)e:\operatorname{im}(e)\longrightarrow S
$$

is represented by an algebraic idempotent r∈A and realizes exactly R. This is also elementary matrix algebra in every simple factor of A; no algebraic projector onto an arbitrary Qℓ-subspace is being assumed.

Suppose additionally that the adjoint involution a↦a† for a specified nondegenerate pairing Φ on V belongs to A and satisfies

$$
\operatorname{Tr}_S(a^\dagger a)>0\qquad
\text{for every nonzero }a\in A.
$$

Then Φ restricted to R is nondegenerate. Indeed its radical is the intersection of the algebraic image of r with the kernel of r†. It is therefore again an algebraic subobject by semisimplicity. If it were nonzero, choose an idempotent q whose image is that radical. The pairing vanishes on im(q)×im(q), so q†q=0. This contradicts the displayed positive trace. This proof uses positivity of the correspondence trace form over Q; it does not claim that every Qℓ-subspace of a negative-definite rational divisor space is nondegenerate.

In the surface application, Φ on all of H²(S) is the Lefschetz-modified pairing Φ(x,y)=Tr(x∪*y), with Saito's signed Lefschetz involution. It is not the raw intersection pairing on all of H²(S). On primitive H² the involution is −1, so Φ is the negative of the raw intersection pairing; their restrictions to the primitive space have the same radical. This distinction is necessary: the raw intersection pairing on all of H² of a surface can be indefinite even on the rational algebraic classes, and its adjoint trace need not be positive.

## 3. A sufficient package for the actual threefold restriction map

Fix a coefficient prime ℓ different from the residue characteristic. Let X=Y^(1) be the disjoint union of the threefold components of a projective strictly semistable model, and let S=Y^(2) be the disjoint union of its double-intersection surfaces. We may extend the finite residue field to define all individual geometric components. Such a finite extension does not change the weight–monodromy assertion.

Let

$$
f=\rho:H^2(X_{\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell)
\longrightarrow H^2(S_{\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell).
$$

The signed restriction map is algebraic. Rational algebraic Künneth projectors over a finite field allow us to regard its source and target as homogeneous degree-two homological objects. These projectors are obtained from Frobenius polynomials; no conjectural characteristic-zero Künneth projector is used. For the surface target the primitive projector is algebraic explicitly: on each surface it is the degree-two projector minus the rank-one projector

$$
x\longmapsto \frac{\operatorname{Tr}(xL)}{\operatorname{Tr}(L^2)}\,L.
$$

The following hypotheses suffice:

* the Hodge standard conjecture I(S×S,L⊞L) for the surface-square product with the chosen product ample class, including the products of distinct components of S;
* equality of homological and numerical equivalence for rational cycles on X×S. It is enough to assume the resulting mixed trace pairing on the homogeneous degree-two correspondence blocks is nondegenerate; the full conjecture D(X×S) is a convenient published sufficient formulation.

The first assumption, together with the known Lefschetz standard conjecture for surfaces, gives the finite-dimensional semisimple target algebra and positive adjoint trace for the Lefschetz-modified pairing Φ required above. To make the rational finiteness step explicit, the known conjecture B(S) is stable under products, so B(S×S), and hence A(S×S), holds. Combined with I(S×S), this gives D(S×S). The homogeneous homological endomorphism algebra therefore identifies with its numerical corner, which is finite-dimensional over Q and semisimple by Jannsen's Theorem 1. We do not infer finite dimension over Q merely from the existence of a finite-dimensional Qℓ realization. The positive adjoint trace is exactly Morihiko Saito's section 4.1, equation (4.1.3), with the semisimplicity discussion in the paragraph following it (author pp. 14–15).

For the second assumption, an algebraic degree-zero map H²(X)→H²(S) is represented covariantly by a codimension-three cycle on X×S; a reverse map is represented by a codimension-two cycle there after transposition. Their composition trace is their intersection number, by the algebraic correspondence trace formula. If the first class pairs trivially with all reverse homogeneous correspondences, it pairs trivially with all algebraic complementary cycles: projecting a complementary cycle onto the reverse degree-two block does not change that intersection. D(X×S) then makes its homological class zero. This proves the required mixed nondegeneracy. The same statement holds componentwise and for signed sums.

The lemma therefore produces an algebraic projector onto im(ρ), and section 2 produces one onto the actual intersection

$$
\operatorname{im}(\rho)\cap
\ker\!\left(L:H^2(S)\longrightarrow H^4(S)(1)\right).
$$

Positive adjoint trace makes its intersection form nondegenerate. Saito's dimension-three converse in section 2.7, together with the already established automatic Gysin-image condition, proves weight–monodromy for H³ of the generic threefold. Equivalently, it settles the specific C4 inertia-invariant upper-weight-three endpoint under this sufficient package.

In this formulation neither the Lefschetz standard conjecture on X nor semisimplicity of the source endomorphism algebra is required. The retained mixed D hypothesis is essential to this proof and is not supplied by the known conjectures for individual surfaces.

## 4. What a supplied inverse Hard Lefschetz on X actually changes

Suppose instead that for each smooth projective threefold component Xᵢ an inverse to

$$
L_i:H^2(X_i)\longrightarrow H^4(X_i)(1)
$$

is induced by a rational algebraic correspondence. Choose a smooth ample surface inclusion jᵢ:Tᵢ→Xᵢ whose class is mᵢLᵢ. Over a finite field a finite constant extension is permitted for this choice. Then

$$
r_i=m_i^{-1}L_i^{-1}j_{i*}:H^2(T_i)\longrightarrow H^2(X_i)
$$

is algebraic and satisfies rᵢjᵢ*=1, by the projection formula. Let T be the disjoint union of these surfaces and let r be the sum of the retractions. The map ρr:H²(T)→H²(S) has exactly the image of ρ, because r is surjective.

We may now replace the mixed D(X×S) hypothesis in section 3 by D(T×S), or just its relevant homogeneous mixed trace pairing. The target positivity hypothesis remains I(S×S). Thus a supplied degree-two inverse Hard Lefschetz reduces the unknown correspondence pairings to products of surfaces. It does not eliminate those product hypotheses.

The primitive-intersection step is still the one in section 2. We do not infer that primitive projection preserves the restriction image.

## 5. Why the standard results for a surface do not finish this argument

The known Hodge index theorem I(S) concerns rational divisor classes on S. The missing positivity here concerns algebraic endomorphisms of primitive H²(S), equivalently the relevant codimension-two correspondence classes on the fourfold S×S. These are different statements.

Likewise, known D(S) for divisors does not imply D(T×S) for codimension-two cycles on a product of surfaces. Algebraicity of an inverse Hard Lefschetz on a threefold gives the algebraic retraction above; it supplies neither the mixed trace nondegeneracy on T×S nor positive adjoint trace on S×S.

We have therefore identified an exact weaker sufficient package than imposing all the standard conjectures on the threefold components and all their self-products. We have not proved that the retained surface-product hypotheses hold, and we have not produced a geometric counterexample to the claim that another argument might avoid them.
