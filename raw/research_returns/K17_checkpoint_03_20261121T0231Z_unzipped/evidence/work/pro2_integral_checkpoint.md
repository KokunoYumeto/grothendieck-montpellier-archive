## 1. The canonical rational normal form

Use the established arithmetic presentation P_2=C_2*Z_2, with c of order2 and chi(gamma)=u=5. Put h=c gamma c gamma^(-1). Then c h c=h^(-1). After placing c in a rational Levi torus, a=log(h) has only odd weight components.

Write gamma=v u. The relation h=c(v)v^(-1) shows that z=exp(a/2)v is c-fixed. Conjugate by an element b in the c-fixed unipotent subgroup solving b z u(b^(-1))=1. Such b is constructed successively in positive even weights by dividing by u^w-1, which is nonzero in Q_2. This is a rational weight-complete operation. It leaves c in the torus and gives

    h=exp(a),              gamma=exp(-a/2)u.

Every odd homogeneous component of a has nonzero indecomposable projection: the conjugates of h topologically generate the cyclotomic kernel, their linear coordinates scale by u^(mx), and density would be contradicted if any such coordinate vanished. Brown's rational freeness therefore permits choosing these homogeneous components as the free generators s_m. In these coordinates,

    a=sum_(m odd, m>=3) s_m.

The induced conjugation on the free Lie algebra is

    D=Ad(exp(-a/2)) composed with tau,
    tau(s_m)=5^m s_m.

At weight cutoff W, let H_W be the closure of the group generated by exp(D^x a) for x in Z_2. This is exactly the projected compact rational image of the cyclotomic kernel. The formal expressions have only finitely many terms at every cutoff; the script uses rational coefficients and no floating-point approximation.

## 2. Exact group closure, not the linear span of logarithms

Order a rational Hall/Lyndon basis by increasing arithmetic weight, putting the primitive odd generator first within an odd weight. Each candidate group generator is stored as its logarithm. Products and commutators are evaluated by multiplying the finite associative exponentials and applying the finite logarithm; all Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff denominators are thus retained.

For a candidate g whose first logarithmic coordinate is c, and an existing pivot b with leading coordinate d, eliminate by b^(-c/d)g whenever v_2(c/d)>=0. This is a legitimate Z_2 power in the compact group. If c has smaller valuation, replace the pivot by the candidate and reduce the old pivot in turn. Normalize pivots using only unit Z_2 powers. New generators are added from D(g_i) and [g_i,g_j]. Every generator produced therefore belongs to H_W.

Once the process stops, the script checks exactly that h, every D(g_i), every D^(-1)(g_i), and every pair commutator reduce to the identity. It also checks that the pivots cover the full rational Lie basis. No assertion of termination in every possible weight is needed for a finite certificate: these final exact checks are what certify the completed run.

### Compact collection lemma

Let g_1,...,g_N be a triangular list of unipotent elements, in increasing weight-coordinate order, and put

    N_i={g_i^(t_i)...g_N^(t_N): t_j in Z_2}.

Suppose every [g_i,g_j] lies in the appropriate later ordered product N_(j+1) for i<j. The condition is verified by reduction; commutator weights are strictly greater than both input weights. Then N_i is a compact subgroup, by descending induction. Indeed N_(i+1) is already a subgroup, and conjugation by g_i carries each later generator into it. Hence it carries N_(i+1) into itself. Inverse conjugation follows because g_i^(2^n-1) tends to g_i^(-1); compactness makes N_(i+1) closed. Thus N_(i+1) is normalized by the whole procyclic group g_i^Z_2, and N_i is its product with that group. Compactness follows from the displayed continuous parametrization by Z_2^(N-i+1).

Triangularity makes this parametrization injective: the first nonzero exponent has a nonzero leading logarithmic coordinate, while commutator corrections have higher weight. It also proves that the script's successive reductions are an exact membership test.

At a stable output, N_1 contains h and is invariant under D and D^(-1), so it contains all integer conjugates of h. Their closure contains all Z_2 conjugates by continuity. Conversely every constructed generator belongs to H_W. Thus N_1=H_W. This proves that the procedure computes the actual compact group, rather than merely a Lie lattice suggested by its logarithms.

## 3. Reading the graded lattice and comparing odd generators

An element of N_1 has vanishing coordinates below weight m exactly when all its product exponents on earlier pivots are zero. Its weight-m logarithmic component is consequently the Z_2-span of the weight-m leading parts of the pivots of that weight. This is the full graded lattice for the pullback filtration.

For each odd m, choose the pivot whose first coordinate is s_m. Its weight-m component sigma_m has the smallest possible nonzero s_m-coordinate in the full graded lattice. It is therefore primitive for the actual image ideal of the corresponding abelian character. It may include decomposable terms in its own weight, and the script retains them.

For every Hall word in the odd sigma_m, calculate its Lie bracket expansion exactly. These vectors lie in the full graded lattice because they arise as leading parts of group commutators. They form a rational basis. Hence the 2-adic valuation of the quotient of their determinant by the full-lattice determinant is precisely the Z_2-module index exponent. A zero valuation proves equality of the lattices. The code never replaces group products by a Z_2-linear logarithmic span in constructing the full group.

## 5. The Newton pattern and the remaining all-weight problem

The observed primitive odd pivot at m=2r+3 has valuation

    d_m=3r+v_2(r!).

This is the valuation of the abelian Newton evaluation product

    product_(j=0,...,r-1) (5^(2r+3)-5^(2j+3)).

Indeed v_2(5^(2s)-1)=3+v_2(s), so the product has the displayed valuation. The evaluation kernel in Lambda for the preceding odd weights is generated by the corresponding monic product, via successive formal division. This completely explains the abelian necessary lower bound.

What requires a structural proof is that the Newton generators can always be corrected in lower even and decomposable weights by integral group commutators, and that the resulting Hall family is invariant under D without producing a smaller pivot. Finite computations verify that collection condition in the stated range; the scalar Newton determinant alone does not prove it in every weight. An all-weight Hall/Newton collection theorem, or a concrete failure of that collection condition, is the next meaningful gate. Additional finite weights alone are not an all-weight proof.
