## SG-CHECK-AR05 — Divisibility and slope bounds

**Required hypotheses.** Let L and M be finite-rank Euclidean lattices, with L nonzero, and let φ:L→M be an injective integral map whose extension to real spans has operator norm at most C. Thus C>0. Let d be a positive integer and suppose φL⊂dM. Use deg(L)=−log covol(L) and μ(L)=deg(L)/rank(L), and the inspected slope inequality μ(L)≤μ_max(M)+log||u|| for an injective integral map u:L→M.

**Complete check.** The map ψ=φ/d has image in M and is injective. Its norm is at most C/d. Applying the slope inequality to ψ gives

    μ(L) ≤ μ_max(M)+log(C/d)
         = μ_max(M)+log C−log d.

Rearrangement yields log d≤μ_max(M)−μ(L)+log C. This verifies the direction of every sign.

If both lattices have the same rank r>0, use the determinant of the extension φ_R between their r-dimensional real spans. Its absolute value is the product of r singular values, each at most C; hence covol(φL)≤C^r covol(L). Since φL is a full-rank subgroup of M, its index I is finite and covol(φL)=I covol(M). Consequently

    log I ≤ r log C + log covol(L)−log covol(M)
          = r log C + deg(M)−deg(L).

If φL⊂dM, the index factorization gives I=[M:dM][dM:φL]≥d^r. Dividing the preceding bound by r gives the sharper log d≤μ(M)−μ(L)+log C, since μ(M)≤μ_max(M). Both inequalities are sharp for φ=±d·id on the standard lattice Z^r. The associated symmetric forms ±d⟨x,y⟩ have opposite signatures, while both maps have norm d and index d^r. Thus this valid quantitative consequence does not choose an intersection sign.

**Verdict:** PASS with the explicit nonzero-lattice hypothesis. The final fragment should not leave μ(L) undefined at rank zero.

## SG-CHECK-SL05 — Monomial and binomial lattices

Its permutation-invariant tensors have a basis w_r consisting of the sum of the distinct tensors with r copies of y and d−r copies of x. Every group element acts diagonally on T and commutes with these permutations, hence preserves this invariant lattice. The symmetric multiplication map T→Sym^d(Z^2) is equivariant and sends w_r to binom(d,r)v_r. These images are independent over Z, so its restriction identifies the invariant lattice with Γ_1.     Σ_{0≤i<d} p^i x_{s,i} + Σ_{0≤i<d} x_{s−i,i} ≤ A

for every s. The objective is S=Σ_{s,i}x_{s,i}.

Summing the d constraints and using that s↦s−i permutes Z/dZ gives

    Σ_{s,i}(p^i+1)x_{s,i} ≤ dA.

For every supported i, one has i≥1 and p^i+1≥p+1, so S≤dA/(p+1). This is attained by x_{s,1}=A/(p+1) for every s and all other variables zero. The support condition is satisfied because d≥2; each constraint is an equality. Thus the feasible objective set is nonempty and bounded above and its supremum is dA/(p+1). Substituting into the definition gives

    lpF(p,d,A)=pdA/(p²−1)−dA/(p+1)=dA/(p²−1).

This also covers A=0. It does not apply to d=1, where the only feasible variables vanish and lpF=pA/(p²−1).

## SG-CHECK-SL09 — A specified integral order and its first three indices

The ambient order is A_n=∏_{i=0}^n Mat_{n−i+1}(Z), one unit-step string for each bottom degree i. On a string of length d+1, L raises with coefficient one and D lowers the rth vector with coefficient c_r=r(d−r+1). Degree projectors are included, so O_n=Z[L,D,π_0,…,π_{2n}]. This explicitly specified order is distinct from an unspecified exterior or automorphic integral order.

**The auxiliary order.** Put u_0=1 and u_r=∏_{j=1}^r c_j. Let T_d be the subgroup of Mat_{d+1}(Z) whose (r,s) entry for r<s is divisible by u_s/u_r; lower-triangular and diagonal entries are unrestricted. This is a subring. For r<s, consider the tth summand a_{rt}b_{ts} of a product. If r≤t≤s, its divisibility factors multiply to u_s/u_r. If t<r, the second factor is divisible by u_s/u_t, which is a multiple of u_s/u_r. If t>s, the first factor is divisible by u_t/u_r, again a multiple of u_s/u_r. Thus every summand has the required divisibility. Both L and D lie in T_d.

**n=1.** The degree projectors separate every diagonal entry in the length-two and length-one strings. L and D are the two off-diagonal units on the length-two string. Hence O_1=A_1, of index one.

**n=2.** The odd-degree length-two string yields the full Mat_2(Z). The even part consists of a length-three string and a scalar at its middle degree. Here u=(1,2,4). Its ring is

    R_2 = {(A,b)∈T_2×Z : A_11≡b mod 2}.

Their product E_10·(2E_01)=2E_11 produces the remaining kernel unit. The middle degree projector is (E_11,1), lifting the scalar quotient. Thus these generated elements span R_2 as an abelian group, proving equality.

The index of T_2 in Mat_3(Z) is the product 2·4·2=16 of its upper-entry divisibilities. The one scalar congruence adds an index2. Consequently [A_2:O_2]=16·2=32.

**n=3.** The odd-degree part is the preceding R_2 and contributes32. The even part has lengths four and two.
