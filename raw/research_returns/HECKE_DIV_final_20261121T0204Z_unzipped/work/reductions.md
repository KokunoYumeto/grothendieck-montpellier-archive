## Scope and conventions

Work over C. Let S be a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety of finite type, f:A→S a polarized abelian scheme of relative dimension g, and L a fixed relatively ample line bundle (or a fixed polarization with a relative ample line bundle after a finite étale cover). Write θ=c1(L). All cycle degrees are **effective-cycle degrees** relative to L: deg_L Z=∫_Z θ^{dim Z}. Let t∈H^0(S^an,R^{2p}f_*Q(p)) be one global rational flat section, of Hodge type (0,0) after the Tate twist. Put

c_t(s)=min{max(N,deg Z+,deg Z−): N∈Z_{>0}, Z± effective integral codimension-p cycles on A_s, cl(Z+)−cl(Z−)=Nt_s},

with c_t(s)=∞ when the set is empty. Multiplicities are included in degree. Allow the zero effective cycle. The normalization of t and L is part of the data. A change t↦qt changes numerical constants; no numerical degree bound is intrinsic without these choices.

All claims below are about a **fixed section t** unless explicitly quantified over all Hodge classes. Extra Hodge classes appear on special fibres. Algebraicity of t on every fibre is not, by itself, the Hodge conjecture for every class on every fibre.

## R20. Finite-monodromy normalization

**Statement.** If a rational flat class on the universal covering of S remains of type (p,p) everywhere, the local system generated by its monodromy orbit has finite monodromy. After a finite étale cover its orbit consists of global flat sections. This is a normalization, not a boundedness theorem.

**Proof.** Its monodromy translates are also everywhere of type (p,p); take their finite-dimensional rational span in one fibre and transport it. This gives a rational sublocal system V of R^{2p}f_*Q(p) whose Hodge structure has only type (0,0). The cohomological variation is polarizable; the restriction of a polarization to V is positive definite. The monodromy representation preserves the full integral lattice V_Q∩(R^{2p}f_*Z/torsion) and the positive definite form. The group of integral automorphisms preserving a positive definite form is finite: each column of an integral matrix has bounded length and thus has only finitely many possibilities. The kernel of the finite monodromy representation gives the required finite topological cover, which is algebraic and étale by the Riemann existence theorem (SGA 1, Exposé XII, Théorème 5.1). ∎

**Caveat.** The finite cover must still be used consistently for the family, polarization, level and tensor-compatible Hecke action. One must not treat a multivalued Weil class as a global section on an arbitrary coarse unitary quotient.

## R21. Closedness of bounded complexity and the exact uniformity equivalence

**Statement.** For C∈Z_{≥1}, S_C={s:c_t(s)≤C} is Zariski closed. The following are equivalent:

1. t_s is algebraic for every s∈S(C).
2. t_s is algebraic for very general s∈S(C), where “very general” means outside a countable union of proper closed algebraic subsets.
3. S_C=S for some C.
4. c_t is bounded on a Zariski-dense subset of S.

The assertion supplies an existential constant, not an algorithm or a numerical value.

**Proof of closedness.** For integers 0≤d+,d−≤C let

T_{d+,d−}=Chow_{g−p,d+}(A/S)×_S Chow_{g−p,d−}(A/S).

These relative Chow spaces can be taken reduced/seminormal; that distinction does not change their complex points. They are projective and of finite type over S. Hence they have finitely many connected components. Over their analytifications the two universal relative cycles give locally constant sections of the pulled-back cohomology local system. To see the constancy directly, connect parameter points by chains of algebraic curves, normalize the curves, and pull back the smooth proper family. The fundamental class of a relative cycle on a smooth curve restricts to its fibre-cycle classes, including specialization; these are parallel transports in the smooth proper local system. Thus the cycle-class difference is a section of that local system, and its equality to Nt is constant on a connected component. This argument concerns cycle classes; it does not incorrectly assume that the Chow universal cycle is a universal flat subscheme.

For each 1≤N≤C, let T_{N,d+,d−}^t be the union of connected components on which cl(Z+)−cl(Z−)=Nt. This is an open-and-closed algebraic subspace of T_{d+,d−}, hence projective over S. Its image is Zariski closed. S_C is the union of those images over the finitely many choices N,d+,d−, so is closed.

For a merely relatively ample L, replace it by a fixed very ample tensor power L^r during the Chow construction. All fibre degrees are then multiplied by the same r^{g−p}; selecting the original allowable degrees gives the same locus and preserves finiteness.

**Proof of equivalences.** Algebraicity of t_s in rational cohomology is exactly c_t(s)<∞, because the denominators of a finite rational cycle can be cleared and its positive and negative parts separated. Thus (1) implies S=∪_{C≥1}S_C. If every S_C were proper, each would have empty analytic interior. A closed small ball in the smooth locus of S is a complete metric space and cannot be a countable union of its nowhere dense closed intersections with S_C, by the Baire theorem. Hence some S_C=S. More generally, under (2), S is covered by the S_C together with the countable proper exceptional algebraic subsets; the same Baire argument proves (3). Implication (3)⇒(1) is immediate. Implication (3)⇒(4) is immediate, and (4)⇒(3) follows because the closed S_C containing the dense subset equals S. Finally (1)⇒(2). ∎

**Source framework.** Bounded-degree cycle parameter spaces: Kollár, *Rational curves on algebraic varieties* (1996), I.3.21; corroborated by the author's *Families of varieties of general type*, 2018-01-05 manuscript, Definition 3.21, equation (3.21.1), p.119. Fixed-projective-space version: Friedlander (1991), Proposition 1.1, pp.57–58. FGA connection: Grothendieck, Bourbaki exposé 221, Théorèmes 3.1–3.2, pp.259–260, projectivity of Quot/Hilbert for fixed Hilbert polynomial. **Do not cite fixed Hilbert polynomial projectivity alone as bounded-degree Chow projectivity**: embedded points make arbitrary subschemes of fixed degree unbounded in Hilbert polynomial.

## R22. Correct tensor-compatible Hecke reduction

**Statement.** Let S=Γ\X+ be a connected Shimura variety of Hodge type with its polarized abelian scheme after neat level. Suppose a connected rational algebraic subgroup H of the ambient similitude group (for the Weil setting one may use the appropriate special unitary group) acts transitively on X+ through H(R)^+, and fixes the chosen rational tensor t, with Tate-similitude convention included. For x0∈S, the image

O_H(x0)={π(q·x̃0):q∈H(Q), q·x̃0∈X+}

is analytically dense. Its points are tensor-compatible rational isogeny points. For x∈O_H(x0), there is an isogeny ψ_x:A_x→A_x0 with ψ_x^*t_x0=c_xt_x for some nonzero rational c_x. If t_x0 is algebraic, every t_x is algebraic. Moreover

t_s algebraic for all s∈S ⇔ c_t is bounded on O_H(x0).

The same boundedness equivalence is valid for any larger Hecke orbit on which t is coherently transported, because it contains this dense suborbit.

**Proof.** By real approximation, H(Q) is dense in H(R); see Milne (2017), Theorem 5.4. Given ỹ∈X+, choose h∈H(R)^+ with h·x̃0=ỹ and approximate h by rational points q. For q sufficiently close, q·x̃0∈X+. Hence the rational orbit is dense in X+, and its image is dense in S. This is also the mechanism of Milne's Lemma 13.5.

The rational Hodge isomorphism on H_1 represented by q yields a rational homomorphism of complex abelian varieties, by Milne (2017), Corollary 6.9 (Riemann's equivalence). Clearing an integer denominator gives an isogeny ψ_x in the desired direction; because q preserves the tensor (up to the prescribed rational similitude weight), clearing denominators scales its action on degree 2p cohomology by a nonzero rational number c_x. If q acts exactly on the untwisted cohomological tensor and ψ_x=dq, then c_x=d^{2p}; other conventions insert the explicit rational similitude factor. Pullback by an isogeny is an algebraic correspondence. If cl(Z0)=N0t_x0 for an integral signed cycle Z0, then cl(ψ_x^*Z0)=N0c_xt_x. Multiplication or division by N0c_x in rational Chow groups proves algebraicity of t_x. Finally, apply R21 to the analytically, hence Zariski, dense orbit. ∎

**Necessary distinctions.** (a) An arbitrary unmarked isogeny need not preserve a chosen Weil class. The full unitary group can act nontrivially on the two-dimensional rational Weil space via its K-determinant. Use a tensor stabilizer/special unitary suborbit or record that action explicitly. (b) Algebraicity of one t_x0 proves only the transported class, whereas the full Hodge conjecture is isogeny invariant if it is known for **every** Hodge class at x0. (c) The formula ψ_x^*Z0 exhibits representatives whose degrees and scalar division factors can grow. It does not give bounded division. (d) The reduction to all abelian varieties requires applying the criterion to the Hodge locus of every rational Hodge class, not only one Weil family.

## R23. André–Oort dichotomy with correct sequence quantifiers

**Definition.** A sequence (x_j) of CM points of S is strictly special-generic if for every proper special subvariety T⊊S, only finitely many x_j lie in T. “Not contained in T” is too weak; eventual avoidance is required.

**Statement.** In the setting of R21 with S a special subvariety of a Siegel moduli space, precisely one of the following holds:

- S_C=S for some C;
- for every C, the CM points of S_C lie in a finite union of proper special subvarieties of S, and c_t(x_j)→∞ for every strictly special-generic CM sequence.

Such sequences can be chosen in the tensor-compatible Hecke orbit of any CM point. Thus algebraicity of t on all fibres is equivalent to bounded c_t along one strictly special-generic CM sequence. Indeed, a fixed bound on an infinite subsequence already suffices.

**Proof.** If S_C⊊S, André–Oort says that the Zariski closure of its special points is a finite union of special subvarieties contained in S_C. None equals S. For any fixed C, a strictly special-generic sequence eventually avoids all these finitely many subvarieties, so eventually c_t(x_j)>C. This is exactly c_t(x_j)→∞. Conversely, if one such sequence has c_t≤C infinitely often, its infinite bounded subsequence cannot be contained in the finite union supplied by André–Oort, so S_C=S. R21 gives algebraicity on all fibres.

For existence, enumerate the proper special subvarieties T1,T2,…; they are countable because Shimura subdata and the relevant Hecke translates are described by rational/finite-adelic data modulo an open level. At step j choose x_j in the dense CM orbit outside T1∪…∪Tj. The complement is a nonempty Zariski open subset, so this choice is possible. The resulting sequence has the asserted eventual-avoidance property. ∎

**Source and unconditional status.** Tsimerman (2018), *The André–Oort conjecture for A_g*, Theorem 1.3, p.380, with the finite-maximal-special-subvariety formulation in Conjecture 1.1, p.379. For arbitrary polarization type and neat finite level, pass through the usual finite Shimura maps/isogeny moduli to a Siegel moduli space; specialness and the assertion are preserved. No new GRH claim is needed for this use of André–Oort.

## R24A. The valid bounded-effective-divisor assertion

**Statement.** Suppose the very general fibre has NS(A_s)_Q=Qθ_s. For each D, the set of s for which A_s has an effective divisor Z of degree at most D with [Z]∉Qθ_s is a proper Zariski-closed subset of S.

**Proof.** On Chow_{g−1,d}(A/S), for fixed d, set v=∫_{A_s}θ_s^g, independent of s. The section cl(Z)−(d/v)θ is flat on the pulled-back cohomology local system; thus its being zero or nonzero is constant on every connected component, by the curve argument in R21. The union of the nonzero components is open and closed and proper over S. Its image is closed. Take the finite union over 1≤d≤D. Very general NS rank one makes the image proper. ∎

**R24A-refutation of the unqualified wording.** If “degree of a divisor class” means just the signed number ∫ηθ^{g−1}, the statement in the sketch is false. At every point of the E^g isogeny orbit, g≥2, NS_Q has rank greater than one. Choose integral η∉Qθ. The nonzero integral class

ζ=(∫θ^g)η−(∫ηθ^{g−1})θ

is not proportional to θ and has degree zero. Thus the degree≤D locus for any D≥0 contains the dense Hecke orbit but excludes a very general rank-one-NS point. It is not Zariski closed. Effectivity (or a genuine norm) is indispensable.

## R24B. Bounded abelian-subvariety locus

**Statement.** Suppose the very general fibre is simple. For D≥1, the set B_D of fibres containing a nonzero proper abelian subvariety B with deg_L B≤D is proper Zariski closed.

**Proof.** Fix its dimension 1≤d≤g−1 and degree e≤D. Riemann–Roch on an abelian d-fold gives its Hilbert polynomial P_B(m)=(e/d!)m^d, since its tangent bundle has trivial Chern classes. Thus only finitely many Hilbert polynomials occur. For each, use the projective relative Hilbert scheme Hilb^P(A/S), with its flat universal subscheme U. The conditions that U contain the identity and be closed under inversion and addition form a constructible locus: use finite equations for the universal ideal, pull them back along the identity, inverse and addition maps, and stratify the base so that the relevant finitely presented sheaves and their fibrewise image conditions commute with base change. This locus is stable under specialization. Indeed, pull U back to a discrete valuation ring R along a curve through a proposed specialization, with generic fibre a subgroup. Flatness makes the generic fibre schematically dense in U_R and in U_R×_R U_R. The pullback of the ideal of U_R along the identity, inverse or addition vanishes on the generic fibre, hence vanishes identically because the corresponding structure sheaf is R-flat. The special fibre is therefore also a subgroup. A constructible subset of a finite-type C-scheme stable under specialization is closed. Equivalently this is the closed subgroup locus in the Hilbert scheme. No connectedness condition is imposed on this closed locus.

Its projective image in S is closed. In characteristic zero a finite-type group scheme over a field is smooth. Every nonempty fibre of such a subgroup scheme is therefore a finite disjoint union of translates of its connected identity component, an abelian subvariety of dimension d. The identity component has degree at most e. Conversely, every connected abelian subvariety of degree≤D is a point of one of these group loci. Taking all finitely many (d,e) therefore gives precisely B_D as a finite union of closed images. Simplicity of a very general fibre makes B_D proper. ∎

**Consequence.** Along any sequence eventually avoiding every proper closed subset, minimum degrees of nonzero proper abelian subvarieties, when such subvarieties exist, tend to infinity. Along a strictly special-generic CM sequence the same holds by applying André–Oort to each B_D. The analogous statement for extra effective divisors follows from R24A.

## R24C. Refutation: extra endomorphisms can have degree one everywhere on the orbit

**Statement.** For every A isogenous to E^g with g≥2 and E a CM elliptic curve with End^0(E)=K, there is an automorphism u∈End(A) outside any prescribed embedded field ι(K)⊂End^0(A), including the balanced Weil action, with deg(u)=1. Hence sketch 2.4's sentence that the degree of every endomorphism outside the generic ring must tend to infinity along a generic Hecke sequence is false when degree means isogeny degree.

**Proof.** Fix an identification End^0(A)≅M_g(K) and a nonzero nilpotent matrix n with n²=0, for example a matrix unit E12. Because End(A) is a full lattice in End^0(A), a positive integer m clears its denominator, giving v=mn∈End(A), v≠0 and v²=0. Then u=1+v and u^{-1}=1−v both belong to End(A). Thus u is an automorphism and has degree one. An embedded field has no nonzero nilpotents, so v∉ι(K) and u∉ι(K). This does not require the prescribed Weil action to be central in M_g(K). ∎

**Correct replacement invariant.** A polarization-sensitive graph degree, not deg(u), controls complexity of an endomorphism. With H=p1^*L⊗p2^*L on A×A,

deg_H Γ_u=∫_A(θ+u^*θ)^g.

For A=E^g with product principal polarization and u_m=I+mE12, the associated Hermitian matrix is I+u_m^†u_m; its determinant is 2^{g−2}(m²+4). Consequently

deg_H Γ_{u_m}=g!·2^{g−2}(m²+4), while deg(u_m)=1.

This calculation shows concretely why the scalar degree of an isogeny is inadequate. It does not, by itself, prove a global moduli boundedness theorem for arbitrary graph classes.

## R24D. What the input-degree obstruction does and does not prove

**Statement.** R24A–B rule out constructing nontrivial generic Weil classes by a uniformly bounded list of effective extra divisors or proper abelian-subvariety supports along a strictly special-generic CM sequence. They do **not** rule out bounded final cycles whose cohomology classes are expressed using unbounded divisor data and cancellations.

**Justification.** For any fixed input bound D, eventually no extra effective divisor or proper abelian subvariety of degree≤D exists at the sequence points. All remaining effective divisor classes of that bounded degree are proportional to θ; polynomials in those classes lie in Q[θ]. A nonzero middle Weil class is outside Qθ^n: for a∈K with a/abar not a root of unity of order at most 2n, a^* has eigenvalues a^{2n}, abar^{2n} on the Weil space, whereas it has eigenvalue (a abar)^n on θ^n. These three eigenvalues are distinct. Thus that restricted method cannot give it. This is a statement about bounded ingredients. Every Hodge class on a power of one CM elliptic curve is divisor-generated; here is the short linear-algebra verification of the exact form needed. The Hodge group is the one-dimensional norm-one torus, and over C the first cohomology splits as V+⊕V− with weights +1 and −1, each of dimension g. Its degree-2p invariants are Λ^pV+⊗Λ^pV−. Pairing the p plus and p minus factors shows that the multiplication map from degree-two invariants onto them is surjective. Surjectivity descends from C to Q; Lefschetz (1,1) makes the degree-two rational Hodge classes divisor classes. Isogenies preserve this rational-cohomological property. Therefore, on the Hecke fibres, a class of a bounded geometric cycle can still have a divisor-polynomial expression involving large degrees and coefficients/cancellations. No argument above bounds those expressions or forbids them.

**Additional correction.** A K-stable abelian n-fold has zero projected Weil component for elementary exterior-algebra reasons: the restriction of the 2n-fold K-eigenspace wedge to a K-module of complex dimension n cannot retain 2n independent eigenvectors. But the phrase “a subvariety stable under an order of K” is ambiguous unless stability is specified (under all endomorphisms, a particular integer, or a finite-order unit). Stability under a finite-order CM unit alone does not force zero Weil component. This last issue is not needed by R21–R23 and has not been expanded into a separate theorem here.

## R25. A bound on effective degrees already bounds the multiplier for a fixed Weil class

**Statement.** Let g=2n and let t be a nonzero rational flat section of the primitive middle Hodge cohomology of a polarized abelian scheme. In particular, t may be a nonzero Weil class on the Weil family. Define

V=∫_A θ^{2n}/(2n)!,   Q=(-1)^n∫_A t∪t>0.

Both quantities are independent of the fibre. If, on any fibre,

Nt=cl(Z+)−cl(Z−),  N∈Z_{>0},

for effective integral n-cycles Z±, then

N ≤ (deg_L Z+ + deg_L Z−)/(n! sqrt(QV)).

Consequently, for a fixed t, uniformly bounded degrees of integral effective cycles representing **some positive integer multiple** of t already imply uniformly bounded multipliers. In the middle Weil setting, the two complexity requirements in the sketch are not independent. This does not apply to a bound defined using rational cycle coefficients instead of integral effective cycles.

**Proof.** On each abelian fibre choose the translation-invariant Kähler metric with Kähler form representing θ. Every translation-invariant differential form is harmonic. Let α be the translation-invariant representative of t. For a primitive real (n,n) form in complex dimension 2n the Hodge-star identity is *α=(-1)^nα. Thus its L² norm is ∫α∧*α=Q, positive because t≠0. Since α and the metric are translation invariant, its pointwise Euclidean exterior norm is the constant sqrt(Q/V).

The comass of a form is at most its Euclidean exterior norm: evaluation on a unit simple multivector is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz. On the smooth part of an n-dimensional complex subvariety, θ^n/n! is the Riemannian volume form, as follows by choosing a unitary frame of its tangent space. Therefore an effective n-cycle Z of degree d, with multiplicities counted, satisfies

|∫_Z α| ≤ sqrt(Q/V)·d/n!.

Singular loci have lower real dimension and do not change the integration-current mass. Pair the assumed equality with t and use the triangle inequality:

NQ = |∫_{Z+}α−∫_{Z−}α| ≤ sqrt(Q/V)(deg Z+ + deg Z−)/n!.

Division by Q gives the stated bound. The flatness of θ and t makes V and Q constant, so the estimate is uniform without a compactness hypothesis on the base. For a Weil class, primitivity holds because θ has one K-eigenfactor of each type while either extremal Weil wedge already uses all 2n factors of one type; θ∧t=0. ∎

**Check.** Whole proof and constants reread by the author on 2026-11-21. This is an analytic/cohomological bound within algebraic geometry; it does not construct a cycle or solve the existence gate. It uses the usual primitive Hodge-star identity and the elementary complex-subspace volume identity. No GRH or special-value estimate occurs.
