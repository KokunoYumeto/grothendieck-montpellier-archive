# F59: the bounded ordinary-Hecke derived lift and its quadratic gate

Author: `/root/grothendieck_dictionary`, GPT-6 Astra Pro; effort not exposed. Date: 2026-11-21 UTC. This is a new elementary obstruction and two explicit tests, not a theorem attributed to the ordinary Hecke sources. It goes beyond F27's zero-differential Euler lift by testing an actual categorical quadratic isomorphism. The coupled module route is being investigated independently by theta.

## 1. Setting and the actual named element

Work over a characteristic-zero field `k` with an ordinary faithful reflection realization of a finite Coxeter group `W`; the usual type-A realization over the rationals suffices in every rank needed here. Let `R` be its polynomial coordinate ring, with linear coordinates in internal degree2. Let `SBim(W)` be the ordinary category of Soergel bimodules. Its morphisms have internal degree zero, and the internal shift is `M(1)^d=M^(d+1)`. This shift is distinct from cohomological shift `[1]`.

For a simple reflection `s`, write

\[
B_s=R\otimes_{R^s}R(1),\qquad b_s=[B_s],\qquad
\delta=v+v^{-1},\qquad c_s=b_s-\delta.
\tag{SG1}
\]

Here `b_s` is the upper KL generator, and `c_s` is the complementary generator in the question. The conventions give `b_s^2=delta b_s` and `c_s^2=-delta c_s`. Their product is zero in the Hecke algebra. These are identities of classes, and the last two are not being asserted as object identities.

Take two independent copies of the realization and put `A=R tensor_k R`. Work in the bounded homotopy category of `SBim(W times W)` over `A`. The two ordinary generators will also be denoted `B_1=B_s external 1`, `B_2=1 external B_s`, with `s_1=(s,e)`, `s_2=(e,s)`, and `u=s_1s_2=(s,s)`. The actual nonstandard generator has class

\[
P_s=b_s\otimes b_s+c_s\otimes c_s.
\tag{SG2}
\]

The coefficient object for its quadratic relation is

\[
\mathscr D=\mathbf1(2)\oplus\mathbf1^{\oplus2}\oplus\mathbf1(-2),
\qquad[\mathscr D]=\delta^2.
\tag{SG3}
\]

The necessary categorical relation under test is the actual homotopy isomorphism

\[
X_s\otimes_A X_s\simeq\mathscr D\otimes_A X_s.
\tag{SG4}
\]

No choice of the adjacent higher relation maps can repair failure of this one-generator condition.

## 2. The generic graph category and the amplitude lemma

Let `Q=Frac(A)`. After localization and forgetting the internal grading, the ordinary Soergel category maps monoidally to the additive category generated by the twisted graph bimodules `Q_w`, for `w in W times W`. As a left vector space `Q_w=Q`, with right action `z a=w(a)z`.

For completeness, the needed structure is elementary. Faithfulness implies

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{Q-Q}(Q_w,Q_z)=
\begin{cases}Q,&w=z,\\0,&w\ne z,\end{cases}
\qquad Q_w\otimes_Q Q_z\simeq Q_{wz},
\tag{SG5}
\]

where the tensor map is `a tensor b mapsto a w(b)`. A nonzero map between two distinct graphs would give a nonzero scalar annihilated by some `w(a)-z(a)`, which is impossible in a field. These formulas show that the additive graph category is semisimple, and that the tensor product of any two nonzero objects is nonzero. Indeed every object is a finite direct sum of graph lines, and every pair of such lines has a nonzero product.

For a simple reflection, the localized splitting is given explicitly by

\[
Q\otimes_{Q^s}Q\longrightarrow Q_e\oplus Q_s,
\qquad a\otimes b\longmapsto(ab,a\,s(b)).
\tag{SG6}
\]

Choose a nonzero root `alpha` with `s(alpha)=-alpha`. The two idempotent vectors `(1 tensor1 plus/minus alpha^-1 tensor alpha)/2` prove that SG6 is an isomorphism. Tensor products give the decomposition for Bott--Samelson bimodules, and direct summands stay in the graph category. The ordinary localization functor is monoidal; denominators can be moved through a graph line using its automorphism `w`. Thus the same functor extends to bounded complexes and sends homotopy isomorphisms to homotopy isomorphisms. Call it `Lambda`. Internal shifts become invisible in this ungraded test.

**Lemma SG-A (generic amplitude).** If a bounded complex `Z` of finite sums of graph lines is nonzero in the homotopy category and

\[
Z\otimes_Q Z\simeq Z^{\oplus m},\qquad m>0,
\tag{SG7}
\]

then its cohomology is concentrated in degree zero.

**Proof.** In a semisimple category every bounded complex splits as its cohomology with zero differential plus contractible pairs. Tensoring is exact and preserves contractibility, so the Kunneth direct-sum formula holds. Let `p` and `r` be the least and greatest nonzero cohomology degrees. The groups in degrees `2p` and `2r` of `Z tensor Z` include, respectively, the nonzero objects `H^p(Z) tensor H^p(Z)` and `H^r(Z) tensor H^r(Z)`. There is no cohomology beyond those extremes. The right side of SG7 has extremes `p,r`. Therefore `2p=p` and `2r=r`, hence `p=r=0`. No positivity of an Euler trace, purity, t-exact geometric convolution, or absence of arithmetic Jordan blocks is used. This is a statement about this semisimple graph category. ∎

The graph Euler Grothendieck ring is `Z[W times W]`. In it,

\[
[\Lambda B_s]=e+s,\qquad [\Lambda c_s]=s-e,
\qquad [\Lambda P_s]=2(e+u),
\tag{SG8}
\]

where the middle notation means the image of that virtual Hecke class. It is not a graph object with negative dimension. Formula SG8 is precisely the specialization `v=1` of the ordinary Hecke character homomorphism, which is enough for this necessary test.

## 3. A direct-sum obstruction independent of every cone choice

**Theorem SG-B.** There is no bounded complex `X_s` satisfying SG4, with Euler class SG2, and with a direct-sum decomposition

\[
X_s\simeq (B_s\boxtimes B_s)\oplus Y_s,
\qquad [Y_s]=c_s\otimes c_s.
\tag{SG9}
\]

In particular, for any bounded ordinary-Hecke complexes `C_s^(1), C_s^(2)` whose Euler classes are `c_s`, the recipe

\[
X_s=(B_s\boxtimes B_s)\oplus
       (C_s^{(1)}\boxtimes C_s^{(2)})
\tag{SG10}
\]

fails the quadratic relation. The two cone choices need not agree, and their differentials and amplitudes are arbitrary.

**Proof.** Apply `Lambda`. SG3 becomes four copies of the tensor unit, so SG4 becomes SG7 with `m=4`. The localized complex is nonzero because its Euler class is `2(e+u)`, not zero. Lemma SG-A forces `Lambda(X_s)` into cohomological degree zero.

The direct summand `Lambda(Y_s)` must therefore also have cohomology only in degree zero. Its Euler class must have nonnegative integer graph multiplicities. But the asserted class gives

\[
[\Lambda(Y_s)]=(s_1-e)(s_2-e)
               =e+u-s_1-s_2.
\tag{SG11}
\]

The four graph labels are distinct in the product group. The two negative coefficients contradict effectivity of a degree-zero graph object. This proves the theorem. The only property of the two complexes in SG10 used is their Euler class, so SG10 follows from the same argument. ∎

This proof works for each simple generator in every ordinary type-A rank, and more generally in the faithful setting above. It is not merely a rank-one numerical check and it is not the F27 term-parity argument: it tests arbitrary differentials and the requested actual quadratic isomorphism. It would already fail after generic localization, so enlarging the coefficient field without changing the category or direct-sum premise cannot repair it.

**A second necessary condition.** Any bounded `X_s` with class SG2 and SG4, whether decomposed as SG9 or not, must satisfy

\[
H^i\Lambda(X_s)=0\quad(i\ne0),\qquad
H^0\Lambda(X_s)\simeq Q_e^{\oplus2}\oplus Q_u^{\oplus2}.
\tag{SG12}
\]

This follows from the same lemma and the Euler character. It does not prohibit a complex whose differentials connect the two virtual summands. In particular, no general impossibility theorem for all coupled bounded complexes is being asserted.

## 4. The actual unit cone and its explicit failed square

The usual dot and multiplication maps make the first test concrete. In one factor choose the root `alpha`, and set

\[
\eta:R(-1)\longrightarrow B_s,
\quad f\longmapsto f(\alpha\otimes1+1\otimes\alpha),
\qquad
m:B_s\longrightarrow R(1),\quad f\otimes g\longmapsto fg.
\tag{SG13}
\]

The maps preserve the displayed internal grading. The invariant polynomial ring is a direct summand decomposition `R=R^s direct-sum alpha R^s`. It gives exact sequences of graded bimodules

\[
0\longrightarrow R_s(-1)
 \xrightarrow{\ f\mapsto f(1\otimes\alpha-\alpha\otimes1)\ }
 B_s\xrightarrow{m}R(1)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{SG14}
\]

\[
0\longrightarrow R(-1)\xrightarrow{\eta}B_s
 \xrightarrow{\ f\otimes g\mapsto f\,s(g)\ }R_s(1)
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{SG15}
\]

One can verify them on the left `R` basis `1 tensor1, 1 tensor alpha`. The first kernel is the anti-invariant graph line and the second kernel is the invariant graph line; the two displayed target maps are surjective. This also proves injectivity, since each displayed nonzero kernel vector is part of the corresponding rank-one kernel over the domain.

Take the specified complementary cone

\[
C_s=\operatorname{Cone}\bigl(R(-1)\oplus R(1)
       \xrightarrow{(\eta,0)}B_s\bigr),
\tag{SG16}
\]

with the source in degree minus one and target in degree zero. It has Euler class `c_s`. In fact every internal-degree-zero map from the two displayed unit shifts to `B_s` has this form up to a scalar: a central element of `B_s` is a polynomial multiple of `alpha tensor1+1 tensor alpha`, whose smallest degree is1. There is no eligible central element in degree minus one, and the degree1 space is one dimensional. The zero scalar remains covered by Theorem SG-B.

For the displayed nonzero map, SG15 identifies its image in the ordinary derived category of bimodules as

\[
C_s\simeq R_s(1)\oplus R(1)[1].
\tag{SG17}
\]

Consequently SG10 has, in that derived category, the direct-sum description

\[
(B_s\boxtimes B_s)\oplus A_u(2)
\oplus A_{s_1}(2)[1]\oplus A_{s_2}(2)[1]
\oplus A(2)[2].
\tag{SG18}
\]

All five displayed modules are free on either side. Its least nonzero cohomological degree is minus two, with cohomology `A(2)`. The square therefore has nonzero degree-minus-four cohomology `A(4)`, while SG3 tensored with SG18 has no degree-minus-four cohomology. This gives a concrete failed quadratic, visible before generic localization. The natural maps genuinely construct a bounded complex; what they fail to construct is the relation SG4. The opposite counit-cone convention yields the corresponding upper-amplitude failure. Theorem SG-B makes a separate calculation of every convention unnecessary.

## 5. A coupled Rouquier Euler lift passes the generic test but still fails the quadratic

Let

\[
F_s=[B_s\xrightarrow{m}R(1)],\qquad
E_s=[R(-1)\xrightarrow{\eta}B_s],
\tag{SG19}
\]

where `B_s` is in degree zero in each complex. They are the familiar positive and inverse Rouquier complexes in this normalization. Their classes are `b_s-v` and `b_s-v^-1`. The following coupled Euler lift uses their actual differentials:

\[
X_s^{\rm Rouq}=(F_s\boxtimes F_s)
 \oplus(E_s\boxtimes E_s)\oplus A^{\oplus2}.
\tag{SG20}
\]

Expanding its class gives

\[
(b_s-v)\otimes(b_s-v)
 +(b_s-v^{-1})\otimes(b_s-v^{-1})+2
=2b_s\otimes b_s-\delta(b_s\otimes1+1\otimes b_s)+\delta^2=P_s.
\tag{SG21}
\]

Here the scalar in each factor is understood over the common Laurent coefficient ring; the two constant terms are `v^2` and `v^-2`. The missing constant2 is supplied by the two unit summands. Thus this is the actual required Euler class, not the changed inclusion of F16.

Sequences SG14--SG15 give

\[
F_s\simeq R_s(-1),\qquad E_s\simeq R_s(1)
\tag{SG22}
\]

in the derived category of bimodules. Therefore

\[
X_s^{\rm Rouq}\simeq A^{\oplus2}\oplus A_u(-2)\oplus A_u(2)
\tag{SG23}
\]

is concentrated in cohomological degree zero there. It satisfies SG12 after ungraded generic localization, which demonstrates why Theorem SG-B cannot be extended to every Euler lift by the same argument.

Nevertheless SG4 still fails, now for an internal-grading reason. The two graph modules `A` and `A_u` have no nonzero bimodule map between them: a putative map is killed by a nonzero `a-u(a)` in the domain. Accordingly any isomorphism between sums of their internal shifts preserves the two graph summands and their graded free ranks. On the identity graph, SG23 squared has character

\[
4+(v^2+v^{-2})^2=v^4+6+v^{-4},
\tag{SG24}
\]

whereas `mathscr D tensor X_s^Rouq` has identity-graph character

\[
2\delta^2=2v^2+4+2v^{-2}.
\tag{SG25}
\]

They are unequal. Since all graph modules in SG23 are free on either side, these are the actual derived tensor products, with no omitted Tor groups. Thus the two sides are not isomorphic even in the derived category of graded bimodules, and hence not in the bounded homotopy category of Soergel bimodules.

This test does not use the known braid relations of Rouquier complexes. It only uses their specified maps, the displayed exact sequences, and the actual quadratic relation demanded here. In particular ordinary braid coherence does not automatically give nonstandard quadratic coherence.

## 6. What remains after these tests

The direct-sum lift that treats the two written terms of `P_s` as separate objects is excluded in the bounded ordinary Hecke category. Any proposed repair must either couple them before passing to homotopy, change the category or coefficient comparison, or weaken the required quadratic isomorphism. A specific coupled complex was constructed in SG20 and its failure was proved; this is not an exhaustion of coupled complexes.

At the generic ungraded level the required support itself is consistent: `Q_e^2 direct-sum Q_u^2` squares to four copies of itself. This follows by multiplying the two-element subgroup `{e,u}`. Thus there is no generic semisimple obstruction to a coupled object with SG12. The unresolved issue is descending a finite graded complex with the actual quadratic relation, and then giving the higher nonstandard relation maps coherently. Nothing here supplies an identification with VIII's designated global lattices or a finite-field arithmetic realization.

These facts sharpen F27 without repeating it. F27 constructed an Euler lift and established a parity obstruction in the specified KL cone. Theorem SG-B instead allows all bounded differentials in the complementary object and uses the required categorical quadratic identity to force generic concentration. SG20 explicitly shows that connecting the positive and negative class terms can overcome generic cohomological amplitude while leaving a genuine grading obstruction.
