## Direct checks behind the retained early algebra statements

For a nilpotent element x in any associative Q-algebra, the formal identities exp(log(1+T))=1+T and log(exp(T))=T become finite polynomial identities after quotienting by a power of T. If x and y commute, all identities lie in their commutative subalgebra and x+y is nilpotent. A subspace L consisting of nilpotents and closed under powers is therefore exchanged with1+L by these formulas. If L is also closed under multiplication, (1+x)(1+y)=1+(x+y+xy) and the finite geometric inverse keep1+L a group. Its commutativity is equivalent to xy=yx. This verifies the scope of the first prior SA22-10 event without requiring an associative or commutative hypothesis on an unrelated algebra multiplication in the later derivation criterion.

For Example1, write a particular element of JA as a finite sum sum(j_k*a_k). The finitely many j_k generate a nilpotent ideal of the **commutative** scalar ring because each j_k is nilpotent; a sufficiently long monomial must contain too many copies of one generator. The coefficients are central in A, so the same nilpotence bound kills the element of JA. No uniform bound for the whole ideal J is inferred. For Example2, u(M) contained in JM implies u^r(M) contained in J^r M by K-linearity, giving a uniform bound when J is nilpotent. To see why J merely nil does not suffice in general, use K=Q[x_1,x_2,...]/(x_i^(i+1):i>=1), J=(x_i), and M=direct_sum_i K e_i with u(e_i)=x_i e_i. The ideal J is nil, each vector has finite support, and u(M) is contained in JM; but u^r(e_i) is nonzero whenever i>=r. Thus u is not uniformly nilpotent.

For the fixed algebra-units functor U_R=U tensor_K R, a natural group homomorphism from G_a is determined at t in K[t] by one element of U[t], hence by globally finitely many coefficients a_j in U. The group law gives a_0=1 and binom(i+j,i)a_{i+j}=a_i*a_j, so a_j=a_1^j/j!. Global polynomial finiteness forces a_1 nilpotent. The analogous universal nilpotent rings K[t]/t^(n+1) give formal actions for arbitrary a_1, with compatibility under n. For an arbitrary module, evaluating on each vector gives only pointwise polynomial finiteness, exactly as proved in C-qcoh.

The scheme-local reduced assertion also has a direct check. A derivation in characteristic zero preserves the nilradical: if a^r=0, then differentiating r times shows r!*(Da)^r is a multiple of a (all other product-rule terms leave an undifferentiated factor a); raising to a further r-th power gives Da nilpotent. On a reduced Q-algebra, if D^N=0, the multiplicative polynomial exp(tD)(a) has degree at most N-1 for every a. A nonconstant such polynomial with nonzero leading coefficient would have arbitrarily large degree after raising to powers, because the reduced leading coefficient has no zero power. But its m-th power is exp(tD)(a^m), still of degree at most N-1, a contradiction. Hence D=0 on the reduced algebra. This proves identity on the reduced scheme when uniform nilpotence holds on every suitable open set; it does not apply to d/dx on Q[x].

For the retained bracket at68/022, a pair(f,f) is pretasse exactly when an aligned reflected copy of f realizes its self inner product. The equality criterion gives equality of all aligned positive superlevel sets (their sizes agree), hence f=delta_s*check(f). This is a peak/pretasse condition, not full majorization/tasse. This verifies the mathematical content of the inserted “pré” without making a new palaeographic claim.
