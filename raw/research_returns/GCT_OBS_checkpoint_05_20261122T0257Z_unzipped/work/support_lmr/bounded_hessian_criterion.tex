\subsection*{OBS-008. A bounded-Hessian criterion and the size-23 row-18 exclusion}
Let $X$ be a nonempty open subset of an affine space over a
characteristic-zero field, and let $f=(l,g)$ be a polynomial map into
$L\oplus G$.  Suppose $l$ is dominant, $df$ has generic rank $r$,
and $c=\dim L+\dim G-r$.  On a pivot open choose a rational frame
$W$ of its left kernel and rational directions
$a_1,\ldots,a_s\in\ker dl$.
Allow a change of high coordinates
\[
                         y=B(l)g+b(l),
\]
where $B(l)$ is invertible on that open.  Other centered lower
coordinates may be polynomial functions $z=z(l)$ with dominant
image.

Let $\mathcal B\subset k[z,y]$ be a vector space closed under the
partials $\partial/\partial y_j$.  Suppose there is a fixed
finite-dimensional space $Q\subset\operatorname{Sym}^2(G^*)$
such that the quadratic Taylor term in the high variables of every
$P\in\mathcal B$, at every point, belongs to $Q$.  Choose a basis
$q_1,\ldots,q_h$ of $Q$, and write the same letters for their symmetric
bilinear polarizations.  Form $c+h$ symmetric bilinear forms on the
span of the $a_i$:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  &(a_i,a_j)\longmapsto W_\nu\,\tfrac12d^2f(a_i,a_j),
                    &&1\leq\nu\leq c,\\
  &(a_i,a_j)\longmapsto q_\mu(B(l)dg(a_i),B(l)dg(a_j)),
                    &&1\leq\mu\leq h.
 \end{aligned}                                                 \tag{BH-1}
\]
If these forms are linearly independent at one point, then no nonzero
element of $\mathcal B$ vanishes on the image of the centered map
$(z(l),B(l)g+b(l))$.

\begin{proof}
The selected nonzero minor persists on a nonempty open subset, since
all frames, directions and coordinate changes vary rationally there.
Suppose $P$ vanishes on the image, and view it as a function of the
ordinary coordinates $(l,g)$.  Its gradient $w$ lies in the left
kernel of $df$, so $w=\eta W$.  For $a\in\ker dl$, the quadratic
Taylor identity is
\[
 0=w\,\tfrac12d^2f(a,a)
       +\tfrac12 d_y^2P(B(l)dg(a),B(l)dg(a)).                  \tag{BH-2}
\]
The second term belongs to $Q$ by hypothesis.  Polarization of
(BH-2), followed by the independence of all forms in (BH-1), forces
both its normal and its $Q$ coefficients to be zero.  In particular
$w=0$ on the open set.  The ordinary high-gradient therefore vanishes.
Since $B(l)$ is invertible, every \emph{centered} partial
$\partial P/\partial y_j$ also vanishes on the image.

Induct on the degree in $y$.  The centered partials belong to
$\mathcal B$ and have smaller degree, so by induction they are zero
as polynomials.  Thus $P$ is independent of $y$.  Dominance of the
lower centered coordinates gives $P=0$.  This also proves the degree
zero base case.  The induction is on centered partials; it does not
assume that multiplication by the lower-dependent coefficients of
$B(l)$ preserves the representation bound.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The first-row-at-most-eighteen class.}
For the application let $E=\mathbb C^3$, put
\[
 Z=\bigoplus_{j=2}^{16}\operatorname{Sym}^jE,
 \qquad G^*=\operatorname{Sym}^{17}E\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^{18}E,
\]
and take $\mathcal B$ to be the sum of the polynomial
$\mathrm{GL}_3$ isotypic components with first row at most18 in
$\operatorname{Sym}(Z\oplus G^*)$.  This is a vector space, not an
asserted subalgebra.  It is closed under all centered high partials:
the degree-one comultiplication is equivariant, and a constituent
$S_\beta$ in $S_\mu\otimes S_\nu$ requires both $\mu$ and $\nu$
to be contained in $\beta$.  Thus each constituent of the remaining
factor after differentiation still has first row at most18.
The same argument with degree-two comultiplication bounds every high
Hessian component by the summands of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(G^*)$
whose first rows are at most18.  They are exactly
\[
 Q=S_{(18,16)}E\oplus S_{(18,17)}E\oplus S_{(18,18)}E.         \tag{BH-3}
\]
The three dimensions, from the $\mathrm{GL}_3$ Weyl formula, are
\[
  \frac{3\cdot20\cdot17}{2}=510,\qquad
  \frac{2\cdot20\cdot18}{2}=360,\qquad
  \frac{1\cdot20\cdot19}{2}=190,
\]
and hence $\dim Q=1060$.  Every polynomial in this bounded class
has total $\mathrm{GL}_3$ weight at most54, so its highjet degree
is at most three, although the abstract induction only needs finiteness.

\paragraph{The determinant test.}
For $F=\det(x_0I_{23}+\sum_{i=1}^3x_iA_i)$, choose ordinary jets
1 through16 as $l$ and ordinary jets17 and18 as $g$.  Their dimensions
are968 and361.  The established generic rank bound is1059, so the
full normal space has dimension270.  A complement to the full
Jacobian kernel inside the lower kernel has dimension91.  Testing
all symmetric pairs of91 independent relative tangent directions
gives $91\cdot92/2=4186$ columns.  The test matrix therefore has
\[
                         270+1060=1330
\]
rows and4186 columns.

The coordinate change must be made in normalized tensor entries.
Along lower-kernel directions, $dC_{17}=dT_{17}$ and
$dC_{18}=dT_{18}-18\ell\,dT_{17}$, where
$\ell=T_1/F_0$, with $T_1$ the normalized order-one tensor.
On the chart $F_0=1$ this means
$\ell_k=\operatorname{tr}(A_k)/23$.
For a tensor multi-index $\alpha$ of order18,
the latter formula is
\[
 d(C_{18})_\alpha=d(T_{18})_\alpha
      -\sum_{k=1}^3\alpha_k\ell_k\,d(T_{17})_{\alpha-e_k}.
\]
This explains the coefficient $\alpha_k$, rather than18, in the
coordinate implementation.  The transformation is triangular and
invertible.  Lower dominance also gives dominance of
$(F_1,C_2,\ldots,C_{16})$ by the polynomial centering change.

The exact certificate below, (BH-1)--(BH-3), S-001's
coefficient support, and the fixed-degree injectivity of $F_0=1$
prove $K_{23}(\lambda)=a_\lambda$ for every four-row partition
with $\lambda_2\leq18$, in every outer degree.  This excludes an
actual padded-permanent multiplicity obstruction there even without
assuming its padded multiplicity is ambient.  The resulting necessary
condition for the main $(17,23)$ problem is $\lambda_2\geq19$.
