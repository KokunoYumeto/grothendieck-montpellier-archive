\subsection*{BR-009: a complete permanent ten-plane gate with 595 remaining orbits}
\textbf{Status: proved.  Complete author independent standard-library computation
PASS, with root whole-proof and full new-checker/source review completed.
No fresh root rerun of all 225702 representatives is claimed.}

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the question
$\mu_{10}(U^{\mathrm{per}}_{4,2})\ge18$ reduces to exactly $595$ explicit
coordinate-subspace orbits under row permutations, column permutations,
and transposition.  The complete ordered list is the $596$ records in
\texttt{certificates/per10\_global\_gate.json}, with the record
of mask $2015$, namely $\{0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10\}$, removed by BR-008.
This is a finite necessary gate, not a proof of the desired minimum.
An orbit left open need not have border rank seventeen.

\paragraph{Why coordinate orbits suffice.}
The row/column diagonal torus acts on the thirty-six permanent
quadratics with thirty-six distinct characters: the character is the
sum of the two selected row weights and the two selected column
weights.  The locus in $\operatorname{Gr}(10,U)$ where the tautological
inclusion tensor has border rank at most seventeen is closed and
invariant.  This follows locally on Grassmannian frame charts from
closedness of the affine secant cone; changes of the first-factor
frame preserve border rank.  If the locus is nonempty, its projectivity
and the torus fixed-point theorem give a fixed point.  Since every
weight space of $U$ is one-dimensional, that point is the span of ten
coordinate quadratics.  Thus it suffices to consider all
$\binom{36}{10}=254186856$ ten-subsets.

The group $(\mathfrak S_4\times\mathfrak S_4)\rtimes\mathbb Z/2$
acts on these subsets by row and column permutation and transposition.
Its order is $1152$, and every action is induced by invertible changes
in the three tensor factors.  All tensors in one orbit have the same
border rank.  Encode a subset $S$ by the integer
$m(S)=\sum_{q\in S}2^q$, using the established lexicographic rectangle
indexing.  Select the smallest integer in each orbit.

\paragraph{An independent complete orbit certificate.}
A short C++ generator enumerates subsets in colex order and marks a
bitset indexed by the combinatorial-number-system rank.  It generated
$225702$ candidate representatives in $15.8132$ seconds.  No conclusion
below relies on trusting that generator.  The independent checker
\texttt{check\_per10\_global\_gate.py}, using only Python's standard
library and the already reviewed exact linear-algebra module, reads
only the representative masks and asserted orbit sizes.  For every
record it constructs all $1152$ group images from the four-point
permutations, verifies that their minimum equals the stated
representative, and verifies the exact number of distinct images.
It also verifies that all representative masks are distinct and have
exactly ten bits in the prescribed thirty-six positions.

Two distinct minimal representatives cannot lie in the same orbit:
otherwise the two orbits would have the same minimum.  Thus their
orbits are disjoint.  The checker sums their independently recomputed
sizes and obtains exactly $254186856$.  This proves exhaustive
coverage, without any assumption about a random sample or about the
C++ bitset implementation.

\paragraph{Every numerical exclusion is recomputed.}
For each representative the checker reconstructs the forty nonzero
ordered coefficients of its literal permanent tensor.  It constructs
Koszul maps along the ten-dimensional first factor in exterior degrees
$1,2,3,4$, followed when necessary by exterior degrees $2,3,4$ along
the used variable factor.  Every map is computed by its full torus
weight blocks using exact elimination modulo the verified prime
$1000003$.  A reported rank $\rho$ supplies a nonzero integral
$\rho$-minor modulo that prime and therefore a nonzero minor over
$\mathbb Q$.  A pure tensor contributes rank at most
$\binom{d-1}{p}$ to a degree-$p$ Koszul map along a factor of dimension
$d$.  Consequently
\[
 \rho>17\binom{d-1}{p}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \underline R(T_S)\ge18
 \qquad\text{in characteristic zero}.
\]
The computation does not extrapolate this numerical exclusion to every
positive characteristic.
