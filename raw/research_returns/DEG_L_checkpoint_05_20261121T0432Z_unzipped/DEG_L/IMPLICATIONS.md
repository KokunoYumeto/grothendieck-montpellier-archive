## The degree and L-value question

T6.1 identifies the effective surface degree with a positive sum of norms of all pulled-back polarization-coordinate wedges. T6.2 separates it from the pure four-factor codimension-two output, whose degree for the product polarization is zero. T6.3 refutes a raw degree determined by source level alone, because the construction permits multiplication of its denominator-clearing maps at the same level. The meaningful normalized Chow cycle is unchanged under auxiliary covers and these map multiplications once its divisor kernels are chosen as biextensions (T6.4–T6.5).

T6.7 removes a proposed analytic obstruction: algebraic norm and trace clear the nonzero extracted period without requiring a lower bound for its individual complex absolute value. T6.8 bounds the effective degrees created by fixed divisor kernels. These statements retain the coefficient field, integral generator lattice and kernel constants explicitly.

T6.9 uses the supplied human proof and the checked theta spanning literature to cover every pulled-back holomorphic coordinate. The Hodge-index estimate B5 therefore reduces the effective surface degree to a finite character-theta Gram form. The global character factor occurs at 3/2 and has uniform elementary Euler-product bounds for fixed field degree (B1). B1a also computes the archimedean factor in the actual cohomological normalization. B14 proves that unit input norm and fixed target level do not bound a split local factor; it supplies sharp shell-support and weighted-seminorm bounds instead. B17 then removes arbitrary radial support from a specified existing split theta vector by an exact one-shell section, using the global norm identity to prove preservation of the vector itself. It also gives uniformly bounded analytic test inputs, with actual compact level and averaging denominators retained. The remaining issue is the size of the output in its integral Hom normalization. B18 gives a uniform positive lower bound for the normalized Weil period once a generic middle-dimensional integral witness is already realized; it does not supply that preceding realization or degree bound. T6.12–T6.13 now prove the exact finite wedge-norm and degree formulas through rank-two sources, including actual multiplicity copies, local forms and unitary intertwiners. T6.14 verifies the dependence on finite adelic level. T6.15 now constructs an actual balanced theta wedge and CM-Albanese surface map whose degree formula contains a rank-two source with nonzero Steinberg monodromy. This refutes universal character-only reduction of the source parameters. Its scope is a decomposable balanced relation; it does not decide a preselected indecomposable switch relation or compare isolated numerical L-values.

## Route 2 and the Hodge conjecture

B6 constructs the Weil projector using only a fixed generic imaginary quadratic action, with an explicit resultant denominator. B7 bounds the projected component of an effective cycle by its degree in a positive, flat Weil metric. B8 turns any nonzero effective Weil witness into a representative of every fixed Weil class, with both denominator and signed effective degree bounded quadratically in the witness degree. DIV1 supplies the proper parameter-space argument; DIV2 carries a uniform witness bound on a dense set to every fibre.

T6.16 extends the source-parameter counterexample to an actual 032 witness for four distinct labeled CM types satisfying an indecomposable switch. Prescribed mixed real discrete series, fixed-input continuity, a finite Hecke projector and sequential extraction preserve both a nonzero four-map period and a degree term with Steinberg monodromy. This resolves the existential distinct-switch question left open by T6.15. It does not claim the same occurrence in every prescribed relation or identify numerical special values with geometric Galois constituents.

T6.17 gives a positive analytic conclusion for this actual fixed witness and its four CM isogeny classes. Short integral isogenies control each polarization eigenvalue, including for imprimitive CM types. Further actual integer multiplications balance the four factors. A single common metric-period normalization then bounds the energy, the ratio of raw degree to metric period, and all coefficients in fixed finite theta presentations. This removes the fixed-source analytic normalization gate in that setting. The metric period and the algebraically normalized Betti coefficient are different data; the construction does not bound integral cycle degree or the denominator of the Betti division.

T6.18 computes the actual integral Betti coefficient eigenlines. Their full pullback image is the core/hull ideal, with additive index J_i H_i in each factor, where H_i is the maximal-order hull index; a compatible principal polarization gives H_i=J_i. The specified four-factor period ideal therefore has index product(J_i H_i), and product(J_i squared) in the principal case. The final period remains in the fixed Galois CM coefficient field. This closes the missing eigenline-normalization calculation behind T6.17. It does not infer an integer trace denominator from ideal index alone: a fully computed Gaussian-prime example shows why that inference fails.

This is a strict reduction of the denominator and projector problem. It is not a proof of uniformly bounded witnesses in the open Weil families. No new general Hodge-conjecture case is asserted from the presently established estimates. The supplied Markman theorem yields a posteriori bounded complexity for abelian fourfold families by DIV5. That existence argument gives no numerical constant or explicit theta representative. B15 gives a different unconditional quantitative result: from one fixed representative, an O-linear polarized isogeny supplies a bound linear in its degree, even if it rotates the Weil plane. When it transports the prescribed flat class by a scalar, the bound improves to the square root of the isogeny degree. Neither is a bound independent of the isogeny.

B19 removes an additional comparison in a fixed simple CM isogeny class. The positive metric covolume of the integral Hom lattice is exactly proportional to the index J of the maximal-order core of the target lattice. Its smallest nonzero squared norm has order J^(1/g). A small genuine isogeny therefore yields effective witness degree O(sqrt J) and signed complexity O(J), with explicit fixed-field constants and a conductor-norm upper bound. On a fixed source, normalizing the generic Weil period gives uniformly bounded analytic energy and finite theta coordinates; that real normalization changes the integral map lattice. B20 proves that taking reduced images of a fixed finite list of reference witnesses does not remove the index: finite stabilizers leave their degrees bounded below by a positive constant times sqrt J. B21 strengthens this from a finite list to the entire family of bounded-degree source subvarieties: a universal Hilbert stabilizer is finite, giving a uniform stabilizer-order bound. B22 makes the bound explicit by intersecting at most n+1 translates and applying the generalized Bezout inequality. A uniformly bounded target degree would require source degree at least c J^(1/(2(n+1))); for surfaces the exponent is1/6. Thus even target-dependent selection among source witnesses of bounded degree cannot remove square-root index growth. These results identify the arithmetic cost of the fixed-reference construction while leaving unrelated target-dependent bounded cycles open.

B22a strengthens that necessary source-degree growth to J^(1/4) for middle-dimensional subvarieties of a simple source. The difference map has positive degree equal to the self-intersection; quotienting by the full stabilizer leaves a positive even integral square. A self-dual middle-form comass estimate then gives |Stab Z| at most D squared divided by 4(n!) squared V0. The sharper explicit integer bound also reduces the lcm needed for reduced images below.

B23 eliminates cancellation among arbitrarily many integral reference transports as a way to remove the arithmetic index. In the generic Weil determinant lattice the sum of all isogeny pushforward images is exactly the conjugate determinant-core ideal times the target lattice, of index J, including ramified primes. A relation Nt made from arbitrary integral pushes must satisfy J dividing R squared N squared c_t. With a bounded source degree, the same assertion holds for reduced images after multiplying N by the explicit lcm of all possible mapping multiplicities. A fixed nonmaximal quadratic order costs only a controlled conductor factor. This is an obstruction to the specified construction, not to arbitrary target cycles; reduced-image constructions with unbounded stabilizers remain to be attacked.

B24 rules out a bounded-index escape on an infinite fixed-field, fixed-type, fixed-volume polarized CM set. It counts bounded-index targets using ideal classes, bounded-index overlattices and polarization image lattices modulo real-unit squares. Such an infinite set necessarily has unbounded J. No Hecke-density theorem is asserted. Together B23 and B24 identify a common integral-content obstruction throughout every infinite fixed-data target collection, beyond an estimate for a single preferred isogeny.

T6.19 computes both scalar trace content and the full unscaled tensor-presentation index on actual principally polarized CM families. The scalar formula uses the minimum local valuation; the full tensor formula uses a maximum. T6.20 then removes a possible overinterpretation: all input-isogeny factors cancel exactly in the normalized Chow class at fixed output, by birigidification and the projection formula. Fixed integral input eigenvectors give a common Chow denominator N0 equal to the absolute norm of their nonzero period, for every factorwise integral output tensor.

T6.21 identifies the remaining primitive-lattice comparison exactly. If M is the additive product of the four output homology lattices, with the prescribed conjugations, the integral orbit is its trace dual and the factorwise trace span is the inverse maximal-order hull. Their index is |Disc(E)|[Mplus:M]. If one output factor is stable under the maximal order, M is already an ideal and the saturation exponent is the fixed different exponent d_E. Thus N0*d_E represents every integral orbit class by an integral algebraic cycle uniformly over the other three lattices. When all four factors vary, actual principal families have saturation exponent p^r. That is a limitation of the factorized construction, not an intrinsic obstruction to other algebraic cycles. A comparison with the generic quadratic Weil plane still requires the actual integral correspondence, and no signed-cycle degree bound is inferred from this division result.

B25 now treats the unbounded-stabilizer escape left by B23. Saturating the kernel intersection with the source stabilizer under the quadratic order produces an intermediate quotient on which the residual map is birational on the image. Bounded target degree forces bounded residual degree and an intermediate core index at least a fixed positive multiple of the target core index. If this saturated kernel is stable under the full CM order, the intermediate core is one and the original obstruction remains. B25a shows the alternative is real when simplicity is dropped: explicit product CM fourfolds have degree-two source and target surfaces but target core m^4, with multiplicity m^2 removed by the quotient. The quotient has already imported the target arithmetic complexity.

B26 imports the known principal-angle Weil calibration and equality rigidity from rStation4/v1, Theorem1(b) in Section2 and Theorem3(a),(c) in Section4. Its new contribution here is the exact q/V normalization and integral gcd margin. It improves the residual-degree constants in B25 and makes the simple-ambient inequality strict, without changing the growth exponent or constructing new witnesses.

## Semiregularity and deformation

The 032 paper supplies a morphism with two-dimensional image, not a smooth embedded surface. S-01 shows that its permitted covers do not even preserve injectivity of that morphism. S-02 is the exact conormal-cohomology test for an actual smooth embedding. S-03 and S-11 refute general shortcuts from a nonzero period or from an ample complete intersection to semiregularity.

For the rigid ball-quotient source, S-04 forces the generated quotient to remain constant in any embedded semiregular deformation of its entire class. S-05–S-06 refute universal semiregularity of all deeper-level morphism presentations even for the full generalized map: the obstruction space grows beyond its fixed Hodge target. S-07 removes that cover growth by equivariant descent but returns to the base-level obstruction.

S-09 produces a perfect-complex K-class whose surviving Chern character is the desired projected class plus a chosen polarization polynomial. It removes extraneous Hodge conditions at the K-class level. It does not construct a semiregular object. S-10 explains why an equivalence alone does not remove an existing full trace kernel. S-12 shows that preserving a pure Weil character without polarization data can force a contradiction with Voisin's nonprojective torus family for coherent sheaves. S-13 excludes a single exponential repair in that precise coherent fourfold setting. S-14, using Perry Theorem1.2, reduces the construction to one gluable weakly target-equivariant perfect object whose full B-normalized character is P(theta)+lambda t. It already implies bounded division. S-15 shows that canonical source-cover lifts do not provide such a repair, and S-16 separates bundle realization from trace injectivity. DER1 gives a strict discriminant constraint for a Hermite–Einstein attempt; DER2–DER3 show that the canonical globally generated syzygy can pass that constraint while preserving a full trace kernel. DER5 removes their positivity restrictions: nonzero-Euler convolution and nonzero-virtual-rank tensor functors are faithful on all morphisms, so the ideal-diagonal evaluation cone preserves the kernel for every perfect object on a positive-dimensional abelian variety. DER6 shows that a polarization-polynomial tensor correction either has nonzero virtual rank and retains the kernel, or has rank zero and removes the desired Weil component.

DER4 makes the successful-object implication constructive without requiring nonzero virtual rank. An Adams operation whose degree equals the band order of the Kummer gerbe descends to ordinary perfect K-theory and multiplies the Weil component by M^n. A relative bundle presentation, a generation twist, and a Chern degeneracy locus yield fixed explicit N and D once those finite choices are supplied. DIV1 carries that very bound from the local etale image to every fibre. This step requires no proper moduli space of complexes and no Baire-category extraction of an unspecified constant. The remaining geometric problem is existence of the original weakly equivariant semiregular object with the complete compatible character. DIV3 is the separate embedded-cycle implication.

DER7 further makes the multiplier independent of the gerbe order when the virtual rank r is nonzero. The Adams operation of degree |r|, tensored by the appropriate determinant power, has exactly zero inertia weight and retains |r|^n times the Weil coefficient. Integrality of the resulting Chern-cycle degree gives N*=v|b beta0||r|^n without estimating the denominators of the whole polarization polynomial. Its common degree bound still depends on the finite generation choices. S-17 is the tensor–determinant alternative, and S-18 excludes canonical categorical induction as a way to remove an existing full kernel. These operations are used at different stages: untwisting happens after the successful deformation and is not claimed to preserve semiregularity.

S-19 and S-20 quantify the failure of the actual genus-four isolated-point and partial-normalization secant seeds. Their qualitative failure and the CY4 adjunction criterion were already recorded in rStation paper37/v4; the present bounds are respectively ext2 at least24d+6 and full-kernel dimension at least4d-3. S-21 extends the obstruction from the prior free-translation setting to every effective coherent finite translation/tensor action. Vanishing of nonidentity Hochschild coefficient sectors, induction/forgetting and categorical Riemann--Roch force an equivariantly semiregular imaginary-secant seed to have endomorphism dimension strictly larger than2. Equivariantly simple seeds are therefore excluded even when the underlying object is nonsimple. The new argument does not impose a free-translation Fourier--Mukai model.

S-22 tests the balanced real-multiplication character which passes the earlier numerical secant test. On a genuine real-quadratic eigenspace decomposition, positivity would force an effective surface in its prescribed second-Chern class to have an irrational constant tangent space; its Albanese map rules that out. The character thus has no rank-one torsion-free realization. The degree-one and degree-two contraction ranks are8 and18, and a simple weakly equivariant semiregular lift must have one of two precisely specified finite-group orders. S-23 provides a necessary control: O_A plus O_p is fully semiregular, with endomorphism dimension3 and a nilpotent. It prevents promoting the simple-seed exclusion to all nonsimple objects. S-24 does exclude arbitrary iterated self-extensions of a fixed scalar-endomorphism imaginary-secant factor of Euler characteristic at least2; a heart filtration bounds End by its squared length, contradicting the semiregularity rank equality. Different constituents, different compatible characters, and genuinely new derived constructions remain open.

S-25 removes a further concrete candidate family: an external product containing a simple gluable ample imaginary-secant factor cannot become fully semiregular under any effective finite coherent translation/tensor action, even if that action correlates the factors and even after any derived equivalence. Framing the factor actions at their scalar-endomorphism objects supplies coherence; a derived-fibre cancellation proves that every stabilizing product functor stabilizes each factor without a shift. The nonzero factor kernel therefore remains invariant for the full correlated group. This applies to the actual genus-four secant-square construction. It leaves genuinely nonsplit real-multiplication derived objects and nonsimple constructions as concrete next tests; it asserts no exclusion for all perfect objects.

S-26 reduces the balanced real-multiplication simple-object test to an exact tangent dimension: equivariant Euler characteristic4 requires Ext1 dimension8, while Euler characteristic2 requires dimension9. It also excludes the usual amplitude[0,1] rank-one form with torsion-free H0 and higher cohomology in codimension at least two. S-27 supplies an actual conditional construction template in amplitude[0,2]: a nonzero Ext3(T,A) class between two scalar-endomorphism sheaves with the specified lower cross-Ext vanishing produces a simple gluable cone. Its projective extension parameters inject into Ext1, so in the principal required-character case the extension space has dimension at most9 and full semiregularity is exactly Ext1 dimension8. No pair of sheaves with the required nonsplit real-multiplication character has yet been constructed.

S-28 identifies the two-sheaf cone tangent space by an exact Yoneda compatibility sequence. S-29 computes that sequence for transverse smooth surface sheaves: it gives a unique-extension/evaluation test and proves that acyclic positive twists retain a kernel of dimension at least d-1. These remain valid conditional tools, but the previously proposed principal rank-one balanced character is now excluded arithmetically.

S-30 closes the entire nonequivariant simple gluable rank-one balanced real-multiplication branch, including the non-pure two-sheaf cone attempt. The proof uses the actual integral cohomology lattice: integrality of aC forces the squarefree real-quadratic radicand to divide a squared times the polarization volume. Exterior-square Smith exponents prove this even at two. The required Euler pairing is then too large for the fixed rank-eighteen full semiregularity map. The principal q=1 character is not the character of any rank-one perfect object, with no simplicity assumption. In higher rank the proof gives exact rank/group divisibility and rules out every nonequivariant simple gluable realization with an integral B-normalizer. S-31 independently excludes vector-bundle ranks one, two and three by Newton identities. Fractional determinant normalization and a genuinely new higher-rank or nonsimple construction are therefore necessary in that route.

S-32 strengthens the higher-rank test at two. With maximal local real multiplication and the minimal rational divisor space, an odd polarization volume and a two-adic unit Nq make the second Chern class nonintegral. Every equivariantly simple gluable fully semiregular balanced object in this setting must therefore have even D times the group order. The proof includes the local trace-dual symplectic normal form, and applies to all perfect objects rather than only bundles. Its principal nonequivariant corollary excludes every positive rank when the real quadratic field is ramified at two.

S-33 supplies the odd-prime counterpart, and its proof works at every odd ramified prime where the polarization is locally unimodular and the real multiplication order is maximal. The second-Chern congruences force the integer Euler characteristic to have negative valuation; an independent third-Chern calculation confirms the same obstruction, including at3. Combined with S-32, every prime ramified in the real quadratic field must divide D times the effective group order. Consequently every nonequivariant positive rank is excluded on the principally polarized maximal-order locus with minimal rational divisor space. The remaining hypotheses that can fail are now concrete: local maximal-order action, unramified polarization volume, a determinant lying in the RM divisor plane, or absence of a sufficiently ramified effective group. Nonsimple equivariant objects remain separately outside the simple-lift Euler argument.

T6.22 extends the construction obstruction beyond factorwise pure kernels. On the actual indecomposable principal-shear family, arbitrary integral divisor polynomials on a fixed CM source times the outputs have the same local orbit content, even for source cycles of unbounded degree. It also prevents returning a maximal-order auxiliary factor by a joint integral cohomological correspondence of bounded denominator on any proper block of one, two or three output factors, even when the class on the anchored product is an arbitrary integral Hodge class. The proof uses pure Hodge rows and the uniqueness of the allowed vertex in a conjugate-coordinate sign cube. Genuinely joint all-four correspondences, orbit-mixing maps with specified integral content, and intrinsic Chow divisibility remain unexcluded.

S-34 removes the rank-one equivariant exception entirely. The determinant of a rank-one perfect object carries exactly the same action coherence, so tensoring by its inverse turns the whole translation–tensor action into an ordinary free translation action. Perfect descent produces a rank-one object on the quotient abelian variety with the same normalized real-multiplication character after rational polarization rescaling. S-30 then excludes it. The theorem requires simplicity and gluability only in the equivariant category and imposes no maximal-order or minimal-divisor-space hypothesis. Higher-rank or nonsimple lifts are now necessary for this balanced-character route.

The supplied 032 and Milne consequences remain the background theorems requested by the task: CM Hodge, Tate for abelian varieties over finite fields in the cited formulation, and the Hodge standard conjecture for abelian varieties. The new degree estimates do not enlarge their scope. The correspondences in B6 exist algebraically and define projectors in homological realizations and numerical motives.
