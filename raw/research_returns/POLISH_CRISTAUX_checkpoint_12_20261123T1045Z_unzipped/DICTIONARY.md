## Frobenius-stable cohomology and unit roots

**Algebraic geometry.** Taking the eventual bijective part of Frobenius is exact on the finite modules in question. Frobenius on negative ample ideal cohomology is nilpotent by Serre vanishing, so weak Lefschetz survives on stable parts in every positive degree. Derived crystalline base change and universal coefficients then give the stable torsion exact sequence and a torsion-free unit-root quotient.

**Weights and correspondences.** Katz–Messing identifies the rational crystalline and prime-to-p characteristic polynomials. The number of p-adic unit reciprocal roots therefore measures the rank of the unit-root quotient. This is a p-adic slope statement; a complex absolute-value weight bound alone does not supply it. Ogus's stable/infinitesimal/etale comparison is a published foundation, not a new theorem of this work. The ample-divisor formula is presented as a consequence of those foundations and the cone argument, without a priority claim.

The archive connection is cote 4, §8.5, formula (8.5.1), with the exact sheet and scan-page mapping in the source audit. No additional K-theoretic interpretation is claimed.

## Divided-power operation

For a Z_(p)-algebra A and ideal I, the operation pi is gamma_p. Its two scalar/addition rules imply p!pi(x)=x^p, with no torsion-freeness assumption. If n=sum a_i p^i, gamma_n(x)=c_n product pi^i(x)^{a_i}, with c_n=(p!)^{v_p(n!)}/n! a p-local unit, and c_n modp=product(a_i!)^{-1}. 'Integral' here means p-local when applied to (r^p-r)/p!, not necessarily an ordinary integer.
