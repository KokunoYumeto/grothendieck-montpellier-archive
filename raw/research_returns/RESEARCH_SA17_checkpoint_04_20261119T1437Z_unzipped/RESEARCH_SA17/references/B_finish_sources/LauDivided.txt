1    Introduction
Let p be a prime and let X be an Fp -scheme. We consider the crystalline
Dieudonné functor from p-divisible groups to Dieudonné crystals,
                            DX : BT(X) → D(X),
which is outlined in [Gr1, Gr2] and constructed in [MM, BBM]. The Hodge
filtration of a p-divisible group gives a refinement of DX to a functor
                           DFX : BT(X) → DF(X)
from p-divisible groups to Dieudonné crystals with an admissible filtration
as defined in [Gr2], called filtered Dieudonné crystals in the following.
     The functors DX and DFX can be expected to have good properties
only if X is PD torsion free in the sense that the universal PD envelopes
of affine open subschemes of X are torsion free. We will define a category
of divided Dieudonné crystals which compensates the effect of PD torsion.
Here ‘divided’ refers to the fact that the Frobenius operator of the crystal
is divided by p on the filtration module. It is clear that the image of the
filtration under the Frobenius is divisible by p, but if torsion occurs, the
division is not unique and thus carries additional information.

category of X-schemes defined as the direct image of the crystalline struc-

and by infinite successions of extractions of p-th roots (Definition 7.2). Then
OX cris is a pr-sheaf, which carries an endomorphism σ induced by the Frobe-

nius of X and a PD ideal IX    cris ⊆ O cris with quotient O . The definition of

     The category of divided Dieudonné crystals will be denoted by DD(X).
There is a forgetful functor DD(X) → DF(X) from divided Dieudonné crys-
tals to filtered Dieudonné crystals, which is an equivalence if X is PD torsion
free; see Proposition 8.15.
     The scheme X is called F -finite if the Frobenius morphism ϕ : X → X
is finite, and X will be called F -nilpotent if the kernel of ϕ : OX → OX is
locally a nilpotent ideal. Every locally noetherian Fp -scheme is F -nilpotent.

Theorem 1.3. For every Fp -scheme X there is a functor

                          DDX : BT(X) → DD(X)

from p-divisible groups to divided Dieudonné crystals. The functor DDX is
an equivalence of categories if X is F -finite and F -nilpotent.

   See Proposition 9.1 and Theorem 9.3.
Remark 1.4. The usual description of crystals by modules with a connection
extends to a description of divided Dieudonné crystals by windows with
a connection; see Section 10 for details, in particular Definition 10.1 and
Corollary 10.14.
Remark 1.5. Definition 1.2 can be extended to schemes X where p is locally
nilpotent, and again there is a functor DDX as above. If p ≥ 3 and the
reduction XFp is F -finite and F -nilpotent, then the functor DDX is an
equivalence by an obvious extension of Theorem 1.3.

Outline of the proof
By pr-descent, Lemma 1.1 and Theorem 1.3 are reduced to the case where
X = Spec R is affine and semiperfect in the sense that the Frobenius endo-
morphism of R is surjective. Then OX    cris (X) = A

complete PD thickening of R, and Lemma 1.1 follows from the corresponding

Implications for the classical functors
We assume again that X is an Fp -scheme and refer to the main text for
variants when p is locally nilpotent. By a simple analysis of the forgetful
functor DD(X) → D(X), Theorem 1.3 implies the following.




                                       3
Corollary 1.6. (Corollary 9.5) If X is F -finite and F -nilpotent, then the
functor DX is fully faithful up to isogeny. More precisely, for p-divisible
groups G, H over X the homomorphism

                   Hom(G, H) → Hom(DX (G), DX (H))

is injective with cokernel annihilated by p.

   If X is PD torsion free, the functors DD(X) and DF(X) are inter-
changeable, and Theorem 1.3 can be restated as follows.

Corollary 1.7. (Theorem 7.9) If X is F -finite, F -nilpotent, and PD torsion
free, then the functor DFX is an equivalence.

    An l.c.i. scheme is a locally noetherian scheme whose complete local rings
are complete intersection rings. Every F -finite locally noetherian scheme is
excellent, and every excellent l.c.i. scheme is PD torsion free. Hence the
following result is a particular case of Corollary 1.7.

Corollary 1.8. (Corollary 7.10) If X is an F -finite l.c.i. scheme, then the
functor DFX is an equivalence.

   This applies in particular if X is F -finite and regular, or equivalently if
X is locally noetherian and locally has a finite p-basis.
   More generally, if X locally has a p-basis, then X is PD torsion free,
moreover the forgetful functor DF(X) → D(X) is an equivalence, and hence
the three functors DDX , DFX , and DX are interchangeable. In this case,
the F -finiteness condition of Corollary 1.7 can be omitted as follows.

Theorem 1.9. (Theorem 7.6) If X locally has a p-basis, then the functor
DX is an equivalence.

    This applies for example when X is smooth over a field. The proof of
Theorem 1.9 is again a reduction to the semiperfect case by pr-descent, using
that the relevant semiperfect rings have an explicit description that allows
to apply a variant of the methods of [La5].
Remark 1.10. Corollary 1.7 can be proved directly by pr-descent from the
semiperfect case, without reference to divided Dieudonné crystals. This
route will be followed in the main text in order to reach the results for the
classical functors as directly as possible. This causes a slight duplication of
effort, notably the proofs of Theorems 7.9 and 9.3 are essentially parallel.
Remark 1.11. The intersection of Theorems 1.3 and 1.9 consists of Fp -
schemes which locally admit a finite p-basis. It is not clear if the finiteness
hypotheses in Theorem 1.3 are necessary.

Remark 1.12. The forgetful functor DF(X) → D(X) is fully faithful if X
is an l.c.i. scheme and an equivalence if X locally has a p-basis. Hence the
conclusions of Theorem 1.9 and Corollary 1.8 mean that the functor DX
is fully faithful with essential image DF(X), which confirms [Gr2, p. 108,
Problème 1] in these cases.
Remark 1.13. A weaker version of Corollary 1.8 was announced in [La4],
based on a relative version of the theory of Dieudonné displays of [Zi2, La2].

Previously known properties of the Dieudonné functors
Corollaries 1.6, 1.7, 1.8 and Theorem 1.9 extend known results on the func-
tors DX and DFX , which we try to collect here.

  1. If X is perfect (the case of an empty p-basis), the functor DX and
     its analogue for finite group schemes are an equivalence by a result of
     Gabber, proved in [Be2] for perfect valuation rings; see also [La3].

  2. If X is normal and locally irreducible and locally has a p-basis, then
     DX and its analogue for finite group schemes are fully faithful by [BM].

  3. If X is a formally smooth formal scheme in characteristic p such that
     Xred is of finite type over a field with a finite p-basis, the functor DX is
     an equivalence by [dJ]. This can be deduced from the case of F -finite
     regular schemes, which is part of Theorem 1.9; see Remark 7.8.

  4. If X is of finite type over a field with a finite p-basis, the functor DX
     is fully faithful up to isogeny by [dJ].

  5. If X is a locally noetherian excellent l.c.i. scheme, then DX is fully
     faithful by [dJM]. Since F -finite and noetherian implies excellent, this
     covers the full faithfulness part of Corollary 1.8.

  6. If X is the spectrum of a perfect ring modulo a finite regular sequence,
     the functor DX is fully faithful by [SW]. This is also a consequence of
     Corollary 1.7.

  7. If OK is a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero with
     perfect residue field of characteristic p ≥ 3, Corollary 1.8 for the
     scheme X = Spec OK /p is equivalent to Breuil’s classification of p-
     divisible groups over OK by filtered modules in [Br]. Breuil’s result
     was extended to a relative setting (formally smooth over OK ) in [Ki]
     and to complete regular local rings of higher dimension in [CL]; this
     corresponds to additional cases of Corollaries 1.7 and 1.8.

Relation with recent work
Shortly after this article appeared online, a prismatic Dieudonné theory was
developed in [ALB], whose main result is an equivalence between p-divisible
groups and prismatic Dieudonné crystals over quasi-syntomic (p-complete)
rings. For Fp -algebras, prismatic and crystalline Dieudonné modules coin-
cide, moreover quasi-syntomic Fp -algebras are PD torsion free. So [ALB]
and Theorem 1.3 above extend crystalline Dieudonné theory in different
directions. The results of [ALB] also yield Corollary 1.7 and Theorem 1.9.
    More recently, a classification of p-divisible groups and their truncated
analogues over general p-nilpotent schemes in terms of vector bundles on the
syntomification was proved in [GM], building on the theory of prismatization
of Bhatt–Lurie and Drinfeld.

This article is organized as follows. Sections 2–5 contain generalities on F -
finite schemes, the crystalline Dieudonné functor, schemes with torsion free
PD envelopes, and the notion of frames, including a sheaf version. After the
results of [La5] are extended to more general classes of semiperfect rings in
section 6, Corollary 1.7 and Theorem 1.9 are deduced using the pr-topology
in section 7. The notion of divided Dieudonné crystals is introduced in sec-
tion 8, and Theorem 1.3 is proved in section 9. Section 10 gives an explicit
description of divided Dieudonné crystals in terms of windows over PD en-
velopes with a connection. Finally, the relation between divided Dieudonné
crystals and the display associated to a p-divisible group as in [Zi1, La3] is
briefly discussed in section 11.

2    Finiteness conditions on the Frobenius map
An Fp -algebra R is called F -finite if the Frobenius endomorphism ϕR : R →
R is finite. An F -finite noetherian Fp -algebra is excellent by [Ku2, Th. 2.5].
Following [BM], a p-basis of an Fp -algebra R is a family of elements (xi )i∈I
of R such that the homomorphism

                       R[{Ti }i∈I ]/({Tip − xi }i∈I ) → R                 (2.1)

that extends ϕR by Ti 7→ xi is bijective. For reference we recall:

Lemma 2.1. For a noetherian F -finite Fp -algebra R the following are equiv-
alent:


                                       6
  1. R locally has a finite p-basis,

  2. R is formally smooth over Fp ,

  3. R is formally smooth over Fp with respect to the I-adic topology for
     an ideal I ⊆ Rad(A), where Rad(R) is the Jacobson radical,

  4. R is regular.

Proof. (1)⇒(2) follows from [EGA, 0 IV (21.2.7)] or [dJ, Lemma 1.1.2],
and (2)⇒(3) is clear. (3)⇒(4) follows from [EGA, 0 IV (22.5.8)] or [Ma,
Thm. 28.7] applied to the localizations of A at all maximal ideals. To prove
(4)⇒(1) one can assume that R is local. Let a1 , . . . , ar ∈ R map to a p-basis
of the residue field R/mR and let ar+1 , . . . , an be a minimal set of genera-
tors of the maximal ideal mR . Since the completion R̂ is isomorphic to a
power series ring, a1 , . . . , an form a p-basis of R̂, and thus a p-basis of R by
faithfully flat descent; note that R̂ ⊗R,ϕ R ∼   = R̂ via ϕ ⊗ 1.

Remark 2.2. The basic rings in [dJ, 1.3.1] are noetherian Fp -algebras A
which are I-adically complete and formally smooth for the I-adic topology
such that A/I is of finite type over a field k with a finite p-basis. Such rings
are F -finite and thus satisfy the equivalent conditions of Lemma 2.1.
Remark 2.3. The implication (1)⇒(2) of Lemma 2.1 generalises as follows:
For any Fp -algebra R with a p-basis (xi )i∈I , the homomorphism Fp → R
satisfies the lifting criterion of formal smoothness with respect to thickenings
of Fp -algebras π : B → B/J with ϕB (J) = 0. This follows from the proof
of [EGA, 0 IV (21.2.7)]. Explicitly, let ϕ̄ : B/J → B be the homomorphism
induced by ϕB . If a homomorphism g : R → B/J is given and if bi ∈ B
is an inverse image of g(xi ), the homomorphism R[(Ti )i ]/((Tip − xi )i ) → B
defined by ϕ̄ ◦ g on R and by Ti 7→ xi defines a lift g̃ : R → B of g via (2.1).
Remark 2.4. In addition to the equivalences of Lemma 2.1, a noetherian
Fp -algebra R is regular iff ϕ : R → R is flat by [Ku1, Thm. 2.1].

Definition 2.5. An Fp -algebra R will be called F -nilpotent if the kernel of
the Frobenius endomorphism ϕR : R → R is a nilpotent ideal. An Fp -scheme
X will be called F -nilpotent if for every affine open subscheme U = Spec R
of X the ring R is F -nilpotent.

Remark 2.6. If the kernel of ϕR is finitely generated, then R is F -nilpotent,
in particular every noetherian Fp -algebra is F -nilpotent.

3     Dieudonné crystals
In this section we recall Dieudonné crystals, admissible filtrations, and the
crystalline Dieudonné functor. As a base we take the PD scheme (Σ, pOΣ , γ)
with Σ = Spec Zp where γ are the canonical divided powers. Sometimes we
will also consider Σn = Spec Z/pn , viewed as a PD subscheme of Σ.
    Let X be a scheme on which p is locally nilpotent and X0 = X ×Spec Fp .
Let CRIS(X/Σ) be the big fppf crystalline site as in [BBM]. Its objects
are PD thickenings (U, T, δ) compatible with Σ where U is an X-scheme
and p is locally nilpotent on T , and coverings of (U, T, δ) correspond to
fppf coverings of T . The crystalline structure sheaf OX/Σ on CRIS(X/Σ)
is defined by OX/Σ (U, T, δ) = OT (T ). An OX/Σ -module M gives an OT -
module M(U,T,δ) , in particular an OU -module MU = M(U,U,0) , and the
restriction of M to CRIS(X0 /Σ) will be denoted by M0 .
    A Dieudonné crystal over X is a triple (M, F, V ) where M is a finite
locally free OX/Σ -module and F : ϕ∗ (M0 ) → M0 and V : M0 → ϕ∗ (M0 )
are linear maps with F V = p and V F = p; here ϕ is the Frobenius map of
X0 . Let D(X) denote the category of Dieudonné crystals over X.


                                                8
    For (U, T, δ) ∈ CRIS(X0 / Spec Fp ), which implies that T is an Fp -scheme,
the Frobenius morphism ϕT : T → T has image in U and thus induces a
morphism ϕU/T : T → U ; this holds since a PD ideal in characteristic p
is annihilated by the Frobenius. An admissible filtration for a Dieudonné
crystal (M, F, V ) over X is a locally direct summand Fil1 MX ⊆ MX such
that for every (U, T, δ) ∈ CRIS(X0 /Fp ) we have

          ϕ∗U/T ((Fil1 MX )U ) = Ker(F : ϕ∗ (M)(U,T,δ) → M(U,T,δ) )        (3.1)

inside ϕ∗U/T (MU ) = ϕ∗ (M)(U,T,δ) . A variant of this definition appears in
[Gr2, Chap. V]. The category of Dieudonné crystals over X with an admis-
sible filtration, also called filtered Dieudonné crystals in the following, will
be denoted by
                                      DF(X).
The categories D(X) and DF(X) do not change if the fppf topology on
CRIS(X/Σ) is replaced by any topology finer than Zariski and coarser than
fpqc, and one can also replace CRIS(X/Σ) by the corresponding small site.

The crystalline Dieudonné functor
Let
                            DX : BT(X) → D(X)                              (3.2)
be the contravariant crystalline Dieudonné functor from p-divisible groups
to Dieudonné crystals as defined in [BBM] and [MM]. For G ∈ BT(X) there
is a natural exact sequence of locally free OX -modules

                 0 → Lie(G)∨ → DX (G)X → Lie(G∨ ) → 0,

called the Hodge filtration of G, which gives an extension of DX to a functor

                           DFX : BT(X) → DF(X)                             (3.3)

from p-divisible groups to filtered Dieudonné crystals defined by Fil1 DX (G)X =
Lie(G)∨ . The Hodge filtration is admissible by [BBM, Prop. 4.3.10].
Remark 3.1 (Reduction modulo p). The restriction functor of Dieuonné crys-
tals D(X) → D(X0 ) is an equivalence, and lifts of a filtered Dieudonné crys-
tal M ∈ DF(X0 ) to DF(X) correspond to lifts of Fil1 MX0 ⊆ MX0 to a
locally direct summand of M(X0 ,X,γ) . If p ≥ 3, then lifts along the func-
tor BT(X) → BT(X0 ) correspond to lifts of the Hodge filtration by the
Grothendieck-Messing Theorem [Me], and thus the diagram

is 2-cartesian. In particular, if p ≥ 3 and the functor DFX0 is an equivalence,

Forgetting the filtration
Lemma 3.2. If X is an Fp -scheme which is reduced or a locally noetherian
l.c.i. scheme, the forgetful functor DF(X) → D(X) is fully faithful.

Proof. We have to show that a Dieudonné crystal (M, F, V ) carries at most
one admissible filtration.1 If X is reduced this holds since the Frobenius en-
domorphism ϕR of a reduced Fp -algebra R is injective, so a direct summand
of a projective R-module is determined by its scalar extension along ϕR . In
the l.c.i. case we can assume that X = Spec R where R is a complete local
ring. Then R = A/J where A is a power series ring in finitely many variables
over a field k and the ideal J is generated by a regular sequence t1 , . . . , tr .
Let J ′ = (tp1 , . . . , tpr ) and R′ = A/J ′ . There are divided powers δ on the ideal
J/J ′ with δp (ti ) = 0. Indeed, this is clear when A equals Λ = Fp [[T1 , . . . , Tr ]]
with ti = Ti , and the general case follows since the homomorphism Λ → A
defined by Ti 7→ ti is flat. The homomorphism ϕR/R′ : R → R′ induced by
ϕR′ is injective because it is a base change of ϕA : A → A, which is faithfully
flat. Hence a direct summand of a projective R-module is determined by its
scalar extension along ϕR/R′ , and the lemma follows.

Lemma 3.3. If X is an Fp -scheme which locally has a p-basis, the forgetful
functor DF(X) → D(X) is an equivalence.

Proof. See [dJ, Prop. 2.5.2]. We may assume that X = Spec R where R
has a p-basis (xi )i∈I , and we have to show that for a Dieudonné crystal
(M, F, V ) over Spec R an admissible filtration Q ⊆ MR exists, which is
then unique since X is reduced. Let

                          Q′ = Ker(FR : ϕ∗R (MR ) → MR ).

Since R has a p-basis, every affine (Spec A, Spec B, δ) in CRIS(X/ Spec Fp )
maps non-uniquely to the trivial PD thickening (X, X, 0), using Remark 2.3.
Hence a direct summand Q ⊆ MR is admissible iff ϕ∗R (Q) = Q′ . Let R′ = R
as an R-algebra via ϕR , let R′′ = R′ ⊗R R′ , and let M′ and M′′ denote
the base change of M to Dieudonné crystals over Spec R′ and Spec R′′ .
Then Q′ is an admissible filtration for M′ , and we have to show that Q′
descends to R, or equivalently that the scalar extensions of Q′ along the
two homomorphisms R′ ⇒ R′′ are equal. This holds because a Dieudonné
crystal over Spec R′′ carries at most one admissible filtration, i.e. the functor
DF(Spec R′′ ) → D(Spec R′′ ) is fully faithful, which is a variant of Lemma 3.2.

4    Torsion in PD envelopes
Let R be a ring in which p is nilpotent. In this section we collect some
permanence properties of torsion in PD envelopes.
   In the following, a presentation of R will be a surjective ring homomor-
phism A → R where A is p-complete and Zp -flat and A0 = A/p has a p-basis.
For a presentation A → R = A/I we consider the p-completion of the PD
envelope relative to the PD ring (Zp , pZp , γ)

                       D = Dγ (A → R)∧ = DA,γ (I)∧ .                      (4.1)

One particular choice is A = Zp [R]∧ , the p-complete polynomial ring with
set of variables R. In that case we write D = D(R) as in [dJM] and call
D(R) the universal p-complete PD envelope of R.
    It will be sufficient to consider the case where R is an Fp -algebra because
in general, with R0 = R/p we have Dγ (A → R) = Dγ (A → R0 ), and every
presentation A → R0 can be lifted to a presentation A → R.

Lemma 4.1. If R is a semiperfect Fp -algebra, i.e. the Frobenius homomor-
phism ϕR : R → R is surjective, and if A0 is perfect, then D coincides with
Acris (R), the universal p-complete PD thickening of R.

Proof. One verifies that D has the universal property of Acris (R).

5    Frames and windows
We use the terminology of frames and windows as in [Zi3] and [La1] with
some modifications adapted to the present situation.
Definition 5.1. A frame A = (A, I, R, σ, σ1 ) consists of p-complete rings A
and R = A/I for an ideal I ⊆ A, a ring homomorphism σ : A → A which
induces the Frobenius on A/p, and a σ-linear map σ1 : I → A with pσ1 = σ.
    An A-window M = (M, M1 , Φ, Φ1 ) consists of a finite projective A-
module M , a submodule M1 ⊆ M with IM ⊆ M1 such that M/M1 is
projective over R, and σ-linear maps Φ : M → M and Φ1 : M1 → M with
pΦ1 = Φ and Φ1 (ax) = σ1 (a)Φ(x) for a ∈ A and x ∈ M , such that M is
generated by Φ(M ) + Φ1 (M1 ).
    Homomorphisms of A-windows are module homomorphisms that pre-
serve all data. The category of A-windows is denoted by Win(A).
Remark 5.2. For an A-window M as in Definition 5.1 there is a decomposi-
tion M = L ⊕ T with M1 = L ⊕ IT , called a normal decomposition, and the
pair (Φ, Φ1 ) corresponds to a σ-linear isomorphism Ψ : L ⊕ T → M defined
by Ψ = Φ1 on L and Ψ = Φ on T . Proof: The existence of σ1 implies that
xp = 0 for all x ∈ Ker(A/p → R/p), hence finite projective R-modules can
be lifted to A, and the decomposition of M follows. See [La1, Lemma 2.6]
for more details. It also follows that I + pA ⊆ Rad(A), which means that A
is a frame in the sense of [La1, Def. 2.1].
Remark 5.3 (Functoriality). Let A′ = (A′ , I ′ , R′ , σ, σ1 ) be another frame. A
frame homomorphism α : A → A′ is a ring homomorphism A → A′ that
induces a homomorphism R → R′ and commutes with σ and σ1 . It induces
a base change functor α∗ : Win(A) → Win(A′ ); see [La1, Lemma 2.10]. Uni-
versal property: If windows M over A and M ′ over A′ are given, define an
α-homomorphism M → M ′ to be a homomorphism of A-modules that pre-
serves the filtration and commutes with Φ and Φ1 . Then α-homomorphisms
M → M ′ correspond to homomorphisms of A′ -windows α∗ M → M ′ .
Example 5.4. For a p-complete ring R the ring of p-typical Witt vectors
W (R) carries a frame structure W (R) = (W (R), I(R), R, σ, σ1 ) where σ is
the Witt vector Frobenius and σ1 is the inverse of the bijective Verschiebung
homomorphism v : W (R) → I(R). A window over W (R) is a 3n-display
over R in the sense of [Zi1], commonly called display in later literature.
Example 5.5. Let R be a p-complete ring. A frame for R in the sense of [Zi3]
is a p-complete PD thickening A → R = A/I where A is torsion free, with a
