### C4(i), noncommutative bounded unipotent formal kernel — still being attacked

The old commutative bounded-kernel argument uses Ext¹ additivity and cannot be used for a noncommutative kernel. The old perfect-field Frobenius counterexample has multiplicative kernel and does not settle the bounded unipotent alternative. Ordinary semidirect products only refute a direct-product conclusion; they do not refute the splitting asserted in (I).

Approach1: filter the kernel centrally. A central bounded-exponent kernel over a divisible abelian quotient forces the total group commutative, because its commutator is biadditive into the bounded kernel. This kills that extension after a quotient p-isogeny. To use induction, one must control the quotient action on successive central factors; a formal multiplicative quotient can act nontrivially, so triviality cannot be assumed.

Approach2: finite Frobenius kernels of a formal extension with multiplicative quotient. SGA3 XVII5.1.1 gives algebraic finite-level splittings over a perfect field when the quotient is connected. It does not prove compatible splittings. Its conjugacy assertion has extra smoothness hypotheses absent for both finite infinitesimal groups. Example α_p⋊μ_p admits many complements although α_p(k)=0, so uniqueness via k-point conjugacy is false. Need a formal Levi theorem, a verified Mittag–Leffler argument, or a direct construction.

Approach3: for an a-toric quotient use conjugation on finite coordinate jets of U. If each affine algebraic image of G is unipotent modulo the image of U, bounded exponent and divisibility force the images equal. A finite noetherian equation argument may then imply C_G(U)→P is surjective, reducing to the central case. Formal-image, finite-detection, and quotient representability details still require proof. No theorem is asserted from this route yet.

### 13:50 UTC: compatible finite-level route and primary structural source

The multiplicative/vector case now has a direct compatibility construction. After equivariant additive-coordinate linearization, restrict the normalized formal factor set f to T[p^n]. The normalized invariant integral on the finite multiplicative group gives H^1=H^2=0 for its algebraic vector representation. Given b_n with delta b_n=f_n, choose any b_(n+1), express its restriction minus b_n as delta a_n, and subtract delta a_n. These algebraic zero-cochains a_n need not be formal-group k-points; their coboundaries vanish at identity and are valid formal cochains. Hence the adjusted b_n are compatible. This removes the earlier finite-level compatibility gate.

A finite-dimensional representation of a multiplicative formal group is semisimple: over an algebraic closure its restrictions to T[p^n] give refining weight decompositions which stabilize in finite dimension; the compatible weights then lie in Z_p^r. Splittings descend because equivariance is a linear system over the original field. The finite quotients of primitive additive power series are such representations, so compatible equivariant lifts of the cotangent basis give additive coordinates linearizing the action.

An atoric connected p-divisible formal group has no nontrivial finite-dimensional linear representations: the commutative affine algebraic closure of the image has no characters, hence is unipotent, hence has bounded p-exponent, whereas the source is p-divisible. Thus its action on all finite coordinate jets of a vector kernel is trivial. The resulting central extension is commutative by the bilinear commutator/divisibility argument and has a unique lift of [p], central even in the full extension. Over a perfect field the multiplicative/bi-infinitesimal decomposition follows by dualizing the split connected-etale decomposition; combining the two lifts gives a lift of [p] on the full quotient.

Induct along a characteristic finite vector-factor filtration of a smooth solvable bounded-exponent kernel. The closed derived subgroups are reduced (closed subgroup generated by images of smooth products), hence smooth over a perfect field; refine their commutative factors by images of p-powers. A smooth commutative group killed by p is a formal vector group, by the V-reduced Cartier equivalence. For nonsmooth kernels use a sufficiently large Frobenius image, whose kernel is finite and characteristic. A finite-kernel extension of a p-divisible formal group splits up to isogeny by its reduced subgroup: this subgroup is smooth, commutative and finite dominant over the quotient.
