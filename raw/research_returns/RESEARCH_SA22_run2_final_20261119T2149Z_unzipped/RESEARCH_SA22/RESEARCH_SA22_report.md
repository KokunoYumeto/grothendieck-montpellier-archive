### Scope of the functor criterion

The category consists of functors from commutative K-algebras to modules, with R-module values and semilinear transition maps, as the diagram on scan043 explicitly requires. Actions are natural and R-linear in the module variable. Formal exponential fibers are additive cosets of the kernel of the explicit map End(M)→Hom(Nil⊗M,M). Surjectivity has three exact tests: pointwise unipotence at nilpotent parameters, scalar linearity of the logarithm, and vanishing of its class in the cokernel of that map. Ordinary exponential is injective; its image is the uniformly unipotent actions with scalar-linear logarithm. This is a necessary-and-sufficient image/fiber/bijectivity answer. It does not classify arbitrary functors up to isomorphism.

The strengthened theorem gives directly checkable sufficient functor properties: commuting with all finite free truncated-polynomial extensions makes formal exponential bijective, including the internal Hom functors in scan043 for arbitrary source/target modules, and infinite product functors. Commuting with ordinary polynomial extension classifies ordinary actions by natural pointwise locally nilpotent endomorphisms. These sufficient properties are not claimed necessary. Separate examples now witness the kernel obstruction (R_red), nonunipotence (free module on Nil(R), with translation), scalar-linearity failure (R⊕Ω_R/Q), and a nonzero cokernel even after both prior tests pass (R⊕Nil(R), with the nilpotent-parameter shear). Thus the criterion is not merely a false universal assertion replaced by unexplained hypotheses.
