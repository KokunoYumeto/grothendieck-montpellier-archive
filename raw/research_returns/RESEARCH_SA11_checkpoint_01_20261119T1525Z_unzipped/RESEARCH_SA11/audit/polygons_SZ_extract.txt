   Abstract. We study the space of all extensions of a real hyperplane arrangement by
   a new pseudohyperplane, and, more generally, of an oriented matroid by a new
   element. The question whether this space has the homotopy type of a sphere is a
   special case of the "Generalized Baues Problem" of Billera, Kapranov, and Sturmfels,
   via the Bohne-Dress theorem on zonotopal tilings.
      We prove that the extension space is spherical for the class of strongly euclidean
   oriented matroids. This class includes the alternating matroids and all oriented
   matroids of rank at most 3 or of corank at most 2. In general it is not known whether
   the extension space is connected for all realizable oriented matroids (hyperplane
   arrangements). We show that the subspace of realizable extensions is always con-
   nected but not necessarily spherical. Nonrealizable oriented matroids of rank 4 with
   disconnected extension spaces were recently constructed by MnEv and Richter-
   Gebert.

5.    Remarks

A potential application of extension spaces lies in the interplay of combinatorics
and (differential) topology. This interplay is highlighted by the recent cominatorial
construction of Pontrjagin classes due to Gelfand and MacPherson [13]. Their
work suggests in particular that the MacPhersonian Mc(n, r) of all oriented
matroids of rank r on I-n], ordered by weak maps, could serve as a discrete model
for the real Grassmannian G(n, r). See also Mn~v and Ziegler [20] for a more
detailed discussion of such models.
   The crucial question about MacPhersonians is whether Mc(n, r) and G(n, r)
have isomorphic cohomology. A possible inductive approach is to consider the
projection G(n, r)\G(n - 1, r -- 1) ~ G(n - 1, r) whose fibers are (r - 1)-spheres.
The combinatorial analogue is the deletion map

                       Mc(n, r)\Mc(n - 1, r - 1) --* Mc(n - 1, r)

on the poset of rank r oriented matroids on [n] for which n is not a coloop. The
fibers of this map are the extension spaces 8(~'). A different approach to the
MacPhersonian problem is taken in Babson's work on the homotopy type and
the cohomology of the MacPhersonian [1]. In this context our extension spaces
8(d/) appear as flag spaces Mc(Jr r -- 1).
    In spite of the results in this article, it remains an open problem whether the
extension space r        is connected for every realizable oriented matroid Jr'. We
only know that 8(~r always contains a large connected subspace r                   by
Theorem 1.1, and that it is spherical (and hence connected) for r < 3 by Corollary
4.5. It would be worthwhile to investigate the scope of the methods developed in
this paper. For instance, a challenging problem is to construct an explicit realizable
oriented matroid which is not strongly euclidean.
