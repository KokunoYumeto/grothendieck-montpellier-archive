## Picard example

Read Raynaud's *Spécialisation du foncteur de Picard*, Publ. Math. IHES 38 (1970), 27–76, Definition 6.1.4 p.48 and Theorem 8.2.1 pp.66–67. The theorem's (N)* concerns absence of embedded special-fibre components, normality of the **total space** at generic special-fibre points, and f_*O=O. It does not require the double special fibre to be normal. For a locally factorial total space and perfect residue field, δ=d′ and all eight conditions in 8.2.1 are equivalent. Thus δ=2 rules out both scheme representability and separation of Pic0.

The quotient construction was checked against Stacks Lemma 66.14.2 in Section 66.14 (tag 07S5), and algebraic-space representability against Proposition 99.11.8 (0D2C). The action is free because translation by the nonzero two-torsion point has no fixed point. The quotient map is finite étale. Regularity and proper flat finite presentation descend. The generic curve becomes the elliptic curve after C((s))/C((t)). The divisor equality is div(t)=2C.

Universal H0 was proved directly on every R-algebra A, including nonreduced A: H0(Y_A)=A⊕As, the involution is +1 on A and −1 on As, and 2 is a unit. Torsor descent gives invariants A. This verifies the actual universal hypothesis of the Picard representability theorem.

## Nori example

## E6: selected ordinary squares and exceptional bands

I read the complete original E6 proof after the frozen attempt. In each ordinary cube, the coordinate-midplane subdivision has face centers as vertices. A small square is a two-coordinate interval of the face order; it is the union of the two order-complex triangles in its incidence diamond. The ordinary four-corner theorem identifies the selected squares exactly by adjacency of the two specified resolution ports in the complete boundary cycle. Distinct such diamonds are distinct small cells and meet along common faces. This is a literal cell inclusion, not an argument from Euler characteristic.

Replacing a whole parallel polygon by a Möbius band gives the same space as removing a small central disk and inserting a band: the remaining polygonal annulus is a collar. If C_i is the ordinary boundary cycle of that polygon, the quotient `(C_i x [-1,1])/((z,t)~(a_i z,-t))` is the band, with boundary parametrized by z at t=1. Its fiber edges pair opposite vertices, and its rectangles pair opposite edges. There are 2 n_i fiber edges and 2 n_i rectangles, with four distinct vertices in each rectangle because n_i >= 2. It includes the complete one-skeleton. Each omitted two-cell adds its attaching relation to the fundamental group; all higher cells leave it unchanged.
