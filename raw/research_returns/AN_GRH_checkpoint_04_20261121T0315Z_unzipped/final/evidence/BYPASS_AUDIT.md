# Additional-cover routes and the exact remaining incidence gate

## New result: A15

`A15_COMPOSITE_BRIDGE.tex` gives a full proof that, on a genus-two characteristic-two curve of 2-rank one, a cyclic odd-degree cover with zero 2-rank in its primitive factor suffices for separation. The recovered source criterion is **SUCCESSOR final A.14 = working AC.11**. Its extra exclusion of a separable double cover of a genus-one curve is automatic here: Riemann–Hurwitz forces exactly one ramification point, and Deuring–Shafarevich then forces the 2-rank to be even. Final A.35 is a distinct finite Fourier counting criterion and is not this source.

The endgame uses the integral product A=product over ell|n of (1−sigma^(n/ell)). Its coefficient norm is d=2^omega(n). Applied to the difference of two points in disjoint free deck orbits, it gives a degree-d function with reduced zero and pole divisors. The degree condition n>(d−1)² holds for **every odd n>1**, and gcd(n,d)=1 is automatic. The field generated by all its deck translates has index e|d. The genus bound, faithful free deck action on this field, and a reduced fibre force e=2 and a genus-one quotient, giving the contradiction. No whole-new-factor vanishing is needed.

The one-point theta criterion <2,−1>=U_n forces omega(n)<=2, but the projector criterion itself does not. The new same-cyclic-subgroup formulation is exact: for a fixed odd-order torsion class M, primitive rank zero is equivalent to the set of indices i for which (M^(1))^i lies on theta meeting every doubling orbit in U_n. Several appropriately placed points in this **same** cyclic subgroup can therefore work even if omega(n)>2.

Root's A16 supplies an actual degree-fifteen example in which this new distinction matters for the cover: primitive rank zero, proper conductor-three and conductor-five factors ordinary, total rank seven. I whole-checked its proof and reran a copied symbolic certificate successfully; see `A16_WHOLE_PROOF_REVIEW.md`.

## Disconnected and abelian combinations

A pointed disjoint union of connected covers supplies no additional connected geometric test: the chosen lift selects a component, and the section action relevant to that lift stays in that component. After a finite constant extension the same observation applies to geometric components. This is not a claim that arbitrary nonabelian iterated covers add no information.

For several connected cyclic covers of pairwise coprime odd degrees n_i, their connected fibre product has cyclic group of order N=product(n_i). The original theta incidences control the characters pulled back from each individual quotient. Those have proper conductor dividing n_i. Primitive conductor-N characters are mixed tensor products of their eigensheaves; theta incidence for each original eigensheaf does not establish theta incidence for these products. The exact orbit-coverage criterion above pinpoints what additional information is required.

An existing stronger calculation already handles finite abelian **norm-freeness tests**: recovered ANABELIAN_A1 final B.13 = working B.AB1, in `sections/B_abelian_norm.tex`, proves that the rational torsion set satisfying all quotient norm-freeness conditions is nonempty exactly when every cyclic prime-power quotient has the prescribed nonzero rational primitive packet at its first pointed field of definition. I read its entire proof. It uses integral Z_2[A] character-kernel packets and Frobenius orbit sums, without dividing by an orbit length. Consequently an empty set at this level already occurs in a cyclic prime-power subquotient. This does not identify arbitrary torsion vectors with actual point differences. It does not invalidate A15, whose extra multi-term divisor and gonality argument exploits the geometry of a point difference.

## Iterated covers over a higher-genus base

Passing from degree ell*m over C to a degree-ell cover over the intermediate degree-m curve does not turn primitive ell*m information into vanishing of the entire relative block. That relative factor contains conductors ell*d for all d|m. Its character spaces over the new base have dimension m(g_C−1)=m, whereas the original theta condition concerns only one constituent. A singular map of an m-dimensional space need not have zero stable part: over F2, diag(0,1,...,1) is an explicit counterexample to that linear-algebra inference. A successful nonabelian or iterated-cover approach must control the full relevant orbit-product matrices, or supply another geometric obstruction. No such conclusion follows merely from the original line-map vanishing.

## Why choosing the primary set does not yet solve the new criterion

Poonen's primary paper `Multiples of subvarieties in algebraic groups over finite fields`, Theorem1.8 and full Lemmas6.1–6.6, was inspected. In Lemma6.4, one first finds a point h in a bounded-index subgroup supported on a sparse set of prime divisors of Jacobian orders along a constant-field tower. One then defines U to be the finite set of **all** primes dividing ord(h). The estimate is eta(U)<epsilon, not a bound on |U|. For any prescribed cardinality R, choosing R primes all above a cutoff with pi(cutoff)>R/epsilon yields a finite U with eta(U)<epsilon. Thus the density estimate alone cannot bound omega(ord(h)).

Taking a selected ell^a component and including 2,ell in S0 gives theta order ell^a*m with the primes of m outside S. Enlarging finite S0 excludes finitely many unwanted primes but does not force m to have one prime divisor, does not force the local primitive-root conditions at its factors, and does not force the compatibility of the two local orders. For ell=3 and rank one, A10 proves m>1. A15 now permits certain such composites; it does not show that Poonen produces one of them. This is a weaker and more precise open incidence gate than pure prime-power order, with an explicit successful instance in A16.

## The arithmetic candidate family n=3q

If q is prime, q=5 mod8, and 2 is primitive modulo q, then ord_(3q)(2)=q−1=phi(3q)/2. A power of 2 equal to −1 modulo q has exponent (q−1)/2 modulo q−1, which is even; a power equal to −1 modulo3 has odd exponent. Thus −1 does not belong to <2> modulo3q, and <2,−1>=U_(3q).

Under the paper's Dedekind-GRH premise M, these q have natural density A/2, where A is Artin's constant. Here is the finite-field-extension calculation, separated from any theta assertion. For odd squarefree m, let K_m=Q(zeta_m,2^(1/m)) and F=Q(zeta_8). Since 2 is unramified in Q(zeta_m), x^m−2 is Eisenstein at every prime above2; hence [K_m:Q]=m*phi(m). The group is C_m semidirect U_m. The element 2 in U_m has commutator with a translation generator equal to that generator, so its commutator subgroup is C_m. Every abelian subfield of K_m lies in Q(zeta_m), and K_m intersect F=Q. For q=5 mod8 the quadratic obstruction to primitivity is absent. Inclusion-exclusion over any finite set of odd prime obstructions therefore gives density sum mu(m)/(4*m*phi(m)), with m over divisors of their product. Lenstra1977 Theorem3.1 identifies the limiting Chebotarev density with the natural density under the stated Kummer-field GRHs. It is (1/4) product over odd ell of (1−1/(ell(ell−1)))=A/2>0.

This proof is an arithmetic candidate supply only. Neither it nor Poonen's theorem proves theta incidence of exact order 3q for one of these primes on every rank-one base.
