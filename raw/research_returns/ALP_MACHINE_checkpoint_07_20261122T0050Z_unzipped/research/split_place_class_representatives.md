# AM-R-007: unitary lattice classes have representatives supported at relative split places

Author: `/root`, GPT-6 Astra Pro; effort not externally exposed;
ALP_MACHINE, 21 November 2026. This is a concrete application of
strong approximation to the moved-lattice comparison. It is a
statement about double-coset representatives, not an assertion that
the two-average theta family already has the required rational
equivariance.

## Statement

Let \(E/F\) be a quadratic extension of number fields, with its
nontrivial involution denoted by a bar. Let \(V\) be a nondegenerate
Hermitian space of dimension \(n\geq2\), let
\(G=U(V)\), \(G^{\rm der}=SU(V)\), and
\(T=\ker(N_{E/F}:\operatorname{Res}_{E/F}\mathbf G_m\to\mathbf G_m)\).
Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of finite places of \(F\) that split
in \(E\), and choose one \(v_0\in\mathcal S\). For every compact
open subgroup \(K\subset G(\mathbb A_{F,f})\),
\[
 G(\mathbb A_{F,f})=G(F)\,G_{\mathcal S}^{\rm fin}\,K,
 \tag{R7.1}
\]
where \(G_{\mathcal S}^{\rm fin}\) denotes adeles equal to the
identity outside a finite subset of \(\mathcal S\). The finite
subset is allowed to depend on the represented class.

For the actual field \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)\),
\(F=E^+\), standard form \(V=E^n\) with Gram matrix \(I_n\),
and its full standard lattice stabilizer
\(K_0=\prod_vU(I_n,\mathcal O_{E,v})\), the stronger statement is
\[
 G(\mathbb A_{F,f})=G(F)\,G(F_{v_{11}})\,K_0,
 \tag{R7.2}
\]
where \(v_{11}\) is the unique place of \(F\) above 11. It splits
in \(E/F\), since the subgroup generated by \(11\bmod7=4\)
has order three and does not contain complex conjugation.

Thus every standard-genus Hermitian lattice class needed at maximal
level has a representative obtained by moving only at 11. At a
smaller level, (R7.1) supplies representatives using finitely many
relative split places. Neither assertion bounds the denominators of
the representative, the index of an intersection lattice, its
stabilizer order, or a terminal mass.

## Proof of the split-place reduction for arbitrary compact level

Choose a diagonal Hermitian basis over \(E\); this is possible by
Hermitian Gram--Schmidt in characteristic zero. It gives an explicit
section of determinant,
\[
 s:T\longrightarrow G,\qquad t\longmapsto
             \operatorname{diag}(t,1,\ldots,1).
 \tag{R7.3}
\]
Indeed multiplying the first diagonal coordinate by a norm-one
scalar preserves the diagonal form. This also proves surjectivity
of determinant on all local and global point groups.

Replace \(K\), if necessary, by a product compact open subgroup
\(K'=\prod_vK'_v\subset K\), standard at almost every place.
Proving (R7.1) for \(K'\) proves it for \(K\), so write this
product subgroup as \(K\) from now on. Put
\(K_{T,v}=\det K_v\). This is an open subgroup of \(T(F_v)\):
the local analytic section (R7.3), near the identity, shows that
the image of an open identity neighborhood is open. At almost all
places it contains all integral points of \(T\), and in the
standard unramified model it equals them.

At a nonsplit finite place every norm-one element of \(E_v^\times\)
is a unit: its valuation multiplied by the residue degree is the
valuation of its norm, which is zero. Thus \(T(F_v)\) is compact,
and \(K_{T,v}=T(F_v)\) at all but finitely many nonsplit places.

Given \(g\in G(\mathbb A_{F,f})\), set \(t=\det g\).
There exists \(t_0\in T(F)\) such that
\[
 t_0^{-1}t_v\in K_{T,v}
       \quad\text{at every nonsplit finite place }v.
 \tag{R7.4}
\]
Only finitely many conditions need attention. For completeness the
required weak approximation on this particular torus has a direct
proof. Local Hilbert 90 writes each prescribed norm-one local
element as \(a_v/\bar a_v\). Weak approximation in the number
field \(E\) gives a nonzero \(a\in E\) sufficiently close to
these finitely many \(a_v\). The continuous map
\(a\mapsto a/\bar a\) then puts \(t_0=a/\bar a\) in the
required open cosets. This uses only finite local approximation,
not strong approximation for a torus with a finite omitted set.

At the split places, \(t_0^{-1}t_v\) belongs to \(K_{T,v}\)
for all but finitely many \(v\), since it is an adele and \(K\)
is standard almost everywhere. We may therefore write
\[
 t=t_0\,t_{\mathcal S}\,k_T,
 \tag{R7.5}
\]
where \(t_{\mathcal S}\) is supported at finitely many split
places and \(k_T\in\prod_vK_{T,v}\). Choose \(k\in K\)
with \(\det k=k_T\), choosing a lift at each place, and let
\(a=s(t_{\mathcal S})\), a finite-support split-place adele.
Then
\[
 g_2=s(t_0)^{-1}gk^{-1},\qquad h=g_2a^{-1}
                     \in SU(V)(\mathbb A_{F,f}).
 \tag{R7.6}
\]

The group \(SU(V)\) is connected, simply connected, and
absolutely almost simple for \(n\geq2\); over an algebraic
closure it is \(SL_n\). At the chosen split place \(v_0\),
it is \(SL_n(F_{v_0})\), hence noncompact. The strong
approximation theorem for simply connected absolutely almost simple
groups therefore applies with the omitted set consisting of all
archimedean places together with \(v_0\). The exact input is
Rapinchuk (2013), Theorem 2.3, printed p. 281, recording the
Kneser--Platonov theorem in characteristic zero.

Apply it to the compact open subgroup
\(K''=(aKa^{-1})\cap SU(V)(\mathbb A_{F,f})\). It gives
\[
 h=\gamma u_0k'',\qquad
 \gamma\in SU(V)(F),\quad u_0\in SU(V)(F_{v_0}),
 \quad k''\in K''.
 \tag{R7.7}
\]
To see the factorization directly from density, approximate \(h\)
outside \(v_0\) by \(\gamma\) so that
\(\gamma^{-1}h\in K''_v\) at each other finite place; put
these components into \(k''\) and put its \(v_0\)-component
equal to one. The remaining component is \(u_0\).

Since \(k''a=a(a^{-1}k''a)\), substitution in (R7.6) yields
\[
 g=s(t_0)\gamma\,(u_0a)\,(a^{-1}k''a)k.
 \tag{R7.8}
\]
The first factor is rational, the middle factor is supported at
finitely many relative split places, and the last two factors lie
in \(K\). This proves (R7.1). The order of factors in this
argument matters: no nonrational adele was commuted past a rational
matrix without conjugating the compact subgroup.

## Proof of the maximal-level statement for the cyclotomic field

The torus calculation in ALP-LIFT-09, equations (V2)–(V6),
proves
\[
 T(\mathbb A_{F,f})=T(F)\,T(\widehat{\mathcal O}_F)
 \tag{R7.9}
\]
for \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)\). Its ingredients are the class-number
one calculation for \(E\) and the explicit full signatures of
\(-1,\zeta+\zeta^{-1},\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}\), which imply
that every totally positive unit of \(F\) is a square. That
proof is cited once under its own finding identifier; it is not
counted again here.

For the standard lattice, diagonal norm-one units in (R7.3) show
\(\det K_0=T(\widehat{\mathcal O}_F)\). Thus for each
\(g\), equation (R7.9) permits \(t_{\mathcal S}=1\) in
(R7.5). In the preceding proof \(a=1\), and (R7.8) has its only
noncompact finite factor at \(v_{11}\). This is (R7.2). The
unique prime of \(F\) above 11 follows because the image of 4
in \((\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\) has order
three; its two lifts to \(E\) exhibit the relative splitting.

## What the representative theorem reduces, and what it does not

There is a useful exact conditional transport principle. Suppose
two assignments on \(G(\mathbb A_{F,f})\) have the same rational
left-equivariance and the same right-\(K\) equivariance, with
specified invertible transport operators, and agree on all
finite-support split-place representatives. Factorization (R7.1)
then proves equality everywhere: apply the common right transport
and common rational left transport to the representative equality.
This short argument proves the principle, including its explicit
equivariance hypotheses.

AM-DO-017 gives exact transport when all lattices, including the
base lattice, are carried by a fixed complex-linear isometry.
That does not yet give the rational left-equivariance needed for
the fixed-base two-average family (DO18.3). Returning the moved
base to the original base introduces its finite triangle phase.
Therefore the present theorem, even combined with split-place
phase equality, leaves the rational triangle/order law and compact
vacuum character as real proof obligations. It cannot erase them
by choosing representatives.

This theorem also has no quantitative height assertion. Strong
approximation supplies existence in an open adelic set; it does
not bound the matrices \(\gamma\), the supports or denominators
of \(a\), the Smith indices \(q_1,q_2\), or the finite
stabilizer factors. Those quantities enter AM-DO-018's explicit
costs and AM-R-002's terminal content formula. A shorter lift chain
still requires those actual data and all six linked identities.

In geometric terms the double cosets classify the indicated
Hermitian lattice genus with level structure. The determinant
sequence \(1\to SU\to U\to T\to1\) separates its simply
connected derived part from its norm-one torus part. This is the
reductive-group and torsor side of the arithmetic comparison; it
does not identify integral theta sections, crystalline filtrations,
or an equality of motivic L-functions. The exact SGA3 and Picard
framework locators are maintained in the geometric dictionary.
