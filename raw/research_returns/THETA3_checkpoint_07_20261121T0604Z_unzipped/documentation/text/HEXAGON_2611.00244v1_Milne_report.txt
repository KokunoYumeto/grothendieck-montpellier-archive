 1.5. What was my motivation for the conjecture? Deligne’s theorem allows you to
define a category of abelian motives over a field of characteristic zero with all the prop-

conjecture allows you to extend this to mixed characteristic. There are many heuristic
arguments proving things assuming a sufficiently good theory of motives. With the proof
of the conjecture, and the existence of the categories of motives, some of these heuristic
arguments become proofs.

2 The Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties
Theorem 2.1 (OpenAI). The Hodge conjecture is true for abelian varieties of CM type.

 2.2. Why is this so important? After all, we know the Hodge conjecture for many abelian
varieties. Indeed, we know the Hodge conjecture for any variety for which the “known”
algebraic classes exhaust the ℚ-algebra of Hodge classes. For example, since we know
the Hodge conjecture for the (1, 1) Hodge classes, we know the conjecture for any variety
for which these classes generate the ℚ-algebra of Hodge classes. Results of this nature,
while interesting, tell us nothing about the veracity of the Hodge conjecture. For that, we
have to prove the conjecture for a class of algebraic variety for which the known classes
do not exhaust the Hodge classes. The abelian varieties of CM type are such a class.

 2.3. The Hodge conjecture for CM is not known to imply the Hodge conjecture for all
abelian varieties, but it would be strange if this wasn’t true. The CM-points are dense in
the moduli space of abelian varieties. If the ℚ-algebra of algebraic classes differed from
the ℚ-algebra of Hodge classes at some point, it would be very odd if this doesn’t persist
over a CM point.

 2.4. Finally, as we shall see, once the Hodge conjecture is known for CM abelian
varieties, it is possible to prove other very interesting results.

  We now sketch the proof of the conjecture. Note that this all takes place over ℂ, so
much of it is complex analysis.

3 The Hodge, Tate, and standard conjectures
The Hodge, Tate, and Grothendieck standard conjectures were stated in the 1950s and
1960s, and have haunted mathematicians ever since. Except for divisors on abelian
varieties (Tate, Zarhin, Faltings), little progress has been made in proving the conjectures.
Thus, the recent proofs for abelian fourfolds have attracted considerable attention.

3.7. By a specialization argument, it suffices to prove the conjecture over finite fields.

 3.8. The Hodge standard conjecture has a tannakian interpretation. It holds for a
collection of algebraic varieties over a field 𝑘 if and only if the category of motives 𝖬𝗈𝗍(𝑘)
generated by the varieties admits a polarization for which the geometric pairings are
positive.

 3.9. Roughly speaking, a polarization on a tannakian category is a compatible family
of positive hermitian forms on the objects. There is an elaborate, and deep, theory of
polarizations on tannakian categories. For example, if 𝐺 is an algebraic group over a
field, then the (tannakian) category of finite-dimensional representations of 𝐺 admits
a polarization if and only if 𝐺 is an inner form of a compact group. See Chapter VI of
Milne 2027.

Applications
Let 𝖬𝗈𝗍(ℚal ) be the category of abelian motives over ℚal for absolute Hodge classes,
and let 𝖬𝗈𝗍′ (ℚal ) be the subcategory of those with good reduction (suitably defined).
With the proof of the rationality conjecture, it is possible to show that there exists a
tannakian category of motives 𝖬𝗈𝗍(𝔽) and reduction functor 𝖬𝗈𝗍′ (ℚal ) → 𝖬𝗈𝗍(𝔽) having
the properties that Grothendiecks categories would have if the Hodge, Tate, and standard
conjectures were known (Milne 2009). With the proof of the Hodge conjecture for CM
abelian varieties, we even know that 𝖬𝗈𝗍(𝔽) is defined by algebraic cycles and 𝖬𝗈𝗍′ (ℚal )
by almost-algebraic cycles. This allows us to turn some heuristic arguments into proofs.

 3.11. Let 𝐴 be an abelian variety over a number field 𝑘, and let 𝐺 be the Mumford-Tate
group of 𝐴ℂ . It follows from Deligne’s theorem on absolute Hodge classes that, after a
finite extension of 𝑘, there are well-defined Frobenius conjugacy classes in 𝐺(ℚ𝓁 ), 𝓁 a
prime. It is an immediate consequence of the above theory that these are rational, i.e.,
arise from a single conjugacy class in 𝐺(ℚ). See Serre 1994, 12.6.

3.12. We leave it as a challenge to the reader to prove the conjecture of Langlands and
Rapoport (1987) following the heuristic argument they use to derive it.
