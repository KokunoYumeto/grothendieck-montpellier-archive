1    Introduction
Let p be a prime, given in binary, and let f ∈ Fp [x] be a nonzero polynomial, given by
its dense coefficient list. Write n = deg f and L = ⌈log2 p⌉. Complete factorization means
computing the leading coefficient c and distinct monic irreducible polynomials gi , with
positive integer multiplicities ei , such that
                                               Y e
                                        f =c    gi i .
                                                   i

The empty product is permitted when n = 0. The complexity parameter is (n + 1)L, so
an algorithm polynomial in p does not give a polynomial-time algorithm in the prescribed
representation.
    Finite-field factorization is a basic constructive problem in algebra. It also exposes a
persistent distinction between randomized and deterministic computation: a random element
of a product of finite fields often separates its components, whereas constructing a separator
uniformly can require additional arithmetic information. The present paper isolates one
form of that information and gives a deterministic splitting procedure once it is available.
To supply it uniformly, we use the zero-free theorem for finite-order Hecke L-functions in
the companion paper [21, Theorem 1.2]. We state that input precisely in Theorem 11.1
and prove its auxiliary-prime consequence here. The analytic theorem itself belongs to the
companion.
