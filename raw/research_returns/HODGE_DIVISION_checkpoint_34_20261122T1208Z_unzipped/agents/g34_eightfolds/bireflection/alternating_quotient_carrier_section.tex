\section{An alternating quotient must pass through one extraction image}
\label{sec:alternating-quotient-carrier}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}
D-TD17--19, reserved question Q2eg, is a new structural reduction by
\texttt{/root/g34\_eightfolds}. The parent proposed using its general
tensor-orbit bound to isolate a possible Heisenberg carrier. The
factor-through-one-coordinate lemma, and its application to the original
coarse extraction images, are the author's subsequent derivation.
The orbit bound D-TD16 is accepted. Complete author, root and independent
whole-proof reviews of D-TD17--19 pass, with the source and dependency
scopes recorded separately. No finite computation is
claimed or needed for these universal group and representation arguments.

Throughout this section $n\ge5$ and
\[
                         T\in\{A_n,S_n\}.
 \tag{TDC.1}
\]
Every nontrivial normal subgroup of $T$ contains $A_n$.
Indeed this is simplicity when $T=A_n$. For $T=S_n$, a normal
subgroup with trivial intersection with $A_n$ centralizes $A_n$,
and $C_{S_n}(A_n)=1$. The latter equality follows, for example,
by commuting with all three-cycles: their supports show that an
element of that centralizer fixes every three-element subset,
and hence every letter. In particular two nontrivial normal
subgroups of $T$ cannot commute, since $[A_n,A_n]=A_n$.

\subsection*{D-TD17. Descent through a genuine coordinate quotient}

\begin{lemma}[D-TD17]
Let $P$ be a finite group with an injective homomorphism
\[
              P\longrightarrow\prod_{j=1}^s P_j
 \tag{TDC.2}
\]
whose coordinate homomorphisms $\pi_j$ are onto. Every surjection
$\psi:P\to T$ factors through at least one coordinate:
\[
                 \psi=\psi_j\pi_j,
                 \qquad\psi_j:P_j\longrightarrow T
                 \text{ is onto}.
 \tag{TDC.3}
\]
Neither an independent direct-product decomposition of $P$ nor
subgroup lifts of the coordinate groups are assumed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First suppose there are two coordinates. Their kernels $K_1,K_2$
are normal and have trivial intersection. Thus
$[K_1,K_2]\subset K_1\cap K_2=1$. Their images under $\psi$
are commuting normal subgroups of $T$. By the observation preceding
the lemma, at least one image is trivial. Its coordinate kernel
is therefore contained in $\ker\psi$, giving (TDC.3).

For more coordinates let $P_{>1}$ be the actual image in the
remaining coordinates. Then $P$ embeds subdirectly in
$P_1\times P_{>1}$. The two-coordinate conclusion either selects
$P_1$, or factors $\psi$ through $P_{>1}$. Apply induction to
the latter group, whose projections onto the remaining $P_j$
are still onto. The case of one coordinate is immediate.
This proof uses actual quotient kernels, and remains valid for
diagonal or other dependent coordinate images.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The original coarse coordinates of D-TD12}

Fix one complete recursive decomposition D-TD12 of a finite primitive
projective image on $V$. At step $j$ let $B_j$ be the whole extracted
irreducible space, before refining a nonabelian minimal normal group
into its simple components, and put
\[
 V\simeq\bigotimes_{j=1}^s B_j,
 \qquad P_j=\operatorname{im}
       \bigl(P\longrightarrow\operatorname{PGL}(B_j)\bigr).
 \tag{TDC.4}
\]
These coarse algebras are individually stable. Their homomorphisms
are the successive genuine projective quotients in D-TD11--12,
pulled back to the original image. Their product map is injective:
an operator with scalar local operators on every $B_j$ is scalar
on $V$, and hence is the identity in its projective group.
Each coordinate is onto by its definition. This verifies (TDC.2),
including all scalar ambiguities, at the original projective level.

There are exactly two types of coordinate. In the Heisenberg case
$P_j$ contains a normal elementary abelian group $Q_j$ with a
nondegenerate scalar commutator pairing, of order $p^{2m}$, and
$\dim B_j=p^m$. In the nonabelian case it contains a normal
\[
                  N_j=S^t,
 \tag{TDC.5}
\]
where $S$ is nonabelian simple. Its factors are transitively permuted
by $P_j$, and their perfect linear lifts act irreducibly on $B_j$
as a central product. These are statements about the actual
coordinate images. Later cores need not lift to subgroups of the
original linear coefficient group.

The same statements hold for any subcollection of the coarse spaces.
Its tensor product has an actual projective quotient image, which
embeds subdirectly in precisely those original $P_j$. To verify
well-definedness, an original operator which is scalar on that
tensor product is scalar on every local factor: conjugation on
each embedded full matrix algebra is the identity. The tensor
product has no omitted multiplicity, so the reverse implication
also holds. This observation allows a whole complement to be
treated without choosing a new recursion or changing a previously
fixed maximal alternating degree.

\subsection*{D-TD18. Exact alternatives for the selected coordinate}

\begin{theorem}[D-TD18]
Suppose $P$ has the complete coarse coordinates (TDC.4), and has
a surjection onto $T$ in (TDC.1). Assume that every nonabelian
coordinate (TDC.5) has $t<n$. Then at least one of the original
coordinates has one of the following forms.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It has $t=1$, $S\simeq A_n$, and its surjection onto $T$
is an isomorphism $P_j\simeq T$.
\item It is a Heisenberg coordinate and its actual conjugation
image is an irreducible symplectic subgroup admitting a surjection
\[
 Q_j\triangleleft P_j,\qquad
 H_j=P_j/Q_j\ \subset\ \operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(p),
 \qquad H_j\longrightarrow T\ \text{ onto}.
 \tag{TDC.6}
\]
Here the symplectic action is irreducible on the vector space
$Q_j\simeq\mathbf F_p^{2m}$, not an assertion that $H_j$ is
the full symplectic group. In either alternative the original
surjection factors through the indicated actual image.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply D-TD17 to obtain a coordinate surjection
$\alpha:P_j\to T$. We treat both types of that coordinate.

\paragraph{Nonabelian coordinate and its full normal core.}
Write $N=S_1\times\cdots\times S_t$ as in (TDC.5). Conjugation
embeds $P_j$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(N)$. Here is the needed
injectivity, including possible linear central kernels. Let $E$
be the represented perfect central product of the linear component
lifts on $B_j$. If a local operator centralizes $N$ projectively,
then for every $x\in E$ its conjugation is $x\mapsto c(x)x$.
The scalar function $c:E\to\mathbf C^*$ is a homomorphism.
Perfectness makes it trivial. Schur's lemma then makes the
operator scalar, since $E$ acts irreducibly. Thus the projective
centralizer of $N$ is trivial and
\[
                    N\le P_j\le\operatorname{Aut}(N).
 \tag{TDC.7}
\]
The first inclusion is an actual inclusion of the whole normal
inner group, not merely a subgroup of its automorphism group.

If $\alpha(N)\ne1$, this image is a perfect normal subgroup of
$T$, so it equals $A_n$. The image of each simple factor $S_i$
is normal in $\alpha(N)$, and is either trivial or all of $A_n$.
A nontrivial such image identifies $S$ with $A_n$. Transitivity
of the component action makes all these images nontrivial. If
$t\ge2$, two images equal to $A_n$ would commute, which is
impossible. Hence $t=1$ and $S\simeq A_n$.
Moreover $\alpha|_N$ is injective. Any element of $\ker\alpha$
centralizes $N$, because its commutators with $N$ lie both in
$N$ and in that kernel. By (TDC.7) it is the identity.
Consequently $\alpha:P_j\to T$ is an isomorphism, proving
the first alternative.

Suppose instead that $\alpha(N)=1$. Automorphisms of $S^t$
permute its simple direct factors, and the kernel of that
permutation action consists of componentwise automorphisms.
Dividing by the inner group gives
\[
 \operatorname{Out}(S^t)
       \simeq\operatorname{Out}(S)^t\rtimes S_t.
 \tag{TDC.8}
\]
This identity follows directly by restricting an automorphism
to each factor; the factors are exactly the minimal normal
subgroups of $S^t$. Let $\Pi\le S_t$ be the actual permutation
image of $P_j$. The kernel $D$ of
$P_j/N\to\Pi$ embeds in $\operatorname{Out}(S)^t$.
The Schreier theorem states that $\operatorname{Out}(S)$ is
solvable. The exact published input is Babai--Luks--Seress,
Theorem~2.3, printed p.~1314.\cite{HD:BabaiLuksSeress1997}
It is explicitly classification dependent; no new proof of
that classification is being asserted here.
Thus $D$ is solvable and normal in $P_j/N$. Its image under
$\alpha$ is a solvable normal subgroup of $T$, necessarily
trivial. The surjection therefore factors through $\Pi$.
This is impossible, since
\[
              |\Pi|\le t!\le(n-1)!<n!/2=|A_n|.
 \tag{TDC.9}
\]
This argument uses the full normal subgroup $N$ in (TDC.7).
It does not assert that an arbitrary subgroup of
$\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ has no alternating section.

\paragraph{Heisenberg coordinate.}
Now $Q=Q_j$ is abelian and normal in $P_j$, so
$\alpha(Q)=1$. Conjugation preserves its scalar commutator
pairing. D-TD05, specifically (TDE.7), proves that its
projective centralizer is exactly $Q$; the elementary proof
uses the Weyl-operator basis and nondegeneracy, also for $p=2$.
Consequently the conjugation kernel inside $P_j$ is exactly
$Q$, and the resulting quotient embeds in
$\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(p)$. This proves the actual quotient
and surjection in (TDC.6); no normalizer extension splitting
has been used.

Finally $Q$ came from a minimal normal subgroup at its extraction
step. The map from that step's current projective image onto
$P_j$ is onto and is injective on $Q$. An $H_j$-invariant
proper nonzero subspace of $Q$ would therefore be a proper
nontrivial normal subgroup of the current image inside that
minimal normal subgroup. This is impossible. Hence the indicated
symplectic action is irreducible. This completes both cases.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{D-TD19. The faithful partner of a maximal standard layer}

\begin{theorem}[D-TD19]
Fix one complete D-TD12 decomposition of an actual finite primitive
rank-nine coefficient source in the scope of HD-ML02. Suppose its
maximal alternating natural degree is $n\ge N_*$, with the precise
integer $N_*$ of (ML.1). Select one of its singleton standard
layers and write its actual linear factorization as
\[
 V=\operatorname{Std}_n(x)\otimes U,
 \qquad x:J\longrightarrow T\in\{A_n,S_n\}\ \text{ onto}.
 \tag{TDC.10}
\]
Assume that the actual linear representation $U$ is faithful.
Assume also the orbit conclusion D-TD16 for this same complete
decomposition. Then some Heisenberg extraction coordinate in
the entire complement $U$ has
\[
 \dim B_j=p^m,\quad p<7744,\quad
 H_j\le\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(p)\text{ irreducible},\quad
 J\longrightarrow P_j\longrightarrow H_j
           \longrightarrow T
 \tag{TDC.11}
\]
as a chain of surjections whose composition is $x$.
The image $P_j$ contains the full normal Heisenberg group
$Q_j\simeq\mathbf F_p^{2m}$ and $H_j=P_j/Q_j$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $U(g)$ is scalar, then $U([g,h])=1$ for every $h\in J$.
Faithfulness of $U$ implies $g\in Z(J)$. The image $x(g)$
therefore centralizes $T$, so it is the identity. Thus $x$
descends to a surjection from the actual projective partner
image:
\[
         P_U=\operatorname{im}(J\to\operatorname{PGL}(U))
                      \longrightarrow T.
 \tag{TDC.12}
\]
This is the point where faithfulness of $U$ is required.
No such factorization is asserted merely from faithfulness of $V$.

Use all original coarse coordinates except the selected standard
coordinate. By the subcollection argument after (TDC.5), $P_U$
embeds subdirectly in those coordinate images. In particular no
new decomposition of $U$ with an uncontrolled maximal degree
is being chosen. D-TD16 gives, for each component orbit of one
of its nonabelian coordinates,
\[
                  t\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor<n.
 \tag{TDC.13}
\]
Here $n\ge N_*>81$, so its all-layer parameter is $M=n-1$.
D-TD17--18 now applies to (TDC.12).

The first alternative of D-TD18 would be another singleton
extraction layer with simple quotient $A_n$ in the same original
decomposition. It is maximal there as well. HD-ML02 makes it
an ordinary standard layer. Thus there would be two separately
stable whole standard layers of natural degree $n$, with their
entire remaining partner retained. HD-MS03 forbids this, since
its inequality $m\le(n+1)/2$ fails for $m=n>1$.
That theorem does not require the two natural quotient actions
to be independent, so a diagonal relation between them provides
no exception. We are left with the Heisenberg alternative.

All maps in (TDC.11) are actual finite quotient maps pulled back
to the original coefficient group. In particular $P_j$ defines
the connected quotient cover
$Z=C/\ker(J\to P_j)\to\mathbf P^1$.
The entire complement of $B_j$ in $V$ is an actual tensor partner,
and the original moving-rank budget is still $2\dim V+6$.
These are precisely the hypotheses (TDE.1)--(TDE.4) of D-TD05--07;
no twelve-mark assumption is imposed on this projected tuple.
If $p\ge7744$, D-TD07 forces
$P_j=E[p]\rtimes C_d$, $d\in\{2,3,4,6\}$, an elliptic and
solvable quotient group. Such a group cannot surject onto $T$.
Therefore $p<7744$, proving the remaining assertion.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact remaining gate.}
The new conclusion is an actual alternating quotient of an irreducible
symplectic subgroup over one of finitely many primes. It is stronger
than the occurrence of an $A_n$ composition factor in an unspecified
subgroup of a product. It does not identify $H_j$ with the full
symplectic group, lift $T$ to a subgroup of $H_j$ or of $J$, bound
$m$, or produce a source. Existing theorems for a full symplectic
quasisimple core cannot be applied to this arbitrary subgroup image
without another proved interface.

The other large-partner branch, where $\ker U$ is nontrivial and
transitive on the natural letters, is not covered by D-TD19.
HD-SS06--07 retains its precise source-descent range and minimal-source
consequence; neither is strengthened here. Original coefficient fields,
period ranks, polarizations, and determinant classes are not changed
or newly descended by this structural reduction.
