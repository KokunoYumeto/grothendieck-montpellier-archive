\subsection{A common partial-normalization test for mixed degree three}
\begin{lemma}[The purely graph obstruction, conditional on the norm-family image]
\label{lem:root-mixed-d3-partial-normalization-candidate}
\status{Candidate; the conditional graph argument is written, and the norm-family image hypothesis remains under investigation.}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic two and let
$\phi:E'_0\to E_0$ be a cyclic etale isogeny of degree three
between supersingular elliptic curves. Put $X'=E\times E'_0$,
$X=E\times E_0$ with $E$ ordinary. Consider the norm family
from smooth sharp source curves of type $(3,9)$, including every
Picard twist whose norm is trivial. Suppose its target relative
rank-$80$ representation has a graph kernel $T$ of dimension $54$
with full permutation image $S_{54}$, an actual natural
$\mathrm{GL}_{26}(\mathbf F_2)$ normalization block, and all
$\operatorname{Hom}(U,T^0)$ gluing translations, where $T^0$
is the sum-zero heart. Assume these statements also apply after
any such source Picard twist. Then the full geometric rank-$80$
representation in the balanced system of type $(9,9)$ is irreducible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any invariant subspace with nonzero projection to the normalization
contains $U$ modulo $T$, of dimension $26$, and contains the heart
$T^0$, of dimension $53$. A proper such subspace would therefore
have codimension one. This contradicts the already proved absence
of an invariant covector in odd balanced degree. Thus every proper
nonzero invariant subspace $S$ lies in $T$ on each norm family.

Choose a smooth sharp curve $C_1$ of type $(3,3)$ whose norm
$D$ of type $(9,3)$ has precisely eighteen ordinary self-nodes,
and choose six distinct ordinary horizontal target fibres so that
their union with $D$ lies in the balanced $(9,9)$ system. Require
that all their inverse images on $C_1$ be unramified and that no
target intersection be a self-node of $D$. These are the explicit
nodality and transversality requirements on this construction;
their generic existence must be checked with the norm-family
geometry, not inferred from the graph computation.
Denote the resulting fixed target curve by $Y$.

For each target horizontal fibre choose one of its three source
lifts, independently, and let $C_{\mathbf r}$ be the union of
$C_1$ with those six chosen ordinary fibres. Each is a nodal sharp
curve of type $(3,9)$ with a possible Picard twist in the kernel
of the norm. Its norm is the same $Y$ for all $3^6$ choices.
The full source two-rank is $9+6+12=27$. The target two-rank is
$9+6+18+48=81$, so its relative rank is $80$, the generic value.
Thus $Y$ belongs to the maximal-rank open: this comparison has no
rank drop at the target and the fibre of $S$ has its generic dimension.

The partial normalization $C_{\mathbf r}\to Y$ separates the
eighteen self-nodes of $D$ and six of the nine cross-nodes on each
horizontal fibre. Its unit kernel $T_{\mathbf r}$ has dimension
$18+6\cdot6=54$. It extends as a constant unit kernel locally in
the corresponding norm family, since these additional nodes stay
ordinary and disjoint. The hypothetical proper $S_Y$ lies in each
$T_{\mathbf r}$.

Use the graph with one normalization vertex for $C_1$, six vertices
for the ordinary fibres, eighteen loops, and nine parallel edges to
each other vertex. Its first cohomology has dimension $66$. For one
horizontal fibre the three source choices select three disjoint
triples of its nine edges. Membership in $T_{\mathbf r}$ says that
the cochain is constant on the selected triple, modulo the common
nine-edge star coboundary. Membership for all three choices says
it is constant on all three triples separately, a two-dimensional
subspace modulo the full star. Consequently
\[
             \dim\bigcap_{\mathbf r}T_{\mathbf r}
                        =18+6\cdot2=30.
\]
An $S_{54}$-invariant subspace of its permutation module containing
a nonconstant vector contains the sum-zero heart: subtract its
image under a transposition exchanging unequal coordinates, then
conjugate the resulting pair vector. Hence its dimension is at least
$53$. It follows that $S_Y$, if nonzero, must be the diagonal line
of every $T_{\mathbf r}$.

Those diagonal lines are different. Modulo the nine-edge star,
the sum of the six unselected edges equals the indicator of the
selected triple. Changing one source lift changes the diagonal by
the sum of two distinct triple indicators, a six-edge vector which
is neither zero nor the full nine-edge star. It is therefore nonzero
in graph cohomology. The two diagonal lines over $\mathbf F_2$ are
distinct, so have zero intersection. This contradicts $S_Y\ne0$.
\end{proof}
