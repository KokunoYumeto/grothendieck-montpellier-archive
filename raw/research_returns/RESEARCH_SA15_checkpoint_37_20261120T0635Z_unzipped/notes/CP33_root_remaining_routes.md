# Remaining geometric routes after the CP33 polar theorem

These are dated research-scope notes from the root work on 20 November 2026, approximately 03:10--03:28 UTC. They are not additional promoted theorems and do not change the exact-degree table. The full written, independently audited results are the canonical polar, main-orbit and full-radical group sections.

## Exceptional images and their tangent spaces

On the section cone the actual Lang equations are B(y^[2])s=y. Their derivative in y is minus identity, so the Lang scheme is etale over the section cone. If a dominating component has generic dual rank t and image cone Y in y-space, then its fibre at a fixed compatible y is the full irreducible affine solution space of dimension N-t. The proposed dimension-and-differential calculation therefore gives dim(Y)=t and, generically, T_yY=im B(y^[2]). The connected scaling action preserves the component. This is a necessary condition for an exceptional component; no argument excluding all cones with that condition has yet been supplied. OC has independently checked this route's scope. It does not follow merely from the birational kernel map.

## The degree-parity route has a boundary problem

The main component is now known to have degree 2^r-2^dim(K). Proving this degree odd would force K=0. A formal rank-two solution-bundle calculation on the maximal-rank polar open suggests an odd contribution, but that bundle is not automatically defined on a projective compactification. Mono identified an additional strict-transform issue: in the N=5 height-two Pfaffian countermodel, a point can have order(sigma)=1 and order(q)=3, leaving an odd exceptional contribution after division by the squared base factor. Thus the residual total divisor is not automatically the strict main transform.

A more precise proposed formulation uses the graph in a flag variety of the radical line and the solution plane. The radical line is an even line bundle O(-2L), while the quotient line has a map to O(H) and may be O(H-D) for an effective boundary divisor D. The resulting parity calculation would require control of D; evenness has not been proved. Over a valuation ring, squaring the pencil gives even Smith valuations, but the transformed coordinates U^[2]y can have odd vanishing. Squared matrix coefficients alone therefore do not establish the required evenness. This is a limitation of the proposed argument, not a counterexample to the desired final parity.

## Whole-wall irreducibility is not an available shortcut

Root proposed combining polar irreducibility with birational reciprocal kernel maps to show that every wall divisor belonged to the common maximal-rank wall. OC pointed out that already in the fixed degree-one symmetric theta model the maximal Pfaffian vector has the form Q(s)s, and the actual Euler determinant has the form Q(s)^2 F(s). Thus extra first-rank-drop divisors are present. This invalidates the proposed whole-wall irreducibility target. A corrected route would need to prove that the residual submodule K is unramified along all those extra divisors. Even determinant multiplicity does not supply that conclusion: a determinant of order two can permit a tame order-three contribution from a rank-two block. No unramified-extension or generation-by-all-divisorial-inertia assertion is made.

## An unsuccessful abstract countermodel design

Root considered an odd alternating pencil whose first three coefficient matrices were supported only on a three-dimensional subspace, attempting to preserve a lower-rank Fermat cone. The same support restriction forces the left kernel of the section matrix to stay in a projective plane. If the section matrix attains maximal rank, its reciprocal dual kernel therefore lies entirely in the lower-rank dual locus. The construction fails the actual common-maximal-rank hypothesis and cannot refute the geometric target. This was a symbolic failed design; no numerical program was run or omitted.
