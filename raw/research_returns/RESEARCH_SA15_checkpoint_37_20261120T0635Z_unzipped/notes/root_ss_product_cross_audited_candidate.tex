\subsection{Twisted Cartier blocks on a supersingular elliptic product}

Work over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic two.
Let \(E:y^2+y=x^3\), with origin \(O\), and write
\(M_E=\mathcal O_E(3O)\). We use relative Frobenius and relative
Cartier throughout. Thus, on the Frobenius twist, the coordinates
\(X,Y\) pull back to \(x^2,y^2\), while coefficients in \(k\)
remain fixed. If
\[
 f=h_0(x^2,y^2)+x h_1(x^2,y^2),
\]
with the coefficients of \(h_i\) unchanged, then
\(C_{\rm rel}(f\,dx)=h_1(X,Y)\,dX\).
The target line of a product of two sections of \(M_E\) is
\(M_E^{(2)}\), by projection formula. This convention avoids
identifying absolute Cartier with a \(k\)-linear map.

\begin{lemma}[A universal twisted elliptic block]
\label{lem:DE-SS-cross-elliptic}
\status{Candidate; complete direct proof and exact identities supplied.}
For every nontrivial \(P\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)(k)\), the
relative Cartier pairing
\[
 H^0(M_E P)\otimes H^0(M_E P^{-1})
       \longrightarrow H^0(M_E^{(2)})
\]
has a functional giving a perfect pairing of the two
three-dimensional spaces. More precisely, identify
\(P=\mathcal O_E(P_0-O)\), with \(P_0=(a,b)\) and
\(b^2+b=a^3\). There are bases in which its matrix is
\[
 A_0+X A_1+Y A_2=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&a&X\\ a&X&Y+b\\ X&Y+b+1&a
 \end{pmatrix}.                                      \tag{DE-XC1}
\]
Here \(A_1=R\) is the reversal matrix, \(R^2=1\), and
\[
 \det(rA_0+sA_1+tA_2)=r^2t+rt^2+s^3.                 \tag{DE-XC2}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The divisors of the two spaces are \(2O+P_0\) and
\(2O+(-P_0)\), not a divisor involving a negative point.
Their bases are respectively
\[
 \left(1,x,\frac{y+b+1}{x+a}\right),\qquad
 \left(1,x,\frac{y+b}{x+a}\right).
\]
Indeed \(\operatorname{div}(x+a)=P_0+(-P_0)-2O\);
the two rational functions have a simple pole at the indicated
finite point and at most a simple pole at \(O\). Together with
\(1,x\) they are independent, and Riemann--Roch gives dimension
three. The product trivialization into \(M_E^2\) multiplies
these rational functions by \(x+a\).

The bottom-right product simplifies to \(x^2+ax+a^2\), since
\[
 (y+b)(y+b+1)=x^3+a^3=(x+a)(x^2+ax+a^2).
\]
The nine products are the entries of
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 x+a&x(x+a)&y+b\\
 x(x+a)&x^2(x+a)&x(y+b)\\
 y+b+1&x(y+b+1)&x^2+ax+a^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their relative \(p\)-basis decompositions have coefficient
of \(x\) exactly (DE-XC1). For example,
\(y+b=(y^2+b)+x\cdot x^2\) and
\(x(y+b)=x^4+x(y^2+b)\). The constant parts, expressed on
the target, form the matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 a&X&Y+b\\ X&aX&X^2\\ Y+b+1&X^2&X+a^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
This verifies every entry, including its coefficient convention.
Expanding the determinant and using \(b^2+b=a^3\) gives
(DE-XC2). In particular the functional extracting \(X\)
gives the invertible matrix \(R\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Every nontrivial product character has a perfect cross block]
\label{lem:DE-SS-cross-product}
\status{Candidate; complete direct proof and exact identities supplied.}
Put \(B=E\times E\) and \(M=M_E\boxtimes M_E\).
For every nontrivial \(P\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)(k)\),
there is a functional \(\ell\in H^0(M^{(2)})^\vee\)
for which
\[
 (s,t)\longmapsto\ell(C_{\rm rel}(st\,dx\wedge dz))
 \quad\text{on }H^0(MP)\times H^0(MP^{-1})
                                                               \tag{DE-XC3}
\]
is perfect. For \(P=\mathcal O_B\), some such functional
instead gives alternating rank eight on \(H^0(M)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every degree-zero line bundle on the product is
\(P_1\boxtimes P_2\). Kunneth and relative Cartier identify
(DE-XC3) with the tensor product of the two elliptic pairings.
If both \(P_i\) are nontrivial, the functional extracting
\(X\otimes Z\) gives \(R\otimes R\), which is invertible.

For a trivial factor, the untwisted elliptic matrix in the
basis \((1,z,w)\) is
\[
 B(1,Z,W)=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1&Z\\1&0&W\\Z&W&0\end{pmatrix}
       =B_0+ZB_1+WB_2.
\]
Suppose just the first factor is nontrivial, and take \(\ell\)
to have value one on \(X\otimes1,1\otimes Z,Y\otimes W\)
and zero on the other six product basis elements. Ordering
the second factor outside, the resulting cross matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&R&A_0\\R&0&A_2\\A_0&A_2&0\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{DE-XC4}
\]
The upper-left six-dimensional block is invertible. Its
Schur complement is, in characteristic two,
\[
 A_0RA_2+A_2RA_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1&a\\1&0&0\\a&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det=1.                                      \tag{DE-XC5}
\]
Thus (DE-XC4) is invertible for every \(P_1\ne0\).
Interchanging the factors treats the other case.

Finally, when both factors are trivial, take the functional
whose coefficient matrix on the two target bases is the identity.
The alternating matrix is \(\sum_{i=0}^2 B_i\otimes B_i\).
On the six off-diagonal tensor basis vectors it swaps the three
pairs. On the diagonal three-space it has zero diagonal and
all off-diagonal entries one, hence rank two in characteristic
two. Its total rank is eight; equivalently, deleting the first
row and column gives a unit eight-dimensional minor.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Maximal dual rank for odd etale pullbacks from the product]
\label{thm:DE-SS-odd-pullback-dual-rank}
\status{Candidate; complete geometric proof supplied.}
Let \(\pi:A\to B=E^2\) be an etale isogeny of odd degree
\(n\), and put \(L=\pi^*M\), where \(M=\mathcal O_E(3O)
\boxtimes\mathcal O_E(3O)\). The family of alternating forms
\[
 \mathcal B_\lambda(s,t)=
 \lambda\bigl(C_{\rm rel}(st\,\omega_A)\bigr),\qquad
 \lambda\in H^0(L^{(2)})^\vee,
 \quad s,t\in H^0(L),                                \tag{DE-XC6}
\]
has generic rank \(9n-1\). Here \(\omega_A\) is the
pullback of \(dx\wedge dz\), and target differentials are
identified using its Frobenius twist.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Over \(k\), the odd finite kernel is constant and its characters
split the etale algebra as
\[
 \pi_*\mathcal O_A=\bigoplus_{\chi}P_\chi,
 \qquad P_\chi P_\psi\simeq P_{\chi\psi}.
\]
The eigensheaves are degree-zero line bundles. Only the trivial
character has \(P_\chi\simeq\mathcal O_B\), since a nonzero
global eigenfunction on the connected proper variety \(A\)
would be constant. Consequently
\[
 H^0(L)=\bigoplus_\chi H^0(MP_\chi),\qquad h^0(L)=9n.
\]
Use functionals on \(H^0(L^{(2)})\) that vanish on all
nontrivial character summands. Relative Cartier is compatible
with etale pullback and the deck action. Its Frobenius relabeling
of characters preserves triviality, so a block can survive
exactly when \(\chi\psi=1\). Since the character group has
odd order, only the trivial character is its own inverse.

The trivial block is the nine-dimensional pairing of the
preceding lemma. Every other block pair has matrix
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&C_\chi\\C_\chi^t&0
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), with \(C_\chi\) nine-dimensional.
The multiplication trivializations of inverse eigensheaves
can change these matrices only by nonzero scalars and basis
changes. The preceding lemma proves that each polynomial
\(\det C_\chi(\ell)\) on the same nine-dimensional functional
space is nonzero. The rank-eight open for the trivial block
and these finitely many determinant opens therefore intersect:
their coordinate ring is a polynomial domain over the infinite
field \(k\). At any point of the intersection, the rank is
\[
             8+18\frac{n-1}{2}=9n-1.
\]
All forms (DE-XC6) are alternating: \(C_{\rm rel}(\omega_A)=0\)
and projection formula gives
\(C_{\rm rel}(s^2\omega_A)=s^{(2)}C_{\rm rel}(\omega_A)=0\).
On the odd-dimensional space of dimension \(9n\), their rank
is at most \(9n-1\). The attained bound is consequently the
generic rank on the full dual functional space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Odd rectangles on the supersingular product]
\label{cor:DE-SS-odd-rectangle-dual-rank}
\status{Candidate; complete deduction supplied.}
For positive odd integers \(a,b\), the same family on
\(E^2\) with \(L=\mathcal O_E(3aO)\boxtimes
\mathcal O_E(3bO)\) has generic rank \(9ab-1\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose odd etale endomorphisms of the two elliptic factors
of degrees \(a,b\), supplied by the explicit quaternion-norm
construction in Lemma~\ref{lem:DE-A3-ss-norm}. The
sum of an odd-order kernel is zero, by pairing every point
with its inverse. Thus the pullback of \(\mathcal O_E(O)\)
is \(\mathcal O_E(aO)\), respectively \(\mathcal O_E(bO)\).
The product isogeny has degree \(ab\) and pulls \(M\) back
to the stated line bundle. Apply the theorem.
\end{proof}
