## Cyclic source and character cycles

The compatible intermediate theorem reduces a prime target to particular nonprincipal cyclic sources under explicit full-image hypotheses. On a central cubic slice a nontrivial odd character is permuted by Frobenius, so the actual F2 Lang summand is its entire Frobenius orbit. For a prime cyclic deck group its rank is 9 ord_ell(2), rather than nine over F2. Proving only that a nine-dimensional Cartier contraction is invertible does not prove the monodromy of this cycle irreducible or full.

A possible next route is to show that the central coefficient monodromy is irreducible on every such actual cycle, then prove that any complete nontrivial cycle contains an actual Lang vector of maximal dual rank. The canonical exceptional K cannot contain such a vector. Neither assertion has been proved. Distinct deck eigenvector slices may be disjoint, so translation between them cannot silently be turned into an identification inside one fixed fibre.

## Exterior vectors and the extra divisors

The canonical K is regular at the maximal common-rank wall: its vectors lie in the regular hyperplane already compared in the full deck-slice proof. If its determinant vector extended regularly across every remaining coefficient divisor, a nonzero invariant wedge would give a global section of a positive exterior power of the Euler cotangent bundle, which has no such sections. The required extension is the missing step. Frobenius is degenerate on the extra rank-defect divisors, and leading poles of a vector may cancel through its kernel. The earlier regular-extension argument for dual covectors does not imply this vector extension. The degree-one Hasse divisor already has an extra squared Pfaffian factor, so the existence of extra divisors cannot be dismissed.
