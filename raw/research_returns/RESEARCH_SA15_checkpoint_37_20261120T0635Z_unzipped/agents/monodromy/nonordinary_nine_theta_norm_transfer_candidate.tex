\subsection{Norm transfer from nine theta without a nodal principal theta}
\label{subsec:nine-theta-norm-transfer-candidate}
\status{Candidate; complete proof submitted for independent audit.}

Work geometrically in characteristic two. Let $(B,\Theta)$ be an
indecomposable nonordinary principally polarized abelian surface and
let $\phi:B\to A$ be an etale polarized isogeny of odd multiplier
$n>1$. Write $K=\ker\phi$, $k=|K|=n^2$, and
$\mathcal H=(K\setminus0)/\{h\sim-h\}$. No condition on the
singularities of the principal theta image is imposed.

\begin{lemma}[Clean contacts between cubic components]
\label{lem:nine-theta-clean-cubic-contacts-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
Three general members $C_i\in|3\Theta|$ are smooth, sharp, and
have integral nodal norms. Their norms have distinct self-nodes,
meet transversely, and have no triple identifications. These open
conditions can be imposed simultaneously after all independent
translations $C_i+r_i$, with $r_i\in K$ and $\sum r_i=0$.

For any $h\in K$, there is also a boundary with precisely one
ordinary tangency between $C_1$ and $C_2+h$, all other shifted
intersections and all individual norm identifications remaining
distinct ordinary nodes. If $h=0$ it is an internal source
tangency; if $h\ne0$ it is precisely one additional norm tangency.
The chosen contact image has no further branch. All components
remain smooth and sharp.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The generic nodality of a cubic norm for arbitrary odd $K$ is the
incidence calculation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonordinary-ppav-odd-sharp-norm-candidate}.
It uses two independent values, full joint first jets away from
finitely many translated pairs, and independence of three values.
Smoothness and sharpness are dense opens by the proved degree-one
rank and full-image theorems.

We give the simultaneous contact counts, since those are the new
point. Fix a translation $h$. The incidence consisting of a point
$P$, a tangent direction $\ell$ at $P$, and cubic sections with
$C_1(P)=C_2(P-h)=0$ and both tangent to $\ell$ (after translation)
is a $\mathbf P^6\times\mathbf P^6$ bundle over the projective
tangent bundle of $B$. It is irreducible of dimension $15$.
Both projections to the coefficient $\mathbf P^8$ are dominant:
on a given smooth component choose a point and impose its two
linear value/direction conditions on the other component.
The single-point second-jet theorem supplies nonzero first
derivatives and exact order-two contact. Thus smoothness, sharpness, and individual norm nodality hold on a dense open of
this incidence, and ordinary simple contact is a nonempty open.

Recall the precise cubic jet bounds already proved. Joint first
jets at two distinct points have rank six off a locus $R$ of
dimension at most two, rank at least five off a locus of dimension
at most one, and rank at least four everywhere. Each fixed-point
exceptional fibre is finite. After imposing the two independent
values, the gradient image has dimension at least two, and its
projection to each of the two cotangent spaces is nonzero: one
additional value condition cannot kill both gradients at the other
point. For direction pairs in $\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1$,
let $s\in\{0,1,2\}$ be the rank of the two derivative rows on this
gradient image. The locus with rank at most $s$ has dimension at
most $s$. For $s=0$, each direction must annihilate its nonzero
gradient projection, so at most one direction pair works. For
$s=1$, the determinant is not identically zero: the two nonzero
spaces of derivative functionals together span a space of dimension
at least two. The assertion for $s=2$ is immediate.

For two distinct contacts, with shifts $h,h'$, the base consists
of two contact points and two directions. Off both joint-jet
exceptional sets, eight independent equations against six
parameters give dimension $14$. If one joint-jet pair is
exceptional, its point-pair base has dimension at most two.
If both are exceptional and the derivative ranks are $s_1,s_2$,
the dimension is at most
\[
       16-4-s_1-s_2+2+\min(s_1,s_2)\le14.        \tag{N9.1}
\]
If only one is exceptional, the full pair has derivative rank two
and the same bound is stronger. A coincidence of contact points
on a smooth component forces the two directions to agree.
The other component has two distinct points unless the contacts
are identical. Away from its finite translated-pair exceptions
the count is $16-6+3=13$; at those exceptions the point parameter
is finite and the count is still at most $13$.
Thus no second contact, of any prescribed kernel shift, is forced
on the irreducible contact incidence.

We also exclude triple packets, rather than merely checking
tangencies. A packet split between the two components has one
point on one component and two prescribed kernel translates on
the other. Away from the marked contact image, the generic count
is seven value/direction equations against five parameters, so
has dimension $14$. Here is an exhaustive exceptional-rank check. Denote by $r_1$
the dimension of the marked derivative image after the two values
on the first component, and by $r_2$ its dimension after the three
values on the second. One extra value kills at most one of the
two marked gradients, so $r_1\ge1$. If $r_1=1$, the extra value
vector lies in the three-dimensional marked first-jet span:
indeed adding that value to the first-jet span has dimension three,
not four. The pair therefore lies in $R$, and the four-dimensional
$(P,Q)$ point base has dimension at most two. Even if both
derivative rows are then lost, this saves the required two
parameters.

Suppose next that $r_1=2$. If $r_2=2$, neither derivative row can
be lost. If $r_2=1$, losing the second row forces the common
$\mathbf P^1$ direction to its unique annihilator; this one
parameter saving exactly compensates for the one equation lost.
Finally, if $r_2=0$, the span of the three independent value
vectors is the full marked first-jet span: both spaces have
dimension three, contain the marked value, and all marked
derivatives lie in the value span. Each extra value consequently
lies in that first-jet span. The marked point and either extra
point form a pair in $R$, again reducing the point base to
dimension at most two. These alternatives exhaust all possibilities
and give dimension at most $14$ throughout.

Coincidences among the packet and marked points put the packet
over the marked contact image. An extra branch there is a value
at a nonzero $K$-translate of one marked point. For a generic
marked point, full joint first jets at that translated pair make
this value independent of the two marked contact conditions,
so it cuts the $15$-dimensional contact incidence. The exceptional
translated point-pairs form a finite set; over them the point
parameter drops from dimension two to zero, so even a dependent
extra value cannot dominate. There are only finitely many such
translates. Individual norm triples were already excluded by the
dense component-nodality opens.

Choose the third cubic afterward. It avoids the finite singular
and pair-intersection sets of the first two norms. A two-point
norm packet of the third cubic meeting a fixed component is a
proper incidence: its point parameter lies on a curve, while
its two values impose two independent conditions. All shifted
pair tangencies with it are proper by the same jet calculation.
There are only finitely many shifts and component choices.
This proves the clean boundary assertions. With no marked contact
the same open conditions give the general clean three-component
boundary. Translating the three components by $r_i\in K$ does
not change their target union; the theorem of the square and
$\sum r_i=0$ keep every source union in $|9\Theta|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The complete node product in the nine-theta norm family]
\label{lem:nine-theta-node-product-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
The generic norm of a smooth sharp source in $|9\Theta|$ is nodal
with $\delta=81(k-1)$ additional nodes. The permutation image is
$\prod_{h\in\mathcal H}S_{162}$. The norm family lies in the
target maximal relative-rank open of rank $R=81k-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The generic-nodality incidence for $3\Theta$ applies also to
$9\Theta$ by multiplication with a section of $6\Theta$ nonzero
at the finitely many prescribed points. An oriented node of type
$h$ is $P$ with $s(P)=s(P+h)=0$. Its incidence is an irreducible
projective bundle, and its degree is $(9\Theta)^2=162$.

Four distinct points impose four independent values on $9\Theta$:
to separate one, multiply three cubic sections, each vanishing
at one of the other points and nonzero at the chosen point.
Thus the four-distinct-point part of the ordered two-node
incidence is an irreducible projective bundle over a four-dimensional
base. The overlap strata use three independent values over a
two-dimensional base and have codimension at least one in the
total incidence. The generic finite etale ordered-pair cover
therefore has only the main component. Each type action is
two-transitive.

For $h\ne0$, use the preceding lemma's single shifted contact
boundary in $|9\Theta|$. Precisely two nodes of that type
coalesce, and every other additional node is etale. The local
ordinary-contact equation gives a genuine quadratic transposition,
also in characteristic two; this is the local calculation already
proved in the cubic-intersection and theta-difference lemmas.
The node covers are defined on the larger normal coefficient
open allowing singular sources, so this inertia belongs to the
generic node-cover image. Restriction to its dense smooth sharp
open surjects on the fundamental group. No cohomological rank
comparison is used in this permutation argument.
Two-transitivity plus a transposition gives the full symmetric
factor, and the isolated support gives that factor independently
of every other type. This proves the product.

The source has relative rank $80$ by the fixed nonordinary
degree-three theorem. Its nodal norm gains $81(k-1)$ graph-unit
directions, giving $R=81k-1$, the exact odd target rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The weighted three-component graph]
\label{lem:nine-theta-weighted-graph-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
For an odd group $K$ of order $k>1$, form a graph with three
vertices, eighteen loops per vertex and unordered nonzero type,
and eighteen cross edges per vertex pair and label $g\in K$.
Its cohomology $G$ has dimension $81k-29$. For each
$r_1+r_2+r_3=0$, select the eighteen cross edges with label
$r_j-r_i$ on each pair, and let $T_r$ be the kernel of restriction
to the selected graph. Then
\[
 \dim T_r=81(k-1),\qquad
 T_r=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathcal H}T_{r,h},\qquad
 \dim T_{r,h}=162.                              \tag{N9.2}
\]
All coordinate hearts $T_{r,h}^0$ together span the total-edge-sum
hyperplane $G^0$. An $\prod_hS_{162}$-invariant subspace of $T_0$
contained in every $T_r$ is zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There are $81k-27$ edges and two vertex coboundaries. The selected
connected graph has $54$ edges and cohomology dimension $52$.
Unselected edge cochains are independent modulo coboundaries,
since a coboundary vanishing on the connected selected graph is
zero. Each type consists of $54$ loops and $108$ cross edges.

The proof of the doubled-triangle graph lemma applies verbatim
with eighteen parallel edges. For completeness, a cycle annihilating
every heart is constant on each type. The loops make its type
constant independent of $r$; varying $r_j-r_i$ through all of $K$
forces all cross-edge and type constants to be the same. The
all-ones vector is a cycle, since every cross degree is $36k$.
This proves the asserted codimension-one span.

If a vector in a purported common invariant subspace is not
constant in one type, a transposition difference and its conjugates
give every pair vector in that type. A pair supported on a loop
and a cross edge cannot restrict to zero when that cross edge is
selected: precisely one of eighteen parallel edges then has value
one. Thus every vector must be type-constant. Extend its constants
to an even function $a:K\to\mathbf F_2$ with $a(0)=0$.
Restriction at $r=(-u,0,u)$ forces
$a(u)+a(u)+a(2u)=0$. Since multiplication by two is bijective on
$K$, all constants vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gluing all type hearts from eighteen selected labels]
\label{prop:nine-theta-full-gluing-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
On the nine-theta norm family, write
$0\to T\to V\to U\to0$ with $\dim U=80$.
The image contains an actual natural $\mathrm{GL}_{80}(\mathbf F_2)$
fixing a complement and every translation in $\operatorname{Hom}(U,T_h^0)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The joint source and node image is the product of
$\mathrm{GL}_{80}$ and $N=\prod_hS_{162}$. Indeed the simple source
group cannot be a composition factor of the node product:
$v_2|\mathrm{GL}_{80}|=3160>159=v_2|S_{162}|$.
Goursat applies. Its translation kernel is $U^*\otimes T'$ for
an $N$-stable $T'\subset T$. The already proved natural-module
$H^1$ vanishing and absence of invariant natural functionals split
the quotient as $(T/T')\oplus U$. Removing its actual kernel
columns also supplies the actual natural source block.

Use the normal coefficient open where all additional norm
identifications are distinct nodes at smooth source points.
Internal source nodes and selected tangencies are allowed.
The partial-normalization sequence is an exact sequence of
coherent Frobenius bundles with graph-unit kernel. After quotienting
by $T'$, the full Lang projection is a finite etale torsor under
$T/T'$, locally given by $z^2-z=b$. The generic splitting extends
over the whole normal source Lang scheme: the closure of its
section on each component is finite birational over that normal
component. Addition and descent extend by density. This is exactly
the finite-torsor argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:good-theta-uniform-gluing-candidate}, and
does not identify the full stable fibre at a rank drop.

Pull the splitting back to the clean three-cubic boundary.
At a selected tangency between components $i,j$, the inertia
difference has pure graph image
$q=e_a+e_b$ in both source and target. Component torsors are
unramified and trivial on the strict henselian local branch
neighbourhoods; all additional node torsors are etale there.
Thus there is no other image column. In the source graph there
is a vector with inertia difference $q$, and $q\ne0$ modulo
vertex coboundaries and the ambient image. Equivariance of the
extended splitting consequently sends this source $q$ to its
exact target pair cochain modulo $T'$.

The selected intersection cover $C_i\cap C_j$ has full $S_{18}$
monodromy by the proved cubic-section quasireflexivity/jet lemma.
The clean conditions only remove a proper closed subset of its
normal parameter base. Conjugating the isolated tangency gives
the preceding lift identity for every pair difference. Any
simultaneous permutation of additional nodes is irrelevant:
pure selected graph cochains remain pure selected graph cochains.
Linearity therefore sends the sum of all eighteen selected edges
to that exact target sum, since it is a sum of nine disjoint pair
differences. At a vertex the totals from its two incident selected
pairs add to a source coboundary. Their target lifts must add to
an element of $T'$. Modulo the target vertex coboundary, this is
the unselected-edge star. In each type it has $72$ ones and $90$
zeros. The independent $S_{162}$ factor and a transposition
difference give all pair vectors of that type, hence its entire
heart. This proves $T_h^0\subset T'$ for every $h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An arbitrary odd norm step from nine theta]
\label{thm:nine-theta-arbitrary-odd-norm-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
For the isogeny stated at the start of this subsection, the relative
geometric image of $|9n\Theta_A|$ is
$\mathrm{GL}_{81n^2-1}(\mathbf F_2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Correct the fixed degree-zero discrepancy of the norm line by a
single target translation, as in the previous norm-step theorem.
Let $S$ be a nonzero proper invariant subspace of the target
system. Its projection on the connected maximal-rank norm family
to the simple source $U$ is uniformly zero or surjective.

For the common target union of three cubic norms, take all source
translations $r_i\in K$ with sum zero. They belong to the same
source system $|9\Theta|$, and their partial-normalization kernels
are the $T_r$ of (N9.2). The full graph embeds in relative Lang
cohomology because ambient cohomology injects into each smooth
ample cubic normalization. The source boundary relative stable
rank is $76+2f$, while its generic rank is $80$; the target
boundary rank is $81k-5+2f$. Apply only the proved clopen closure
lemma on the full coherent relative Lang scheme.

If the projection is zero, the closure retains its full dimension
inside every finite etale $T_r$ and is invariant under the node
product. The weighted graph lemma forces it to be zero. If it is
surjective, all type hearts lie in $S$ by the gluing proposition.
Their specializations span $G^0$, whose intersection with any
$T_r$ has dimension $\delta-1$. The clopen finite-etale
intersection carries that lower bound back to the generic family,
so $\dim S\ge\delta-1+80=R-1$. A proper $S$ would give a fixed
quotient covector, forbidden in odd nonordinary degree. Hence the
representation is irreducible.

Its normal transvection subgroup has semisimple representation
by the usual normal-socle argument. The actual natural source
block, of multiplicity one, lies in one simple summand; all hearts
lie in that same summand by the rank-one gluing maps. Its dimension
is at least
\[
              80+161(k-1)/2>(81k-1)/2.
\]
Its conjugates cannot be distinct disjoint summands, so the
transvection subgroup is irreducible. The previously used
characteristic-two transvection classification and actual-block
no-alternating-form argument give the full general linear group.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every even degree in two-rank zero]
\label{cor:f0-all-even-nine-theta-transfer-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
For every fixed two-rank-zero principally polarized surface over
a perfect field of characteristic two, all odd degrees divisible
by three have geometric and arithmetic group
$\mathrm{GL}_{9d^2-1}(\mathbf F_2)$. Every even degree has group
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_2^{9d^2-4})\rtimes
            \mathrm{GL}_{9d^2-4}(\mathbf F_2).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For an indecomposable surface, the explicit geometric quaternion
automorphisms constructed above satisfy
\[
 (a+bg+ch+dgh)^\dagger(a+bg+ch+dgh)
                         =[a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2].
\]
The complete four-square descent already proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:DE-A3-ss-norm} supplies every positive odd integer
as such a sum. Therefore the same fixed surface has an etale
polarized self-isogeny of every odd multiplier. Degree three
($n=1$) is the already proved fixed degree-three theorem; for
odd $n>1$, apply the present transfer theorem. This gives every
odd degree $3n$. Products were already classified in every degree.

Use even addition with the known odd degrees one and five.
If $d\equiv0\pmod6$, write $d=3+(d-3)$; both summands are odd
multiples of three. If $d\equiv4\pmod6$, use $d=1+(d-1)$.
If $d\equiv2\pmod6$ and $d\ge8$, use $d=5+(d-5)$.
Degree two was already proved separately. The even-addition
theorem gives exactly the stated stabilizer. Arithmetic equality
and perfect-field descent use the already proved unique invariant
covector and the geometric full-image bound.
\end{proof}
