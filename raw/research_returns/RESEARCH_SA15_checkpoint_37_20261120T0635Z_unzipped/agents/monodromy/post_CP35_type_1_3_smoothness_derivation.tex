\subsection*{Type \((1,3)\): stability, evaluation rank and smooth members}

This isolated derivation concerns a globally generated ample line bundle
\(L\) of type \((1,3)\) on an abelian surface over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic two. Thus \(L^2=6\) and \(h^0(L)=3\).
It was independently supplied to the parent while the parent's complete
primitive-polarization candidate was being written. It does not record a
full audit of an unseen final parent file.

Write \(F:A\to A^{(2)}\), \(H=L^{(2)}\), and \(E=F_*L\).
The Frobenius filtration makes
\(F^*E\otimes L^{-1}\) an iterated extension of four trivial
line bundles: the truncated cotangent graded pieces have ranks one,
two and one, and the cotangent bundle of an abelian surface is trivial.
Consequently \(E\) is slope semistable with respect to \(H\),
of slope three.

Here is an elementary strictness argument. Any iterated extension of
trivial line bundles is trivialized by some multiplication map
\([2]^m\): induct on its filtration length, first trivialize the
subobject, then use \([2]^*=0\) on \(H^1(A,\mathcal O_A)\) to kill
the resulting extension of trivial bundles. Suppose a proper saturated
subsheaf \(Q\subset E\) of rank \(r\) has equal slope. It is
locally free, because it is saturated in a vector bundle on a smooth
surface. The bundle \(S=F^*Q\otimes L^{-1}\) has slope zero and is
saturated in the preceding unipotent bundle. After the same isogeny it
is saturated in a trivial four-bundle. Its determinant inverse has a
nonzero section from a Plucker coordinate and has zero intersection
with an ample divisor, hence is trivial. The nonzero Plucker coordinates
are therefore constants. They describe a fixed \(r\)-plane, and the
subbundle itself is trivial. Isogeny pullback multiplies Euler
characteristic by its degree, so \(\chi(S)=0\). Riemann--Roch gives
\[
 \chi(S)=4\chi(Q)-c_1(F^*Q)\cdot L+\frac{rL^2}{2}
        =4\chi(Q)-3r.
\]
Thus four divides \(r\), impossible for a proper nonzero rank.
This proves slope stability without importing a general stability
theorem for simple semihomogeneous bundles.

Let \(W=H^0(L)\), and take the saturated evaluation image
\(Q\subset E\) of \(W\otimes\mathcal O\). A rank-one image has
\(h^0(\det Q)\ge3\). For a rank-two image, the nonzero equivariant
map \(\Lambda^2W\to H^0(\det Q)\) is injective, because
\(\Lambda^2W\) is the irreducible weight-two three-dimensional
representation of the finite level-three theta group. Thus its
determinant again has at least three sections.

Every effective line bundle \(D\) with \(h^0(D)\ge3\) satisfies
\(D\cdot H\ge6\). If \(D^2>0\), it is ample on the abelian
surface; ample vanishing gives \(D^2=2h^0(D)\ge6\), and Hodge
index gives the claim. If \(D^2=0\), its components are parallel
elliptic curves and \(D\equiv nC\) with \(n=h^0(D)\ge3\).
Global generation of \(H\) implies \(H\cdot C\ge2\), since a
degree-one line bundle on an elliptic curve is not globally generated.
This proves the claim in both cases. Stability excludes evaluation
rank one or two. Its generic rank is therefore three.

The complete morphism \(f:A\to\mathbf P(W^*)=\mathbf P^2\) is
finite because \(L\) is ample. At the generic point, normalize the
three sections by a nonzero one to obtain \(1,u,v\). Their independence
over \(k(A)^2\) is the just-proved Frobenius-evaluation rank three.
If \(du,dv\) were dependent, the field \(k(A)^2(u,v)\) would have
degree at most two over \(k(A)^2\); hence \(1,u,v\) would be dependent.
Thus \(df\) has generic rank two.

It remains to exclude a rank-zero critical divisor; generic
separability alone is not being used as a Bertini theorem. Let
\(\Delta\) be the divisorial zero locus of the determinant of
\(W\otimes\mathcal O\to Q\), where now \(Q\) has rank three.
Then \(\mathcal O(\Delta)=\det Q\), and stability gives
\(\Delta\cdot H<9\). Theta translations preserve the evaluation
map, its saturation and this divisor. Therefore \(\Delta\) descends
through the etale quotient by the full order-nine kernel \(K(H)\).
Its self-intersection is divisible by eighteen. Hodge index and
\(H^2=6\) then force \(\Delta^2=0\): positive square would give
\((\Delta\cdot H)^2\ge18\cdot6>9^2\).
Every component of a nonzero effective divisor of square zero on an
abelian surface is a translate of an elliptic curve.

At a point where \(df=0\), choose regular parameters \(x,y\) and
normalize by a locally nonzero section. The three normalized sections
have zero linear terms, so modulo \((x^2,y^2)\) they span at most
\(\langle1,xy\rangle\). Their Frobenius evaluation has rank at most
two. Thus every rank-zero critical divisor, after applying relative
Frobenius, lies in \(\Delta\). The reduced inverse image of an
elliptic component under relative Frobenius is again an elliptic curve;
call such a critical component \(C\subset A\).

Let the image plane curve \(f(C)\) have degree \(m>0\). Zero
differential on the tangent of \(C\) makes its function-field
extension inseparable, so
\(L\cdot C=\deg(f|_C)m\ge2m\). Zero transverse differential
implies that \(C\) has multiplicity at least two in \(f^*f(C)\):
a multiplicity-one local pullback of a uniformizer has nonzero normal
differential. Thus \(mL-2C\) is effective. But
\[
             (mL-2C)^2=6m^2-4m(L\cdot C)\le-2m^2<0,
\]
which is impossible for an effective divisor on an abelian surface.
Consequently the rank-zero critical locus is finite.

The rank-one critical locus has dimension at most one and at each
point specifies exactly one hyperplane with singular pullback. The
finite rank-zero locus contributes finitely many pencils of such
hyperplanes. At a rank-two point no hyperplane pullback is singular.
The incidence image of all singular members is therefore a proper
closed subset of \(\mathbf P(W)\). Smooth members exist and form a
nonempty open; ample cohomology vanishing makes them connected and
hence irreducible.

For the subsequent group argument, the actual alternating nonzero
theta-equivariant map \(\beta:\Lambda^2W\to W^{(2)}\) is an
isomorphism. The finite theta symmetrizer makes it a nonzero volume
form. Its Euler Lang pencil has the Fermat cubic wall and, away from
zero, no exceptional contraction-rank stratum. The nonzero Lang
incidence is an affine-line torsor over the punctured irreducible
Fermat cone, so is irreducible. A full order-two wall radical and
transitivity on the three nonzero vectors give \(\operatorname{GL}_2
(\mathbf F_2)\). This last paragraph is a proposed assembly to be
checked against the parent's final complete proof. A smooth member
of the wall is unnecessary if the already proved whole-coefficient
Euler model is used: the nonempty smooth open has the same generic
image, and the wall inertia belongs to that generic image.
