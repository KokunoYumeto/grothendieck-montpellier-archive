\subsection{Odd-cover polar components and a cyclic-block criterion}

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two. Let
\(\eta:A\to B\) be an etale isogeny of positive odd degree \(n\),
where \((B,\Theta_B)\) is a nonordinary principally polarized
abelian surface. Put \(N_B=\mathcal O_B(3\Theta_B)\),
\(L=\eta^*N_B\), \(W=H^0(A,L)\), \(N=9n\), and \(r=N-1\).
The relative Lang local system \(\mathcal V\) has rank \(r\)
on the smooth, sharp open \(U\subset\mathbf P(W)\).
Write \(K\) for the canonical proper submodule given by the
full-radical group theorem, and \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal V\)
for the corresponding local system.

\begin{proposition}[The main polar component for an arbitrary odd cover]
\label{prop:arbitrary-odd-cover-polar-main-orbit}
\status{Candidate; complete direct proof supplied for independent audit.}
The geometric image of \(\mathcal V\) has no invariant covector.
Its primitive reduced Lang polar is irreducible, and the unique
Lang component meeting maximal dual-contraction rank is the orbit
\(V\setminus K\), of degree \(2^r-2^{\dim K}\).
Every point of \(\mathcal K\setminus\{0\}\) over \(U\),
including over a special coefficient slice, has strictly smaller
dual-contraction rank.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The extension of the exact-covector proof requires no principal
polarization on \(A\). The etale Frobenius square gives
\[
 F_{A,*}L\simeq\eta^{(2)*}F_{B,*}N_B.
\]
The right-hand bundle is globally generated by the pulled-back
sections, since the cubic principal bundle has the previously
proved Frobenius-fibre generation. Finite Frobenius duality makes
the Cartier pairing perfect. These are exactly the common-kernel
and descent hypotheses in
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-covectors-any-nil-length} and the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonordinary-ppav-all-covectors}.
Thus each invariant covector gives a linear endomorphism \(D\)
of \(W\) satisfying \(\beta(s,Ds)=D(s)^{[2]}\), and
polarization and fibrewise perfection descend it to an endomorphism
of \(F_{A,*}L\).

The polarization is
\(\lambda_L=\widehat\eta\lambda_{N_B}\eta\).
Both isogenies have odd degree, and
\(\lambda_{N_B}=3\lambda_{\Theta_B}\); consequently
\(\ker\lambda_L\) has odd order and intersects \(\ker F_A\)
trivially. The same Oda simplicity criterion used in the cited
exact-covector proof yields \(\operatorname{End}(F_{A,*}L)=k\).
The scalar lift must be zero because the Cartier pairing is
alternating on a nonordinary surface. This proves the covector
assertion, with no restriction that \(n\) be a square.

Corollary~\ref{cor:ppav-odd-cover-maximal-dual-Cartier} supplies
generic dual rank \(N-1\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:DE-common-Cartier-incidence} supplies the
actual bijective Euler open. The abstract
Theorem~\ref{thm:DE-Lang-polar-irreducible} therefore applies
literally, with the coefficient-square-root pencil specified there.
The nonempty common wall of
Proposition~\ref{prop:odd-cover-common-rank-wall-nonempty}
shows that the polar meets the original maximal-dual-rank open,
including its uncancelled Pfaffian factor condition. Over that open
the soluble Lang equations are an affine-line torsor over the
irreducible polar; their Jacobian in the Lang variable is \(-I\).
Thus they give the unique dominant component meeting that open,
exactly as in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:DE-odd-polar-main-component}.

The pole-vector proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:DE-Lang-main-orbit}
uses only this component, the actual Euler model and the common
full-radical wall. All three have just been supplied for arbitrary
odd \(n\). More explicitly, a nonregular wall Lang vector \(y\)
can be scaled to an integral \(z=ay\) with nonzero reduction;
the relation \(M_s^tz^{[2]}=az\) puts
\(\bar z^{[2]}\) on the maximal-dual-rank left-kernel line of
the wall. Hence \(y\) belongs to the main component. The full
radical and exact group structure identify that component with
\(V\setminus K\), giving its degree.

Finally, the nonzero \(\mathcal K\)-cover is a union of
components of the finite etale Lang cover over \(U\). Each is
contained generically in the closed lower-dual-rank locus by the
preceding orbit identification. That closed condition therefore
holds on the entire component. It persists at every point of
\(U\), not just at the generic point. No whole-fibre comparison
across a rank drop is used in this last statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The actual cyclic character-cycle modules]
\label{prop:cyclic-Lang-character-cycle-decomposition}
\status{Candidate; complete direct proof supplied for independent audit.}
Suppose \(D=\ker\eta\simeq\mathbf Z/\ell\mathbf Z\), with
\(\ell\) an odd prime, and put \(f=\operatorname{ord}_\ell(2)\).
On a connected common full-rank open \(S\) of the central
coefficient slice \(\mathbf P(H^0(B,N_B))\), one has
\[
 \mathcal V|_S=\mathcal V_0\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{(\ell-1)/f}
 \mathcal V_j,\qquad
 \dim_{\mathbf F_2}\mathcal V_0=8,\qquad
 \dim_{\mathbf F_{2^f}}\mathcal V_j=9.
 \tag{CLB1}
\]
The slice monodromy is \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\)-linear on each
\(\mathcal V_j\), and
\[
                 \mathcal K|_S=\bigoplus_j\mathcal K_j,
                 \qquad \mathcal K_j\subseteq\mathcal V_j
 \tag{CLB2}
\]
with \(\mathcal K_j\) an \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\)-sub-local system.
In particular a single nine-dimensional coherent character space
is not by itself an \(\mathbf F_2\)-Lang local system.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The central common-rank open and its actual deck action are those
of the deck-slice theorem. The group algebra is
\[
 \mathbf F_2[D]\simeq\mathbf F_2\times
       \prod_{j=1}^{(\ell-1)/f}\mathbf F_{2^f}.
\]
Indeed the nontrivial \(\ell\)-th roots have Frobenius orbits
of length \(f\). Each individual coherent character space has
dimension nine, and Frobenius cycles through its orbit; its
bijective Lang realization therefore has \(\mathbf F_2\)-rank
\(9f\). The group-algebra factor acts faithfully and makes
that space nine-dimensional over \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\).
Deck transformations commute with the slice local-system monodromy,
giving (CLB1) with the asserted scalar structure.

We also justify that the idempotents preserve \(\mathcal K\).
The canonical \(D\)-linearization of \(L=\eta^*N_B\) makes
each deck translation an automorphism of the full coefficient
family over its projective action on \(U\). It transports the
unique maximal proper geometric sub-local system to itself, by
its uniqueness. On the central fixed slice this is the ordinary
deck action on the fibre, so \(D\) preserves \(\mathcal K|_S\).
The deck-slice bound kills its trivial factor. The remaining
group-algebra idempotents yield precisely (CLB2).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A conditional cyclic-block test]
\label{thm:cyclic-Lang-block-test}
\status{Candidate; both block hypotheses remain explicit.}
In the cyclic situation, assume for each \(j\) that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the slice monodromy on \(\mathcal V_j\) is irreducible
over \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\);
\item at some geometric point of \(S\), the actual Lang fibre
\((\mathcal V_j)_s\) contains a vector \(y\) for which
\(B(y^{[2]})\) has rank \(N-1\).
\end{enumerate}
Then \(K=0\) and the full relative geometric image on
\(|L|\) is \(\operatorname{GL}_{9\ell-1}(\mathbf F_2)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Irreducibility makes each \(\mathcal K_j\) either zero or
all of \(\mathcal V_j\). In the latter case its fibre would
contain the vector in the second hypothesis, contradicting the
everywhere lower-dual-rank assertion of the first proposition.
Thus every \(\mathcal K_j\) vanishes, and (CLB2) gives
\(K=0\). Apply the full-radical image theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The two unresolved block hypotheses]
\label{rem:cyclic-Lang-block-test-scope}
\status{Candidate scope; no new full-image assertion.}
The nine-by-nine Moore determinant being nonzero, and not divisible
by the central Fermat cubic, proves neither hypothesis of the
theorem. In particular it does not prove reducedness of a twisted
cycle wall, full inertia over \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\), or
irreducibility of the cycle local system. Nor does a generic
maximal-rank vector in the coherent span automatically give a
maximal-rank vector among its finite Lang points. The hypotheses
are retained separately for that reason. No numerical experiment
is used or claimed.
\end{remark}
