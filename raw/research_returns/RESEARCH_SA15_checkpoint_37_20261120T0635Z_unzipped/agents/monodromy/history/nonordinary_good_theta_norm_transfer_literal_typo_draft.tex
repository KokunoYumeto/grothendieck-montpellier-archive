\subsection{Norm transfer through an odd isogeny with nodal theta image}
\label{subsec:good-theta-norm-transfer-candidate}
\status{Candidate; full proof submitted for independent audit.}

Work first over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two.
Let $\phi:(B,\Theta_B)\to(A,\Theta_A)$ be an etale polarized
isogeny of odd multiplier $n>1$ and degree $k=n^2$. Assume that
$B$ is nonordinary and indecomposable, and that the image of its
smooth principal theta curve is integral and nodal. Put
$K=\ker\phi$ and $\mathcal H=(K\setminus0)/\pm$.
Each type $h\in\mathcal H$ then has two distinct transverse
theta identifications, and there are no theta triple identifications.
Let $m=3e$ be odd. Suppose the relative system of $|m\Theta_B|$
has full geometric image $\GL_r(\mathbf F_2)$, where $r=m^2-1$.

\begin{lemma}[The full node product for a good theta kernel]
\label{lem:good-theta-uniform-node-product-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
The generic norm of a smooth sharp curve in $|m\Theta_B|$ is nodal
and has $\delta=(k-1)m^2$ additional nodes. Their permutation
image is
\[
                       \prod_{h\in\mathcal H}S_{2m^2}.
\]
The source norm family lies in the target's maximal relative-rank
open, of rank $R=km^2-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $M=m^2-1$, the dimension of the source coefficient space.
For fixed $0\ne h\in K$, an oriented node is a point $P$ with
$s(P)=s(P+h)=0$. Two values are independent, so this incidence
is a projective-$(M-2)$ bundle over $B$ and is irreducible.
It has degree $(m\Theta_B)^2=2m^2$ over the transverse open.

For two distinct oriented points $P,Q$, the four values at
$P,P+h,Q,Q+h$ are independent except at finitely many pairs,
by Lemma~\ref{lem:nonproduct-three-theta-four-values-candidate}.
For $m>3$, multiply a separating section of $3\Theta_B$ by a
section of the globally generated $(m-3)\Theta_B$ nonzero at
the four specified points. Thus the same rank bound holds for
$m\Theta_B$. On the dense rank-four open the ordered-two-point
incidence is an irreducible projective-$(M-4)$ bundle over a
four-dimensional base, of total dimension $M$.
At four distinct exceptional points the rank is at least three,
so the finite exceptional base contributes dimension at most
$M-3$. If $Q=P+h$ or $P-h$, there are three distinct points,+whose three values are independent; this contributes at most
$2+(M-3)=M-1$. Consequently the finite etale ordered-pair cover
over the generic coefficient open has just the main component.
The node action in each type is two-transitive.

Here the generic norm is indeed nodal. Three distinct points in
one $K$-orbit impose three independent values over a two-dimensional
point parameter, a proper coefficient condition. For two points
$P,P+h$, the six surface first jets have rank six except at
finitely many $P$: the exceptional conditions in the proved theta
jet theorem are $3P+h,3P+2h\in\Theta_B$, up to fixed translations.
The two theta translates are distinct, and $[3]$ is finite etale.
Multiplying by a local-unit section preserves this jet rank for
$m>3$. On this open, after imposing the two values, tangency is
one nonzero determinant condition on four free gradient entries.
The resulting incidence is proper; the finitely many exceptions
are already excluded generically by their two value conditions.
Bertini supplies a smooth source, and the full-image hypothesis
supplies its dense sharp open. Thus all additional identifications
are distinct ordinary nodes. There are $2m^2$ in each of
$(k-1)/2$ types, giving $\delta$.

It remains to obtain single transpositions without assuming a
simultaneous higher-jet separation theorem. Use a union of $m$
theta translates $\Theta_B+a_i$ with $\sum_i a_i=0$.
For a fixed pair $i<j$, the two oriented cross intersections in
node type $h$ have branch supports
\[
                 a_j-a_i\in\Delta+h,\quad\Delta-h,
             \qquad\Delta=[2]\Theta_B\text{ reduced}.
\]
The two-point theta difference cover and its characteristic-two
inertia were proved in
Lemma~\ref{lem:theta-difference-cover-char2-candidate}.
The curve $\Delta$ has class $2\Theta_B$ and has no nonzero
odd torsion translation stabilizer. Hence all the shifted
supports for this pair are distinct. The branch conditions for
different pairs are distinct too: their difference variables
are independent, using the determinant-three isogeny when
$m=3$, or an unused translating component when $m\ge5$.

We specify that a generic chosen branch point can avoid every
unwanted collision, not only other tangent pairs. For $m=3$
write $a_1=a$, $a_2=a+d$, $a_3=-2a-d$, and choose $d$ generically
on the specified shifted $\Delta$. Each individual theta norm
is nodal by hypothesis. Incidence of its finitely many self-nodes
with another component requires $d$ to belong to a finite union
of translates of $\Theta_B$ after pulling back by $\phi$.
The irreducible curve $\Delta+h$, of class $2\Theta_B$, cannot
be contained in any such theta translate. Thus these incidences
are avoidable, including coincidence of the chosen contact with
a source node or a norm self-node.
For fixed allowable $d$, the first two component norms have
finite intersection. Their intersection points, including the
chosen contact, avoid the third component for general $a$:
each prohibited membership is a theta-norm curve condition on
$3\phi(a)$, and $[3]\phi$ is an etale isogeny.
The same argument handles self-nodes of the third component.
The other two pair tangencies are avoided by their independently
varying difference variables. For $m\ge5$, at least three
remaining translating components are available after the contact
pair is fixed; their independent choices, with the last absorbing
the sum, give the same proper exclusions for every pair and triple.
There are finitely many kernel shifts and component choices.

At such a generic boundary point precisely one two-point cluster
of this type is ramified, while every other additional node cover
is etale. The difference-cover inertia exchanges its two points
and fixes every other node of every type. This is a permutation
in the generic coefficient-space image. Indeed the additional
node covers are defined on the normal open where their intersection
schemes are finite and etale, allowing nodal source curves; a
punctured neighbourhood of the displayed boundary maps to that
open. Restriction to its dense smooth sharp source open surjects
on the geometric fundamental group. No cohomological rank
specialization is involved in this node-cover argument.

A two-transitive subgroup containing a transposition is the full
symmetric group: conjugates give all pair transpositions.
Moreover the chosen transposition is supported in its type alone;
conjugating by the surjective projection gives the whole symmetric
factor supported there. Repeating for all types proves the product.
Finally normalization contributes the source relative rank $r$,
and its graph contributes $\delta$, so the target relative rank
is $r+\delta=R$, the maximal odd-degree rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All type hearts and the actual source block]
\label{prop:good-theta-uniform-gluing-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
On the norm family write
\[
 0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow0,
 \quad\dim U=r,\quad
 T=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathcal H}T_h,\quad\dim T_h=2m^2.
\]
Its image contains an actual natural $\GL_r$ fixing a complement,
and every translation in $\Hom(U,T_h^0)$, where $T_h^0$ is the
coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $N=\prod_hS_{2m^2}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:RD-A4-three-power-groups}
applies with $2m^2=2r+2$ and any number of factors: $N$ and
$\GL_r$ have no common nontrivial quotient. Their joint image
is their product. Its translation kernel is $U^*\otimes T'$
for an $N$-stable $T'\subset T$. The same lemma gives
$H^1(\GL_r,U^*)=0$; after quotienting by $T'$, this splits the
$\GL_r$ factor. The commuting $N$ factor has no residual column,
since $(U^*)^{\GL_r}=0$. Thus
\[
                         V/T'=(T/T')\oplus U.
\]
Before quotienting, all remaining columns lie in the actual
translation kernel and can be removed by its elements. This also
gives the asserted actual natural block.

We recall precisely the rank-drop comparison needed to force
the hearts. Choose $m$ general theta translates with sum zero.
Their norms are nodal and mutually transverse, have distinct
self-nodes and no triples, by the preceding incidence arguments.
Impose these finitely many open conditions also after all shifts
$r=(r_i)\in K^m$ with $\sum r_i=0$. Each source union remains
in the same complete system $|m\Theta_B|$ and has the same target.
The common graph and the partial-normalization kernels are exactly
those of Lemma~\ref{lem:RD-A4-many-theta-graph}, which was stated
and proved for arbitrary odd $K$, not only three-torsion.

Let $\mathcal B$ be the normal source coefficient open where all
additional norm identifications are distinct transverse pairs of
smooth source points. Internal source nodes and their selected-pair
tangencies are allowed. The normalization sequence gives an exact
sequence of coherent Frobenius bundles with graph-unit kernel.
The odd isogeny identifies the ambient coherent maps. The subspace
$T'$ extends as a finite etale subgroup of the graph system, since
the dense maximal-rank open surjects on $\pi_1(\mathcal B)$.
The projection of the full Lang schemes, after quotienting by $T'$,
is a finite etale torsor under $T/T'$: locally its lift equations
are $z_i^2-z_i=b_i$. Its generic splitting extends uniquely over
the full source Lang scheme, which is normal and has every
component dominant over $\mathcal B$. The closure of a section
in a finite etale torsor is finite birational and hence is that
normal component. Addition and descent compatibility follow by
density. This is the complete extension argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:nonordinary-three-torsion-gluing-hearts-candidate};
it uses no equality of full unit ranks at the nodal source.

Now use inertia for a selected, unshifted component-pair
intersection. Its branch support is $\Delta$, distinct from all
the additional shifted supports $\Delta\pm h$. At its generic
point it fixes every additional node and exchanges just the two
selected gluing labels. Constant component torsors are simultaneously
trivial over the strict henselian local branch neighbourhood,
so the image on cohomology is exactly the graph line
$q_{ij}=e_{ij,0}+e_{ij,1}$. This remains valid for the purely
inseparable residue extension of the quadratic theta collision.
In the source doubled complete graph,
$\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}=0$ at every vertex. The extended splitting
therefore places the corresponding target sum in $T'$.
Modulo the full vertex coboundary this is the unselected-edge
star at that vertex. In each type it has
\[
                 4(m-1)\text{ ones and }
                 2m^2-4(m-1)\text{ zeros},
\]
both positive. A transposition in one factor produces a pair
vector of that type alone; conjugates span its whole heart.
Thus $T_h^0\subset T'$ for every $h$, proving all the translations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The good-theta odd norm step]
\label{thm:good-theta-odd-norm-step-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
Under the stated hypotheses the geometric relative image of the
complete target system $|mn\Theta_A|$ is
$\GL_{km^2-1}(\mathbf F_2)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The norm line bundle has numerical class $mn\Theta_A$.
Correct its fixed degree-zero discrepancy by one fixed target
translation, using surjectivity of the polarization homomorphism.
This identifies the entire family with the actual complete system.

Let $S$ be a nonzero proper global invariant subspace. On the
connected dense maximal-rank source open its projection to the
simple source module $U$ is zero or surjective, uniformly.
Use the clopen closure of
Lemma~\ref{lem:normal-base-clopen-Lang-closure-candidate} over the
target's normal coefficient space. The full common graph injects
in relative cohomology, since ambient cohomology injects into each
theta normalization and misses the graph.
In the zero case the closure retains its full dimension inside
every finite etale partial-normalization kernel $T_r$ and is
invariant under the node product. The second assertion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:RD-A4-many-theta-graph} makes it zero, a contradiction.
In the surjective case the gluing translations put every heart
in $S$. Their specializations span the total-edge-sum-zero graph
hyperplane. Its intersection with each $T_r$ has dimension
$\delta-1$; clopen finite-etale intersection transports that
dimension back to the generic norm family. Hence
\[
                    \dim S\ge\delta-1+r=R-1.
\]
A proper $S$ would be a hyperplane and give a fixed quotient
covector over $\mathbf F_2$, contrary to the exact odd-degree
nonordinary covector theorem. The representation is irreducible.

Let $J$ be the normal subgroup generated by transvections.
Its nonzero socle is invariant under the full irreducible image,
so its representation is semisimple. The actual natural
$\GL_r$ block, of multiplicity one, lies in one simple summand.
The rank-one gluing maps place every heart in the same summand.
Writing $t=(k-1)/2$, that summand has dimension at least
\[
                r+(2m^2-1)t=R-t>R/2.
\]
Its conjugates cannot be disjoint other summands. It is therefore
invariant under the full image and equals $V$.
The same transvection classification and actual-block no-form
argument as in Theorem~\ref{thm:RD-A4-three-step} gives
$\GL_R(\mathbf F_2)$: a preserved alternating form would have
the whole actual natural block in its radical.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All supersingular principal degrees generated by three and five]
\label{cor:f0-three-five-degree-groups-candidate}
\textup{[Candidate.]}
Let $(A,M)$ be a principally polarized surface over a perfect field
of characteristic two with $f(A)=0$, and put $L=M^3$.
For every $i,j\ge0$ and $d=3^i5^j$, the geometric and arithmetic
relative images for the complete system $|L^d|$ equal
\[
                           \GL_{9d^2-1}(\mathbf F_2).
\]
For every sum $d=3^i5^j+3^{i'}5^{j'}$, they equal
\[
 \Hom(\mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_2^{9d^2-4})
                          \rtimes\GL_{9d^2-4}(\mathbf F_2).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Work first geometrically. A decomposable two-rank-zero principal
surface is the already proved supersingular product case, where
all degrees are known. Otherwise it is a genus-two Jacobian.
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-f0-five-self-isogeny-nodal-candidate}
supplies a good theta five-self-isogeny on this same surface.
The preceding theorem therefore transfers any known odd degree
$e$ to degree $5e$. Starting from the already proved degrees
$3^i$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:RD-A4-powers-three}, induction on $j$
gives all displayed odd degrees. The even assertions follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonordinary-even-addition-candidate}.
All choices may be made over a finite algebraic extension of the
field of definition. The geometric conclusions persist after
algebraically closed extension. In odd degree the arithmetic
group is between the full geometric general linear group and
that same group. In even degree the one invariant covector is
defined over every perfect base field by the exact covector
theorem, so the same sandwich gives the displayed full stabilizer.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Precise scope of the new transfer]
\label{rem:good-theta-norm-transfer-scope-candidate}
The four-value argument replaces the three-torsion eigenmap input;
the isolated transpositions come from the explicit theta boundary,
not from an unproved simultaneous higher-jet claim. The graph and
finite-Lang arguments used here were already proved for arbitrary
odd kernel groups. The new unconditional extension concerns
two-rank-zero surfaces. A good degree-five theta source for every
fixed two-rank-one nonproduct surface is not asserted. No assertion
about all odd integers or all even integers follows from the stated
semigroup of degrees. No new finite computation is used for this
symbolic norm-step assembly.
\end{remark}
