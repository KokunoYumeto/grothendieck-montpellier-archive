\subsection{The intrinsic lifted section in the twisted pencil}

\begin{lemma}[A compulsory two-dimensional boundary domain]
\label{lem:DE-A3-twisted-pencil-lift}
\status{Proved; complete independent root and companion audits passed.}
Keep the sharp-source hypotheses of the residual-boundary kernel lemma.
Thus $D\subset T$ is a projective Cartier divisor, possibly nonreduced,
$\mathcal L=LP$, $R=\omega_D A^{-1}$, $\deg R=b>0$, and
$\chi(D,AP)=0$. In the primitive case write the actual extension as
\[
0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow0,
\qquad [G]=\gamma\in H^1(T,\mathcal O_T),
\]
using the fixed $P^2\simeq\mathcal O_T$ identification. A nonzero
scalar change of the extension coordinates does not affect any kernel
below. The quotient $G\to i_*R$ in (DE-RB1) defines a nonzero section
\[
\sigma_G\in H^0(D,RP),\qquad
                   \gamma|_D\cup\sigma_G=0.
\tag{DE-TL1}
\]
Put $m=h^0(D,AP)=h^0(D,RP)$. Then $m\ge1$ and
\[
\begin{split}
h^0(D,A\otimes G|_D)
 &=m+\dim\ker\bigl(\gamma|_D\cup-:
                    H^0(D,AP)\longrightarrow H^1(D,AP)\bigr)\\
 &\ge m+1\ge2.
\end{split}
\tag{DE-TL2}
\]
If $m=1$, the displayed dimension is exactly two. The same argument
applies to the split actual-local-index case with $\gamma=0$, when
the dimension is $2m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Restrict the actual extension to $D$; it remains exact because its
quotient is locally free. The map $G|_D\to R$ is surjective, since
it is the restriction of the sheaf quotient in (DE-RB1). Its restriction
to the subline $P|_D$ is $\sigma_G$, using $P^{-1}=P$.
If this restriction were zero, the quotient map would factor through
the other line $P|_D$. The resulting surjection $P|_D\to R$ between
invertible sheaves would be an isomorphism, by the local rank-one
criterion. But $P|_D$ has degree zero and $R$ has degree $b>0$.
This contradiction proves $\sigma_G\ne0$, without an assumption that
$D$ is integral or reduced.

Apply $\operatorname{Hom}_D(-,R)$ to the restricted extension.
The obstruction to extending a map $P|_D\to R$ across $G|_D$ is
the Yoneda product with its extension class, namely cup product
with $\gamma|_D$. The actual quotient $G|_D\to R$ extends
$\sigma_G$, so this obstruction vanishes, proving (DE-TL1).

Serre duality on the projective Gorenstein curve $D$, together with
$P^2=\mathcal O_T$ and $R=\omega_D A^{-1}$, identifies
\[
H^1(D,AP)^*=H^0(D,RP),\qquad
H^0(D,AP)^*=H^1(D,RP).
\]
Since $\chi(AP)=0$, both $H^0(AP)$ and $H^0(RP)$ have dimension $m$.
The dual of cup product by $\gamma|_D$ from $H^0(AP)$ to $H^1(AP)$
is cup product by the same class from $H^0(RP)$ to $H^1(RP)$.
Its kernel contains the nonzero $\sigma_G$, so its rank is at most
$m-1$. The original cup map therefore has nullity at least one.
Finally the initial long cohomology sequence of
\[
0\longrightarrow AP\longrightarrow A\otimes G|_D
                    \longrightarrow AP\longrightarrow0
\]
has connecting map exactly this cup product. It gives (DE-TL2).
For $m=1$ the cup map is zero and the dimension is two; for the
split extension it is zero in every dimension. No further
cohomology vanishing has been used.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The section and the unresolved map]
\label{rem:DE-A3-twisted-pencil-lift-scope}
\status{Proved scope; no restricted-boundary lower bound asserted.}
The section $\sigma_G$ is defined intrinsically from the actual
quotient $G\to i_*R$. The proof does not require identifying it with
the earlier norm section $\sigma\in H^0(D,RP)$; if $m=1$, the two
nonzero sections are necessarily scalar multiples. In particular
the expectation that a one-dimensional twisted pencil would make
$H^0(D,A\otimes G|_D)$ one-dimensional is false: its dimension is
then exactly two. This compulsory domain still maps to
$H^1(T,\operatorname{Sym}^2G)$ in (DE-SK2). Its kernel, its
interaction with the residual products, and the subsequent restriction
to $\Delta_s$ are not determined here. No new generic-rank result,
variation-image lower bound, or main-gate closure is asserted.
\end{remark}
