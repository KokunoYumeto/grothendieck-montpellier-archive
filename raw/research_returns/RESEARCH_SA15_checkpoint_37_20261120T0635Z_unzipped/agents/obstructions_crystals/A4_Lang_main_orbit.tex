\subsection*{The irreducible polar component is the large monodromy orbit}

Use the notation of the reduced-polar theorem, with
\(N=9d^2\), \(r=N-1\), and \(d\) odd. Work geometrically.
The exact full-radical group proposition writes the generic relative
image as the full inverse image of a subgroup of
\(\operatorname{GL}(K)\) in the stabilizer of its unique maximal
proper submodule \(K\). The unique large orbit is \(V\setminus K\).

\begin{lemma}[Pole vectors lie in the maximal-dual-rank component]
\label{lem:DE-Lang-main-orbit}
\status{Proved; full independent proof audits passed.}
The unique irreducible component of the nonzero Lang incidence meeting
\(\operatorname{rank}B(y^{[2]})=N-1\), supplied by
Corollary~\ref{cor:DE-odd-polar-main-component}, corresponds to the
orbit \(V\setminus K\). Its degree over the section space is therefore
\[
                             2^r-2^{\dim K}.
\]
Every other component, if present, corresponds to a nonzero orbit
inside \(K\) and lies over the lower dual-rank strata. No oddness of
the displayed degree is inferred.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the geometric generic point of a component of the nonempty
common wall \(H_{\max}\), and a strictly henselian discrete valuation
ring \(R\) at that divisor. A coefficient chart trivializes the Euler
bundle as a subbundle of the constant \(W^*\)-bundle. Its regular
Frobenius matrix is the actual restriction of \(M_s^t\).
On the special fibre, \(M_{s_0}\) has rank \(N-1\), its left kernel
is \(k\lambda_0\), and \(B(\lambda_0)\) has rank \(N-1\).
The relative Frobenius has stable rank \(r-1\), while its generic
rank is \(r\), by the complete common-wall theorem.

The regular Lang vectors form the hyperplane \(H\subset V\) of
dimension \(r-1\). Indeed the special Lang scheme has exactly
\(2^{r-1}\) geometric points, every one lifts uniquely in its etale
henselian neighbourhood, and every integral Lang vector reduces
to one of them. This is the same regular unit subspace used in the
local full-inertia proof. After a valuation-field extension, the
same uniqueness shows that no additional generic vector can become
integral. Thus each \(y\in V\setminus H\) has a pole in the constant
ambient coordinates.

Choose a coordinate of smallest valuation and let \(a\) be its
inverse. Then \(v(a)>0\), \(z=ay\) is integral, and its reduction
\(\bar z\) is nonzero. Multiplying the actual Lang equation by
\(a^2\) gives
\[
                       M_s^t z^{[2]}=a z.
\]
Reducing in the valuation residue field yields
\(M_{s_0}^t\bar z^{[2]}=0\). Consequently
\(\bar z^{[2]}\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\lambda_0\).
It follows that \(B(\bar z^{[2]})\) has rank \(N-1\). A corresponding
minor of \(B(z^{[2]})\) is a unit, so
\[
                       \operatorname{rank}B(y^{[2]})=N-1
\]
over the generic valuation field as well. Scaling has no effect on
this rank. In particular every vector outside the regular hyperplane
at this wall belongs to the maximal-dual-rank open of the Lang cover.
No assertion about transporting an entire stable fibre across the
rank drop has been used.

The wall inertia contains the full radical \(U_\ell\) with
\(H=\ker\ell\). Its fixed space contains \(K\), since \(K\)
is fixed by the normal closure of that full radical. Hence
\(V\setminus H\subset V\setminus K\). This is nonempty, and the
exact group structure makes \(V\setminus K\) a single orbit.
The irreducible main Lang component meets that orbit by the preceding
valuation argument. Components of the finite generic Lang cover
are the monodromy orbits, so it is exactly that large orbit.
The remaining assertions follow from the same classification and
from uniqueness of the component meeting maximal dual rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The remaining parity and exceptional-component questions]
\label{rem:DE-Lang-main-orbit-scope}
\status{Proved; full independent proof audits passed.}
An independent proof that this main component has odd degree would
force \(K=0\), since its exact degree is
\(2^r-2^{\dim K}\). Such oddness is not proved here. In particular,
a projective compactification by an apparent rank-two solution bundle
must handle the original Pfaffian-factor and rank-drop divisors;
their contributions cannot be discarded. This lemma identifies the
main orbit but does not remove lower-rank components or supply a
new full-monodromy degree. It uses only the actual local Euler model
and the already proved wall and group structure. No finite computation
or new external-source assertion is involved.
\end{remark}
