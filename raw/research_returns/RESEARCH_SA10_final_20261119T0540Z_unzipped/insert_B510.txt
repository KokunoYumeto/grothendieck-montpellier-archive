### B5.10. An explicit all-index local filling criterion

**Status: Known criterion, derived here in the required form.** The exact stronger condition $H_\varphi=1$ in the hypercomplete infinity-topos has an explicit test using the original $U_a$, finite limits, coproducts and a sequential colimit. It does not require one to guess boundary objects in the arbitrary category $A$.

Let $N=B_\varphi$ be the simplicial replacement from B5.6; equivalently it is the nerve of the internal element category, whose objects are $\coprod_aU_a$ and whose arrow object is $\coprod_{a\to b}U_a$. For a finite simplicial set $K$, let $[K,N]$ be the object of simplicial maps, expressed as a finite limit using the nondegenerate simplices of $K$. Put

$$R_n=\operatorname*{colim}_{r\ge0}[\operatorname{Sd}^{r}\Delta^n,N],$$

with the transition maps induced by the standard last-vertex subdivision maps. Here colimits and finite limits are in $\mathcal X$, and $\operatorname{Sd}$ is barycentric subdivision. Then the following are equivalent:

1. $H_\varphi\to1$ is an equivalence after hypercompletion.
2. The simplicial replacement $N\to1$ is a local weak equivalence.
3. For every $n\ge0$, the following explicit map is an epimorphism in $\mathcal X$:

$$\operatorname*{colim}_{r\ge0}[\operatorname{Sd}^{r}\Delta^n,N]
\longrightarrow
\operatorname*{colim}_{r\ge0}[\operatorname{Sd}^{r}\partial\Delta^n,N].\tag{B5.10.1}$$

For $n=0$ the right side is terminal. Since filtered colimits commute with finite limits in a Grothendieck topos, the right side is precisely $M_nR$. Thus (3) is an ordinary epimorphic matching-map scheme on the explicitly derived simplicial object $R$. It says that every finite boundary admits a filling locally after sufficiently many subdivisions. The required subdivision number may vary over the covering family; no assertion of algorithmic decidability is made.

**Proof.** Present $\mathcal X$ by a small site, and regard $N$ as a simplicial sheaf there. Apply $\operatorname{Ex}^{\infty}$ sectionwise to obtain a simplicial presheaf $F$. For every object of the site, $F$ is a Kan complex and $N\to F$ is a weak homotopy equivalence, by Kerodon Propositions 3.3.6.7 and 3.3.6.9 (Tags 00ZH and 00ZK). Therefore $N\to F$ is a local weak equivalence, and $F$ is locally fibrant. Dugger--Hollander--Isaksen Proposition 3.2, reformulated as their equation (3.4), says that $F\to1$ is a local weak equivalence if and only if every map $F_n\to[\partial\Delta^n,F]$ is a local epimorphism: its local fibration hypothesis is automatic here. Sheafification preserves finite limits and colimits. Sheafifying these maps gives precisely (B5.10.1), by the subdivision--extension adjunction and finite presentation of the boundaries. This proves (2) equivalent to (3), independently of the chosen site. The local model category presents the hypercompletion, so (1) is equivalent to (2). This last restriction matters: local weak equivalence gives an infinity-connected augmentation in a nonhypercomplete topos, not necessarily an equivalence there. $\square$

This supplies an effective mathematical description for every small $A$, in addition to the direct sufficient condition on the manuscript's $c_F$ in B5.1 and the sharper boundary schemes of B5.7. The weaker forest scheme fails by B5.2--B5.3. Omitting any dimension from the strict matching scheme fails by B5.8, in ordinary and symmetric indexing. Conditions for shape equivalence alone can be weaker than universal local descent; their precise criterion remains B5.6.2.

### Scope of the comparison result

The answer separates the literal ordinary comparisons, the universal local descent condition, and the Artin--Mazur target. All finite ordinary comparisons epic is exactly flatness and suffices even before hypercompletion; the forest subcollection fails. The boundary comparisons in ordinary and symmetric indexing give Verdier hypercovers, with every matching degree indispensable. For arbitrary indexing, (B5.10.1) is an explicit necessary-and-sufficient higher local descent criterion. For equality only of shapes, (B5.6.2) is the exact criterion, and original Artin--Mazur homotopy types require the locally connected and pro-homotopy conventions stated there. A universal minimal subset of the ordinary finite diagram shapes for every possible $A$ is not asserted or needed for these results.
