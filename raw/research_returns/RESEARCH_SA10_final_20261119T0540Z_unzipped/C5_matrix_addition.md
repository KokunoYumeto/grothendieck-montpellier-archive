## C5.08 — Proved and refuted: the complete margin matrix and the category digression

For the matrix below, **raw fibrancy** means a proper Hurewicz fibration, even when its underlying map happens to be PL or smooth. A **regular PL source** is a locally trivial PL bundle; a **regular smooth source** is a neat smooth submersion with the stated compatible horizontal collars. A transversality assertion refers to the manuscript's product-collar condition, not merely to equality of inverse-image subsets. These distinctions are necessary to evaluate R7.

The proposed boundary formulas in the positive rows are part of C5.05, not extra assumptions about the answer: horizontal faces are the inverse images of lower faces of the base, and vertical faces are exactly those inserting an intermediate label. Their union is the boundary of the arrow/flag space; only the vertical part is the intrinsic boundary of the source fibre. Empty fibres cause no exception. These arguments are local in every dimension, including zero, one, and two. The counterexamples in larger dimensions refute the unrestricted claims without purporting to be minimal-dimensional ones.

The general categorical paragraph /067–070 can also be settled for manifold object components, without assuming the category is thin. Let a topological category have Hausdorff object and arrow spaces, open manifold components of its object space, proper locally embedding target map \(b\), Hurewicz source map \(s\), and locally nowhere-dense target image of the strict arrows. Assume every invertible arrow is an identity. Then the induced relation on object components is a partial order, and every nonidentity arrow connects distinct components.

Here is a full proof which avoids the manuscript's dimension inequalities. The identity section is closed by Hausdorffness and open by local injectivity of \(b\): near an identity, both that section and a sufficiently small arrow neighbourhood lie in a target-injective chart, so the only arrow in that chart with a given nearby target is its identity. Hence strict arrows form a clopen subspace. Source lifting along a path cannot change from a strict arrow to an identity, and target continuity keeps its target in one component. It follows that the existence of an arrow from one component to another is independent of which source point in the first component is prescribed. This also proves transitivity by lifting the second arrow along a path joining the intermediate endpoints.

Suppose a strict arrow has source and target in the same connected \(n\)-manifold component \(D\). Restrict to strict arrows whose source and target lie in \(D\). Over a sufficiently small open coordinate ball \(U\subset D\), a contraction of \(U\) to a point and the Hurewicz lifting property give a continuous section \(\sigma:U\to R^{<}\) of \(s\). Starting from a constant arrow lift proves this for arbitrary parameter space \(U\); the lift stays strict and its target stays in \(D\). Shrink \(U\) around a point so that \(\sigma(U)\) is contained in one target-injective neighbourhood. Then \(b\sigma:U\to D\) is injective, because \(\sigma\) is a section and \(b\) is injective on that neighbourhood. Invariance of domain makes \(b\sigma(U)\) open in \(D\), contradicting the locally nowhere-dense strict target image. Thus no strict arrow has both endpoints in one component. This argument includes dimension zero directly: a nonempty image in a discrete component is open.

If there are arrows from \(D\) to \(E\) and back, path lifting aligns their middle endpoints. Write the resulting arrows as \(u:d\to e\) and \(v:e\to d'\), with \(d,d'\in D\). The composite \(vu\) must be an identity by the preceding paragraph, so \(d'=d\). The composite \(uv\), with endpoints in \(E\), must likewise be an identity. Thus \(u\) and \(v\) are inverse; the assumption on invertible arrows forces \(D=E\). This proves antisymmetry. If the category is an ordered space with totally ordered lower intervals, the extra componentwise monomorphism statement and incomparable-face disjointness follow exactly as in C5.01; no extra monomorphism axiom is needed.

The same categorical proof applies to finite-dimensional locally finite PL object spaces, even if they are polyhedra rather than manifolds. For an alleged strict arrow within a component of maximal dimension \(n\), use source path lifting to move its source into the interior of a maximal \(n\)-simplex; this is an open \(n\)-ball. A local source section embeds that ball into a target-injective strict arrow chart. The locally rare PL target image contains no \(n\)-simplex and has local dimension at most \(n-1\). For completeness this cannot contain an embedded \(n\)-ball: a compact sub-ball meets only finitely many target simplices; the closed preimages of these simplices cover it, so one has interior by the Baire theorem. That gives an injective map from an open \(n\)-ball into a Euclidean space of dimension at most \(n-1\), contradicting invariance of domain after inclusion into \(\mathbb R^n\). The remaining component-order argument is unchanged. The TOP counterexample C5.07 explains why arbitrary connected finite-dimensional components do not admit this argument.

**Doubtful and ill-typed readings.** The original /051, /062, /063, /067, /070, and /071 scans have been opened visually. In /063(d) the transcription flags the source letter as \(s\) or \(b\); the source reading is the one explicitly typed as \(p_1\) in the preceding cartesian square on /062 and used in /065–066. All nontrivial statements above use that reading. If one literally requires the target boundary embedding \(b\) to be product-collar transverse to itself, a nonempty strict arrow chart is impossible: its inverse image of that boundary is its whole domain, which cannot itself be a nonempty collared boundary subset of that domain. Under that alternative the strict relation is empty and the nonsingular object's boundary is empty, so the corresponding mixed assertions are vacuous. The uncertain indices in /067's displayed source are typed as \(s_{jk}:\Sigma_{jk}\to\Sigma_j\); every other reading with a different source label must be reindexed to this type or is not a composable map. The uncertainty in /051(d) between deducing and separately requiring manifold flag spaces does not affect the mixed counterexamples: their arrow spaces are themselves manifolds. Finally the dimension and connected-component wording on /070–071 is clear in the original; C5.07 does not depend on an uncertain reading.
