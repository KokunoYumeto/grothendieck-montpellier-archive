## 1. Δ is a reciprocity defect at the primes 2 and 3 (sketched; ranked first)

**The objects:**
- **The groups.** GT̂ is the profinite Grothendieck–Teichmüller group, and the absolute Galois group G_Q maps into it.
  Our LM7 runs work with GT̂₀, the part of GT̂ defined by the relations at the two orbifold points of the modular group
  B = C₂ ∐ C₃ = ⟨s, r | s² = r³ = 1⟩, without the pentagon relation:
  - s, of order 2, sits at the harmonic point j = 1728, whose CM is by Z[i];
  - r, of order 3, sits at the equianharmonic point j = 0, whose CM is by Z[ω].
- **The frames and the sign.** An element F of GT̂₀ conjugates s and r: F(s) = β_F s β_F⁻¹ and F(r) = A_F r A_F⁻¹.
  The two conjugators are its *frames* (E.65, where both are proved unique). From the frames, E.65 reads off a unit
  u(F) in (Z/12)^× = {1, 5, 7, 11}.
- **The values.**
  - Elements coming from G_Q have u = 1 or 11.
  - E.58 constructs an element, by relative pronilpotent lifting, with u = 5 or 7: the "extra CM coset".
- **The open question.** E.3 of LM7_PUSH asks whether the full GT̂, the group with the pentagon, reaches that
  extra coset. Δ is the parity that tells the two cosets apart: Δ = 0 on Galois, and Δ ≠ 0 on the element of E.58.
  The question is whether Δ = 0 on all of GT̂.

**What Fable proposes:**
- **Δ is a quadratic character of u.** Δ(F) = χ₁₂(u(F)), where χ₁₂ = χ₋₄·χ₋₃ is the quadratic character of
  conductor 12. That is the character of Q(√3), the real subfield of Q(i, ω), the field generated by the two CM
  points. Its values are χ₁₂(1) = χ₁₂(11) = +1 and χ₁₂(5) = χ₁₂(7) = −1, matching the two cosets.
- **Δ splits into a 2-bit and a 3-bit.** Write ε₂ for u mod 4 (0 if u ≡ 1, 1 if u ≡ 3) and ε₃ for u mod 3 (0 if
  u ≡ 1, 1 if u ≡ 2). Then Δ = ε₂ + ε₃ (mod 2):
  - u = 1 gives (0, 0) and u = 11 gives (1, 1), so the bits are equal;
  - u = 5 gives (0, 1) and u = 7 gives (1, 0), so the bits are unequal.
- **ε₂ belongs to the order-2 point and ε₃ to the order-3 point.** Fable reads Δ = 0 as a *product formula over the
  two places*: the 2-sign and the 3-sign of a frame agree.
  - Galois elements satisfy it because both signs come from one global cyclotomic character. That is a reciprocity law.
  - The question of E.3 becomes: **do the hexagon and pentagon relations force ε₂ = ε₃?**
- **The link to "ghosts".** An element with unequal bits passes each local test (each frame is fine at its own point)
  and fails the global product formula. That is the user's notion of a ghost: something that passes every test at
  one prime and fails globally.

**The numerology Fable checked by hand** (labelled "numerology to test, not yet an identification"):
- **The Hilbert symbols.** The Hilbert symbol of the two CM fields, (−1, −3), is +1 at 2, −1 at 3 and −1 at ∞, so the
  product is +1 as it must be. **[added, checked]** At 3: (−1, 3)₃ = (−1/3) = −1; at ∞ both entries are negative;
  the value at 2 follows from the product formula.
- **The quaternion algebras.**
  - (−1, −3) over Q is ramified at 3 and ∞. It is the endomorphism algebra of the supersingular elliptic curve in
    characteristic 3, which is exactly where the two CM points meet (1728 ≡ 0 mod 3, so j = 0 and j = 1728 coincide).
  - In characteristic 2 the corresponding algebra is (−1, −1), ramified at 2 and ∞.
- **What this hints.** ε₂ and ε₃ might be local symbols, at 2 and at 3, of the pair of frames.

**Where the note comes in (steps 3 and 5).**
- **The cocycle.** Alpöge uses Gauss sums of order #μ_K as coefficients of θ on the #μ_K-fold cover and speaks of
  "the reciprocity law of y(c·c′) vs. y(c)·y(c′)". The cocycle of such a metaplectic cover is a power reciprocity
  law (Kubota), and it is trivial on rational points. That is the same shape as "Δ = 0 on Galois".
- **The triangle groups.**
  - The (2,3,6)-triangle group of p. 2 is the quotient Ẑ[ω] ⋊ C₆ of our tripod group, on which GT̂₀ acts.
  - The (2,4,4) group gives Ẑ[i] ⋊ C₄ in the same way.
  - Step 2 works at inert primes of the CM curve.

## 2. The multiplicity in step 2 is the divided-power structure of a height-2 formal group (proved for formal groups; its use is proposed)

**What step 2 of the note says.** On a CM curve, the invariant derivation D satisfies D^p ≡ 0 at primes p inert in
the CM field. The values D^a θ(0)/Ω^a are divisible by p to the power a/p + a/p³ + a/p⁵ + … − O(log a). This is how
the proof gets "multiplicity" over Z: Stepanov's method needs an auxiliary function vanishing to high order, which is
hard over the integers ("differentiating whole numbers").

**Fable's theorem.**
- **The setting.** Let F be a one-dimensional formal group of height h over W(k), the Witt vectors of a perfect field
  of characteristic p. Let t be a parameter, D the invariant derivation and f any power series in W[[t]].
- **The bound.**

  v_p(D^a f(0)) ≥ v_p(a!) − a/(p^h − 1) − O(log a).

  By Legendre, v_p(a!) = (a − s_p(a))/(p − 1), about a/(p − 1). So the gain per derivative is
  1/(p − 1) − 1/(p^h − 1).
- **Height 2 gives exactly the note's exponent.** 1/(p − 1) − 1/(p² − 1) = p/(p² − 1) = 1/p + 1/p³ + 1/p⁵ + … .
  **[added, checked]** p/(p² − 1) = (1/p)·1/(1 − p⁻²).
- **Height 1 gives no gain.** That is why the prime must be inert:
  - at an inert prime, a CM elliptic curve has supersingular reduction, so its formal group has height 2;
  - at a split prime the reduction is ordinary, height 1.

**Fable's proof:**
- D = d/dλ, where λ is the logarithm of F. Writing f = Σ c_a λ^a gives D^a f(0) = a!·c_a.
- In Lubin–Tate normal form the logarithm is t + t^q/p + t^(q²)/p² + …, with q = p^h. Its inverse, the exponential,
  has coefficients e_n with v_p(e_n) ≥ −n/(p^h − 1) up to a bounded term.
- The series with v_p(c_a) ≥ −a/(p^h − 1) form a ring that contains W[[t]] composed with the exponential.
- So c_a satisfies the bound, and with a! the inequality follows.

**Why D^p ≡ 0.** In characteristic p the p-th power of the invariant derivation is the Hasse invariant times D. It
vanishes exactly at supersingular points: the p-power map on the Lie algebra is zero.

**What it suggests.**
- **Where the auxiliary function lives.** "Differentiating whole numbers" is done by a divided-power structure at
  supersingular points: Stepanov's auxiliary function lives on the *crystalline site* of the CM curve.
- **Higher height.** The gain 1/(p − 1) − 1/(p^h − 1) grows toward the full 1/(p − 1) as h grows. So CM abelian
  varieties whose reduction has a formal summand of large height should gain multiplicity faster than a chain of
  theta lifts.
- **A shorter proof.** That is a candidate route to the shorter proofs Alpöge says he expects.

  - his boxed "t_A^∨ est muni de la puiss. p-ième nulle", which is the vanishing p-power map above;
  - "comparer … avec ce que donne la dualité de Cartier".

## 3. The count qN = M^k + R is the k-th moment of "multiply by t, then Fourier transform" (proved as a toy; the link to cote 1 is proposed)

**What the note does (steps 3 and 5).** It counts weighted zeros of the "path quadric"
Q(x) = x₁x₂ + x₂x₃ + … + x_{k−1}x_k. It shows the count equals a main term M^k plus an error R of square-root size,
"by iterating Parseval k times".

**Fable's toy theorem.**
- **The setting.** Let q be a prime, t ≥ 0 a weight on Z/q, M = Σ_x t(x), and
  N_k = Σ over the zeros of Q of t(x₁)⋯t(x_k).
- **The identity.** q·N_k = M^k + R_k, with

  |R_k| ≤ (q − 1)·q^((k−1)/2)·‖t‖₂²·‖t‖_∞^(k−2).

**Fable's proof:**
- Detect Q(x) = 0 with characters: q·1[Q(x) = 0] = Σ_h e_q(h·Q(x)), with e_q(y) = e^(2πiy/q).
- **h = 0** gives Σ_x ∏ t(x_i) = M^k.
- **h ≠ 0.** The sum is a chain ⟨t, K_h D_t K_h ⋯ D_t K_h t⟩. Here K_h(x, y) = e_q(hxy) is the finite Fourier kernel,
  and D_t is multiplication by t.
- **The bound.** K_h/√q is unitary, so each kernel costs √q and each D_t costs ‖t‖_∞. That gives the bound for each
  h, then the sum over the q − 1 values of h.

**What Fable proposes for cote 1.**
- **What the cote 1 intake found** (SA23; Fable did not read those sheets itself). Grothendieck's sheets 79–80 and
  111–112 prove a Hilbert-space trace formula, from which Ξ(√w)/Ξ(0) = det(1 − wB) with B trace class. RH is
  equivalent to B ≥ 0.
- **The proposal.** B is the operator "Fourier after multiplication", and the sums over h above are its moments.
- **The positivity.** The note's "opposite signs, so no cancellation of inverse roots" is Landau's lemma for the
  series Σ N_k w^k. Landau's lemma says a series with non-negative coefficients is singular at the real point of its
  circle of convergence. It is the same positivity Deligne uses on even tensor powers in Weil I.

**[added]** With Alpöge's RH, B ≥ 0 now holds outright (SA23 intake).

**How to check:** read cote 1 against the Stepanov determinant of steps 1, 4 and 7 in the Lean chain.

## 5. Step 17 may close our open row on the pro-ℓ kernel at irregular primes (sketched)

**Our open row:**
- **A.44 (known).** For an odd *regular* prime ℓ, the kernel of the Galois action on the pro-ℓ fundamental group of
  the tripod P¹ − {0, 1, ∞} is exactly the Galois group of Ω_ℓ, the maximal pro-ℓ extension of Q(μ_ℓ) unramified
  outside ℓ (Sharifi 2002; Brown 2012).
- **A.45 (open).** The same question at ℓ = 2 and at *irregular* primes.

**Fable's sketch:**
- **Soulé characters.** The Galois action on the pro-ℓ tripod group is governed by the Soulé characters
  κ_m : G_Q → Z_ℓ(m), for odd m ≥ 3. They give the generators σ_(2k+1) of the Galois image.
- **Their image.** The image of κ_m has index equal to the order of H²(Z[1/ℓ], Z_ℓ(m)), which is the ℓ-part of
  K_(2m−2)(Z) (from memory).
- **What step 17 would give.** Alpöge's step 17 and footnote 6 say that a weakening of Vandiver's conjecture gives
  the vanishing of the K-groups K_(4k)(Z). For m = 2k + 1 these are exactly the K_(2m−2)(Z). If they vanish:
  - the Soulé characters are onto;
  - the generators σ_(2k+1) exist integrally for every ℓ in that range;
  - the kernel field would again be Ω_ℓ beyond the regular primes, which is where Sharifi's proof needed regularity.

**[added]** Two connections:
- Vandiver's conjecture is equivalent to K_(4k)(Z) = 0 for all k (Kurihara); this is row Q06 of the bridges ledger.
- The groups H¹(o_E[1/ℓ], Q_ℓ/Z_ℓ(−e)) of step 17 are the "Tate–Shafarevich-type" groups of cyclotomic subfields.
  In Birkbeck's reading, a growth bound on them is one of the three main inputs of the whole proof. So this
  suggestion sits on the core of the proof, not on a side road.

## 7. What GRH for Dirichlet L-functions gives our files directly (proposed)

- **Bach's theorem, now unconditional.** On GRH, (Z/N)^× is generated by the primes below 2 log² N, and with
  Alpöge's result this holds outright. In our GT̂₀ shadow computations it gives a certificate for the cyclotomic
  coordinate at every finite level.
- **Route 2 for A1 (cote 149, scans 35–36: "Artin's conjecture on average").** This needs GRH for Kummer fields,
  that is, for Hecke L-functions over cyclotomic fields. Alpöge's note announces that extended hypothesis as
  expected, not proved.

**[added]**
- Masullo's draft (940 pp., unrefereed) states GRH for Hecke L-functions of every number field, and that covers these
  Kummer fields.
- Hooley's 1967 proof of Artin's primitive-root conjecture assumes GRH for the Dedekind zetas of the Kummer fields
  Q(ζ_k, a^(1/k)). So if Masullo's proof holds, Hooley's theorem, and route 2 for A1 with it, would be unconditional.
- This is one place where the two proofs do different work.
