## Geometric proof

The norm-polynomial identity proves that the connected image of `sigma−1` is the full new Prym. A collision supplies a rational 2-primary Abel difference, so its image there has an exact order `m` dividing the actual group exponent `M`. The principal four-point divisor has positive and negative degree `2m`. Exactness of the order excludes a square when `m>1`, while simple poles exclude a square when `m=1`. Over the algebraically closed perfect field this gives a separable function. The distinction between exact order and a merely annihilating multiple is necessary and is correctly preserved.

The field generated by all deck translates has index `e` dividing `2m`. I rechecked the complete Castelnuovo–Severi induction, including its algebraic equality and its field degrees. The resulting genus bound excludes `e=1` because the original covering curve has genus `n+1` and `n>(2M−1)^2`. Thus `e` is a power of two greater than one.

The induced cyclic action on the intermediate curve is faithful because any kernel order divides both odd `n` and `e`. It is free because a nontrivial odd prime stabilizer would partition a degree-`e` fibre divisor into equal-multiplicity free orbits of that prime length. Riemann–Hurwitz then forces the intermediate curve and its quotient to have genus one. The different of the separable map from the covering curve to this genus-one curve consequently has degree `2n`.

For any zero of the descended function, its fibre has at most two points, each with ramification index `m/s`, where `s` is the downstairs zero order. This is a power of two. If `e>2`, every such index is at least two. In the local uniformizer calculation the leading even-power derivative vanishes, so the different exponent is at least the ramification index. The `n` distinct deck translates of the downstairs point would then contribute at least `ne>2n`, a contradiction. This proves `e=2` without an unproved tame-ramification assumption.

In degree two, if the two positive points had different images, each would be ramified. Their two disjoint free deck orbits would contribute at least `4n` to the different, again impossible. They therefore form one unramified fibre. The two negative points are then also unramified and must form a single fibre. Equivariance gives `x=sigma y` and `y=sigma x`, contradicting freeness of the odd-order action. This final argument works for composite odd `n` as well as primes; it uses neither a rank-one base hypothesis nor an exclusion of bielliptic curves.

## Arithmetic exponent measurement

For a prime degree `r` with `p` primitive modulo `r`, the ring `O=Z_p[zeta_r]` is the unramified discrete valuation ring of residue degree `r−1`. Its action on the rational Prym primary group follows from the vanishing of the cyclotomic deck sum. A finite module over this ring is a sum of cyclic `O/p^a` modules. Each nonzero summand would contribute `r−1` dimensions to the subgroup killed by `p`, whereas the geometric p-rank of the Prym is at most its dimension `r−1`. Hence there is at most one summand. The character-factor norm gives its order and identifies its exponent as `p^{v(L_chi(1))}`. This argument does not assume a crystalline rank-one comparison or confuse order with exponent.

Because there is a unique unramified prime above `p` in the cyclotomic field, the stated local divisibility is also divisibility by the rational integer in the full ring of integers.
