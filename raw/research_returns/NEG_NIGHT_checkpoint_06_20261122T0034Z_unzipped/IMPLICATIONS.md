## Boolean algorithms and algebraic complexity

The useful new algorithmic parameter is the original witness dimension. NN-C001 compresses a quasilinear number of sparse equations over F2 to a linear number of equations without adjoining variables. It uses exact counting to choose instance-dependent tests. NN-C002 extends this to every fixed field F(2^r), exact preimages, lexicographic witnesses and exact uniform sampling. Weighted local factors, fixed algebraic tensor entries and entire bounded-cost histograms give additional restricted consequences, with their coefficient and bitlength hypotheses retained.

These results are subexponential algorithms, not polynomial algorithms. They do not imply P=NP, polynomial-time inversion of all one-way functions, or a general polynomial identity test. The Boolean quotient identifies x² with x; formal polynomial identities do not. The complexity chapter makes this distinction explicit and records the quantitative gap between E's exponent and the much smaller NSUBEXP hypotheses used in some algorithm-to-lower-bound theorems.

The parity of a Boolean zero set is the top multilinear coefficient of the product of its indicator factors. An efficiently explicit universal Frobenius-trace or coherent-cohomology presentation would therefore have strong complexity consequences; the trace identity alone supplies no algorithm for constructing or evaluating such a presentation. Hoza's log-space isolation result does not carry to NP merely by writing an exponentially wide configuration graph. Bürgisser's general comparison runs from a suitable Boolean counting lower bound to the algebraic separation. Its complex-constant elimination needs estimates for Dedekind zeta functions of arbitrary solution fields, not only Dirichlet L-functions. A suffices for appropriate abelian special cases; M supplies the general analytic premise conditionally. Neither premise supplies the Boolean lower bound. NN-C020 further removes the formal-degree restriction for promised bounded integer outputs over an explicitly presented polynomial-rank coefficient algebra. Arbitrary complex constants need not have this small presentation, so the general Bürgisser gate remains distinct.

## Split coefficients, weights and the Boolean structure

For a general G(R)-semimodule, the sign-fixed part is the disjoint union of the2-torsion in its amplitude fibres. It equals the Boolean skeleton only when those fibres have no2-torsion. The fixed scheme of sign on affine d-space is Spec Z[x_i]/(2x_i), with the full affine space in characteristic two. The rank-one phenomenon is already in the corrected SGA3 I Remark4.7.1.2, page29; the application to split coefficients is the new point here.

Additive linearization kills an idempotent support monoid. Inclusion–exclusion valuations form a different construction, with a free group on the join-irreducibles of a finite distributive lattice. The representation cone and finite-set cone are different. For C2, the effective finite-set cone maps to a+b epsilon with a≥b≥0; the effective representation cone only requires a,b≥0. The regular representation1+epsilon is not itself idempotent. It maps to the supported zero under a quotient of the effective representation semiring; no unital map from the whole representation ring to G(Z) is asserted. These distinctions are relevant to cote133 scan19 and to its known subset/exterior-power correction. They prevent an effective signed lift from silently becoming a literal point-count formula.
