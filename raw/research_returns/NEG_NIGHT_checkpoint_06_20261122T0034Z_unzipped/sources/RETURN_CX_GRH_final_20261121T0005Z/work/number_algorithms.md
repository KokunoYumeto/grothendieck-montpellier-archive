### CX-N01 — Deterministic Miller testing with the Bach cutoff

**Statement.** On the user's stipulated D theorem, the following specific primality test is deterministic and polynomial in the bit length of the integer. Handle n<3 and even n directly. For odd n write n−1=2^s d with d odd. For every integer 2≤a≤ceil(2(log n)^2), reject on gcd(a,n)>1 (unless a is divisible by n, which can be avoided by restricting a<n), or unless a^d=1 mod n or a^{2^j d}=−1 mod n for some 0≤j<s. Accept exactly primes.

**Conditional source and exact hypothesis.** René Schoof, *Four primality testing algorithms*, MSRI Publications 44 (2008), Theorem 2.2, published pp.105–106, explicitly says immediately before the theorem that GRH for **quadratic Dirichlet characters** suffices. PDF https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~stevenhagenp/ANTproc/05rene.pdf . The source traces the cutoff to Eric Bach, *Explicit bounds for primality testing and related problems*, Math. Comp.55 (1990), 355–380, DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8. Gary Miller's original conditional result is Theorem2, JCSS13 (1976), p301; the Appendix pp315–316 uses Dirichlet L-functions: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf . Original Bach now recovered and read: §6 pp369–373 deals exclusively with ordinary Dirichlet characters; Theorem2 p372 gives the 2(log m)^2 subgroup bound; the first paragraph on p373 explicitly gives the Miller/Solovay–Strassen witness consequence. Original journal PDF is work/primary/bach1990.pdf, recovered by root from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xayahrainie4793/pdf-files-cached-copy/main/pdf_83.pdf .

**Full transfer proof.** All quadratic Dirichlet characters occurring in Schoof's theorem are among D. Consequently the theorem's analytic hypothesis holds. Its deterministic strong-test criterion therefore accepts precisely odd primes. Extending the loop from the source's a<2(log n)^2 to the stated ceiling cutoff cannot introduce false acceptance; prime inputs pass every nonzero base. Small n and bases a≥n are handled directly, as specified. There are O((log n)^2) bases. Each strong test uses O(log n) multiplications modulo n, so with b-bit multiplication cost M(b) the cost is O(b^3 M(b)) plus lower-order gcd work. This is polynomial, e.g. O(b^5) using schoolbook multiplication. No factorization of n−1 is needed beyond extracting its power of two. This establishes the specific Miller–Bach implementation; PRIMES was already in deterministic P by AKS.

**Source-proof audit.** Schoof's complete Theorem2.2 proof was read. Its squarefree case uses quadratic characters χ_p, χ_q, and χ_pχ_q for distinct primes p,q dividing n; the repeated-prime case uses a smooth-number estimate and finite-group counting. Thus the hypothesis is actually smaller than D. A wording typo in the source theorem says “composite integer” before concluding “prime”; the surrounding proof and test criterion unambiguously concern an odd integer. This does not affect the transfer.

### CX-N02 — Prime-field power nonresidues

**Statement.** Given prime p and a prime r dividing p−1, an r-th power nonresidue in F_p can be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in log p on the stipulated D theorem, without factoring all of p−1.

**Conditional input.** Bach's small-subgroup-escape bound: under Dirichlet GRH every proper subgroup of (Z/pZ)^× omits an integer at most 2(log p)^2. Equivalent analytic input is the Ankeny bound for a nontrivial Dirichlet character, with an absolute constant if the sharp 2 is not used. Exact original source is now verified: Bach1990 Theorem2 p372, with §6 p369 explicitly restricting to ordinary Dirichlet characters. For nonzero power nonresidues, if p≤2(log p)^2 enumerate all F_p^×; otherwise the bound lies below p, so the omitted integer is a nonzero residue. Theorem3 p372 gives 3(log m)^2 when only integers coprime to m are quantified. Do not confuse these two hypotheses. A fully locatable fallback is Shoup's Proposition9 (author preprint p10), together with the short weighted-sum argument below.

**Full transfer proof without relying on the exact constant.** Set H=(F_p^×)^r. Choose a complex character χ of order r on F_p^×. Its kernel is H. Shoup's Proposition9, applied to χ and the principal character χ_0 modulo p, gives for the weighted prime sums I(χ,x)=O(sqrt(x)log p) and I(χ_0,x)=x/2+O(sqrt(x)log p), with absolute constants. D supplies precisely their RH hypotheses. If every integer 1≤a≤x not divisible by p belonged to H, then χ(a)=χ_0(a) for all primes contributing to the sums, so I(χ,x)=I(χ_0,x). This forces x/2=O(sqrt(x)log p), impossible for x>C(log p)^2 with a sufficiently large absolute effective constant C. Thus an r-nonresidue occurs in that range. Enumerate a and test a^{(p−1)/r}≠1 modulo p. Cyclicity of F_p^× proves that this is exactly membership outside H. Each test costs polynomially many bit operations. The number r is part of the input; only divisibility r|(p−1) and its primality are required, not the rest of the integer factorization.

The same argument works for any explicitly testable proper power subgroup; the statement with prime r is the standard root-extraction input.

### CX-N03 — Certified primitive roots in prime fields with factored group order

**Statement.** Given a prime p and the complete prime factorization p−1=∏r_i^{e_i}, D yields a deterministic polynomial-time construction of a multiplicative generator of F_p^×. The factorization is an input assumption; D alone does not supply polynomial-time integer factorization.

**Full proof.** For each r_i use CX-N02 to find a_i with a_i^{(p−1)/r_i}≠1. Write F_p^×=<g> and a_i=g^{u_i}. The test implies r_i∤u_i. Hence b_i=a_i^{(p−1)/r_i^{e_i}} has order exactly r_i^{e_i}. The product b=∏b_i has order ∏r_i^{e_i}=p−1 because its factors have pairwise coprime orders in an abelian group: raising b to an exponent k and projecting to each primary component shows r_i^{e_i}|k. There are at most log_2(p−1) distinct r_i, and all exponentiation and search costs are polynomial in log p. The same supplied factorization verifies a candidate by checking g^{(p−1)/r_i}≠1 for every i.

This is a finite multiplicative-group generator, not merely a field-extension generator. A small guaranteed candidate set without a recognition algorithm does not by itself give this certified construction.

### CX-N04 — Shoup's least primitive root theorem uses D only

**Source.** Victor Shoup, *Searching for primitive roots in finite fields*, Math.Comp.58(197) (1992), 369–380. Author PDF https://www.shoup.net/papers/primroots.pdf . Author-preprint **Theorem3 p4**, proof **§6 p13**, analytic **Proposition9 p10**. The PDF has sixteen pages with its own numbering; use those explicit locators until the publication-page offset is checked. Local copy and rendered/OCR pages in work/number_sources.

**Statement and transfer.** If r=ω(p−1), the least positive primitive root mod p is O(r^4(log r+1)^4(log p)^2). Section6 uses Iwaniec's finite combinatorial sieve and only Proposition9, whose characters are ordinary rational Dirichlet characters modulo p. Thus D discharges all analytic hypotheses of this theorem. Since r=O(log p/loglog p), the bound is O((log p)^6). Enumerating that many residues deterministically gives a polynomial-size list containing a primitive root. With the complete factorization of p−1 supplied, the usual order tests identify one in polynomial time. Without it, retain the candidate-list formulation; do not claim efficient recognition follows from the size bound.

**Full transfer proof.** Apply D to the finite family of powers of characters modulo p in §6/Proposition9. The sieve and cyclic-group identities are unconditional, so Theorem3's conclusion holds with its unchanged effective absolute constants. The displayed ω bound converts its search range to polynomial size. Certification with a supplied factorization is proved in CX-N03.

### CX-N05 — Shoup's quadratic-extension search has a genuine number-field gate in its published proof

**Source.** Same paper, **Theorem2 author p3**, proof **§5 pp12–13**, ray-class analytic estimate **Proposition13 pp11–12** and definitions **§4 pp10–11**.

**Exact fields and characters.** First choose the least prime δ≡1 mod4 for which −δ is a quadratic nonresidue mod p; Proposition10 supplies δ=O((log p)^2) using only D (characters mod4p). Put K=Q(sqrt(−δ)), O_K=Z[sqrt(−δ)], and M=pO_K. The inertness of p identifies O_K/M with F_{p^2}. The relevant characters are characters of the ray-class group I_M/P^+_M. Because K is imaginary, the positivity condition is vacuous; they are finite-order Hecke characters over K, not necessarily rational Dirichlet characters. Section5 uses certain subgroups H_d of this character group, for each squarefree d dividing p²−1; their orders are h_K d. Proposition13 bounds their weighted ideal sums by RH for these Hecke L-functions and the trivial factor ζ_K. It is enough to assume RH for ζ_{H_{K,M}}, the Dedekind zeta function of the ray class field, because its factorization contains all these Hecke factors.

**Transfer and output.** Conditional on H, every required character is covered, hence Theorem2 produces in deterministic polynomial time a candidate set in a constructed model of F_{p²}, guaranteed to contain a primitive root. The model can be moved to the input field by unconditional deterministic field-isomorphism algorithms. Given the complete prime factorization of p²−1, order tests identify a generator. Given a specified prime r|p²−1, scanning the candidate set and testing a^{(p²−1)/r}≠1 finds an r-nonresidue without requiring complete factorization. H removes the analytic gate but not the optional primitive-root recognition input.

**Full transfer proof.** The initial rational-prime selection is covered by D⊂H. Every later nontrivial analytic term in §5 is a sum I(χ,x,y) for χ in the finite ray-class family above, and H supplies its critical-line assertion. Proposition13 and the combinatorial sieve therefore give the same norm bound as in the published proof. Since δ and h_K are bounded by powers of log p, enumerating a+b sqrt(−δ) in that bound has polynomial size. Reducing modulo M gives the promised list. The elementary power tests and, when supplied, factorization tests are correct by cyclicity. No arbitrary higher-dimensional Artin-holomorphy conjecture is needed.

**D status.** D supplies the first prime-selection step and ζ_K because K/Q is quadratic, but the published ray-class character estimates have not been reduced here to rational Dirichlet GRH. The fact that K/Q is abelian does not make the ray class field abelian over Q.

### CX-N06 — Buchmann–Shoup generating sets in general finite extensions

**Source.** J.Buchmann and V.Shoup, *Constructing nonresidues in finite fields and the Extended Riemann Hypothesis*, Math.Comp.65(215) (1996),1311–1326; readable primary-paper mirror https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=27f9bf0a94da9a503525701827a93423760c8933&repid=rep1&type=pdf ; author PDF https://shoup.net/papers/nonresidues.pdf . Main statement pp1311–1312, ERH definition p1314, Fact2.1 p1314, Fact4.3 p1319, final algorithm §5 and conclusions §7.

**Exact scope.** Their ERH is RH for Dedekind zeta functions of all number fields, explicitly p1314. For p∤n they choose a prime q≡1 mod2n with gcd((q−1)/ord_q(p),n)=1, then take the unique degree-n subfield K of Q(ζ_q). K is real cyclic, Δ_K=q^{n−1}, and p is inert. Fact2.1 uses Adleman–Lenstra's Chebotarev construction to ensure q=O(n^4(log(np))²). Fact4.3 invokes Bach1990 **Theorem4** for generation of the ray-class group of modulus pO_K by small prime ideals. Its characters are finite-order ray-class Hecke characters over K. The full analytic input includes this ray-class family, and the Chebotarev auxiliary fields used for prime selection; the auxiliary prime-selection field is E=Q(ζ_{2n},p^{1/n}), as proved in CX-N11 below. Thus RH for ζ_E, ζ_Q, and the ray class field zeta ζ_{H_{K,pO_K}} is a concrete sufficient package.

**Transferred statement.** Conditional on H, there is a deterministic algorithm constructing a model of F_{p^n} and a set S generating its multiplicative group in time (n log p)^{O(n)}. For fixed n this is polynomial in log p. For variable n it is not a polynomial bound. Any prescribed prime r|p^n−1 has a nonresidue in S, since a generating set cannot be contained in the proper subgroup of r-th powers. Scanning S with exponentiation finds one. With the complete factorization of p^n−1, separate primary generators can be assembled exactly as in CX-N03; a group-generating set need not contain one primitive element.

**Full transfer proof.** H includes the trivial character for every required number field, so it implies the precise all-Dedekind hypothesis explicitly assumed in this paper. Therefore each conditional prime-selection and ray-class-generation bound used by the deterministic construction holds, and the published operation counts remain (n log p)^{O(n)}. Field isomorphism transfers the set to any supplied model. The group-theoretic deductions are as just proved. The proof requires no unproved Artin holomorphy. Under D the displayed broader proof gate is not yet discharged; this says nothing about whether a different algorithm could have a smaller hypothesis.

### CX-N07 — Ideal-class generators and exact class-field packaging

**Sources.** Bach1990 **Theorem4** as explicitly cited in H.W.Lenstra, *Algorithms in algebraic number theory*, Bull.AMS26(2) (1992), §5 immediately after Problem5.6, author PDF p25, https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1992a/art.pdf . K.Belabas, F.Diaz y Diaz and E.Friedman, *Small generators of the ideal class group*, Math.Comp.77(262) (2008),1185–1197, DOI10.1090/S0025-5718-07-02003-0; readable author preprint dated5Dec2005, https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~kbelabas/research/OnBach.pdf , introduction pp2–3, **Theorems1.1 p3 and2.1 p5**, proof pp4–6.

**Statement.** For a number field K of discriminant Δ_K, the usual Bach bound says Cl(O_K) is generated by prime ideals of norm at most 12(log|Δ_K|)^2 under the relevant GRH. The published analytic proof uses ζ_K and the L_K(s,χ) for the nontrivial characters χ of Cl(O_K). These χ are unramified finite-order Hecke characters over K. BDF's more refined *computed* bound uses only RH for the nontrivial ideal-class-character L-functions; its numerical inequality is Theorem1.1 or the general test-function criterion Theorem2.1. One should not replace either gate indiscriminately by RH for ζ_K alone.

**Full transfer and class-field proof.** Let H_K be the Hilbert class field. Artin reciprocity gives Gal(H_K/K)=Cl(O_K). Since this extension is abelian,
ζ_{H_K}(s)=∏_{χ∈Cl(O_K)^∧}L_K(s,χ).
The trivial factor is ζ_K. Every nontrivial finite-order Hecke factor is entire, and the trivial factor has its only pole at1. At any s with 0<Re(s)<1 no factor has a pole. Therefore a zero of any factor away from Re(s)=1/2 would be a zero of ζ_{H_K} away from that line. RH for ζ_{H_K} consequently suffices for the entire class-character family, including ζ_K. H supplies this directly (or supplies the character family itself), so conditional on H the Bach/BDF generator conclusion follows with exactly the published bound/criterion. For a ray modulus m, replace H_K by the ray class field H_{K,m} and Cl(O_K) by the ray class group; the same factorization argument works. Local factors at the finitely many omitted primes have zeros only on Re(s)=0 and do not change this critical-strip argument.

**Scope.** Even if K/Q is abelian and D therefore supplies RH for ζ_K, the class-field extension H_K/Q need not be abelian. The required class-character estimates are an additional gate of the published proof. Small norm generators give a finite factor base, not the relations among them, and hence not on their own a polynomial-time class-group algorithm.

### CX-N08 — Class-group/regulator verification preserves a distinct relation-search gate

**Exact source.** Lenstra1992, §5 following Problem5.6, **equation(5.7)** and the paragraph following it, author PDF p25; BDF introduction pp2–3 describes the same certification and explicitly notes that the general-number-field subexponential analysis of Buchmann is conjectural even under GRH, whereas the quadratic case has rigorous results.

**Statement and proof.** Suppose the factor base generates the class group, a subgroup H of the full S-unit group K_S is generated by the sampled S-units, and H contains all roots of unity. Here K_S is not a relation lattice: the valuation map φ:H→Z^{S_fin} has conjectural units kerφ and conjectural class group cokerφ. The resulting tentative class number h' and regulator R' satisfy h'R'=hR [K_S:H] by Lenstra(5.7). Thus their product is a positive integer multiple of the true hR. If rigorous analytic bounds give a/2<hR<a and the computed product also obeys a/2<h'R'<a, then
1≤[K_S:H]=(h'R')/(hR)<2,
so the index is1. This proves completeness of the relations and correctness of the resulting class group and unit lattice. H discharges the analytic GRH used for factor-base completeness and for controlled approximations to the residue of ζ_K at1. It does not prove a smoothness distribution or a heuristic rate of finding independent relations. Consequently any additional smoothness/randomness assumptions and any subexponential, expected, or fixed-degree qualifiers in the selected algorithm remain.

This is an exact verification theorem, distinct from obtaining generators and distinct from a complexity theorem for finding enough relations. D alone can supply the ζ_K approximation when K/Q is abelian, but the separate class-character factor-base gate remains unless independently certified.

### CX-N11 — Explicit Kummer field for auxiliary primes in finite-field construction

This is the auxiliary prime gate of Buchmann–Shoup1996 Fact2.1 p1314, and the closely related Adleman–Lenstra1986 Proposition3 p351. It is stronger information than naming an unspecified number field in Chebotarev.

**Full proof.** The prime p is unramified in F. For odd n, Q(ζ_{2n})=Q(ζ_n), so this remains true if p=2; for even n, p is odd and coprime to2n. For every P|p in F, v_P(p)=1. Hence X^n−p is Eisenstein over O_{F,P}, and is irreducible over F. Thus [E:F]=n. Since F contains ζ_n, E is the splitting field over Q of X^n−p together with the 2n-th roots of unity, so E/Q is Galois. The F-automorphism σ defined by σ(α)=ζ_nα has order n. If τ is any Q-automorphism of E and τ(ζ_n)=ζ_n^u, then τστ^{-1}=σ^u; u is a unit modulo n. All members of the conjugacy class C of σ therefore have order n and fix F.

Chebotarev in the stated Bach–Sorenson form gives an unramified q with Frobenius in C and the displayed discriminant bound. Its Frobenius restricts to the identity on F, so q splits completely in F. This implies q≡1 mod2n: when n is even, F has conductor2n; when n is odd, splitting gives q≡1 modn, and q is odd. Indeed q=2 cannot split in Q(ζ_n) for odd n>1 since 2≢1 modn. Also q∤pn: p ramifies in E/F by the Eisenstein polynomial, and primes dividing n ramify in F.

Choose a prime Q of E above q whose Frobenius is σ^u. In its residue field, α^q=ζ_n^uα, so
p^{(q−1)/n}=α^{q−1}=ζ_n^u.
The reduction of ζ_n has order n, since q∤n. Thus for every prime r|n,
p^{(q−1)/r}=(ζ_n^u)^{n/r}≠1.
Write p=g^a in the cyclic group F_q^×. The last condition is equivalent to r∤a. As ord_q(p)=(q−1)/gcd(a,q−1), no prime dividing n divides (q−1)/ord_q(p), which is the desired condition.

Finally |Δ_F|≤(2n)^{[F:Q]} and the relative discriminant ideal D_{E/F} divides the polynomial discriminant ideal (n^n p^{n−1}). The discriminant tower formula gives
log|Δ_E|≤n[F:Q]log(2n)+[F:Q](nlog n+(n−1)log p)=O(n^2 log(np)),
and [E:Q]=n[F:Q]≤2n^2. Substitution into Theorem5.1 yields q=O(n^4 log^2(np)). The constants are effective. Enumerating primes to this bound and testing the order condition, factoring the polynomially bounded q−1 by trial division if desired, costs polynomially many bit operations in n and log p.

**Field-model link.** The unique degree-n subfield K⊂Q(ζ_q) is totally real because q≡1 mod2n. The quotient Gal(K/Q) has order n; the image of p has order n by the gcd condition, so p is inert in K and O_K/pO_K≅F_{p^n}. Thus the exact fields in the Buchmann–Shoup construction can be taken to be E and the ray class field H_{K,pO_K}. Conditional on H all their zeta hypotheses are covered. Alternatively ζ_E factors into the Hecke L-functions of the cyclic extension E/F; no general Artin-holomorphy conjecture is used. D covers ζ_F since F/Q is abelian, but the additional Kummer characters over F have not been reduced to D in this proof.

**Source link.** Adleman–Lenstra, *Finding irreducible polynomials over finite fields*, STOC1986 pp350–355, **Theorem1 p350, AlgorithmA pp350–351, Proposition3 p351, Lemma6 p352**, author PDF https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1986a/art.pdf . Its polynomial-time field-construction result is a different problem from factoring an arbitrary given polynomial.

### CX-N12 — A rigorously conditional Las Vegas class-group algorithm

**Source and input.** Jean-François Biasse and Muhammed Rashad Erukulangara, *A proof of the conjectured run time of the Hafner–McCurley class group algorithm*, DOI10.3934/amc.2021055, primary accepted PDF https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10324557 . **Main Theorem and Conjecture1 author p4; Algorithms1–4 pp9,11,13,15; Corollary1 p16.** The statement discusses quadratic orders, but the actual algorithm boxes require a negative fundamental discriminant −d. **Statement.** Conditional on H, their modified algorithm computes the complete abelian-group structure of Cl(O_K) with Las Vegas expected bit complexity
exp((3/√8+o(1))√(log d loglog d)).
It remains randomized and subexponential, not deterministic or polynomial in log d. Their Conjecture1 explicitly asks RH for Hecke L-functions over number fields. **Full transfer, certification, and expectation proof.** H supplies the exact Hecke hypothesis explicitly declared in Conjecture1, so every GRH invocation in Theorem2.1, Proposition1, subsequent relation-probability bounds, and the class-number approximation is valid. All sampled integer relation vectors lie in the true relation lattice Λ⊂Z^n.
