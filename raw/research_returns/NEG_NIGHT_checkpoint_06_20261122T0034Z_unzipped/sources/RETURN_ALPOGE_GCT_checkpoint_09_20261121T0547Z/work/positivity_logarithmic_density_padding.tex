\paragraph{P-F67: logarithmic overhead suffices for a narrow density obstruction.}
The broad product argument in P-F63 leaves an exact representation-theoretic
question: can a single ordinary plethysm triple, or a single Kronecker
triple, be padded to a strict-saturation exception at sufficiently small
cost?  Constant additional cost is not necessary.  We give a criterion
allowing logarithmic additional raw partition length and polynomially
many preimages.  No map satisfying the criterion in either narrow
population is asserted here.

For a positive integer $a$, let $\ell(a)=1+\lfloor\log_2a\rfloor$;
for a partition, sum $\ell$ over its positive parts and omit zero parts.
For an ordered partition triple $x$, let $L(x)$ be the sum over its
three partitions.  This is the raw partition input convention in
GCT VI, Section~1.1, printed p.~9.  In particular no ambient rank,
description tree or multiplicity encoding of repeated equal parts is
substituted for that convention.  The narrow ordinary clause is
Hypothesis~1.6.5(b), printed p.~25, and uses length exactly $N$;
the at-most-$N$ clause in Hypothesis~3.3.2(b) is a different source
population, as discussed in P-F48:
\url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.0229}.

Let $\mathcal D$ be either the ordered triples with nonzero ordinary
plethysm coefficient or the ordered triples with nonzero Kronecker
coefficient.  Let $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal D$ consist of the
triples with nonzero literal strict saturation index.  Write
\[
 A(N)=|\{x\in\mathcal D:L(x)\le N\}|,\qquad
 E(N)=|\{x\in\mathcal E:L(x)\le N\}|.
 \tag{P-F67a}
\]
Suppose there are fixed constants $\kappa>0$, $d\ge0$ and an integer
$C\ge0$ such that, for every sufficiently large integer $N$, there
is a map
\[
 \Phi_N:\{x\in\mathcal D:L(x)\le N\}
       \longrightarrow
       \{y\in\mathcal E:L(y)\le F(N)\},
 \quad F(N)=N+\lceil\kappa\log(N+2)\rceil+C,
 \tag{P-F67b}
\]
whose fibers have size at most $(N+2)^d$.  Here and below $\log$
without a subscript is the natural logarithm.  The maps need not be
the same for different $N$, and no algorithmic complexity is assumed.
Then the exceptional proportion fails to decrease faster than every
inverse polynomial, both in the cumulative population (P-F67a) and
in the source's exact-length population.  Thus an injective map with
$O(\log N)$ overhead is sufficient, and the conclusion remains valid
with polynomially bounded fibers.

\emph{1. A self-delimiting serialization of raw partition triples.}
For a positive part $a$ of binary length $\ell$, encode it as
\[
             1\,0^{\ell-1}\,\operatorname{bin}(a).
 \tag{P-F67c}
\]
Terminate each partition by a single zero.  The first bit determines
whether the partition ends or another part follows.  In the latter
case, the number of subsequent zeros before the first one determines
$\ell-1$; that first one and the next $\ell-1$ bits recover the
ordinary binary expansion of $a$.  Consequently decoding is unique.
Concatenating the three partition blocks gives a prefix-free code:
after the third terminator a complete triple has ended, so a complete
triple code cannot be a proper prefix of another one.  Empty partitions
cause no problem; their block is just the terminator.

The code for a part has length $2\ell$, exactly, so the triple code
has length $2L(x)+3$.  Padding all codes with $L(x)\le N$ by zeros
to length $2N+3$ preserves injectivity by prefix-freeness.  Therefore
the number of all ordered partition triples of raw length at most
$N$, and in particular the number in $\mathcal D$, satisfies
\[
                         A(N)\le 2^{2N+3}.
 \tag{P-F67d}
\]
No choice of delimiters is being charged back to the source's raw
length: (P-F67c) is an auxiliary injection used solely to bound the
number of source inputs.

\emph{2. Iterating the logarithmic padding.}
The fiber hypothesis gives the exact counting inequality
\[
                    E(F(N))\ge\frac{A(N)}{(N+2)^d}.
 \tag{P-F67e}
\]
Assume for a contradiction that $E(m)/A(m)\le m^{-u}$ eventually
for every fixed $u>0$.  Choose one fixed
\[
                         u>d+2\kappa\log 2.
 \tag{P-F67f}
\]
Take $N_0$ so large that the padding maps and this one density
bound apply at every subsequent cutoff, that $A(N_0)>0$, and
that $F(N)\ge N+2$ for $N\ge N_0$.  Equations (P-F67e)--(P-F67f)
then give
\[
 A(F(N))\ge\frac{F(N)^u}{(N+2)^d}A(N)
             \ge (N+2)^{u-d}A(N).
 \tag{P-F67g}
\]
Define $N_{i+1}=F(N_i)$.  Summing the logarithm of (P-F67g) yields
\[
 \log A(N_k)\ge\log A(N_0)
                  +(u-d)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\log(N_i+2).
 \tag{P-F67h}
\]
On the other hand the definition of $F$ gives
\[
 N_k-N_0=\kappa\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\log(N_i+2)+O(k),
 \tag{P-F67i}
\]
where the absolute error is bounded by $(C+1)k$.
Because $N_i$ increases to infinity, its successive increments
increase without bound in the following sufficient sense: for any
fixed $B$, every sufficiently late increment is at least $B$.
Hence $N_k/k\to\infty$ and $k/N_k\to0$.
Dividing (P-F67i) by $N_k$ therefore proves
\[
 \frac{1}{N_k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\log(N_i+2)
                         \longrightarrow\frac1\kappa.
 \tag{P-F67j}
\]
It follows from (P-F67h) that
$\liminf_k\log A(N_k)/N_k\ge(u-d)/\kappa>2\log2$,
contrary to (P-F67d).  In particular, for any fixed $u$ satisfying
(P-F67f), the cumulative proportion exceeds $N^{-u}$ at arbitrarily
large cutoffs.  It is not necessary to choose a new density exponent
at each step of the iteration.

\emph{3. Exact-length shells cannot evade the contradiction.}
Write $a_j=|\{x\in\mathcal D:L(x)=j\}|$ and
$e_j=|\{x\in\mathcal E:L(x)=j\}|$.  If $a_j=0$, then $e_j=0$;
the assertion about that shell is read as the inequality
$e_j\le j^{-v}a_j$.  Both populations under consideration have an
elementary exponential lower bound for $A(N)$.  For ordinary
plethysm, all triples
\[
                         ((1),(a),(a))
\]
have coefficient one.  For $h=\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor\ge1$, the
$2^{h-1}$ integers of binary length $h$ give distinct inputs of
length $1+2h\le N$.  For Kronecker coefficients, all triples
$((a),(a),(a))$ have coefficient one, since the one-row Specht
module is the trivial representation.  Choosing
$h=\lfloor N/3\rfloor$ gives $2^{h-1}$ distinct inputs.  Thus in
either case, for all sufficiently large $N$,
\[
                           A(N)\ge 2^{N/3-2}.
 \tag{P-F67k}
\]

If the exact-length exceptional proportions decreased faster than
every inverse polynomial, fix $v>0$ and use the shell bound with
exponent $2v$ at every $j\ge\lceil\sqrt N\rceil$, for sufficiently
large $N$.  Splitting the cumulative sum at this point and using
(P-F67d), (P-F67k) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \frac{E(N)}{A(N)}
 &\le\frac{A(\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor)}{A(N)}+N^{-v}\\
 &\le 2^{2\sqrt N-N/3+5}+N^{-v}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{P-F67l}
\]
The harmless possible overlap when $\sqrt N$ is an integer only
enlarges the upper bound.  The first term is smaller than every
inverse polynomial.  Taking $v$ larger than any desired exponent
would make the cumulative proportions decrease faster than every
inverse polynomial as well, contradicting Step~2.  This proves the
exact-length assertion. \hfill$\square$

The fractions here use the nonzero-coefficient population, namely
the conditional reading of the narrow density wording specified in
P-F48.  The same counting criterion works for a different chosen
population only if the maps (P-F67b) cover that population and its
count has the lower bound needed in Step~3; a map covering only
nonzero inputs does not by itself refute an unconditional density
statement with all triples in its denominator.

An exact product identity of stretching functions would be one way
to verify that the image lies in $\mathcal E$: the fixed factor of
P-F63 has a nonzero constituent vanishing at one, and multiplication
by an input function with every constituent nonzero retains that
failure of strict saturation.  But a direct-product subgroup input
is not an ordinary partition triple.  One must still construct a
map within the chosen narrow family, prove its complete stretching
identity or otherwise prove its strict-index property, and verify
the raw length and fiber bounds in (P-F67b).  Semigroup inequalities,
a single-value coefficient identity, and padding with linear or
polynomial additional raw length do not establish this logarithmic
criterion.  This proposition reduces that remaining gate; it does
not settle narrow SH(b).
