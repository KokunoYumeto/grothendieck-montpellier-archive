\subsection{Which exact Kronecker bridges preserve this stretching ray?}
\label{sec:kronecker-bridge-audit}

\paragraph{P-F58 (a scope audit of three concrete transfer attempts).}
Write $k(\lambda,\mu,\nu)$ for the ordinary Kronecker coefficient,
and set $\gamma=\gcd(n,3)$, $d_s=ns/\gamma$,
$q_s=3s/\gamma$.  The cubic family of P-F57 is
\[
 f_n(s)=\bigl[S^{(q_s^n)}\mathbb C^n:
                    \operatorname{Sym}^{d_s}
                         (\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^n)\bigr].
                                                        \tag{P-F58a}
\]
For $n>9$ and every integer $s\ge1$,
\[
 k\bigl((q_s^n),(d_s^3),(d_s^3)\bigr)=0.                \tag{P-F58b}
\]
The symmetric-square and exterior-square Kronecker coefficients
with these same partitions also vanish.  In contrast, $f_n(s)$
is nonzero for infinitely many $s$.  Thus the direct
height-three rectangular substitution suggested by the binary
plethysm identity does not transfer P-F57.

There is also an exact exterior identity
\[
 f_n(s)=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{GL_{q_s}}
 \left(\det^n,
       \bigwedge^{d_s}(\bigwedge^3\mathbb C^{q_s})\right),
 \qquad s\ge1.                                        \tag{P-F58c}
\]
Here the group rank and the length of the target partition grow
with $s$; this displayed identity is not a fixed-partition
Kronecker stretching identity.  Finally, Manivel's stabilization
does give a complete fixed Kronecker ray, but its inner module
is the adjoint module: for a fixed $r\ge2$, a partition $\rho$,
and an integer $D\ge\max(1,|\rho|)$, put
$\Lambda=(rD-|\rho|,\rho)$.  Then for every $s\ge1$,
\[
 k\bigl(s\Lambda,(sD)^r,(sD)^r\bigr)
       =\dim\bigl(S^{s\rho}(\mathfrak{sl}_r)\bigr)^{SL_r}.
                                                        \tag{P-F58d}
\]
No equality between the right side of (P-F58d) and (P-F58a)
has been established here.  These conclusions exclude the
specified direct transfers; they do not prove that every
possible Kronecker encoding of the cubic family is impossible.

\paragraph{The length bound, including the two square summands.}
For partitions of the same integer $N$,
\[
 k(\lambda,\mu,\nu)>0
          \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \ell(\lambda)\le\ell(\mu)\ell(\nu).                   \tag{P-F58e}
\]
For completeness, take $U=\mathbb C^{\ell(\mu)}$ and
$V=\mathbb C^{\ell(\nu)}$.  Schur--Weyl duality, applied
first to $U^{\otimes N}$ and $V^{\otimes N}$ and then to
$(U\otimes V)^{\otimes N}$, gives
\[
 S^\lambda(U\otimes V)
   \cong\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta}
       \mathbb C^{k(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)}
                        \otimes S^\alpha U\otimes S^\beta V.
                                                        \tag{P-F58f}
\]
This is valid even if the left side is zero: it follows by
applying $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak S_N}([\lambda],-)$
to the two decompositions of the same tensor power.  If
$\ell(\lambda)>\dim(U\otimes V)$, the left side vanishes.
The summand with $\alpha=\mu$, $\beta=\nu$ contains two
nonzero Schur modules, so its multiplicity must be zero.
This proves (P-F58e), and taking both lengths equal to three
proves (P-F58b).  The primary Schur--Weyl statement and
tensor-product consequence are Manivel, \emph{On rectangular
Kronecker coefficients}, arXiv:0907.3351v1, Section~2,
printed pp.~1--2, especially equation~(1).

Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 sk(\lambda,\mu)&=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak S_N}
                           ([\lambda],\operatorname{Sym}^2[\mu]),\\
 ak(\lambda,\mu)&=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak S_N}
                           ([\lambda],\bigwedge^2[\mu]).
 \end{split}
\]
In characteristic zero the flip of $[\mu]\otimes[\mu]$
splits it as its $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces, whence
$k(\lambda,\mu,\mu)=sk(\lambda,\mu)+ak(\lambda,\mu)$.
Both terms are nonnegative, so both vanish in (P-F58b).
Transposing both rectangular partitions does not evade this
conclusion: $[\mu^t]\cong[\mu]\otimes\operatorname{sgn}$,
and the square of this one-dimensional sign character is
trivial, in both symmetric and alternating squares.

The nonzero cubic discriminant used in P-F19 has ordinary
coefficient degree $n2^{n-1}$, hence stretch degree
$\gamma2^{n-1}$.  Its powers remain nonzero in the polynomial
ring.  Thus $f_n(t\gamma2^{n-1})\ge1$ for every $t\ge1$,
which already disproves equality with (P-F58b) without
requiring any asymptotic index estimate.  P-F54 gives the
stronger eventual support statement.  The binary identity
itself remains valid in its proper scope: over $\mathbb C$,
Ikenmeyer--Omar--Tsintsilidas, arXiv:2509.10069v2,
Proposition~4.2 and Theorem~4.3, printed p.~7, identify
\[
 \left(\operatorname{Sym}^{ab}
          (\mathbb C^b\otimes\mathbb C^b\otimes\mathbb C^2)
       \right)^{SL_b\times SL_b}
       \cong\operatorname{Sym}^{a}
                       (\operatorname{Sym}^{b}\mathbb C^2)
                                                        \tag{P-F58g}
\]
as $GL_2$-modules, with a compatible regraded algebra
isomorphism.  Taking a Schur multiplicity gives the usual
two-row rectangular Kronecker identity.  The displayed
two-dimensional factor is part of this theorem's hypothesis.

\paragraph{Why the exterior identity has a different stretch.}
Put $d=d_s$, $q=q_s$, and $N=3d=nq$.  In
$\mathfrak S_N$ let $H=\mathfrak S_3\wr\mathfrak S_d$ act
by permutations inside $d$ blocks of three positions and by
permutations of the blocks.  Symmetrizing the tensor positions
gives
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}^{d}(\operatorname{Sym}^3 V)
                       \cong (V^{\otimes N})^H.
\]
Alternating inside each block and then between the blocks
instead gives the $H$-character
$\chi=(\operatorname{sgn}_{\mathfrak S_3})^{\otimes d}
          \operatorname{sgn}_{\mathfrak S_d}$ in
$\bigwedge^d(\bigwedge^3 E)$.  A block transposition has
sign $(-1)^3=-1$ as a permutation of the $N$ tensor positions.
Consequently $\chi=\operatorname{sgn}_{\mathfrak S_N}|_H$.
The identity $[\lambda^t]=[\lambda]\otimes\operatorname{sgn}$
now gives
\[
 \dim[\lambda]^H
       =\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(\chi,[\lambda^t]).
\]
Schur--Weyl duality on the two tensor spaces proves equality
of the corresponding Schur multiplicities.  For
$\lambda=(q^n)$, its transpose is $(n^q)$ and the latter
Schur module on $E=\mathbb C^q$ is precisely $\det^n$.
This proves (P-F58c).  The same standard odd-inner-degree
duality is stated explicitly in Fischer--Ikenmeyer,
arXiv:2002.00788v1, Fact~2.2 and equation~(2.3), printed p.~3.

Since $q_s=(3/\gamma)s$ is unbounded, no fixed finite
ambient rank contains the target partitions $(n^{q_s})$
for all $s$.  Nor are these targets of the form $s\tau$
for any fixed nonzero partition $\tau$: their largest part
is the constant $n$, whereas the largest part of $s\tau$
is $s\tau_1$.  Thus neither the literal rank nor the
literal partition family in (P-F58c) supplies a usual
rowwise stretching ray.  This is an obstruction to that
specific inference, not to all further transformations
of the identity.

\paragraph{A stable identity that really does preserve every stretch.}
Manivel's Theorem~1, printed p.~1, states
\[
 k\bigl((rD-|\rho|,\rho),(D^r),(D^r)\bigr)
                  =\dim(S^\rho\mathfrak{sl}_r)^{GL_r}
       \quad(D\ge|\rho|).
\]
Apply it with $D$ replaced by $sD$ and $\rho$ by $s\rho$.
Its inequality remains valid for every $s\ge1$.
Moreover $rD-|\rho|\ge\rho_1$, so the padded sequence
is a partition and scales exactly to $s\Lambda$.
The center of $GL_r$ acts trivially by conjugation on
$\mathfrak{sl}_r$, and any $GL_r(\mathbb C)$ element is
a scalar times an $SL_r(\mathbb C)$ element.  Their
invariant subspaces therefore coincide.  This proves
(P-F58d).  Manivel's Remark~1, printed p.~4, permits the
weaker condition $2D\ge|\rho|+\rho_1$; this also implies
$rD-|\rho|\ge\rho_1$ for $r\ge2$ and remains homogeneous
under stretching.

An equality on every positive integer determines the same
global quasipolynomial: pass to a common period, and on
each residue class the two constituent polynomials agree
at infinitely many positive integers, hence identically.
Thus whenever the strict indices are defined for these
quasipolynomials, (P-F58d) preserves them exactly.  The
unresolved step for using it on P-F57 is an additional
identity with the cubic symmetric-power inner module;
stabilization alone does not provide such an identity.

\paragraph{Other supplied comparisons do not assert that identity.}
Ikenmeyer--Panova, \emph{Rectangular Kronecker coefficients
and plethysms in geometric complexity theory},
Proposition~2.8, printed p.~7 of arXiv:1512.03798,
is a multiplicity inequality in its specified range.
The determinant-orbit and orbit-closure comparisons in
the supplied B\"urgisser--Ikenmeyer--Panova text,
Section~1(b), printed pp.~3--4 of arXiv:1604.06431v3,
similarly involve inequalities.  Inequality of actual values does
not identify the constituent polynomials or their
off-residue signs, so it does not transfer P-F57's indices.
Fischer--Ikenmeyer's Theorem~7.1, arXiv:2002.00788v1,
printed p.~15, does prove exact plethysm--Kronecker
equalities, but for the explicitly constructed tomography
instances with complete-pyramid padding and transposition.
It does not state equality for arbitrary cubic plethysm
shapes.  Its padding is additive before transposition,
so scaling the tomography data does not by itself turn
its formulas into simultaneous rowwise scaling of fixed
partitions.  No identification of the cubic rectangular
targets with that special class has been proved here.

The general question of ordinary or symmetric Kronecker
strict-index bounds therefore remains separate from the
proved cubic plethysm result.  A direct analysis of the
actual tensor invariant ring, or another exact ray identity,
is required for the next transfer attempt.
