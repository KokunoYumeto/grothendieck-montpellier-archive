\tag{C2}

## 2. The cyclic Cayley operator

Let J=C as a free rank-two S-module. Give it the invertible S-linear operator
\[
F_J=\frac{1+ix}{1-ix}
      =1+s x,\qquad
s=\frac{2i}{1-i\Delta},
\quad
\lambda=1+s\Delta=\frac{1+i\Delta}{1-i\Delta}.
\tag{C7}
\]
Here multiplication by a C element means its regular action on J. Both s and lambda are units in S. In the column basis (1,x),
\[
F_J=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\s&\lambda\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{C8}
\]
The formulas follow from x squared equal to Delta x. The inverse is obtained by replacing i with -i.

**Lemma C-A.** The S-algebra generated by F_J is C, and
\[
\operatorname{End}_{F_J}(J)=C.
\tag{C9}
\]
The same assertion holds after every base change S to T.

**Proof.** Since s is a unit, multiplication by x equals s inverse times (F_J-1). Thus an S-linear map commutes with F_J exactly when it commutes with C. The endomorphisms of the regular C-module are C itself. This proof uses no inverse of Delta and works over every base-changed coefficient ring. Equivalently the Cayley inverse is x=(F_J-1)/(i(F_J+1)); its denominator is invertible. QED.

In particular J is cyclic even when its two eigenvalues coincide. The basis (1,F_J1) has determinant s. Its characteristic polynomial is
\[
(T-1)(T-\lambda).
\tag{C10}
\]
At v=1, Delta=0, s=2i and J becomes the dual-number regular module with
\[
F_J=1+2i x,\qquad x^2=0,\quad x\ne0.
\tag{C11}
\]
It has a nontrivial size-two Jordan block. Replacing this operator by its semisimplification would change its centralizer and lose the actual collision algebra.

## 3. Exact End and Hom, not merely matching spectra

For a scalar a in S, write S_a for a rank-one module with linear Frobenius a. Define
\[
H=S_{-1}\oplus S_i\oplus J^{\oplus2},
\]
\[
K=S_{-1}^{20}\oplus S_i^4\oplus L_1^{16}\oplus L_2^4,
\tag{C12}
\]
where the branch Frobenius operators are respectively 1 and lambda. Then H has S-rank6 and K has S-rank44.

**Theorem C-B.** With the prescribed linear Frobenius operators,
\[
\operatorname{End}_{F}(H)=A,\qquad
\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(H,K)=M
\tag{C13}
\]
as an algebra and its actual right module. The right action on Hom is precomposition. These two identities commute with every coefficient base change S to T, including v=1. In particular the ranks are 10 and64 through the collision.

**Proof.** The scalar sectors are separated by units. In C,
\[
F_J+1=\frac2{1-ix},\qquad
F_J-i=\frac{(1-i)(1-x)}{1-ix}.
\tag{C14}
\]
The first is a unit. The second is a unit because 1-x has norm 1-Delta and that element was inverted. The difference -1-i is also a unit. Consequently there are no intertwiners between different displayed scalar sectors or between a scalar sector and the J sector. The same statements hold for the branch quotients.

By Lemma C-A, intertwiners on J squared are exactly matrices over C. Thus End_F(H)=S times S times M2(C), retaining the nonzero collision nilpotent. For maps from J to a branch line, the equation of commuting with F implies commuting with x=s inverse(F-1), hence C-linearity. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{F}(J^{\oplus2},L_j)
=\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(C^{\oplus2},L_j)
=\operatorname{Row}(L_j).
\]
The scalar maps contribute ranks20 and4, and the branch maps contribute ranks32 and8. This proves the Hom identity of rank64.

Use columns for H's matrix factor and rows for maps out of it. Precomposition by an endomorphism matrix is then exactly right multiplication of these rows. Under (C6), every named generator therefore acts as the actual F19/F21 generator on M. This is an action comparison, including the branch labels and the specified regular lattice.

Every proof used unit identities valid after arbitrary base change and the regular module C_T itself. Thus the two identifications commute with base change even when lambda-1 becomes zero. QED.

There is a different base-change behavior for the endomorphisms of K. Over the domain S the two branch eigenvalues differ by the nonzero element
\[
\lambda-1=s\Delta.
\]
A map between their rank-one S-modules must be killed by this difference and is therefore zero. Hence
\[
\operatorname{End}_{F}(K)
=M_{20}(S)\times M_4(S)\times M_{16}(S)\times M_4(S)

has rank688. At v=1 the branch lines both have Frobenius one, so the last two blocks combine into M20 of the residue field and the dimension becomes816. It does not contradict the absence of a jump in (C13), whose source J stays cyclic rather than becoming a sum of two scalar lines.

There is also an exact comparison of the other action:
\[
\operatorname{End}_{F}(K)\ \longrightarrow\
\operatorname{End}_{A}(M),\qquad g\longmapsto(u\longmapsto g\circ u)
\tag{C15a}
\]
is an isomorphism, over S and after every coefficient base change. On the scalar factors this is immediate. On the C factor it is the elementary matrix-Morita identity

## 4. Smooth proper coefficient geometry on a projective line

The construction has an ordinary coherent realization, not only a matrix description. Let T be the affine coordinate on the projective line over S. Put
\[
Z=V((T-1)(T-\lambda))\subset\mathbf P^1_S.
\tag{C16}
\]
This is a finite flat degree-two divisor, wholly inside the affine line and also inside the multiplicative group because lambda is a unit. The homomorphism
\[
S[T,T^{-1}]/((T-1)(T-\lambda))
 \longrightarrow C,\qquad T\longmapsto1+s x
\tag{C17}
\]
is an isomorphism: its inverse sends x to s inverse(T-1), and the polynomial relation becomes s squared times x(x-Delta). In particular the two-point divisor retains its nonreduced collision fiber.

Let O_a denote the structure sheaf of the section T=a, and O_Z the degree-two divisor sheaf. Define perfect coherent objects
\[
\mathscr H=O_{-1}\oplus O_i\oplus O_Z^{\oplus2},
\]
\[
\mathscr K=O_{-1}^{20}\oplus O_i^4
\oplus O_1^{16}\oplus O_\lambda^4.
\tag{C18}
\]
Each section has its standard Cartier resolution by O(-1) and O; Z has the resolution by O(-2) and O. Hence all these sheaves are perfect. The ambient projective line is smooth and proper over S.

All supports are finite over the affine base. Their derived global sections are therefore their finite free modules in degree zero, namely H and K. The sheaf Hom groups can be computed on the affine chart containing their supports. Thus
\[
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf P^1_S}(\mathscr H)=A,\qquad
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf P^1_S}(\mathscr H,\mathscr K)=M,
\tag{C19}
\]
with exactly the same named action. Multiplication by the coordinate T on these supported sheaves gives the extra linear operators in (C12).

This is smooth proper geometry over the characteristic-zero parameter/coefficient base S. It is not thereby a variety over F_q whose geometric Frobenius is multiplication by T. The evaluation v=q^{1/2} takes place in a characteristic-zero coefficient field E. It cannot simultaneously mean the same characteristic-p base evaluation, where the integer q becomes zero while v is required to be invertible. The coherent construction and the added linear Weil data are separate structures.

### Both crossed extensions retain the collision order

The standard section resolution is
\[
0\longrightarrow O(-1)\xrightarrow{T-a}O
\longrightarrow O_a\longrightarrow0.
\]
After applying Hom into O_b, its two terms are S and the differential is multiplication by b-a. Higher cohomology of these Hom terms vanishes because they are supported on a section over affine Spec(S). Consequently
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(O_a,O_b)=\operatorname{ann}_S(b-a),\qquad
\operatorname{Ext}^1(O_a,O_b)=S/(b-a),
\tag{C20}
\]
and there are no higher Ext groups. For a=b, these groups are both S. The differences between -1,i and either of1,lambda are units by (C14). The sole nonunit cross difference is lambda-1=sDelta, giving S/(Delta) in both branch directions. Therefore

## 6. Weil representations versus continuous Galois representations

Here a finite Weil representation on a point means a finite-dimensional E-space with a specified invertible linear geometric Frobenius. The point Weil group is infinite cyclic with its discrete topology, so this data always define a Weil representation.

Extending it to a continuous representation of the profinite Galois group is an additional condition. For a matrix over a finite extension E/Q_l, such an extension exists if and only if every eigenvalue is an l-adic unit.

For necessity, a continuous image of the compact Galois group is compact. Both positive and negative powers of Frobenius are then bounded, forcing every eigenvalue to have absolute value one in the l-adic norm. For sufficiency, pass to a finite coefficient extension containing the eigenvalues and choose Jordan form. A Jordan matrix with unit eigenvalues and its inverse preserve a suitable integral lattice; the binomial coefficients in all integer powers are integral. Its powers therefore lie in a compact matrix group. Modulo each lattice congruence subgroup the cyclic image is finite, and the maps extend compatibly from Z to its profinite completion. Conjugating back gives the desired continuous representation. Descent is not needed here because the coefficient field may be enlarged finitely.

The scalar roots -1,i,1 are units at every place. For each fixed q, lambda is algebraic and fails to be a unit at only finitely many finite places. Thus one may choose l different from the characteristic and outside that finite bad set, obtaining genuine continuous l-adic representations with all the exact End/Hom identities above. No claim that every coefficient prime works, or that one fixed prime works uniformly for all q, is made.

For example at q=2, the lambda computed below is a unit at l=3, so the construction is continuous there. At l=5 it is not a unit: writing it as (81+7i)/(81-7i), the two possible residues i=2 and i=-2 modulo5 make exactly one numerator or denominator divisible by5. This illustrates why the distinction matters.

## 7. Exact failure of extension-wise parameter retuning

The otherwise exact pure model does not obey the proposed identification of formal quantum grading with arithmetic extension. Define lambda(q) by (C22), with D=q+2+q^{-1}. For q>1,
\[
\lambda(q)\ne1,\qquad
\Delta(q^n)\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\lambda(q^n)\longrightarrow i.
\tag{C24}
\]
If lambda(q) to the nth power equaled lambda(q^n) for every sufficiently large n, the ratios of consecutive terms on the right would tend to one, since their common limit i is nonzero. The ratio on the left is constantly lambda(q), forcing lambda(q)=1, a contradiction.

Thus extension of the Weil object at a fixed q, which raises its Frobenius eigenvalue to lambda(q)^n, is not parameter evaluation of a fresh object at q^n. The named branch is retained in the actual module comparison, so this is an exact incompatibility for that comparison law, not only an absence of a supplied proof. It is consistent with F42: on the fixed invariant module M the Frobenius is one and the actual operator traces remain fixed-parameter traces.

## 8. A concrete arithmetic-integrality boundary

At q=2,
\[
D=\frac92,\qquad \Delta=\frac7{81},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{81+7i}{81-7i}
       =\frac{3256+567i}{3305},
\]
\[
\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=\frac{6512}{3305}\notin\mathbf Z[1/2].
\tag{C25}
\]
The last fraction is reduced and its denominator has odd prime factors. If lambda were integral over Z[1/2], its conjugate and their sum would be integral over that ring. Its rational sum would then belong to Z[1/2], because this localization is integrally closed. Hence lambda is not integral away from2. No multiplication by an integer power of2 can repair that failure.

In particular this lambda cannot occur as an eigenvalue in a Tate twist of constant-coefficient cohomology of a smooth projective variety over F2. Deligne's Weil I, Theorem(1.6), gives a monic integral characteristic polynomial in that setting; a Tate twist only introduces powers of2 into denominators. The same elementary exclusion applies to any proposed realization whose eigenvalues are required to be integral over Z[1/2]. This report uses the precisely verified smooth-projective source statement and does not require a broader unsourced integrality theorem.
