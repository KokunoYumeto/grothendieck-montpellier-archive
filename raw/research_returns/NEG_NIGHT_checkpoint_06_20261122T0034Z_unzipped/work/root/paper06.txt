1     Introduction
Let
                                      S = G(Z) = Z ⊔ {τ }
be the globalization semiring of the integers. The identity

                       F1 (s) = ζ(s) − 1,     ζ(ρ) = 0 =⇒ F1 (ρ) = −1,

shows that every zeta zero hits the same supported unit of S. Multiplication by that unit fixes
exactly the Boolean pair A = {0, τ }. Therefore every zero has the same pointwise split-zero
shadow.
   The mathematical content begins when one refuses to stop there. A pointwise constant
shadow is too small to remember even the multiplicities of the zeros, and it says nothing
about the local jet of ζ at a zero, the effect of compactifying the ambient line to P1 (C), or the
precise relation between the Boolean semiring geometry and the classical nilpotent thickening
C[s]/(s − ρ)m .
   The present paper replaces the pointwise shadow by three exact geometric objects.

 (1) The Boolean divisor sheaf VD , whose stalk over a zero of multiplicity m is the Boolean
     cube Am .

 (2) The Artin jet sheaf JD and the derivative-enhanced jet sheaf KD , which carry the
     actual local nilpotent structure.

 (3) The compactified tail object at infinity, which measures the difference between direct
     image and extension by zero after one-point compactification.

    Every negative statement in the paper is made in an explicitly named category and
is accompanied by a positive theorem describing the actual relation. Thus, when we say
that the classical thickening and the Boolean shadow are not isomorphic, we prove the non-
isomorphism in Sh(C, CMon) and then construct a pointed-set retraction. Likewise, when
we say that j! VD and j∗ VD differ after compactification, we prove the failure of isomorphism
in Sh(P1 (C), PtSet∗ ) and then identify the exact tail stalk causing the defect.
    Three bodies of literature are relevant background. Semiring and idempotent methods,
especially over the Boolean semiring and max-plus type semirings, supply the natural
algebraic setting for A-semimodules [2, 3, 1]. Lorscheid’s theory of blueprints gives a
common enlargement containing semirings and monoids and provides a scheme-theoretic
home for Boolean and F1 -style objects [6]. Finally, ordinary sheaf theory and zero-dimensional
thickenings provide the classical comparison side [8, 5, 7]. None of the core results below
depends on a black-box theorem from these sources; the arguments are carried out directly.


2     Globalization semirings and split-root fixed loci
Definition 2.1. Let R be a commutative ring with identity. Its globalization semiring is

                                       G(R) := R ⊔ {τ },

with operations

                   a ⊕ b := a + b,       a ⊕ τ = τ ⊕ a := a,      τ ⊕ τ := τ,

                    a ⊙ b := ab,        a ⊙ τ = τ ⊙ a := τ,      τ ⊙ τ := τ.
Thus τ is the additive identity and multiplicative absorber, while 1 ∈ R ⊂ G(R) is the
multiplicative identity.

                   χR : G(R) → B,          χR (r) = 1 (r ∈ R),    χR (τ ) = 0.

These are exactly the Boolean addition and multiplication tables under the displayed identifi-

Corollary 2.6 (zeta pointwise shadow). Let

                              S = G(Z),          F1 (s) = ζ(s) − 1.

If ρ is any zero of the Riemann zeta function, then

                         F1 (ρ) = −1,      FixS (F1 (ρ)) = {0, τ } ∼
                                                                   = B.

Moreover −1 is the unique unit of exact order 2 in S.

Proof. The identity F1 (ρ) = −1 is immediate from ζ(ρ) = 0. The fixed-locus formula follows
from Theorem 2.5 with R = Z and u = −1.
   If u ∈ Z× has order 2, then u2 = 1. Since Z× = {±1}, the only possibility with exact
order 2 is u = −1.

Remark 2.7. The algebraic pattern of G(Z) is the arithmetic analogue of the usual idempotent
or tropical construction in which one adjoins a bottom element serving simultaneously as
additive identity and multiplicative absorber. This is why the Boolean semiring naturally
appears as the support skeleton; compare [2, 3, 1].

Remark 3.10. Theorem 3.9 makes the F1 -style geometry completely explicit: each Boolean
stalk Am is not merely analogous to an affine Boolean cube, it is precisely the set of B-points of
affine m-space over B. In Lorscheid’s framework semiring schemes are among blue schemes [6];
hence the Boolean divisor sheaf can be read as a sheaf of B-point sets of affine blue-geometric
cubes.

8    Conclusion
The exact outcome is as follows.

  (1) The pointwise split-root shadow of a zeta zero is the Boolean pair {0, τ }, because every
      zeta zero hits the nontrivial unit −1 ∈ G(Z).

  (2) The global multiplicity-refined object is the Boolean divisor sheaf VDζ , not the reduced
      shadow ADζ alone. The reduced shadow is a split retract, and it coincides with the
      refined object if and only if every multiplicity equals 1.

  (3) The classical nonreduced thickening is the Artin sheaf JDζ . It is not isomorphic to the
      Boolean shadow in the additive category of commutative monoids, but the Boolean
      shadow is a retract of it in pointed sets.

  (4) The derivative-enhanced sheaf KDζ detects the leading nonzero jet. At a zero of order
      m, the actual zeta germ occupies exactly the terminal Boolean slot in Am+1 .

  (5) Compactification to P1 (C) does not create higher cohomology for the Artin jet sheaf,
      but it does create a genuine tail fibre at infinity, measured by product modulo direct
      sum. For the Boolean shadow, the analogous product-mod-finite-difference tail is the
      exact obstruction to j! = j∗ .


                                                 17
 (6) At the trivial zeros, the Boolean stalk is weighted by the exact analytic size |ζ ′ (−2n)|,
     with explicit closed formula.

    Thus the Boolean shadow of the zeta divisor is neither a mere restatement of the equality
F1 (ρ) = −1 nor a replacement for the classical nilpotent thickening. It is a rigorously defined
semiring-geometric retract of the nonreduced jet theory, represented stalkwise by affine
Boolean space, and compactified globally by a tail fibre at infinity.
