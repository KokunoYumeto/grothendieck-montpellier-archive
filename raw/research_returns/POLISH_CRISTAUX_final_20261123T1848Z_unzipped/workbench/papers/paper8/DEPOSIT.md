## Title

Congruence sections and non-split co-Lie complexes after Godeaux and Serre

## Subject

Algebraic geometry in positive characteristic; representations of finite groups; finite flat group schemes and deformation obstructions.

## Keywords

Congruence sections; modular representations; co-Lie complexes; lifting obstructions; Godeaux–Serre construction; finite étale monodromy; smooth projective surfaces.

## Abstract

The classical linear congruence-section classifications are due to Sah and Ginosar, and Detinko, Flannery, and Hulpke prove the higher-rank symplectic nonsupplement theorem. We give elementary proofs of the complete section classifications for reduction from $\mathbb Z/p^2$ to $\mathbf F_p$ in general linear, special linear, and symplectic groups. Explicit small-prime obstructions and finite subgroups handle every exceptional case. We then use the Godeaux--Serre construction to realize a nonlifting representation as the nonzero extension class of a co-Lie complex on a smooth projective surface. The identification of the co-Lie complex is due to Bergh and Rydh. Explicit invariant equations and a coherent cohomology injection give full general-linear monodromy in every nonsplit case, while the class in the Grothendieck group of perfect complexes is zero. For primes at least five, rank two is enough. Dimension two is minimal: every such co-Lie complex on a smooth projective curve splits. The mathematics and text were produced and checked by GPT-6 Astra (ChatGPT Pro mode), including separate same-model derivations; no human has checked them.

## Main results

Theorem 1.1 realizes every nonlifting finite-group representation as a non-split co-Lie complex on a smooth projective surface, with full prescribed monodromy and zero K_0 class. Theorem 1.2 gives complete, classical reduction-section classifications for GL, SL and Sp over Z/p^2, including every small-prime case. Corollary 1.3 applies the surface theorem exactly in the nonsplit general-linear cases: p=2 and d>=4, p=3 and d>=3, or p>=5 and d>=2. Theorem 1.4 proves splitting on every smooth projective curve and the minimum dimension two.

The GL split list is d=1 or (d,p)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2). The SL split list is d=1 or (d,p)=(2,3),(3,2). For positive symplectic rank g, only (g,p)=(1,3) splits. The determinant/form-preserving assertions are carefully distinguished from lifting the underlying representation.

## Sources and known status

The congruence classifications are classical; no novelty claim is made. Singla's 2010 institutional thesis, printed page 3, states the general-linear list and credits Sah and Ginosar. Stasinski's arXiv v2 Section 6 attributes the special-linear result to Sah 1974 Theorem 7 and gives the p>=5 transvection proof. Detinko–Flannery–Hulpke's arXiv v4 Theorem 2.5 proves the absence of a proper supplement in higher-rank symplectic groups. Its exceptional proper-supplement list is not confused with a splitting list.
