## DIV1. A proper parameter space for a bounded signed representative

**Setup.** Let `f:X -> S` be a smooth projective morphism over a smooth irreducible complex algebraic variety of finite type. Its fibres have dimension `g`. Fix a relatively very ample line bundle `H`, an integer `0 <= p <= g`, and a global flat section `t` of `R^{2p}f_* Q(p)`. Put `r=g-p`. For a complex point `s`, let `c_t(s)` be the least integer `C >= 1` for which there are an integer `1 <= N <= C` and effective integral `r`-cycles `Z+`, `Z-` on `X_s`, each of `H`-degree at most `C`, such that

`N t_s = cl(Z+) - cl(Z-)`.

Set `c_t(s)=infinity` if no such representative exists. Zero cycles in the sense of the zero element of the cycle group are allowed among the effective cycles, including when `r>0`.

**Statement.** For each integer `C`, the subset `S_C={s : c_t(s)<=C}` is Zariski closed. Consequently:

1. A bound on any Zariski-dense subset gives the same bound on all of `S`.
2. If `t_s` is algebraic at every complex point, then `S_C=S` for some `C`.
3. The same conclusion holds if `t_s` is algebraic outside a countable union of proper Zariski-closed subsets.

Thus algebraicity on the entire family is equivalent to bounded signed integral complexity on a dense subset. This conclusion supplies existence of a bound, not an algorithm for computing it.

**Proof.** We spell out the finiteness which the informal reference to a bounded-degree Chow scheme suppresses. Grothendieck, *Techniques de construction et théorèmes d'existence en géométrie algébrique IV: Les schémas de Hilbert*, Séminaire Bourbaki, exposé 221 (1960/61), Lemme 2.4, printed p.255, states that the reduced, pure-dimensional closed subschemes of fibres of a projective family with bounded degree form a bounded family. Applying it in dimension `r` and degree at most `C`, and stratifying a finite-type parameter space by flatness, gives a finite list of Hilbert polynomials `P_1,...,P_k` containing those of all reduced irreducible subvarieties which can occur as components of these cycles. Each corresponding relative Hilbert scheme is projective over `S`, by the same exposé, Théorème 3.2, printed p.260.

Every nonzero effective cycle of degree at most `C` is a sum `sum_i m_i[V_i]`, where `V_i` is reduced and irreducible of dimension `r`, `m_i` is a positive integer, there are at most `C` summands, and `m_i<=C`. Indeed every component has positive integral degree. There are only finitely many lists of the polynomials and multiplicities with total degree at most `C`. For each list take the corresponding fibre product over `S` of Hilbert schemes. Include `S` itself to parameterize the zero cycle. This gives finitely many projective parameter schemes whose universal top-dimensional fundamental cycles include every desired effective cycle. Additional points with nonreduced schemes or lower-dimensional embedded components cause no problem: the top-dimensional fundamental cycle is still effective and its degree is the leading coefficient of the fixed Hilbert polynomial, multiplied by `r!`.

Take pairs of these parameter schemes for the two signs. On each such finite-type projective parameter scheme `T -> S`, the class of the difference of the two universal fundamental cycles is a flat section of the pulled-back cohomology local system. The following argument verifies this also when `T` is singular or nonreduced. For each universal subscheme `Z -> T`, its structure sheaf is coherent and `T`-flat, and the ambient morphism `X_T -> T` is smooth. Thus `O_Z` is an absolutely perfect complex on `X_T`: locally the ambient ring is flat over the base with regular fibres of bounded dimension, and a base-flat coherent module has finite Tor amplitude over that ambient ring. Precisely this assertion is Stacks Project, Lemma 15.79.5, tag `068X`, with base Tor amplitude `[0,0]`. It is absolute perfection on `X_T`, rather than only relative perfection over `T`, that is used here.

One may work over affine opens of `S`; then `X_T` is quasi-projective, so the perfect complex admits a bounded locally free resolution and defines a topological Chern character on `X_T(C)`. Derived restriction to any fibre is the ordinary `O_{Z_t}` because of `T`-flatness. Its component `ch_p(O_{Z_t})` equals the Betti class of the top-dimensional fundamental cycle `[Z_t]_r`, including its multiplicities. An exact algebraic locator is Fulton, *Intersection Theory*, second edition, Theorem 18.3(3),(5), printed p.353, together with Example 18.3.11, printed p.363: the latter identifies the top term of the Riemann--Roch class with the associated cycle of any coherent sheaf; on a smooth ambient fibre the Riemann--Roch class is `ch(O_{Z_t}) td(TX_t) cap [X_t]`, and the Todd factor has constant term one. Lower-dimensional support components do not contribute in codimension `p`.

Therefore all the fibre classes in question are restrictions of one topological class `ch_p(O_Z)` on `X_T(C)`. The smooth proper family `X -> S` is locally a differentiable fibre bundle; its pullback to `T(C)` is topologically locally trivial. Restricting a global cohomology class in such a family gives a flat section, since the fibre inclusions over any path in a trivializing neighborhood are homotopic. Complex algebraic spaces are locally path connected, and their finitely many connected components agree in the analytic and algebraic topologies. The argument also treats nilpotents because passing to the reduction does not alter the underlying analytic topological space. Taking sums and differences gives the asserted flat section for the universal signed cycle.

For any fixed `N` between 1 and `C`, equality of this section with `N t` therefore selects a union of connected components of `T`. There are finitely many such components, each open and closed. The selected union is projective over `S`. Its image is Zariski closed. A finite union over the parameter schemes and `N` is precisely `S_C`, proving the first assertion. A closed subset containing a Zariski-dense subset is all of `S`, which proves (1).

Algebraicity of a rational class at a point is equivalent to finite `c_t(s)`: clear all coefficients of an algebraic rational cycle and split the resulting integral cycle by sign. Hence algebraicity at all points says `S(C)=union_{C>=1} S_C(C)`. If every `S_C` were proper, choose a small closed analytic ball with nonempty interior in `S(C)`. Each `S_C` meets that ball in a closed nowhere-dense set, since a proper algebraic subset of an irreducible smooth complex variety contains no analytic open subset. The Baire category theorem rules out a countable covering of the ball by these sets. Thus some `S_C=S`. The same argument, adding the given countably many exceptional proper closed subsets to the purported covering, proves (3). QED.

**Scope and Grothendieck locator.** This is a statement about relative algebraic cycles and their Betti realization. It uses FGA 221, Lemme 2.4 and Théorème 3.2 for boundedness and properness. The analogous cycle classes in any compatible Weil realization are transported by the same algebraic correspondences, but a new assertion of integral Tate surjectivity is not part of the statement. It proves the precise form needed from sketch Route 2, statement 2.1; it does not use André–Oort or any asserted density of a particular Hecke orbit.

## DIV2. Bounded witnesses on a dense set suffice in a Weil family

**Statement.** Let `S` be a smooth irreducible complex algebraic variety of finite type, as in DIV1. Let `A -> S` be a smooth projective family of polarized abelian varieties of dimension `2n` and Weil type for a fixed imaginary quadratic order, with globally defined order action, fixed polarization type and a fixed rational flat Weil class `t`. A finite base change may be made to define the action and the class. Assume there is a constant `D_0` and a Zariski-dense subset `T` such that for every `s in T` there is an effective integral `n`-cycle `Z_s` of degree at most `D_0` whose Weil component is nonzero. Then `c_t` is bounded on all of `S`. An explicit bound on `T`, and therefore on `S`, is the ceiling of the maximum of the two quadratic expressions in B8 evaluated at `D=D_0`, together with 1.

**Proof.** Apply B6–B8 with the fixed generic quadratic action. Their projector denominator, signed-degree multiplier, polarization volume and Weil-lattice discriminant are constant on the family; so is the norm of a fixed flat multiple of `t`. B8 produces, for each witness, an integral multiple of `t_s` and signed effective representatives with the displayed uniform quadratic bounds. This gives `T subset S_C` for one integer `C`. DIV1 gives `S_C=S`. If the polarization is only relatively ample, replace it by a fixed relatively very ample power; degrees change by the fixed factor equal to that power raised to `n`, so boundedness is equivalent. QED.

The conclusion removes the need to control denominators of a projector defined using each point's larger CM field. It leaves a geometric requirement: uniformly bounded effective witnesses with a nonzero component. It does not prove that requirement from the existence of rational cycles on each CM fibre.

## DIV3. What a successful semiregular cycle construction would prove

**Statement.** In the setting of DIV1, with `1 <= p <= g`, suppose at one point `s_0` there are smooth regularly embedded subvarieties `Y_1,...,Y_k` of codimension `p` and fixed integers `a_j,N`, `N>=1`, with `N t_{s_0}=sum_j a_j cl(Y_j)`. Suppose each embedding is Bloch semiregular and the flat continuation of **each full class** `cl(Y_j)` is of Hodge type on an analytic neighborhood of `s_0`. Then `c_t` is bounded on all of `S`.

**Proof.** Apply Buchweitz–Flenner, *A semiregularity map for modules and applications to deformations*, Compositio Math.137 (2003), Theorem 5.2, p.175, with the singleton set of indices `I={p}`. The hypotheses are a deformation of a compact complex algebraic central manifold over a smooth complex germ, a horizontal Hodge section `alpha_p`, and an `I`-semiregular subspace with `alpha_p(s_0)=ch_p(O_Y)`. Our smooth projective family over the analytic germ of `S` meets the ambient hypotheses. For a regular codimension-`p` immersion, `T^2_{Y/X}(O_Y)=H^1(Y,N_{Y/X})`, `ch_p(O_Y)=[Y]`, and Proposition 8.2, p.200, identifies the component `tau_{p-1}` with Bloch's map. Thus Bloch semiregularity is exactly the required `I`-semiregularity, and preservation of this full fundamental class is sufficient; no other component of `ch(O_Y)` is required.

The proof of Theorem 5.2, p.180, invokes the same lifting argument as the proof of Theorem 5.1, p.179, using the subspace obstruction maps of §§4.11 and 4.13 in place of the sheaf maps. That argument constructs formal lifts over the infinitesimal neighborhoods of the smooth base germ, uses a versal deformation, and applies Artin approximation to obtain an actual local flat deformation. For subspaces this is a local section of the relative Douady space, equivalently of the analytification of the relative Hilbert scheme in the present projective setting. Apply it separately to every `Y_j` and intersect the finitely many parameter neighborhoods. The local extensions have the original Hilbert polynomials.

The degrees of the fundamental cycles of these extensions are constant, because their Hilbert polynomials are fixed. Their cohomology classes are the flat continuations of the original classes, so the displayed signed equality with `N t` persists. Splitting the fixed integers `a_j` by sign gives effective representatives of degrees at most `sum_j |a_j| deg_H(Y_j)`, uniformly on a nonempty analytic open set. It is Zariski dense in `S`. DIV1 therefore extends the bound to every fibre. Equivalently, the finite product of the relevant projective Hilbert schemes has a component dominating that analytic open set; its proper image is all of `S`. QED.

**Necessary distinctions.** Preserving only `sum_j a_j cl(Y_j)` does not meet the stated hypotheses. Semiregularity of a perfect complex is likewise a statement about its Atiyah-trace map and the Chern components detected by that map; it is not supplied by its `K_0` class. A projective bounded parameter space or an independent local-to-cycle argument is needed when a sheaf or complex replaces the embeddings. S-01–S-07 show why the original rigid 032 ball quotient does not directly satisfy these conditions on a varying generated abelian family.

## DIV4. An explicit upper bound for the natural Hecke representative

**Statement.** Let `psi:A_x -> A_0` be an isogeny of polarized abelian `g`-folds, with equal polarization type and `psi^*L_0=m L_x` at the level of first Chern classes, where `m` is a positive integer. Suppose `psi^* t_0=(a/b)t_x`, with coprime integers `a!=0`, `b>0`. If `N_0t_0=cl(Z_0^+)-cl(Z_0^-)` and both degrees are at most `D_0`, then

`c_t(x) <= max(N_0 |a|, b m^p D_0)`.

**Proof.** Isogenies in characteristic zero are finite flat, so flat pullback of each effective integral cycle is effective integral. Pulling back the given equality and multiplying by `b` gives `N_0 a t_x=b(psi^*cl(Z_0^+)-psi^*cl(Z_0^-))`. Reverse the signs if `a<0`. B4 computes the degree of each pullback as `m^p` times the old degree. This gives exactly the bound asserted. QED.

This upper bound is for a specified representative. It is not a lower bound for the minimum `c_t(x)`. When `a/b=m^p`, normalizing the rational pullback keeps its numerical degree fixed while its displayed denominator grows. T6.4–T6.5 cancel auxiliary source covers and target multiplication for specially normalized correspondence constructions; they do not cancel an arbitrary Hecke transport between different target fibres.

## DIV5. What the known fourfold theorem already supplies

**Statement.** On any fixed finite-type family of complex abelian fourfolds and any global rational flat Hodge class, the signed integral complexity is uniformly bounded, provided one admits the supplied Markman theorem of the rational Hodge conjecture for all abelian fourfolds. The same conclusion applies to any higher-dimensional family for which the supplied theorem proves algebraicity of the particular flat class on every fibre.

**Proof.** The admitted theorem supplies algebraicity pointwise. If the base is smooth and irreducible, DIV1(2) supplies a uniform bound. For a general finite-type base, choose a finite stratification of its reduction into smooth irreducible locally closed complex subvarieties, apply DIV1(2) to the restricted family and flat class on each stratum, and take the maximum of the finitely many resulting bounds. Fibres over the original base and its reduction are identical at complex points. QED.

This bound is a posteriori and ineffective in this argument. It does not calculate a theta degree, identify a Rallis factor, or extend the known fourfold theorem to the open Weil families in sketch T8. The exact supplied Markman locators are Theorem 1.5.1 (`thm-algebraicity`), proving algebraicity of the Hodge--Weil classes on polarized abelian sixfolds of Weil type for an arbitrary imaginary quadratic field and discriminant `-1`, and Corollary 1.6.1, asserting the Hodge conjecture for all abelian fourfolds. These numbers were checked from the source's subsection-based shared theorem counter after removing comments and hidden draft blocks; the sixfold assertion must not be broadened to arbitrary discriminant or arbitrary Hodge classes.
