## 1. Geometric premise and exact conclusion

Let A be a complex abelian surface with End(A)=Z and a primitive polarization of type (1,2). The genus-five construction considered here starts with a genus-three curve D and its elliptic quotient, with the complementary abelian surface of type (1,2), and an étale double cover F→D obtained from a degree-two principally polarized isogeny B→A. We assume the principally polarized Prym of F/D is the indicated B, with the compatible lattice markings described below. This is the precise Prym premise in the Clingher–Malmendier–Shaska construction, not an assumption that every conceivable genus-five map has this shape.

Under this premise, **every actual composite A→J(F)→A is even**. More precisely the ideal of all such integer scalars is either 4Z or 2Z, depending on the marking. All 720 possible symplectic mod-two markings give 4Z in 672 cases and 2Z in 48 cases. Thus this particular family of full-span genus-five curves does not remove the residual factor two, even if arbitrary integral homomorphisms through its Jacobian are allowed. This conclusion is stronger than computing only the class of the displayed curve, but does not constrain arbitrary Jacobians.

The primary source is Clingher, Malmendier, and Shaska, *Geometry of Prym varieties for certain bielliptic curves of genus three and five* (7 September 2021 author version), <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04839v2>. The complete PDF was opened; diagram(1.3) p3 and Theorem1.2 p5 were read. Its letters differ from ours. The ramified-Prym quotient convention must not be confused with the principally polarized étale Prym used below. The proof below is an independent integral lattice computation under that exact premise.

## 2. The genus-three lattice

Write Λ=H₁(A,Z)=Z(e₁,f₁,e₂,f₂), with alternating polarization form

`E_L=diag(J,2J)`, where `J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]`.

Its dual lattice is `Λ#=Z(e₁,f₁,e₂/2,f₂/2)`. Let u,v be a symplectic elliptic basis. On the rational decomposition `H₁(J(D),Q)=H₁(A,Q)⊕H₁(E,Q)`, the principal form is `2E_L⊕2J`. An integral symplectic basis is

`a₁=(e₁+u)/2, b₁=v, a₂=(f₁−v)/2, b₂=−e₁, a₃=e₂/2, b₃=f₂/2`.

Direct pairing gives one in each pair and zero between distinct pairs. The projection J(D)→A acts as multiplication by two on the A coordinate. The duality calculation uses this principal form; it does not treat the nonprincipal E_L as a unimodular form.

Choose the degree-two principally polarized cover B→A with lattice `Λ_B=Z(2e₁,f₁,e₂,f₂)`. The restricted form E_L/2 is principal on this lattice. All changes of its symplectic marking are represented by

`P=diag(2,1,1,1) G`, with `G∈Sp₄(Z)`.

Write P_j for its columns. The character of the cover detects the e₁ coordinate; on J(D), it is nontrivial on a₁ and trivial on the remaining displayed symplectic basis vectors.

## 3. The genus-five lattice

Use the rational decomposition `H₁(J(F),Q)=H₁(A,Q)⊕H₁(B,Q)⊕H₁(E,Q)`, identifying the B vector space with that of A via its isogeny. The principal polarization form is

`4E_L ⊕ E_L ⊕ 4J`.

An integral lattice is generated by

`A₁=(e₁/2,0,u/2), B₁=(0,0,v/2)`,

`a₂±=(f₁/4, ±P₁/2, −v/4), b₂±=(−e₁/2, ±P₂/2,0)`,

`a₃±=(e₂/4, ±P₃/2,0), b₃±=(f₂/4, ±P₄/2,0)`.

This is the standard integral transfer/difference lattice of the étale double cover with the chosen character, glued to its marked principally polarized Prym. In particular the factor two in the transfer and the factor four in the invariant form are retained. Omitting either factor changes the answer incorrectly.

Since A is simple and End(A)=Z, its rational homomorphisms to either A-vector-space factor are scalar and its homomorphisms to E vanish. Therefore every rational A→J(F) is `(a,b,0)` and every rational J(F)→A is `(c,d,0)`.

## 4. All incoming integral maps

Testing an incoming map against the dual lattice under the principal form amounts to testing `(4a,b)` against the E_L-dual conditions. The resulting necessary and sufficient form is

`(a,b)=(x/2,y)`, with x,y integers satisfying

`x+yG₂₁=0 (mod2)`, and `yG₂j=0 (mod2)` for j=2,3,4.

These conditions are not merely necessary for a convenient pair of maps: they describe the full integral Hom lattice. The use of the principal dual lattice is essential; testing only the visible A projection would miss the half-integral incoming coordinate.

## 5. All outgoing integral maps

Testing every displayed lattice generator shows that an outgoing integral map has the form `(c,d)=(2u,v)` for integers u,v with

`u f₁+vP₁=0`, `vP₂=0`, `u e₂+vP₃=0`, `u f₂+vP₄=0` modulo2.

The first factor two follows already from A₁. The remaining congruences arise from the four plus/minus pairs. Again this is the full integral Hom lattice. The composite of an incoming and an outgoing map is the scalar

`xu+yv`.

## 6. Parity and the exact ideals

If v is even, the outgoing equations force u even, so the composite is even. If v is odd, the equations force u odd and imply that the second row of G is `(1,0,0,0)` modulo2. The incoming equations then force x≡y, so `xu+yv≡x+y=0`.

If row₂(G) differs from `(1,0,0,0)`, the incoming conditions force both x and y even; the outgoing conditions force both u and v even. Each lattice is precisely 2Z², so the scalar ideal is 4Z.

If row₂(G) equals `(1,0,0,0)`, incoming `(x,y)=(1,1)` is allowed. Outgoing `(u,v)=(2,0)` is always allowed, giving the scalar two. The parity proof gives the reverse divisibility, so the ideal is exactly 2Z. We do **not** need to claim that the row condition guarantees an outgoing vector with v odd; that stronger assertion is unnecessary and was deliberately not used.

The full mod-two symplectic group has 720 elements. Exactly 48 have the indicated second row, giving the 48/672 split. The program enumerates all matrices by their four columns, tests every symplectic pairing, constructs both inverse-image lattices from their mod-two residues together with 2Z², and computes the gcd of all pairwise scalar products of generators. This is an exact finite certificate of the stated gluing calculation. The arbitrary-marking proof above is independent of the enumeration.
