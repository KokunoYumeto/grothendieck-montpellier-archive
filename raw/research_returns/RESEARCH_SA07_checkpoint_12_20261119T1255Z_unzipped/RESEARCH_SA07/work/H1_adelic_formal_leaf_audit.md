## 1. Actual leaf and source maps

Use the algebraic universal vector extension G=E^dagger of an elliptic curve over a number field K, with algebraic invariant coframe (du,ds). Its analytic uniformization is C^2 modulo the lattice generated by (omega_i,eta_i). For a in K the invariant line L_a is s=a u and the analytic leaf is Theta_a(u)=[(u,a u)]. Its formal completion at the identity is an actual formal curve C_a in G, and in the projective compactification X=P_E(V) used in agent_elliptic.md section 32.

In the standard algebraic chart, D_a=y partial_x+(6x^2-g_2/2) partial_y-(x+a) partial_t and V=-partial_t. Existing section 31 gives D_a^p=H_p D_a-(K_p+a H_p)V. Thus the reduction is p-closed exactly when K_p+aH_p=0. Existing A.4.15 proves the leaf is Zariski dense in the surface G; A.4.32 proves that the weighted lower density beta_K(a) of p-closed places is at most 1-1/(4d), d=[K:Q]. These inputs are already proved in the main work; this note does not replace them.

The actual h quotient to the conjugate elliptic curve remains [(u,s)] maps to [(s-hu)/c] modulo the conjugate lattice. The differential factor 1/c is retained. None of the calculations below replaces that map by an algebraic map.

## 2. Exact local coefficient at a non-p-closed prime

Choose one etale coordinate pair X,Y at the identity over K with X(e)=Y(e)=0, D_a X(e)=1 and D_a Y(e)=0. Such a pair is obtained by an invertible K-linear change of any etale parameters, since D_a(e) is nonzero. Clearing fixed denominators gives the same integral coordinates and primitive invariant vector field outside a finite set of places. The formal leaf is Y=phi(X), phi(X)=sum b_n X^n, b_1=0. The actual etale map from an identity neighborhood in the smooth model to A^2 induces this formal graph isomorphism. Bost--Chambert-Loir Theorem 3.1(b), printed p10, proves equality of their sizes under this precise map.

Let v be a remaining absolutely unramified good place of residue characteristic p>3. The formal flow is X(t)=sum D_a^n X(e)t^n/n! and Y(t)=sum D_a^n Y(e)t^n/n!. All coefficients through degree p-1 are integral. The inverse of X(t) has integral coefficients through degree p-1. In composing Y with this inverse, its potentially nonintegral degree-p term cannot enter through the linear term of Y, because that term is zero. Every contribution involving lower degrees is integral. Consequently b_p equals D_a^pY(e)/p! modulo O_v.

Translation invariance implies that failure of p-closure of the line is equivalent to the transverse component of D_a^p(e) being nonzero. In these two coordinates this says D_a^pY(e) is a unit. Thus |b_p|_v=|p|_v^(-1), while b_n is integral for n<p.

The integral shear (X,Y) maps to (X,Y+X), with determinant one, changes the graph to X+phi(X), whose derivative is a unit, without changing geometric size. Its higher coefficients, in particular b_p, are unchanged. Analytic ODE existence gives positive convergence radius. Bost--Chambert-Loir Proposition 3.5(2) identifies the size with the minimum of 1 and exp(first Newton slope), which bounds it above by |b_p|_v^(-1/(p-1))=|p|_v^(1/(p-1)). Proposition 3.6 gives the reverse bound for every integral nonsingular rank-one foliation. Therefore

    S_v(C_a)=|p|_v^(1/(p-1))

at every such non-p-closed place. At a p-closed unramified good place Proposition 3.6 gives S_v(C_a)>=|p|_v^(1/(p(p-1))). These are sizes of the actual formal embedding, via etale coordinates and an integral shear.

## 3. Consequence for the actual adelic product

Use local absolute values |p|_v=p^(-[K_v:Q_p]). The preceding formulas and convergence of sum_p log(p)/(p(p-1)) give

    sum_(p_v<=T) log S_v(C_a)^(-1)
      = sum_(p_v<=T, non-p-closed) [K_v:Q_p] log(p)/(p-1) + O(1).

The remainder is bounded independently of T. Removing finitely many places changes only that bounded remainder. A.4.32 implies the upper weighted harmonic density of the complementary non-p-closed places is at least 1/(4d). Their harmonic sum is therefore unbounded. The geometric-size product is zero. Thus the actual formal leaf for every algebraic a fails the A-analyticity requirement in Bost--Chambert-Loir Definition 3.7. This identifies the exact failed hypothesis of their Theorem 6.1 and Theorem 7.9, rather than merely recording that it was not verified.

At the archimedean place, the actual entire uniformization Theta_a gives canonical tangent norm zero by their Proposition 5.15 applied to discs of arbitrarily large radius. That observation does not restore the missing positive finite-place product.

## 4. Positive lower bound for André's tau

Normalize instead |p|_v=p^(-epsilon_v), epsilon_v=[K_v:Q_p]/d, as in André section 5.1. For the fixed graph series phi, section 5.3 defines tau(phi) as the infimum over finite prime thresholds P_0 of

    limsup_(n->infty) (1/n) sum_(p_v>=P_0) max_(m<=n) log^+|b_m|_v.

At every non-p-closed good place with p<=n, b_p contributes epsilon_v log p. Write theta_B(T)=sum_(p_v<=T, non-p-closed) epsilon_v log p. Hence tau(phi)>=limsup theta_B(T)/T. Fixed finite prime thresholds do not change this inequality.

Partial summation gives H_B(T)=theta_B(T)/T+integral_2^T theta_B(t)/t^2 dt+O(1), where H_B uses weights epsilon_v log p/(p-1); replacing p-1 by p contributes a bounded sum. Therefore limsup H_B(T)/log T <= limsup theta_B(T)/T. By A.4.32 the left side is at least 1/(4d). Thus the draft quantitative conclusion is

    tau(phi) >= delta_upper(B) >= 1/(4d) > 0,

where delta_upper(B)=limsup theta_B(T)/T is André's weighted upper natural density (his Lemma 5.1.1, printed pp70--71).

This tests and disproves the zero-tau hypothesis for this actual graph in André Theorem 5.4.3. No equality of tau with a connection invariant is claimed. No lower density for the non-p-closed places is claimed: the argument uses only upper densities and limsup throughout.
