## 2. Joint derived group and the exact forgetting kernel

Let H be the connected joint derived group. Its map into the product of the individual derived groups is a closed embedding because those individual representations together form its faithful representation. Its projections are surjective because the projections of the joint MT group are surjective and passage to derived groups preserves surjectivity.

Over Qbar take H^sc and its simple factors. A simple target factor of any individual H_i receives the images of commuting connected normal subgroups of H^sc. Exactly one source simple factor maps nontrivially, and it surjects. Its kernel is finite central, and the target is simply connected, so the map is an isomorphism. Every source factor is detected by at least one target, since otherwise it would be killed by the faithful total representation. An element of the kernel H^sc to H therefore has trivial coordinate in every simple source factor. This proves that the cover has trivial kernel and H itself is simply connected. This argument checks the full central kernel, not only the Lie algebra.

The same reasoning on Q-simple factors shows that every factor of H is isomorphic to a noncompact-real Q-simple factor of one of the individual groups. For a forgetting map H_n to H_(n-1), the intrinsic simple factors are partitioned into the kernel set and the set mapping isomorphically onto a target factor. No two source factors can surject onto the same target factor, since their images commute. Both sets are Galois stable. Their products descend, giving H_n=L_n times C_n and the rational section j_n of the forgetting map. Thus L_n is connected and simply connected, and all its Q-simple factors have noncompact real points. This is stronger than a splitting up to finite central isogeny, and is exactly what the later approximation uses.

## 4. Complex conjugation at every local simple factor

The block map C comes from actual algebraic isogenies after conjugation. Its naturality equation is consequently an equation for the actual Tate representations. Its square is blockwise an algebraic endomorphism and therefore centralizes M, while C h(z) C^-1=h(bar(z)). Hence its induced theta is involutive on M and H.

On a theta-fixed Q-simple factor S, theta cannot be the identity. Otherwise its adjoint quotient sends h(z) and h(bar(z)) to the same element. On the unit circle these are inverse, so connectedness makes the adjoint Hodge map trivial; on the positive real weight torus it is already central. The projected Hodge map would therefore be trivial, contrary to the defining MT generation of that nontrivial adjoint factor. This proof uses the actual quotient M to S^ad.

The local refinement is valid. Writing S=Res_F/Q T, a fixed local factor with nontrivial action on its centroid F_v has a nontrivial local automorphism. Identity on F_v would imply identity on the faithfully embedded F. If theta fixes F, an F-automorphism which becomes identity over F_v was already identity over F. The remaining local factors are paired by theta. Outside finitely many primes the nonzero matrices of 1-d(theta) retain a nonzero reduction, with the centroid permutation kept.

At a good fixed factor, the claim that all g theta(g)^-1 are central would give a homomorphism from the perfect finite group to its center, hence theta would fix every finite point. Because theta is induced by the actual linear conjugation C on the representation, its effect on the root matrices retains their bounded polynomial degree; interpolation recovers the Lie generators. For a residue-field automorphism, allowing all residue parameters in those same root matrices detects the nontrivial centroid action. Thus a noncentral finite commutator exists. For a swapped pair, the second commutator with an independently supported first-factor element isolates that factor exactly.

A finite product of these curves has invertible derivative and yields an open neighborhood by the p-adic inverse-function argument. Swapped factors use the exact second commutator and the tangent vectors (1-Ad(h))X. Thus no split local model or diagonalizability of C is assumed.
