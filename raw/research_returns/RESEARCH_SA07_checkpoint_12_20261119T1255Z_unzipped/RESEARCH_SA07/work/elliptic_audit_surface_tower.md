## 2. Actual groups, multiplier and Hodge maximality

The non-CM absolutely simple surface alternatives are exactly the
three listed in AS-1. In particular, the apparent imaginary-quadratic
endomorphism alternative has not been silently omitted: Lombardo's
proof of Corollary4.5 explicitly excludes it for an absolutely simple
surface in characteristic zero. Non-simple surfaces are included later
by the actual geometric product-isogeny construction.

For RM the trace pairing determines Psi uniquely. Self-adjointness
of F gives Psi(av,w)=a Psi(v,w)=Psi(v,aw); alternation and nondegeneracy
follow by applying trace against all a. On an F-plane the multiplier
of an F-linear map is exactly its determinant. Requiring the rational
polarization multiplier to be rational therefore gives the displayed
M_i, with kernel Res(F/Q)SL2.

For QM the left B-module V has dimension one over B and its rational
centralizer is the right algebra B^op. The opened large-prime theorem
identifies the actual right Galois action with a full GL2(Z_l), and
Lemma5.6 identifies its determinant with the cyclotomic multiplier.
Zariski density at one such prime makes the reduced-norm multiplier
identity an identity of the entire rational centralizer group. Thus
M_i=(B^op)^* and its multiplier-one subgroup is SL1(B^op).
The same density argument identifies each displayed group with the
actual Mumford--Tate group, using the separately established
Mumford--Tate equality. It does not assume maximality from the names
of the endomorphism algebras.

The product theorem yields the full product of derived groups in
the actual block representation. For H=product H_i, the map
G_m x H -> M, (t,h) -> th, has exactly the common diagonal mu2 as
kernel, because a scalar in any listed multiplier-one group satisfies
t^2=1. Its multiplier is t^2. Over an algebraic closure a chosen square
root of any multiplier shows that the kernel of nu:M->G_m is exactly
H, and this is an equality of smooth algebraic groups. In particular
there is no additional finite component in this kernel.

For a maximal torus T in M, T intersect H is a maximal torus of H
and is connected. Over Qbar the cocharacter exact sequence of these
tori identifies X_*(T)/X_*(T intersect H) with Z via nu. Because H
is simply connected, X_*(T intersect H) is its coroot lattice.
Therefore pi1(M)=Z, with trivial Galois action under this identification.
The Hodge cocharacter has multiplier z or z^-1, and hence maps to a
generator. This is precisely Cadoret--Moonen's Section2.2 criterion,
including its Z[Gal]-module formulation. Taking inverse dual from H^1
to H_1 changes only that sign. Thus both hypotheses of TheoremA(ii)
are verified for each actual finite product, and its output is openness
in the full restricted product M(A_f).

A compact open subgroup in this topology contains independently
supported open groups at the finitely many exceptional primes and the
full fixed integral model at every other prime. This full adelic
statement is used before taking commutators; it is not inferred from
separate l-adic openness. Further finite field enlargements give
compact finite-index subgroup images and preserve this openness.

## 3. Exact conjugation action and local nontriviality

The enlarged geometric class collection is finite and Gamma_Q-stable.
The models and all chosen isogenies are defined over a finite Galois K.
The actual maps C_i=phi_i,* c_i obey AS2.1 by composing naturality
of c_i with the Tate map of phi_i. The latter is K-defined. Thus
conjugation of the actual Galois image is the displayed permutation
and C_i-conjugation map, with no unidentified linear conjugacy.

As c acts centrally on Qcm, [gamma,c] lies in Gamma_K intersect
Gamma_Qcm. Its image is g theta(g)^-1. This proves membership in
N for every commutator used below. N is compact, closed and normal.
Since Qab is contained in Qcm, its multiplier is1, so N lies in H.

For a swapped pair, a first-factor-supported g gives a two-factor
commutator. Commuting that element with a first-factor-supported h
isolates [g,h] in the first factor. Normality supplies membership
in N. The second factor is treated identically. This same support
argument applies to swapped RM factors at split primes.

In the fixed Sp case the scalar centralizer of the standard
symplectic representation excludes trivial conjugation: C_i is
anti-complex-linear and cannot be a nonzero real scalar. A nonzero
rational matrix entry or diagonal difference remains nonzero at
every Q_l and modulo all sufficiently large primes.

In the fixed RM case, a nontrivial center automorphism swaps the
split local factors. At an inert or ramified prime it acts nontrivially
on the centroid F_v of sl2(F_v), so the induced Q_l-Lie automorphism
is nontrivial. That Lie algebra is Q_l-simple: its scalar extension
splits into simple factors on which the local Galois group is
transitive, and a descended ideal must be a stable sum of them.

If the center automorphism is trivial, C_i is F-linear and preserves
each real embedding plane. Its anti-linearity gives trace zero on
each plane and positive scalar square. Hence its F-trace is zero,
its square is a totally positive F-scalar, and C_i is nonscalar.
The injection F->F_v preserves that nonscalarity at every local
factor. Excluding the prime divisors of a fixed nonzero entry or
diagonal-difference norm proves the good-prime reduction statement.

In the QM case the ring automorphism of B induced by C_i is inner.
For u realizing it, D_i=u^-1 C_i centralizes B and is therefore right
multiplication by b_i in B^op. The left action of u commutes with H_i
and is a rational Hodge endomorphism. Consequently D_i still
anticommutes with the complex structure. On the real multiplicity
plane in B_R=M2(R), an invertible anti-linear map has trace zero and
negative determinant. Thus b_i is noncentral. This proof retains
the right action and does not presume B splits at a finite prime.
Its noncentrality survives every completion; modulo almost every
prime the nonzero commutator with one fixed B-element survives.

## 4. Bad-prime analytic neighborhoods and good-prime full groups

The Cayley curves used in AS2.3 are genuine curves in the claimed
groups. For symplectic X, (1+tX)^t psi(1+tX) and
(1-tX)^t psi(1-tX) both equal psi+t^2 X^t psi X.
For trace-zero 2-by-2 or quaternionic X, the determinant or reduced
norm of 1+tX equals that of 1-tX. Shrinking the parameter ball
makes the inverse defined and places each curve in the selected
actual open subgroup, including at l=2 and division primes.

The derivatives of the theta-commutator curves are
2(X-theta X). Their U-conjugates span a nonzero ideal in the local
simple Lie algebra: the span is finite-dimensional and closed,
so differentiating its Ad(U)-stability is legitimate. Simplicity
then gives the entire Lie algebra. A product of finitely many
curves with independent derivatives has invertible Jacobian.
The written small-ball contraction proof supplies actual solutions
and proves that the image contains an open group neighborhood.
The same argument applied to ordinary commutator derivatives proves
openness of the derived subgroup of every sufficiently small open
semisimple local subgroup. It is valid for every exceptional prime.

For l>=5 outside a finite set, the normal theta-commutator image
has a noncentral reduction. If all theta-commutators were central,
perfectness would make the resulting central character trivial.
Then theta would act identically; in the Sp case this would force
C scalar, and in the RM semilinear case the fact that SL2(k) traces
cover k would force the field automorphism to be trivial. The
remaining linear SL2 case again has scalar centralizer. These are
contradictions to the reductions already checked.

The opened finite symplectic normal-subgroup lemma and the opened
Lombardo/Serre lifting lemma therefore give the full Sp4(Z_l).
The proof of PSL2 simplicity retained from E.2.24 works over every
finite field of characteristic at least5; the perfect group argument
removes the residual order-two quotient in SL2.

I independently checked the unramified lifting calculation. For
 g=U(a_tilde)(1+lB) mod l^2,
expanding g^l leaves the first-order sum of Ad(U(-j a_tilde))B.
Every entry has degree at most2 in j, and the sums of1,j,j^2 vanish
in the residue field for l>=5. Therefore g^l=1+l a E12 mod l^2.
Varying a, taking lower roots and conjugates supplies the full
sl2(k_v) as an F_l-vector space. The l-th-power formula transports
every layer onto the next, and closedness supplies their limits.
Thus the entire integral SL2(O_v), not merely its reduction, lies
in the actual supported subgroup.

The root commutator formulas make these good integral groups
perfect. For Sp4, the specified torus root values2 and4 give unit
coefficients1 and3; symplectic elimination over the local ring
shows these elementary root groups generate. Hence the derived
image retains every good integral factor. At bad primes it retains
an open subgroup by the preceding analytic proof.

All factors were supported independently. The closure of their
finite products is the full product of the chosen bad-prime open
subgroups and the good-prime integral groups. It lies in the compact
closed N and is open in H(A_f). Finally Fcm/Qcm is abelian, so the
closed derived subgroup of Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Qcm lies in
Gamma_K intersect Gamma_Fcm. Compactness identifies the image of
this closed derived subgroup with the closed derived image of N.
This proves AS2.4 with its full restricted-product meaning.

## 6. One global element and all geometric quotient lattices

Freezing the restriction to K_n fixes the target model and its
chosen Betti basis before matrix approximation. If sigma_n is a
point in that cylinder, any other point is sigma_n k with k fixing
K_n. The actual transport composition formula shows that previous
full matrices remain fixed exactly for k in their entire adelic
kernels. This proves the exact right-coset formula AS4.1.

The finite-family open image contains a product neighborhood with
all previous factors equal to1. Its image on the next factor is
therefore an open subgroup inside the integral stabilizer. The
rational-coset step just proved supplies the next integral matrix
in this actual image coset. Its preimage is a nonempty compact set.
The nested compact sets have a common element, which is unique
because the frozen finite Galois fields exhaust Qbar. The starting
quartic action remains unchanged. No effective algorithm for
selecting the first extendible branch is claimed or needed.

For an actual geometric isogeny q:B->A, its pullback homology
lattice is the finite superlattice L_A=q_*^-1 Lambda_A, whose
quotient by Lambda_B is exactly ker(q) under analytic uniformization.
The prescribed completion of T_B is the actual sigma transport,
so it sends this finite subgroup to ker(sigma q). It follows that
T_B sends the whole superlattice to the conjugate superlattice.
The resulting rational formula for T_A is therefore integral.
Its completion is rho_A(sigma), so completion injectivity proves
independence of the isogeny presentation and every compatibility
with algebraic maps and correspondences. For abelian cohomology
the torsion-free integral lattice makes this recovery exact.
CM factors, the common Tate line, products and the Artin component
permutation all use this same sigma. Thus every abelian surface,
including each nonsimple surface and every geometric isogeny
lattice, belongs to the claimed comparison diagram.
