**A** is Alpöge’s final RH/Dirichlet-GRH results cited as stipulated theorems;

## 1. Q01: schemes over the integers

**Exact Artin–Tate point counts are sufficient.** If, at every rational
prime and every extension degree,

\[
\#X(\mathbf F_{p^r})=\sum_i p^{ir}
\operatorname{Tr}(F_p^r\mid V_i^{I_p}),
\]

with finitely many virtual finite-image representations $V_i$, then ZT01
proves the complete Euler-product identity

\[
\zeta_X(s)=\prod_iL(V_i,s-i).
\]

The identity itself is unconditional. If the representations are abelian,
it is a product of primitive Dirichlet functions and **A** supplies the
global noninteger divisor support on the lines $\Re s=i+1/2$.
For general Artin representations, **M** supplies the same divisor-support
conclusion by integer Brauer induction. Poles are allowed: the induction
does not establish Artin holomorphy. This is a sufficient class, not a
classification of every scheme whose zeta function might satisfy RH.

The sheaf formulation is an equality of compact-support classes

\[
[Rf_!\mathbf Q_\ell]=\sum_i[\mathcal V_i(-i)]
\]

over a good open subscheme of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z$, with finite
monodromy. SGA 4 XVII §§5.1–5.3 and SGA 5 XV §3, no. 2, unnumbered theorem, equation (1),
give the trace interpretation. No unproved algebraicity of projectors is
needed. Equality of realizations or point counts suffices for the Euler
product; a Chow-motive decomposition is a separate assertion.

**Every ring of integers is an example.** ZT03 uses the permutation
representation on embeddings of a number field. Its inertia orbits recover
the correct ramified local factors, so $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_K$
has exact Artin counts. F2 is therefore established from **A** for abelian
fields and, under **M**, extends to every number field. This does not
convert the source’s separate class-group, gerbe, or place-separation
arguments into consequences of RH.

**Finite factors must remain visible.** A good-open identity has the
correction factor in ZT04. ZT05 gives an explicit obstruction to removing it:

\[
\zeta_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z[1/p]}(s)
=(1-p^{-s})\zeta(s),
\]

with noninteger zeros $2\pi i k/\log p$, $k\ne0$, on $\Re s=0$.
The original all-prime F1 is preserved; an overextension of its conclusion
to arbitrary integral models is refuted. Exceptional local divisor lines
are determined by the moduli of local inverse roots. They must be tracked
separately from the shifted global critical lines, even when the lines coincide numerically.

**The remaining scheme gate is finite and geometric.** ZT06 expresses each
finite-type scheme’s zeta function as a finite signed product of good-prime
$L(H^j(Y_a),s)$ for smooth projective varieties over $\mathbf Q$, together
with its actual exceptional-fiber zeta functions. Bittner Theorem 3.1,
EGA IV₃ (8.8.2), the trace formula, and Deligne’s purity theorem supply the
reduction. Meromorphic continuation and functional equations are retained
as explicit requirements where not already known. Strip RH alone is not
used to assert support outside the strip. Under M, every identified CM
factor is removed from the remaining RH problem.

## 2. Q02: pure motives and the first residual obstruction

For a pure motive of weight $w$, geometric Frobenius and Tate twists are
normalized by

\[
\operatorname{wt}N(n)=w-2n,\quad L(N(n),s)=L(N,s+n),\quad
\Re s=(w+1)/2.
\]

For motives, the Tate index may be any integer. The nonnegative index in
the chosen scheme count definition is not a restriction on motivic twists.

**A** gives the abelian Artin–Tate class. **M** gives all Artin motives and
their twists. CM01 extends the conditional result to the absolute-Hodge
category generated by Artin and Tate motives and cohomology of potentially
CM abelian varieties, closed under sums, summands, tensors, and duals.
Anderson, §5.7, Corollary 5.7.2 and Remark 5.7.3, supplies a quotient of
Hecke functions whose algebraic characters all have the same weight.
After the norm twist by $w/2$, the characters are unitary. This is why M
must include infinite-order unitary characters; finite-order Hecke GRH
alone is insufficient. Descent does not require all CM endomorphisms to be
defined over $\mathbf Q$. The conclusion is meromorphic divisor RH,
without asserting that absolute Hodge projectors are algebraic.

The remaining obstruction is **non-CM geometric monodromy up to Tate
twist**, rather than positive weight by itself. For a non-CM elliptic curve,

\[
\zeta_{\mathcal E}(s)
=\frac{\zeta^S(s)\zeta^S(s-1)}{L^S(E,s)}.
\]

ZT07 proves more than the occurrence of the elliptic factor. Under A,
inside $1/2<\Re s<3/2$ and off $\Re s=1$, both numerator factors and
the deleted local elliptic factors are invertible. Hence

\[
\operatorname{ord}_{s=s_0}\zeta_{\mathcal E}(s)
=-\operatorname{ord}_{s=s_0}L(E,s).
\]

An off-line elliptic zero cannot cancel. Its analytic continuation and
functional equation are known; its RH remains the exact additional
assertion. For modular eigenforms of weight $k\ge2$, the motivic line is
$k/2$. Weight-one Artin forms belong to the Artin case. The weight-zero
object $\operatorname{Sym}^2h^1(E)(1)$ retains the non-CM obstruction,
showing why the earlier positive-weight slogan needs refinement.

Deligne 1979 §§1.1–1.3 and (3.1.2) supply the conventions. Its numbered
Conjectures 1.8 and 2.8 concern special values and periods, not RH. The
programme’s Q02 is retained as an explicitly formulated analytic question.

### Explicit Dirichlet, class-number, and prime-witness constants

IA02 uses the corrected version of Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan,
arXiv:1309.3595v3, Theorem 1.5. For a primitive nonprincipal character of
conductor $q\ge10^{10}$, define

\[
U(q)=2e^\gamma\left(\log\log q-\log2+\tfrac12+
\frac1{\log\log q}\right),
\]

\[
V(q)=\frac{12e^\gamma}{\pi^2}
\left(\log\log q-\log2+\tfrac12+
\frac1{\log\log q}+\frac{14\log\log q}{\log q}\right).
\]

Under **A**, $V(q)^{-1}\le|L(1,\chi)|\le U(q)$. For a negative fundamental
discriminant $-D$, $D\ge10^{10}$, the analytic class-number formula gives

\[
\frac{\sqrt D}{\pi V(D)}\le h(-D)\le\frac{\sqrt D}{\pi}U(D).
\]

The same source’s Corollary 1.1 gives the least quadratic non-residue
below $(\log p)^2$ for prime $p\ge5$. Corollary 1.2 gives a prime in
every reduced residue class modulo $q>3$ at most
$(\varphi(q)\log q)^2$. These GRH-conditional literature statements are
now **A** consequences. Small conductors outside a displayed range are
not silently covered by its constant.

For an abelian number field, the residue formula and the same factors bound
$h_KR_K$. A separate regulator lower bound is needed to isolate $h_K$.
This is the correction relevant to SA06 B.4a–B.4e; existing algebraic
verdicts, including the refuted B.4c formulation, are not reversed.

SA06 A.8a receives the rational supersingular count in IA03:

| Congruence for $p>3$ | Discriminant magnitude $D_p$ | $\#\{j\in\mathbf F_p\text{ supersingular}\}$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $p\equiv1\pmod4$ | $4p$ | $\tfrac12 h(-4p)$ |
| $p\equiv7\pmod8$ | $p$ | (h(-p)) |
| $p\equiv3\pmod8$ | $p$ | (2h(-p)) |

Thus multiply the explicit class-number bounds by (1/2,1,2), respectively,
when $D_p\ge10^{10}$. The geometric supersingular count is a different
quantity of order $p$, not $\sqrt p$. In A.8c, the precise definition
of $R$ remains missing. If it is the maximal order of an imaginary
quadratic field, IA02 applies with its actual discriminant; the neighboring
row suggests that interpretation but does not replace the original proof.

IA09 refutes the SA20 uniform shorthand $L(1,\chi)\asymp\log\log q$:
for every odd prime there is a primitive odd character with
$|L(1,\chi)|\le\pi/\sqrt6$. The proof computes the exact mean square over
odd characters. A reciprocal lower bound is valid under A; a positive
uniform (loglog q) lower bound is false.

### Heights and exceptional zeros

IA04 states a fully explicit CM elliptic-height bound in a fixed Deligne
normalization, including the conductor correction for nonmaximal orders.
For $D>e^6$, set $t=\tfrac12\log D$, $b=\gamma+\log(2\pi)-2$, and

\[
R_-(D)=\frac{t-1}{t-3}
\left[2(\log t+1)-\frac{b-\log2}{t-1}
+\frac{\log2+1/4}{t^2-1}\right].
\]

The error in the exact centered height formula is less than $R_-(D)/2$
under A. This uses Ihara–Murty–Shimura, Corollary 3.3.2, (3.3.3), and the
unconditional height identity; RH does not supply the identity itself.

IA05 sharpens the dependency classification. **A already removes the CM
exceptional-zero gate** by quadratic Dirichlet zero exclusion and Stark
descent. It need not wait for M. For degree $2g$, full CM multiplication
defined over $K$, and an individual Colmez identity where needed, the
explicit consequence is

\[
h_F^{\rm st}(A)<
\frac{75}{4}g((2g)!)^2\log\left(|D_K|^{1/[K:\mathbf Q]}\right)
-\frac g4(\gamma+\log(4\pi)).
\]

The coefficients are 75 for $g=1$ and 21600 for $g=2$. The abelian-CM
Colmez identity is known, so that case is **A**. The general individual
identity remains **B** after the zero gate is removed. Wei’s averaged
Colmez arguments give additional known bounds; they do not automatically
supply every individual identity.

For full multiplication by the maximal order $\mathbf Z[\zeta_5]$,
IA06 gives the already **U** exact height

\[
\tfrac12\log5-
\tfrac32\log\frac{\Gamma(1/5)}{\Gamma(4/5)}-
\tfrac12\log\frac{\Gamma(2/5)}{\Gamma(3/5)}
=-1.4525092396\ldots.
\]

The prompt associates these heights with SA07, but no corresponding height
or Colmez row was found in that intake; the audit identifies them as prompt

The official 14-part Lean archive was retrieved after the attachment-only

**ALP01** gives a complete independent proof of prime-iterate divisibility:
for an additive derivation $D$ and an augmentation $\epsilon$,
$\epsilon(F)=\epsilon(G)=0$ implies $\epsilon(D^p(FG))\in pR$.
Its constant-coefficient specialization occurs in
ConstantCoeffIteratePInvariantDerivationMul.lean, line 38.
In characteristic $p$, $D^p$ is a derivation; this alone does not say that it
vanishes.

The recovered source theorem
GlobalTheory.T.detDepth_lower_bound_saturated_even_type, in
DetDepthLowerBoundSaturatedEvenTypeStatement.lean, line 8, has an explicit
bound. For an integral formal group with identity-linear logarithm over an
algebraically closed valued characteristic-zero field, $p\ge3$, $v(p)=1$,
the stated root-of-unity action, an even type, and a saturated $i$-frame,
it gives

\[
\operatorname{detDepth}(G,u,c)\ge
\frac{(i-r_0)cp}{p^2-1}-i^2(\log_p c+1),
\qquad
0\le r_0\le i,\quad
r_0\le j\left(\frac32+\frac12\log_p c\right).
\]

Here $c\ge1$ is an integer and $j$ bounds each odd-type multiplicity.
The paper states all structural hypotheses and the definition of depth.
**ALP02** is the checked rank-one consequence when no odd type occurs:
every integral formal series has ordinary derivative valuation at least
$cp/(p^2-1)-\log_p c-1$ along the specified saturated direction.
This is a consequence of the cited intermediate source theorem, with no
RH premise. Its statement, definitions, and local proof were read; its
imported proof chain was not independently rebuilt.

The remaining step-2 translation is precise: one must identify the CM
integral formal model, the root-of-unity action, an integral theta
trivialization, the saturated tangent, and the chosen period normalization.
The map's earlier CMCurveGoodOutsidePrimeSt candidate concerns the
ramified prime defining $\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-p})$, a different prime role.
The actual Bessel-period predicate asserts nonvanishing of an operator-valued
integral with detailed representation and measure hypotheses; it does not
assert values $0,1$. The actual theta-lift predicate gives eventual-rank
existence with an existential threshold, rather than the unspecified
quantitative claim about every chain of lifts.

For Masullo, the global trace formula 12.5.1.1 (PDF pp. 519–520) requires
the test function to vanish on the norm-one idele classes. ML02 proves that
no nonzero standard test $\psi*\psi^*$ can satisfy this restriction,
because its value at the identity is $\|\psi\|_2^2$. The restriction
cannot simply be dropped to obtain a positivity argument. The draft itself
states this in §1.7.2, PDF p.35; ML02 checks the general locally compact
formulation, rather than identifying a new objection to its route. ML01 supplies an
independently checked local-pole separation statement, with the necessary
finite-order qualification for the infinite-common-pole conclusion.
The draft’s 13.3.1.2 (PDF p. 522) is still an unfilled divisor-theorem
heading; M is therefore explicitly formulated as a conditional input.
The long topological and nuclearity arguments have not been certified here.

SV01 describes the exact lambda-ring mechanism: an adapted Frobenius
matrix is block triangular, so its determinant forgets extension classes.
SV02 gives an actual counterexample in mixed Tate motives over
$\mathbf Z[1/2]$: the nonzero Kummer extension defined by the unit 2 and
the split object have the same incomplete (L)-function
$\zeta^S(s)\zeta^S(s+1)$, but are not isomorphic. At ramified places,
inertia invariants need not preserve extensions, so the incomplete
qualification is retained.

Deligne–Goncharov’s supplied February 2003 source, (2.1.3), identifies
mixed Tate extension spaces with rational units and $K$-groups.
Propositions 2.2–2.3 give freeness of the graded motivic Lie algebra;
§2.4 warns that free generators are not canonical. Borel’s rank theorem
and the Bernoulli special-value formulas are already U. They do not
compute integral torsion or regulator matrices from zero locations.
RH can imply nonvanishing at off-center integers and improve estimates;
it does not determine central orders, exact periods, or integral $K$-groups.
