# Mathematical dictionary

This dictionary translates the proved statements into adjacent mathematical languages; it asserts no unproved equivalence with lambda rings or K-theory. Statement tags are stable local tags pending assignment of a registry number.

## FS.001: sections and rational boundary points

In algebraic geometry, the arithmetic fundamental group extension of the affine Fermat curve has a rational splitting only in the three rational cusp packets when the splitting is everywhere locally geometric. A rational cusp has tame inertia Zhat(1); a choice of tangent supplies a base splitting, and the different splittings are H1(Q,Zhat(1)). The global proof combines finite etale neighborhoods, finite descent, modular representations and modular curves.

Over Z, the Fermat curve and its boundary have good logarithmic models away from primes dividing the exponent. This explains the unramified action used in the restricted pro-3 theorem. It does not convert arbitrary nonabelian cocycles into integral ones. The cyclotomic module Zhat(1) is the Tate twist recording the action on roots of unity.

## FS.006: the arithmetic content of a leading coefficient

The formal Tate parameter is 2^-8 t^(2n) times a principal unit. Principal units admit division by every integer in the characteristic-zero formal power-series ring, but the rational leading coefficient keeps its own Kummer class. In motivic language the boundary contributes a Tate module, and the leading rational unit determines its extension class. Discarding that class changes the arithmetic representation.

The Kummer class of a rational unit is related to the usual unit group K1(Q)=Q*, through the standard Kummer map. No identification of nonabelian sections with elements of a K-group is asserted, and no lambda-ring structure on a section set is constructed.

## FS.005 and FQ.001: descent and localization

Finite-support descent forces local centers to come from a single rational point of the compactification. For solvable quotients, the generalized Jacobian and the exact boundary sequence identify the remaining tangent class. Saidi localization promotes equality at density-one finite places to equality of finite-derived and prosolvable sections. This is a statement about nonabelian arithmetic cohomology, not a consequence of equality of motives alone.

## FQ.003 and FQ.004: the quintic motive

Three explicit maps from the Fermat quintic to v²+v=u⁵ give six independent regular differentials. Their pullbacks yield a rational isogeny Jac(F5)~A³. Thus the first cohomological motive of the Fermat quintic decomposes into three copies of the genus-two factor, in the category where isogenies are inverted. The proof is a correspondence calculation, not only an equality of L-functions. The central-value certificate, CM modularity and Nekovar's theorem give rank zero and finite Tate-Shafarevich group. Stoll's theorem then has the required exact arithmetic hypotheses.

## FQ.005 and FQ.006: local geometry and abstract sections

A free pro-3 arithmetic Galois source allows many abstract full pro-3 splittings. Their compact Tate-module images have infinite order and cannot be the Kummer classes of the permitted local centers at3. The restricted Selmer theorem instead uses a surjection from the local Galois source at3 to the global ramification-restricted source. This distinction prevents the arbitrary-section construction from refuting the Selmer theorem.
