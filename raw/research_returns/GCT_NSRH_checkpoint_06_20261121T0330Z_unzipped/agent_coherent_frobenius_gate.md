## 1. Exact base and Frobenius conventions

Let `k=F_q`, with characteristic `p>3`, and put

\[
R=k[[t]],\qquad \Delta=t^m u(t),\quad m\ge1,\quad u(t)\in R^*,
\]
\[
C=R[x]/(x(x-\Delta)),\qquad
L_1=C/(x),\quad L_2=C/(x-\Delta).
\tag{F36-1}
\]

The actual completed center from F19 has `m=2`: take `t=v−1`, `x=z_1−z`, `Delta=z_1−z_2`; the leading unit is nonzero in exactly the stated good characteristics. The general `m` calculation isolates the collision-order dependence. Each branch `L_i` is isomorphic to `R` as an `R`-module, while its `C` action is evaluation at the appropriate branch.

Write `F_C:C->C`, `c->c^p`, and `F_R:R->R`, `r->r^p`, for **absolute** Frobenius. These are characteristic-p ring homomorphisms; `F_C` is semilinear on the coefficient field unless `k=F_p`. The `q`th-power version is the `f`th iterate when `q=p^f`. This is coherent absolute Frobenius, not geometric Frobenius on an ℓ-adic Weil realization and not a matrix's noncommutative `p`th power.

For a coherent `C`-module `L`, the center pullback is

\[
F_C^*L=C\otimes_{F_C,C}L.
\]

A unit coherent Frobenius structure in this paragraph means a `C`-linear isomorphism `F_C^*L -> L`. Section 6 uses the distinct parameter-base linearization `R tensor_(F_R,R) L -> L`; these two conditions are not interchangeable.

## 2. Exact underived pullback on each branch

In `C`, the relation `x^2=Delta x` implies

\[
x^p=\Delta^{p-1}x,
\qquad
(x-\Delta)^p=x^p-\Delta^p
=\Delta^{p-1}(x-\Delta).
\tag{F36-2}
\]

Tensoring the cyclic presentations therefore gives

\[
F_C^*L_1=C/(x^p)=C/(\Delta^{p-1}x),
\]
\[
F_C^*L_2=C/((x-\Delta)^p)
=C/(\Delta^{p-1}(x-\Delta)).
\tag{F36-3}
\]

The ring `C` is free over `R`, with basis `1,x`. The ideal generated by `Delta^(p−1)x` is exactly the `R`-submodule `R Delta^(p−1)x`: multiplying `x` by `a+bx` gives `(a+b Delta)x`. Consequently

\[
F_C^*L_1\simeq R\oplus R/(\Delta^{p-1})
\quad\text{as an }R\text{-module}.
\tag{F36-4}
\]

Using the equally valid `R`-basis `1,x−Delta` proves the same formula for `F_C^*L_2`. Each extra torsion summand has length

\[
\operatorname{length}_R R/(\Delta^{p-1})=m(p-1).
\tag{F36-5}
\]

Since `L_i` is `R`-free, no `C`-linear isomorphism `F_C^*L_i -> L_i` can exist. This excludes every candidate isomorphism, not just one chosen map. The displayed direct sum is only an `R`-module decomposition; no split `C`-module decomposition is asserted.

There is, however, a natural surjection: the ideal `(x_i^p)` is contained in `(x_i)`, where `x_1=x` and `x_2=x−Delta`, so reduction gives

\[
\theta_i:F_C^*L_i\twoheadrightarrow L_i.
\tag{F36-6}
\]

It is the linearization of the branchwise semilinear map `r->r^p`. Its kernel is precisely the torsion just computed. More accurately as `C`-modules,

\[
\ker\theta_1\simeq L_2/\Delta^{p-1}L_2,
\qquad
\ker\theta_2\simeq L_1/\Delta^{p-1}L_1.
\tag{F36-7}
\]

For example `ker theta_1` is generated by the class of `x`, which is annihilated by `x−Delta`. This identifies the action on the discarded torsion as well as its length.

All formulas hold for an iterated power `p^e`, replacing `p−1` with `p^e−1`. In particular the coefficient-field-linear `q`-Frobenius has torsion length `m(q−1)` per branch.

## 3. Derived center pullback is unbounded on the same object

Put `f_1=x`, `f_2=x−Delta`. The alternating resolution

\[
\cdots\xrightarrow{f_1}C\xrightarrow{f_2}C
\xrightarrow{f_1}C\longrightarrow L_1\longrightarrow0
\tag{F36-8}
\]

is exact. Indeed, in the domain `R[X]`, cancellation from divisibility by `X(X−Delta)` gives `ann_C(f_1)=(f_2)` and `ann_C(f_2)=(f_1)`. The ring `C` is `R`-free, so multiplication by the nonzero scalar `Delta^(p−1)` is injective.

Applying Frobenius pullback to this free resolution replaces its differentials by multiplication by `f_1^p` and `f_2^p`. By (F36-2), these are `Delta^(p−1)f_1` and `Delta^(p−1)f_2`. Their kernels are still `(f_2)` and `(f_1)`, whereas the preceding images are only `Delta^(p−1)(f_2)` and `Delta^(p−1)(f_1)`. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Tor}^{C}_{2j+1}(C_{F_C},L_1)
\simeq L_1/\Delta^{p-1}L_1\quad(j\ge0),
\]
\[
\operatorname{Tor}^{C}_{2j}(C_{F_C},L_1)
\simeq L_2/\Delta^{p-1}L_2\quad(j\ge1).
\tag{F36-9}
\]

Here `C_(F_C)` denotes `C` with its right `C` action through Frobenius and its ordinary left action. The same formulas with the two branches exchanged hold for `L_2`. Every positive Tor degree is nonzero and, as an `R`-module, is `R/(Delta^(p−1))`.

Thus `L F_C^* L_i` has nonzero cohomology in arbitrarily negative degrees. It cannot be isomorphic to the degree-zero branch module, nor does this derived functor land in the bounded coherent derived category on that object. A different bounded resolution representing the same derived object cannot change this conclusion. This does not exclude replacing the object by a genuinely different complex, or working in an unbounded/completed category; those are additional constructions.

## 4. The actual nonstandard algebra, module, and exact torsion multiplicities

Reduce the F19/F21 construction in good characteristic and complete at `v=1`. Its actual algebra with the checked named-generator model is

\[
A=R\times R\times M_2(C).
\tag{F36-10}
\]

Define `phi_A` by `F_R` on the two scalar factors and **entrywise** `F_C` on the matrix factor. This is a unital ring homomorphism. For matrices, the required identity is

`(sum_j a_ij b_jl)^p=sum_j a_ij^p b_jl^p`,

using the commutativity of `C` and characteristic `p`. This has no relation to the generally nonadditive map `a->a^p` obtained by multiplying a noncommutative matrix by itself `p` times. The matrix units are fixed by `phi_A`, so the standard Morita idempotent is preserved.

The actual right module from F21 is

\[
M=R^{20}\oplus R^4\oplus
 (L_1\otimes_C C^{1\times2})^{16}
 \oplus (L_2\otimes_C C^{1\times2})^4,
\tag{F36-11}
\]

where the notation denotes the standard row modules on the two branches. This is the original 64-dimensional tensor lattice through the regular action-preserving isomorphism, not merely a replacement with the same character.

Right extension of scalars along `phi_A` is

\[
F_A^*M=M\otimes_{A,\phi_A}A.
\]

It commutes with the standard Morita equivalence because the matrix units are fixed: the row module with branch multiplicity `L_i` is sent to the row module with multiplicity `F_C^*L_i`. Equivalently, for the free bundle `E=(O,O,O_C^2)`, base change of `Hom(E,F)` is `Hom(F^*E,F^*F)`, and the trivialization of `F^*E` gives exactly this entrywise algebra map. Finite projectivity of `E` justifies this Hom base change even though Frobenius on the center is not flat.

On the branch multiplicity sheaf `F=L_1^16⊕L_2^4`, underived pullback has 20 extra torsion summands, of total `R`-length

\[
20m(p-1).
\tag{F36-12}
\]

The actual row tensor module has twice this multiplicity, so

\[
F_A^*M\simeq R^{64}\oplus
 (R/(\Delta^{p-1}))^{40}
\quad\text{as an }R\text{-module}.
\tag{F36-13}
\]

Its extra torsion length is `40m(p−1)`, which is **`80(p−1)` for the actual order-two collision**. The two scalar sectors remain free: pulling back a free module on `R` preserves its rank. No `A`-linear unit Frobenius isomorphism `F_A^*M -> M` can exist, because the source has this nonzero underlying `R`-torsion and the target does not. The natural semilinear map linearizes to an `A`-linear surjection with exactly the stated torsion kernel.

Derived Morita equivalence transfers (F36-9) as well. In every positive Tor degree the actual tensor module has, as an `R`-module, 40 copies of `R/(Delta^(p−1))`, and the scalar sectors contribute none. Thus the same actual module does not acquire a unit Frobenius by merely replacing underived center/algebra pullback with its bounded derived version.

## 5. Relative Frobenius identifies the source of the defect

The parameter-base pullback of the center is

\[
C^{(p)}=R\otimes_{F_R,R}C
\simeq R[X]/(X(X-\Delta^p)).
\tag{F36-14}
\]

Relative Frobenius is the `R`-algebra map

\[
F_{C/R}:C^{(p)}\longrightarrow C,
\qquad X\longmapsto x^p=\Delta^{p-1}x.
\tag{F36-15}
\]

In bases `1,X` and `1,x`, its matrix is `diag(1,Delta^(p−1))`. It is injective and its cokernel has length `m(p−1)`. The source has branch separation of order `mp`, whereas the target separation has order `m`. In the normalized branch presentation this is precisely the inclusion of the order-`mp` collision subring in the order-`m` collision ring. It is finite and gives the same kind of universal homeomorphism studied in F22, while failing to be an isomorphism of coherent rings.

Center pullback factors through parameter pullback followed by extension across this relative Frobenius map. The latter extension is where the extra torsion arises. Ordinary étale topological invariance cannot detect the difference of these coherent rings. This explains why the obstruction is compatible with the constant nearby-cycle calculation in F22.

## 6. A unit parameter-base semilinear structure does exist

There is no obstruction to unit linearization over the parameter ring alone. On each branch `L_i=R`, let `tau_i(r)=r^p`. Branch evaluation commutes with absolute Frobenius, so

\[
\tau_i(c\,r)=F_C(c)\,\tau_i(r)
\]

with the appropriate branch action. On the actual adapted decomposition (F36-11), use coefficientwise `p`th powers on all scalar and row coordinates. This gives a semilinear map `tau_ad` satisfying

\[
\tau_{\rm ad}(m a)=\tau_{\rm ad}(m)\,\phi_A(a).
\tag{F36-16}
\]

Its parameter linearization

\[
\Phi_R:R\otimes_{F_R,R}M\longrightarrow M
\tag{F36-17}
\]

is an isomorphism: in the adapted free `R`-basis it sends `1 tensor e_j` to `e_j`. This remains true although its center/algebra linearization is the nonunit surjection computed above. The two tensor products impose different relations and have different source modules.

### Exact transport to the original right tensor basis

Let `B` be the regular invertible row-basis matrix built from the F21 changes of basis, including its final branch/Morita change. Our convention is

\[
\rho_{\rm ad}(a)=B\rho_{\rm old}(a)B^{-1},
\qquad r_{\rm old}=r_{\rm ad}B.
\]

Write `B^[p]` for entrywise Frobenius and define

\[
U=(B^{[p]})^{-1}B\in GL_{64}(R).
\tag{F36-18}
\]

Transporting the adapted semilinear map gives, in the original row coordinates,

\[
\tau_{\rm old}(r)=r^{[p]}U.
\tag{F36-19}
\]

Indeed `r_ad=r_old B^(-1)`, so applying coefficientwise Frobenius there and multiplying back by `B` gives exactly (F36-19). It is an `R`-unit linearization because `U` is invertible. The actual action satisfies

\[
\rho_{\rm old}(\phi_A(a))
=U^{-1}\rho_{\rm old}(a)^{[p]}U.
\tag{F36-20}
\]

This follows by taking entrywise Frobenius of `B rho_old(a) B^(-1)` and conjugating back. Substitution into (F36-19) verifies

`tau_old(r rho_old(a))=tau_old(r) rho_old(phi_A(a))`

exactly, including the order of the row matrices. Thus the parameter-base repair is compatible with the actual nonstandard action after the regular transport.

One must **not** replace (F36-20) by the unsupported assertion that naive entrywise Frobenius in the original tensor basis preserves the original algebra image. For the displayed generator matrices with prime-field coefficients, entrywise Frobenius has the numerical form `P_i(v)^[p]=P_i(v^p)`, but the corresponding intrinsic algebra endomorphism acts in that old basis by the conjugated matrix in (F36-20). Whether the un-conjugated matrix itself belongs to the same embedded algebra is a separate question and is not used here. For the regular basis matrices with prime-field coefficients, `B^[p]=B(v^p)` as well; the conjugating factor cannot simply be omitted.

## 7. Precise geometric consequence

The original rank-three algebra/module already has a coherent model on the singular center. Equipping that same branch object with a unit structure for pullback by the center's absolute Frobenius fails by an explicit torsion calculation. It also fails for derived pullback of the same bounded object, whose higher Tor groups do not terminate. These are concrete restrictions on a specified coherent realization, not an assertion that no Frobenius-compatible model exists anywhere.

In fact a unit parameter-base semilinear structure does exist, and the preceding formulas preserve its actual module action after the necessary regular conjugation. Passing to normalization, changing the object, using an unbounded construction, or choosing an étale/Weil replacement may behave differently. None of these operations by itself identifies the quantum grading with Frobenius weights or proves the path-output sign hypothesis. Absolute coherent Frobenius and geometric Frobenius on ℓ-adic cohomology must still be related by an explicitly stated comparison if the sign-only purity route is to use them.
