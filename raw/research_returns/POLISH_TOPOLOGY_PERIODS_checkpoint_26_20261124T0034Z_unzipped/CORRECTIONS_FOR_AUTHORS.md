# Specialization issue in a consulted Bhowmik–Pathak preprint

Prepared 23 November 2026 by GPT-6 Astra in ChatGPT Pro mode. No human has checked this note. It has **not been sent to the authors**. This material is excluded from Paper 3.

## Version and precise claim

Tapas Bhowmik and Siddhi S. Pathak, *A note on transcendence of special values of functions related to modularity*, arXiv:2408.00271v1 (1 August 2024), Theorem 1.11, p. 3; proof in §3.5, pp. 17–18. The author's currently available 18-page copy has the same statement and proof step. Both complete PDFs were fetched and read in this preparation:

* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00271v1 — SHA256 `58604953a3679a03b781eb5d267af16b2ffe89aeac85c24fe9223f8edcf51a20`.
* https://www.cmi.ac.in/~siddhi/TranscModFn.pdf — SHA256 `f2e14bf53aad20cf8fd83b0fa3607954d972d0e7da4b8b582f04449461395273`.

The theorem asserts a lower bound of three for the transcendence degree of the field generated by q=exp(2πiτ), a nonconstant modular form f(τ), a nonconstant modular function g(τ), and a nonconstant positive-depth meromorphic quasimodular expression F(τ), with algebraic Fourier coefficients. The stated specialization conditions exclude poles and require f(τ) and F(τ) to be nonzero. They do not require a positive-depth coefficient to remain nonzero after evaluation.

The journal publication is *Acta Arithmetica* **218** (2025), 1–23, DOI https://doi.org/10.4064/aa240128-7-9. The official metadata page was opened. Its download link redirected to the publisher's homepage; the journal PDF was not obtained. **This note confirms a problem in the two consulted preprint copies; it does not assert that the published 23-page version has identical wording.** Before sending any correction, the final published text and any erratum should be compared by someone with access.

## Exact counterexample

Take τ=i, f=E4, g=j, and

\[
F_0=1+\frac{E_2E_6}{E_4^2}.
\]

This version has weight zero and depth one, so it also accommodates a restrictive reading of the word “function.” Its coefficient E6/E4² is a nonzero meromorphic modular form of weight -2. All Fourier coefficients are rational. The first terms of the normalized Eisenstein series are E2=1-24q+…, E4=1+240q+…, E6=1-504q+…, giving F0=2-1008q+…; hence it is nonconstant.

At the fixed point i, the weight-six transformation under τ↦-1/τ gives E6(i)=i⁶E6(i)=-E6(i), so E6(i)=0. The identity E4³-E6²=1728Δ and the nonvanishing of Δ in the upper half-plane give E4(i)≠0 and j(i)=1728. Every required value is finite, f(i)≠0, and F0(i)=1≠0. Nevertheless

\[
\overline{\mathbf Q}(e^{-2\pi},E_4(i),j(i),F_0(i))
=\overline{\mathbf Q}(e^{-2\pi},E_4(i)),
\]

whose transcendence degree is at most two. No numerical approximation or unproved transcendence assertion is needed.

The source's weight-four alternative F4=E4+E2E6/E4 also works under the wider usage of “quasimodular function”: F4=2-528q+…, F4(i)=E4(i)≠0. The new certificate program checks both first coefficients by exact arithmetic. The weight-zero version above avoids dependence on this terminology.

## Where the proof needs an additional condition

The displayed argument in §3.5 first proves algebraicity of the four chosen values over the field of q,E2,E4,E6, and then asserts equality of transcendence degrees. That first algebraicity statement proves only one inequality. Recovering E2 from a quasimodular expression requires its specialized polynomial in E2 to have positive degree. Positive depth as a function does not ensure this: in the example, every positive-depth coefficient vanishes at i. The additional nonvanishing of the whole expression does not prevent that collapse.

## A sufficient repaired statement

Let f be a nonzero meromorphic modular form of integer weight k≠0, g a nonconstant modular function, and F=Σ(r=0 to d) f_r E2^r, where f_r has weight w-2r. Work on a common congruence subgroup and assume algebraic Fourier coefficients. At τ require f,g,and all f_r to be finite, f(τ)≠0, and **at least one f_r(τ)≠0 for r≥1**. Then the field of q,f(τ),g(τ),F(τ) and the field of q,E2(τ),E4(τ),E6(τ) have the same transcendence degree. Nesterenko's theorem gives the lower bound three.

Here is a proof of the equality, independent of the failed specialization step. A nonconstant function g on the compact modular curve gives a finite morphism to the projective line. Consequently every modular function finite at τ has value algebraic over Qbar(g(τ)). This applies also at a transcendental value, through the residue-field extension at the generic point. Put δ=Δ(τ)≠0. The finite nonzero modular function f^12/Δ^k has algebraic value over that field, and k≠0 implies that δ is algebraic over Qbar(f(τ),g(τ)). The same field contains j(τ), E4(τ), E6(τ) algebraically, using E4³=jδ and E6²=(j-1728)δ. For each nonzero f_r(τ), the ratio f_r^12/Δ^(w-2r) shows that f_r(τ) is algebraic over this field; zero values need no argument. The polynomial Σ f_r(τ)X^r-F(τ) is nonconstant by the additional hypothesis and vanishes at E2(τ). Thus E2(τ) is algebraic over the chosen-value field. Conversely the finite morphism j and the same weight-ratio identities make f(τ),g(τ),and all f_r(τ) algebraic over Qbar(E4(τ),E6(τ)); adjoining E2 recovers F. Adjoining q preserves the equality of transcendence degrees.

This is a sufficient repair, not a claim of minimal hypotheses or priority. The lower-bound input is Y. V. Nesterenko, *Modular functions and transcendence questions*, *Sbornik: Mathematics* **187** (1996), 1319–1348, Theorem 1, p. 1320, DOI https://doi.org/10.1070/SM1996v187n09ABEH000158. The repair does not replace E2 by its nonholomorphic completion E2*. In particular it supplies no proof of the unresolved elliptic Hodge-splitting converse.

## 1. Steenrod instability inequality: a literal formula defect

**Locator:** Lemma 4.9, final sentence of its proof, printed p. 2526 (physical PDF p. 32). The original page was read as text and directly inspected as an image. The last sentence asserts the vanishing of `Sq^i` on degree `j` when `i < j`.

**Correct formula:** the instability vanishing is

\[
\operatorname{Sq}^i(a)=0\qquad\text{when }i>\deg a.
\]

The displayed inequality should be reversed. The proof needs only `Sq^2` on degree one, so its intended application is correct after this reversal.

Two direct checks show that the printed general inequality is false. First, `Sq^0` is the identity, including on every nonzero positive-degree class. Second, for degree-one generators `a,b` of `H^*(RP^∞ × RP^∞; F_2)=F_2[a,b]`, the Cartan formula and `Sq^1(a)=a^2`, `Sq^1(b)=b^2` give

\operatorname{Sq}^1(ab)=a^2b+ab^2\ne0.

Here `1 < 2`, and the two nonzero monomials are distinct. This second check does not use `Sq^0` as the operation under examination. These are exact algebraic checks, not a numerical test of the surgery theorem.

**Effect on this project:** the A142 proof uses the normal invariant `(0,y)`, so its characteristic class is simply `red_2(y)`. It does not require a nonzero degree-two coordinate or the incorrectly written inequality. No correction discussion is included in the publication input.

# External correction record: the finite-group case list in Galvin's thesis

This record is outside the paper. No author has been contacted. It concerns a specific case list and an abelianization assertion, not a claimed disproof of the main Casson–Sullivan realization theorem.

## Exact inspected source

Galvin, D. A. P. (2024). *Non-smoothable homeomorphisms of 4-manifolds* [Doctoral thesis, University of Glasgow]. https://doi.org/10.5525/gla.thesis.84595

## 1. A missing case in the stated exhaustion

The proof of Proposition 9.3.2, printed pp.116–117, claims an exhaustive five-case list of finite free sphere-action groups. Its second case is characterized as nonabelian with cyclic Sylow two-subgroups; its last three cases have orders divisible by 24, 48 and 120, respectively. With those literal descriptions, Q8 belongs to none of the five cases.

There is a direct witness. Regard the unit quaternions as the round sphere S3. Left multiplication by the eight elements ±1, ±i, ±j, ±k gives a free orientation-preserving linear action. Freeness follows because qx=x for a nonzero quaternion x implies q=1. Equivalently, the exact determinants of A−I for the seven nonidentity left-multiplication matrices are 16 and six copies of 4. Each matrix is orthogonal with determinant one. Thus S3/Q8 is a closed oriented elliptic three-manifold with fundamental group Q8.

The group Q8 is noncyclic, is itself its Sylow two-subgroup, and has order eight. It therefore is not cyclic, fails the literal criterion in case (ii), and cannot have any of the orders specified in cases (iii)–(v). This refutes the stated exhaustion, independently of any terminology assigned to “metacyclic prism”. If that term is intended to include Q8, then the characterization within case (ii) must be changed.

## 2. The abelianization sentence

The proof of Proposition 9.3.3, printed p.117, assigns even cyclic abelianization to all groups in its prism and octahedral cases. For Q8 the commutator subgroup is {±1}, and

\[
 Q_8^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]

One proof abelianizes the presentation
\(\langle a,b\mid a^4=1,\ b^2=a^2,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle\): the relations force 2a=2b=0, and the map a↦(1,0), b↦(0,1) proves that both generators survive independently. Thus if the prism case includes Q8, the cyclicity assertion is false. If its scope is restricted as in Proposition 9.3.2(ii), the exhaustive classification still omits the elliptic manifold S3/Q8, whose H1 with mod-two coefficients has rank two.

This record does not assert a complete replacement classification of spherical space-form groups or their abelianizations.

## 3. General arithmetic repair and theorem-level limit

The conclusion of Proposition 9.3.2 is supported by a separate classical argument. An oriented finite cover of a closed three-manifold is an integral homology sphere when the cover is universal. Its lifted cellular complex gives a four-periodic resolution. Hambleton, Milgram, Taylor, and Williams (1988), printed p.352, explicitly state that the Sylow two-subgroups of finite groups with periodic cohomology have trivial integral SK1. This assertion is about the Sylow subgroup; it does not claim trivial SK1 for the entire finite group. Oliver (1988), Introduction Example 2 p.14 and Example 14.4 p.333, cover arbitrary generalized quaternion two-groups.

Accordingly, the group-theoretic input needed for the realization construction can be established without the flawed case exhaustion. The full theorem about pseudo-isotopy spaces has additional geometric and category conventions; this correction record alone does not reprove or negate it. A144's separate publication candidate proves its own ordinary-component realization statement with explicit relative markings and declared classical inputs. No comparison-image/kernel equality is inferred.

## 1. M0 and M9: a distinction between drafts, not an attributed author contradiction

## Exact source and exposure

## The disputed proof step

In the first paragraph of the proof of Theorem 3, the source treats a set E intersected with the open product window

\[
 U_{ab\delta\varepsilon}=B(a,\delta)\times B(b,\varepsilon).
\]

It gives a criterion for this intersection to be the graph of a continuous function over the whole horizontal ball. The criterion consists of the relative closed-graph condition `closure(E) intersect U subset E` and uniqueness of the vertical point in `closure(E) intersect U` above each horizontal point. Those conditions permit graph values to approach the excluded boundary of the vertical ball. They therefore do not by themselves imply continuity.

## Exact compact semialgebraic counterexample

Take horizontal coordinate u and vertical coordinate v, and let

\[
 E=\{(u,0):-1\le u\le0\}
   \cup\{(u,1-u):0\le u\le\tfrac12\}.
\]

This is a compact closed semialgebraic subset of the plane. It is contained in the monic Weierstrass equation

\[
 P(u,v)=v(v-1+u)=0.
\]

Choose a=b=0, delta=1/4, and epsilon=1, so that the source's window is

\[
 U=(-\tfrac14,\tfrac14)\times(-1,1).
\]

The center (0,0) belongs to E. Since E is closed, its closure intersected with U is contained in E. For every u in the horizontal interval there is exactly one vertical coordinate in E intersect U:

\[
 f(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&-\tfrac14<u\le0,\\
 1-u,&0<u<\tfrac14.
 \end{cases}
\]

For u<0 only the first segment is present. At u=0 the second segment contributes (0,1), which is excluded from U because the vertical ball is open, so the sole value in U is 0. For u>0 only the second segment is present and its value lies strictly between 3/4 and 1. Thus all the printed conditions hold, including their closure bars and the monic-polynomial setting.

Nevertheless f is discontinuous at zero. Explicitly, for any rho>0 choose

\[
 u=\tfrac12\min\{\rho,\tfrac14\}.
\]

Then 0<u<rho and u<=1/8, while `|f(u)-f(0)|=1-u>=7/8`. The epsilon-delta continuity test fails already with output tolerance 1/2. Equivalently, the right-hand graph tends to (0,1), which lies on the omitted vertical boundary, rather than to (0,0).

## First-order repair and its exact consequence

For parameters p=(a,b,delta,epsilon), define the semialgebraic relation

\[
 G_p(u,v)\iff (u,v)\in E\ \land\
 \|u-a\|^2<\delta^2\ \land\
 \|v-b\|^2<\varepsilon^2,
 \qquad \delta,\varepsilon>0.
\]

Require that for every u in the horizontal ball there is exactly one v with G_p(u,v). In addition require the genuine continuity formula

\[
 \begin{split}
 \forall u_0\,\forall v_0\bigl(G_p(u_0,v_0)\Longrightarrow
 \forall\eta>0\ \exists\rho>0\ \forall u\,\forall v\,
 \bigl[ G_p(u,v)\land\|u-u_0\|^2<\rho^2\\
 \Longrightarrow \|v-v_0\|^2<\eta^2\bigr]\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]

Existence, uniqueness, and this formula are all first-order conditions over the ordered real field with semialgebraic predicates. By Tarski–Seidenberg, the set F of parameters satisfying them is semialgebraic. It is exactly the parameter set of continuous graphs that the proof needs. This direct argument replaces the false criterion while retaining the definition and semialgebraicity of F.

The rest of Theorem 3's displayed argument uses F only to know that the relevant intersection is the graph of a continuous function. It can therefore proceed with this corrected definition. Its analytic approximation and uniqueness inputs are unchanged, and Theorem 4's subsequent coordinate argument remains available. The present correction is bounded to a proof-detail equivalence; no theorem-level failure or change of the credited smooth-locus theorem is asserted.

# Finite convex combinations in Walsberg's lower-semicontinuity lemma

Affected source: Walsberg, E. (2015). *On the topology of metric spaces definable in o-minimal expansions of fields* (arXiv:1510.07291v2), Lemma7.23 and its proof, p.24. Exact version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.07291v2 . The arXiv version was submitted11November2015; the downloaded PDF internally displays13July2018. The lemma is correct. Its proof contains a local invalid inference: an open set meeting the convex hull of a set in arbitrary dimension need not meet a line segment joining only two of the original points. This is an exposition correction, not a counterexample to the lemma or its application.

Every segment joining two points of S lies on an axis or on the line x+y=1. The set V meets neither an axis nor that line, since both coordinates are positive and their sum is less than5/6. Thus two original points do not suffice in the quoted step.
