\section{Actual multiplicity obstructions at every dilation of the cubic Hessian weight}

\subsection*{Finding F-028: a dominant binary permanent--Hessian pair}
Put
\[
 \lambda=(13,3,2^7),\qquad
 g_s=g\bigl(s\lambda,((10s)^3),((10s)^3)\bigr),\qquad s\geq1,
\]
and define
\[
 C_s=[z^s]\,
   \frac{1}
   {(1-z)(1-z^2)(1-z^3)\prod_{j=2}^{9}(1-z^j)}.
                                                               \tag{F-028.1}
\]
For the actual, unpadded \(3\)-by-\(3\) permanent,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 q^3_{s\lambda}(10s[3])
       \ \geq\ C_s\ >\
 g_s=[z^s]\frac1{(1-z^2)(1-z^3)}
       \ =\ K_3(s\lambda).
 \quad}                                                        \tag{F-028.2}
\]
Thus a strict actual multiplicity obstruction persists
at every positive dilation of this weight.
This is stronger than positivity of powers of one section:
the lower bound counts independent highest-weight copies.
Combining with A-034, the determinant multiplicity is
exactly \(g_s\) for every \(s\geq1\), and is positive
for every \(s\geq2\).
Hence the strict obstruction persists after the determinant
occurrence obstruction has disappeared.
All these assertions have \(n=m=3\); no IP or BIP padding
range is asserted.

\paragraph{The binary pair uses the full nine-variable Hessian.}
Let \(f=\operatorname{per}_3\), regarded as a cubic on
the nine-dimensional vector space of \(3\)-by-\(3\) matrices.
For \(v\) in that space set
\[
                     h(v)=\det\operatorname{Hess}_9 f(v).
                                                               \tag{F-028.3}
\]
The Hessian here is taken in all nine matrix entries.
It is not the two-variable Hessian of a restricted binary form.
For two matrices \(A,B\), consider the coefficient morphism
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal B:\operatorname{Mat}_3^2
       &\longrightarrow
            \operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^2{}^*
                  \ \oplus\ \operatorname{Sym}^9\mathbb C^2{}^*,\\
 (A,B)&\longmapsto
      \bigl(f(xA+yB),\,h(xA+yB)\bigr).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{F-028.4}
\]
Its source has dimension \(18\), and its target has
dimension \(4+10=14\).
The following complete integer witness proves that
(F-028.4) is dominant:
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}4&1&-1\\1&4&3\\4&0&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&-4\\4&-1&-2\\4&-1&2\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{F-028.5}
\]

Here is an exact specification of the Jacobian and its
nonzero minor. Order the nine matrix coordinates by rows,
numbered \(0,\ldots,8\), and order source columns by
the nine entries of \(A\), then the nine entries of \(B\).
Use target coordinates
\[
 \bigl(f(A+tB):t=0,1,2,3\bigr),\qquad
 \bigl(h(A+tB):t=0,1,\ldots,9\bigr)
                                                               \tag{F-028.6}
\]
in the displayed order. These are invertible Vandermonde
changes from the binary coefficient coordinates.
For a polynomial \(p(v)\), the row at \(t\) is
\[
     \bigl(\partial_0p,\ldots,\partial_8p,\,
            t\partial_0p,\ldots,t\partial_8p\bigr)_{v=A+tB}.
                                                               \tag{F-028.7}
\]
To specify \(h\) and its derivatives without an expanded
degree-nine expression, write
\[
 \mathcal T=
   \{\,(p(0),\,3+p(1),\,6+p(2)):p\in S_3\,\},
 \qquad f(v)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal T}\prod_{a\in T}v_a .
\]
Let \(S(v)=\operatorname{Hess}_9 f(v)\), obtained by
differentiating this six-term sum. For each coordinate \(k\),
\[
 \partial_k h(v)=
       2\sum_{\substack{T\in\mathcal T\\k\in T}}
              \operatorname{adj}(S(v))_{ab},
                  \qquad \{a,b\}=T\setminus\{k\}.
                                                               \tag{F-028.8}
\]
The symmetry of \(S\) makes the order of \(a,b\) immaterial.

Take columns \(0,1,\ldots,13\) of this \(14\)-by-\(18\)
Jacobian at (F-028.5). Its determinant is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Delta=
 74225698308427431762191786581639618962627273701228066915866112824442880000000,
 \\
                       \Delta\equiv8357\pmod{1000003}.
 \end{gathered}                                                 \tag{F-028.9}
\]
The table, formulas, row ordering, and selected columns
are a finite self-contained certificate.
Restricting to these fourteen source directions yields
a square polynomial map with invertible differential.
Translate its source and target, and normalize its linear
part; the resulting formal substitution is \(t+O(t^2)\).
It induces an injection on polynomial coordinate rings,
so (F-028.4) is dominant.

\paragraph{Realizing the dominant pair on the actual permanent orbit.}
Let \(V=\mathbb C^9\), with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_9\).
For an arbitrary cubic \(F\) on \(V\), put
\[
 H_F(v)=\det\operatorname{Hess}_9F(v).
 \]
Its coefficients are homogeneous of degree nine in
the coefficients of \(F\), and it is a degree-nine
form in \(v\). For every \(g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\),
the chain rule gives
\[
 H_{F\circ g}(v)=(\det g)^2 H_F(gv).
                                                               \tag{F-028.10}
\]
Define the binary restrictions \(F|_{12}\) and \(H_F|_{12}\)
by substituting \(v=xe_1+ye_2\).

Every linearly independent pair \(A,B\in V\) extends
to the first two columns of an element \(g\in\operatorname{SL}(V)\).
Indeed extend it to any basis and scale one of the seven
remaining basis vectors to make the determinant one.
For \(F=f\circ g\), (F-028.10) then gives exactly
\[
                     (F|_{12},H_F|_{12})
                         =(f(xA+yB),h(xA+yB)).
                                                               \tag{F-028.11}
\]
Independent pairs form a dense open subset of the pencil
parameter space, and (F-028.4) is dominant. Consequently
the pullback of the entire binary-pair coefficient algebra
along \(F\mapsto(F|_{12},H_F|_{12})\) is injective after
restriction to the actual permanent orbit.
In particular every finite linearly independent family
of binary-pair polynomial functions remains independent
in the permanent orbit-closure coordinate ring.

\paragraph{The complete highest-weight space that survives.}
Use the polynomial coordinate representation
\((g\cdot P)(F)=P(F\circ g)\), dual to the usual action
\(F\mapsto F\circ g^{-1}\) on forms.
For clarity, consider joint bidegree \((a,b)\) in the
two binary forms. Its polynomial representation is
\[
             \operatorname{Sym}^a\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^2
                \ \otimes\
             \operatorname{Sym}^b\operatorname{Sym}^9\mathbb C^2.
                                                               \tag{F-028.12}
\]
A binary highest-weight vector of weight
\(\nu=(\nu_1,\nu_2)\) in (F-028.12) pulls back to a
highest-weight vector for \(\operatorname{GL}_9\) of weight
\[
                  (\nu_1+2b,\nu_2+2b,(2b)^7)
                                                               \tag{F-028.13}
\]
and polynomial coefficient degree \(a+9b\).
To verify this directly, an upper triangular matrix
preserves \(\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\).
Its upper unipotent part acts on the two restricted forms
through the binary upper unipotent group; its determinant
is one. The original highest-weight vector is therefore
fixed. On diagonal matrices, the binary weight contributes
\((\nu_1,\nu_2,0^7)\), while each occurrence of \(H_F\)
contributes the determinant-square weight \(2\mathbf1_9\)
by (F-028.10). This proves (F-028.13), including the
unipotent condition and every diagonal exponent.
The injectivity just proved preserves the entire
highest-weight multiplicity space, not merely one vector.

Take \(a=b=s\) and \(\nu=(11s,s)\).
Then (F-028.13) is \(s\lambda\), and the coefficient
degree is \(10s\). We compute its full binary multiplicity.
Write \(w_k\) for the dimension of weight
\((12s-k,k)\) in (F-028.12). For \(0\leq k\leq s\),
the bounds of \(s\) on the numbers of selected generators
are inactive after padding with zero-weight indices:
every positive index is at least one, so there can be
at most \(k\leq s\) of them in either factor. Hence
\[
 w_k=[z^k]\,
       \frac1{\prod_{i=1}^{3}(1-z^i)
                      \prod_{j=1}^{9}(1-z^j)}
                    \qquad(0\leq k\leq s).
                                                               \tag{F-028.14}
\]
For a binary irreducible of highest weight
\((12s-j,j)\), each weight with second coordinate
between \(j\) and \(12s-j\) occurs once.
Since \(s\leq6s\), subtracting successive weight
dimensions therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{mult}_{(11s,s)}\bigl(\text{representation in (F-028.12)}\bigr)
      &=w_s-w_{s-1}\\
      &=[z^s]\,
       \frac1{(1-z)(1-z^2)(1-z^3)
                            \prod_{j=2}^{9}(1-z^j)}
      =C_s.
\end{aligned}
                                                               \tag{F-028.15}
\]
The injective highest-weight pullback proves
\(q^3_{s\lambda}(10s[3])\geq C_s\).

\paragraph{Exact ordinary upper bound and strictness at every dilation.}
Subtracting the determinant rectangle gives
\[
                s\lambda-2s\mathbf1_9=(11s,s).
 \]
For \(V=U\otimes W\), \(\dim U=\dim W=3\), the identity
\(\det(U\otimes W)=(\det U)^3(\det W)^3\) yields
\[
 g_s=g\bigl((11s,s),((4s)^3),((4s)^3)\bigr).
                                                               \tag{F-028.16}
\]
The two-row rectangular formula proved in O-002 counts
partitions of \(s\) in a \(3\)-by-\(4s\) rectangle,
minus the corresponding count for \(s-1\).
The width \(4s\) is inactive at these two sizes.
Thus
\[
                        g_s=[z^s]\frac1{(1-z^2)(1-z^3)}.
                                                               \tag{F-028.17}
\]
The generating series in (F-028.1) is the product of
that in (F-028.17) and
\[
                           Q(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{9}(1-z^j)^{-1}.
 \]
Every coefficient of \(Q\) is nonnegative and
\([z^s]Q\geq1\) for every \(s\geq0\), by the partition
using only parts one. For \(s\geq1\), the terms with
core degrees \(s\) and zero in this product are distinct,
so
\[
                              C_s\geq g_s+[z^s]Q>g_s.
                                                               \tag{F-028.18}
\]
The general upper bound \(K_3\leq\operatorname{sk}\leq g\)
now proves (F-028.2).
For example the first six values, with \(s=1,\ldots,6\), are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 s&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
 C_s&1&3&5&9&14&24\\
 g_s&0&1&1&1&1&2 .
 \end{array}
\]
Already \(s=2\) gives the strict comparison
\[
              q^3_{(26,6,4^7)}(20[3])\geq3>1=g_2
                         \geq K_3(26,6,4^7).
                                                               \tag{F-028.19}
\]
In fact A-034 proves \(K_3(26,6,4^7)=1\), so both
actual sides are positive already in (F-028.19).

A-034 proves, using actual multiplication by the
highest-weight Hessian coefficient, that
\[
                    K_3(s\lambda)=g_s
                      \quad\hbox{for every }s\geq1,
             \qquad g_s>0\quad(s\geq2).
                                                               \tag{F-028.20}
\]
Together with (F-028.18), this gives infinitely many
actual multiplicity obstructions with both sides positive,
on one explicit ray, at every dilation \(s\geq2\).
The orbit-closure saturation hole at \(s=1\) and the
persisting rank comparison after doubling are separate conclusions.
O-028's earlier eventual statement remains valid and
is superseded on this ray by the exact A-034 construction.

\paragraph{Grothendieck dictionary and scope of the certificate.}
The pencil map and the full Hessian construction are
polynomial morphisms defined by integral formulas.
The nonzero minor proves the coefficient-algebra injection
in characteristic zero; the evaluated-coordinate witness
also requires the two Vandermonde determinants to be
invertible if one works over a localized integral base.
The highest-weight and multigrading statements are the
coordinate-comodule and character constructions of
SGA~3, I, Propositions~4.7.2--4.7.3, pp.~34--36.
The Schur-functor realization and binary weight subtraction
use the characteristic-zero polynomial decomposition
in SGA~6, VI, Theorem~3.3, pp.~392--393, and Macdonald,
Chapter~I, Appendix~A (5.3)--(5.6), pp.~155--156.
The determinant-square factor is an actual covariance
identity, not a virtual Grothendieck-ring equality.
The independent surviving copies are established by
an injective coordinate pullback on the permanent orbit.
The exact positive determinant value is the separate
highest-weight algebra construction A-034; the present
binary-pair dominance supplies the stronger cubic
permanent lower bound \(C_s\).
No priority claim for the present construction is asserted.
