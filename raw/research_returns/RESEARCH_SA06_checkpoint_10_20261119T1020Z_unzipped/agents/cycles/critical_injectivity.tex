\paragraph{C.3am (Refuted, with explicit counterexamples): ordinary restriction injectivity in every critical dimension $N=2r$.}
For every $r\ge2$, there is a specified excellent normal
strictly henselian local complex ring of dimension $2r$,
with regular puncture $X$ and regular punctured principal
divisor $Y$, such that
\[
 \ker\!\left(\mathrm{CH}^r(X)_{\mathrm{alg}}
       \longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^r(Y)_{\mathrm{alg}}\right)
\]
contains a specified class of infinite order. Together with
C.3al, this refutes ordinary restriction injectivity for
every $r\ge2$ and $r+1\le N\le2r$. C.3ao below settles
the remaining range by a different construction.

\begin{proof}
\emph{The source class.}
Use the odd cubic and its elliptic correspondence from C.3r,
with coordinates
\[
 F=\sum_{j=0}^{r-2}(a_j^3+b_j^3)+y_0^3+y_1^3+y_2^3,
 \qquad V=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^{2r},
 \qquad E=V(y_0^3+y_1^3+y_2^3)\subset\mathbb P^2.
\]
For $p=[p_0:p_1:p_2]\in E$, the incidence fibre is
\[
 \Lambda_p=
 \{[-b_0:b_0:\cdots:-b_{r-2}:b_{r-2}:tp_0:tp_1:tp_2]\}
                       \simeq\mathbb P^{r-1}.
\]
Choose $u$ transcendental over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$,
$v^3=-1-u^3$, and put
\[
 p=(u:v:1),\qquad o=(1:-1:0),\qquad
 z=[\Lambda_p]-[\Lambda_o].
\]
C.3r proves, for this same specified point and correspondence,
\[
 z\ne0\quad\hbox{in}\quad
 \mathrm{CH}^r(V)_{\mathbb Q}/
       H\mathrm{CH}^{r-1}(V)_{\mathbb Q},
       \qquad H=c_1(\mathcal O_V(1)).
\]
The proof there uses the integral transverse left inverse
of the two Fermat joins and the Abel--Jacobi map; thus this
nonvanishing is an already proved assertion about the actual
cycle, not an assumption on a general point.

Let
\[
 R=\left(\mathbb C[a_0,b_0,\ldots,a_{r-2},b_{r-2},
                        y_0,y_1,y_2]/(F)\right)_{(a,b,y)}^{\mathrm{sh}},
 \qquad f=a_0.
\]
Then $\dim R=2r$, and $R$ is excellent and normal. Its
puncture $X$ is regular. The coordinate hyperplane section
\[
 W=V\cap\{a_0=0\}
   =\left\{b_0^3+\sum_{j=1}^{r-2}(a_j^3+b_j^3)
                         +y_0^3+y_1^3+y_2^3=0\right\}
                    \subset\mathbb P^{2r-1}
\]
is smooth, so $Y=X\cap V(f)$ is regular as well.
The cone pullback of $z$ is ordinarily algebraically trivial,
using the actual elliptic incidence family, and is nontorsion
by C.3e. We prove that it restricts to zero integrally.

\emph{The restricted family factors through a cubic surface.}
Inside $W$ take the Fermat cubic surface
\[
 S=\{b_0^3+y_0^3+y_1^3+y_2^3=0\}\subset\mathbb P^3.
\]
For $r\ge3$, impose $a_j+b_j=0$ for $1\le j\le r-2$.
The resulting subvariety is the join of $S$ and the
coordinate $\mathbb P^{r-3}$. Its incidence resolution
over $S$ has fibres $\mathbb P^{r-2}$ and defines
\[
 \Theta\in\mathrm{CH}^r(S\times W),
 \qquad \Theta_*:\mathrm{CH}_0(S)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^r(W).
\]
Indeed the incidence has dimension $2+(r-2)=r$, whereas
$\dim(S\times W)=2r$. For $r=2$, we have $S=W$ and take
$\Theta=\Delta_S$, with the same codimension and action.
Regard $E$ as the section $b_0=0$ of $S$. The equation
$a_0=0$ cuts $\Lambda_p$ in its reduced hyperplane
$b_0=0$, and therefore
\[
             z|_W=\Theta_*([p]-[o]).
\]
There is no multiplicity: $a_0=-b_0$ is a nonzero linear
form on each $\Lambda_p$.

\emph{An explicit rationality and zero-cycle calculation.}
Write the coordinates on $S$ as $c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3$.
Fix a primitive cube root $\zeta$. The two lines
\[
 L_0:\ c_0+c_1=c_2+c_3=0,
 \qquad
 L_1:\ c_0+\zeta c_1=c_2+\zeta c_3=0
\]
lie on $S$ and are disjoint: simultaneous satisfaction
forces all four coordinates to vanish. The pencil of
planes through $L_0$ is
$c_0+c_1=t(c_2+c_3)$. Set
\[
 c_1=x,\qquad c_3=y,\qquad
 c_2=z-y,\qquad c_0=tz-x.
\]
The cubic equation becomes $zQ_t$, where
\[
 Q_t=3tx^2+3y^2-3(t^2x+y)z+(t^3+1)z^2.
\]
The symmetric matrix of this conic has determinant
\[
                         \frac94,t(t^3+1).
\]
The line $L_1$ gives the section
\[
                         [x:y:z]=[t:1:1-\zeta],
\]
which satisfies $Q_t=0$ using $\zeta^2+\zeta+1=0$.
After removing the fixed line $L_0$, the residual
conic pencil has no base point: distinct planes meet
only along $L_0$, and there the two residual equations
$tx^2+y^2=0$ for distinct finite $t$ have no common
projective solution; the fibre at infinity gives
$x^2=0$ and the same conclusion. Thus it defines a
morphism $S\to\mathbb P^1$. The general conic fibre
is smooth and has this section. Its function field is thus
$\mathbb C(t)(s)$: projection from the displayed point
parametrizes the smooth conic over $\mathbb C(t)$.
This proves rationality with an explicit pencil and
section.

For the integral zero-cycle assertion one can also work
directly with that pencil. The moving lemma on the smooth
projective surface moves any zero-cycle away from its
finitely many singular fibres and exceptional locus.
Each remaining point lies on a smooth rational conic and
is rationally equivalent, on that fibre, to its section
point. All section points are rationally equivalent on
the rational section. Consequently
\[
                         \mathrm{CH}_0(S)=\mathbb Z
\]
by degree, integrally. The same conclusion follows by
the birational invariance of the zero-cycle group for
smooth projective surfaces, using the explicitly given
rational parametrization. In particular $[p]-[o]=0$
in $\mathrm{CH}_0(S)$, so $z|_W=0$ integrally.

Finally, flat pullback along the punctured line-bundle
cones commutes with the refined hyperplane restriction.
These operations and the displayed rational equivalence
commute with the pointed etale limit defining the strict
henselization. The source class remains nonzero rationally
by C.3e, whereas its target restriction is zero integrally.
This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}
