## Exact scope

The theorem concerns the strict henselization at the vertex of the normal, degree-one-generated full section ring of a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. Its extra condition is the vanishing of H²(V, Ω¹ over Q tensor L^j) for every j≥1. It does not silently replace absolute differentials by relative differentials. Under that condition the conclusion is integral: CH² of the puncture is CH₀(V)/(L CH¹(V)), and the ordinary algebraic-equivalence quotient is Z/gZ, where g is the positive gcd of intersection degrees of L with integral curves. The stronger assertion for arbitrary three-dimensional local germs is not made.

## Proof checks

1. **Resolution and infinitesimal neighbourhoods.** The section-ring assumptions identify the blow-up with the total space of L inverse. Strict henselization preserves the Artinian quotients because the residue field is already algebraically closed. The closed-fibre thickenings are therefore the actual split truncated graded algebras O⊕L⊕…⊕L^(a−1), with their projections to V. This splitting is used, correctly, at both the relative K-group and determinant steps.

2. **Finite detection, not an unjustified formal limit.** Morrow's Theorem 3.7(i) applies to the noetherian quasi-excellent Q-schemes in this square. Its relative pro-isomorphism supplies the displayed pro Mayer–Vietoris exact sequence. Since K₀(R) and each K₀(R/m^a) are Z with identity transition maps, quotienting the graph n↦(n,n[O_Z]) identifies the cokernel with constant K₀(Z). Thus K₀(Z) maps monomorphically to the pro-system K₀(V_a). The kernel system consists of decreasing subgroups of one fixed group and inclusion transition maps. Pro-zero therefore forces one of these subgroups to be zero. The claim of injectivity at all sufficiently large finite thickenings is justified and stronger than pointwise separation by an inverse limit.

3. **Relative K₂ retains integral information.** Weibel's Consequence 1.4 explicitly says that the relative K-groups of a nilpotent ideal in a Q-algebra are Q-vector spaces. Consequently Goodwillie's rational comparison is an isomorphism of the underlying integral abelian groups here. For A[t]/(t^a), in positive degree j<a the quotient of t^jΩ¹_A plus A t^(j−1)dt by d(b t^j) has the unique horizontal representative t^j(ω−db/j). Horizontal inclusion is injective and surjective. Under t′=u t it transforms by u^j, so the global summand is Ω¹ over Q tensor L^j. The relation a t^(a−1)dt=0 removes degree a. No extra conormal summand remains. The displayed draft had a literal comma in `t^j,db`; the owner was asked to replace it by a space.

4. **Descent spectral sequence and determinant.** Thomason–Trobaugh's Zariski descent theorem applies to both K-theory spectra and hence their fibre. On a surface, only H¹ of relative K₁ and H² of relative K₂ can contribute to relative K₀, since relative K₀ sheaf is zero. The latter term vanishes by hypothesis, and the H¹ term has no incoming or outgoing differential. The determinant identifies the surviving piece with relative Picard. The split restriction then identifies SK₀(V_a) with SK₀(V), and finite detection plus the section p* identifies SK₀(Z) with SK₀(V).

5. **Integral vanishing of CH₀(Z).** At each closed point z of the proper regular resolution, a regular system of parameters with first parameter the equation of V produces a one-dimensional integral transverse germ. Its closure D has zero-dimensional closed fibre. The proper image of a non-quasi-finite locus would meet the closed point of the local base, so D is quasi-finite and proper, hence finite. Its normalization is finite by excellence and local because it is an integral finite algebra over a henselian local ring. It is a DVR with residue field k. A uniformizer has the unique closed point as divisor; proper pushforward kills [z] with coefficient one. This is an integral argument.

6. **Codimension-two Riemann–Roch.** Fulton's Example 15.3.6 gives the two composites with multiplier (−1)^(2−1)(2−1)!=−1, so CH² and F²K₀/F³K₀ are integrally isomorphic. The limiting argument through smooth pointed étale blow-up neighbourhoods carries vector bundles, support filtrations, cycles, and finite rational-equivalence witnesses to the strict-local Z. Its F³ group is generated by point classes and vanishes by the previous step. Thus the Chern-class comparison and pullback really give CH²(Z)≅CH₀(V).

7. **Localization and ordinary algebraic equivalence.** Self-intersection sends the boundary map to multiplication by −L. Actual flat pullback on the fixed products with parameter curves preserves ordinary algebraic equivalence. The already audited C.3e injection modulo that equivalence is the same map as the resulting surjection, so it is an isomorphism. Zero-cycles on V modulo ordinary algebraic equivalence are classified by degree, yielding Z/gZ. Positivity of g follows from ampleness.

8. **Vanishing examples.** Akizuki–Kodaira–Nakano gives H²(Ω¹ over k tensor L^j)=0 on every such characteristic-zero surface. For k=Qbar the absolute and relative sheaves agree. For arbitrary k, the exact differential sequence and H²(L^j)=0 give the absolute condition; the possibly infinite constant vector space Ω¹_(k/Q) causes no problem because coherent cohomology on this finite affine cover commutes with direct sums. Serre duality proves the additional H²(L^j) vanishing for K3 surfaces and for surfaces with p_g=0, exactly as claimed.
