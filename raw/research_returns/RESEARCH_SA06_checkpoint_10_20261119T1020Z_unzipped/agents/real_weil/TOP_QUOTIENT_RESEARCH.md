# Residual top ordinary quotient: research record

**Updated 2026-11-19, after the certification of C.3ab and C.3ad.**

The unrestricted groups still at issue are ordinary
\(B^{N-1}(\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak m\})\), for \(N\ge3\).
Here ordinary algebraic equivalence always means differences of fibres on
actual fixed products with smooth parameter curves. An equality in a
henselized product or a compatible system of classes on infinitesimal
neighbourhoods is not an actual-product family.

## 1. Certified positive subset and its precise limit

The final, independently checked `C3_surface_cone_top.tex` proves, integrally,
for the stated smooth surface cones with
\(H^2(V,\Omega^1_{V/\mathbb Q}\otimes L^j)=0\) for all \(j>0\),
\[
 CH^2(X)=CH_0(V)/(L\,CH^1(V)),\qquad B^2(X)=\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z.
\]
This includes every such cone over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), and over arbitrary
algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields when \(H^2(V,L^j)=0\) for
all \(j>0\). It is a restricted-family result. It does not settle the
unrestricted top question or establish finiteness for all complex surface
cones. Root and Frobenius independently checked the complete eight-step
argument and the exact primary sources. It is integrated after C.3aa.

The final `C3_rational_top_cones.tex` (C.3ad), independently audited by
root and Frobenius, additionally proves for every such smooth projective
cone over Qbar in every dimension d+1>=3 that
`CH^d(X)_Q = CH0(V)_Q / L.CH^(d-1)(V)_Q` and `B^d(X)_Q=0`.
This is rational in dimensions d>2 and remains a restricted smooth-cone
statement; it is not counted as main-gate movement.

## 2. An explicit proper-curve family for every field \(K_2\) symbol

**Auxiliary lemma, proved; independently checked by Frobenius. No Chow
consequence is inferred.** Let \(k\) be algebraically closed and let
\(a,b\in k^*\). There exist a smooth projective connected curve \(C/k\),
points \(P,Q\in C(k)\), and \(\xi\in K_2(C)\) such that
\[
                    P^*\xi-Q^*\xi=\{a,b\}\in K_2(k).
\]

If either input is 1 the symbol is zero and the claim is immediate. Otherwise
put \(c=(a-1)^2(b-1)^2/(ab)\ne0\), and consider in
\(\mathbb P^1_f\times\mathbb P^1_g\) the closure of
\[
                 (f-1)^2(g-1)^2=c f g.                 \tag{T.1}
\]
Choose a component through \((a,b)\) and normalize its reduced structure.
There are no vertical or horizontal components: substituting any fixed
value in either coordinate never makes the homogeneous equation identically
zero. Thus both coordinate maps on the normalization are nonconstant and
surjective. The only point of (T.1) on \(f=0\) is \((0,1)\), and the
derivative with respect to \(f\) there is \(-c\). Hence this is a smooth
point of the original curve, with one point \(Q\) on the normalization.
The normalization of the chosen integral component is a smooth proper
connected curve over the algebraically closed field. A point \(P\) over
\((a,b)\) exists even if that point of (T.1) is singular.

The boundary points of the curve have the following forms:
\[
 (f=0,g=1),\quad(f=\infty,g=1),\quad
 (g=0,f=1),\quad(g=\infty,f=1).
\]
Consequently every tame residue of \(\{f,g\}\in K_2(k(C))\) is 1.
The localization sequence for a smooth curve therefore lifts this symbol
to \(\xi\in K_2(C)\). The exact primary input is Quillen (1973), Section 7,
Proposition 3.2, equation (3.3), printed p.127, together with the filtered
limit over nonempty affine opens; the geometric Gersten theorem, Section 7,
Theorem 5.11, printed pp.133–134, gives the injectivity
\(K_2(\mathcal O_{C,x})\to K_2(k(C))\) needed to identify evaluations
with the following local expressions. These statements were directly
opened in the primary PDF, not inferred from a later survey.

Near \(Q\), the curve equation gives
\[
 f=u(g-1)^2,\qquad u=(f-1)^2/(cg)\in\mathcal O_{C,Q}^*.
\]
The Steinberg relation shows
\(\{g-1,g\}=\{g,-1\}\), which is killed by 2. Thus
\(\{f,g\}=\{u,g\}\) in the function field, hence also for its unique
local lift. Its specialization at \(Q\) is \(\{u(Q),1\}=0\).
At \(P\), both functions are units and specialization is \(\{a,b\}\).
This proves the lemma. The proof also applies in characteristic 2: the
derivative \(-c\) at \(Q\) remains nonzero and the same Steinberg identity
holds. The integral statement for field \(K_2\) uses the usual Matsumoto
presentation of \(K_2(k)\) by Steinberg symbols.

This prevents treating a bare \(K_2(k)\) summand in a prospective
normal-crossing calculation as automatically rigid under proper parameter
curves. It does **not** show that a family on an exceptional divisor lifts
to its regular resolution. It also does not assert that all higher Quillen
\(K_n(k)\) are generated by Milnor symbols. There is, however, a precise
further consequence: for n>=2, multiplying the constructed class by
constant classes \(\{a_3,\ldots,a_n\}\) realizes the image of every
Milnor symbol \(\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\) in Quillen K-theory as an actual
proper-curve evaluation difference. Additivity treats a finite sum.
Thus no Milnor-symbol term in a prospective top-weight exceptional-divisor
descent calculation is automatically rigid. The map from that exceptional
calculation to actual fixed-product families on the puncture is still
required; no such map is constructed by this observation alone.

## 3. The analogous absolute-differential spanning statement

**Auxiliary statement proved at the nilpotent-parameter level.** In
characteristic zero, every element of \(\Omega^1_{k/\mathbb Q}\) is a sum
of differences of evaluations of global absolute 1-forms on smooth proper
curves. It suffices to treat \(a\,db\), since these generate the module.
There is nothing to prove if \(b\) is algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\).
Take the fixed elliptic curve \(E:y^2=x^3-x\) over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), with its regular form \(\omega=dx/y\), and
choose \(P=(b,y)\in E(k)\), where \(y^2=b^3-b\). Since \(b\) is
transcendental, \(y\ne0\). If \(Q\) is an algebraic point, then
\[
          P^*(a y\omega)-Q^*(a y\omega)=a\,db.
\]
The coefficient \(ay\) is a scalar multiplying the differential; no
differentiation of that scalar is implicit. The absolute form \(\omega\)
is the base extension of the algebraic invariant differential, and it
vanishes on pullback to an algebraic point because
\(\Omega^1_{\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q}=0\).

For \(E_\epsilon=E\times\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/\epsilon^2\),
the calculation in C.3ab identifies
\(\mathcal K_2(E_\epsilon,E)=\Omega^1_{E/\mathbb Q}\).
The dimension-one descent spectral sequence has only columns 0 and 1,
so its edge map
\[
 K_2(E_\epsilon,E)\longrightarrow
 H^0(E,\mathcal K_2(E_\epsilon,E))
\]
is surjective. Consequently the preceding differences of forms really
are evaluations of actual relative \(K_2\) classes on the fixed proper
curve times the dual numbers. The same finite-order argument works for
each specified positive homogeneous degree of a split truncated polynomial
thickening. This is still a statement about finite thickenings.

## 4. The missing actual-family step

For a complex surface cone, the new part that C.3ab does not eliminate
comes from the absolute coefficient term in
\[
0\to\Omega^1_{k/\mathbb Q}\otimes\mathcal O_V
 \to\Omega^1_{V/\mathbb Q}\to\Omega^1_{V/k}\to0.
\]
The cohomological candidate is a quotient of
\(\Omega^1_{k/\mathbb Q}\otimes H^2(V,L^j)\); the relative term in
\(H^2(\Omega^1_{V/k}L^j)\) vanishes by Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano.
The elliptic-curve construction explains why an assertion that all such
classes are constant on proper parameter curves would be false at the
differential level. It does not prove that the corresponding class on
\(V_a\times E\) is the restriction of a class on \(Z\times E\).

The two exact missing steps are: (i) construct the compatible classes or
objects on all exceptional thickenings, with the desired endpoint classes;
(ii) algebraize a family on the **actual** fixed product and, in the
henselian case, descend it from the completion. Finite-level injectivity of
\(K_0(Z)\to K_0(V_a)\), proved in C.3ab, alone implies neither step and
does not imply injectivity after quotienting by algebraic equivalence.

This is not an abstract precaution without a counterexample. Bloch,
Esnault, and Kerz (2014), *Deformation of algebraic cycle classes in
characteristic zero*, Appendix B, Proposition B.5, directly proves that
\(K_0(Y_{\mathbb C[[t]]})_\mathbb Q\to
(\varprojlim K_0(Y_{\mathbb C[t]/t^n}))\otimes\mathbb Q\)
is not surjective for a smooth projective even-dimensional \(Y/\mathbb Q\)
with positive geometric genus. Their Lemma B.7 nevertheless realizes the
specified finite-order differential contributions by actual moving point
sections. The counterexample assembles these with unbounded differential
complexity across orders. Both the complete statement and proof of
Proposition B.5 and Lemmas B.2, B.3, B.7 were read in the primary text.

The ample conormal bundle in the cone problem makes the relevant coherent
cohomology vanish in sufficiently high degree, unlike the trivial
\(\mathbb C[[t]]\) deformation in that counterexample. This is a possible
route to a stronger theorem, **not a proof** of a uniform algebraization
statement. Ordinary zero-cycle moving on a resolution and a proper-parameter
version of finite-order algebraization remain the next precise targets.

## 5. Literature examined and rejected in this scope

1. Kohrita (2024), *The algebraic part of motivic cohomology*, arXiv
   1512.02320v3, 10 April 2024, Theorem 1.1, Definition 2.3 and Proposition
   2.10: the general motivic equivalence uses smooth parameters and
   semiabelian varieties. It cannot silently replace actual proper-curve
   families on our non-finite-type puncture.
2. Kerz–Saito–Tamme (2019), *K-theory of non-Archimedean rings I*,
   Documenta Mathematica 24, 1365–1411, Section 1.1, p.1366: directly read
   the formulation of the \(K_0\)-algebraization problem and the warning
   referring to BEK Appendix B. Formal GAGA for coherent sheaves is not
   surjectivity on an inverse limit of Grothendieck groups.
3. Results of Lüders, Esnault–Wittenberg/Bloch, Saito–Sato and
   Binda–Krishna on relative zero-cycles over henselian **DVRs** do not
   directly apply to a resolution proper over the higher-dimensional
   local ring in this question. Lüders (2019), *Algebraization for
   zero-cycles and the p-adic cycle class map*, Mathematical Research
   Letters26(2),557-585, was subsequently opened in full at
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.05622 (v3,28October2019). Theorem1.2,
   Proposition5.1 and its proof, and Corollary5.4, authorpp1-2,9-12,
   were read directly. The introduction and Section5 explicitly assume
   a smooth projective scheme over an excellent henselian DVR, despite
   the abstract's broader phrase “henselian local ring.” Its idelic
   proof may motivate a separately proved endpoint extension, but
   would still not give the one-dimension-higher proper-parameter
   family required here. The other listed works were search leads;
   no theorem from their unopened full text is claimed as an input.
4. A prospective normal-crossing exceptional divisor with spherical dual
   complex could produce a field \(K_n\) term in a descent computation.
   The resolution, exact differentials and actual-family survival must
   all be proved. The lemma in Section 2 defeats the simplest claimed
   rigidity for the \(K_2\) case. No top counterexample is asserted.

## 6. Additional local-cycle literature check

Dao and Kurano, *Hochster's theta pairing and numerical equivalence*,
full primary author PDF at
https://www.isc.meiji.ac.jp/~kurano/ronbun/kurano32.pdf, was opened.
Remark6.4, authorpp20-21, proves that its actual-product notion of
algebraic equivalence implies numerical equivalence. Its local numerical
Chow groups and Conjecture3.2 are not the ordinary quotient sought here;
the numerical factorization supplies no missing ordinary-family theorem.
Only these exact portions, not the entire paper, were read in this pass.

Ceresa and Collino (1983), *Some remarks on algebraic equivalence of cycles*,
Pacific Journal of Mathematics105(2),285-290, was also opened in the primary
9-page scan. The introduction and Theorem1.1 with its proof, pp285-287,
concern exceptional lines and Abel-Jacobi on projective nodal threefolds.
Their statement does not assert survival after localization at the node,
and no such local consequence is inferred. The result is therefore not
used as a counterexample to the top local quotient.

## 7. Descent of an already existing actual K0-family from completion

**Auxiliary reduction proved at the K0 level; this does not construct the
missing completed family.** Let (R,m,k) be an excellent henselian local
ring with a specified coefficient field k equal to its residue field.
Let Z be a quasi-compact, quasi-separated R-scheme, and write
\(\widehat Z=Z\times_R\widehat R\). For either scheme W, define
\(A_{K_0}(W)\) to be the subgroup of K0 of perfect complexes generated by
\[
 i_{t_1}^*\beta-i_{t_0}^*\beta,
 \qquad \beta\in K_0(W\times_k T),
\]
where T is a smooth projective connected k-curve and t0,t1 are k-points.
All fibre pullbacks are derived pullbacks of perfect complexes. Then
\[
 K_0(Z)/A_{K_0}(Z)\longrightarrow
 K_0(\widehat Z)/A_{K_0}(\widehat Z)
\]
is injective, integrally.

Indeed the completion map R→Rhat is regular by excellence. Popescu's
theorem writes Rhat as a filtered colimit of smooth finitely presented
R-algebras A_i (Stacks, Theorem16.12.1, Tag07GC). Suppose the image of
alpha in K0(Zhat) is a finite sum of the indicated fibre differences.
Keep the finitely many curves and points fixed over k. Each of the
systems Z_(A_i) and Z_(A_i)×_kT has quasi-compact, quasi-separated terms
and affine transition maps. The category of perfect complexes commutes
with such inverse limits (Stacks, Lemma36.29.3, Tag09RF; its exact
hypotheses are Situation36.29.1, Tag09RC). Thus the finitely many
perfect complexes representing all beta descend to one A_i. Increasing
i further also descends the equality in K0: by the presentation of K0
of a triangulated category, that equality uses only finitely many
isomorphisms and distinguished-triangle relations. Morphisms and their
cones descend by the same limit theorem. Consequently the desired
finite sum of fibre differences equals the pullback of alpha already
in K0(Z_(A_i)).

The structure map A_i→Rhat induces an R-algebra map A_i→k. Since A_i is
smooth over R and (R,m) is a henselian pair, this map lifts to an
R-algebra map A_i→R (Stacks, Lemma15.13.3, Tag0H74). Pull back every
perfect complex and every descended relation along this one map.
The resulting objects lie on the actual products Z×_kT, and their
fibre differences sum to alpha in K0(Z). This proves the assertion.
The maps to R preserve the fixed coefficient field because they are
R-algebra maps; no extension or change of parameter fields occurs.

The argument concerns **actual** objects over the completion. It does
not turn a compatible sequence of K0 classes on exceptional thickenings
into an object or class over Zhat×T, and makes no claim of K0 continuity
for that inverse limit. It also does not assert, without a separately
verified Riemann–Roch comparison, that the analogous Chow quotient for
an arbitrary non-finite-type regular scheme equals this K0 quotient.
For the top problem, this isolates the harder step as the construction
of an actual completed family, rather than its subsequent descent to
the henselian base. The exact Stacks statements and relevant proofs
were opened on19November2026; this reduction is not counted as a solved
case of the top integral finiteness problem.
