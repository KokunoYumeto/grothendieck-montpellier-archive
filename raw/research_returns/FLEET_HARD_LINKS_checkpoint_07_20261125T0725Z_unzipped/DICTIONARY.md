# Geometric and arithmetic dictionary

This dictionary records mathematical translations, not analogies asserted as theorems. A connection to motives, weights, or K-theory is asserted only where a map or identity is specified. Permanent registry tags are pending assignment by the owner; local tags below are stable within this workbench.

## A: level spaces and finite-group slices

**A-CONNECTED.** The equations S_k are homogeneous integral polynomials in the matrix entries. Their common zero scheme is defined over Z; after base change to C it parametrizes individual level directions. On a Grassmannian, restricting S_k to the tautological subbundle gives a section of its kth symmetric dual power. The simultaneous zero scheme parametrizes linear spaces on which those polynomials vanish. This is a scheme-theoretic reformulation, not a smoothness or component theorem. The connected-partition identities have rational coefficients, so their displayed normalization is over Q; the unnormalized matching equations remain integral.

**A-ZETA.** The squarefree coefficient of det(I−diag(x)A)^{-1} is per(A), by MacMahon's theorem. The inverse determinant is the generating function of traces on symmetric powers; equivalently it is the symmetric-power operation complementary to exterior powers. This gives a precise λ-ring interpretation of the generating identity. It does not turn a coefficient constraint into an eigenvalue or weight bound. Archive context: `ours/structural/GCT.md`, §4.5, names this matrix-zeta route; the proof here identifies its homogeneous coefficient ideal exactly.

**A-CYC-02 and A-SYM6.** A finite row or column action splits a complex representation into eigenspaces. The proved dimension bounds apply to invariant spaces or single-character spaces under their stated hypotheses. The projectors are finite-group idempotents in characteristic zero. No assertion that these are algebraic correspondences on a smooth projective variety, or that the decomposition controls arbitrary level spaces, is made.

**A-LIE4.** For the graded ideal of the four-by-four equations on S_1=0, the linear stabilizer has one-dimensional scalar Lie algebra. The exact coefficient matrix is integral and has a rank-241 minor nonzero modulo 1009. The scalar kernel gives the matching characteristic-zero upper rank bound. Every linearly acting algebraic torus preserving this ideal therefore acts trivially on the Grassmannian. This concerns the ideal, not an unproved identification with its radical. Archive context: the proposed torus route in `ours/structural/AG_HODGE_ANT.md`, B.2 and B.7.1, needs precisely the group action whose existence is obstructed here.

**A-KLEIN-EXACT.** The level ideal in the invariant ring of a non-real three-dimensional PSL₂(7) component is (Φ₄,Φ₆). Its invariant quotient is a cusp ring C[Φ₁₄,Φ₂₁]/(Φ₂₁²−Φ₁₄³). Pulling back to the representation cuts out twenty-four lines, the cone over the ordinary flex orbit. A prime ideal in the invariant quotient can have reducible inverse image; this is a finite quotient phenomenon, not a contradiction. The original numerical slice is located in `ours/structural/MOONSHINE_LEECH_ALGEBRA.md`, A2. The exact coefficient normalization and the mixed-pairing restrictions are proved here.

**A-AMPLIFICATION.** The rook polynomial of a direct sum is a product. Its logarithm is consequently additive, and zero loci of all positive rook numbers are compatible with block sums. This gives superadditivity of the extremal dimension and its ratio limit. It does not provide an additive category of motives or a motivic measure.

## A: the weighted cubic bound

**A-WEIGHTED-CUBIC.** On the integral hyperplane S1=0, a rational linear embedding sends an n×n matrix to its n² shifted entries and 2n scaled marginal sums. The first three weighted coordinate moments become 0, 2S2, and 3S3/2. Over Q this embeds the locus defined by the first three level equations into a weighted linear/quadratic/cubic intersection in affine space of dimension n²+2n. Polarization turns a linear space on that intersection into a coordinatewise-product orthogonality relation. The stabilizer algebra of the square is a finite product algebra, hence a partition of the coordinate set; its nonzero idempotents are the block indicators. The arithmetic of possible zero-weight blocks and the marginal-sum relation force the level space to miss this stabilizer. Mirandola–Zémor then gives 3 dim L + dim H ≤ n²+2n. This is a statement over Q and C with explicitly nonzero denominators, not an all-characteristic theorem. It uses finite-dimensional commutative algebras; no motivic weight or K-theory inequality is inferred.

**A-BORDER-TRANSFER-CUBIC.** The graph's checked transfer turns the preceding dimension inequality into a lower bound for the coefficientwise closure of affine determinant representations. With permanent order m the bound is m²−2m+1−floor((m²−2)/3). The closure is an algebraic-geometric orbit/image question, while the proof here uses the exact cited transfer statement. We do not identify the level-space parameter scheme with the determinant orbit closure. Archive connection: the graph's GCT transfer, L-gct-077, and the exact public definition of BorderDetExpression.

## B: positive circuits, support, and quadratic extraction

**B-SUPPORT and B-FOUR.** A variable-input AND/OR DAG lifts to a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients. Its image in the idempotent Boolean support semiring forgets multiplicities and powers. Thus repeated-variable monomials can witness an edge without contributing to the squarefree quadratic piece. The extraction arguments are statements about a positive graded semiring and this quotient, not about cancellation in a field or equality of schemes. Four missing induced matching edges require four products outside the extracted quadratic circuit. Cover-gain equalities then restrict how these products may enter the quadratic frame. The optimal six- and seven-deletion gain-four profiles are both excluded; in the latter, the one-visible branch reduces to a sharing-preserving twelve-clause DAG bound, and the two/three-visible branches have complete finite refutations. The finite Boolean templates are proved supersets of the relevant DAG classes; their RUP refutations certify those finite obstructions, not the unrestricted conjecture. Archive connection: the companion circuit paper's minimum-degree extraction, full-carrier erasure, and at-most-three-missing theorem.

**B-DEGREE-TWO.** In the specified shared two-level bilinear architecture, a product of a pure X-clause and a pure Y-clause is a combinatorial rectangle. The absence of a four-cycle constrains the safe rows of an unsafe rectangle. Under the universal-row hypothesis each unsafe signal can be replaced simultaneously by one row, with its shared wires preserved. This is a support-semiring normalization and a gate-count argument. It is not an arbitrary tensor-rank decomposition, and does not settle all circuits of bidegree (2,2).

**B-COMPONENT.** The lowest bihomogeneous component can be extracted without circuit growth by the classical Jerrum–Snir theorem. This corresponds to taking the initial part for a positive grading. A Boolean support identity does not, by itself, compare the circuit cost of the missing higher-support edges with the cost of this initial part. The proposed additional charge is therefore an open inequality, not an automatic consequence of a grading.

**B-WEIGHT and B-NEGATION.** Bounded-weight correctness is a promise on Boolean points and gives no identity of the formal polynomials. Singer convolution instead uses the cyclic group algebra and its Fourier diagonalization to compute incidence outputs with general Boolean gates. Negation reduction is invoked at its source-verified budget. Neither construction is a correspondence or a weight decomposition of an algebraic variety.

## C: counting hypersurfaces, formal groups, and slopes

**C-CONSTRUCTION and C-SCALAR.** The incidence polynomial is an integral polynomial whose designated squarefree coefficient is #SAT. After homogenization and the explicit smooth-padding construction it defines a smooth Calabi–Yau hypersurface over Fp. The coefficient of (x0⋯x_{d−1})^{p−1} in F^{p−1} is the Frobenius scalar on its one-dimensional top coherent cohomology. This is the concrete bridge from a combinatorial count to geometry over a finite field; the paper proves the smoothness, cohomology normalization, and point-count congruence. Archive connection: `ours/COUNTING_VARIETIES_cp03/counting/`, the construction and first Hasse–Witt calculation.

**C-HEIGHT and C-ORBIT.** Stienstra's logarithm for an integral lift has coefficients a_m/m, where a_m is a coefficient of the (m−1)st power of that lift. On successive height strata, the normalized coefficient a_{p^r}/p^{r−1} detects the next term of the p-series. For the explicit counting equation, the early a_{p^r} become unit multiples of balanced replica counts T_M. At r=2 the cyclic action on p of the p+1 replicas gives T_{p+1}=S²+pO_p. On S≡0 the second height test is O_p mod p. This is an integral congruence controlling the formal group, not a claim that the integer S determines the group. Logically equivalent formulas can define different hypersurfaces and different second height strata.

**C-HEIGHT-COMPLEXITY.** The rank of the top coherent group is one, but the integral coefficient precision needed to distinguish height strata carries modular-counting hardness. For the full exact height, the crystalline slope-less-than-one part identifies finite height h by slope 1−1/h and multiplicity h; its absence gives infinite height. The cohomological rank gives a finite stopping bound. The zeta-function algorithm is an upper algorithm; the lower modular-counting reductions do not match its full complexity. This separates a geometric rank bound from the cost of a succinct presentation.

**C-POINT-COUNTS.** Rational point counts give Frobenius power sums. Newton identities recover the second elementary symmetric function integrally. On the Hasse-zero locus its p-adic valuation detects height two. At p=2 division by2 requires one extra bit of point-count precision, and the paper exhibits two smooth quintics that show the weaker precision fails. This is a precise use of Frobenius eigenvalues and their p-adic slopes, not a complex absolute-value estimate.

**C-CHARACTER and C-TRACE.** The equivariant Cech generator has diagonal character (product λ_i)^{-1}, and Frobenius sends its character to the pth power. A first variation belongs to the Frobenius-twisted Hom module; it is not obtained by differentiating the absolute Frobenius map of the base. In the richer succinct Artin-scheme/projector model, constant-rank images can encode the isolation bit of a formula. Trace hardness depends on that presentation, especially the non-split finite field blocks and base-change projectors. An explicitly presented split matrix has no such hidden input. Archive connection: the parity/pencil and finite-Weil-object sections of `ours/VPVNP_WIRE_GEOMETRY_cp01/`; the precise model correction is part of this work.

**C-WITNESS-DEGREE.** For fixed integral data of a general-linear-group orbit, every bounded-degree restriction map is an integer matrix. Its rank and separation properties stabilize outside finitely many primes. A Groebner-elimination spread handles the case of no separating equation, giving c_p=c_0, including infinity, outside finitely many primes. This is fixed-data spreading over an open subset of Spec Z. It supplies neither a uniform size-dependent bound nor unrestricted commutation of orbit closure with every special fiber. Archive connection: the graph's GCT witness-degree question, recorded in the crosswalk.

**C-CLASS-GROUP.** A composite finite abelian character table has zero2×2 minors whenever the column quotient lies in the kernel of a nontrivial character. The explicit imaginary-quadratic example realizes such a pair by distinct degree-one primes. Chebotarev can produce primes in classes under a stated Hecke-GRH hypothesis; it cannot remove the finite group obstruction. No all-minors statement follows from a discriminant or conductor bound. The prime-order contrast is the classical Chebotarev/Fourier theorem used in the attached Tao source.

**C-DIV.** For nonzero integral F with F(0)≠0, the integral of the number of roots of positive p-adic valuation, counted with multiplicity, equals v_p(F(0))−v_p(content(F)). Summing against log p is the product formula log(|F(0)|/content(F)). Divisibility by a rising factorial gives the stated factorial lower bound. The translated-root calculations distinguish multiplicity at one root from many distinct prescribed factors. This is an arithmetic height identity with content; it does not prove a height-free division lower bound. Archive connection: the graph's certified polynomial examples L-gct-132 and147.

**C-THETA.** The parity of a theta characteristic is the Arf invariant of a quadratic refinement of the symplectic Weil pairing on2-torsion. An affine family entirely of odd characteristics has maximal dimension g−1. Its translation space is isotropic, and the symplectic quotient determines a unique odd coset for each isotropic(g−1)-space. The counting and covering statements are exact finite symplectic geometry, consistent with the classical Gopel description. Passing from this classification to a proof-complexity mass lower bound needs an additional premise; no such premise is imported from a geometric analogy.

## Limits of the dictionary

The common language of graded rings, finite group actions, Frobenius, and base change gives precise translations where a map has been written down. It does not make the three main open complexity questions equivalent. No use of EGA, SGA, or FGA is claimed merely from terminology; where a paper relies on a theorem of those works its source must be the supplied edition with the exact locator. There is no asserted motivic equivalence, lambda-ring lower bound, or K-theoretic obstruction beyond the explicit symmetric-power generating identity above.

## Four missing graph edges: the maximal cover gain is excluded

The four missing quadratic witnesses are not merely four absent coefficients: in a positive polynomial computing an edge function they must be supplied by higher monomials supported on those same edge assignments. When adjoining them increases the vertex-cover number by four, they form an induced matching. The remaining six-deletion, one-excess profile has four private product gates and two additions. Functional rigidity forces two quartic signals, each covering two matching edges. The no-four-cycle hypothesis then restricts each full linear context to its two designated vertices; an outside variable would create a four-cycle through an old star center. The two contexts affect disjoint quadratic-product gates. Their four vertices, together with the centers of the unaffected stars, cover the whole graph using at most K+2 vertices, which contradicts the assumed gain-four deficit.

In geometric language, the missing edges are specified squarefree quadratic monomials in the positive-support presentation, and the proof is a restriction on how their coordinate two-planes can be recovered from higher-degree supports while retaining a small positive arithmetic circuit. No passage to a general field, scheme-theoretic degeneration, lambda-ring or motive is claimed: positivity and Boolean support are essential. The attached relation to the programme is the edge-function/read-one correspondence and the four-missing list in the second zero-adjoined-semiring companion (paper4, Corollary5.34); the new argument closes that list's maximal cover-gain row.

## Uniform height as an exact-counting oracle

For a varying unary-encoded prime, the promised height-two decision for the counting hypersurface carries enough information to recover the full integer satisfying count. Parsimonious shifts make the count S+a. A fixed-characteristic height-two gadget enforces vanishing of the first Hasse–Witt scalar while its second quotient tests whether S+a is nonzero modulo p. All p shifts reveal S modulo p, and polynomially bounded primes have a product exceeding the possible count range. Chinese remaindering therefore recovers S. The reduction is polynomial and nonadaptive; it does not assert the same hardness at one fixed characteristic.

This is a comparison across fibers in varying finite characteristics, not a claim that all query hypersurfaces are fibers of one fixed smooth integral scheme. The family of counting constructions supplies the explicit fibers. Their formal-group strata turn modular information into a global integer through CRT. The archive connection is COUNTING_VARIETIES and the height question in MAP_COMPLEXITY; no additional K-theoretic or motivic compatibility is asserted.

## Exact height in polynomial space

The top Artin–Mazur height is the first index j for which the j-th primitive crystalline characteristic coefficient is not divisible by p^j, with a finite cohomological cutoff. The finite-height slope segment has length h and slope1−1/h; its endpoint coefficient has valuation h−1, whereas earlier integral coefficients have valuation at least their indices. Infinite height has no slope below one.

Lauder–Wan finite trace matrices compute the necessary extension-field point counts. Their arrays and coefficients may be exponentially large, but Teichmuller lifting by doubled precision, balanced splitting-function products, repeated matrix squaring and balanced integer reconstruction form Boolean circuits of polynomial depth. Hesse–Allender–Barrington uniform division supplies exact quotient and remainder gates. Recursive evaluation of one output bit at a time uses polynomial space. This proves FPSPACE membership for binary input characteristic, including infinity. It does not prove PSPACE-hardness, nor infer small space merely from exponential time.

The arithmetic reading concerns Frobenius on rational crystalline cohomology and the Newton polygon of its characteristic polynomial. Only the slopes below one encode the formal group; ambient projective cohomology is removed explicitly. The proof uses no unproved general motivic algorithm, positivity assertion or complexity of arbitrary cohomological bases. The geometry supplies a finite spectral criterion, while the finite trace formula supplies access to its integer coefficients.

## One deleted addition and smaller cover gain

For a C4-free graph, a minimum-degree extraction that removes exactly one addition has a single private product-DAG visible to the quadratic frame. Restriction outside any cover of the old edge graph turns that output into a conjunctive normal form. Its clauses cannot contain the outer vertices of a missing two-edge path, whereas a maximal independent set through the middle vertex forces just such a minimal cover clause. Thus the uncovered missing graph is a matching. If the cover gain is d, its at least 2^d minimal cover clauses require D+1 at least 2^d, where D counts deleted gates. This is a Boolean positivity and vertex-cover statement, with no claimed motivic extension. The two additional optimal five-deletion exclusions for P4 disjoint K2 and K3 disjoint K2 use the same cover substitutions and fully checked finite certificates.

## The quasi-F-split operator and unary heights

Kawakami–Takamatsu–Yoshikawa's Fedder criterion, implemented as a fixed matrix by Batubara–Garzella–Pan, identifies height with the first nonzero scalar output of an operator on homogeneous polynomials of degree d(p−1). Yobuko's theorem compares quasi-F-split and Artin–Mazur heights. The finite vector-space dimension gives a Cayley–Hamilton cutoff. Coefficient recomputation and repeated matrix powering prove polynomial-space evaluation; a separate dense implementation has time 2^{O(d log p)} times a polynomial in circuit size, d and log p. These are distinct algorithms, and the operator itself is prior work.

When p is fixed or unary, the divided monomial expansion has coefficients counting free cyclic orbits of p positive parse witnesses with not-all-equal exponent vectors. Unary-length matrix paths are then polynomial-size counting witnesses. A Fermat product selects the first nonzero scalar, giving Mod_pP completeness for fixed p and unary requested height, and exact-counting oracle equivalence when the prime is unary too. This is a counting presentation of the existing Cartier-type operator; no claim is made that its matrix is a canonical motivic basis or that a binary-height path has polynomial witness length. The archive connection is the explicit COUNTING_VARIETIES family and its formal-group-height question.

## The optimal six-deletion path profile

When the four missing edges form a three-edge path and a disjoint edge, the cover gain is three and the quadratic frame is optimal, six deleted gates also cannot violate the star bound. The additional ingredient is a common-neighbor restriction on singleton supports of cut signals that meet a fixed high product. Its independent derivation uses replacement of a high parse by a positive quadratic parse and full-graph safety. In the restricted Boolean algebra, the union of those singleton supports must lie in one old graph neighborhood; girth at least five excludes both adjacent pairs and outer pairs of a two-edge path. Seven finite gate profiles then have RUP-certified propositional refutations. This closes the entire optimal quadratic-frame path shape, together with the five-deletion result. The statement remains about positive support presentations and graph covers; no general geometric degeneration or motivic consequence is asserted. Its archive connection is the four-missing-witness reduction of the second zero-adjoined-semiring companion.
