# DP51 — the sharp asymptotic rate of a two-column harmonic family

Date: 2026-11-21 UTC. Author: `/root`, GPT-6 Astra Pro; runtime reasoning effort is not exposed. This is a local theorem about the actual primitive display kernel and the precise algebraic harmonic conditions in the recovered source. It is not a construction of global theta sections or a shorter lift chain.

## 1. Statement

Use the arbitrary-unit singleton/complement pair of DP46 over the unramified discrete valuation ring `R=W(k)`, where `k` is perfect and contains `F_(p^h)`, `p` is odd, and `h≥3`. Write `d=h−1`, `s=1/(p−1)`, and let `β(x,y_1,…,y_d)` be its primitive Poincaré logarithm in normalized additive coordinates. Fix `1≤j≤d` and a unit `λ∈R^×`. Put

\[
N=p^{h-j},\qquad S=\frac{N-1}{p-1},\qquad
w=\frac{sN}{N+1},\qquad c=s-w=\frac{s}{N+1},
\]
\[
F(x,y)=\exp z(x,y),\qquad z(x,y)=\lambda\beta(x,e_jy).
\tag{HA1}
\]

Here `e_jy` means that the only nonzero additive dual input is its `j`th coordinate. For any integers `a,b≥0`, define the integral polynomial in two columns

\[
P_{a,b}=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(a,b)}(-1)^i
 {a\choose i}{b\choose i}
 X_1^{a-i}Y_1^{b-i}X_2^iY_2^i.
\tag{HA2}
\]

It has fixed row degrees `(a,b)` and is killed by

\[
\Delta=\partial_{X_1}\partial_{Y_1}
       +\partial_{X_2}\partial_{Y_2}.
\]

Its value on two copies of the actual primitive kernel is

\[
H_{a,b}=P_{a,b}(\partial)
       \bigl(F(x_1,y_1)F(x_2,y_2)\bigr)\big|_0.
\tag{HA3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{v_p(H_{a,b})\ge c(a+b)
 -\frac{s_p(a)+s_p(b)}{p-1},}
\tag{HA4}
\]

and this slope is asymptotically exact:

\[
\boxed{\liminf_{n\to\infty}\;
 \min_{a+b=n}\frac{v_p(H_{a,b})}{n}=c.}
\tag{HA5}
\]

Zero values have valuation `+∞`. There is an infinite sequence of nonzero selected values attaining the limit. Along every sequence for which `a+b→∞` and `v_p(H_(a,b))/(a+b)→c`, one necessarily has

\[
\frac{a}{a+b}\longrightarrow\frac1{N+1},\qquad
\frac{b}{a+b}\longrightarrow\frac{N}{N+1}.
\tag{HA6}
\]

For `j=1`, regard the positive type as having one row and the negative type as having `d` rows, and use only its first row. Then all of the exact source's `HaO` algebraic conditions hold: summed harmonicity, row invariance, fixed row degrees, and integral coefficients. Thus their sharp universal asymptotic slope on this family is `1/((p−1)(p^(h−1)+1))`. This refines DP39's one-jet counterexample into an asymptotic statement. For arbitrary `j`, the harmonic theorem is unchanged; a full `HaO` interpretation requires the chosen direction to be a permitted first row. Reordering the rows of a prescribed geometric datum is not assumed.

The values are unchanged by multiplying the two-column kernel by `exp(Q)` whenever `Q` lies in the formal ideal generated by the column-sum quadratic contractions that annihilate `P_(a,b)`. In particular the corresponding balanced quadratic canonical-gauge ambiguity does not change these selected values.

## 2. The full two-variable initial form

We first spell out the input from DP42/DP46 in two independent variables. For every rational

\[
t\in I=\left[\frac{S}{N+1},w\right],
\]

the scalar and dual contraction profile used in those proofs gives

\[
\nu_{(t,t)}(z)=(N+1)t-S,
\tag{HA7}
\]

and its entire least-weight form is one nonzero scalar multiple of `x y^N`. The coefficient of that monomial has valuation `−S`; every other supported monomial has strictly greater weight in (HA7).

This is not an inference from the first nonintegral coefficient. In the proofs of DP42 and DP46, replace the contracted scalar input `uT` by the independent variable `X`, and the surviving dual input `v_jT` by the independent variable `Y`. The reduced scalar inverse remains `X`. The reduced dual inverse has successive coordinates zero before `j`, then unit multiples of `Y,Y^p,…,Y^(p^(d-j))`. The complete primitive logarithm has the unique least group-coordinate term `S_group Y_d^p/p`. Its composition is therefore a unit times `X Y^(p^(h-j))=X Y^N`. Every other term remains strictly above it because the same integral substitutions preserve the reduction calculation. The common contraction powers satisfy `μ=γ^(N+1)/p^S`, exactly as in DP42. This proves the coefficient valuation and (HA7) for the full two-variable series, with arbitrary unit arrows and the unit `λ` included. Invariant integral frame corrections are strictly higher, by DP46, so the assertion holds in every such frame.

Choose any rational `t_0` in the interior of `I` below `w`. Since the series is bounded at `(t_0,t_0)`, for every supported exponent `(a,b)` one has

\[
v_p([x^ay^b]z)+w(a+b)
\ge (N+1)t_0-S+(w-t_0)(a+b).
\tag{HA8}
\]

Thus these weights tend to `+∞` with total degree. At `(w,w)` the unique least monomial is `xy^N`, of weight `s`.

There is consequently a small positive rational `ε` for which both weight vectors

\[
(w-N\varepsilon,w+\varepsilon),\qquad
(w+N\varepsilon,w-\varepsilon)
\tag{HA9}
\]

have positive entries and give `z` coefficient infimum `s`, with the same unique least monomial. Here is a precise finiteness justification. First require `Nε<(w−t_0)/2`; then every weight in (HA9) is at least `t_0+(w−t_0)/2`. By (HA8), all sufficiently large degrees have weight greater than `s+1` for every such perturbation. Only finitely many other monomials remain. Their strict inequalities at `ε=0` persist after shrinking `ε`. The monomial `xy^N` keeps weight `s` exactly, since the change `−Nε+Nε` is zero. This proves (HA9) without assuming a uniform strict gap for infinitely many unexamined terms.

For a series with zero constant coefficient and coefficient infimum at least `s`, every exponential coefficient is integral after the contraction: `v_p(m!)≤ms` bounds every term `z^m/m!`. The same holds for the inverse exponential. Thus `F` is integral with constant one at both weight vectors (HA9), and also at `(w,w)`.

## 3. The harmonic generating series and its diagonal factor

Write `f_(a,b)=[x^ay^b]F` and define

\[
D(T)=\sum_{i\ge0}f_{i,i}T^i,\qquad
C(x,y)=F(x,y)D(-xy)=\sum_{a,b\ge0}C_{a,b}x^ay^b.
\tag{HA10}
\]

These are well-defined formal series, and `D(0)=1`. Integrality of `F` at the first vector in (HA9) gives

\[
v_p(f_{i,i})+i\bigl(2w-(N-1)\varepsilon\bigr)\ge0.
\tag{HA11}
\]

It follows that `D(−t²)` is integral at the strictly smaller weight

\[
w_D=w-\frac{N-1}{2}\varepsilon<w.
\tag{HA12}
\]

Moreover `D(−xy)` is integral at both vectors in (HA9): their coordinate sums are respectively `2w−(N−1)ε` and `2w+(N−1)ε`, and (HA11) suffices for both. Hence `C` is integral at both vectors as well.

The recurrence verifying harmonicity of (HA2) is exact. The first contraction applied to its `i`th summand has coefficient

`(−1)^i binom(a,i)binom(b,i)(a−i)(b−i)`,

while the second contraction applied to the `(i+1)`st summand has coefficient

`(−1)^(i+1)binom(a,i+1)binom(b,i+1)(i+1)^2`.

These cancel. The boundary terms vanish because one of the relevant exponents is zero. Thus `ΔP_(a,b)=0`.

Factorial coefficient extraction gives, for every `i`,

\[
{a\choose i}{b\choose i}
(a-i)!(b-i)!\,i!^2=a!b!.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{H_{a,b}=a!b!\sum_i(-1)^i
f_{a-i,b-i}f_{i,i}=a!b!C_{a,b}.}
\tag{HA13}
\]

This identity keeps every correction term from harmonic projection. It does not discard them individually.

## 4. Exact radius, factorial slope, and the degree ray

On the diagonal,

\[
C(t,t)=F(t,t)D(-t^2).
\tag{HA14}
\]

For every rational `t` sufficiently close to `w` from below, (HA7) says that the logarithm of `F(t,t)` has weighted coefficient infimum in `[0,s)`. Its initial form is a unit multiple of a monomial of degree `N+1`, which is prime to `p`; its derivative is nonzero in the residue field. The nonzero factor `D(−t²)` is bounded at every weight at least `w_D`. DP41's bounded-multiplier lemma therefore gives

\[
\nu_t(C(T,T))=-\infty\quad(t<w\text{ sufficiently close to }w).
\]

Monotonicity gives the same assertion for every nonnegative weight below `w`. At and above `w` both factors in (HA14) are integral with constant one. Thus `C(T,T)` has exact coefficient threshold `w`.

If the two-variable coefficients `C_(a,b)` were bounded at some weight `(t,t)` below `w`, their finite sums on each total degree would make `C(T,T)` bounded there. They are therefore unbounded at every such weight. Conversely they are integral at `(w,w)`. Equivalently,

\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\min_{a+b=n}
\frac{v_p(C_{a,b})}{n}=-w.
\tag{HA15}
\]

Indeed a strictly larger lower limit would give bounded coefficients at a sufficiently close smaller rational weight, a contradiction; the lower bound follows from integrality at `(w,w)`.

Legendre's formula applied to (HA13) gives

\[
v_p(a!b!)=s(a+b)-s\bigl(s_p(a)+s_p(b)\bigr).
\]

The digit-sum term is uniformly `O(log(n+1))` for `a+b=n`. This proves (HA4) and (HA5) from (HA15), including the existence of infinitely many nonzero values.

Finally, integrality of `C` at both vectors (HA9) gives the stronger coefficient inequality

\[
v_p(C_{a,b})\ge-w(a+b)+\varepsilon|Na-b|.
\tag{HA16}
\]

Combining (HA16) with (HA13) shows that any sequence attaining the slope `c` must satisfy `|Na−b|/(a+b)→0`. This is exactly (HA6). In particular the asymptotically sharp part of the family has a specific row-degree ray; the result does not identify a different prescribed global degree sequence with that ray.

## 6. Corollary: the sharp universal bound for the entire polynomial module

Keep the same pair and a unit primitive parameter. Put

\[
N_*=p^{h-1},\qquad w_* =\frac{sN_*}{N_*+1},\qquad
c_* =\frac{s}{N_*+1}.
\tag{HA17}
\]

Take `ℓ≥1` independent copies, with `h` additive variables `Z_b=(x_b,y_(1,b),…,y_(d,b))` in copy `b`. Let each `U_b` be an arbitrary integral formal function on the corresponding actual group, and let `λ_b∈R^×`. Define

\[
\Theta(\mathbf Z)=\prod_{b=1}^{\ell}
U_b(E_G(x_b),E_H(\mathbf y_b))
\exp\bigl(\lambda_b\beta(x_b,\mathbf y_b)\bigr).
\tag{HA18}
\]

For any integral homogeneous differential polynomial
`P=Σ_(|α|=n) p_α Z^α` of degree `n`,

\[
\boxed{
v_p\bigl(P(\partial)\Theta(0)\bigr)
\ge c_*n-s\max_{\alpha\in\operatorname{supp}P}
            \sum_{i=1}^{\ell h}s_p(\alpha_i).}
\tag{HA19}
\]

The zero polynomial is harmless and may be excluded from the maximum. For `n≥1`, in particular,

\[
v_p\bigl(P(\partial)\Theta(0)\bigr)
\ge c_*n-\ell h\bigl(1+\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor\bigr).
\tag{HA20}
\]

For fixed `h` and `ℓ≥1`, the coefficient `c_*` is optimal as a uniform asymptotic slope over this module and all integral prefactors. When `ℓ≥2`, it is already optimal on the source-shaped `HaO` submodule with one positive row and `d` negative rows: take two equal primitive kernels, the other factors equal to one, and the polynomials of §§1–5 with `j=1`. Extra copies can be ignored. This assertion of a sharp asymptotic slope keeps `ℓ` fixed; if the number of columns grows with `n`, the exact digit-sum error (HA19) must be retained.

**Proof.** Use the profile in DP42/DP46 with `j=1` and `w=w_*`. Its scalar weight and first dual weight are `w_*`. For every later dual coordinate the weight is

\[
\omega_i=s-p^{i-1}(s-w_*)<w_*\qquad(i>1).
\]

All these weights are positive and exceed the bare dual contraction weights, as proved there. The scaled inverse logarithms are integral, and the full group-coordinate primitive logarithm has coefficient infimum `s` at this profile. Now give **every** independent additive variable weight `w_*`. The input to the scalar and first dual contracted variables has weight zero after dividing by their contraction factors; the input to every later contracted dual variable has the nonnegative extra weight `w_*−ω_i`. Substitution into the integral inverse maps therefore remains integral. Substitution into the scaled primitive logarithm cannot decrease its infimum below `s`. Its exponential, with any unit `λ_b`, is consequently integral on this full polydisc. The same is true of each integral prefactor `U_b` after substitution. Multiplication proves

\[
v_p([\mathbf Z^\alpha]\Theta)+w_*|\alpha|\ge0
\quad\hbox{for every }\alpha.
\tag{HA21}
\]

In additive coordinates, the invariant derivative of multiorder `α` at zero is `α!` times this coefficient. Legendre's formula gives

\[
v_p(\alpha!)=s|\alpha|-s\sum_i s_p(\alpha_i).
\]

Since the coefficients `p_α` are integral and every supported `α` has total degree `n`, the nonarchimedean triangle inequality proves (HA19). If `0≤α_i≤n` and `n≥1`, then
`s_p(α_i)≤(p−1)(1+floor(log_p n))`, proving (HA20).

For the full polynomial module with even one copy, take prefactor one and `P=(X+Y_1)^n`. Its value is the `n`th ordinary derivative on the primitive line `x=t, y_1=t`, and DP42/DP46 gives the exact slope `c_*`. Extra copies with prefactor one do not change the value. For sharpness on the `HaO` submodule when `ℓ≥2`, choose `U_1=U_2=1`, `λ_1=λ_2=λ`, and let the polynomial involve only the scalar and first dual variable of these two copies. On setting the other variables to zero its value is exactly `H_(a,b)` of (HA3) with `j=1`. The remaining factors can all have prefactor one; their value at zero is one. By (HA5), these integral polynomials attain slope `c_*`. The first-row argument in §5 gives their full algebraic `HaO` membership. Thus a larger uniform slope with sublinear error is impossible both on the full integral polynomial module and on that `HaO` submodule. The particular prefactors or a prescribed geometric sequence of row degrees can have additional divisibility; universal sharpness makes no claim that every such sequence attains the minimum. This proves the corollary. ∎

As in §5, a balanced quadratic change annihilated by the chosen harmonic polynomial leaves its selected value unchanged. The full-series inequality itself was proved in the indicated invariant product normalization and does not assert integrality for an arbitrary rational quadratic gauge before harmonic selection.
