## Outcome

I read all of `positivity_universal_denominator.tex` and independently checked every argument in the proposition and the subsequent examples. **PASS.** This is an independent-agent targeted whole-proof review. It establishes an exponential cyclotomic factor in a universal denominator and an actual pole in some fixed-target covariant series. It does not establish the same pole in the scalar invariant Hilbert series or in a genuine simultaneous-stretch ray with nontrivial target.

## Checks actually performed

1. Every generator of the ideal `I^[M]` vanishes at the chain point by P-F19. Thus every element of that ideal vanishes there, while no positive power of the discriminant can belong to it. The quotient is infinite-dimensional because those powers are homogeneous of distinct positive degrees. It is a finitely generated positively graded algebra and has positive dimension.
2. The quotient has no components outside degrees divisible by `M`, so its Hilbert series is exactly a rational function of `t^M`. The positive-dimensional Hilbert series has a pole at one. In this particular argument one can also prove the required pole without using the precise dimension theorem: the surviving discriminant powers give `Hilb_Q(t)>=1/(1-t^(gM))` for real `0<t<1`, and a rational function unbounded at one has a pole there. Nonzero derivative of `t^M` at every primitive `M`th root transfers the pole.
3. In the exact sequence with the ideal, the quotient's pole cannot be absent from both the ideal and the ring series. Since both are among the graded submodules used in the universal denominator definition, the cyclotomic factor follows. This reasoning does not conflate the ring denominator with the universal denominator.
4. In the original coefficient grading, nonvanishing degrees are divisible by `d0*M` and the discriminant has degree `g*d0*M`. Repeating the same argument gives the second universal factor with the stated, larger order.
5. Derksen's Theorem 3.6 applies exactly: the ambient algebra is a polynomial ring, the complex special linear group is linearly reductive, and its action is homogeneous and rational. The theorem uses ordinary reduced covariant denominators, over all irreducible target modules. A cyclotomic divisor of their least common multiple occurs in at least one of those denominators. No effectiveness or target-size estimate is claimed.
6. If an eventual coefficient quasi-polynomial has period `L`, splitting its tail into residue classes expresses the series with poles only at `L`th roots. A primitive `d0*M`th pole therefore forces `d0*M` to divide `L`. Conversely the required eventual quasi-polynomiality exists because the covariant module is finite over a positively graded finitely generated invariant algebra and has a Hilbert denominator that is a product of factors `1-t^a`.
7. For the alternating-group example, `1+t^45` cancels the only factor at a primitive sixth root in the denominator product through ten. The quotient generated by `e6` is explicitly proved in the primary source. Thus the example genuinely separates ordinary and universal factors in a normal characteristic-zero invariant domain.
8. For `B_M`, I independently checked the cyclic-quotient presentation: the invariants of `(u,v)->(zeta*u,zeta^(-1)*v)` are generated by `u^M,uv,v^M`; their only relation is the indicated product relation, as verified using monomials. A sign change gives `xz+y^M`. The assigned positive weights make this a homogeneous nonzero relation of degree `M`; they need not be the original total grading on `u,v` for the underlying normal/rational variety assertion. Adding the `w` variable preserves normality and rational singularities. The hypersurface is Cohen--Macaulay.
9. The Hilbert numerator of `B_M` cancels its weight-`M` free variable factor, leaving only poles of order dividing `M-1`. There is a primitive `(M-1)`th pole if `M>2`, and the special case `M=2` has period one. Its quotient by `(x,y,z)` is a polynomial ring with generator of degree `M`. Thus even a prime-power universal factor need not be an ordinary factor in this class. The explicit warning about the still-large different pole order is correct.
10. The multivariate example is globally quasi-polynomial and nonnegative, has the displayed rational series, and takes value one at the origin. On a simultaneous ray, even stretch makes the indicator vanish; odd stretch is invertible modulo a power of two. Therefore every ray has period at most two, while the fixed-target slice has a primitive `M`th pole. No domain realization is claimed. This invalidates the proposed purely Fourier/chamber transfer but does not purport to settle transfer for the actual branching algebra.
