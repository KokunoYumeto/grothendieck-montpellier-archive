## Attempts that remain incomplete

One tempting route would represent every relative weight-two perfect class on S×P by one-cycles whose closures avoid D×P. If D is ample, the complement S minus D is affine; the projection of a proper curve avoiding D×P to that complement is constant. Such representatives would therefore have constant endpoint zero-cycle classes. This observation alone does not prove relative-family rigidity: the needed relative weight-two cycle-surjectivity theorem in dimension three has not been proved or found. In particular, it cannot be used either to kill or to preserve T(S).

Nor does an abstract double S glued to itself along an ample D automatically give an admissible negative exceptional divisor in a regular threefold. If D has positive self-intersection on both components, the restrictions of both component normal divisors to D are positive, incompatible with a positive exceptional combination having ample conormal on D. Thus this naive configuration does not provide a geometric counterexample to UK.7. The actual exceptional geometry and exceptional-cycle denominator must both be retained.

The regular-target specialization theorem RS is separately sound in its stated supplied-model scope. A possible next transfer step would take a finite closure of the etale stage defining a symbolic-unit lift and push to the constant target. This has not been proved: one must account for every closed-fibre branch, extension across the complement of the chosen branch, the localization boundary, and the exact endpoint equality after trace. The Artin–Schreier model y^p−y=x/u illustrates the nonconstant etale-stage issue; its finite closure after rescaling changes the special fibre. Finite closure alone supplies none of the missing equalities.
