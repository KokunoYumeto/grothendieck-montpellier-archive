\paragraph{C.3bf (Proved): an integral Picard kernel.}
Let $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5$. For each $N\ge4$ we specify an
excellent normal strictly henselian local $k$-algebra $R$ of dimension
$N$, with residue field $k$, regular puncture $X$, and a nonzero
$f\in\mathfrak m_R$ with regular punctured divisor $Y$, for which
\[
 (\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^3
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
 \ker\bigl(\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\rm alg}
                  \longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\rm alg}\bigr).
\]
The equivalences in this assertion are actual fixed-product curve
equivalences. The exhibited kernel is integral and is killed by five;
no rational failure is asserted.

\emph{1. The specified ring and divisor.}
Put $m=N-2\ge2$ and, over $k$, set
\[
 F=z_0^4+z_1^4+z_2^4,\qquad
 H=z_0^2z_1^2+z_0^2z_2^2+z_1^2z_2^2,\qquad
 \ell=x_0z_0+x_1z_1+x_2z_2.
\]
Let $C=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^2_k$, let
$T=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m\times\mathbb P^2}(-1,-1))$,
and write $t$ for its tautological fibre vector. Define
$Z\subset T$ by $F+t\ell H=0$. Contract the zero section by the
Segre coordinates $w_{ij}=tx_i z_j$, and let $W$ be the reduced affine
image. Equivalently, $W$ is the radical zero scheme of the two-by-two
minors of $(w_{ij})$ and the equations, for all ordered quadruples
$(i_1,i_2,i_3,i_4)$,
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^2\prod_{a=1}^4w_{i_a j}
 +(w_{00}+w_{11}+w_{22})
       \sum_{0\le b<c\le2}w_{i_1b}w_{i_2b}w_{i_3c}w_{i_4c}=0.
\]
Normalize $W$, localize at its unique vertex, and take the strict
henselization: this is $R$. Set $f=w_{00}+w_{11}+w_{22}$.

Here every geometric verification of C.3m is valid in characteristic
five: $4$ is invertible, $C$ is smooth, $dF$ proves smoothness of $Z$
along its zero section, and the graph description $t=-F/(\ell H)$
proves integrality. The proper Segre contraction is an isomorphism
away from the zero section. Shrinking around the vertex proves that
the normalization is regular away from that vertex; it does not
change the specified local ring. Its unique closed preimage follows
from the connected zero-section fibre. Denote the resulting regular
projective resolution over $R$ by $\mathcal Z$. Its closed fibre is
\[
 V=\mathbb P^m\times C,\qquad
 L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m}(1)\boxtimes K_C,
 \qquad I_V=\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}(-V),\quad I_V/I_V^2=L.
\]
In fact $\mathfrak m_R\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}=I_V$: the Segre
coordinates generate $I_V$ locally, while every function vanishing
at the normalized vertex vanishes on $V$. Thus $X=\mathcal Z-V$.

The strict transform of $f=0$ is the split line bundle
$\operatorname{Tot}(L_H^{-1})$ over
$H_0=V(\ell)\subset V$, near its zero section. The basepoint-free
map $\mathcal O_C^{m+1}\to K_C$ with entries $z_0,z_1,z_2,0,\ldots,0$
identifies $H_0$ with a projective bundle of relative dimension $m-1$
over $C$. It is smooth. After the same base change its complement of
the zero section is exactly the regular punctured divisor $Y$.

\emph{2. The complete formal Picard calculation.}
Write $V_n=V(I_V^n)$. Coherent Künneth and the projective-space
cohomology give
\[
 H^1(V,L)=k^{m+1},\quad H^2(V,L)=0,\qquad
 H^1(V,L^n)=H^2(V,L^n)=0\quad(n\ge2).
\]
The first equality uses $H^1(C,K_C)=k$; for $n\ge2$ we use
$H^1(C,K_C^n)=0$. The exact square-zero unit sequence therefore
gives, on Picard functors,
\[
 0\longrightarrow U_0:=\mathbb G_a^{m+1}
 \longrightarrow G:=\operatorname{Pic}^0_{V_2/k}
 \longrightarrow J:=\operatorname{Pic}^0_{C/k}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Constants lift, so the unit term introduces no quotient of
$H^1(V,L)$. Every later Picard restriction is an isomorphism.
These assertions hold after every affine $k$-base change: the
cohomology groups tensor with its coordinate ring. The compatible
unit systems have surjective transition maps from the second stage
onward, because the same $H^1(V,L^n)$ vanish. Hence no inverse-limit
unit obstruction occurs.

We use this calculation on actual line bundles over affine parameter
charts and on individual formal fibres. We do not infer a global
actual-product family merely from a pointwise Picard comparison.
Step~5 constructs the needed proper-curve family directly.

For the target, the neighborhood is split. From
$0\to\mathcal O_V\xrightarrow{\ell}L\to L|_{H_0}\to0$ and
$H^2(V,\mathcal O_V)=0$ we obtain
\[
 H^1(H_0,L|_{H_0})=H^1(V,L)/U,
 \qquad U:=\ell H^1(C,\mathcal O_C).
\]
The three canonical sections make multiplication by $\ell$ injective
on $H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$, by Serre duality; hence $\dim U=3$.
The analogous sequence with $L^n$ gives
$H^1(H_0,L^n|_{H_0})=0$ for $n\ge2$; also
$H^2(H_0,L^n|_{H_0})=0$ for $n\ge1$, since the corresponding
$H^2(V,L^n)$ and $H^3(V,L^{n-1})$ vanish.
The split projection identifies the compatible formal target
Picard identity component with
\[
 J\times(U_0/U).
\]
Restriction induces the identity on $J$, and on vector kernels it
is the quotient $U_0\to U_0/U$. In particular it annihilates $U$
as a group scheme. The full formal unit calculation, rather than
only its tangent map, justifies this last assertion.

\emph{3. The extension class has rank three.}
The class of $V_2$ is the boundary of $\ell H$ in the twisted normal
sequence. On its curve factor it is
\[
 \kappa=\sum_{i=0}^2 x_i\otimes\delta(z_iH),\qquad
 \delta:H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(5))\to H^1(C,\mathcal O_C).
\]
The Euler and normal sequences identify the latter quotient with
the degree-five piece of $k[z_0,z_1,z_2]/(z_0^3,z_1^3,z_2^3)$.
The three images are respectively
$z_0z_1^2z_2^2,z_0^2z_1z_2^2,z_0^2z_1^2z_2$; they are a basis.
The identical calculation from C.3m uses only division by $4$,
which is valid here.

The extension of $J$ by $U_0$ has class
\[
 c_\kappa:H^0(C,K_C)\longrightarrow H^1(V,L),
 \qquad \omega\longmapsto\kappa\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega.
\]
This is the actual Picard-extension class: the obstruction to
lifting a normalized Poincaré bundle is contraction of its mixed
Atiyah class with $\kappa$, exactly the transition-unit calculation
and \citet[Theorem~2.6 and formula~(2.7.2), pp.~166--167]{D_Illusie1972Torsors}, as used in C.3m.
The three coefficients are the perfect Serre pairings with the
displayed basis, so $c_\kappa$ has rank three and image exactly $U$.
Thus the three nonzero vector-extension classes are linearly
independent; the other $m-2$ vector directions are split.

\emph{4. An embedded proper abelian subgroup with an étale kernel.}
The curve $C$ is ordinary. In the standard Čech basis of
$H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$ indexed by the positive triples
$(2,1,1),(1,2,1),(1,1,2)$, the Hasse--Witt matrix has entry at
$(a,b)$ equal to the coefficient of $z^{5a-b}$ in $F^4$.
This coefficient formula follows by applying Frobenius to the
Čech representatives in $H^2(\mathbb P^2,\mathcal O(-4))$ and
multiplying by $F^{5-1}$ in the hypersurface exact sequence.
The off-diagonal coefficients vanish and each diagonal coefficient
is $4!/(2!1!1!)=12=2$ in $k$. Frobenius is therefore bijective. The Abel map identifies
$H^1(J,\mathcal O_J)$ with $H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$ compatibly with
Frobenius \citep[Lemma9.5]{D_MilneJacobians2021}. Coherent cohomology
of an abelian variety is the exterior algebra on $H^1$
\citep[Theorem1.1, p.~1]{D_BrionCoherent2012}, so Frobenius is
bijective in every degree. By
\citet[Lemma2.3.1 and Proposition2.3.2, p.~12]{D_HaconPatakfalvi2014v2},
$J$ is ordinary and Verschiebung
\[
 v:J^{(5)}\longrightarrow J
\]
has étale kernel $K\simeq(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^3$.
Its pullback on $H^1(\mathcal O)$ is zero: the composite with the
bijective Frobenius pullback is $[5]^*=0$.

Using the bifunctorial identification
$\operatorname{Ext}^1(J,\mathbb G_a)=H^1(J,\mathcal O_J)$
\citep[equation(3.8), Section3.2.3, p.~23, before the
characteristic-zero restriction]{D_BrionIsogenyII},
the extension $v^*G$ splits. A splitting defines
$b:J^{(5)}\to G$ over $v$. There are no nonzero homomorphisms from
an abelian variety to $\mathbb G_a$. The Hom--Ext sequence of
$0\to K\to J^{(5)}\to J\to0$ therefore identifies
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(K,\mathbb G_a)
       \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\operatorname{Ext}^1(J,\mathbb G_a),
\]
because $v^*$ on the latter Ext group is zero. The three independent
classes in Step~3 imply that $b|_K:K\to U$ has rank three and is an
injection. As $\ker b\subseteq K$, the whole morphism $b$ is a closed
immersion. Its image $B$ is therefore a proper abelian subgroup
isomorphic to $J^{(5)}$, and
\[
 B\cap U=b(K),\qquad b(K)\simeq(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^3.
\]
Under target restriction, $B\to J\times(U_0/U)$ is $(v,0)$:
the first component is $v$ and the second is zero because a proper
connected group has no nonconstant morphism to an affine group.
Its group-scheme kernel is exactly $b(K)$.

\emph{5. Actual proper-curve families and nontriviality on the puncture.}
Fix $\alpha\in b(K)(k)$. Choose a smooth connected proper curve
$P\subset B$ through $0$ and $\alpha$, by successive sufficiently
ample smooth sections through these two points. Pull the normalized Poincaré bundle on $C\times J$ back
along $V\to C$ and $v|_P:P\to J$, obtaining an actual line bundle
$\mathcal Q_1$ on $V\times P$. Its obstruction to extension to
$V_2\times P$ is the contraction of its mixed Atiyah class with
$\kappa$. The only possible component is in
$H^1(V,L)\otimes H^1(P,\mathcal O_P)$: both $H^2(V,L)$ and
$H^2(P,\mathcal O_P)$ vanish. That mixed component is
$c_\kappa$ applied to the Poincaré tensor pulled back by
\[
 v^*:H^1(J,\mathcal O_J)\longrightarrow H^1(J^{(5)},\mathcal O_{J^{(5)}}),
\]
which is zero by Step~4. The explicit transition-unit obstruction
formula of C.3m, equivalently \citet[Theorem~2.6 and formula~(2.7.2)]{D_Illusie1972Torsors}, therefore
produces a line bundle $\mathcal Q_2$ on the actual product
$V_2\times P$.

The morphism $P\to G$ it represents and $b|_P$ have the same
projection to $J$, so their difference is a morphism $P\to U_0$.
Since $P$ is proper and connected and $U_0$ is affine, it is constant.
Tensor $\mathcal Q_2$ by the inverse of its actual fibre at $0$,
pulled back from $V_2$. Its represented map is now exactly $b|_P$,
with endpoint classes $0$ and $\alpha$. This argument avoids any
assumption of a universal line bundle over all of $B$ and needs
no separate Picard-gerbe neutralization theorem.

For every $n\ge2$, coherent Künneth gives
\[
 H^2(V\times P,\operatorname{pr}_V^*L^n)
 =H^2(V,L^n)\otimes H^0(P,\mathcal O_P)
  \oplus H^1(V,L^n)\otimes H^1(P,\mathcal O_P)=0.
\]
The square-zero unit obstruction therefore vanishes at every later
stage. Choose compatible extensions $\mathcal Q_n$ to $V_n\times P$.
Their uniqueness as families is neither claimed nor needed:
$H^1(V\times P,L^n)$ may be nonzero. Their individual endpoint
Picard classes are unique, since $H^1(V,L^n)=0$ for $n\ge2$.

Because $\mathfrak m_R\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}=I_V$, this is the
closed-fibre formal system of the proper scheme
$\mathcal Z_{\widehat R}\times P$ over $\widehat R$. Proper formal
existence algebraizes the system to a line bundle there. The
regular completion map $R\to\widehat R$ is a filtered colimit of
smooth $R$-algebras. Descend the line bundle, its inverse, and the
specified identification of its first-jet restriction with
$\mathcal Q_2$ to one smooth $R$-algebra. The map from this algebra
to $R/\mathfrak m_R^2$ lifts to $R$ by smooth lifting over the
henselian pair $(R,\mathfrak m_R^2)$. We obtain an actual line bundle
$\mathcal Q$ on the fixed product $\mathcal Z\times P$, with exactly
the prescribed restriction on $V_2\times P$. These are the same
proper formal-existence, Popescu, finite-presentation descent and
smooth henselian-lifting inputs checked in C.3az and C.3w; matching
modulo $\mathfrak m_R^2$, not merely modulo $\mathfrak m_R$, is essential.

Let $\mathcal Q_0,\mathcal Q_\alpha$ be its two endpoint bundles.
The first has trivial class on every $V_n$, by the individual
uniqueness just proved. Proper formal existence makes its completed
bundle trivial. Elkik's Picard comparison for the henselian pair
$(R,\mathfrak m_R)$, precisely the unnumbered corollary on printed p.~573 \citep{D_Elkik1973} used
in C.3p, is injective on the puncture. Hence
$\mathcal Q_0|_X$ is trivial. The actual family
$\mathcal Q|_{X\times P}$ shows that
$\mathcal Q_\alpha|_X$ is ordinarily algebraically trivial.

It is nontrivial when $\alpha\ne0$. If it were trivial on $X$,
Cartier closure on the regular $\mathcal Z$ would give
$\mathcal Q_\alpha\simeq\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}(aV)$ for an integer
$a$, since $V$ is the only exceptional prime. Restriction to $V$
would give $L^{-a}\simeq\mathcal O_V$, forcing $a=0$ by ampleness.
Restriction to $V_2$ would then contradict $\alpha\ne0$.
The same argument applied to tensor differences proves injectivity
on all the distinct elements of $b(K)(k)$; uniqueness of the
individual formal Picard lift makes this assignment a homomorphism.

Finally, on the split strict transform of $f=0$, the class of
$\mathcal Q_\alpha$ on $H_2$ is zero by Step~4. The vanishing
$H^1(H_0,L_H^n)=0$ for $n\ge2$ makes its class zero on every higher
$H_n$. Proper formal existence followed by Elkik's comparison for
$(R/(f),\mathfrak m_R/(f))$ proves that its actual restriction to
$Y$ is trivial. This yields the asserted integral ordinary kernel.
No universal line bundle over the entire abelian variety was lifted;
the proper curve is what removes the extra $H^2$ parameter obstruction.

The conclusion leaves all rational degree-one results intact and
does not settle a higher-codimension restriction range. In particular,
no claim is made that multiplying one of these classes by a divisor
gives a nonzero higher Chow class.
