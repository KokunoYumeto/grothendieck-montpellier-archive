\paragraph{C.3bl (Proved): all higher integral restriction kernels in positive characteristic.}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$.
For every $r\ge2$ and $N\ge2r$ there are an excellent normal
strictly henselian local $k$-domain $R$ of dimension $N$, with residue
field $k$, and $0\ne f\in\mathfrak m_R$ such that
\[
 X=\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak m_R\},\qquad
 Y=X\cap V(f)
\]
are regular and
\[
 \ker\bigl(\mathrm{CH}^r(X)_{\rm alg}
             \longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^r(Y)_{\rm alg}\bigr)
 \text{ contains a class of exact order }p.
 \tag{C.3bl.1}
\]
The class is realized by an actual family on the fixed product of
$X$ with a smooth proper curve. All data may be chosen over
$\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ before extension of the algebraically
closed field. This integral statement does not imply a nonzero
rational kernel. C.3bk is retained with its independent, simpler
whole-boundary injectivity proof in the ranges stated there.

\begin{proof}
\textbf{1. The geometry in every dimension, including $m=0$.}
Put $s=r-1$ and $m=N-2r\ge0$. Choose the ordinary quartic $C=(F=0)$,
quartic $H$, smooth cubics $W_i$, hyperplane bundles $A_i$ and lines
$D_i$ exactly as specified in C.3bk. Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 T_0=\mathbb P^m\times\prod_{i=1}^sW_i,\qquad
 L_0=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^m}(1)\boxtimes\Bigl(\boxtimes_{i=1}^s A_i\Bigr),\\
 V=T_0\times C,
 \qquad L=L_0\boxtimes K_C,
 \qquad \dim V=m+2s+1=N-1.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{C.3bl.2}
\]
The independent rank-two divisor cups $\mu_i$ of (C.3bk.3) are
valid in every characteristic. The coherent vanishings in
(C.3bk.4) hold for every $m\ge0$ and $s\ge1$, independently of the
safe dimension restriction used later in that proof.

We require a smooth divisor $T=(\ell=0)$ of $L$, where
\[
 \ell=\sum_{j=0}^2g_jz_j,
 \qquad g_j\in H^0(T_0,L_0),
\]
the three coefficients are independent, and their span has nonzero
image under $1\otimes\bigotimes_i\mu_i$.
For $m\ge1$, choose the ordered sections $a_0,\ldots,a_m$ of
$(\boxtimes_iA_i)\boxtimes K_C$ and the incidence equation
$\ell=\sum x_i a_i$ as in C.3bk Step 2.
Its proof of simultaneous rank and Bertini conditions, and its
derivative proof of smoothness, apply to every $m\ge1$.
Those arguments impose no bound such as $m\ge r-1$.

Here is the additional $m=0$ verification for arbitrary $s$.
Choose $s_i,t_i\in H^0(W_i,A_i)$ with independent images under
$\mu_i$, and choose $u_1$ independent of $s_1,t_1$ in the
four-dimensional $H^0(W_1,A_1)$. The three tensors
\[
 g_0=s_1\prod_{i>1}s_i,\qquad
 g_1=t_1\prod_{i>1}s_i,\qquad
 g_2=u_1\prod_{i>1}s_i
 \tag{C.3bl.3}
\]
are independent. Their first two product cups are nonzero and
independent, because all the other tensor factors have nonzero
images. Thus the required coefficient conditions define a nonempty
open subset of the affine space of sections of
$(\boxtimes_iA_i)\boxtimes K_C$. This line bundle is very ample:
all its factors give their closed projective embeddings, followed
by the Segre embedding. Smooth divisors form another nonempty open
by the exact immersion version of Bertini
\citep[Lemma 33.47.3, Tag 0FD6]{D_StacksBertiniUniformHigher}.
The affine parameter space is irreducible, so the two opens meet.
This is the needed smooth section when there is no projective-space
coordinate derivative. As in C.3bk, choosing the first qualifying
closed point in the finite-field enumeration specifies the data;
no universal search-time bound is asserted.

\textbf{2. The actual local model and the ordinary endpoint.}
Use the equation
\[
 F+\tau\ell H=0
 \tag{C.3bl.4}
\]
in the total space of $L_0^{-1}\boxtimes\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)$
over $T_0\times\mathbb P^2$. Contract by all its degree-one Segre
coordinates, take the reduced affine image and its normalization,
localize at the unique vertex and strictly henselize. This gives
$R$ and its projective regular source $\mathcal Z$ as in C.3bk
Step 3. That argument uses only the smooth product factors, the
nonzero $dF$ along $C$, and the nondivisibility $F\nmid\ell H$;
it applies unchanged when $m=0$ and $s$ is arbitrary. Its dimension,
closed-fibre and conormal assertions are
\[
 \dim R=N,\qquad
 \mathfrak m_R\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z}=I_V,
 \qquad I_V/I_V^2=L.
 \tag{C.3bl.5}
\]
The original projection gives an actual morphism
$q:\mathcal Z\to T_0$ with $q|_V=\operatorname{pr}_{T_0}$.
It and the resolution descend to the algebraic local model.
Thus all geometric hypotheses (PB.1)--(PB.2) of the included
auxiliary proposition are satisfied, including $m=0$.
The principal element $f=\tau\ell$ cuts out a strict transform
which is exactly the split line bundle over the smooth $T$;
hence $X$ and $Y$ are regular.

We spell out why the endpoint construction also has no omitted
low-$m$ restriction. The first Picard extension has vector part
$U_0=H^1(V,L)$ and extension-class image
$U=\langle g_0,g_1,g_2\rangle$ of rank three, by the nondegenerate
quartic form $Q_H$. The ordinary Verschiebung
$v:J^{(p)}\to J$, for $J=\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$, lifts to this
extension. Its etale kernel $K=(\mathbb Z/p)^3$ maps injectively
with span $U$, as proved in C.3bk Step 4 using the integral
Hom--Ext sequence. The product cup is nonzero on $U$ by Step 1.
Choose $\eta\in K$ whose image $a$ satisfies
\[
 P\left(a\smile\prod_{i=1}^s d\log\mathcal O_{W_i}(D_i)\right)\ne0,
 \tag{C.3bl.6}
\]
where $P$ is the direct K\"unneth projection in (PB.3).
Each divisor belongs to a distinct factor, so the fixed ordered
cup has coefficient one up to sign, in every characteristic.

The divisor sequence gives
$H^1(T,L|_T)=U_0/U$ and
$H^1(T,L^n|_T)=H^2(T,L^n|_T)=0$ for $n\ge2$.
The target first neighbourhood is split. The lifted abelian variety
has zero vector coordinate there, and its other coordinate factors
through $v$; consequently $a$ restricts to zero.
Choose a smooth proper curve in $J^{(p)}$ through $0$ and $\eta$.
The actual normalized Poincare pullback has zero first obstruction
because $v^*H^1(J,\mathcal O_J)=0$. The higher family obstruction
groups vanish by $H^j(V,L^n)=0$ for $j\ge2$, $n\ge0$,
$H^1(V,L^n)=0$ for $n\ge2$, and the dimension-one parameter.
The formal-existence, finite-data Popescu and smooth henselian
lifting argument of C.3bk Step 4 therefore produces an actual
line bundle $\mathcal A$ on $\mathcal Z$, as a fibre of the fixed
actual product family, with
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal A|_V=\mathcal O_V,
 \qquad\mathcal A|_{V_2}\text{ has relative class }a,\\
 \mathcal A|_X\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\rm alg},\qquad
 (\mathcal A|_X)^p=\mathcal O_X,
 \qquad\mathcal A|_Y=\mathcal O_Y.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{C.3bl.7}
\]
This repeats the actual endpoint argument, including first-jet
matching and higher uniqueness, not a formal-family inference.
It uses neither a projection $\mathcal Z\to C$ nor a universal
bundle on the whole abelian variety.

\textbf{3. The stronger whole-boundary conclusion.}
Every $\mathcal D_i=q^*\mathcal O_{W_i}(D_i)$ is an actual line
bundle on $\mathcal Z$. Put
\[
 \beta=c_1(\mathcal A)\prod_{i=1}^s c_1(\mathcal D_i)
                                  \in\mathrm{CH}^r(\mathcal Z).
 \tag{C.3bl.8}
\]
Conditions (C.3bl.5)--(C.3bl.6) are precisely those of the included
primitive exceptional-boundary proposition. Its conclusion gives
$\beta|_X\ne0$ without assuming that multiplication by $c_1(L)$
is injective on the full exceptional Chow group.
For clarity, its treatment of the extra torsion is integral:
the exact kernel is expressed by the Smith factors $d_j$ of
multiplication by the ample class on the free cubic/projective
Chow lattice. The designated cup factors through the free lattice
$\bigotimes_i(\operatorname{Pic}(W_i)/\mathbb Z A_i)$, so it kills
the saturation of that image even if $p\mid d_j$.
The remaining curve-line-bundle products have a unique relative
Milnor differential, independent of the chosen actual lift; its
additivity and the divisibility of $\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)(k)$
make it zero. The literal Chow projection formula (PB.18), followed
by the finite smooth-model Milnor--Bloch comparison, therefore
kills the entire exceptional image in the designated component.
These steps replace only C.3bk Step 6; its actual geometry,
ordinary families and relative cup remain as stated.

Finally the actual family for $\mathcal A$ multiplied by the fixed
$\mathcal D_i$ proves $\beta|_X$ ordinarily algebraically trivial.
Equation (C.3bl.7) makes it zero on $Y$ and annihilates it by $p$.
Its nonzero value consequently has exact order $p$, proving
(C.3bl.1).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence: the universal integral injectivity range in positive characteristic.}
Fix a prime $p$. With universality taken over all algebraically
closed coefficient fields and all regular-puncture principal cuts
in the stated local scope, the ordinary integral map
\[
 \mathrm{CH}^r(X)_{\rm alg}\longrightarrow
             \mathrm{CH}^r(Y)_{\rm alg},\qquad r\ge1,
\]
is injective for every such pair exactly when $N\le r$.
For $N\le r$ the source has no codimension-$r$ points.
For $r\ge2$ and $r+1\le N\le2r-1$, C.3aw gives the
counterexamples with its specified transcendental coefficient field;
for $N\ge2r$ use (C.3bl.1), which works already over
$\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. For $r=1$ use the complete integral
Picard classification of C.3bh. This consequence is a universality
statement over the stated class of coefficient fields, not a claim
that every lower-dimensional example of C.3aw exists over every
fixed field. It makes no assertion about the remaining rational
injectivity or higher-codimension surjectivity questions.
