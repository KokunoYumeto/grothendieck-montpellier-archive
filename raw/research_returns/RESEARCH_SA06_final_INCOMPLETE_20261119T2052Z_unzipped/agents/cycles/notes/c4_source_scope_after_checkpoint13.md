19. **Bruin–Kulkarni (2025), an isogeny construction with an integral-order caution.** We opened the full published primary, *On (2,2)-decomposable genus 4 Jacobians*, DOI10.4153/S0008414X25101557, published online12September2025. Theorems1.1/1.3 and §7, printedpp22–24, including Example7.1, were read; the exact §7 assertions were freshly reopened again after checkpoint16. Primary: https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/651E1FC3F6A593EB7B03A0642E81C7F0/S0008414X25101557a.pdf/on_22decomposable_genus_4_jacobians.pdf . The characteristic is assumed different from2. The constructions give genus-four Jacobians isogenous to squares of genus-two Jacobians on the specified loci. They do not establish that the generic three-dimensional type-(1,2) family has an odd-degree Jacobian realization.

   We do not use the literal integral endomorphism-ring sentence in §7 as an integral input. The text says that the square isogeny yields a copy of M₂(Z) in End(Jac(X)), and Example7.1 concludes End(Jac(X))=M₂(Z) from the corresponding isogeny and minimal endomorphism ring of its genus-two factor. An isogeny alone identifies the rational endomorphism algebra, not this integral order. There is also a direct consistency test in the stated minimal-order situation: a unital M₂(Z) action would give actual complementary matrix-unit idempotents, hence an unpolarized product B×B with End(B)=Z. A principal polarization on this product has the form λ_B times a positive integral symmetric binary matrix. Its degree forces λ_B to be principal and the matrix unimodular; such a positive binary form is equivalent to the identity. The principally polarized product is then decomposable, unlike the Jacobian of a smooth connected genus-four curve. Thus the literal integral assertion cannot serve as the needed splitting certificate without an additional correction or clarification. This observation does not reject the paper's geometric isogeny constructions, which are the assertions used in the source-scope check.
