# An Artin–Schreier obstruction to closing a henselian parameter branch

**2026-11-19, 16:36 UTC. Status: a method countermodel, not a local Chow counterexample or a change to any main-task status.**

The proposed repair of the varying-etale-neighbourhood problem would extend a coherent ideal from an open of a proper henselian parameter space, then use henselian Chow algebraization. The extension step is false in general, including in positive characteristic.

Let k be any field of characteristic p>0, let A=k[[t]], and let X=P1_A times P1_A, with affine coordinates x,y. Put U=Spec A[x,x^{-1},y]. In the t-adic henselization of A[x,x^{-1}] there is a unique element u congruent to zero modulo t satisfying

    u^p-u=t/x.

The derivative is -1, so this is the elementary simple-root henselian lifting property. The principal ideal (y-u) defines a finitely presented closed henselian subscheme of U^h.

**Claim.** This ideal has no extension to a finitely presented ideal on all X^h.

Suppose such an extension existed. Devadas (2025), Theorem 4.3.1 and Corollary 4.3.2, apply because X is proper and finitely presented over the Noetherian henselian pair (A,(t)). They would algebraize it to a closed subscheme of X. On U, let J be the resulting algebraic ideal in C=A[x,x^{-1},y]. We would have J C^h=(y-u).

Write K=k((t)). The polynomial

    F(y)=y^p-y-t/x

is irreducible in K(x)[y]. Indeed, after the reciprocal substitution z=1/y, the polynomial x-t z^p-x z^{p-1}, up to a nonzero scalar and sign, is Eisenstein for the x-adic valuation: its leading coefficient is a unit, every other coefficient is divisible by x, and its constant coefficient is divisible by x but not x^2. This proves irreducibility in every characteristic p, without a separability or cardinality qualification on k.

Evaluation y=u embeds C/(F) into the field generated by this henselian root, and has kernel (F). Therefore every element of J lies in (F), so J C^h is contained in F C^h. But in C^h one has

    F=product_{a in F_p}(y-u-a).

These p factors are pairwise comaximal. In particular F C^h is strictly smaller than (y-u): evaluation at y=u+1 kills F and sends y-u to 1. This contradicts J C^h=(y-u), proving the claim.

The example is about closure across the missing parameter points. It neither refutes Devadas's algebraization theorem nor gives a counterexample to coherent algebraization for a sheaf already defined on all of X^h. It shows why a genuine henselian ideal over an open parameter space is not automatically the input required by that theorem.

## K-theoretic consequence not inferred

For B=Spec(R) times a proper smooth curve P, replacing B by its henselization along P does not itself justify a negative-K descent calculation. One would need an actual henselian Nisnevich descent theorem with the required stalks and continuity, and then an actual K0 algebraization statement for the resulting lift. The known subobject theorem does not supply that second statement for an arbitrary perfect-complex class. No vanishing of K_{-1}(B), K_{-1}(B^h), or ordinary restriction cokernel is asserted here.
