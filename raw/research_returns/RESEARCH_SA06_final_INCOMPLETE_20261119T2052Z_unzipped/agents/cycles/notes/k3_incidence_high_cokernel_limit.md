# Why the ambient K3-incidence zero-cycle construction does not give the high cokernel

**Research note, 2026-11-19, 16:39 UTC. Restricted method analysis only; no new main-task result.**

Consider the proposed nonsplit model with exceptional divisor V=P^m times S, where S is a smooth quartic K3 in P3 and m>=3. Its conormal line is O(1) external O_S(1). The principal target is split over the smooth incidence H={sum x_i s_i=0}, where the s_i span the four coordinate sections on S. Thus H is a projective bundle over S of rank m. In codimension two, its quotient by the normal hyperplane class identifies with CH0(S): the projective-bundle relation has degree m>2 and the quotient substitutes h=-c1(O_S(1)). This made CH0(S)^0 a possible target for a cokernel.

There is, however, an actual support lift in this model. We state its exact geometric hypotheses rather than claiming that an unverified new local contraction has been constructed. Assume the proposed regular projective resolution Z over the excellent henselian local source exists as described, its puncture is X, and its actual map to P^m times P3 extends the exceptional embedding. Assume its principal target has the split incidence model just described. These are precisely the intended properties of the equation F+tau*ell*G=0.

Let p,q be any two points of S. Choose a sufficiently high-degree smooth connected hypersurface curve C in S through p and q. The linear system can separate the required point jets, so the usual immersion Bertini argument supplies such C; choose its ambient homogeneous equation g on P3. On Z, the divisor g=0 is regular near its exceptional fibre P^m times C: its restriction to the smooth exceptional divisor is transverse. The principal target cuts on it the incidence projective bundle H_C over C, also smooth. Bad loci away from the exceptional fibre can be removed at the original local construction stage; properness ensures that this removes no point above the vertex.

The divisor's restriction to X is a closed regular Cartier support W. Its closure at the vertex is an integral local codimension-one support. If normalization is needed at the vertex, finite normalization changes no point of W; the connected exceptional fibre selects the local component. The resulting local ring is excellent henselian, has residue field k, and has dimension N-1=m+2>=5. Its principal f-section has regular puncture W intersect Y. Thus the already proved integral ordinary Picard surjectivity theorem C.3z/C.3bh applies to this support pair.

The line bundle O_C(p-q) pulls back to an actual ordinary Picard class on W intersect Y. Lift that class to an actual ordinary Picard class on W, and push forward the defining fixed proper-curve family along the closed immersion W into X. Refined Gysin base change identifies its restriction with the corresponding pushforward on Y. Under the projective-bundle quotient this is exactly [p]-[q] in CH0(S), with coefficient one. Indeed the incidence map is flat, so pulling the divisor C and then the point divisor commutes with their proper pushforwards.

Point differences generate CH0(S)^0 over an algebraically closed field. Consequently this entire indicated target subgroup lifts **integrally and ordinarily** in the proposed model. The argument neither uses a formal K0 lift nor a varying henselized parameter. It does not assert surjectivity for arbitrary local germs or for arbitrary singular divisor-sized supports.

The first-order Hodge detector also does not resolve the candidate: the mixed curve-family cohomology in degree three on a K3 vanishes, and the apparent remaining contraction involves H2(S,O_S(1)), which vanishes. These facts explain why the earlier complex divisor-times-Picard obstruction does not transfer to this zero-cycle variant, but the actual support argument above is the stronger reason the proposed target classes do not furnish a counterexample.
