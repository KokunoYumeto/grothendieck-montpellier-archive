# A normalized residue does not descend to ordinary proper-family quotients

**Research-method exclusion, 19 November 2026.** This note proves no nonvanishing of an ordinary local Chow quotient and supplies no main-gate movement. It uses the already fully audited tetrahedral construction TG/TS and the actual proper-symbol lemma EL (also reproduced in C.3bq). The coefficient fields and the degree of the relation group are essential.

## 1. The fixed exceptional surface and its natural product calculation

Fix a prime p and put F=overline(F_p). Let E_F/F be the ordered tetrahedral exceptional surface of TG.1–TG.5. The complete, parameter-natural external-product comparison TS.1 and a single choice of the splitting TS.2 give, for every noetherian parameter scheme P/F,

\[
 K(E_F\times_F P)_{\mathbf Q}
 \simeq K(P)_{\mathbf Q}^{\oplus33}
       \oplus\Sigma^{-2}K(P)_{\mathbf Q}.
\]

The splitting was chosen once over F and then transported by the natural external-product equivalence. Consequently its point-evaluation maps are the ordinary point-evaluation maps on the displayed K(P) factors. This is stronger than a numerical calculation of the ranks of the individual groups. Let kappa denote the selected nonzero generator of the final K_{−2}(E_F)_Q=Q factor. It has Adams weight zero.

Now set

\[
 k_0=\overline{\mathbf F_p(v)},\qquad a=v\in k_0^*,
 \qquad E_0=E_F\times_F k_0,
 \qquad k=\overline{k_0(u)}.
\]

Here u and v are algebraically independent over F_p. The element a has infinite multiplicative order, hence is nonzero in k_0^* tensor Q. The preceding equivalence applies after either coefficient-field extension; it is not a claim that the rational negative groups are unchanged under extension. In fact,

\[
 K_{-1}(E_0)_{\mathbf Q}=\kappa\,K_1(k_0)_{\mathbf Q},
 \qquad
 \kappa K_2(k)_{\mathbf Q}\subset F_\gamma^2K_0(E_k)_{\mathbf Q}.
\]

For the original constant field F itself, K1(F)_Q=0. That vanishing cannot be carried over to k0.

## 2. The ordinary degree-minus-one relation group is zero

Define the ordinary smooth-proper endpoint subgroup in degree minus one by

\[
 J_{-1}^{\mathrm{prop}}(E_0)=
 \left\langle i_t^*\xi-i_o^*\xi:
 \begin{array}{l}
 P/k_0\text{ smooth connected projective curve},\\
 o,t\in P(k_0),\quad
 \xi\in K_{-1}(E_0\times_{k_0}P)_{\mathbf Q}
 \end{array}\right\rangle.
\]

The natural product calculation and regularity of P identify

\[
 K_{-1}(E_0\times P)_{\mathbf Q}=\kappa K_1(P)_{\mathbf Q}.
\]

Both endpoint maps K1(P)→K1(k0)=k0* are the same. To see this directly, smooth-curve localization gives

\[
 \bigoplus_{x\in P(k_0)}K_1(k_0)\longrightarrow K_1(P)
 \longrightarrow k_0(P)^*
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_x\mathbf Z.
\]

The image in the function field is the constant unit group k0*, since P is proper and connected. This summand splits by pullback from k0. Its kernel is generated by point-unit pushforwards, equivalently by u'([O_P(x)]−1). Every such class restricts to zero at every point, including x: the derived restriction of a rank-zero line class is zero. Thus every endpoint restriction of a K1(P) class is its same constant-unit part. Therefore

\[
                 J_{-1}^{\mathrm{prop}}(E_0)=0.
\]

In particular the nonzero class kappa a remains nonzero after quotienting by this ordinary degree-minus-one relation subgroup.

## 3. A genuine ordinary degree-zero relation has nonzero residue

The actual proper-symbol lemma EL produces a smooth connected projective curve P/k, two points o,t, and a class xi in K2(P) with endpoint values 0 and {u,a}. It is the normalization of a suitable integral component through (u,a) of

\[
 (f-1)^2(g-1)^2=c f g,
 \qquad c=\frac{(u-1)^2(a-1)^2}{ua}.
\]

The whole proof, including all characteristics and the zero evaluation at the unique smooth boundary point (0,1), was retained and independently audited in EL and again in C.3bq. Multiplication by kappa is an actual external product in ordinary perfect K-theory. Apply the weight-two K0 projector on the projective threefold E_k times P, as in EL/C.3bq. Its endpoint difference is exactly

\[
                   s=\kappa\{u,a\}
                     \in J_K(E_k).
\]

Thus s is already zero in the intrinsic ordinary top quotient, before adding any exceptional-cycle image.

Choose an extension to k of the u-adic valuation on k0(u), normalized by v(u)=1. At a finite intermediate extension L/k0(u), use the associated discrete valuation w with w(u)=e. Its residue field is k0 because k0 is algebraically closed. In additive rational K1 notation, define the normalized residue on the field coefficient by

\[
             \partial_v^{\mathrm{norm}}=
                    \frac1e\,\partial_w.
\]

The tame-symbol ramification formula makes these maps compatible under finite extensions along the chosen valuation; hence they give a map on the filtered colimit K2(k)_Q. With the convention partial({uniformizer,unit})=unit,

\[
 \partial_v^{\mathrm{norm}}\{u,a\}=a,
 \qquad
 \partial_v^{\mathrm{norm}}s=\kappa a\ne0.
\]

A different uniform sign convention changes the displayed result to its negative and does not affect nonvanishing. This calculation is on the certified tetrahedral coefficient. It does not require a yet-unproved normalized residue theorem for arbitrary singular schemes: any proposed construction compatible with the smooth-stratum localization diagram and external products must give this value on this coefficient.

It follows that neither a raw target K_{−1}(E0)_Q nor its quotient by J_{−1}^{prop}(E0) can receive a residue map from the ordinary quotient S(E_k)/J_K(E_k) with this normalization. The latter quotient does not repair the former failure, since J_{−1}^{prop}(E0)=0.

## 4. What the countercheck says about bad reduction

Ordinary proper-family relations in degree zero can have residues outside every smooth-proper endpoint relation in degree minus one. Thus reducing a generic proper parameter to a smooth proper special parameter, or imposing only those special-fibre relations, cannot justify a residue detector. A valid target must also account for the extra degeneration relations arising from models with singular special fibres and the support K-theory used by localization. This note does not classify those degeneration relations or prove that they exhaust a Jacobian quotient.

There is a separate elementary warning about using normalization of a nodal parameter. If nu:C→D identifies distinct points x,y on a smooth proper curve, then the nontrivial class O_C(x−y) can have

\[
 \nu_*([O_C(x-y)]-[O_C])=[O_{\nu(x)}]-[O_{\nu(y)}]=0
                     \quad\text{in }G_0(D),
\]

although it is nonzero in Pic0(C). This shows why replacing a singular special parameter by its proper smooth normalization while retaining only separate ordinary endpoint relations can discard information. It is not a construction of a generic perfect family with a prescribed residue.
