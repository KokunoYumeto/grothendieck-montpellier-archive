# A relative-curve obstruction to the proposed proper specialization principle

It is not a counterexample to the negative exceptional-surface problem or to UK.7, and it supplies no main-gate movement.

## 1. Fixed all-characteristic data

Fix a prime p. Over F=algebraic closure of F_p choose the smooth elliptic curve A given by y²=x³−x for p odd, and by y²+y=x³ for p=2, with its point at infinity 0. Put k0=algebraic closure of F(A), and let q∈A(k0) be the generic point of the original curve, viewed as a k0-point. It is nontorsion: for each n>0 the finite group scheme A[n] has finitely many geometric points, all algebraic over F, whereas q is transcendental over F. Set D={0,q} with its reduced scheme structure.

Let

    F(T) = fib(K(A×T) → K(D×T))_Q

be the actual relative nonconnective perfect K-spectrum. For each integer r write J_r^prop(F/k0) for the subgroup of π_r F(Spec k0) generated by endpoint differences of actual classes in π_r F(P), with P a smooth connected proper k0-curve and two k0-rational endpoints. The target quotient below is the full component-image quotient, not an unspecified numerical or algebraic-representative quotient.

The ordinary rank/determinant description of K0(A) gives

    K0(A)_Q = Q ⊕ Pic(A)_Q,

and restriction to D sends (r,L) to (r,r). The long exact sequence of the defining fibre therefore gives a surjective map

    φ : π0 F(k0) → Pic(A)_Q.                            (RC.1)

Its kernel is the image of K1(D)_Q; no calculation of that image is needed.

## 2. Every actual proper relative K0 relation has zero Picard image

Let P/k0 be smooth connected proper and β∈π0F(P). Clear a denominator in the relative class β itself, so that it is represented by an integral relative K0 class before mapping to K0(A×P). Write α for that integral image. Its restrictions on both marked sections are then zero in integral K0(P) by the relative fibre exact sequence. It has rank zero, and its determinant line bundle M on A×P restricts to the trivial line bundle on both {0}×P and {q}×P. Dividing by the chosen integer after the argument recovers the original rational assertion.

Let f:P→Pic^d(A) be the morphism classifying the fibre line bundles M_t, with their constant degree d. Fix a point o∈P(k0) and translate Pic^d(A) to J=Pic^0(A) by M_o. The resulting map f0:P→J sends o to 0. The line bundle

    M ⊗ pr_A^*(M_o)^−1

is normalized at the origin section, up to a line bundle pulled from P which is itself trivial because M is trivial there. By the normalized Poincare universal property, it is the pullback of the normalized Poincare bundle on A×J under 1×f0. Restriction to q consequently gives

    f0^* N_q = O_P,                                    (RC.2)

where N_q is the evaluation line bundle on J associated with q−0. Under the principal duality J≃A this is a nontorsion degree-zero line bundle, since q is nontorsion. The fixed one-dimensional fibre M_o|q only tensors by a constant line, which is a trivial line bundle on P.

If f0 were nonconstant, it would be a finite surjective morphism of smooth proper curves P→J. Such a map is finite flat, including in the inseparable case. The norm identity is

    Nm_f0(f0^*N_q)=N_q^{deg(f0)}.

Together with (RC.2), this would make N_q torsion, a contradiction. Hence f0 is constant. All fibre determinants are the same, so the endpoint difference of α has zero Pic(A)_Q class. The rank difference is already zero. This proves

    φ(J_0^prop(F/k0))=0.                               (RC.3)

Consequently the quotient after killing BOTH the full component kernel and every proper relation is still

    π0F(k0)/(ker φ + J_0^prop(F/k0)) ≃ Pic(A)_Q.         (RC.4)

In particular its degree-zero part is nonzero. No universal regular-quotient theorem is needed: the preceding determinant/Poincare/norm argument proves exactly the proper-family assertion used here.

## 3. A genuine proper relative K1 relation with nonzero normalized residue

Choose L=O_A(q−0), which is nonzero in Pic^0(A)_Q. Adjoin a transcendental u and put k=algebraic closure of k0(u). Apply the complete marked-family lemma VE.2 to the smooth proper curve A_k, with marks 0 and q, line bundle L, and unit u. It gives a smooth connected proper curve P/k, two points o,t, and an actual ξ∈K1(A_k×P) such that

    ξ_o=0,    ξ_t=u([L]−1),
    ξ|{0}×P=ξ|{q}×P=0 in K1(P).

The defining fibre sequence gives an actual lift η∈π1F(P). Its endpoint difference

    s = η_t − η_o ∈ J_1^prop(F/k)

has component image exactly u([L]−1) in K1(A_k). No assertion that either relative endpoint is a unique lift is made or needed.

All the finite-presentation data, K-classes, equalities and endpoints descend to a finite extension K/k0(u). This follows from filtered-colimit compatibility for the K-theory of perfect complexes, with one further finite extension allowed to realize the finite equality witnesses. In particular the descended class s_K is an actual element of J_1^prop(F/K), given by the descended proper parameter and its K-rational endpoints. Fix an extension of the u-adic valuation to K, with discrete valuation ring R, ramification index e=v_R(u), and residue field k0. This single finite-K relation and residue suffice for the countermodel; no residue map on the whole algebraic closure is asserted.

For the fixed pair (A,D) over k0, relative vertical localization has a residue

    ∂_R : π1 F(K) → π0 F(k0).

To justify its target, form the localization fibre sequences for A×R and D×R. These ambient schemes and their special fibres are regular. Regular vertical purity identifies the support spectra with K(A) and K(D), respectively. Those identifications commute with the two marked-section pullbacks by Tor-independent base change. Taking the fibre gives the displayed relative residue; no purity statement for a singular ambient scheme is used.

Set ∂^norm=(1/e)∂_R on rational groups. Projection/module compatibility with the unit residue gives

    φ(∂^norm s) = [L]−1,

or simply L under the rank-zero Picard identification. Indeed ∂_R(u)=e and L is defined over the constant field, so the product residue is e([L]−1). Changing the common sign convention only changes this to its negative.

By (RC.4), this residue is nonzero in the quotient by both the full component kernel and all ordinary proper K0 endpoint relations. Thus no residue map with this normalization can descend from the proper K1 quotient to that target.

## 4. Exact scope of the obstruction

The example is a relative K-functor of a marked elliptic curve. It is not asserted to be a retract of K(E) for any negative exceptional SNC surface. A negative exceptional surface may have additional component families outside a chosen support, and those are precisely part of the unresolved UK.7 image. This countermodel therefore does NOT prove that the desired E-specific specialization assertion is false.

It does prove that eliminating the full degree-zero component kernel in the target is not, by itself, a sufficient formal explanation for descent of proper-family residues. Any successful specialization theorem for E must use additional E-specific geometry or an explicit construction of those outside-support proper families. A claim that the support node relations automatically force the abstract PL tensor to vanish is also unavailable.

For the supported K1 construction HK, the curves A_i and their marked intersections produce exactly this kind of relative-curve behaviour before allowing other supports in E. This identifies why pushing the new higher families through a valuation needs an argument beyond the existence of the higher family and beyond C.3bt alone.
