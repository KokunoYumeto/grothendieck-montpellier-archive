# A polynomial removal of the K1 component kernel

**Research proof; unregistered; low-degree field-K3 source audit remains explicit below.** Written 19 November 2026 by `/root/frobenius_continuation`. The purpose is to make the endpoint of the supported HK construction exact on the whole exceptional scheme, before applying the normal-base K1 lifting theorem. It does not solve the symbolic-unit/base-change gate.

## HG.1. Setting and field input

Retain the exceptional SNC surface and ample negative combination hypotheses of C.3bt. All groups in this note are rational. Let

\[
\mathcal T_P=\operatorname{fib}\left(
 \bigoplus_eK(C_e\times P)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_vK(P)\right),
\]

with the same canonical totalization and geometric nullhomotopy as UK.2. For a point or a smooth proper curve (P), the actual exact sequences in the next degree are

\[
\bigoplus_vK_3(P)\longrightarrow\pi_2\mathcal T_P
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_eK_2(C_e\times P)
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_vK_2(P),
\tag{HG.1}
\]
\[
\pi_2\mathcal T_P\xrightarrow{\partial_P}K_1(E\times P)
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_iK_1(E_i\times P).
\tag{HG.2}
\]

Let (J_1(E)) be the subgroup generated by endpoint differences of actual (K_1) classes on fixed products (E\times P), with (P) smooth connected projective. Put

\[
                    r=(\psi^2-4)/4.
\tag{HG.3}
\]

The extra field input is the standard low-degree statement

\[
(\psi^2-4)K_3(k)_{\mathbf Q}
   \subseteq\operatorname{im}\bigl(K_3^M(k)_{\mathbf Q}\to K_3(k)_{\mathbf Q}\bigr).
\tag{FK}
\]

Only this containment is used; no decomposition of (K_1(E)), (\pi_2\mathcal T_P), or (K_1(E\times P)) into eigenspaces is assumed. Weibel's ChapterVI, low-degree exact sequence4.3.1 and Adams action4.9, give FK: the quotient by Milnor (K_3) is (H^1(k,\mathbf Z(2))), and its rational Adams action has weight two. ChapterIV, Example5.9.1, gives weight (n) on every (n)-fold Milnor symbol.

## HG.2. The asserted kernel inclusion

The proposed conclusion is

\[
r^2\ker\left(K_1(E)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_iK_1(E_i)\right)
                              \subseteq J_1(E).
\tag{HG.4}
\]

The subgroup (J_1(E)) is preserved by the Adams operation because it is functorial on the actual products and under the two endpoint pullbacks. Thus all the polynomial operations below preserve actual proper families.

Take (\kappa) in this component kernel. By HG.2 for the point, choose (y\in\pi_2\mathcal T_k) with (\partial_k y=\kappa). Apply (r). On a smooth edge curve (C), restriction to its function field intertwines the operation, and (K_2(k(C))) has pure weight two by Matsumoto and product compatibility. Therefore the edge image of (r y) belongs to the kernel of

\[
                     K_2(C)\longrightarrow K_2(k(C)).
\]

Localization for the regular curve identifies that kernel with the image of the sum of point pushforwards of (K_2(k)). This is a kernel statement; no global injectivity of (K_2(C)) is assumed.

Matsumoto expresses each coefficient as a finite sum of symbols ({a,b}), (a,b\in k^*). By the projection formula, the point pushforward equals ({a,b}[\mathcal O_x]). On a regular curve,

\[
[\mathcal O_x]=[\mathcal O_C(x)]-1
\quad\text{in }K_0(C),
\]

because the twist on the finite point is trivial. Thus every edge image is a finite sum of

\[
                    \{a,b\}([L_e]-1),
                   \qquad L_e\in\operatorname{Pic}(C_e).
\tag{HG.5}
\]

These are pure weight-three classes, directly by multiplicativity and the curve's square-zero rank-zero ideal.

## HG.3. Remove degrees using the same actual component corrections

For each of the finitely many symbols ({a,b}), use the surjective degree map ET.9

\[
\bigoplus_i\operatorname{Pic}(E_i)_{\mathbf Q}
                  \longrightarrow\bigoplus_e\mathbf Q
\]

to lift the vector of edge degrees. Multiply the component divisor (K_0) differences by that constant (K_2(k)) symbol. Their actual images in (\pi_2\mathcal T_k) are canonical images from the component term in the totalization, so their boundaries in HG.2 vanish. Subtract them from (r y). The remaining edge vector is a sum of HG.5 with every (L_e\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(C_e)). This step uses only degree surjectivity, not full Picard restriction surjectivity. Rational divisors and denominators are cleared before choosing their actual line-bundle representatives.

## HG.4. Proper K2 edge families from marked VE

For a degree-zero tuple in HG.5, apply the marked VE lemma with constant unit (b). It gives actual (\xi_e\in K_1(C_e\times P)), with endpoint difference (b([L_e]-1)) and literally zero restriction at every marked triple point. Multiply by the other constant unit (a\in K_1(k)). The resulting actual classes

\[
                \Gamma_e=a\cdot\xi_e\in K_2(C_e\times P)
\]

have endpoint difference ({a,b}([L_e]-1)) and still have literally zero marked restrictions in (K_2(P)). HG.1 therefore lifts the edge tuple to (\widetilde y\in\pi_2\mathcal T_P). Its boundary in HG.2 is an actual proper (K_1) family on (E\times P).

Apply this to the finitely many symbol tuples separately, or combine them on a smooth curve in the product of their proper parameter curves through their two endpoint tuples. Let (h\in J_1(E)) be the sum of the resulting endpoint differences. Because their edge images equal the remaining edges of (r y), HG.1 for the point supplies a tuple (\nu\in\bigoplus_vK_3(k)) with

\[
                      r\kappa-h=\partial_k(\nu).
\tag{HG.6}
\]

The notation on the right denotes the canonical composite vertex map and boundary. This equality retains the entire coherent-lift ambiguity; no uniqueness is assumed.

## HG.5. Remove the vertex ambiguity with one more polynomial

Apply (r) to HG.6. The natural Adams operation commutes with the totalization and the closed-union boundary, at the same arbitrary-scheme scope used in UK.4. Thus

\[
                 r^2\kappa-rh=\partial_k(r\nu).
\tag{HG.7}
\]

By FK, each entry of (r\nu) is a rational sum of Milnor triple symbols ({a,b,c}). The actual proper-curve (K_2) symbol construction of C.3bq realizes every ({b,c}) as an evaluation difference. Multiplication by the constant (a\in K_1(k)) gives an actual (K_3) family with endpoint ({a,b,c}). Applying the vertex map and then the boundary in HG.1–HG.2 produces an actual proper (K_1) family on (E) with endpoint (partial_k(r\nu)). Hence the right side of HG.7 lies in (J_1(E)), as does (rh). This proves HG.4, subject only to the stated standard field input FK and the already audited bt/bq inputs.

## HG.6. Exact HK endpoints and fixed-base ambient correction

Let (\beta\in F_\gamma^2K_0(E)_{\mathbf Q}) have component zero-cycle restrictions of degree zero, and let (u\in k^*). HK constructs actual proper (K_1) families whose summed endpoint class (\delta\in K_1(E)) has component image (\{u\}\beta|_{E_i}). Thus

\[
                 \delta-\{u\}\beta\in\ker(K_1E\to\bigoplus_iK_1E_i).
\]

By HG.4, (r^2(\delta-\{u\}\beta)\in J_1(E)). The desired product has pure weight three, so (r(\{u\}\beta)=\{u\}\beta). Since (r^2\delta\in J_1(E)), it follows that

\[
                          \{u\}\beta\in J_1(E).
\tag{HG.8}
\]

This equality is on the whole exceptional scheme, rather than only after restricting to its components. It means a finite sum of actual fixed proper families; combining parameters as above gives a single family if desired.

For a normal local base in NK.7, each family in HG.8 lifts on the actual fixed (Z\times P). Under the henselian point isomorphism FF.4, let (\beta_Z\) be the unique weight-two lift of (\beta). The discrepancy between the lifted total endpoint and ({u\}\beta_Z) lies in the image of the residue-zero subgroup of (R^*=K_1(R)). Applying (s=(\psi^2-2)/6) kills it and preserves the desired weight-three product. Consequently

\[
                         \{u\}\beta_Z\in J_1(Z)
                                  \qquad(u\in k^*).
\tag{HG.9}
\]

This argument uses polynomial operations and exact sequences only. It makes no global higher-(K) weight projector and assumes no Adams semisimplicity on the singular totalization.

## HG.7. The remaining limitation

HG.9 is for constants (u\in k^*\) on the original fixed local coefficient-field model. A valuation argument needs a symbolic unit after a transcendental scalar extension. The ring (R\otimes_k k(u)) is generally nonlocal, so NK.7 does not apply there by formal base change. The regular-target specialization theorem RS can be used only once an actual constant-target proper family and exact endpoint have both been supplied. HG removes the endpoint ambiguity at a fixed local base; it does not provide that missing family over the nonlocal scalar extension.

No change to UK.7 or main-gate status is claimed. No tensor-exterior-product injectivity or general singular-specialization theorem is assumed.
