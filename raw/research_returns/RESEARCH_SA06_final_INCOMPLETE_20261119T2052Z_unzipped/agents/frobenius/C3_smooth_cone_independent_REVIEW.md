# Independent audit of C.3u: ordinary Picard classes on a smooth cone

**Conclusion:** the complete typed proof in ../real_weil/C3_smooth_cone_draft.tex passes. We checked ordinary algebraic equivalence, integral coefficients, arbitrary characteristic, nonhomogeneous divisors, and each new external input. The result is restricted to the stated smooth cone source; it does not extend to a deformation or an arbitrary local germ by this argument.

## Exact statement checked

Let \(V/k\) be smooth projective integral of dimension at least three over an algebraically closed field, with \(L\) very ample and its full section ring generated in degree one. On the strict cone puncture \(X\), pullback identifies the **point group** of \(\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{red}}(V)\) with line bundles ordinarily algebraically equivalent to zero. For any nonzero local element \(f\) with regular divisor puncture \(Y\), ordinary restriction is integrally surjective. The kernel is zero in characteristic zero and a finite \(p\)-primary point group in characteristic \(p\). Ordinary rational \(J^1\) therefore restricts isomorphically.

## Proof checks

1. **Resolution and boundary.** Degree-one generation identifies the vertex blow-up with the total space of \(L^{-1}\). Localization and ind-etale strict henselization preserve regularity. The resulting integral noetherian \(Z\) is proper over \(R\), its closed fibre is the single reduced prime divisor \(V\), and its normal bundle is \(L^{-1}\). The actual projection \(\pi:Z\to V\) survives. Closing a rational divisor extends each line bundle on \(X\), and the kernel of \(\operatorname{Pic}(Z)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X)\) consists of powers of \(\mathcal O_Z(V)\).

2. **Actual family and pointwise injectivity.** The normalized Poincare bundle on \(V\times A\), with \(A=\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{red}}(V)\), pulls back to an actual bundle on \(X\times A\). If \(\pi^*Q=\mathcal O_Z(bV)\), restriction gives \(Q=L^{-b}\); algebraic triviality and ampleness force \(b=0\). The proof correctly asserts injectivity on points without assuming a trivial infinitesimal kernel. Restricting to chains of curves in \(A\) proves ordinary algebraic triviality.

3. **All torsion at one suitable prime.** Choose \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\) avoiding torsion in \(\operatorname{NS}(V)/\mathbf Z[L]\). An \(\ell^n\)-torsion bundle \(M\) extends to \(\widetilde M\) with \(\widetilde M^{\ell^n}=\mathcal O_Z(bV)\). Its restriction \(Q\) has class in \(\mathbf Z[L]\). If \([Q]=a[L]\), twisting by \(\mathcal O_Z(aV)\) has the correct sign and makes the restriction algebraically trivial. Restriction of the remaining power identity and ampleness force its boundary coefficient to vanish. Both the new extension and \(Q\) are torsion. The quotient \(T=\widetilde M\otimes\pi^*Q^{-1}\) defines a finite etale Kummer torsor trivial on \(V\), because its defining unit lies in the divisible group \(k^\times\). Proper henselian finite-etale equivalence lifts its section to \(Z\). Thus \(M=\rho^*Q\), giving the claimed equality of all \(\ell^n\)-torsion groups.

4. **Density without arbitrary family algebraization.** C.3n places an ordinary class in a closed abelian image \(B\) of the local Picard scheme. Its dense prime-to-characteristic torsion lies in the closed proper image \(P\) of \(A\); therefore \(B\subset P\). For the target, the proof correctly uses the product of local Picard schemes of finite normalizations. C.3p lifts all target \(\ell^n\)-torsion to the source. Thus each target ordinary abelian image is contained in the closed proper image \(r(P)\). Every point of this image lifts to a point of \(P\) over the algebraically closed field, and that point already comes from the actual Poincare family. This gives integral surjectivity without inferring that arbitrary henselized-product families algebraize.

5. **Kernel.** The closed kernel in \(A\) has no nonzero prime-to-characteristic torsion by C.3p and pointwise injectivity. A positive-dimensional reduced connected subgroup would be an abelian variety with such torsion. The kernel scheme is therefore finite; its finite point group is zero in characteristic zero and \(p\)-primary in characteristic \(p\). This is precisely the claimed conclusion.
