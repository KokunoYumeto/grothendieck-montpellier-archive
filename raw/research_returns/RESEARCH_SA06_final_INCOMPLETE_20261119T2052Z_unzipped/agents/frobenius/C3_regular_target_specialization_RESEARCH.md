# Regular-target specialization of actual proper K1 relations

**Proved auxiliary under the displayed semistable-model hypotheses; unregistered, pending full independent review.** Written 19 November 2026 by `/root/frobenius_continuation`. This isolates the regular-target part of the proposed valuation argument. It does not construct the requisite lift on a constant regular target after adjoining a transcendental coefficient.

## RS.1. Precise setting

Let (k) be algebraically closed, and let (A) be an algebraic discrete valuation (k)-algebra obtained by localization of a finite extension of (k[u]_{(u)}). Its residue field is (k); write (F=\operatorname{Frac}A), choose a uniformizer (\pi), and let (e=\operatorname{ord}_\pi(u)>0). Let (Y/k) be smooth and quasi-projective.

Suppose (\mathscr P\to\operatorname{Spec}A) is a proper flat regular scheme of relative dimension one, its generic fibre (P_F) is smooth and connected, and its special fibre is a connected reduced strict normal-crossing curve

\[
T=\bigcup_{j\in V}P_j.
\]

Every (P_j) is smooth connected projective over (k), every node joins two different components transversely, and there are no triple intersections. Multiple nodes between the same pair are permitted. This is an assumption on a supplied regular semistable model; this note does not invoke a semistable-reduction theorem to construct it.

Let (o_F,t_F\in P_F(F)) and let (o,t:\operatorname{Spec}A\to\mathscr P) be their extensions, which exist by properness. Both sections meet the smooth part of (T). Indeed the local equation at a node is (xy=\pi\) up to a unit. A section through the node would make both (x) and (y) have positive integral valuation, contradicting (\operatorname{ord}_\pi(xy)=1). Thus no separate assumption that the dual graph is a tree is made.

Define (J_0^{\mathrm{prop}}(Y)\subset K_0(Y)_{\mathbf Q}) to be the subgroup generated by endpoint differences of actual perfect (K_0) classes on fixed products (Y\times_k C), for smooth connected projective curves (C/k). Define the residue

\[
\partial_Y:K_1(Y_F)_{\mathbf Q}\longrightarrow K_0(Y)_{\mathbf Q}
\]

by localization for (Y_A), followed by purity for its regular special fibre. All the schemes used are noetherian and regular: the algebraic DVR and its regular model are essentially of finite type over the perfect field (k), and product with the smooth (Y) preserves regularity. Perfect and coherent (K)-theory agree on these regular schemes.

## RS.2. The specialization assertion

For every actual class (\eta\in K_1(Y_F\times_FP_F)_{\mathbf Q}),

\[
\partial_Y\bigl(t_F^*\eta-o_F^*\eta\bigr)
              \in J_0^{\mathrm{prop}}(Y).
\tag{RS.2}
\]

In particular, if for a fixed (z\in K_0(Y)_{\mathbf Q}) the actual endpoint difference equals

\[
t_F^*\eta-o_F^*\eta=\{u\}\cdot z_F,
\tag{RS.3}
\]

then (z\in J_0^{\mathrm{prop}}(Y)), since its residue is (e z) and (e) is invertible over (\mathbf Q).

The condition in RS.3 is an equality in the actual (K_1(Y_F)), not merely after restriction to exceptional components. No assertion that the HK construction supplies such a lift or equality is made here.

## RS.3. Decompose the supported boundary

Set (\mathscr X=Y\times_k\mathscr P), with closed support (Y\times T). Localization gives

\[
b=\partial_{\mathscr X}(\eta)
 \in K_0(\mathscr X\text{ on }Y\times T)_{\mathbf Q},
\qquad b\mapsto0\text{ in }K_0(\mathscr X)_{\mathbf Q}.
\tag{RS.4}
\]

There are classes (\beta_j\in K_0(Y\times P_j)_{\mathbf Q}) whose regular Gysin images sum to (b). One way to check surjectivity is the support-union Mayer–Vietoris sequence. Its intersections are disjoint copies of (Y) at the nodes. Purity identifies their supported negative term with (K_{-1}(Y)=0), so the direct sum of the component-supported (K_0) groups surjects onto the union-supported group. Iterating over a union of components introduces no triple intersection term. Purity for each regular component identifies its supported (K_0) group with (K_0(Y\times P_j)).

There is also a direct coherent proof of this exact step. Since (\mathscr X) is regular, its supported perfect (K_0) is the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves with the indicated support. A finite filtration by the component ideals reduces each such sheaf to coherent sheaves supported scheme-theoretically on individual (Y\times P_j). Those components are regular, so their coherent classes are perfect classes. This proves the same surjection without any assumption about a singular ambient purity statement.

Let (i_j:Y\times P_j\hookrightarrow\mathscr X). After forgetting supports, RS.4 consequently says

\[
\sum_j (i_j)_*\beta_j=0\quad\text{in }K_0(\mathscr X)_{\mathbf Q}.
\tag{RS.5}
\]

No uniqueness of the tuple (\beta_j) is needed.

## RS.4. Restrict to a component and push to the regular target

Fix (j). Its normal line bundle in (\mathscr X) is pulled back from

\[
N_j=\mathcal O_{\mathscr P}(P_j)|_{P_j}
   \simeq\mathcal O_{P_j}\left(-\sum_{b\in B_j}b\right),
\tag{RS.6}
\]

where (B_j) is the set of nodes on (P_j), counted once per adjacent branch. To verify this, the total special fibre is the principal divisor of (\pi). Restricting its trivial line bundle to (P_j) gives the self-intersection normal line tensored with the effective node divisor cut out by all other components. The fibre is reduced and every intersection is transverse, so each multiplicity is one.

Pull RS.5 back to (Y\times P_j). The self-intersection formula for the regular divisor is multiplication by

\[
\lambda_{-1}(N_j^\vee)=1-[N_j^\vee]
               =-\sum_{b\in B_j}[\mathcal O_b]
                    \quad\text{in }K_0(P_j).
\tag{RS.7}
\]

The last equality follows from successive Cartier sequences; twists of sheaves on the (k)-rational points have the same class. For (i\ne j), transversality gives Tor-independent base change, so the cross term is the sum of the point Gysin images of (\beta_i|_{Y\times\{b\}}) over nodes joining (i) to (j). Let (j(b)) denote the other component at a node (b\in B_j), and write (\beta_{j,b}) for that point restriction. We obtain

\[
\sum_{b\in B_j}(\iota_b)_*
       \bigl(\beta_{j(b),b}-\beta_{j,b}\bigr)=0
       \quad\text{in }K_0(Y\times P_j)_{\mathbf Q}.
\tag{RS.8}
\]

Push forward under the proper smooth projection (Y\times P_j\to Y). Its composite with the (k)-rational point section is the identity on (Y), so

\[
\sum_{b\in B_j}
       \bigl(\beta_{j(b),b}-\beta_{j,b}\bigr)=0
                     \quad\text{in }K_0(Y)_{\mathbf Q}.
\tag{RS.9}
\]

All pushforwards here are proper perfect pushforwards between the indicated regular schemes; no singular Gysin map is being used.

## RS.5. The connected graph forces equal classes modulo proper relations

Put (W=K_0(Y)_{\mathbf Q}/J_0^{\mathrm{prop}}(Y)). Because (P_j) is an allowed proper parameter, every point restriction of (\beta_j) has the same image (a_j\in W). Thus RS.9 becomes

\[
\sum_{b\in B_j}\bigl(a_{j(b)}-a_j\bigr)=0
                         \qquad(j\in V).
\tag{RS.10}
\]

These are the graph-Laplacian equations for the finite connected dual graph, with the edge multiplicities retained. Its rational kernel consists precisely of constant vectors. For example, over (\mathbf R) its quadratic form is the sum over edges of squared coordinate differences; connectedness gives kernel dimension one. The matrix has rational coefficients, so its rational rank is the same, and its kernel over (\mathbf Q) is the span of the all-ones vector. Tensoring that exact finite-dimensional rational calculation with the rational vector space (W) proves that all the (a_j) coincide. A single-vertex graph gives the same statement directly. This uses no ordering of the graph and no absence of cycles.

## RS.6. Compute the two endpoint residues

Naturality of localization under either section, together with Tor-independent regular base change at its smooth special-fibre point, gives

\[
\partial_Y(o_F^*\eta)=\beta_{j_o,o(0)},
\qquad
\partial_Y(t_F^*\eta)=\beta_{j_t,t(0)}.
\tag{RS.11}
\]

The section meets only the indicated component transversely. The pullback of every other component-supported term is zero. Consequently the two classes in RS.11 have images (a_{j_o}) and (a_{j_t}) in (W), and these agree by RS.10. This proves RS.2. Compatibility of localization with products gives (\partial_Y(\{u\}\cdot z_F)=\operatorname{ord}_\pi(u)z=e z), proving RS.3's consequence.

The same argument applies to an actual regular algebraizable target for which the displayed products and pushforwards are defined and regular, by descent of the finite geometric and perfect-complex data to one smooth finite-type model and passage to its filtered limit. This does not turn a product over a henselization after scalar extension into a constant regular product.

## RS.7. Why this does not settle the current SNC gate

The fixed-local-base gamma estimate and WP's whole proper-family theorem concern (R\) and a fixed proper parameter over its coefficient field. A symbolic unit requires a transcendental field extension. In general (R\otimes_k k(u)) is not local or henselian. Descending a lift from its henselization to one finite étale stage does not provide a constant regular model with controlled special fibre at (u=0). For example, a chosen branch of (y^2-y-x/u=0) near (x=0) has a model whose naive special-fibre equation forces (x=0). Changing integral coordinates or taking a finite closure introduces additional branches whose contribution has not been controlled.

Furthermore, HK prescribes the component images of its exceptional (K_1) endpoint difference. It does not supply the exact constant-target equality RS.3. Both issues precede the regular-target argument proved here. No universal specialization statement for singular (E) follows: its node-supported negative groups can be nonzero, and the prior relative marked-curve countermodel remains valid.

The proof is an independent reconstruction from the supplied semistable geometry and the elementary rational graph calculation. The needed perfect (K)-theory inputs are Thomason and Trobaugh (1990), §§3.16–3.18 (proper perfect pushforward, projection and base change), §7 (localization), and §8.1.4 (support-union Mayer–Vietoris), together with regular dévissage and negative-(K) vanishing.
