# Residual-prime research: exact lower-dimensional subvariety gate

## Status and deliverables

`C4_residual_gate.tex` proves C.4q. It does not resolve the remaining all-case surface or threefold direct-summand question. The all-characteristic upper bounds remain

\[
s_J(A)\mid\gcd(2,\delta(A))\quad(g=2),\qquad
s_J(A)\mid\gcd(6,\delta(A))\quad(g=3).
\]

The new result identifies the entire lattice generated by a natural further source of curves, beyond divisor products. For a simple complex A with End(A)=Z and primitive rank-one NS type (1,d2,...,e), curves supported in translates of proper abelian subvarieties of X=A×Â have Fourier images exactly

\[
\mathbb ZD\oplus\kappa(e)\mathbb ZP\oplus\mathbb Z\widehat D,
\qquad \kappa(e)=\prod_{\ell\mid e}\ell^{\lceil v_\ell(e)/2\rceil}.
\]

Thus that construction cannot produce the primitive Poincare curve class whenever e>1. For type (1,3) surfaces, for instance, this subgroup retains a factor three even though the generalized-Prym argument already proves actual splitting. This is a concrete distinction between a limitation of this subgroup and a true obstruction to all algebraic curves.

The proof is valid for both g=2 and g=3 over C. We do not claim the identical integral Fourier/divisor lattice assertion in positive characteristic, especially at the characteristic prime. This restriction does not weaken the earlier all-characteristic upper bounds.

## Nearby theorems and their exact limitations

Grabowski's Corollary3.1.9 proves IHC for curves on every complex abelian threefold, including nonprincipally polarized ones. Proposition3.1.8 explains the mechanism: express integral divisor classes by differences of ample classes, principalize each ample class, and push the Fourier images of principal theta classes from the dual principalized varieties. The output is a curve class on the dual threefold. It is not a theorem about all curve classes on the sixfold A×Â.

Totaro's Theorem7.1 proves the integral Tate theorem for abelian threefolds over the separable closure of a finitely generated field. Its Section1 formulation uses primes invertible in that field; no crystalline-at-p assertion follows merely from that theorem. Proposition7.2 is the Fourier-and-principalization analogue of Grabowski's reduction. It likewise does not address the needed class on A×Â.

The new proof uses the full strength of the dimension-three theorem for the proper abelian subvarieties of X. Even after allowing every integral curve class on all those subvarieties, the off-diagonal coefficient remains divisible by kappa(e). Thus the missing curves would have to include at least one component whose smallest containing translated abelian subvariety is all of X. This conclusion follows from the proved lattice equality and does not claim that such generating curves cannot work.

## Other independent approaches considered

1. **Same-dimensional principalization.** The earlier delta criterion is exact at each prime for this route; if a prime divides every Euler characteristic, changing the ample polarization does not give a prime-to-that-prime principalization. No additional improvement emerged.
2. **Curves on proper subvarieties or graph subvarieties.** This now has the exact C.4q answer above, stronger than a check of individual graph examples. It does not settle generating curves on X.
3. **Very-general ppav parity or sixfold sharpness.** The very-general results do not apply to the special isotypic family A×Â. The earlier rank-of-monodromy calculation also explains why the published rank-one matroid-family hypothesis cannot be applied directly to that family.
4. **Prym or Prym–Tyurin constructions of another exponent.** The exponent-two result in dimensions four/five and exponent-six result in dimension six give the proved upper bounds. We found no primary theorem furnishing a coprime exponent uniformly on the required special products. No claim that none exists is made.
5. **K-theory/Chern-class route.** The fact that the Poincare line bundle is algebraic does not make its divided Chern-character components integral algebraic cycles. The integral Fourier problem is precisely sensitive to these denominators. No additional divisibility removal was proved by this route.

No novelty, priority, or literature-wide open-status claim is made. The residual cases remain unproved in this contribution, with the exact theorem and method gates recorded rather than replaced by an assertion of completion.
