## 1. Results to lead with

1. Koiran requires effective prime-ideal estimates for the **Dedekind zeta function of a number field generated by the coordinates of a suitably bounded algebraic solution**. These fields need not be Galois, let alone abelian. For unrestricted inputs the family is the family of arbitrary number fields. The method does not require GRH for individual nonabelian Artin L-functions, Artin holomorphy, or passage to the Galois closure. Merely knowing Dirichlet GRH does not discharge this analytic hypothesis by the known factorization argument.
2. Masullo's claimed Hecke GRH for every number field supplies precisely the missing input: take the trivial Hecke character of each such field. Consequently the Koiran conclusion is conditional on Masullo's claim, with no additional GRH assumption. It is not made unconditional by the supplied Alpöge Dirichlet statement alone.
3. GCT V **Theorem 3.4** concerns homogeneous equation-given varieties with rational circuit data. Its constructive bound means a functional version of PH. The proof yields the more precise bound **FP^{Σ₂^P}**, conditional on the same Dedekind hypothesis, hence conditional on Masullo's claim.
4. Distinguish three different GCT statements. **Theorems 4.1 and 5.5**, for an optimal-cardinality h.s.o.p. of a succinct explicit variety, require an additional singly exponential-time construction of its defining equations for their EXPH conclusion. **Theorems 4.10 and 5.12**, for succinct small systems of parameters (s.s.o.p.'s), have no such additional equation hypothesis. Their analytic dependence is again through Koiran, hence through Dedekind zeta functions.
5. The historical EXPH bound for **s.s.o.p. construction** is already superseded by stronger, genuinely unconditional results: Forbes–Shpilka (STOC 2018), Theorems 1.1 and 7.1, and Guo–Saxena–Sinhababu (Theory of Computing 2019), Theorems 1.5 and 1.6, give PSPACE constructions. This advance does not come from either new RH premise. It does not put equation-given h.s.o.p. construction in P or PH without the analytic premise, nor does it assert that an optimal-cardinality h.s.o.p. has been constructed.

**Representation scope.** Theorem 3.4 is stated broadly but its standard specification immediately preceding the theorem is a homogeneous affine cone `Z ⊂ K^t` defined by homogeneous integer polynomials represented by rational-constant arithmetic circuits. N is their total encoding length. The PH assertion is in characteristic zero; the unconditional PSPACE assertion also applies in arbitrary characteristic under an appropriate effective field encoding. Do not turn this into a Turing complexity assertion for arbitrarily specified transcendental coefficients.

Definition 5.1 precisely has `W_n=closure(ψ_n(K^{r_n}))⊂K^{m_n}`, `r_n=poly(n)`, `m_n=n^{Ω(1)}`, and `log m_n=O(poly(n))`; in particular `dim W_n≤r_n` although the ambient dimension may be exponential. ## 3. Checked findings with complete proofs

Write H_D for RH for ζ_E for every number field E. Write H_M for the all-number-field Hecke-GRH claim the parent has extracted from Masullo; only its trivial-character case is used here. Write H_A for the supplied Alpöge Dirichlet-GRH theorem, treated as stipulated in this task.

### CX-K01 — The root-field gate, without a Galois closure

**Statement.** In Koiran's proof, H_D can be localized inputwise to RH for ζ_E, where E is the field of a bounded algebraic solution chosen by K96 Theorems 4 and 7. Apart from using ζ_Q if one retains Koiran's particular comparison with π_Q instead of Li, no larger number field is necessary. The uniform family of possible E is unrestricted: it is not confined to abelian extensions of Q. No hypothesis on Artin holomorphy is required.

**Proof.** Let S be a satisfiable integer polynomial system. Invoke K96 Theorems 7 and 4 to choose a solution a=(a_1,...,a_t), a primitive element θ for E=Q(a_1,...,a_t), a minimal polynomial R for θ, and integer denominators in representations of the a_i. These cited theorems give singly exponential bounds, in the input length N, on `[E:Q]`, `log |disc(R)|`, and the logarithms of the necessary denominators. Replace θ by an integral multiple β that is an algebraic integer. If R has leading coefficient c and degree d, `c^{d-1}R(T/c)` is a monic integral polynomial for cθ; this replacement only changes the stated logarithmic bounds by a polynomial in the previous bounds. Thus `Z[β] ⊂ O_E` has finite index, and the solution coordinates lie in `O_E[1/B]` for a nonzero integer B with singly exponentially bounded logarithm.

Let `π_E(x)` count prime ideals of `O_E` of norm at most x. LO77 Theorem 1.1 applied to **E/E** gives, assuming RH for ζ_E,

`π_E(x) = Li(x) + O(sqrt(x) log(|D_E| x^d) + log |D_E|)`,

with an effective absolute implied constant. Prime ideals of residue degree at least two have underlying rational prime at most sqrt(x); there are at most d prime ideals above each such prime. Their number is therefore at most `d π_Q(sqrt(x)) ≤ d sqrt(x)`. Discard also all prime ideals above primes dividing `B [O_E:Z[β]]`; their number is at most `d ω(B [O_E:Z[β]])`, bounded by the same singly exponential scale. At every remaining degree-one prime ideal `𝔭`, the residue field is F_p. Reducing the coordinates a_i modulo 𝔭 gives a solution of S over F_p. Since at most d distinct prime ideals lie above a rational prime, the count of rational primes p≤x for which S is soluble is at least

`(Li(x) - O(sqrt(x) log(|D_E| x^d) + d sqrt(x) + log |D_E|) - d ω(B [O_E:Z[β]]))/d`.

All terms outside x and log x have singly exponential magnitude in N. Taking `x=2^{P(N)}` for a sufficiently large fixed polynomial P makes this lower bound dominate the unsatisfiable-instance bound in K96 Theorem 5 by the factor needed in K96 Theorem 1. Thus the same AM protocol of K96 Theorem 2 applies. This verifies exactly where the zeta hypothesis enters. It is a statement about the single ζ_E in the prime-ideal theorem; the extension E/E is Galois trivially, whatever the extension E/Q is. Passing to the splitting field of R is unnecessary, and may introduce a factorial growth of degree that this proof avoids.

Finally, for any number field E, choose a primitive element θ and its irreducible polynomial R over Q, clearing denominators. Every root of R generates a conjugate of E. The one-equation system R(x)=0 therefore exhibits fields of precisely this isomorphism type among the possible residue fields. Hence the unrestricted input family cannot be characterized by assuming these fields are abelian. This is a statement about the proof's possible fields, not a proof that the complexity conclusion logically requires full H_D. ∎

**Checks and limits.** For the prime-ideal estimate we use a cited theorem, not a purported reproof of the analytic number theory. The exclusion of bad primes and the residue-degree count above were checked directly. `D_E` denotes the absolute field discriminant; it divides the discriminant of an integral defining polynomial up to the square index relation. With Li in place of π_Q there is no separate RH-for-Q assumption. A proof of HN∈AM from Dirichlet GRH alone is not ruled out; it is simply not provided by this argument.

### CX-K02 — Conditional HN, including circuits and projective feasibility

**Statement.** H_M implies HN∈AM⊆Π₂^P for polynomial systems given by arithmetic circuits with rational constants in the Turing model. The same holds for existence of a nonzero solution of a homogeneous such system. Consequently their complements lie in Σ₂^P. The only remaining premise in these assertions is H_M; H_A alone does not discharge the root-field hypothesis of CX-K01.

**Proof.** For every number field E, the L-function of its trivial Hecke character is ζ_E. Thus H_M implies H_D. Apply K96, DIMACS §2 Theorem 2, with the corrected Theorem 8 estimate identified by the erratum, or equivalently CX-K01 and K96's unchanged hashing protocol. This gives HN∈AM for the sparse input convention of K96.

To extend to circuits, introduce a variable y_g for every circuit gate g. For a sum gate impose `y_g-y_h-y_j=0`, for a product gate impose `y_g-y_h y_j=0`, and for a rational constant a/b impose `b y_g-a=0`, with b nonzero. Wire input gates to the original variables and set the output gate variables to zero. This produces only polynomially many sparse equations of degree at most two with polynomial total coefficient bit length. Every solution of the original system extends uniquely to the gate values; conversely the gate equations inductively force correct evaluation. Hence this is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction to K96's HN problem. AM is preserved under such reductions.

For homogeneous `f_i(x)=0`, require a nonzero x by adjoining variables u_1,...,u_t and the equation `sum_i u_i x_i=1`. A nonzero x admits such a u by setting one u_i to x_i^{-1}; a pair satisfying the equation cannot have x=0. Again this is a polynomial-size circuit system. K96 gives AM and the standard containment used explicitly there is AM⊆Π₂^P. Complementation gives the stated Σ₂^P bound. No assertion that AM lies in Σ₂^P is being made. ∎

**Nature of the result.** This is a hypothesis-transfer and encoding refinement of cited results, not a new unconditional proof of Koiran's original theorem independent of H_M.

### CX-K03 — A precise restricted consequence of H_A

**Statement (promise version).** Fix an explicit polynomial bound P. Consider the promise problem whose no-instances are all inconsistent rational circuit systems, and whose yes-instances possess a solution as in CX-K01 with an **abelian Galois field E/Q** and with `[E:Q]`, `log|disc(R)|`, and the denominator logarithms at most `2^{P(N)}`. Under the stipulated H_A, this promise problem is in AM. No abelian-field certificate is required in the protocol.

**Proof.** For a finite abelian extension E/Q, the Dedekind zeta function factors as the product of primitive Dirichlet L-functions attached to the characters of Gal(E/Q), with the conductor convention that supplies the correct ramified Euler factors. H_A places their nontrivial zeros on the critical line; the trivial factor is ζ_Q and is covered by H_A as well. Hence RH holds for ζ_E. For every promised yes-instance the bounds in CX-K01 therefore hold with a uniform polynomial exponent. On every no-instance K96 Theorem 5 is unconditional. The gap and the public-coin protocol are identical to those in K96 Theorem 2. The verifier never computes E or tests abelianness: the promise is used solely to establish completeness of the analytic bound. Soundness on no-instances is unchanged. ∎

This does not establish that merely having some abelian-valued solution with no quantitative bound suffices. The quantitative promise is explicit because Koiran's bounds for a chosen solution do not imply such bounds for every special arithmetic class of solutions.

### CX-K04 — Counterexample to the abelian-solution shortcut

**Statement.** The satisfiable system `x^3-2=0` has no solution in any finite abelian extension of Q. Thus replacing Koiran's arbitrary solution field by an abelian solution field is not a generally available reduction.

**Proof.** The polynomial is irreducible by Eisenstein at 2. The field generated by its real root has degree three, lies in R, and omits its two nonreal conjugates; it is not normal over Q. The root fields of the other two roots are conjugate to this field, and so are likewise nonnormal. Every intermediate field of a finite abelian Galois extension of Q is Galois, because every subgroup of its abelian Galois group is normal. No root field just exhibited can therefore lie in a finite abelian extension. A root lying in an infinite abelian extension would lie in a finite subextension, so allowing infinite abelian extensions would not help either. ∎

This counterexample refutes only the arithmetic shortcut, not the existence of an AM algorithm for HN under some other argument.

### CX-K05 — Functional PH made precise for equation-given h.s.o.p.'s

**Statement.** Under H_M, let `R=Q[z_1,...,z_t]/I` be a nonzero standard positively graded algebra, with I specified by homogeneous arithmetic-circuit equations with rational constants and total encoding length N. The input explicitly lists the ambient coordinates, so `t≤N`; it does not encode exponentially many unused free variables by a binary dimension alone. An h.s.o.p. consisting of rational linear forms, and `dim R`, can be constructed in **FP^{Σ₂^P}**, with output of polynomial bit length in N. The same forms work after extending scalars to any algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. The unconditional bound from GCT5 Theorem 3.4 is functional PSPACE.

**Proof.** The bounded-coefficient part of GCT5 Theorem 3.4's proof provides a computable polynomial b(N) with the following property: if `d=dim R`, there exist d integral linear forms, each coefficient having at most b(N) bits, whose common zero on the affine cone is only the origin. The proof obtains this by the degree bound `deg f_i ≤ 2^N`, passing to a common homogeneous degree at most `2^{N^2}`, and a nonzero resultant whose total degree has polynomial logarithm. No enumeration of all monomials is needed to obtain the bit bound.

For a candidate list L=(L_1,...,L_s), let Good(I,L) mean that the homogeneous system consisting of the generators of I and the L_j has no nonzero geometric solution. By CX-K02, Good belongs to Σ₂^P. Consequently the following predicate also belongs to Σ₂^P: there exists a list L of the stated coefficient bit bound, extending a specified prefix of its binary encoding, such that Good(I,L). Indeed the extra existential guess of L can be merged into the first existential block of a Σ₂^P predicate.

For s=0,1,...,t, ask whether such a list exists. The smallest successful s is d. Existence at d follows from the cited bounded-coefficient argument. For s<d, the generalized principal ideal theorem gives `dim R/(L_1,...,L_s) ≥ d-s > 0`; equivalently a positive-dimensional cone cannot be supported only at the origin. Such a list is impossible. This also covers d=0: the empty list is a valid h.s.o.p.

With s=d fixed, recover a successful list one bit at a time. Try each next bit equal to zero and ask whether any successful completion exists. Keep zero if the oracle says yes; otherwise keep one. The invariant that the prefix has a successful completion holds initially and after every query, so the final list is valid. There are only polynomially many coefficient bits and only polynomially many dimension queries. This is an FP^{Σ₂^P} algorithm.

For completeness, the validity condition is exactly the ring-theoretic normalization condition. Good implies that `R/(L)` is finite-dimensional over Q: after extension to an algebraic closure its spectrum is supported at the origin, and the homogeneous irrelevant ideal is nilpotent in the quotient. Choose finitely many homogeneous representatives of a vector-space basis of this quotient. Induction on degree shows these representatives generate R as a module over `A=Q[L_1,...,L_d]`: after subtracting a representative of the same degree, an element lies in the ideal (L), whose coefficients have strictly lower degree. Thus R is a finite A-module, hence integral over A. Conversely, if R is finite over A, tensoring with `A/(L)` makes `R/(L)` finite-dimensional over Q, so its homogeneous cone has no nonzero point. Since `dim A=dim R=d`, the d generators L_i are algebraically independent: a nonzero kernel of `Q[T_1,...,T_d]→A` would lower dimension below d. Therefore L is an h.s.o.p. This argument allows nilpotents and multiple irreducible components.

All output coefficients are rational. Tensoring the finite-module presentation with any characteristic-zero field preserves finiteness and the algebraic independence of the parameters. Finally, replacing the Σ₂^P oracle by the unconditional PSPACE homogeneous-feasibility procedure in GCT5 Theorem 2.10, and enumerating bounded lists in polynomial space, gives the stated unconditional functional PSPACE bound. ∎

**Scope check.** A zero algebra (inconsistent constant homogeneous equations) is a separate trivial case; the statement excludes it. Neither FP^{Σ₂^P} nor functional PH means an ordinary polynomial-time algorithm. The restriction to standard grading matters for linear homogeneous forms.

### CX-K06 — Exact EXPH transfers for succinct varieties

**Statement.** Under H_M:

(a) For an explicit family W_n in GCT5 Definition 5.1, a strict s.s.o.p. can be constructed and verified in the functional exponential hierarchy, as in GCT5 Theorem 5.12. The determinant version is Theorem 4.10.

(b) An optimal-cardinality h.s.o.p. is covered by the EXPH conclusion of GCT5 Theorem 5.5 only when defining equations for W_n are computable in time `2^{poly(n)}`; the determinant version is Theorem 4.1 with the analogous extra hypothesis.

**Proof.** H_M implies H_D as in CX-K02. Every invocation of Koiran in GCT5 Theorem 2.8 is an invocation on an integer/rational circuit system of finite, possibly exponentially large, encoding length. Thus its GRH hypothesis is discharged by the same H_D. The construction of these systems is deterministic exponential time; their good-candidate predicates have constant-alternation polynomial bounds in that expanded length, hence constant-alternation exponential bounds in n. Searching the polynomial-length description of a candidate hitting set, and applying the polynomial-time transformation in GCT5 Theorem 5.11(a), stays in the functional exponential hierarchy. This proves (a), preserving the exact scope of the cited theorems.

For (b), use the conditional theorem exactly as stated in GCT5 Theorem 5.5, whose proof refers to Theorem 4.1 and hence Theorem 3.4. The arithmetic hypothesis in that chain is again the one discharged in CX-K02. The encoding reason for the hierarchy change is explicit: the promised equations take `2^{poly(n)}` time to compute, so their total length is at most that exponential bound; polynomial time and polynomial quantifier lengths in this expanded length become exponential time and exponential quantifier lengths in n, with a constant number of alternations. In the standard-graded case this substitution is directly the algorithm of CX-K05. The varying-weight case is cited with the representation conventions of GCT5 Theorem 5.5, not asserted to follow by taking arbitrary linear combinations across different weights. If equation access is absent, this proof has no first step of the required time bound: GCT5's unconditional elimination method may output equations of double-exponential length. The source theorem explicitly retains that extra assumption, so it cannot be dropped merely because H_D has been supplied. ∎

**Important nuance.** GCT5 Definition 5.1 permits varying positive weights on the coordinate generators. Part (b) invokes GCT5's stated graded theorem, not the unweighted linear-form refinement of CX-K05 in cases where the weights differ. Passing to a common-degree construction preserves homogeneity; arbitrary linear combinations of generators of different weights do not.

### CX-K07 — Existing unconditional PSPACE progress is not a new RH consequence

**Statement.** Polynomial-size succinct separating normalization systems for explicit varieties in GCT5 Definition 5.1 already admit unconditional polynomial-space construction, independently of H_A and H_M. Therefore the assertion that their EXPH construction becomes unconditional for the first time through the new RH premises is refuted by the earlier literature. Optimal-cardinality h.s.o.p.'s and arbitrary candidate verification are distinct assertions.

**Proof of the comparison.** Apply the unconditional border-circuit hitting-set theorem FS18 Theorem 7.1 with the polynomially bounded parameters of the explicit family. Feed its output into GCT5 Theorem 5.11(a)'s reduction. A polynomial-space procedure followed by a polynomial-time output transformation is still polynomial-space. This gives the stated characteristic-zero construction. GSS19 Theorems 1.5 and 1.6 explicitly record the determinant and general-explicit-variety conclusions, and supply the corresponding finite-field treatment. These statements predate the supplied 2026 premises and assume no GRH. The output remains a small succinct system, with the polynomial slack in cardinality allowed by the definition. It is not a conclusion that the number of parameters equals the dimension. ∎

This is a literature-scope correction and a direct composition of known algorithms, not a claim of discovery of the PSPACE algorithm.

### CX-K08 — Exact scheme dictionary for CX-K01 and CX-K05

**Statement.** The Koiran reduction is a statement about the arithmetic fibers of an affine finite-type Z-scheme and finite étale Artin sheaves on an open subscheme of Spec Z. The h.s.o.p. construction is a search for a finite morphism to affine space, with its projective form given by a basepoint-free linear projection. Both dictionaries preserve the proofs above.

**Proof/dictionary.** Given equations f_i, set `X=Spec(Z[x]/(f_i))`. A complex solution exists exactly when `X_Q` is nonempty: a complex point implies the rational ideal is proper, and a maximal ideal of the resulting finitely generated Q-algebra has a finite extension E/Q as residue field. Such a closed point gives `Spec E→X_Q`. After clearing finitely many denominators it extends to `Spec O_E[1/B]→X_{Z[1/B]}`. Every degree-one closed point above a rational prime p gives an F_p-point of X. These are exactly the specialization maps counted in CX-K01; bad fibers are the excluded denominator/index primes.

Enlarge B to include the index `[O_E:Z[β]]` of the chosen integral primitive element. For a fixed auxiliary prime ℓ, on `U=Spec Z[1/(ℓ B D_E)]`, the normalization in E is finite étale. Its direct image of the constant ℓ-adic sheaf is a rank-d permutation local system. For p in U, the trace of Frobenius equals the number of residue-degree-one primes above p, hence the number of roots of the chosen integral primitive-element polynomial. Its Euler product is ζ_E with the finitely many omitted factors. Its Frobenius eigenvalues are roots of unity, so this is a weight-zero Artin object; this elementary purity statement does not itself assert the location of zeros of its global Euler product. For abelian E/Q the representation decomposes into rank-one characters, recovering the Dirichlet factorization used in CX-K03. For arbitrary E the relevant representation is `Ind_{G_E}^{G_Q} 1`, not necessarily a sum of one-dimensional representations. In a representation-ring description this is the class of the permutation object in K_0; no λ-operation manufactures the missing global zero theorem.

For a graded algebra R as in CX-K05, the condition that all L_i vanish only at the vertex is equivalent to the finiteness of `Spec R→A_Q^d` by the finite-module proof already given. If d≥1, on Proj R the same forms have no common zero and define the corresponding finite linear projection to `P_Q^{d-1}`; if d=0, Proj R is empty and there is no projective target to assert. Candidate matrices form an affine parameter scheme T. The incidence where a point of Proj R lies in a candidate kernel is closed in `Proj R×T`; its projection to T is closed by properness of Proj R. Thus the successful matrices form an open subscheme. The bounded-coefficient existence and the oracle search are effective statements about rational points of this open subscheme.

Finally the result spreads out. Finiteness over Q supplies finitely many monic integral-dependence relations for the original generators z_i with coefficients in Q[L]. Clear all denominators in these relations and in their finite expressions modulo the defining ideal; after inverting one nonzero integer B they hold over `Z[1/B]`. Consequently the map is finite over this base. This does not claim flatness at every remaining prime or a simultaneous normalization in every characteristic. No Hilbert- or Quot-scheme representability statement is strengthened by the complexity bounds. ∎
