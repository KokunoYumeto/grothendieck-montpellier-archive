**Verdict:** the inspected results do not force zero or nonzero for the project's ordinary-normalized repeated triple. This is a primary theorem-scope check, not a whole-paper proof audit.

## Precisely inspected source scope

- Section4, pp.17–18: products with consecutive copies of the first Kummer class followed by a final class; roots-of-unity coefficients and explicit defining systems.
- Definitions pp.21–22 and Theorem4.3 p.22: proper norm-based systems, a transgression formula, and lower-product indeterminacy.
- Section5 setup p.24 and Theorem5.4 p.26, with its complete proof pp.26–27: restricted ramification and an S-ideal norm formula under those proper-system hypotheses.
- Section6 setup pp.28–31, Theorem6.3 p.31 with its complete proof, and Proposition6.6 p.33 with its complete proof: compatible Kummer towers, graded quotients and norm sequences.
- Section7 setup p.34 and Propositions7.3–7.5 statements pp.35–38: special cyclotomic generators and additional class-module or tower hypotheses. These are not a uniform assertion for arbitrary supplied cocycles.

## Application analysis

The project fixes u in character eta·psi and a strict v in character psi^-1, together with the quadratic primitive w normalized by the actual ordinary local condition. Its cubic datum also includes chosen local primitives; this retains information lost by taking an unrestricted Massey-product value modulo its indeterminacy.

Restricting to E(mu_p) would trivialize the residual coefficient characters and permit Kummer descriptions. That restriction alone does not identify the linked defining cochains with a proper norm-based system, nor prove that reordering the endpoint-repeat triple to a consecutive-repeat product preserves the ordinary normalization. A shuffle comparison, if established, would still have to track the changed primitive and every local correction. The bound for a triple at m=n=1 is not the obstacle when p>3.

Even after those identifications, an ideal-class formula is an expression requiring arithmetic evaluation; it supplies neither automatic vanishing nor nonvanishing. Passing to a cyclotomic tower additionally requires compatible Kummer and norm sequences, which have not been produced for the arbitrary strict class under consideration. Enlarging the ramification set or passing to the absolute Galois group likewise does not resolve the prescribed relative obstruction.

Accordingly no Sharifi vanishing or nonvanishing theorem is imported into the current ordinary cubic criterion. The finite norm-ideal formula is a possible future evaluation route if its defining-system and local compatibility data are actually constructed. No claim is made that the entire forty-page paper or its foundational citations were independently proved.
