\subsection{The complete finite three-tuple consequence of first cabling}

\begin{statement}{D23.F43}{Proved}{The cabled three-tuple test retains the final normalized case}
\end{statement}
The $1214$ triples in F42 generate a raw $B_3$-invariant set
of size $3642$. For each of C34's nine phase-zero candidates,
the prescribed action on the $1214$ triples has a unique
extension satisfying full $B_3$ covariance with the same
first-pair associator value forced by C28. All nine
extensions exist, and their first-pair values satisfy the
two-cycle and both multiplier-one and multiplier-minus-one
three-cycle equations.

For the final normalized candidate $(1,0)$, the complete
last-meridian test on these $3642$ triples is compatible
with the sixteen affine values forced by the colored
assembly zero. Moreover, it admits one common ordinary
commutator word together with the full H9 braid targets
and the selected thirty-eight-kernel colored extension
of F41. This test does not exclude that final candidate
and does not produce an actual GT lift.

\begin{proof}
Let $A,B$ denote the positive Hurwitz generators on triples.
Write $x=A^2$, $y=B^2$, $z=(xy)^{-1}$, and
$Z=(AB)^3$. Let $\Omega_0$ be the $1214$ merged triples
of F42. It is invariant under $B$, $x$, $y$, and $Z$.
The three disjoint sets
\[
                   \Omega_0,\qquad A\Omega_0,
                     \qquad BA\Omega_0
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.1}
\]
form its entire $B_3$ closure. The exact tuple constructor
checks that their union has $3642$ distinct elements,
is preserved by $A,B$, and satisfies $ABA=BAB$.
One can also distinguish the sectors by the position of
the merged entry: its class in $E_0^{\rm ab}$ differs
from that of each of the other two entries.

Use $a,b,c$ for the original raw four-tuple braid generators.
The old mixed pair is $(X,Y)=(c^2,(aba)^2)$ and
$Z_0=(XY)^{-1}$. On $\Omega_0$, the merge map sends $Y$
to $x$ and $X$ to $y$. The former identity also follows
from the literal cabling $A^2\mapsto Y a^{-2}$: the
internal twist $a^2$ acts trivially on the product of
the first two tuple entries. These equalities are checked
on all $8064$ raw tuples.

Put $R=f(X,Y)$ and $T=f(X,Z_0)$ in the raw four-tuple
representation. C28 identifies $T$ uniquely from $R$ by
the marked reflection $\theta:(X,Y)\mapsto(X,Z_0)$.
The isomorphism between the raw and base mixed images
of P24 makes its lift unique as well. Thus an exact
straight-line evaluation of $R$ with $Y$ replaced by
$Z_0$ gives the actual required $T$, rather than an
arbitrary additional word value. C28's three-cycle gives
$f(Y,Z_0)=TR^{-1}$.

Let a bar denote descent through the merge map. The
first-pair values on $\Omega_0$ are consequently
\[
 \begin{split}
 a_0=f(x,y)&=\overline{R}^{\,-1},\\
 t_0=f(x,z)&=x\,\overline{TR^{-1}}\,x^{-1},\\
 b_0=f(y,z)&=t_0a_0^{-1}.
 \end{split}
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.2}
\]
For the middle identity, $Z_0$ descends to
$(yx)^{-1}=x^{-1}zx$; therefore
$\overline{f(Y,Z_0)}=x^{-1}f(x,z)x$.
The last identity is the multiplier-one three-cycle,
using $f(z,x)=t_0^{-1}$. This proves every orientation
and conjugation in (D23.F43.2).

There is no further freedom in $f(x,y)$ on the other
two sectors. The literal braid identities are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 q&q^{-1}xq&q^{-1}yq\\ \hline
 1&x&y\\
 A&x&x^{-1}zxZ\\
 BA&y&yzy^{-1}Z.
 \end{array}
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.3}
\]
The central factors $Z$ disappear in a commutator
evaluation. Therefore the restrictions of the full value
$F=f(x,y)$, transported back to $\Omega_0$ through the
three maps in (D23.F43.1), are respectively
\[
                   a_0,\qquad x^{-1}t_0x,
                            \qquad yb_0y^{-1}.
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.4}
\]
The certificate checks the exact central powers in
(D23.F43.3) and that these restrictions form a permutation.
It then checks the full two-cycle, positive three-cycle,
and negative three-cycle on all $3642$ elements, without
discarding the raw central twist.

An actual extension $V$ must satisfy
\[
               VA=AV,\qquad VB=(F^{-1}BF)V.
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.5}
\]
Its value on $\Omega_0$ is prescribed by F42. Every point
in the full set is reached from $\Omega_0$ by $A,B$;
hence (D23.F43.5) determines $V$ uniquely if it exists.
Breadth-first propagation checks consistency on every
generator edge and checks bijectivity. For each of the
nine candidates it succeeds. Thus this is a complete
covariance-extension test, rather than the selection of
one unverified extension.

For $(1,0)$, apply F40 to this prescribed full three-tuple
action. Its $108$ possible last entries give twenty-four
marked kernels. Exactly five of them were absent from
the colored thirty-eight-kernel table:
\[
                             40,52,57,67,76.
\]
For each kernel, the certificate exhausts the derived
subgroup of its literal marked pair image and tests the
required conjugation simultaneously at every last entry
with that kernel. It uses $V^{-1}$, as required by F40.
The sixteen colored values used as anchors are the unique
values after P32's full gentle-orbit constraints; the
unfiltered individual conjugator fibers are not all
singletons. This distinction is retained in the replay.

Closing the new kernels under the six gentle orientations
adds precisely the three orbits
\[
 \{40,88,90\},\qquad
 \{52,57,97,100,126,129\},\qquad
 \{67,76,132,136,139,141\}.
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.6}
\]
The complete two-cycle, positive three-cycle, and negative
three-cycle enumeration leaves respectively $2$, $18$,
and $9$ assignments, while retaining the chosen colored
extension. The existing thirteen orbits remain consistent.
This gives $324$ possibilities for the fifteen new kernel
values. Equal marked kernels are transported by their
rooted Cayley isomorphisms throughout, exactly as in C34.

Append those fifteen degree-nine affine representations
to F41's degree-$2290$ joint group. The result has degree
$2425$ and order
\[
79790519192787393605785106814342295305125891722394468352.
                                                        \tag{D23.F43.7}
\]
Its kernel over the previous joint group has order
$531441=3^{12}$. The first of the $324$ specified
extensions already belongs to this exact joint image.
All original generators still have order twelve, and the
old image has abelianization $C_{12}^{\,2}$. As in F41,
projection therefore induces an abelianization isomorphism;
the passing target is in the derived image and is realized
by an ordinary commutator word. The perfect-residual
argument of F41 restores the full first-pair value if
required without altering any of these solvable targets.
This proves all asserted finite compatibility statements.
\end{proof}
