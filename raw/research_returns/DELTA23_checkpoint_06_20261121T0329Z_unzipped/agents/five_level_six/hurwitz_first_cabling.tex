\subsection{A second actual-source map on raw tuples}

\begin{statement}{D23.F42}{Proved}{First-strand cabling gives an exact triple-tuple action}
\end{statement}
For the rightmost free kernels $N_3\subset\widehat B_4$
and $N_4\subset\widehat B_5$, the homomorphism
\[
 \iota:N_3\longrightarrow N_4,\qquad
 (v_1,v_2,v_3)\longmapsto(u_1u_2,u_3,u_4)
\]
is injective and commutes with the standard full-GT actions.
Consequently, for every finite target group $G$, the raw
tuple map
\[
                   \mu(g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4)
                            =(g_1g_2,g_3,g_4)
                                                        \tag{D23.F42.1}
\]
intertwines the actual action defined by the same associator
on four-tuples and three-tuples. The assertion also holds
after simultaneous conjugacy. This is the specified
first-strand cabling operation; it makes no assertion about
an arbitrary deletion of a nontrivial branch generator.

\begin{proof}
In $B_4$, let $A_4$ be the subgroup fixing the first strand,
generated by $a=\sigma_1^2,b=\sigma_2,c=\sigma_3$.
The usual first-strand cabling is
\[
 \delta:A_4\longrightarrow B_5,\qquad
 a\longmapsto\sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2=y_3,
 \quad b\longmapsto\sigma_3,\quad c\longmapsto\sigma_4.
                                                        \tag{D23.F42.2}
\]
Its existence follows geometrically by replacing the fixed
first strand by two parallel strands; equivalently the
images satisfy the annular Artin relations
$abab=baba$, $[a,c]=1$, and $bcb=cbc$.
These identities are checked literally in the certificate.
The map extends continuously to profinite completions.

Here is a direct check that its normalization commutes with
GT. Write $z_j=(\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{j-1})^j$.
Since $y_3=z_2^{-1}z_3$ and the standard action sends each
full twist $z_j$ to $z_j^\lambda$, it sends $y_3$ to
$y_3^\lambda$, exactly $\delta\Phi_h(a)$.
For the other two generators, the literal identities
$\delta(y_i)=y_{i+1}$ for $i=2,3$ and
$\delta(\sigma_i)=\sigma_{i+1}$ identify both sides of
\[
 \Phi_h(\sigma_i)=f(y_i,\sigma_i^2)^{-1}
                         \sigma_i^\lambda f(y_i,\sigma_i^2)
\]
after applying $\delta$. Thus
$\Phi_h\delta=\delta\Phi_h$ on the three generators of
$\widehat A_4$, hence everywhere on that group. Inverses
give the same compatibility for $\Phi_h^{-1}$.

The rightmost kernel $N_3$ is contained in $A_4$. Its
peripheral generators are
\[
 v_1=\sigma_3\sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_3^{-1},
 \quad v_2=\sigma_3\sigma_2^2\sigma_3^{-1},
 \quad v_3=\sigma_3^2.
\]
Substitution into (D23.F42.2), followed by cancellation in
the product $u_1u_2$, gives
$\delta(v_1)=u_1u_2$, $\delta(v_2)=u_3$, and
$\delta(v_3)=u_4$. Thus $\delta$ restricts to the displayed
$\iota$. It is injective because
$(u_1u_2,u_2,u_3,u_4)$ is a free basis of $N_4$; the three
listed images generate a free factor. The completed map
has the same property by the inverse continuous Nielsen
basis change.

Let $\kappa_h^{(j)}$ be the restriction of $\Phi_h$ to
$N_j$. We have proved
$\kappa_h^{(4)}\iota=\iota\kappa_h^{(3)}$.
For a raw representation $\psi:N_4\to G$, precomposition
by $\iota$ is precisely (D23.F42.1). The actual left GT
action precomposes by $\kappa_h^{-1}$, so this identity
proves the asserted equivariance, including its side and
inverse conventions. Restriction commutes with target
conjugation, giving the quotient assertion as well.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The finite partition test.}
On the fixed-product degree-$8064$ raw orbit of H6,
$\mu$ has $1214$ distinct values. Their fiber sizes and
numbers are respectively
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{fiber size}&2&3&6&12\\ \hline
\text{number of fibers}&6&692&36&480.
\end{array}
\]
Every one of C34's nine phase-zero raw candidates preserves
this partition, and hence induces a permutation of the
$1214$ triples. This is only the partition part of the
constraint: compatibility of the induced permutation with
the actual same-associator three-tuple action remains
additional.
