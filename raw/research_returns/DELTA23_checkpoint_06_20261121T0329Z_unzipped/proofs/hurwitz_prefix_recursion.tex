\subsection{The complete tuple-kernel action from prefix evaluations}

\begin{statement}{D23.F44}{Proved}{An explicit multiplier-one recursion for every peripheral generator}
\end{statement}
Let $h=(1,f)$ be an actual full-GT element. In the
rightmost-strand kernel $N_n=\widehat F_n\subset\widehat B_{n+1}$,
use the positive meridians $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ of P18. Write
\[
 P=u_1\cdots u_{n-1},\qquad D=P u_n,
 \qquad F=f(D^{-1},u_n),\qquad J=f(P,u_n).
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.1}
\]
For $n\geq2$, the exact restricted action $\kappa_h^{(n)}$
satisfies
\[
\begin{split}
 \kappa_h^{(n)}(u_n)&=F^{-1}u_nF,\\
 \kappa_h^{(n)}(u_i)&=F^{-1}J\,
       \kappa_h^{(n-1)}(u_i)\,J^{-1}F
                    \qquad(1\leq i<n).
\end{split}
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.2}
\]
In the second line, $\kappa_h^{(n-1)}$ is evaluated after
identifying its peripheral basis with $u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1}$.
The starting action $\kappa_h^{(1)}$ is the identity.
All evaluations in this formula use the same associator $f$.

Equivalently, for a finite target group $G$ and an input
tuple $(g_1,\ldots,g_n)$, the tuple obtained by precomposition
with $\kappa_h^{(n)}$ is computed by the following finite
recursion. Start with the one-entry output $(g_1)$ and the
original prefix $P_1=g_1$. At stage $k=2,\ldots,n$, put
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_k&=P_{k-1}g_k,\\
 F_k&=f(P_k^{-1},g_k),\qquad
 J_k=f(P_{k-1},g_k),\qquad c_k=F_k^{-1}J_k.
 \end{split}
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.3}
\]
Conjugate every previous output entry by $c_k$, and append
$F_k^{-1}g_kF_k$. The prefixes in (D23.F44.3) are products
of the original input entries. They are not recomputed
from intermediate output tuples.

The same conjugator has the useful alternative expression
\[
                    c_k=f(P_{k-1},P_k^{-1}).
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.10}
\]
Indeed, set $a=P_{k-1}$, $b=g_k$, and $z=(ab)^{-1}$.
The two-cycle and the multiplier-one three-cycle give
$f(z,b)^{-1}f(a,b)=f(b,z)f(a,b)=f(z,a)^{-1}=f(a,z)$.
Thus both factors needed at one recursion stage lie in
the same six-orientation orbit of the prefix pair $(a,b)$.

Since P18 defines the actual left tuple permutation by
$V_h(\psi)=\psi\kappa_h^{-1}$, this algorithm computes
$V_h^{-1}$ on raw tuples. Thus a stored raw permutation
$V_h$ must be compared with the array
\[
                  T\longmapsto T[V_h^{-1}],
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.4}
\]
in the permutation conventions of H6, P27, and F40.

\begin{proof}
Let $b=\sigma_n$. In the included braid group
$\widehat B_n$, let $w_1,\ldots,w_{n-1}$ be its rightmost
peripheral meridians. The positive meridian words give
the literal identities
\[
 u_i=bw_i b^{-1}\quad(i<n),\qquad u_n=b^2.
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.5}
\]
The standard GT braid formulas restrict on the included
$\widehat B_n$ to the action with the same pair $(1,f)$.
This follows generator by generator: their words $y_i$
and $\sigma_i^2$ are unchanged by this inclusion. Hence
\[
 b\Phi_h(w_i)b^{-1}
       =\kappa_h^{(n-1)}(u_i),
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.6}
\]
where the right side means the continuous free-group
substitution $w_j\mapsto u_j$. It is not an assertion of
GT compatibility with deleting an arbitrary puncture.

We compute the conjugation of $F$ by $b$. With
$z_m=(\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{m-1})^m$, the positive peripheral
product formulas are
\[
 D=z_n^{-1}z_{n+1},\qquad
 w_1\cdots w_{n-1}=z_{n-1}^{-1}z_n.
\]
The braid $b$ commutes with $z_{n-1}$ by the distant Artin
relations. Multiplying (D23.F44.5) therefore gives
\[
 P=b(z_{n-1}^{-1}z_n)b^{-1},\qquad
 bD^{-1}b^{-1}=z_{n-1}Pz_{n+1}^{-1}.
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.7}
\]
Both $z_{n-1}$ and $z_{n+1}$ commute with $P$ and $u_n$.
For $z_{n+1}$ this is centrality. For $z_{n-1}$ it follows
from its commutation with $b$, $z_n$, and hence the displayed
expression for $P$; it also commutes with $u_n=b^2$.
Furthermore $b$ commutes with $u_n$. Because $f$ has zero
exponent sum in each input, the central factors in the
first input disappear under evaluation. Thus
\[
\begin{split}
 bF b^{-1}
   &=f(bD^{-1}b^{-1},u_n)\\
   &=f(z_{n-1}Pz_{n+1}^{-1},u_n)
     =f(P,u_n)=J.
\end{split}
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.8}
\]
The cancellation is first a literal ordinary-word identity
and then follows for the completed commutator group by
continuity.

F40, with the primary Harbater--Schneps marking, gives
\[
                       \Phi_h(b)=F^{-1}bF.
\]
Its square gives the last-generator formula in
(D23.F44.2). For $i<n$, substitute (D23.F44.5) and expand
without changing the order of any factor:
\[
\begin{split}
 \Phi_h(u_i)
 &=F^{-1}bF\,\Phi_h(w_i)\,F^{-1}b^{-1}F\\
 &=F^{-1}(bFb^{-1})
       (b\Phi_h(w_i)b^{-1})(bF^{-1}b^{-1})F\\
 &=F^{-1}J\,\kappa_h^{(n-1)}(u_i)\,J^{-1}F.
\end{split}
                                                        \tag{D23.F44.9}
\]
This proves the recursion. At $n=1$, the only meridian is
$\sigma_1^2$ and multiplier one fixes it, so the base case
is exact.

Now evaluate (D23.F44.2) under a raw representation
$u_i\mapsto g_i$. Its lower-rank term is evaluated on the
original first $n-1$ entries, while $F$ and $J$ evaluate
on their original prefix products. Induction therefore
gives exactly (D23.F44.3), including the conjugation side.
Finally the definition of the left action in P18 gives
(D23.F44.4). No simultaneous-conjugacy normalization is
inserted: this is an equality of raw tuples of fixed
product. Product preservation also follows independently
from $\kappa_h(D)=D$ in F40.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Primary input and independent literal check.}
The only GT input beyond the commutator condition is the
standard braid formula of Harbater--Schneps, Section 0.1,
equation (1), with $y_i$ as the first input of $f$, and
its restriction to the included smaller braid group.
F40 proves its replacement by $f(D^{-1},u_n)$.
The peripheral and nested-twist identities
(D23.F44.5)--(D23.F44.7) are ordinary braid identities;
the independent Artin certificate
\path{agents/characters/code/full_kappa_recursion.py},
with its JSON record, checks their literal marking for
$n=2,3,4,5$, all lower meridians, both commuting corrections,
and the conjugation of two sample commutator words. The
general proof above is algebraic and does not infer a
universal identity from those sample words.
The resulting finite tests constrain one common table
$f(a,b)$ in the target group. They must not assign
unrelated values to equal marked kernels or to repeated
prefix pairs.
