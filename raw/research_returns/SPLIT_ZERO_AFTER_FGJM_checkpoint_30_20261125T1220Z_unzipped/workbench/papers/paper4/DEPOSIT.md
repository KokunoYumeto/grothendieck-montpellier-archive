## Abstract

Faul, Goswami, Joubert and Manuell adjoined a new zero to a ring and identified the free commutative inverse semiring; Caviglia, Faul, Manuell and Mesiti describe inverse semirings by groups over an idempotent semiring. We use these results to keep the polynomial and Boolean function of one circuit together. For a graph without a four-cycle, the minimum nonnegative arithmetic complexity among quadratic polynomials with its edge support is exactly its number of edges plus its vertex-cover number minus one. By the classical lower-envelope argument, the same count holds when each edge has a squarefree witness in a Boolean circuit's formal polynomial. We extend the full lower bound to circuits missing at most three such witnesses, and to arbitrarily many missing witnesses when extraction of a nonzero quadratic part deletes at most one addition. The proof combines the equality case of the quadratic count, exchanges of star centers, and a normalization that retains the whole quadratic circuit while absorbing redundant higher terms. For a bipartite graph without a four-cycle, the exact count also holds for circuits of degree at most one in one side of the bipartition, by transposition and a disjunction-count lemma permitting arbitrary conjunctions. The unrestricted Boolean problem remains open here. Finally we separate cancellation from repeated-variable identification, and specify the output condition under which zero-adjoined circuits have monotone complexity.
