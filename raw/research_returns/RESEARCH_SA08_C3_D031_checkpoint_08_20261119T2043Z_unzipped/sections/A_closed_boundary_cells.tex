\subsection{An explicit closed-boundary presentation for the first odd cells}

\begin{proposition}[AT21: Proved; a boundary correction with inverse PD maps]
\label{AT21}
Let $P$ be a strict simplicial PD cochain $S_R$-algebra over
$R=\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$.
Suppose $P_0=S_R$ and its first-simplex algebra is the
displayed free even-disk algebra
\[
 P_1=S_R\otimes
       \operatorname{FreePD}_R(x_j,dx_j\mid j\in J),
 \quad |x_j|=0,\quad |dx_j|=1,\quad
 d_1x_j=0,\quad d_0x_j=r_j\in S_R .
 \tag{AT.132}
\]
All generators have positive weights and $d r_j=0$.
For every homogeneous $a\in P_1^{b,1}$ there are explicit
\[
 h\in N_1(P^{b,0}),\qquad t\in N_2(P^{b,1}),\qquad
 \rho=\partial h\in S_R^b
\]
such that
\[
                              a-\partial t=dh .
 \tag{AT.133}
\]
Consequently the relative free PD attachment of an odd
one-form cell $x$ in normalized simplex degree two with
boundary $a$, together with its differential $y=dx$,
is strictly isomorphic over $P$ to the same attachment
with boundary $dh$. The isomorphism and its inverse
are given explicitly below.

This applies simultaneously to all simplex-two odd
one-form cells in the refined C21 construction, without
changing their weights, their specified target maps
after transport, or any property invariant under the
displayed strict isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The free presentation (AT.132) writes every one-form
as a finite sum
\[
              a=\sum_j f_j\,dx_j,\qquad
              s_j=d_1f_j\in S_R,\qquad
              f_j^+=f_j-s_j,\qquad
              h=\sum_j s_jx_j .
 \tag{AT.134}
\]
Each $f_j$ is a zero-form polynomial in the PD generators
with coefficients in $S_R$. Thus $d_1f_j^+=0$.
The structural coefficient copy is used to regard $s_j$
also as an element of $P_1$.
All $s_j$ are vertically closed, so
$dh=\sum_j s_jdx_j$.
Moreover $d_1h=0$ and
\[
                            d_0h=\sum_j s_jr_j\in S_R^b.
\]
This proves the stated assertions about $h$ and $\rho$.

In the actual second simplex of the old algebra $P$ put
\[
           t=-\sum_j s_0(f_j^+)\,
                         d\bigl(s_1x_j-s_0x_j\bigr).
 \tag{AT.135}
\]
This uses only degeneracies, the existing product, and the
vertical differential of $P$. It introduces no new element
by a lifting assertion.
Its first face is zero: $d_1s_1x_j=d_1s_0x_j=x_j$ kills
the differential factor. Its second face is zero because
\[
                         d_2s_0(f_j^+)=s_0d_1(f_j^+)=0.
\]
Hence $t\in N_2(P^{b,1})$.
For its remaining face use
\[
 d_0s_0f_j^+=f_j^+,\qquad
 d_0s_1x_j=s_0d_0x_j=r_j,\qquad d_0s_0x_j=x_j.
\]
Since $dr_j=0$, equation (AT.135) gives
\[
                  d_0t=\sum_j f_j^+dx_j=a-dh.
\]
This proves (AT.133), with every face checked.
Weights are unchanged in every formula.

We specify the two relative attachments and maps.
Let $E_a$ be the pushout over $P$ adjoining the free
vertical disk $x(D(2))\xrightarrow{d}y(D(2))$ in forms
one and two, with bottom restrictions
$x|_{K(1)}=a$, $y|_{K(1)}=da$.
Let $E_{dh}$ be the attachment with generators $x',y'$
and bottom restrictions $x'|_{K(1)}=dh$,
$y'|_{K(1)}=0$.
The normalized element $t$ and its boundary $a-dh$
define an actual linear simplicial map $D(2)\to P^1$;
its vertical differential defines the corresponding
map to $P^2$.

The assignments
\[
 \begin{array}{rcl}
 E_a&\longrightarrow&E_{dh},\\
 x&\longmapsto&x'+t,\\
 y&\longmapsto&y'+dt
 \end{array}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \begin{array}{rcl}
 E_{dh}&\longrightarrow&E_a,\\
 x'&\longmapsto&x-t,\\
 y'&\longmapsto&y-dt
 \end{array}
 \tag{AT.136}
\]
fix $P$. They respect the bottom restrictions by
(AT.133) and its vertical differential.
They respect the vertical differential since $dx=y$,
$dx'=y'$ and $d^2t=0$.
The free relative PD universal property therefore makes
each assignment an actual strict, weight-preserving
simplicial PD-DGA map. Their composites are the identity
on the old algebra and every new generator.
They are the required inverse maps.

For several such cells, (AT.135) depends only on their
boundaries in the fixed first-simplex algebra and its
old degeneracies. The same formulas therefore define
the two inverse maps on every generator of the combined
pushout. Any specified map to a target is transported
by composition with the indicated inverse; no target
value is postulated anew. Later attaching data can be
transported by these actual isomorphisms as well.
This proves the simultaneous assertion.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[AT21b: Proved; the integral comparison of quadratic repairs]
\label{AT21b}
In the situation of AT21, suppose the source $X$ after
the odd-cell attachment is torsionfree. The explicit
elements $D_t,E_t$ in (AT.138) give inverse strict
PD-DGA maps between the resolved $K(2)$ algebra of the
corrected quadratic twist and the relative $D(2)$ algebra
of the original twist with precisely the edges (AT.140).
They fix $X$ and its first skeleton and pass to the
torsionfree quotients.
The resolved comparisons hold over $\mathbb Z$ and
$\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ and identify every corresponding stage
of the iterated based PD closure.
For the specified local resolution of A-S09-22 over
$\mathbb Z_{(2)}$, they additionally extend to inverse
maps of its displayed degree-three repair algebras
by (AT.144).
The application of the local target-map
construction AT20 retains all of its stated source,
target-map, and weight hypotheses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $x^\flat=x-t$ and $y^\flat=dx^\flat$, with $t$ from
(AT.135), and write $x=x^\flat+t$, $y=y^\flat+dt$.
Each summand of (AT.135) has the form $t_i=F_i\,dG_i$.
Thus
\[
                  t_i\,dt_i=0,\qquad \gamma_2(dt_i)=0.
 \tag{AT.137}
\]
The first identity uses the alternating odd square $(dG_i)^2=0$.
For the second, $(dt_i)^2=(dF_i\,dG_i)^2=0$, and
$2\gamma_2(dt_i)=(dt_i)^2$; torsionfreeness permits cancellation.
Choose any ordering of the finitely many summands and put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_t&=\sum_{i<j}t_i\,dt_j,&
 L_t&=\sum_{i<j}t_it_j,\\
 D_t&=t\,y^\flat+K_t,&
 E_t&=x^\flat t-L_t .
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{AT.138}
\]
These are actual old-algebra elements. Their form degrees are
three, two, three and two, respectively; $D_t,E_t$ have weight
$2b$. Their faces $d_1,d_2$ vanish, since this is true of every
$t_i,x^\flat,y^\flat$.
The PD addition rule and (AT.137) give the exact identities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 dK_t&=\gamma_2(dt),&
 dL_t&=t\,dt-2K_t,\\
 dD_t&=\gamma_2(y)-\gamma_2(y^\flat),&
 dE_t&=2D_t-\bigl(xy-x^\flat y^\flat\bigr).
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{AT.139}
\]
For example $dL_t=\sum_{i<j}(t_jdt_i-t_idt_j)$.
For the last identity expand $xy-x^\flat y^\flat$
as $x^\flat dt+t y^\flat+t dt$ and differentiate
$x^\flat t-L_t$. This proves all four formulas without
a choice of primitives.

Here are the precise two resolved algebras compared by
these formulas. Work over the same old source $X$, containing
both $x$ and the displayed element $x^\flat=x-t$.
Use a free coefficient resolution and its $W$ complex as in
AT16, with $m=2$, and let
$\pi:D(2)\to K(2)$ be the quotient map.
The corrected twist
\[
        z^\flat=\gamma_2(y^\flat),\qquad
        \eta^\flat=x^\flat y^\flat
\]
has zero bottom face because $\partial x^\flat=dh$ and
$\partial y^\flat=0$.
Let $Y^\flat$ be its resolved algebra on $W\otimes K(2)$.

Let $Y^{\rm orig}$ instead use the actual original
$D(2)$ twist $z=\gamma_2(y)$, $\eta=xy$ on $W\otimes D(2)$.
Specify its edge relations to be
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u_0|_{K(1)}&=\varepsilon\,D_t|_{K(1)},&
 v_0|_{K(1)}&=\varepsilon\,E_t|_{K(1)},\\
 u_i|_{K(1)}&=v_i|_{K(1)}=0\quad(i>0),&
 W^0|_{K(1)}&=0 .
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{AT.140}
\]
Here $D_t,E_t$ mean the actual linear $D(2)$ maps given
by their normalized values and zeroth faces.
Equations (AT.139) show that (AT.140) is differential-stable;
on higher coefficient-resolution summands the verification
is $\varepsilon\partial=0$.

There are actual inverse strict PD-DGA maps over $X$
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y^{\rm orig}&\longrightarrow Y^\flat,\\
 u_0(q,m)&\longmapsto
       \widetilde u_0(q,\pi m)+\varepsilon(q)D_t(m),\\
 v_0(q,m)&\longmapsto
       \widetilde v_0(q,\pi m)+\varepsilon(q)E_t(m),
 \end{aligned}                                                     \tag{AT.141}
\]
with every higher resolved generator, including the entire
bottom module $W^0$, sent through $\pi$.
The inverse sends the corrected generators to
$u_0-\varepsilon D_t$, $v_0-\varepsilon E_t$, and the same
higher generators. These expressions have zero bottom
by (AT.140), so they factor through $K(2)$ and define the
inverse on that parameter.
The first two differential checks are exactly (AT.139);
all others are $\varepsilon\partial=0$.
The free relative PD universal property proves the maps,
and their composites fix every generator.
They preserve torsion and therefore induce inverse maps
on the torsionfree quotients.
In particular both algebras keep the original first skeleton.

This specifies the boundary choice being used.
The values in (AT.140) need not equal the values obtained
from a different choice of the contraction in AT17.
No equality or isomorphism with such a different prescribed
boundary choice is inferred here.

For the following additional degree-three formulas take
$R=\mathbb Z_{(2)}$ and the actual displayed local
resolution of A-S09-22.
Write $C,H,K,J$ for its zero-forms and put $U=T^{[2]}$.
Besides $TC=2(K-H)$ and $TH=2J$, one has
\[
                            TK-UC=2J.
 \tag{AT.142}
\]
Indeed multiply the first relation by $T$, use $T^2=2U$,
and cancel two in the torsionfree bottom module.
Under (AT.141), the exact polynomial expressions for the
degree-three class and its twice-primitive satisfy
\[
 B_{\rm orig}\longmapsto B^\flat+2JE_t,\qquad
 \zeta_{\rm orig}\longmapsto\zeta^\flat+d(JE_t).
 \tag{AT.143}
\]
For the first, only $a=v_{e_1}$ in $B=aH-Cr-gx$ changes,
by $TE_t$. For the second, the $u_0$ changes contribute
$D_t(TK-UC)=2JD_t$, the $v_0$ changes contribute
$(dJ)E_t$, and the old twist changes contribute
$-J(2D_t-dE_t)$. Their sum is $d(JE_t)$.

Consequently the relative degree-three repair algebras
are also related by actual inverse strict maps: in
addition to (AT.141) send their new generators by
\[
                    U_{\rm orig}\longmapsto U^\flat+JE_t,
                      \qquad V_{\rm orig}\longmapsto V^\flat.
 \tag{AT.144}
\]
The element $J$ has zero bottom face, so $JE_t$ is an
actual $K(2)$-parameter map even when $E_t$ has a nonzero
bottom. Thus these are maps on the stated sphere generators.
Equations (AT.143) check both new differentials; subtraction
gives the inverse. Thus the actual local target maps and
repair values of AT20 transfer to the original C21 Cartier
twist with precisely the chosen edges (AT.140).
For that application retain all hypotheses of AT20:
the actual maps to the two torsionfree admissible targets,
$X_0=S_R$, the coefficient $H^0(X_n)=S_R$ and rational
coefficient comparison in every simplex and weight,
and its displayed weight and target bounds.
The closed-boundary data used there are the actual $h$
and $x^\flat$ constructed in AT21.
The general algebraic comparisons (AT.135)--(AT.141)
are integral over either stated ground ring. Formulas
(AT.142)--(AT.144) here use the specified local resolution;
an integral realization of its low generators is not
being inferred from that local choice.

The same comparisons persist through the based PD saturation
when applied with the corresponding target maps.
An actual rationalized isomorphism preserving coefficients
and vertex maps sends a permitted root to a permitted root:
its differential lies in the corresponding preceding stage
and its vertex-zero coefficient is unchanged.
It therefore identifies the generated PD algebras at each
stage, and induction identifies their countable unions.
This supplies the actual saturated comparison, rather than
only a comparison of its rational cohomology.
\end{proof}
