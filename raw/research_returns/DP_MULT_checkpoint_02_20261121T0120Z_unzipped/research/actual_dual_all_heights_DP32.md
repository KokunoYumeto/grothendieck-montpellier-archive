# DP32: an integral model and sharp directional slopes for the actual Honda dual

Run DP_MULT_20261121. Author: /root, GPT-6 Astra Pro; runtime effort not exposed. Date: 2026-11-21 UTC. This is a new complete proof developed after DP30 and DP31. Independent whole-proof review is requested separately; no machine formalization is claimed.

## Statement

Let p be any prime, h≥2, q=p^h, d=h−1 and O=W(F_q). Let G_h be the one-dimensional Honda/Lubin–Tate group with logarithm

L_h(T)=sum_(n≥0) T^(q^n)/p^n.

There is an integral coordinate system X_1,…,X_d on its actual Cartier-dual formal group H=G_h^D in which the normalized invariant derivations D_i (dual to the logarithm coordinates below) have the following properties. Put

c_i=p^i/(q−1), r_i=1/(p−1)−c_i, 1≤i≤d.

For every integral formal function f and every multiindex a=(a_1,…,a_d), coefficientwise,

v_p(D_1^a_1 … D_d^a_d f) ≥ max(0, sum_i c_i a_i − sum_i s_p(a_i)/(p−1)).

For every i and every n=q^r,

v_p(D_i^n X_i(0))=c_i(n−1).

Thus each directional asymptotic slope is optimal in these primitive integral directions, and their sum is exactly c_h=1/(p−1)−1/(q−1). The statement extends by base change to W(k) containing O. It does not identify any required theta entry with these coordinates.

## 1. Integral construction

Work first over Z_p. Define a vector of monomials M in d variables by

M_1(X)=X_d^(p²), M_i(X)=X_(i−1)^p for 2≤i≤d.

For d=1, this means just M_1(X_1)=X_1^(p²). Define a vector logarithm

L(X)=sum_(n≥0) M^{∘n}(X)/p^n.

Every component is a well-defined formal series, since total degrees of iterates tend to infinity. Its linear term is X. It satisfies

L_i(X)=X_i+p^(-1)L_(i−1)(X^p), i≥2,

L_1(X)=X_1+p^(-1)L_d(X^(p²)).

Use Hazewinkel, Functional Equation Lemma 2.3, conditions (2.2), conclusion (2.5), printed p74: B=Z_p, fraction field Q_p, ideal pB, sigma=id, q_in_lemma=p; the matrix s_1 has entries 1/p just below the diagonal, and s_2 has entry 1/p in position (1,d). All other entries are zero. If d=1, s_1=0 and s_2=1/p. The displayed functional equations verify every input of that known lemma. Therefore

F_H(X,Y)=L^(-1)(L(X)+L(Y))

is an integral commutative formal group. The lemma's homomorphism clause (2.6) also makes [p]_H=L^(-1)(pL) integral.

Composition of the vector series with the monomial vector gives the exact identity

L(M(X))=p(L(X)−X).

Consequently L([p]_H(X))−L(M(X))=pX. By the congruence clause (2.8), printed p75, [p]_H(X)≡M(X) mod p.

## 2. Finite flatness and the actual Cartier-dual identification

The special-fibre map M is finite free of degree p^h: its coordinate algebra is free over the algebra generated by X_d^(p²), X_1^p,…,X_(d−1)^p, with basis

X_1^a_1 … X_d^a_d, 0≤a_i<p (i<d), 0≤a_d<p².

Here is a direct integral lift of this freeness, so no unlocated flatness criterion is needed. Let C=Z_p[[U_1,…,U_d]], B=Z_p[[X_1,…,X_d]], and map U_i to [p]_(H,i)(X). The chosen monomials define a C-linear map C^(p^h)→B, which is an isomorphism modulo p. Both rings are p-adically complete and separated, and B is p-torsion-free. For surjectivity, successively express a given element modulo p, lift the coefficient series, subtract and divide by p; the resulting coefficient sums converge p-adically in C. For injectivity, a relation is zero modulo p only if every coefficient is divisible by p. Divide by p, use p-torsion-freeness of B, and repeat. The intersection of p^nC is zero. Thus C^(p^h)→B is an isomorphism. Multiplication by p on the formal group is finite free of degree p^h, and its iterated kernels form a connected p-divisible group of height h and dimension d. This uses the formal-group/p-divisible-group passage of Tate1967 §2.2, Proposition1, pp162–164; the finite-flat hypothesis has just been checked directly.

Everything so far is defined over Z_p. On the special fibre over F_p, a full cycle of M has total Frobenius exponent 2+(d−1)=h, so

[p]_H^d=M^d=F_H^h.

Let G=H^D be the Cartier dual p-divisible group. Tate1967 §2.3 Proposition3 gives dimension(G)=h−d=1. Exact duality and its interchange of F,V give

V_G^h=[p]_G^d.

Since F_G^h V_G^h=[p]_G^h and d=h−1, cancellation of [p]^d gives F_G^h=[p]_G. The cancellation is valid in Hom of p-divisible groups: if u[p]^d=0, surjectivity of [p]^d as an fppf sheaf implies u=0. All maps here are endomorphisms over F_p, so no unmentioned Frobenius twist is suppressed.

In particular G[p]=ker F_G^h is connected, and the same holds at every level. Thus G has an integral one-dimensional connected formal group over Z_p by Tate's equivalence. Choose an integral coordinate T. Its p-series f(T) has linear term pT and reduction T^q: over F_p the relative Frobenius is T↦T^p in any F_p-coordinate, and F^h=[p]. After base change to O=W(F_q), f is exactly in the Lubin–Tate class for uniformizer p and residue cardinality q.

Hazewinkel1979 §5, pp76–77, states the known Lubin–Tate uniqueness and strict-isomorphism theorem: a series f≡pT mod degree2 and f≡T^q mod p determines its unique normalized formal group, and the groups for the same uniformizer are integrally strictly isomorphic. The Honda group G_h has its p-series in this class, by the same section's (5.2)–(5.4). The existing group G already has f as an endomorphism, hence by uniqueness it is F_f. Therefore G is strictly integrally isomorphic over O to G_h. Dualizing gives an integral isomorphism from the explicitly constructed H to the actual G_h^D. This establishes the promised model without guessing a Dieudonné sign convention or presuming an O-action on a candidate logarithm. The O-action can be transported by the established isomorphism.

## 3. Integral contraction and its exact reduction

Put S_h=(q−1)/(p−1), an integer. For i=1,…,d define a_i=S_h−p^i>0. Adjoin π with π^(q−1)=p and put α_i=π^a_i. Then v_p(α_i)=r_i. Scale both input and output coordinate i by α_i:

L'_i(Z)=α_i^(-1)L_i(α_1Z_1,…,α_dZ_d).

For each arrow in M, with exponent e_i=1 when i≥2 and e_1=2, and preceding coordinate j=i−1 or j=d, the identity

p^(e_i) r_j−r_i=1

holds. Equivalently p^(e_i)a_j−a_i=q−1. Thus the n=1 term of the scaled logarithm has coefficient exactly one. In an n-fold iterate following exponents e_1',…,e_n' in cyclic order, the coefficient valuation after scaling is

1+p^(e_1')+p^(e_1'+e_2')+…+p^(e_1'+…+e_(n−1)')−n.

It is zero for n=0 and n=1, and positive for every n≥2. It follows that L' is integral and reduces modulo π to the additive monomial map

L'(Z) modπ=Z+M(Z).

An integral formal map with identity linear term has an integral inverse E'. In characteristic p, M is additive and has strictly increasing total degree, so

E'(Z) modπ=sum_(n≥0)(−1)^n M^{∘n}(Z).

The cancellation is verified by composing with Id+M; signs are preserved by every p-power, including in characteristic2. At each fixed degree only finitely many terms occur. In component i, after setting all variables except Z_i to zero, a term survives precisely when the number of cyclic steps is a multiple of d. Its degrees are q^r, and the coefficient at each such degree is the nonzero sign (−1)^(dr). In particular,

v_p([t^(q^r)] E_i(t e_i))=−(q^r−1)r_i.

This follows from E'_i(Z)=α_i^(-1)E_i(α_1Z_1,…,α_dZ_d), including both the input and output scaling factors.

## 4. Derivatives, coefficientwise bounds and sharpness

The invariant derivations D_i are normalized by D_iX_j(0)=δ_ij. The group law is integral, so these are integral primitive derivations, and they commute. In the characteristic-zero additive logarithm coordinates they are partial derivatives. Thus

D^a f(0)=a! [t^a] f(E(t)).

Each E_j has weighted coefficient bound v_p(E_(j,b))+sum_i r_i b_i≥r_j>0. Since f is integral, formal substitution gives v_p([t^a]f(E(t)))≥−sum_i r_i a_i. Legendre's exact identity then gives

v_p(D^a f(0))≥sum_i c_i a_i−sum_i s_p(a_i)/(p−1).

Integrality of the derivations also gives the lower bound zero. To get the coefficientwise assertion rather than just evaluation at zero, substitute the integral translated function f(F_H(X,Y)), regarding X as coefficient variables, and apply the same additive-log derivative identity in Y at zero. Translation invariance identifies it with D^a f(X). All estimates are coefficientwise and therefore unchanged by the extra integral coefficient variables.

For f=X_i and n=q^r, the exact coefficient from §3 yields

v_p(D_i^n X_i(0))=v_p(n!)−(n−1)r_i=(n−1)c_i,

because s_p(n)=1. Hence no larger asymptotic slope in that direction is possible with a sublinear error. Finally,

sum_(i=1)^(h−1)c_i=(p+…+p^(h−1))/(p^h−1)=1/(p−1)−1/(p^h−1)=c_h.

This proves all assertions. The result supplies positive asymptotic gains on the dual even though DP30 has a unit derivative at order p in some direction. No relation to a prescribed theta entry is asserted.

## 5. A dimension-normalized numerical consequence

The line G_h contributes slope c_h in its one direction, while this integral dual basis has total slope c_h in h−1 directions. Thus the mean of these displayed slopes on G_h×G_h^D is 2c_h/h. For h>2 this is strictly smaller than c_2:

2c_h/(h c_2)<2(1+1/p)/h≤1,

using DP9's strict ratio bound; the last inequality holds for h≥3 and p≥2. This is an exact comparison for the stated equally weighted list of coordinate directions. It is not a lower bound on an automorphic cost, and a theta construction need not weight all these directions equally. It therefore does not refute every possible shorter chain.
