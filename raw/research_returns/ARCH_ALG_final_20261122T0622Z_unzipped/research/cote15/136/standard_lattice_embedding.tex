\subsection{Every standard-model map is injective, and torsion-free targets are saturated}
\label{arch136:standard-lattice-embedding}

\paragraph{ARCH-136-STANDARD-LATTICE-EMBEDDING.}
Let \(B\) be the standard integral divided-power simplex theory,
and let \(E\) be a simplicial nonnegative differential
\(S=\Gamma_{\mathbf Z}(T)\)-algebra satisfying the uniform
vertical admissibility condition (136.VP1).
Every strict differential \(S\)-algebra map
\[
                            \iota:B\longrightarrow E
 \tag{136.SE1}
\]
is injective in every simplex, weight and form degree.
If in addition every group of forms of \(E\) is torsion-free
over \(\mathbf Z\), then the inclusion is saturated for
integer divisibility:
\[
 N g=\iota(f),\quad N\geq1,\quad f\in B_n^{w,q},
 \quad g\in E_n^{w,q}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
              g\in\iota(B_n^{w,q}).
 \tag{136.SE2}
\]
Equivalently, after identifying the two algebras with their
images in \(E_{\mathbf Q}\),
\[
                             E\cap B_{\mathbf Q}=B.
 \tag{136.SE3}
\]
No horizontal admissibility or bound on the form degree in
each simplex of \(E\) is required.

This is a necessary condition on the torsion-free integral
lattice in the common-target route for cote~136, physical
pp.~117,119,121, footers 116,118,120, supplied scans 117,119,121.
It strengthens the standard-rational-ambient maximality
statement to an intersection assertion inside an arbitrary
rational ambient. It does not force \(E=B\) in that larger
ambient and does not decide existence of a common target.
The proof uses ARCH-136-VERTICAL-PERIODS and the elementary
standard-form pairing; no A, M, or outside preprint is used.

\paragraph{The standard-form pairing.}
If \(q>n\) or \(w<q\), the standard group is zero;
injectivity is immediate, and saturation follows from
torsion-freeness. In all other cases proceed as follows.
In simplex \(n\), eliminate \(e_0\) using
\(e_0+\cdots+e_n=0\), and write
\[
 f=\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\,|I|=q\\
                         |a|=w-q}}
     c_{I,a}\,
       x_0^{[a_0]}\cdots x_n^{[a_n]}e_I,\qquad
                         c_{I,a}\in\mathbf Z.
 \tag{136.SE4}
\]
Fix \(I\), and for \(b\in\mathbf N^{n+1}\) put
\[
                 \omega_{I,b}
                   =x_0^{[b_0]}\cdots x_n^{[b_n]}e_{I^c}.
 \]
All other exterior summands in \(f\omega_{I,b}\) vanish.
This is a standard top form, hence is closed and zero
on every face. Its integral, up to the fixed exterior
orientation sign, is
\[
 \int_{\Delta[n]}f\omega_{I,b}
       =P_I(b)T^{[L]},\qquad
 P_I(b)=\sum_{|a|=w-q}c_{I,a}
                      \prod_{j=0}^n\binom{a_j+b_j}{a_j},
 \quad L=w-q+|b|+n.
 \tag{136.SE5}
\]
The normalization follows from the divided-power simplex
integral. In a sufficiently high weight, this integral
identifies the relative cohomology of the standard theory
with \(S^L\); it agrees with the canonical comparison up
to the orientation sign, which does not affect vanishing
or divisibility.

\paragraph{Injectivity.}
Suppose \(\iota(f)=0\). For fixed \(I,b\), multiply the
top form in (136.SE5) by \(T^{[s]}\), with \(s\) large
enough for the ordinary relative period maps of \(B,E\)
through degree \(n\). Naturality of
ARCH-136-VERTICAL-PERIODS and \(\iota(f)=0\) give
\[
                          \binom{L+s}{L}P_I(b)=0.
 \]
The binomial coefficient is a nonzero integer, so
\(P_I(b)=0\) for every \(b\).
In the multivariate Newton basis,
\[
              \Delta_0^{a_0}\cdots\Delta_n^{a_n}P_I(0)
                              =c_{I,a}.
 \tag{136.SE6}
\]
Indeed \(\binom{a_j+b_j}{a_j}\) has leading Newton
coefficient one at multiindex \(a_j\); another summand
with the same total \(|a|\) contributes at multiindex
\(a\) only if each of its indices is at least \(a_j\),
which forces equality. Formula (136.SE6) now gives all
\(c_{I,a}=0\). Hence \(f=0\), proving injectivity.
The same argument includes \(n=q=0\), with no exterior
factors.

\paragraph{Saturation.}
Assume the forms of \(E\) are torsion-free and
\(Ng=\iota(f)\). For every standard test form put
\(\beta=g\,\iota(\omega_{I,b})\). Then
\[
                        N\beta=\iota(f\omega_{I,b}).
 \]
The right-hand side is closed. Since the group of
forms in the next degree is torsion-free,
\(d\beta=0\). Its restrictions to all faces are also
zero: the right-hand side is a standard top form and
every target face group is torsion-free. Thus \(\beta\)
is an actual closed relative form on
\((\Delta[n],\partial\Delta[n])\), even though \(E\)
may have nonzero forms above simplex dimension.

In sufficiently high weights, the integral relative
period map therefore gives
\[
                     N\ \bigm|\ \binom{L+s}{L}P_I(b)
                  \qquad\hbox{for every sufficiently large }s.
 \tag{136.SE7}
\]
For each prime \(p\mid N\), choose \(s=p^r>L\)
arbitrarily large. The binomial coefficient in
(136.SE7) is one modulo \(p\), by the identity
\((1+Z)^{p^r}=1+Z^{p^r}\) over \(\mathbf F_p\).
It follows that
\[
                v_p(P_I(b))\geq v_p(N).
 \]
Doing this for every prime divisor of \(N\) proves
\(P_I(b)\in N\mathbf Z\) for all \(b\).
The forward differences in (136.SE6) are integral
linear combinations of these values; hence every
\(c_{I,a}\) is divisible by \(N\).
Write \(f=Nf'\) with \(f'\in B_n^{w,q}\).
Then \(N(g-\iota(f'))=0\), and torsion-freeness gives
\(g=\iota(f')\). This proves (136.SE2).

Finally every element of \(E\cap B_{\mathbf Q}\) can
be written \(f/N\) in the rationalized standard
subalgebra. Torsion-freeness identifies the relation
\(Ng=\iota(f)\) already inside \(E\), so (136.SE2)
gives (136.SE3). The converse implication is immediate.
\hfill\(\square\)

\paragraph{Exact consequence for a proposed rational-image repair.}
An admissible lattice \(E\) inside any rational common
target, containing the standard \(B\), cannot acquire
a new element which is a nonintegral pure standard
form. Extra ambient form degrees do not remove this
particular obstruction: after standard multiplication
tests the divided lift is closed and face-zero
because the ambient lattice is torsion-free.

This does not say that every new form in \(E\) lies in
\(B_{\mathbf Q}\), or that all rational primitive
extensions are impossible. It gives a checkable
necessary condition on the boundary specializations
and standard-coordinate parts of those extensions.
For targets with torsion, only the injectivity
assertion is established here; the divided lift
need not then be closed or relatively zero.
