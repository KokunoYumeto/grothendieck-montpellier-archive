## Exact input and its scope

Section 3.2, printed p. 476 (PDF page 8), explicitly reports ambient
multiplicity six for the \(SL_9\) type
\(12\omega_1+5\omega_2+2\omega_7\) in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^{12}(\operatorname{Sym}^3\mathbb C^9)\),
with ideal multiplicity one. The phrase is:

> “but only one copy of it is in the ideal.”

Both preceding paragraphs identify this ideal as the ideal of the
determinant orbit closure. It is not merely the ideal generated by
their displayed Hessian equations, or an ideal of the auxiliary Dual
scheme. The source makes this particular numerical assertion in prose,
without a separate numbered theorem or a displayed computation proving it.
It is therefore recorded as a used published exact statement, not as an
independent numerical re-proof in this run.

## Conversion to the report's notation

Put \(W=\mathbb C^9\). The polynomial \(GL(W)\) partition corresponding
to that fundamental weight at total tensor degree \(36\) is
\[
\lambda=(19,7,2,2,2,2,2,0,0).
\]
Indeed its successive differences are \(12,5,0,0,0,0,2,0\),
and its size is \(36=12\cdot3\). Fixing this degree removes the ambiguity
of an \(SL_9\) representation up to determinant twists.

The \(GL(W)\)-orbit of \(\det_3\) is stable under all nonzero complex
scalars: the central action on a cubic realizes every scalar because
complex numbers have cube roots. Its affine closure is therefore the
cone over the projective orbit closure used in the source. Their degree
twelve homogeneous ideal is exactly the degree twelve part of the
affine ideal used in this report.

Consequently the published input gives
\[
a_\lambda(12[3])=6,\qquad
[\mathbb S_\lambda W:I(\overline{GL(W)\cdot\det_3})_{12}]=1,
\qquad K_3(\lambda)=6-1=5.
\]
The final subtraction uses exactness and complete reducibility of
finite-dimensional complex \(GL(W)\)-modules in the coordinate-ring
quotient. No permanent multiplicity is asserted by this source input.

## Qualifications checked in the surrounding text

The preceding Sections 2.3 and 3.1 distinguish the auxiliary Dual
scheme from the determinant orbit closure and describe the latter as
one component, with local equations near the determinant. That local
qualification does not change the explicit full-ideal wording of
Section 3.2. The source also warns that its equation locus has other
components. This audit does not identify those two global ideals.

There is a degree discrepancy in the paper's introductory summary:
Theorem 1.1.2 on p. 470 states degree \(n(n-1)\), whereas
Section 3.1 on p. 476 states \(2n(n-1)\). The published
Section 3.2 correctly includes the factor two in the general
\(\omega_1\) coefficient, agreeing with Theorem 2.3.1.
An earlier audit conflated the arXiv v1 formula with the published
formula; enlarged inspection of the published p. 476 corrected that
misreading. The explicit \(n=3\) weight and degree are internally consistent:
\(12\omega_1+5\omega_2+2\omega_7\), degree twelve, total size thirty-six.
This audit uses that explicit specialization, not the inconsistent
introductory degree.

pp. 470, 471, 473, 474, 475, and 476; Section 3.2 was read in full.
The corresponding arXiv v1 p. 6 was also inspected and agrees on the
specific six-versus-one assertion. No claim of reading the complete
