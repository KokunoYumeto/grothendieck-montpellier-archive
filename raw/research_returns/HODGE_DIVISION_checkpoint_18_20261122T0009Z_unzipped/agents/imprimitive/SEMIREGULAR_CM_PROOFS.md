## D-SR-01. A finite construction of a disjoint cyclic orbit

Let C be a smooth nonhyperelliptic genus-three curve, J=J(C), and Theta its principal polarization. Put D=C-C in J. Then D is an irreducible divisor of class 2Theta. Indeed the difference map C times C to D is generically one-to-one: a nontrivial equality p-q=p'-q' would yield two different effective degree-two divisors in the same linear system, contradicting nonhyperellipticity. The Poincare formula and the Pontryagin product give [C]*[-C]=2Theta. We use the usual Abel--Jacobi embedding; translating it does not alter D.

For every positive integer r,

    #(D intersect J[r]) <= 108 r^4.                         (SR1.1)

Here points are counted without multiplicity. To prove this, embed J by 3Theta. Hyperplanes through the image of zero have base locus exactly {0}. Their pullbacks by [r] have finite base locus J[r]. Choose two general such hyperplanes so that their pullbacks cut the surface D properly. Each point of D intersect J[r] contributes at least one to the resulting zero-dimensional intersection. Its degree is

    D . ([r]^*3Theta)^2 = (2Theta).(3r^2 Theta)^2
                       =18r^4 Theta^3 =108r^4.

The existence of proper successive cuts follows from Bertini avoidance of the finitely many positive-dimensional components; smoothness of the cuts is unnecessary.

Set

    P=2^2048, L=lcm(1,2,...,P), N=4L^2+1, d=N-1=4L^2.    (SR1.2)

There is a cyclic subgroup H of J[N], of exact order N, such that

    H intersect (C-C) = {0}.                              (SR1.3)

Here is a finite counting proof, with an explicit search specification. Choose v uniformly among the points of exact order N in J[N]=(Z/N)^6. For every divisor q>1 of N, the point (N/q)v is uniformly distributed among the exact-order-q points. Their number is

    J_6(q)=q^6 product_(p|q)(1-p^(-6)) > q^6/2.

The inequality follows, for example, from product(1-a_i)>=1-sum a_i and sum_(n>=2)n^(-6)<1/2. If the cyclic subgroup generated by v has a nonzero point on D, one of its exact-order-q generators lies on D for some q|N. The union bound and (SR1.1) therefore give

    Pr(<v> meets D outside zero)
       <=216 sum_(q|N,q>1) phi(q)/q^2
       <=216 [exp(sum_(p|N)1/p)-1].                       (SR1.4)

The first estimate allows all phi(q) generators of the cyclic order-q subgroup and thus overcounts, harmlessly. The second uses

    sum_(q|N)phi(q)/q^2
       =product_(p^a||N)(1+(1-p^(-a))/p)
       <=product_(p|N)(1+1/p).

Every prime divisor of N exceeds P, because every prime <=P divides L and N is 1 modulo that prime. For P a power of two, the elementary central-binomial argument gives

    log L < P log4.

For completeness, if psi(x)=log lcm(1,...,floor(x)), then psi(2r)-psi(r)<=log binom(2r,r)<=2r log2: every prime power in (r,2r] contributes its prime once to the binomial coefficient. Sum over the dyadic intervals ending at P. Consequently

    sum_(p|N)1/p <= log N/((P+1)log(P+1)) <1/500.

The last bound follows from log N<=log5+2logL<log5+4Plog2 and logP=2048log2. Thus (SR1.4) is strictly less than 216/499<1/2, using exp(x)-1<=x/(1-x) for 0<x<1. This proves existence.

To specify a finite construction, fix any algebraic enumeration of the finite group J[N], test its exact-order-N points in that order, and take the first v for which kv is outside the closed algebraic divisor D for every 1<=k<N. All these tests are finite algebraic membership tests, and the proof guarantees termination. This is an explicit terminating construction, although its deliberately coarse integer N is extremely large. It is not a claim that we have enumerated J[N] in this run.

Let q:J->Z=J/H be the quotient. By (SR1.3), the restriction q|C is a closed embedding: it is injective and unramified, and the source is proper. Write Cbar=q(C). Its full inverse image is the disjoint union of the N translates C+h.

Let theta in NS(Z)_Q be the unique rational class with q^*theta=Theta. It is ample. Uniqueness and existence follow from the rational cohomology isomorphism of an isogeny and the norm of a line bundle; explicitly q^*q_*Theta=N Theta because translations act trivially on cohomology. Put F=I_Cbar. Then

    ch(F)=1-(N/2)theta^2+(N/3)theta^3,                  (SR1.5)
    Delta:= integral_Z theta^3/6 =1/N.

The character follows by pulling back to J: a smooth genus-three curve has ch(O_C)=[C]-2[pt], since the degree of its Euler characteristic is -2 and td(J)=1. Pullback of the ideal is the ideal of the disjoint union, so its character is 1-NTheta^2/2+2N[pt].

**Extension.** For any fixed positive integer m<=163 the same proof applies to N=4mL^2+1 and d=4mL^2, with the same P,L. Replace log5 by log(4m+1) in the final estimate; it still gives sum_(p|N)1/p<1/500. The resulting field below is Q(sqrt(-m)). This extension concerns a CM sixfold isogenous to the Fermat Jacobian square; for m!=1 it is not being identified with a power of an elliptic curve with CM by Q(sqrt(-m)).

## D-SR-02. The descended ideal and its external square are semiregular

In the situation above, F=I_Cbar on the abelian threefold Z has

    dim Ext^0(F,F)=1, dim Ext^1(F,F)=6,
    dim Ext^2(F,F)=6, dim Ext^3(F,F)=1.                (SR2.1)

Its trace maps sigma_F^j:Ext^j(F,F)->HH_(-j)(Z) are injective for j=0,1,2. The same statements hold for the derived dual F^vee. Consequently F external_tensor F^vee on Z times Z is a gluable fully semiregular perfect complex, with Ext^2 dimension 48.

**Proof of (SR2.1).** Since q is etale and q|C is an isomorphism onto Cbar, the normal bundles N_(C/J) and N_(Cbar/Z) are identified. The normal sequence

    0 -> T_C -> O_C^3 -> N_(C/J) -> 0

shows h^0(N)=3. Indeed the next map H^1(T_C)->H^1(O_C)^3 is injective: its dual is the multiplication H^0(K_C) tensor H^0(K_C)->H^0(K_C^2), which is surjective for a smooth plane quartic. In this genus-three case this last statement is elementary: K_C=O_C(1), and restriction of plane quadrics is an isomorphism from the six-dimensional space of quadrics onto H^0(C,O_C(2)); no nonzero quadric contains C. Also deg N=4 and rank N=2, so Riemann--Roch gives h^1(N)=3.

Here is a direct self-Ext calculation, avoiding any global identification of moduli spaces. Apply Hom(F,-) to 0->F->O_Z->O_Cbar->0. The map Hom(F,O_Z)->Hom(F,O_Cbar) is zero, since every global homomorphism F->O_Z is a scalar multiple of the ideal inclusion. Thus there is an exact sequence

    0 -> H^0(N_(Cbar/Z)) -> Ext^1(F,F) -> Ext^1(F,O_Z).

By threefold Serre duality, Ext^1(F,O_Z)=H^2(F)^*. The restriction H^1(O_Z)->H^1(O_Cbar) is an isomorphism: it is the Abel--Jacobi isomorphism after composition with the isogeny q. The cohomology sequence of the ideal therefore gives H^2(F)=H^2(O_Z), of dimension three. Thus dim Ext^1(F,F)<=3+3=6. The three embedded translation directions and the three degree-zero tensor directions are independent. Indeed the embedded ideal deformations have trivial determinant, whereas the trace of a tensor direction is the original class in H^1(O_Z), because rank(F)=1. Hence equality holds and

    Ext^1(F,F) = H^1(O_Z) direct_sum H^0(N_(Cbar/Z)).  (SR2.2)

These assertions can also be checked on the two-generator Koszul resolution of the ideal; no stability or smoothness of a moduli space of sheaves is assumed.

End(F)=C: restriction outside Cbar identifies an endomorphism with a regular function, which extends across codimension two on the normal projective variety Z. Serre duality, with omega_Z=O_Z, then yields (SR2.1).

Write sigma_F^j(x)=tr(exp(at(F))x) under HKR; the Todd correction is one for an abelian variety. For j=0 the trace of the identity is ch(F), whose rank component is 1. For j=1, the infinitesimal action of H^1(O_Z) by tensoring and of H^0(T_Z) by translation gives an isomorphism

    H^1(O_Z) direct_sum H^0(T_Z) -> Ext^1(F,F).

The translation summand is an isomorphism onto H^0(N) by the normal sequence just used, and the tensor summand maps isomorphically to the determinant direction. Composition with sigma_F^1 sends the first summand to H^1(O_Z) by the identity; the H^0(T_Z) summand has zero component there because c_1(F)=0 and maps injectively into H^2(Omega_Z^1) by contraction with -(N/2)theta^2. Nondegeneracy of theta proves that contraction is injective. Thus sigma_F^1 is injective.

For j=2, apply the trace/Atiyah obstruction identity to the subspace

    H^2(O_Z) direct_sum H^1(T_Z) subset HH^2(Z).

The H^2(O_Z) summand maps by the rank-one identity to the H^2(O_Z) component of HH_-2. The H^1(T_Z) summand has zero component there because c_1(F)=0 and maps surjectively onto H^3(Omega_Z^1) by contraction with -(N/2)theta^2. Here is a direct rank check. In holomorphic/antiholomorphic bases theta=sum alpha_i beta_i, contraction by a Kodaira--Spencer matrix kappa gives kappa contraction theta^2=2(kappa contraction theta)theta. The map kappa->kappa contraction theta is onto wedge^2<span beta_i>, and multiplication by theta is an isomorphism of that three-dimensional space onto span(alpha_i) tensor wedge^3<span beta_i>. Hence the rank is three. Therefore the full composite has rank six. Its middle space Ext^2(F,F) has dimension six, so sigma_F^2 is injective. Only the classical trace/Atiyah identity is used, not a conjectural deformation assertion.

Derived duality identifies the self-Ext groups of F and F^vee; the same rank argument applies to ch(F^vee)=1-(N/2)theta^2-(N/3)theta^3. In particular sigma_(F^vee)^j is injective for j=0,1,2.

Finally the trace of an external tensor product is the external product of the traces, because its Atiyah class is at(F) external_tensor id + id external_tensor at(F^vee), and the exponential and trace factor. The Ext^2 Kunneth decomposition is

    Ext^2(F,F) tensor Ext^0(F^vee,F^vee)
    direct_sum Ext^1(F,F) tensor Ext^1(F^vee,F^vee)
    direct_sum Ext^0(F,F) tensor Ext^2(F^vee,F^vee).

The dimensions are 6,36,6. Their trace images lie in the three distinct Hochschild Kunneth components HH_-2 tensor HH_0, HH_-1 tensor HH_-1, and HH_0 tensor HH_-2. Each restriction is injective by the preceding checks, so there is no cancellation between the three pieces. This proves semiregularity and the dimension 48. Negative self-Ext groups vanish by the same Kunneth decomposition, since they vanish for a coherent sheaf and for its derived dual. This proves gluability.

## D-SR-03. An exact Fourier--Mukai formula with a nonzero Weil term

Let A=Z times Zhat. Let Poincare be the normalized Poincare bundle and gamma=c_1(Poincare) on A. Let theta_hat be the rational dual polarization satisfying phi_theta^*theta_hat=theta, where phi_theta:Z->Zhat is the rational polarization quasi-isogeny. Define the positive rational divisor class

    H=N theta_x+theta_hat+gamma.                         (SR3.1)

Let mu:Z times Z->Z times Z be mu(x,y)=(x+y,y). Define the exact equivalence

    Phi=(id_Z external_tensor FM_(Poincare^(-1))) o mu^*,
    E=Phi(F external_tensor F^vee).                      (SR3.2)

No extra cohomological shift is included in this convention. Then E is gluable and fully semiregular, and its complete Chern character satisfies

    exp(-gamma/2) ch(E)=t + 2H-H^3/(12d)+H^5/(960d^2),   (SR3.3)

where t is a nonzero rational Weil class for a K=Q(sqrt(-d)) action of signature (3,3) on (A,H). Thus rank(E)=0 causes no problem for the exact S-14 hypothesis.

**Exact calculation.** Put u=sqrt(-d), s=-1+u, bar_s=-1-u, and

    a=(1+1/u)/2, bar_a=(1-1/u)/2.

Equation (SR1.5) is exactly

    ch(F)=a exp(s theta)+bar_a exp(bar_s theta).         (SR3.4)

It suffices to expand through theta^3; use u^2=-d and N=d+1. The coefficient of theta is zero, that of theta^2 is -N/2, and that of theta^3 is N/3.

Choose rational symplectic Darboux coordinates for theta. In one of its three planes denote the first-factor coordinates by a_i,b_i, the integrated second-factor coordinates by c_i,d_i, and the dual output coordinates by u_i,v_i. Then theta_x=sum a_i b_i, theta_y=sum c_i d_i, theta_hat=sum u_i v_i, and gamma=sum(a_i v_i-b_i u_i). Juxtaposition means exterior multiplication. The Poincare kernel on the integrated factors has class sum(c_i v_i-d_i u_i). Direct extraction of the c_i d_i coefficient gives

    integral_(c_i,d_i) exp(s(a_i+c_i)(b_i+d_i)-t c_i d_i
                                  -(c_i v_i-d_i u_i))
      =(s-t)-st a_i b_i-s b_i u_i-u_i v_i+s a_i v_i
                                  -s a_i b_i u_i v_i.  (SR3.5)

The integration over all three planes has the additional scalar Delta=integral_Z theta^3/6=1/N. This scalar is essential because theta is rational and need not be principal on Z.

When t=s, (SR3.5) equals

    -exp((a_i v_i-b_i u_i)/2)
                         (s a_i-u_i)(s b_i-v_i).

Consequently, putting Q_s=product_(i=1)^3(s a_i-u_i)(s b_i-v_i), the two equal-root terms in the normalized transform are

    t=-Delta(a^2 Q_s+bar_a^2 Q_(bar_s)).                 (SR3.6)

When t=bar_s, set alpha=s-bar_s=2u. Since st=N and s+t=-2, (SR3.5) is

    alpha exp(gamma_i/2-H_i/alpha),
    H_i=N a_i b_i+u_i v_i+a_i v_i-b_i u_i.

The unequal-root pair therefore contributes after normalization

    Delta a bar_a alpha^3 [exp(-H/alpha)-exp(H/alpha)].

Use a bar_a=N/(4d), alpha^2=-4d and H^7=0. Expanding the odd powers gives precisely the polynomial in (SR3.3). Equations (SR3.4)--(SR3.5), or a sparse exterior-algebra expansion of all twelve output generators, are exact identities; a separate script checks the displayed formulas and records the coefficients.

**The Weil structure and positivity.** The rational endomorphism T of A is defined, after the quasi-isogeny identification A~Z times Z supplied by theta, by the matrix

    T=[[-2,-1],[N,0]].

It satisfies T^2+2T+N=0. Thus T+1 defines sqrt(-d). On H^1, its s-eigenspace is exactly the six-dimensional span of the forms s a_i-u_i, s b_i-v_i. Its conjugate eigenspace is the analogous bar_s span. Since the two-by-two matrix acts by scalars on each copy of H^1(Z), each eigenspace has holomorphic dimension three and antiholomorphic dimension three. Thus Q_s and Q_bar_s span the two determinant lines of the rational Weil plane.

The class H is ample. Under id times phi_theta its pullback is

    N theta_1+theta_2+(m^*theta-theta_1-theta_2)
       =(N-1)theta_1+m^*theta,

which is ample because (x,y)->(x,x+y) is an isomorphism and N>1. The generator T+1 is Rosati anti-self-adjoint for H: in these coordinates the polarization matrix is S=[[N,1],[1,1]], and S(T+1)=[[0,-d],[d,0]] is skew-symmetric. Hence the K action is compatible with H. The coefficients a and bar_a are nonzero, and Q_s,Q_bar_s are independent nonzero determinant vectors, so (SR3.6) is nonzero. Galois conjugation interchanges its two summands, proving rationality. This proves the entire character identity and its nonzero Weil component.

Fourier--Mukai equivalence preserves negative self-Ext and injectivity of the categorical trace, so D-SR-02 proves the stated gluability and semiregularity of E. This preservation is the functoriality in Perry Remarks 2.3 and 2.5, and follows directly from transporting the evaluation/coevaluation defining the categorical trace across an equivalence. The result is not inferred from equality of K-classes alone.

## D-SR-04. Transport to the requested balanced CM power

Choose C to be the Fermat quartic x^4+y^4+z^4=0. Its Jacobian is isogenous to E_i^3, where E_i=C/(Z+iZ): Beauville, *Some surfaces with maximal Picard number*, JEP 1 (2014), section 4, Example 3, p.107, with Lemma 2 on p.106. Thus A=Z times Zhat is isogenous to E_i^6.

The isogeny to the CM power and its polarization can be specified explicitly. The three degree-two quotients of the Fermat quartic by sign changes of its coordinates are elliptic curves with j=1728. Choose origin-preserving isomorphisms of them with E_i, and let

    j:E_i^3 -> J

be the sum of the three pullback homomorphisms of Jacobians. Their cotangent images are the three distinct nontrivial characters of the sign-change Klein four group, so j is an isogeny. Each diagonal norm-pullback composition is multiplication by two; cross compositions vanish, since they intertwine different sign characters. Therefore

    j^*Theta=2theta_(E_i^3), deg(j)=8.

Put h=qj:E_i^3->Z, r=2L, d=r^2, s=-1+ri, and define the two-by-two matrices acting on threefold pairs by

    R=[[1,1],[bar_s,s]],
    k=diag(h,phi_theta h),
    f=N k R:E_i^6 -> A.                                (SR4.1)

The map k is initially a rational homomorphism, but f is an actual isogeny: Ntheta is the integral norm-polarization class q_*Theta, so Nphi_theta=phi_(Ntheta) is an actual homomorphism. All entries of R are Gaussian integers. Its two columns are eigenvectors of T+1 with eigenvalues ri and -ri. Thus f is K-linear for the specified balanced source action diag(i,i,i,-i,-i,-i). If theta_prod is the product principal polarization on E_i^6, direct matrix multiplication gives

    R^dagger [[N,1],[1,1]] R=2d I,
    f^*H=4dN^2 theta_prod,
    deg(f)=4096 d^3 N^12.                              (SR4.2)

For the degree calculation, deg(h)=8N, deg(phi_theta)=Delta^2=N^(-2) as a rational isogeny, so deg(k)=64; deg(R)=Norm(2ri)^3=(4d)^3; multiplication by N on a sixfold has degree N^12. Multiplying gives the stated integer.

Let G=ker(f), acting honestly by translations, and E_0=f^*E with its canonical coherent G-linearization. Etale descent identifies

    Ext^2(E_0,E_0)^G=Ext^2(E,E).

Under HKR, the full trace of a pullback along an etale morphism is the pullback of the trace. The map f^*:HH_-2(A)->HH_-2(E_i^6) is injective; indeed each Hodge-cohomology pullback is an isomorphism over Q or C for an isogeny, with inverse f_*/deg(f). Since E is fully semiregular, the trace of E_0 is injective on the invariant Ext^2. Hence E_0 is G-semiregular, and therefore weakly G-semiregular.

We check ordinary negative self-Ext, rather than only its G-invariant part. The finite etale isogeny has

    f_*O_(E_i^6)=direct_sum_(alpha in ker(fhat)) alpha,

where the summands are torsion degree-zero line bundles on A. Adjunction and the projection formula yield

    Ext^j(E_0,E_0)=direct_sum_alpha Ext^j(E,E tensor alpha).

Conjugating tensorization by alpha through Phi gives an autoequivalence of Z times Z consisting of a translation followed by tensorization with a degree-zero line bundle. This can be seen directly from (SR3.2): Fourier--Mukai interchanges a degree-zero tensor on the dual factor with a translation on the original factor, and mu conjugates translations to translations and pulls degree-zero line bundles back to degree-zero line bundles. Every such operation on Z times Z is a product operation, since Pic^0(Z times Z)=Pic^0(Z) times Pic^0(Z). Thus, up to an irrelevant one-dimensional scalar factor,

    Phi^(-1)(E tensor alpha)
       =(t_x^*F tensor L_1) external_tensor
                              (t_y^*F^vee tensor L_2).

The self-Ext comparison with F external_tensor F^vee has no negative degrees: the first factor is Ext between coherent sheaves; by derived duality the second is also Ext between coherent sheaves in the opposite order. Their Kunneth tensor product therefore has no negative cohomology. This proves Ext^j(E_0,E_0)=0 for every j<0, closing the gluability requirement.

The character identity already pulls back exactly: set B_0=-f^*gamma/2, t_0=f^*t, and H_0=f^*H. Then

    exp(B_0)ch(E_0)=t_0+2H_0-H_0^3/(12d)+H_0^5/(960d^2).

By (SR4.2), H_0=4dN^2 theta_prod. We can take the fixed very ample polarization theta_0=3theta_prod and rewrite the right side as t_0+P(theta_0), with the explicitly displayed rational coefficients after substituting H_0=(4dN^2/3)theta_0. Thus this is a seed on the requested balanced CM power with its product principal polarization, not merely on an unspecified isogenous polarized variety. All of the S-14 object hypotheses have now been proved and independently checked.

## D-SR-05. Split determinant and the other imaginary quadratic fields

The K-Hermitian space in D-SR-03 is split, of rank six and Witt index three; in the raw determinant convention its determinant class is [-1]. This does not follow merely from its real signature. Here is a direct rational proof. Let E_theta be the rational alternating polarization form on V=H_1(Z,Q), and write J=T+1 and u=sqrt(-d). Define q_H(x,y)=E_H(x,Jy) and h(x,y)=q_H(x,y)+u E_H(x,y). Since J is skew-adjoint and J^2=-d, q_H is symmetric, h is Hermitian, h(x,Jy)=u h(x,y), and h(Jx,y)=-u h(x,y). Thus h is conjugate-linear in the first argument and linear in the second, with h(v,v)=q_H(v,v).

Choose any rational E_theta-Lagrangian L in V, of dimension three. Under the rational identification H_1(A,Q)=V direct_sum V, put I=L direct_sum L. The scalar two-by-two matrix J preserves I, so I has K-dimension three. The polarization matrix is S tensor E_theta; hence E_H vanishes on I. Because I is J-stable, q_H also vanishes on I, and therefore h vanishes on I. This is a half-dimensional totally isotropic K-subspace. A nondegenerate Hermitian space with such a subspace is a sum of three hyperbolic planes: choose a dual basis for I, and subtract half of its Hermitian self-pairing matrix to make the dual subspace isotropic. Each hyperbolic plane has determinant -1 modulo K-norms. Thus h is split and has determinant (-1)^3 modulo K-norms. This argument concerns the actual polarization and K action and works independently of the additional CM endomorphisms.

For any positive squarefree integer m, choose

    P_m=2^(2048+ceil(log_2(m+1))),
    L_m=lcm(1,...,P_m),
    N_m=4mL_m^2+1, d_m=4mL_m^2.

The proof of D-SR-01 applies verbatim. Indeed every prime of N_m exceeds P_m, log L_m<P_m log4, and

    sum_(p|N_m)1/p
       < [log(4m+1)+4P_m log2]/[P_m log P_m] <1/500.

For the last inequality use m<P_m, log P_m>=2048log2 and P_m>=2^2048; the two lower-order logarithmic terms are less than 2/P_m. Thus the same strict union bound gives the required cyclic subgroup. The construction D-SR-01--03 yields a gluable fully semiregular complex on a CM sixfold isogenous to E_i^6, with the complete character identity and Weil field K=Q(sqrt(-m)). For the nine class-number-one fields the smaller common P=2^2048 already suffices, as stated in D-SR-01. The underlying elliptic CM field is Q(i); the newly constructed Weil action is K. Those two actions must not be confused.

Apply supplied DEG_L S-14 with G trivial on A and with a sufficiently divisible integral multiple of H as the relatively very ample polarization. On a finite level cover of the corresponding split Weil family, the rational Weil plane is a constant local system; take the flat section whose value at this CM point is t. Formula (SR3.3) is its complete Hodge-compatible character. S-14 therefore gives bounded division for this nonzero Weil class throughout that fixed family. The supplied Theorem W then gives the corresponding detector/whole-Weil-plane conclusion. This statement uses Perry's deformation theorem as specified in S-14; it has no Markman, Severn, or 032 theorem as a premise.

The construction settles the existence of the deformation object, including at the standard balanced Gaussian CM power in D-SR-04. It supplies an explicit finite search bound for the cyclic subgroup, an exact Chern character, an actual group action, and an exact CM isogeny degree. D-SR-06 additionally supplies an ordinary, untwisted deformation and the explicit class denominator and norm. This rank-zero argument by itself leaves the detector degree existential. The later positive-rank construction SF-QC01--05 supplies the missing effective presentation and a numerical degree bound; it has a separate full proof and audit. A positive definite form or a divisor-generated CM K-class alone does not supply that presentation.

## D-SR-06. An integral twist, ordinary deformation, and exact class normalization

Retain the Gaussian CM isogeny f of D-SR-04 and put c=4dN^2, so H_0=c theta_prod. The class B_0 is integral. To check this with the correct normalization, let C_2=[[0,1],[1,0]]. The pullback of gamma by k=diag(h,phi_theta h) has matrix 2C_2 relative to the product principal polarization, because h^*theta=2theta_(E_i^3). Consequently

    R^dagger C_2 R=2[[-1,s],[bar_s,-1]],
    f^*gamma=4N^2[[-1,s],[bar_s,-1]] tensor I_3,
    B_0=2N^2[[1,-s],[-bar_s,1]] tensor I_3.             (SR6.1)

The entries of the last Hermitian matrix are Gaussian integers and its diagonal entries are ordinary integers. Such a matrix represents an integral divisor class on the principally polarized Gaussian product. Indeed the map from divisor classes to symmetric endomorphisms, composed with the inverse principal polarization, identifies these classes with the integral Hermitian matrices; equivalently, its alternating Riemann form is integral on the product Gaussian lattice. By the integral (1,1) theorem it is the first Chern class of a line bundle L_B. No ampleness of L_B is needed.

Put E'_0=E_0 tensor L_B. Conjugate the honest G-action by tensorization with L_B. For a translation t_g this replaces the action by t_g^*(-) tensor (L_B tensor t_g^*L_B^(-1)). The additional line bundle is degree zero, and the assignment is a homomorphism into the translation/tensor group; its image is finite because G is finite. The coherent composition isomorphisms are transported by the tensor equivalence. The equivariant structure, weak semiregularity, and ordinary negative-self-Ext vanishing are therefore preserved. Now the entire ordinary character is

    ch(E'_0)=t_0+2c theta_prod
                -(16/3)d^2 N^6 theta_prod^3
                +(16/15)d^3 N^10 theta_prod^5.           (SR6.2)

All three polynomial coefficients are integers: L is divisible by 3 and 5, and d=4L^2. This is a genuine line-bundle twist of the complex; it is not a formal normalization of a rank-zero character.

In Perry's proof of Theorem 1.2 in section 6.2, pp.26--28, one can now choose B'_0=0, hence the initial Kummer class and the associated Brauer class are zero. The band pushout used to extend the group action sends the zero gerbe class to zero. The ambient category in Theorem 6.3 is consequently Perf of the ordinary abelian family. Thus the resulting object on an etale neighborhood is an ordinary perfect complex, even if a separate presentation of the invariant category involves a gerbe. This conclusion uses the actual proof of the cited preprint, rather than strengthening the word "twisted" in its introductory statement without justification.

The class denominator can be sharpened from the safe factorial 6 to 2. The character (SR6.2) has ch_1=2H_0 and ch_2=0, so the integral Chern classes of the perfect complex satisfy c_1=2H_0 and c_2=2H_0^2. Newton's formula gives ch_3=-(2/3)H_0^3+c_3/2. Comparing with (SR3.3) yields

    t_0=c_3(E'_0)/2 + [(16/3)d^2 N^6(1-8d)] theta_prod^3,
    delta=2,
    T_0:= -integral_(E_i^6) t_0^2 =32768 d^4 N^12.      (SR6.3)

The bracket is an integer because 3 divides L. Hence 2t_0 is integral. The same calculation expresses 2t as an integral algebraic cycle on the ordinary-deformation open. The degree bound of its positive and negative parts can be chosen uniformly on a nonempty finite-type open by representing the relative Chern classes and the polarization power by cycles there. The proper relative Chow-space argument of supplied DIV1 then extends this fixed multiplier 2, with some common finite degree bound, to every fiber of the fixed family. This argument establishes a uniform multiplier; it does not calculate the degree bound.

    integral_A t^2=2Delta^2(a bar_a)^2(s-bar_s)^6=-8d,
