# SF-E838. A leading-quadratic criterion for common point defects

Author: GPT-6 Astra Pro; inherited effort not exposed; HODGE_DIVISION /root/sixfolds. Date: 2026-11-21.

**Status:** new complete proof submitted for independent whole-proof review. The actual companion equations and local-to-global characteristic interface are the reviewed SF-E836 and SF-E819 inputs. The new matrix lemma is proved for arbitrary local Artinian algebras. The application excludes a nonempty open family of quadratic complete intersections, and every higher-order perturbation with those quadratic jets. Its residual polynomial locus is a necessary condition only.

## 1. An arbitrary-Artin square-matrix lemma

Let \(A\) be a finite local complex algebra, with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak n\), residue field \(\mathbb C\), and

\[
 V=\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2,\qquad W=\mathfrak n^2/\mathfrak n^3,
 \qquad e=\dim V,\quad h_2=\dim W.
 \tag{E838.1}
\]

**Lemma.** If \(h_2>e\), a square matrix \(C\in\operatorname{Mat}_m(A)\) whose cokernel is annihilated by \(\mathfrak n^2\) is invertible.

Split off unit pivots by actual invertible row and column operations over the local ring. This preserves the cokernel up to isomorphism and leaves an identity block together with a square block \(C'\) of size \(k\), all of whose entries lie in \(\mathfrak n\). If \(k=0\), the assertion holds. Suppose \(k>0\).

Choose complex vector-space splittings of the two successive filtration quotients in \(A/\mathfrak n^3\). They are not algebra splittings. Write \(C'=C_1+C_2\) modulo \(\mathfrak n^3\), with coefficients in the chosen lifts of \(V\) and \(W\), respectively. Set

\[
 L:\mathbb C^k\longrightarrow V\otimes\mathbb C^k,\qquad L(v)=C_1v,
 \qquad a=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}L\le k.
 \tag{E838.2}
\]

An input has a constant part \(v_0\), a linear part \(v_1\), and a part in \(\mathfrak n^2\), which does not contribute modulo \(\mathfrak n^3\). Its image is \(C_1v_0+C_2v_0+C_1v_1\). This lies in the pure second filtration quotient exactly when \(v_0\in\ker L\). Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{im}(C'\bmod\mathfrak n^3)\cap(W\otimes\mathbb C^k)
   =C_2(\ker L)+V\cdot\operatorname{im}L.
 \tag{E838.3}
\]

The multiplication on the right is the actual induced map \(V\otimes V\to W\). The two summands have dimensions at most \(k-a\) and \(ea\). Since \(h_2>e\) entails \(e\ge1\), their sum has dimension at most

\[
 k+(e-1)a\le ek<h_2k.
 \tag{E838.4}
\]

On the other hand, annihilation of the cokernel by \(\mathfrak n^2\) puts every element of \(W\otimes\mathbb C^k\) in (E838.3), a contradiction. Thus the residual block is absent. This proves the lemma in every matrix size, without any grading, matrix commutativity, or simultaneous diagonalization hypothesis.

## 2. A general necessary companion criterion

Let \(R=\mathbb C[[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]]\), and let \(\pi_+,\pi_-\) be constant rank-two bivectors with complementary supports. Let \(I=(f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)\) be an Artinian complete-intersection ideal, and put \(A=R/I\). Consider an actual finite module fitting into

\[
 0\longrightarrow A^n\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow R^r
       \longrightarrow A^m\longrightarrow0,\qquad m+n>0.
 \tag{E838.5}
\]

Suppose that \(M\) has flat first-order module deformations for both multiplication classes \(\pi_+,\pi_-\). The reviewed finite-cone trace in E819 gives \(n=m>0\). The full actual lifting and opposite-order calculation of E836, sections 2--3, gives matrices \(B_{ij},C_+,C_-\in\operatorname{Mat}_m(A)\) satisfying

\[
 C_\pm B_{ij}=B_{ij}C_\pm=P^\pm_{ij}I_m,
 \qquad P^\pm_{ij}=\{f_i,f_j\}_{\pi_\pm}\bmod I.
 \tag{E838.6}
\]

Assume the following three algebraic conditions: all \(P^\pm_{ij}\) belong to \(\mathfrak n^2\); \(h_2>e\); and some constant linear combination \(P^0_{ij}=aP^+_{ij}+bP^-_{ij}\) spans \(W=\mathfrak n^2/\mathfrak n^3\) as \(i<j\) varies. Let \(J\) be the ideal generated by these six \(P^0_{ij}\). Spanning says

\[
 \mathfrak n^2=J+\mathfrak n^3.
 \tag{E838.7}
\]

In the finite \(A\)-module \(\mathfrak n^2/J\), this identity is exactly \(\mathfrak n^2/J=\mathfrak n(\mathfrak n^2/J)\). Iterating it through the nilpotent maximal ideal, or applying Nakayama's lemma, gives \(J=\mathfrak n^2\). This uses the ideal generated by the brackets, not only their complex linear span.

The actual matrix \(C_0=aC_++bC_-\) obeys \(C_0B_{ij}=P^0_{ij}I_m\). Therefore \(J\), and hence \(\mathfrak n^2\), annihilates its cokernel. The lemma proves that \(C_0\) is invertible. No extra deformation for the pencil bivector is assumed; this is a linear combination of two existing matrix identities.

Put \(D_\pm=C_\pm C_0^{-1}\). Solving for \(B_{ij}\) gives

\[
 D_\pm P^0_{ij}=P^\pm_{ij}I_m.
 \tag{E838.8}
\]

Let \(\overline P\) denote the class in \(W\), and let \(D_{\pm,0}\) be the constant matrix modulo \(\mathfrak n\). Some \(\overline P^0_{ij}\ne0\). Applying a complex linear functional on \(W\) that is nonzero on that element to (E838.8) modulo \(\mathfrak n^3\) forces \(D_{\pm,0}=d_\pm I_m\). All six equations then give

\[
 (\overline P^+_{ij})_{i<j}=d_+(\overline P^0_{ij})_{i<j},
 \qquad
 (\overline P^-_{ij})_{i<j}=d_-(\overline P^0_{ij})_{i<j}.
 \tag{E838.9}
\]

Thus the two leading bracket tuples must be linearly dependent in \(W^6\). In particular, the three conditions together with independence of these two tuples exclude the two deformations. This conclusion concerns an actual module over the original algebra; no module deformation or Ext group has been base changed to a smaller quotient.

## 3. Regular quadratic jets make the tuples independent

Let \(S=\mathbb C[z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4]\). Suppose that \(F_1,\ldots,F_4\in S_2\) form a homogeneous regular sequence with Artinian quotient

\[
 A_0=S/(F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4),\qquad
 Q=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}(F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4).
 \tag{E838.10}
\]

The four elementary exact sequences obtained by successively multiplying by a nonzerodivisor of degree two give

\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}_{A_0}(t)
       =\frac{(1-t^2)^4}{(1-t)^4}=(1+t)^4.
 \tag{E838.11}
\]

In particular \(\dim(A_0)_1=4\), \(\dim(A_0)_2=6\), and the length is sixteen.

Take arbitrary formal perturbations \(f_i=F_i+O(\mathfrak m^3)\), where \(\mathfrak m=(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\). Their quotient is again an Artinian complete intersection, and its associated graded algebra is \(A_0\). Here is the filtered justification. If the initial form of an element \(f\) in a separated filtered quotient is a nonzerodivisor, then for every nonzero \(h\) the initial form of \(fh\) is the nonzero product of the two initial forms. Hence the initial ideal of the principal ideal \((f)\) is generated by that initial form, and the associated graded quotient is its graded quotient. Apply this successively to the four regular initial forms \(F_i\). All intermediate quotients are separated Noetherian complete local rings. The resulting graded quotient is (E838.11); alternatively its vanishing in degree five gives \(\mathfrak n^5=\mathfrak n^6\), and Nakayama gives \(\mathfrak n^5=0\). The same initial-form nonzerodivisor argument proves regularity of the \(f_i\).

Thus for the actual perturbed algebra, \(e=4\), \(h_2=6>4\), and

\[
 W=\mathfrak n^2/\mathfrak n^3=S_2/Q,\qquad
 \overline P^\pm_{ij}=\{F_i,F_j\}_{\pi_\pm}\bmod Q.
 \tag{E838.12}
\]

The leading bracket tuples in (E838.12) are independent. To prove this, work in the separate homogeneous complete intersection \(A_0\), not with an unjustified conclusion about the perturbed Jacobian. Let \(J_F\) be the actual Jacobian matrix of the four quadrics, and write

\[
 \mathcal P_\pm=J_F\pi_\pm J_F^{\mathsf t}
       \in\Lambda^2\mathbb C^4\otimes(A_0)_2.
 \tag{E838.13}
\]

Functoriality of exterior powers gives \(\mathcal P_\pm\wedge\mathcal P_\pm=0\), since each original bivector has rank two, and

\[
 \mathcal P_+\wedge\mathcal P_-
       =\det(J_F)\,(\pi_+\wedge\pi_-)\ne0
       \quad\text{in }\Lambda^4\mathbb C^4\otimes(A_0)_4.
 \tag{E838.14}
\]

The nonvanishing is precisely the published complete-intersection Jacobian-socle theorem: Eisenbud--Ulrich, *J. reine angew. Math.* 756 (2019), Theorem 7.1, p. 210, with Appendix A.1, A.3 and A.5, pp. 222--225. Apply it to the completion of the homogeneous Artinian algebra; the finite quotient itself is unchanged. The characteristic is zero, the four generators are a regular sequence, and the Jacobian generates its nonzero socle. This exact published statement and the invoked Appendix proofs were fully read for E836.

If the two six-tuples were dependent over \(\mathbb C\), one would be a scalar multiple of the other, or one would vanish. Either possibility, together with their individual exterior squares being zero, would make (E838.14) zero. Thus they are independent. Section 2 now proves the following theorem.

**Quadratic-jet theorem.** If some pencil of leading brackets spans \(S_2/Q\), every formal perturbation with these regular quadratic jets is excluded from the two-deformation property of (E838.5), in every positive common multiplicity. Consequently no nontrivial point modification of this type can occur in the balanced weak-semiregular coherent-sheaf setting of E819, with its specified coherent finite translation/tensor action and arbitrary rational algebraic B shear.

## 4. An exact seven-coefficient residual test

Choose any ordered monomial basis of the ten-dimensional space \(S_2\). For the ordered four quadrics, form the ten-by-ten coefficient matrix whose first four columns are the \(F_i\) and whose last six columns are their brackets for \(a\pi_++b\pi_-\), in the order \(12,13,14,23,24,34\). Define

\[
 \mathcal D_F(a,b)
   =\det[\,F_1\ F_2\ F_3\ F_4\mid
       (\{F_i,F_j\}_{a\pi_++b\pi_-})_{i<j}\,]
   =\sum_{j=0}^{6}c_j(F)a^{6-j}b^j.
 \tag{E838.15}
\]

The pencil spans \(S_2/Q\) for some \((a,b)\) exactly when this polynomial is not identically zero. Each \(c_j\) is a homogeneous polynomial of degree sixteen in the forty coefficients of the ordered four quadrics: the first four columns have degree one, and the last six have degree two. If the chosen ordered basis of \(Q\) is changed by \(T\in\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb C)\), the first columns transform by \(T\) and the bracket columns by \(\Lambda^2T\), so

\[
 \mathcal D_{TF}(a,b)=(\det T)^4\mathcal D_F(a,b),
 \tag{E838.16}
\]

using \(\det(\Lambda^2T)=(\det T)^3\). The latter identity follows for diagonal matrices by counting the three occurrences of each eigenvalue, and then for every matrix by polynomial identity. A change of the ambient monomial basis contributes a common nonzero scalar as well.

Therefore the condition \(c_0=\cdots=c_6=0\) is basis independent on the Grassmannian \(\operatorname{Gr}(4,S_2)\). On each usual affine Grassmannian chart the seven coefficients are regular polynomial functions, of degree at most sixteen in its twenty-four coordinates. This is a closed residual locus relative to the regular-quadratic complete-intersection locus. The theorem proves only necessity of lying in it. It does not say the seven equations are independent, that this locus has codimension seven, or that any point in it admits a companion matrix or a module deformation.

## 5. Exact nonemptiness and strict new scope

Normalize \(\pi_+=\partial_{z_1}\wedge\partial_{z_2}\) and \(\pi_-=\partial_{z_3}\wedge\partial_{z_4}\). Let \(\ell=Jz\), with

\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&1&0\\
 0&1&0&1\\
 -3/5&1&-9/5&2\\
 -8/5&1&-16/5&3
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det J=-4/5,\qquad F_i=\ell_i^2.
 \tag{E838.17}
\]

Use the monomial basis \((z_1^2,z_2^2,z_3^2,z_4^2,z_1z_2,z_1z_3,z_1z_4,z_2z_3,z_2z_4,z_3z_4)\). The two alternating coefficient forms in the \(\ell\)-coordinates have pair coefficients

\[
 p=(1,1,1,3/5,8/5,1),\qquad
 q=(1,2,3,9/5,16/5,1).
 \tag{E838.18}
\]

In the corresponding \(\ell\)-monomial basis, the first four columns are the four square basis vectors, and the six bracket columns are diagonal with entries \(4(ap_{ij}+bq_{ij})\). Their determinant is \(4^6(24/25)(a+b)^2(a+2b)^2(a+3b)^2\). Passing to the \(z\)-monomial basis multiplies this by \(\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2J)=(\det J)^5=(-4/5)^5\). The exponent five is obtained on diagonal matrices by counting each variable twice in its square and once in each of three pair monomials; polynomial identity gives the general case. Thus

\[
 \mathcal D_F(a,b)
   =-\frac{100663296}{78125}
       (a+b)^2(a+2b)^2(a+3b)^2.
 \tag{E838.19}
\]

This is an exact rank certificate, also reconstructed directly by the adjacent symbolic checker. In particular both pure endpoints \(\mathcal D_F(1,0)\) and \(\mathcal D_F(0,1)\) are nonzero.

There is an equally explicit nonsquare witness. In these same mixed linear parameters put

\[
 F^\sharp_1=\ell_1^2+\ell_2\ell_3,\qquad
 F^\sharp_2=\ell_2^2,\quad F^\sharp_3=\ell_3^2,\quad
 F^\sharp_4=\ell_4^2.
 \tag{E838.20}
\]

Its quotient is a free module with basis \(1,\ell_1\) over
\(\mathbb C[\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4]/(\ell_2^2,\ell_3^2,\ell_4^2)\), because the remaining relation is monic of degree two in \(\ell_1\). Thus its length is sixteen, and it is a quadratic complete intersection. In its quadratic quotient use the six pair monomials as basis. For any constant alternating coefficients \(P_{ij}\) in the \(\ell\)-coordinates, its first three brackets reduce to

\[
 \{F^\sharp_1,F^\sharp_2\}=4P_{12}\ell_1\ell_2,\qquad
 \{F^\sharp_1,F^\sharp_3\}=4P_{13}\ell_1\ell_3,\qquad
 \{F^\sharp_1,F^\sharp_4\}
   =4P_{14}\ell_1\ell_4
      +2P_{24}\ell_3\ell_4+2P_{34}\ell_2\ell_4.
 \tag{E838.21}
\]

The last three brackets are \(4P_{ij}\ell_i\ell_j\), for \(2\le i<j\le4\). The resulting six-by-six matrix is triangular, with exactly the same six diagonal entries as for the four squares. Eliminating the first four columns of the ten-by-ten matrix contributes determinant one. Therefore \(\mathcal D_{F^\sharp}\) is exactly (E838.19).

The quadratic relation space of (E838.20) has only three projective rank-one quadrics. Indeed, a linear combination with nonzero coefficient \(c\) at \(F^\sharp_1\) has an isolated nonzero \(\ell_1^2\) entry and a nonzero \(c\ell_2\ell_3\) entry. If it were the square of a linear form, the nonzero \(\ell_1\) coefficient and the vanishing of all \(\ell_1\ell_j\) coefficients would force the other three coefficients of that linear form to vanish, contradicting the \(\ell_2\ell_3\) term. With \(c=0\), the diagonal quadratic has rank one only when exactly one of its three coefficients is nonzero. In contrast a coordinate-square space contains four independent rank-one quadrics. Hence (E838.20) is not in that orbit, and no formal parameter change or local-algebra isomorphism can turn its algebra into the square algebra: either map preserves the cotangent space and its quadratic relation space. This is an exact nonsquare example, independent of the dimension argument that follows.

For completeness, the regular-quadratic locus is open. It is exactly the locus on which the degree-five multiplication map \(S_3^{\oplus4}\to S_5\), \((g_i)\mapsto\sum F_i g_i\), is surjective. For a regular sequence this follows from (E838.11). Conversely surjectivity puts every monomial of degree five in the ideal, so its quotient is Artinian. A four-element parameter ideal in the four-dimensional regular local ring is a regular sequence; the precise standard source is Stacks Lemmas 10.106.3, tag 00NQ, and 10.104.2, tag 02JN, with the full proof in Proposition 10.103.4, tag 00N6. Homogeneity transfers regularity back to the graded polynomial algebra: a nonzero homogeneous kernel element would survive localization at the homogeneous maximal ideal. Surjectivity is the nonvanishing of a maximal matrix minor, hence is open. The witness (E838.17) lies in this locus.

The Grassmannian is irreducible of dimension \(4(10-4)=24\). Its standard matrix charts are affine spaces with twenty-four coordinates; the space of full-rank four-by-ten frames is an irreducible open subset of affine forty-space and maps surjectively to the Grassmannian, which proves irreducibility. These explicit chart constructions are also Stacks Lemma 27.22.1, tag 089R. Consequently the simultaneous conditions of regularity and nonvanishing of both pure endpoints define a nonempty open subset \(\mathcal U\) of dimension twenty-four.

The orbit of \(\operatorname{span}(z_1^2,z_2^2,z_3^2,z_4^2)\) under linear changes of the four variables has closure of dimension at most fifteen. One does not need an orbit-stabilizer classification: scalar matrices act trivially on this subspace, so on each of the sixteen charts in which a chosen matrix entry is nonzero, normalize that entry to one. The image is parametrized by at most fifteen algebraic coordinates. Its closure has dimension at most fifteen, since the coordinate functions of the image generate a field of transcendence degree at most fifteen. The finite union of these image closures has the same bound. It cannot contain the twenty-four-dimensional open set \(\mathcal U\).

It follows that \(\mathcal U\) contains quadratic spaces outside the closure of the coordinate-square orbit. For such a space, neither its homogeneous ideal nor any higher-order perturbation can be a formal coordinate-square ideal: the linear terms of four formal parameters would put its four-dimensional quadratic relation space in that orbit. The same observation rules out an abstract local-algebra isomorphism to the square algebra, since such an isomorphism preserves the cotangent space and its quadratic relations. Thus the theorem has strict scope beyond E837.

It also has strict scope beyond the E836 commuting-triple criterion for either original bivector. At either endpoint, nonvanishing of the determinant means that the bracket map \(\Lambda^2Q\to S_2/Q\) is an isomorphism. Any other minimal regular generator set has leading quadrics related by an invertible constant matrix. Three independent generators have a three-dimensional exterior-square space, which cannot be killed by this isomorphism. Hence no such presentation has its last three generators pairwise commuting modulo the ideal for either original bivector.

## 6. Global application, actual excluded objects, and boundaries

The global implication is exactly E819's reviewed one: invariant evaluation on each balanced cap-kernel class vanishes under weak semiregularity; forgetting the finite action and restricting to the completed local ring gives the two ordinary module deformations. An algebraic rational B shear preserves their bivector components. No equivariance of the individual quadratic generators or module bases is required.

The excluded family contains actual balanced coherent sheaves, before imposing semiregularity. Choose any homogeneous quadratic complete intersection in the nonempty open subset outside the square-orbit closure, form its length-sixteen subscheme \(Z\) at a point of the abelian fourfold, and take the rank-thirty-two bundle \(H\) of E815/E820 with entire character \(32v_{1/2}\). A finite formal quotient is an actual algebraic subscheme because the local ring and its completion have the same quotients by powers of the maximal ideal. For \(1\le m\le32\), choose \(H\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O_Z^m\), write \(K\) for its kernel, and take any extension \(0\to\mathcal O_Z^m\to E\to K\to0\). The four-generator Koszul resolution gives \(\dim\operatorname{Ext}^1(K,\mathcal O_Z^m)=6m^2\operatorname{length}Z=96m^2\). Its torsion, hull and hull quotient are the displayed ones, and its full character is \(32v_{1/2}\). It is ordinarily gluable as a coherent sheaf. The theorem excludes its weak balanced semiregularity; no finite action, simplicity, or successful seed is asserted.

Common complete intersections whose regular quadratic jets lie in the seven-coefficient residual locus are not decided by this criterion, although E836 or E837 can decide some of them. Ideals with higher initial degrees, unequal Artinian defect algebras, arbitrary Artinian defect modules, unstable torsion-free objects, and unrelated perfect complexes remain outside the new assertion. In particular no passage to a square quotient of a weighted power algebra is used. The exact supplement checks the determinant witness and finite algebra identities; it is not a finite verification of the all-size matrix lemma or of any geometric module deformation.
