# SF-E840: \(a\) principal-image condition at the rank-four boundary

Status: complete new author derivation; independent review pending. This strengthens the residual necessary condition of E839. The example below survives the scalar cross-minor equations and every rank-five pencil minor. No semiregular object is constructed.

## 1. Quadratic multiplication on \(a\) regular four-variable intersection

Let \(V\) be \(a\) four-dimensional complex vector space and let \(Q\) be \(a\) four-dimensional subspace of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)\) generated by \(a\) regular sequence of quadrics. Put

\[
 W=\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)/Q,
 \qquad \dim W=6,
 \qquad \mu:V\otimes V\longrightarrow W.
 \tag{E840.1}
\]

For every subspace \(L\) of \(V\) of dimension at least two,

\[
                     \dim\mu(VL)\ge5.
 \tag{E840.2}
\]

It suffices to take \(\dim L=2\). The degree-two ideal \(VL\) has dimension seven: its quotient is \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(V/L)\), of dimension three. If its image in \(W\) had dimension at most four, then \(\dim(Q\cap VL)\) would be at least three. Thus the four quadrics in \(Q\) restrict to \(a\) space of dimension at most one on the two-dimensional quotient \(V\)/\(L\). Such \(a\) collection has \(a\) common projective zero over \(\mathbf C\). Indeed, either every restriction is zero or they are multiples of one homogeneous binary quadratic, which has \(a\) zero in \(\mathbf P^1\). Lifting the corresponding quotient point gives \(a\) nonzero common zero of \(Q\) in \(V^*\), contradicting the regular-sequence, finite-length hypothesis. This proves (E840.2).

For \(a\) four-dimensional subspace \(J\) of \(W\) define the intrinsic vector space

\[
 K(J)=\{\ell\in V:\mu(\ell V)\subseteq J\}
      =\ker\bigl(V\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(V,W/J)\bigr).
 \tag{E840.3}
\]

Equation (E840.2) shows that \(\dim K(J)\) is at most one.

## 2. The equality case of the square-matrix bound

Let \(A\) be \(a\) commutative local complex Artinian algebra with maximal ideal \(n\). Assume its multiplication through degree two identifies \(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2\) with \(V\) and \(\mathfrak n^2/\mathfrak n^3\) with \(W\) as in (E840.1). Let \(C\) be \(a\) square matrix over \(A\), and suppose elements of \(\mathfrak n^2\) annihilate coker \(C\) and have leading classes spanning \(a\) four-dimensional \(J\) in \(W\).

If \(C\) is not invertible, then

\[
                   J=\mu(\ell V)
       \quad\text{for a unique line }\mathbf C\ell\subset V,
       \qquad \dim K(J)=1.
 \tag{E840.4}
\]

In particular the multiplication map \(V\) to \(W\) given by \(\ell\) is injective.

Here is the complete equality argument. Split unit pivots by invertible row and column operations. The cokernel is unchanged, and \(a\) nonempty residual square block \(C\)' of size \(k\) has entries in \(n\). Choose vector-space splittings modulo \(\mathfrak n^3\) and write \(C'=C_1+C_2\) there. Let

\[
 L_1:\mathbf C^k\longrightarrow V\otimes\mathbf C^k,
 \qquad U=\operatorname{im}L_1,\qquad a=\dim U.
 \tag{E840.5}
\]

The pure degree-two part of the image of \(C\)' modulo \(\mathfrak n^3\) is exactly

\[
             C_2(\ker L_1)+\mu(VU).
 \tag{E840.6}
\]

To verify this, write an input as \(a\) constant vector plus \(a\) vector of linear terms plus quadratic terms. The linear output is \(C_1\) applied to the constant vector, so it vanishes precisely when that constant lies in \(\ker L_1\). The quadratic output is then \(C_2\) on that constant plus multiplication of \(C_1\) with the linear input. Terms of degree at least two in the input contribute nothing modulo \(\mathfrak n^3\). This uses \(a\) vector-space splitting only, not \(a\) graded or algebra splitting of \(A\).

The annihilator hypothesis puts \(J\otimes\mathbf C^k\) inside (E840.6), because every generating annihilator times each constant target vector is an actual column image. Consequently

\[
 4k\le\dim\bigl(C_2(\ker L_1)+\mu(VU)\bigr)
       \le(k-a)+4a\le4k.
 \tag{E840.7}
\]

Every inequality is equality. Thus \(a=k\), \(\ker L_1=0\), and

\[
                     \mu(VU)=J\otimes\mathbf C^k.
 \tag{E840.8}
\]

Let \(L\) be the span in \(V\) of all contractions of \(U\) by linear functionals on \(\mathbf C^k\). Contracting (E840.8) shows that \(\mu(VL)\) is contained in \(J\). The space \(L\) is nonzero because \(U\) has dimension \(k\)>0. By (E840.2), \(L\) cannot have dimension at least two. Write \(L=\mathbf C\ell\). Then \(U\) is contained in \(\ell\otimes\mathbf C^k\), and both spaces have dimension \(k\), so they are equal. Equation (E840.8) gives \(J\)=\(\mu(\ell V)\). Its dimension is four, proving injectivity. Equation (E840.2) also proves uniqueness of the line and \(\dim K(J)\)=1.

All higher-order entries of \(C\) are allowed. No quotient of \(a\) deformation or base change of an Ext group has been made.

## 3. Necessary condition for actual balanced point modifications

Use the precise geometric setup of E819 and E836: \(a\) nontrivial finite modification on \(a\) genuine RM fourfold with locally free hull, whose local torsion and hull quotient are respectively \(A^m\) and \(A^n\) for the same complete-intersection Artin algebra. Weak balanced middle semiregularity, with the allowed coherent finite translation/tensor action and rational algebraic B-field, gives the ordinary vanishings for both RM bivectors after the already proved invariant-to-ordinary and B-shear steps. The finite-perfect cone and supertrace in E819 force \(m=n>0\).

Suppose the ideal has regular quadratic initial forms \(Q\); arbitrary higher terms are permitted. E838 identifies the associated graded algebra with the homogeneous regular intersection. E836 supplies common extension matrices B_ij and square companion matrices C_+,C_- satisfying both orders of the actual equations

\[
 C_\pm B_{ij}=B_{ij}C_\pm=P_{\pm,ij}I_m.
 \tag{E840.9}
\]

Here P are the brackets of the actual ideal generators. Their degree-two initial tuples \(P_+^0\) and \(P_-^0\) are linearly independent in \(W^6\). For completeness, the homogeneous Jacobian calculation gives their two self-wedges zero, while their mixed wedge is \(\det(\operatorname{Jac}Q)\) times the nonzero transverse constant bivector wedge. The Jacobian generates the nonzero socle of the homogeneous characteristic-zero complete intersection. Thus the tuples cannot be proportional. This is exactly the published Eisenbud–Ulrich socle input and full proof scope already read in E836/E838, not \(a\) new claim about arbitrary nonhomogeneous initial forms.

For \(a\) nonzero pair (\(a\),\(b\)), form the linear combinations \(C_0=aC_++bC_-\) and \(P_0=aP_++bP_-\). The two already existing companion equations give

\[
                         C_0B_{ij}=P_{0,ij}I_m.
 \tag{E840.10}
\]

This does not assume \(a\) separate deformation in \(a\) third pencil direction.

If the degree-two tuple \(P_0\) has rank four, let \(J\) be its span in \(W\). The matrix \(C_0\) cannot be \(a\) unit. Otherwise \(B_{ij}=P_{0,ij}C_0^{-1}\), and the other companion equations give \(D_\pm P_{0,ij}=P_{\pm,ij}I\) with \(D_\pm=C_\pm C_0^{-1}\). Passing to degree two uses only the constant matrices \(D_\pm(0)\). \(A\) functional nonzero on some \(P_{0,ij}\) forces each constant matrix to be scalar, and then all six equations make both complete initial tuples proportional to \(P_0\), contrary to their independence.

Equation (E840.10) annihilates coker \(C_0\) by the six \(P_{0,ij}\). Applying the equality lemma therefore proves the new necessary condition:

\[
 \boxed{\text{For every rank-four initial pencil direction,}
        \quad J=\ell V\text{ in }W,
        \quad\dim K(J)=1.}
 \tag{E840.11}
\]

If \(K(J)=0\) in even one rank-four direction, weak balanced middle semiregularity is impossible for every multiplicity \(m\). This condition is intrinsic under changes of the relation basis and local coordinates. It can be tested by the ordinary linear map in (E840.3); no choice of \(a\) matrix companion is required for the test. No codimension or sufficiency statement is made for the remaining locus.

## 4. An exact rank-four witness surviving the previous scalar equations

Use formal parameters \(x\),\(y\),\(z\),\(w\) and the regular quadratic intersection

\[
 A=\mathbf C[x,y,z,w]/(x^2+yw,y^2,z^2,w^2).
 \tag{E840.12}
\]

It has length sixteen: it is free with basis 1,\(x\) over \(\mathbf C[y,z,w]/(y^2,z^2,w^2)\). In particular \(xyzw\) is nonzero. The six squarefree degree-two monomials form \(a\) basis of \(W\).

In these parameter coordinates choose the two constant decomposable transverse bivectors, in the order 12,13,14,23,24,34,

\[
 p=(1,1,0,0,1,1),\qquad
 q=(1,-1,0,0,-1,1),\qquad p\wedge q=4.
 \tag{E840.13}
\]

These are actual transforms of the two standard block bivectors. The four columns

\[
 (1,0,0,-1),\quad(0,1,1,0),\quad
 (1,0,0,1),\quad(0,1,-1,0)
 \tag{E840.14}
\]

form an invertible matrix of determinant four; the first and second column wedges are \(p\), and the third and fourth column wedges are \(q\). Thus the construction is compatible with genuine transverse RM Poisson coordinates and does not use nonintegrable prescribed gradients.

For the actual four generators in (E840.12), direct differentiation and reduction give the entire pencil

\[
 P(a,b)=\bigl(4(a+b)xy,\;
 4(a-b)xz-2(a+b)yz,\;0,\;0,\;
 4(a-b)yw,\;4(a+b)zw\bigr).
 \tag{E840.15}
\]

Its rank is four when \((a+b)(a-b)\) is nonzero. It is three when \(a=b\) is nonzero and two when \(a=-b\) is nonzero. Hence every five-minor and the full determinant vanish identically. The pure endpoints both have rank four.

All scalar cross minors

\[
             P_{+,ij}P_{-,kl}-P_{-,ij}P_{+,kl}
 \tag{E840.16}
\]

vanish in \(A\). Indeed, the only nonzero products among \(xy\),\(xz\),\(yw\),\(zw\) are \(xy\) times \(zw\) and \(xz\) times \(yw\), both equal to \(xyzw\). The coefficients of each complementary pair have the same plus-to-minus ratio. The extra \(yz\) term annihilates each of those four monomials and itself. This verifies every ordered pair, including the zero tuple entries. Nevertheless the mixed wedge is \(64xyzw\), and \(\det(\operatorname{Jac}Q)\)=\(16xyzw\), both nonzero.

At the plus endpoint the image is

\[
                   J=\langle xy,\;2xz-yz,\;yw,\;zw\rangle.
 \tag{E840.17}
\]

For \(\ell=\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z+\delta w\), require \(\ell V\) to be in \(J\). The quotient \(W\)/\(J\) is detected by the \(xw\) coefficient and by the functional taking \(xz\) coefficient plus twice the \(yz\) coefficient. The products \(\ell x\) and \(\ell w\) force \(\delta=\alpha=0\). The second functional on \(\ell x\) forces \(\gamma=0\), and its value on \(\ell z\) then forces \(\beta=0\). Thus \(K(J)=0\). The new criterion excludes this example and every higher-order perturbation having these quadratic initial forms.

## 5. Strictness and actual examples

This witness is outside the earlier families for precise invariant reasons.

First, the relation space has exactly three projective rank-one quadrics. \(A\) general relation is \(\alpha(x^2+yw)+\beta y^2+\gamma z^2+\delta w^2\). \(A\) two-by-two minor involving the \(x^2\) and \(yw\) entries forces \(\alpha=0\) in rank one; the remaining diagonal expression has precisely the three coordinate-square lines. \(A\) coordinate-square regular intersection has four such lines, so no formal coordinate change identifies this algebra with E837's family.

Second, each pure bracket map from the six-dimensional exterior square of the relation space has rank four and kernel dimension two. The exterior square of any independent triple of minimal generators has dimension three, and hence cannot lie in that kernel. Thus no minimal presentation has the commuting triple required in E836, for either pure bivector.

Third, (E840.15) satisfies the identically zero determinant condition of E838 and all rank-five minors of E839. Equation (E840.16) also satisfies the scalar companion consequences, so the new obstruction is not merely \(a\) re-use of \(a\) failed cross-minor test.

Actual coherent examples with these defects exist by the already explicit balanced-character hull construction in E838/E839. Choose the local length-sixteen subscheme with (E840.12) at \(a\) point, \(a\) locally free balanced-character hull \(H\), and \(a\) surjection \(H\) to \(A^m\) with \(m\) at most rank \(H\). Let \(K\) be its kernel. Extensions

\[
                      0\longrightarrow A^m
                      \longrightarrow E\longrightarrow K
                      \longrightarrow0
 \tag{E840.18}
\]

have character \(\operatorname{ch}(E)=\operatorname{ch}(H)\), torsion \(A^m\), double dual \(H\), and hull quotient \(A^m\). The extension space has dimension \(6\cdot16\cdot m^2\), by the complete-intersection Koszul resolution and the vanishing of positive Ext from the locally free hull to the point-supported module. These are actual coherent, ordinarily gluable objects. The theorem excludes weak balanced middle semiregularity for every such extension; no simplicity or successful finite action is asserted.
