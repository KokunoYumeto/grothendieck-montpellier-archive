## D-OL16: the nonsquare involution

Its trace is the original polar form, so it is nondegenerate. The finite-field classification therefore gives a hyperbolic extension-field pair. Its two extension-field lines are opposite, invariant, totally singular two-dimensional spaces over the original field. This constructs the two-plane parabolic used in the argument; it does not presume an invariant singular line.

I checked the entire matrix description of its unipotent radical. Multiplying the displayed matrices gives exactly the alternating central correction in (EO.4), and the center consists of the skew two-by-two block. The resulting alternating form on the `2N`-dimensional quotient is nondegenerate. The generation argument checks without any module-extension hypothesis. The two opposite two-plane radicals contain the opposite full line radicals on the smaller orthogonal summand. Their generated group moves the singular parameters appearing in the center, and the other rows supply all parameters from the complement. This recovers the full opposite radicals for the first hyperbolic line, which generate the whole natural Omega group. The two original radicals are conjugate inside Omega: after interchanging their hyperbolic pairs, determinant and spinor norm can both be corrected on the large complement. Consequently their invariant spaces have the same dimension and zero intersection in a nontrivial irreducible module, giving `t0 <= a/2`.

The exact conjugation action is `X -> A X h^{-1}` and `Z -> mu^{-1} A Z A^t`. On the one-dimensional center its scalar is `det(A)/mu=-epsilon`. I checked the orbit estimate for those rank-two weights, including degenerate prescribed Gram matrices. Witt extension applies to the ordered pair even when its span is degenerate. An isometry extending that map can be corrected to Omega while fixing the pair: a nondegenerate perpendicular space of dimension at least `N-4` supplies all determinant/spinor combinations. The first-vector lower bound and the affine second-vector lower bound in the proof are valid. Thus no nonsingular-pair assumption is hidden in the count. Each factor exceeds half its leading term for odd `q>=3`, `N>=20`, so the whole orbit has more than `q^(2N-3)/4` elements. ## D-OL18: the actual source contradiction

I checked the complete passage from factor support to inertia in the actual inducing representation. The ordinary degree bound is applied to natural Omega modules, for which the cited published GLT theorem applies. It is not applied to faithful spin modules that fail to descend. Its `k=1` case gives `a_i>=q_i^(d_i-4)(q_i-1)>360`, so the product degree has the needed lower bound.

Factor permutations, scalar operators, and factor-preserving operators are treated separately. In the bad range, each active factor has `q_i=3` and is a projective reflection. Two active involutions already have support at least `2beta(1-beta)>1/4>delta`, regardless of their multiplicity proportions. Therefore every bad inertia has exactly one active factor. Its scalar is also constrained by its actual fixed space; projective support is not substituted for moving rank without this step.

The Euler identity `6=2e(h-1)+D` then forces `h<=1`. In genus one there are at most six marked points, so the base-family dimension is at most six. A rank-nine source must consequently have genus zero and at least twelve marked points. The two exact inequalities `13beta-2>1/7` and `11beta+delta-2>1/60`, combined with `e>360`, exclude thirteen marked points and exclude any good inertia among twelve. Thus the putative rank-nine case has exactly twelve bad inertias.

On a sphere these actual inertia elements generate the actual inducing group. All are factor preserving, and each projects to either the identity or a natural reflection on each fixed normal factor. The projection contains that factor's inner group because the factor is contained in the inducing group. Twelve natural reflections have a common fixed space of dimension at least `d_i-12>0`. Any natural Omega element projectively represented by a word in them acts by an original-field scalar on that space. These scalars form a homomorphism from the perfect group Omega to the multiplicative group of the field, so they are all one. This would give a nonzero vector fixed by Omega. Its opposite line unipotents have different fixed lines and generate Omega, ruling this out. A factor with field different from `F3` gives an immediate trivial-projection contradiction instead.

This proves the exact claimed source exclusion. It does not assert that a dimension count alone proves dominance or nondominance for any other family, and it makes no statement about the remaining faithful-spin cases.
