                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                              Suppose X is a smooth projective connected curve of genus g ≥ 2 defined over an al-
                                           gebraically closed field k of characteristic p > 0. Suppose π : Y → X is an unramified
                                           cyclic cover of degree ` for some prime ` 6= p. Then Y has genus gY = `(g − 1) + 1 by the
                                           Riemann-Hurwitz formula. For each of the `2g − 1 unramified Z/`-covers π : Y → X, the
                                           Jacobian JY is isogenous to JX ⊕ Pπ for an abelian variety Pπ of dimension (` − 1)(g − 1),
                                           called the Prym variety of π. In particular, when ` = 2 and π : Y → X is an unramified
                                           double cover, then Y has genus 2g − 1 and Pπ is a principally polarized abelian variety of
                                           dimension g − 1.
                                              In this paper, we study the relationship between the p-ranks of JX and Pπ . The p-rank fA
                                           of an abelian variety A/k of dimension gA is the integer 0 ≤ fA ≤ gA such that the number
                                           of p-torsion points in A(k) is pfA . One says that A is ordinary if its p-rank is as large as
                                           possible (fA = gA ) and is almost ordinary if its p-rank equals gA − 1.
                                              Consider the moduli space Mg whose points represent smooth curves X of genus g and
                                           the moduli space Rg,` whose points represent unramified Z/`-covers π : Y → X. There is a
                                           finite flat morphism of degree `2g − 1, denoted
                                                                                      Π` : Rg,` → Mg ,
                                           which takes the point representing a cover π : Y → X to the point representing the curve X
                                           [8, Page 6]. For 0 ≤ f ≤ g, let Mfg denote the p-rank f stratum of Mg . For most g and f ,
                                           it is not known whether Mfg is irreducible; however, every component of Mfg has dimension
                                           2g − 3 + f [9, Theorem 2.3].

curve X/k of genus g ≥ 2 are ordinary [15, Theorem 2]. In other words, the cover represented
by the generic point of Rg,` has an ordinary Prym.
  The first theorem in the paper generalizes Nakajima’s result by adding a condition on the
p-rank of X. Specifically, if X is a generic k-curve of genus g and p-rank f , then Theorem
1.1 implies that the Prym varieties of all of the unramified Z/`-covers of X are ordinary.

the p-rank stratification of Mg . In Section 3, we analyze the p-ranks of Pryms of covers of
singular curves and the p-rank stratification of the boundary of Rg,` .
   Section 4 contains the proof of Theorem 1.1(1). The proof mirrors Nakajima’s technique of

X → S is as large as possible, namely Sp2g (Z/`) [1, Theorem 4.5]. We use this to prove that

about the p-rank stratification of Rg,` .
   Section 8 contains the definition of the theta divisor ΘX and the proofs of Theorems 1.1(2)
and 1.2(2). We then compare our results with those of Raynaud [17, 18] and Pop/Saidi [16].
Briefly, Raynaud’s results are stronger in that they apply to an arbitrary base curve X but are
weaker in other ways: his result about ordinary Pryms applies only when ` > (p − 1)3g−1 g!,
in which case he shows that at least one of the Pryms is ordinary; and, in his result for
non-ordinary Pryms, the Galois group of the cover is solvable but not cyclic and the p-rank
of the Prym is not determined. In [16, Proposition 2.3], the result is stronger in that it
applies to an arbitrary curve which is either non-ordinary or whose Jacobian is absolutely
simple, but is weaker in that the degree of the cyclic unramified cover and the p-rank of the
Prym are not determined.
   Section 9 contains some open questions. For example, in Section 9.1, we illustrate the
difficulty in proving these results computationally, even for g = 2 and a fixed small prime p.
See [4] for results about p-ranks of ramified cyclic covers of curves.

                   2. Prym varieties and p-rank stratifications
   Suppose X is a smooth projective curve of genus g defined over k. The Jacobian JX of X
is a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension g. A Z/`-cover π : Y → X is a Galois
cover together with an isomorphism ι : Gal(Y /X) → Z/`. For a prime ` 6= p, there is a
bijection between points of order ` on JX and unramified connected Z/`-covers π : Y → X.
2.1. Prym varieties. Suppose π : Y → X is an unramified Z/`-cover. The Prym variety
Pπ is the connected component containing 0 of the norm map on Jacobians. More precisely,
if σ is the endomorphism of JY induced by a generator σ of Gal(Y /X), then
                         Pπ = Im(1 − σ) = ker(1 + σ + . . . + σ `−1 )0 .
The canonical principal polarization of JY induces a polarization on Pπ [14, Page 6]. This
polarization is principal when ` = 2.
2.2. Moduli spaces of unramified cyclic covers. Let Rg,` denote the moduli space whose
points represent unramified Z/`-covers of smooth projective curves of genus g; it is a smooth
Deligne-Mumford stack [7, Page 5].

2.4. The p-rank. Let µp be the kernel of Frobenius on Gm . The p-rank of a semi-abelian
variety A0 is fA0 = dimFp Hom(µp , A0 ). If A0 is an extension of an abelian variety A by a torus
T , then fA0 = fA + rank(T ).
   For an abelian variety A, the p-rank can also be defined as the integer fA such that the
number of p-torsion points in A(k) is pfA . If A has dimension gA then 0 ≤ fA ≤ gA . The
p-rank is invariant under isogeny ∼ of abelian varieties.
   The p-rank of a stable curve X is that of Pic0 (X). There are three p-ranks associated
with an unramified cover π : Y → X, namely the p-rank f of X, the p-rank f 0 of Pπ , and
the p-rank of Y which equals f + f 0 .

2.5. The p-rank stratification. If X/S is a semi-abelian scheme over a Deligne-Mumford
stack, there is a stratification S = ∪S f by locally closed reduced substacks such that s ∈
S f (k) if and only if f (Xs ) = f ([13, Theorem 2.3.1], see also [1, Lemma 2.1]). So, Mfg is
the locally closed reduced substack of Mg whose points represent smooth curves of genus g
with p-rank f .

8.1. Background. Recall the definition of the theta divisor from [19, Section 1.1]. Given a
relative curve X/S, let X 1 be the curve induced by base change by the absolute Frobenius
of S. Consider the relative Frobenius morphism F : X → X 1 . The sheaf of locally exact
differentials B is the image of F∗ d : F∗ (OX ) → F∗ (Ω1X ). There is an exact sequence of
OX 1 -modules:
                                                     ∗    F d
                                 0 → OX 1 → F∗ (OX ) → B → 0.
Also, B is the kernel of the Cartier operator C : F∗ (Ω1X ) → Ω1X 1 , and there is an exact
sequence of OX 1 -modules:
                                                      C
                                 0 → B → F∗ (Ω1X ) → Ω1X 1 → 0.
Now B is a vector bundle on X 1 of rank p − 1 and slope g − 1, where the slope is the quotient
of the degree by the rank. More precisely, if X 1 is not smooth, then B is a torsion-free sheaf,
which is locally free of rank p − 1 outside the singularities of X 1 .
   By [17, Theorem 4.1.1], B admits a theta divisor ΘX . This is a positive Cartier divisor on
the Jacobian J 1 of X 1 (the determinant of the universal cohomology). A point a ∈ J 1 (k) is
in the support of ΘX if and only if H 0 (X 1 , B ⊗ La ) 6= 0 where La ∈ Pic0 (X 1 ) is the invertible
sheaf identified with a.
