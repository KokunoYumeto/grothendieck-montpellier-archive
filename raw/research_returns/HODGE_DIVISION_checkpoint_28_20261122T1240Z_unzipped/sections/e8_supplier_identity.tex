\subsection{HD-G18: the actual-group supplier identity}
\label{sec:e8-actual-suppliers}

\paragraph{Statement and conventions.}
For the actual twelve-point cover of HD-I17, HD-G17 and HD-S22,
the determinant coordinate of its natural eight-dimensional representation
is the integral combination of 95 ordinary cyclic suppliers and three
balanced product suppliers specified below. Every subgroup, cyclic
character, branch profile and coordinate vector in this assertion is
reconstructed from the actual group. The ordinary suppliers satisfy the
simple-branch condition used in HD-S25. The product suppliers have the
positive slot profiles required in HD-R01 and HD-N15.

Write \(z=iI_8\), \(W=W(E_8)\), and
\(G=W\cup zW\), with \(|W|=696729600\).
The tuple is exactly
\[
 (ia^5,a^{-4}ba^4,a^{-4}ca^4,\ldots,a^{-1}ba,a^{-1}ca,b,c,-id^5),
 \qquad d=(abc)^{-1}.
\]
All class calculations below use this tuple. In particular its last
entry is \(-id^5\). Its class multiset, in the numbering certified below,
is \(( (1,6),(0,67)^{10},(1,58))\), where \((t,c)\) denotes
\(i^tw\) for a real Weyl element in class \(c\), with \(t=0,1\).

Choose the regular fibre as \(G\), with right monodromy and left
deck maps \(L_h:g\mapsto hg\), exactly as in HD-S23.
Write \(a_h=L_{h,*}\) for the induced pushforward on the Jacobian
and \(T_h=(L_h^{-1})^*\) on \(H^1(C)\). Both are genuine
representations. Under the Abel-map identification of cohomology,
\(a_h^*\) corresponds to \(L_h^*=T_{h^{-1}}\).
For the coefficient functions
\[
 j_{v,x}(g)=h_0(v,\rho(g)x)/2
\]
one has \(T_hj_{v,x}=j_{\rho(h)v,x}\). Their dependence on \(v\)
is conjugate-linear, so their deck representation is \(\bar\rho\),
whereas right monodromy acts as \(\rho\) on \(x\).
In particular \(T_zj_{v,x}=-ij_{v,x}\). This gives the exact
representation comparison to the local-system factor in HD-S23.

\paragraph{The exact matrix--permutation map and character table.}
The group computation uses the 480 vectors \(R\cup iR\), with \(R\)
the 240 roots in the simple-root basis. Let \(p(M)\) be the permutation
obtained by left multiplication of these column vectors by \(M\).
GAP multiplies permutations as right actions, so
\[
 p(MN)=p(N)p(M).
\]
Consequently the map from the fundamental-weight matrices of HD-S22 is
the genuine homomorphism
\[
                 g\longmapsto p(g^t).
\]
The representation of this permutation group is \(p(M)\mapsto M^t\).
Its composition with the displayed homomorphism is literally \(g\mapsto g\),
including the scalar \(i\). The program
\path{check_actual_bridge.g} verifies membership of all twelve
images, the group orders, product one, and literal recovery of every
matrix; it also checks the multiplication law on the generators.
There is no replacement of a transpose by an inverse.

The initial class labels were candidate data from the attributed archive.
They are verified as follows. Compute all 112 conjugacy classes of the
actual root permutation group. Their element orders, cycle structures
on the roots and characteristic polynomials give distinct labels.
Check each class size, each natural trace and every power against the
labelled table. Then compute \emph{all} irreducible characters of the
actual group, independently of the candidate class labels. After the
computed class fusion, their set agrees exactly with the 112 rows of
the library table \path{W(E8)}. This last comparison, saved in
\path{character_fusion_result.g}, verifies the whole table fusion,
not only a few invariants. The runtime is GAP 4.14 with CTblLib 1.3.9
and SmallGrp 1.5.4. The precise algorithmic reference is the GAP
Reference Manual, Sections 71.14--71.17, especially 71.14-1
\path{IrrDixonSchneider}; the local implementation is
\path{lib/ctblgrp.gi}. The computation uses exact finite-group and
cyclotomic arithmetic.

\paragraph{All determinant coordinates are retained.}
For every actual irreducible character \(\sigma\) of \(W\), the freshly
computed number
\[
 \nu(\sigma)=\frac1{|W|}\sum_{w\in W}\sigma(w^2)
\]
is 1. To see the consequence directly, the difference between the
dimensions of the invariant symmetric and alternating bilinear forms
on an irreducible is \(\nu(\sigma)\); this follows from the character
formulas for \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\) and \(\bigwedge^2\).
Their sum is one for a self-dual irreducible. Hence its invariant form
is symmetric. The irreducibles of \(G\) are
\(\tau_{\sigma,j}(i^tw)=i^{jt}\sigma(w)\), where
\(\sigma(-1)=(-1)^j\sigma(1)\). The even \(j\) factors remain
orthogonal on the representation space, and the alternating polarization
therefore gives symplectic groups on their multiplicity spaces. The
odd \(j\) factors occur in conjugate pairs and give general linear
groups after complexification. Thus the full symplectic centralizer
has no omitted orthogonal determinant component.

The equivariant cellular Euler characteristic gives, for a nontrivial
\(\tau\),
\[
 m_\tau=-2\dim\tau+
       \sum_{j=1}^{12}\bigl(\dim\tau-\dim\tau^{g_j}\bigr).
\]
Indeed a tree joining the branch points has one complementary face;
the vertex modules are the inertia permutation modules, while edges
and faces are regular modules. Also \(H^0\) and \(H^2\) are trivial.
The trivial multiplicity in \(H^1\) is zero.
Exactly 45 conjugate pairs with odd \(j\) occur, and all have positive
multiplicity. Their \(j=1\) Weyl-character indices are
\(68,69,\ldots,112\). Index 69 is the natural representation and has
multiplicity six. Accordingly its determinant is coordinate 2 of
\(\mathbb Z^{45}\).

For a subgroup \(H\leq G\) and a linear character \(\chi\), put
\(V_\chi=\operatorname{Hom}_H(\chi,H^1(C,\mathbb C))\). Its
determinant character on the preceding full centralizer is the vector
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G18-row}
 a_\sigma(H,\chi)=
 \langle\tau_{\sigma,1}|_H,\chi\rangle
 -\langle\tau_{\sigma,3}|_H,\chi\rangle.
\end{equation}
This follows by decomposing \(H^1(C)\) into representation and
multiplicity spaces. The polarization identifies paired multiplicity
spaces as duals; the symplectic factors have determinant one.
Thus \eqref{eq:G18-row} gives the actual determinant character,
with an explicit representation map, rather than a character analogy.
Pullback and trace for \(C\to C/\ker\chi\) identify \(V_\chi\)
with the corresponding cyclic-cover eigenspace: their composite is
the degree, and averaging over \(\ker\chi\) is its invariant projector.

\paragraph{Reconstructing every branch profile.}
Let \(m=\operatorname{ord}\chi\), \(N=[G:H]\), and write
\(\chi(h)=\zeta_m^{k(h)}\). The fibre of \(C/H\) is \(H\backslash G\),
with right monodromy. For a branch element \(g\) and a divisor
\(e\mid\operatorname{ord}(g)\), the number of fixed cosets of
\(g^e\) with return-character exponent \(k\) is
\[
 D_e(k)=\frac{|C_G(g^e)|}{|H|}
       \sum_{\substack{h\in H,\ h\sim_Gg^e\\k(h)=k}}1.
\]
The equality counts the elements \(x\) with \(xg^ex^{-1}=h\)
and divides by \(|H|\). For an orbit of exact length \(\ell\mid e\),
its return exponent is multiplied by \(e/\ell\). Subtracting the
contributions already found for proper divisors of \(e\) therefore
gives the numbers of points in exact-length \(e\) orbits, separately
for each return exponent. Each such number is divisible by \(e\);
division gives the number of quotient points. Summing over the twelve
branches yields integers \(c_k\). All these divisibilities and
nonnegativities are checked.
With \(c=\sum_kc_k\), Riemann--Hurwitz and the cyclic Euler
characteristic give
\[
 q=g(C/H)=\frac{-2N+12N-c+2}{2},\qquad
 s=\sum_{k\ne0}c_k,\qquad h=2q-2+s.
\]
The evaluator independently checks \(h\) against the restriction of
the full \(G\)-character of \(H^1(C)\).
For the ordinary suppliers it checks
\[
 c_k=c_{m-k}\quad(1\le k<m),\qquad
 c_{m/2}\equiv0\pmod2\quad(m\text{ even}),
\]
or the unramified case \(s=0,q\ge2\); in every case \(h\ge2\)
is even. These are precisely the branch-pairing conditions used in
HD-S25, with all unramified cases included.

\paragraph{The three positive product profiles.}
For Weyl classes \(8,33,55\), construct
\(H=\langle i\rangle C_W(w)\), compute all nonzero vectors
\eqref{eq:G18-row} of linear characters, and solve the exact integer
equation for the selected target row. Every negative coefficient is
replaced by its positive opposite and the inverse character.
This preserves the vector because \(a(H,\bar\chi)=-a(H,\chi)\).
For an embedding \(t\in\{1,5,7,11\}\) of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\),
the cyclic Riemann--Roch formulas give the ordered pair
\[
 (h^{1,0}_{\chi^t},h^{0,1}_{\chi^t})
 =\left(q-1+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}c_k\left\{\frac{tk}{m}\right\},
        q-1+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}c_k\left\{-\frac{tk}{m}\right\}\right).
\]
Here the character is for \(T_h=(L_h^{-1})^*\), which fixes the
displayed order of the two Hodge numbers. All slot orders divide 12.
These formulas follow by taking the
cyclic eigensheaves on the quotient: the local valuation correction
is the displayed fractional part, their degrees are the sums of
these corrections, and the nontrivial eigenspaces have no constant
sections. Serre duality exchanges the two characters.
Every computed entry is an integer, nonnegative, and the pair sums to
\(h\). The totals are equal at all four embeddings, not merely at
the distinguished Gaussian embedding.

Write \(R=\sum n_j\), \(H=\sum n_jh_j\), and
\(S=\sum n_j(s_j-2)\), where \(n_j>0\) is a slot multiplicity.
The resulting exact profiles are
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|r|r|r}
\text{row}&q&R&H&S&\sum n_js_j\\\hline
3231&3259&2&19784&6748&6752\\
3244&1730347&45&156341088&609858&609948\\
3269&5341793&4&42739160&4816&4824
\end{array}
\]
All slots in a row have its displayed quotient genus, satisfy
\(q\ge1,s_j\ge2\), and obey \(H=2qR+S\).
At each of the four embeddings the respective Hodge totals are
\((9892,9892)\), \((78170544,78170544)\), and
\((21369580,21369580)\).
The complete positive slots, including generators and character
exponents, are in \path{gale8_profile.json},
\path{gale33_profile.json}, and \path{gale55_profile.json}.
There are respectively 2,6,4 distinct slots, with multiplicities
\((1,1)\), \((1,11,1,20,11,1)\), and \((1,1,1,1)\).

\paragraph{The integral identity and its target.}
The coefficient data are attributed to B.\ Severn's CC BY 4.0
archive, DOI \path{10.5281/zenodo.23549514}; they are used only as
literal integer candidates. The actual downloaded witness has SHA256
\begin{center}\small\texttt{93a68c0760a10eb8\allowbreak 4f2d0760a258f660\allowbreak 75c25aaa230c82ae\allowbreak 33c395ded6fcf3ba}\end{center}
The checker parses its integer lists without executing downloaded code,
replaces every selected vector by the freshly reconstructed vector above,
and verifies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G18-integer-identity}
             \sum_{\nu=1}^{98} b_\nu a_\nu=(0,1,0,\ldots,0).
\end{equation}
Every \(b_\nu\) is a nonzero integer. The exact value of
\(\sum|b_\nu|\) is stored in \path{complete_identity.json}; it has
755 decimal digits and is strictly less than \(10^{755}\).
The checker also rechecks the positive slot sums and all Hodge totals.

Finally, use the Jacobian action \(a_h\) fixed above. Since the
idempotent satisfies \(e=e^*\), its pullback on cohomology is
\(a_e^*=T_{e^*}=T_e\). Thus its primitive corner is the same
rational summand used in HD-S22--HD-S23.
For its inclusion \(j:B\hookrightarrow J(C)\) and the actual map
\(q=Da_e:J(C)\to B\), one has
\(qj=[D]_B\) and \(jq=Da_e\).
The specified integral action is \(i_B=a_z^{-1}\), so its
pullback on cohomology is \(i_B^*=T_z\).
The \(\rho\) determinant coordinate is therefore the \(+i\)
embedding; the local-system factor \(j_u\) in HD-S23 is its
\(-i\) conjugate. This also verifies the homological convention
without altering the polarization scalar: if
\(A_u=j_u+\bar j_u\), then \(A_{iu}=T_z^{-1}A_u\), and naturality
of Poincar\'e duality gives
\[
 \Phi(iu)=\operatorname{PD}(T_z^{-1}A_u)
         =(a_z)_*^{-1}\Phi(u)=(i_B)_*\Phi(u).
\]
Consequently the \(K\)-linear \(\Phi\), the Hermitian Gram matrix
\(DH\), and the maps \(q,j\) of HD-S23 retain exactly their stated
values. Coordinate 2 in
\eqref{eq:G18-integer-identity} is exactly one of the two lines in
\(\bigwedge_K^6H^1(B,\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C\).
Complex conjugation exchanges it with the other line and sends every
supplier character to its inverse; it is the explicit field-embedding
map relating the two lines. The tuple is unchanged.
