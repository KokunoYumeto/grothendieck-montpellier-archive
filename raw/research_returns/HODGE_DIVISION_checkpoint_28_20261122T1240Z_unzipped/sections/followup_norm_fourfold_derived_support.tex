\section[HD-F1-S34: actual derived objects on the norm image]{HD-F1-S34: every actual derived pushforward from the norm image meeting the full S14 character has an invariant trace kernel}
\label{sec:f1-norm-fourfold-derived-support}

Retain $i:Y\hookrightarrow P$, $E=i_*\mathcal O_Y$,
$D=\mathfrak D_P$, and $W_E=\operatorname{ob}_E(D)$
on the geometric open of S29. The support $Y$ is
normal and integral, and $\dim W_E=16-r_C\ge4$.
Take an actual object
\[
 \mathcal G\in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(Y),\qquad
 F=Ri_*\mathcal G=i_*\mathcal G
                       \in D^b_{\mathrm{coh}}(P).
 \tag{F1S34.1}
\]
The functor is the derived functor of exact
restriction of scalars for the displayed closed
immersion. All differentials and extension maps
in $\mathcal G$ are retained.
Set
\[
 r=\sum_a(-1)^a\operatorname{rk}_Y
                         \mathcal H^a(\mathcal G)\in\mathbf Z.
 \tag{F1S34.2}
\]
For $r\ne0$ there is a well-defined surjection
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal T_{\mathcal G}:\quad
 \operatorname{ev}_F
       \bigl(D\oplus H^2(P,\mathcal O_P)\bigr)&\longrightarrow W_E,\\
 \operatorname{ev}_F(\kappa+\eta)&\longmapsto
                                  -r\,\operatorname{ob}_E(\kappa).
\end{split}
 \tag{F1S34.3}
\]
The complete S14 character
\[
 e^B\operatorname{ch}(F)=\lambda t+Q(\beta),\qquad
 B\in H^{1,1}(P,\mathbf Q),\quad
 0\ne\lambda t\in W_K(P),\quad Q\in\mathbf Q[x]
 \tag{F1S34.4}
\]
forces $r\ne0$. Every object (F1S34.1) satisfying
(F1S34.4) therefore has an invariant full
degree-two trace kernel of dimension at least
$16-r_C$ for every specified coherent finite
point lift. It fails the weak equivariant
semiregularity requirement.

\paragraph{1. An intrinsic supertrace of actual ambient endomorphisms.}
Let $U\subset P$ be open. An actual degree-zero
morphism
$b:F|_U\to F|_U$ in $D(U)$ induces endomorphisms
of
$\mathcal H^a(F|_U)=i_*\mathcal H^a(\mathcal G)|_U$.
Exact closed-immersion pushforward is fully
faithful on the abelian categories of sheaves,
so each is an actual endomorphism of
$\mathcal H^a(\mathcal G)|_{Y\cap U}$.
Apply the normal-support trace (F1S33.5) to these
endomorphisms and define
\[
 \operatorname{str}_Y(b)=
       \sum_a(-1)^a
       \operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal H^a(\mathcal G)}
                             \bigl(\mathcal H^a(b)\bigr)
           \in H^0(Y\cap U,\mathcal O_Y).
 \tag{F1S34.5}
\]
This is linear, commutes with restriction to
smaller opens, and is invariant under
conjugation by an actual derived isomorphism,
because the induced maps on cohomology are
sheaf isomorphisms.
For a section $\bar h$ of $\mathcal O_Y$,
the actual $\mathcal O_Y$-action in
(F1S34.1) gives
\[
 \operatorname{str}_Y(\bar h\,b)=
         \bar h\,\operatorname{str}_Y(b),\qquad
              \operatorname{str}_Y(1_F)=r.
 \tag{F1S34.6}
\]
These properties are checked on the generic
cohomology vector spaces and extend as regular
functions by the S33 normal-domain argument.
No identification of an ambient derived
endomorphism with an $\mathcal O_Y$-linear
derived endomorphism is used in (F1S34.5).

\paragraph{2. The actual epsilon triangle and derived adjunction.}
Suppose
$\operatorname{ev}_F(\kappa+\eta)=0$,
with $\kappa\in D$ and $\eta\in H^2(P,\mathcal O_P)$.
Since $P$ is smooth, $F$ is perfect on $P$.
Toda's Proposition~6.1, with the exact sign
comparison (F1S33.9)--(F1S33.10), gives an
actual perfect scalar-twisted lift on $P_\kappa$.
Work on one affine open where its algebra is
\[
 R_\varepsilon=R[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2),
 \qquad s:\operatorname{Spec}R\hookrightarrow
                         \operatorname{Spec}R_\varepsilon.
 \tag{F1S34.7}
\]
Denote the lifted perfect complex by $M$, with
the specified identification $Ls^*M=F_R$.
Tensoring the exact epsilon sequence with a
bounded finite free representative of $M$
gives the natural triangle
\[
 s_*F_R\xrightarrow{\,j\,}M\xrightarrow{\,p\,}
       s_*F_R\longrightarrow s_*F_R[1],
 \qquad pj=0,\qquad jp=\varepsilon\,1_M.
 \tag{F1S34.8}
\]
Indeed, on that representative $L^\bullet$ the
sequence is
$0\to\varepsilon L^\bullet\to L^\bullet
 \to L^\bullet/\varepsilon L^\bullet\to0$,
and multiplication by $\varepsilon$ identifies
the first and last complexes. These are
degree-zero maps with the displayed signs.
There is no assertion that the cohomology
sheaves of $M$ are flat.

Let $I\subset R$ be the ideal of $Y\cap U$.
For $f\in I$, multiplication by $f$ on $F_R$
is zero in $D(R)$: it is strictly zero on the
actual pushed-forward $\mathcal O_Y$-complex
in (F1S34.1).
Thus $p\mu_f=0$, where $\mu_f$ is multiplication
by the chosen constant lift of $f$ on $M$.
Exactness of $\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R_\varepsilon)}
(M,-)$ applied to (F1S34.8) gives
$\mu_f=j a_f$ for some $a_f:M\to s_*F_R$.
Now use the actual derived adjunction
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{D(R_\varepsilon)}(M,s_*F_R)
      =\operatorname{Hom}_{D(R)}(Ls^*M,F_R)
      =\operatorname{End}_{D(R)}(F_R).
 \tag{F1S34.9}
\]
It sends an endomorphism $b_f$ to $s_*b_f\circ p$.
For a bounded finite free representative of $M$,
this adjunction is the literal equality of
Hom complexes after reducing that representative
modulo $\varepsilon$.
Consequently
\[
       \mu_f=j\,s_*b_f\,p
       \quad\hbox{for some }
                b_f\in\operatorname{End}_{D(R)}(F_R).
 \tag{F1S34.10}
\]
Put $\Psi_M(b)=j\,s_*b\,p$. These are actual
derived composites.

\paragraph{3. The trace kills precisely the ambiguity needed here.}
We prove
\[
       \Psi_M(b)=0
              \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                      \operatorname{str}_Y(b)=0.
 \tag{F1S34.11}
\]
The function on the right is already a regular
function on $Y\cap U$ by (F1S34.5).
It is enough to verify its vanishing at the
generic point $\xi$ of the integral $Y$.
The following construction supplies an actual
flat ring map for that verification.

Set $R_0=\mathcal O_{P,\xi}$ and
$K=R_0/\mathfrak m=\mathbf C(Y)$.
Choose a transcendence basis
$t_1,\ldots,t_4$ of $K/\mathbf C$ and lifts in
$R_0$. They define a copy of
$K_0=\mathbf C(t_1,\ldots,t_4)$ in $R_0$:
a nonzero polynomial in the lifts has nonzero
residue and is therefore a unit when a
denominator must be inverted.
The extension $K/K_0$ is finite and separable.
Choose a primitive element $\alpha$ and its
monic separable minimal polynomial
$m_\alpha(T)\in K_0[T]$.
Let $\mathfrak q$ be the kernel of evaluation
onto $K$ in the following actual algebra:
\[
 A=\bigl(R_0[T]/(m_\alpha(T))\bigr)_{\mathfrak q},
 \qquad R_0[T]/(m_\alpha)\longrightarrow K,\quad
                         T\longmapsto\alpha.
 \tag{F1S34.12}
\]
The monic polynomial makes the unlocalized
algebra finite free over $R_0$; localization
then makes $R_0\to A$ flat.
The unital field map
$K=K_0[T]/(m_\alpha)\to A$ is injective.
Its composite with the residue map is the
identity on $K$.
Since $\alpha$ is a simple residue root,
\[
 A/\mathfrak m A
    =\bigl(K[T]/(m_\alpha(T))\bigr)_{(T-\alpha)}
    =K.
 \tag{F1S34.13}
\]
Thus the base change keeps the same generic
cohomology vector spaces and their actual
endomorphisms. It also supplies a coefficient
field $K$ without assuming a global retraction
of a neighborhood onto $Y$.

Let $M_A$ and $F_A$ be the actual flat base
changes. Write $K_\varepsilon=K[\varepsilon]/
(\varepsilon^2)$ and $A_\varepsilon=A\otimes_K
K_\varepsilon$.
The triangle (F1S34.8) becomes the same epsilon
triangle after these maps, and
$M_A\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{K_\varepsilon}K=F_A$.
The cohomology of $F_A$ consists of the
finite-dimensional vector spaces
$\mathcal H^a(\mathcal G)_\xi$.
The long exact sequence of (F1S34.8) therefore
shows that $M_A$ has bounded finite
$K_\varepsilon$-cohomology.
It has finite Tor amplitude over
$K_\varepsilon$ as well: a bounded finite free
$A_\varepsilon$-representative is a bounded
complex of flat $K_\varepsilon$-modules,
because $A$ is a $K$-vector space.
The complete perfectness criteria in Stacks
066E and 0658 now give
\[
              M_A\in\operatorname{Perf}(K_\varepsilon).
 \tag{F1S34.14}
\]

Choose a bounded finite free $K_\varepsilon$
complex $L^\bullet$ representing it, with all
its differentials retained, and put
$Q^\bullet=L^\bullet/\varepsilon L^\bullet$.
The transported endomorphism $b$ of $F_A$
is represented by an actual chain map
$B^\bullet:Q^\bullet\to Q^\bullet$, because
$Q^\bullet$ is a bounded complex of vector
spaces over $K$.
The maps $j,p$ are multiplication by
$\varepsilon$ and reduction on this same
complex. Hence $jBp$ is the actual chain map
whose degree-$a$ matrix is $\varepsilon B^a$.
Its trace is
\[
\begin{split}
 \operatorname{Tr}_{L^\bullet}(jBp)
       &=\varepsilon\sum_a(-1)^a\operatorname{Tr}(B^a)\\
       &=\varepsilon\sum_a(-1)^a
                      \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(H^a(B^\bullet)\bigr)
        =\varepsilon\,\operatorname{str}_Y(b)(\xi).
\end{split}
 \tag{F1S34.15}
\]
The middle equality follows by taking traces
in the cycle and boundary exact sequences
of the finite vector-space complex; the
boundary contributions cancel in adjacent
degrees. It uses the induced cohomology maps,
not a replacement of the lifted complex.

If $\Psi_M(b)=0$, its actual base change
$jBp$ is nullhomotopic.
The supertrace of a nullhomotopic endomorphism
of a bounded finite free complex is zero:
in $dH+Hd$, the traces of $d^{a-1}H^a$
and $H^a d^{a-1}$ occur with opposite signs.
Equation (F1S34.15) then gives
$\operatorname{str}_Y(b)(\xi)=0$.
Since this is the generic value of the
already regular function (F1S34.5),
(F1S34.11) follows.
No independence of a chosen global coefficient
field is required: the function whose vanishing
was tested was intrinsic before (F1S34.12).

\paragraph{4. Derived ideal action, linearity and scalar-twisted gluing.}
For $f\in I$ choose any $b_f$ in (F1S34.10).
Equation (F1S34.11) shows that the value
\[
       u_i(f)=\frac1r\operatorname{str}_Y(b_f)
 \tag{F1S34.16}
\]
is independent of the choice.
It commutes with restriction to smaller opens.
Additivity follows by using $b_f+b_g$ as a
factor for $\mu_{f+g}$.
For $h\in R$, naturality of the triangle
under multiplication gives
$\mu_{hf}=\Psi_M(h|_{F_R}\,b_f)$;
on cohomology $h|_{F_R}$ is multiplication
by $\bar h\in R/I$.
Therefore $u_i(hf)=\bar h\,u_i(f)$.
For $f,g\in I$, the actual product is
\[
 \mu_{fg}
   =j(s_*b_f)p\,j(s_*b_g)p=0,
 \tag{F1S34.17}
\]
by $pj=0$ in (F1S34.8).
Using (F1S34.11) again, $u_i(fg)=0$.
Thus (F1S34.16) is an actual
$\mathcal O_Y$-linear map
$I/I^2\to\mathcal O_Y$.
Moreover $\varepsilon\mu_h=\Psi_M(h|_{F_R})$
because $jp=\varepsilon$, so adding
$\varepsilon h$ to an ambient lift changes
the unnormalized trace by $r\bar h$.

Use the actual local ring transitions

 =f+\varepsilon(h+\kappa_{ij}(f))$
from S33. Let $\phi_{ij}$ be the twisted

\[
 \Psi_{M_j}\bigl(\gamma_{ij}b_{i,f}\gamma_{ij}^{-1}\bigr)
     =\mu_f+\varepsilon\mu_{\kappa_{ij}(f)}
     =\Psi_{M_j}\bigl(b_{j,f}

 \tag{F1S34.18}
\]
Apply (F1S34.11), conjugation invariance of
(F1S34.5), and (F1S34.6). One obtains the

\[
                     u_i(f)-u_j(f)=\kappa_{ij}(f)|_Y.
 \tag{F1S34.19}
\]
The scalar triple products
$1-\varepsilon\eta_{ijk}$ act trivially

Define the actual local ideals by
\[

 \tag{F1S34.20}

Consequently they are flat over

The actual S24 maps now give

\paragraph{6. Exact boundary: a higher-thickening trace need not descend.}
The input (F1S34.1) supplies the actual zero
action of $I_Y$ in the derived category.
Here is a local calculation showing why the
same first-order trace argument cannot
simply be applied to a higher ideal-power
support. It is not a semiregularity verdict
for such objects.

Take
$B=\mathbf C[t_1,\ldots,t_4]$,
$R=B[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$,
$I=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$, and, for $s\ge2$,
the actual central sheaf and flat deformation
\[
\begin{split}
 F_0&=R/(x_1^s,x_2,x_3,x_4),\\
 M_\varepsilon&=
 R[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2,\,
                x_1^s-\varepsilon,x_2,x_3,x_4).
\end{split}
 \tag{F1S34.26}
\]
The latter is free over $B[\varepsilon]/
(\varepsilon^2)$ with basis
$1,x_1,\ldots,x_1^{s-1}$.
The companion matrix of multiplication by
$x_1$ has trace zero, whereas its $s$th
power is $\varepsilon$ times the identity.
Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\mu_{x_1})=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(\mu_{x_1^s})=s\varepsilon.
 \tag{F1S34.27}
\]
Since $x_1^s\in I^2$, the epsilon-coefficient
of the trace fails to vanish on $I^2$.
The representatives $x_1$ and $x_1+x_1^s$
of the same class in $I/I^2$ give different
values, zero and $s$.
The canonical ideal filtration does not
give flat factors either:
\[
 x_1M_\varepsilon
   =\bigoplus_{a=1}^{s-1}
          \bigl(B[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\bigr)x_1^a
                        \ \oplus\ B\varepsilon.
 \tag{F1S34.28}
\]
The last summand is annihilated by
$\varepsilon$ and is not in
$\varepsilon x_1M_\varepsilon$.
Thus this filtered submodule is not flat
over the dual numbers.
In this example multiplication by $x_1$
on the central $F_0$ is already nonzero,
so the reduction step before
(F1S34.9) does not apply.
This identifies the exact failed morphism
for that attempted extension.
It makes no assertion that the normal
norm-image objects with higher-power support
are semiregular or nonsemiregular.
