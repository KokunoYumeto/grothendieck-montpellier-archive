\section{HD-G20: the full Fourier orbit of a single eightfold point ideal}
\label{sec:point-eightfold-full-spin}

\paragraph{The actual object and the assertion.}
Let \(X\) be an abelian eightfold and \(p\in X\). Starting with
the actual ideal sheaf \(I_p\), take any finite composition of actual
Fourier--Mukai equivalences between abelian varieties, isogeny pullbacks
and pushforwards, line-bundle tensor products, translations, shifts,
and derived duals. No resulting object on an abelian eightfold \(A\)
of Weil type, for any imaginary quadratic field with balanced signature
\((4,4)\), has whole Chern character
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-target}
 e^B\bigl(P(\theta)+\lambda t\bigr),\qquad
 B\in\operatorname{NS}(A)_{\mathbb Q},\quad
 P\in\mathbb Q[z],\quad\deg P\le8,\quad
 0\ne\lambda t\in W_K(A).
\end{equation}
Here \(\theta\) is a compatible polarization. Multiplication of the
character by any nonzero rational scalar does not change the assertion.
The proof concerns this actual object and this specified functorial
orbit. It does not assert an obstruction for other eightfold objects
or for cones and extensions.

\paragraph{The source rank and its exact transport.}
Put \(V_X=H^1(X,\mathbb R)\), of real dimension sixteen, and write
\(\omega=[p]\in\bigwedge^{16}V_X\). The whole source character is
\[
                         v_X=1-\omega.
\]
Under the Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg identification for an abelian
variety, whose tangent bundle has trivial Todd class, the degree-one
operators form the maximal isotropic subspace
\[
 U_X=H^{0,1}(X)\oplus H^{1,0}(X)^*
        \ \subset\ (V_X\oplus V_X^*)_{\mathbb C}.
\]
Its two summands act by exterior multiplication and contraction,
respectively. Degree two is exactly \(\bigwedge^2U_X=HH^2(X)\),
acting by products of those operators. Pairings of vectors in \(U_X\)
are zero, so these products are the corresponding Clifford bivectors
without a scalar correction.

On \(1-\omega\), two exterior operators give an injective copy of
\(\bigwedge^2H^{0,1}(X)\) in \(H^{0,2}(X)\). Two contraction
operators give an injective copy of
\(\bigwedge^2H^{1,0}(X)^*\) in \(H^{6,8}(X)\), by contraction
with the nonzero top volume. Both spaces have dimension 28. Mixed
operators vanish: contraction kills the scalar, and an antiholomorphic
exterior factor repeats an already present factor of the contracted
top volume. The two surviving images have distinct bidegrees. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-rank56}
                  \operatorname{rank}c^2_{v_X}=56.
\end{equation}

We spell out rank preservation for every operation in the assertion.
The exact Clifford intertwiner in HD-G19 identifies the cohomological
transform of a given Fourier--Mukai equivalence with the isometry
\[
 f=\iota_B^{-1}(f_E)_*\iota_A,
 \qquad \iota_A(v,\xi)=(\xi,v),
 \qquad Lc_A(w)L^{-1}=c_B(fw).
\]
Under this swap identification, \(U_A\) is precisely the holomorphic
tangent space of \(A\times\widehat A\). The actual graph isomorphism
\(f_E\) is holomorphic, so \(f\) carries \(U_A\) onto \(U_B\).
Taking second exterior powers in the displayed intertwining equation
therefore preserves \(\operatorname{rank}c^2\), with the actual
cohomological transform on its target. These are the explicit graph
and Clifford maps of Orlov, Theorem 2.10, and
Golyshev--Lunts--Orlov, Proposition 4.3.7 and Lemma 4.3.7.1,
as proved and located in HD-G19.

For an isogeny, its map \(u\) on \(H^1\) preserves Hodge types,
and the Clifford isometry is literally
\((v,\xi)\mapsto(uv,(u^{-1})^*\xi)\). Its pullback is
\(\bigwedge^*u\); its pushforward is its inverse times the nonzero
degree. Both consequently preserve the same rank. For a line-bundle
class \(D\) the shear is
\((v,\xi)\mapsto(v-\iota_\xi D,\xi)\). Since \(D\) has type
\((1,1)\), it preserves \(U\) explicitly. Translation is the
identity on cohomology and a shift changes only a scalar sign.
Finally derived duality is the exterior sign operator
\(\tau|_{\wedge^jV}=(-1)^{j(j-1)/2}\). Its conjugation negates
the two pure bivector blocks and fixes the mixed block, as checked in
HD-G19. It therefore preserves the subspace \(\bigwedge^2U\) as
well. These explicit invertible maps prove rank preservation for every
allowed composition, including compositions with contravariant duals.

For a target \eqref{eq:G20-target}, HD-N14 gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}c^2=56+16\operatorname{rank}M_P,
 \qquad
 M_P=(m_{a+b})_{0\le a\le6,\ 0\le b\le2},
 \quad m_j=j!\,[z^j]P.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:G20-rank56} forces \(M_P=0\). Its entries
contain every \(m_0,\ldots,m_8\), so \(P=0\). Consequently it
remains to exclude a whole character \(e^B\lambda t\).

\paragraph{The exact real source stabilizer.}
Use the real Clifford space and spin operators of HD-G19:
\[
 \mathcal W_X=V_X\oplus V_X^*,\qquad
 Q(v+\xi,w+\eta)=\xi(w)+\eta(v),
 \qquad c(v+\xi)=v\wedge+\iota_\xi.
\]
Write a spin Lie operator as
\(\beta\wedge+D_A-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A)+\iota_\gamma\),
with \(\beta\in\bigwedge^2V_X\), \(A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V_X)\)
and \(\gamma\in\bigwedge^2V_X^*\). Its degree-two output on
\(1-\omega\) is \(\beta\), its degree-fourteen output is
\(-\iota_\gamma\omega\), and its degree-zero output after those
vanish is \(-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A)\). Contraction with
\(\omega\) is an isomorphism in degree two. The annihilator is
therefore exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-source-stabilizer}
 \mathfrak s_X=\mathfrak{sl}(V_X)
                    \simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{16}(\mathbb R),
 \qquad \dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak s_X=255.
\end{equation}
Conversely every trace-zero \(A\) kills both terms, so no additional
condition is hidden in this calculation.

\paragraph{The conjugate Weil volumes and their exact annihilators.}
On the target put \(V=H^1(A,\mathbb R)\). Division of the action
of a chosen square root of the negative field discriminant by its
positive real absolute value gives a real complex structure \(I\)
on \(V\). Thus \(V_{\mathbb C}=V_+\oplus V_-\), with eigenvalues
\(i,-i\), and each summand has dimension eight. These are the two
field eigenspaces; their exterior determinant volumes
\(\tau_+\in\bigwedge^8V_+\) and
\(\tau_-\in\bigwedge^8V_-\) can be chosen with
\(\overline{\tau_+}=\tau_-\). The nonzero real Weil class has
the form
\[
                 \lambda t=u\tau_++\overline u\tau_-,
                 \qquad u\ne0.
\]
In \(\mathcal W_{\mathbb C}=(V\oplus V^*)_{\mathbb C}\), the
Clifford annihilators of these two pure volumes are literally
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-annihilators}
 L_+=V_+\oplus V_-^*,\qquad
 L_-=V_-\oplus V_+^*.
\end{equation}
Here \(V_-^*\) means the subspace of the full dual which vanishes
on \(V_+\), and likewise for \(V_+^*\). Exterior multiplication
by a vector of \(V_+\) kills its top volume, and contraction by a
functional vanishing on \(V_+\) also kills it. The reverse inclusion
follows because exterior and contraction outputs have different
degrees, and each of their nonzero complementary inputs is detected
by the nonzero volume. The two annihilators are complementary
maximal isotropic spaces, are complex conjugate, and are paired
nondegenerately by \(Q\).

Define the following actual real linear map on the Clifford space:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-J}
       J(v,\xi)=(Iv,-I^*\xi).
\end{equation}
It satisfies \(J^2=-1\) and \(Q(Jx,Jy)=Q(x,y)\) by direct
substitution. Its two complex eigenspaces are exactly
\(L_+,L_-\) of \eqref{eq:G20-annihilators}. Regard \(\mathcal W\)
as a sixteen-dimensional complex vector space through \(J\), and put
\[
              h_J(x,y)=Q(x,y)-iQ(x,Jy).
\]
This is a Hermitian form, conjugate-linear in its first variable:
\(h_J(Jx,y)=-ih_J(x,y)\) and
\(h_J(x,Jy)=ih_J(x,y)\). Its real part is \(Q\), whose signature
is \((16,16)\). Each complex positive or negative direction
contributes two real directions, so \(h_J\) has signature \((8,8)\).
Consequently the real Lie algebra
\(\mathfrak{su}(\mathcal W,J,h_J)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(8,8)\)
embeds faithfully in \(\mathfrak{so}(\mathcal W,Q)\). Its elements
preserve both \(L_\pm\) and have trace zero on each.

\paragraph{The relative Fock map, including both volume lines.}
Choose bases \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{16}\) of \(L_+\) and
\(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{16}\) of \(L_-\) with
\(Q(\ell_i,\eta_j)=\delta_{ij}\). The explicit map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-Fock}
 \mathcal F:\bigwedge^*L_-\longrightarrow\bigwedge^*V_{\mathbb C},
 \qquad
 \eta_{i_1}\cdots\eta_{i_r}\longmapsto
 c(\eta_{i_1})\cdots c(\eta_{i_r})\tau_+
\end{equation}
is a Clifford intertwiner: \(\eta_i\) acts as exterior multiplication
and \(\ell_i\) acts as contraction. This follows from
\(c(\ell_i)\tau_+=0\) and the exact anticommutator
\(c(\ell_i)c(\eta_j)+c(\eta_j)c(\ell_i)=\delta_{ij}\).
It is an isomorphism. For example the product
\(\prod_i c(\ell_i)c(\eta_i)\) is the vacuum projector;
applying it after the appropriate contractions recovers every
coefficient of a purported relation among the displayed images.
Thus the \(2^{16}\) images are independent and exhaust the target.

The map sends the exterior vacuum to \(\tau_+\), and the exterior
top volume to a nonzero scalar multiple of \(\tau_-\). To check
the latter assertion, its image is nonzero by the isomorphism and is
killed by all \(c(\eta_i)\). The common kernel is one-dimensional
in the displayed Fock model, and \(\tau_-\) lies in it by
\eqref{eq:G20-annihilators}. This is the exact comparison between
the two volume lines, with no uniqueness assertion about arbitrary
decompositions of a spinor.

For a Lie operator preserving \(L_+\) and \(L_-\), the spin
action on this relative Fock model is
\(D_C-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(C)\), where
\(C\) is its action on \(L_-\); the matrix-unit computation of
HD-G19 applies in these dual bases. Every element of
\(\mathfrak{su}(\mathcal W,J,h_J)\) has \(\operatorname{tr}(C)=0\).
It therefore kills the vacuum and the top volume separately. By
\eqref{eq:G20-Fock} it kills \(\tau_+\), \(\tau_-\), and hence
\(\lambda t\). The spin Lie representation is faithful: commuting
a zero represented operator with every \(c(w)\) gives \(c(Zw)=0\),
which forces \(Zw=0\) for every \(w\). We have consequently
constructed an injective real Lie map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G20-target-stabilizer}
       \mathfrak{su}(8,8)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Stab}_{\rm Lie}
                                            (\lambda t).
\end{equation}
For \(e^B\lambda t\), conjugate this literal map by exterior
multiplication by \(e^B\); this preserves the full spin Lie algebra,
as the explicit shear in HD-G19 shows.

\paragraph{The final real-form contradiction.}
All the actual functors listed in the assertion conjugate full real
spin stabilizers, by the explicit maps in HD-G19. Thus a character
\(e^B\lambda t\) in the orbit would give an injection of
\(\mathfrak{su}(8,8)\) into
\(\mathfrak{sl}_{16}(\mathbb R)\). Both have dimension 255, so
this would be an isomorphism.

The Killing form on \(\mathfrak{sl}_{16}(\mathbb R)\) is
\(32\operatorname{tr}(XY)\); its symmetric trace-zero and
skew-symmetric subspaces have dimensions 135 and 120, giving signature
\((135,120)\). For \(\mathfrak{su}(8,8)\), the block-diagonal
compact subspace has dimension \(8^2+8^2-1=127\) and is negative
for \(32\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(XY)\). The off-diagonal
matrices \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\T^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
give a positive subspace of real dimension \(2\cdot8^2=128\).
The two spaces are orthogonal, so this signature is \((128,127)\).
The Killing form is defined intrinsically by traces of adjoint
operators and is preserved by every Lie isomorphism. The unequal
signatures give the contradiction. This proves the assertion.
