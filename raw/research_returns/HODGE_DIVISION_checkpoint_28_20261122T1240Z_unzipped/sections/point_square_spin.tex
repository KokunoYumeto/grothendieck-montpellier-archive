\section{HD-G19: the full Fourier orbit of the fourfold point-ideal square}
\label{sec:point-square-full-spin}

\paragraph{The actual object and the assertion.}
Let \(X,Y\) be abelian fourfolds, let \(p\in X,q\in Y\), and set
\[
                  F=I_p\boxtimes I_q\in D^b(X\times Y).
\]
Starting with this actual object, take any finite composition of actual
Fourier--Mukai equivalences between abelian varieties, isogeny pullbacks
and pushforwards, line-bundle tensor products, translations, shifts and
derived duals.
No resulting object on an abelian eightfold \(A\) of Weil type for an
imaginary quadratic field, with signature \((4,4)\), has whole character
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G19-whole-character}
 e^B\bigl(P(\theta)+\lambda t\bigr),
 \qquad B\in\operatorname{NS}(A)_{\mathbb Q},\quad
 P\in\mathbb Q[z],\quad\deg P\le8,
 \quad0\ne\lambda t\in W_K(A).
\end{equation}
Here \(\theta\) is a compatible polarization. The assertion also holds
after multiplying the character by a nonzero rational scalar.
The line bundles in this assertion are arbitrary; mixed divisor classes
on a product are included. The obstruction concerns this object and its
specified functorial orbit. It makes no assertion about cones, extensions,
or other eightfold objects.

The point ideals and their product have injective trace in every degree,
as proved in HD-G15. Their actual self-Ext dimensions are
\[
 (1,8,12,8,1)\quad\text{and}\quad
 (1,16,88,208,274,208,88,16,1),
\]
respectively. Thus the product survives the degree-two rank test of
HD-N14. We prove the stated whole-character obstruction by an exact
Lie algebra calculation, with the functorial comparison given explicitly.

\paragraph{The full Clifford action.}
For a real vector space \(V\) of dimension 16, put
\(\mathcal W=V\oplus V^*\) and
\[
 Q(v+\xi,w+\eta)=\xi(w)+\eta(v),\qquad
 c(v+\xi)=v\wedge+\iota_\xi\quad\text{on }\bigwedge^*V.
\]
The anticommutator is \(Q\), so these operators give the full
Clifford representation. Its spin Lie algebra has the direct decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G19-spin-decomposition}
 \mathfrak{so}(\mathcal W,Q)
 =\bigwedge^2V\ \oplus\ \mathfrak{gl}(V)\ \oplus\ \bigwedge^2V^*.
\end{equation}
On the exterior algebra the three actions are, respectively, exterior
multiplication by a bivector, \(D_A-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(A)\),
and contraction by a dual bivector. These formulas follow directly by
taking commutators of the displayed Clifford operators. In particular,
\(e_i\wedge\iota_{e^j}-\delta_{ij}/2\) is the spin action of
the matrix unit \(E_{ij}\). This fixes its scalar term.
All three summands in \eqref{eq:G19-spin-decomposition} are actual
operators; their dimensions are \(120,256,120\).

\paragraph{The exact stabilizer of the point-ideal product.}
Take \(V=H^1(X\times Y,\mathbb R)=V_1\oplus V_2\), where
\(\dim V_1=\dim V_2=8\). Let \(\omega_i\) be the nonzero
point volume of its factor. The whole character is
\[
 v_F=(1-\omega_1)(1-\omega_2)
     =1-\omega_1-\omega_2+\omega_1\omega_2.
\]
We claim that its Lie stabilizer under
\eqref{eq:G19-spin-decomposition} is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G19-stabilizer}
 \mathfrak s_F=\mathfrak{sl}(V_1)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(V_2)
              \simeq\mathfrak{sl}_8(\mathbb R)^{\oplus2}.
\end{equation}
Write an annihilating operator as
\(\beta\wedge+D_A-\operatorname{tr}(A)/2+\iota_\gamma\).
Its degree-two output is exactly \(\beta\), forcing \(\beta=0\).
Its degree-fourteen output is
\(\iota_\gamma(\omega_1\omega_2)\). Contraction with this
nonzero top volume is an isomorphism
\(\bigwedge^2V^*\to\bigwedge^{14}V\), so \(\gamma=0\).
Degree zero now gives \(\operatorname{tr}(A)=0\).
In degree eight the two off-diagonal blocks of \(A\) have outputs in
\(\bigwedge^7V_1\otimes V_2\) and
\(V_1\otimes\bigwedge^7V_2\), respectively. Each block-to-output
map is an isomorphism, by contraction with \(\omega_1\) or
\(\omega_2\). The two summands are distinct, hence both blocks vanish.
The remaining equation is
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(A|_{V_1})\omega_1+
 \operatorname{tr}(A|_{V_2})\omega_2=0.
\]
The volumes are independent, so both traces vanish. Conversely all such
block-diagonal matrices annihilate all four terms of \(v_F\).
This proves \eqref{eq:G19-stabilizer}, including its real form.

\paragraph{A real Lie subalgebra fixed by every whole Weil character.}
Let \(V_A=H^1(A,\mathbb R)\). The compatible polarization defines
a Hermitian space of signature \((4,4)\) on its eight-dimensional
complex space for the imaginary quadratic action. The special unitary
Lie algebra \(\mathfrak u=\mathfrak{su}(4,4)\) acts faithfully on
\(V_A\), preserves \(\theta\), and fixes both complex determinant
volumes defining the rational Weil plane. These last assertions are
literal: preservation of the Hermitian form preserves its alternating
imaginary part, and determinant one acts trivially on each of the two
top exterior powers over the quadratic field. Also its real trace is zero.
Thus the operators \(D_Z\), \(Z\in\mathfrak u\), belong to
\eqref{eq:G19-spin-decomposition} and annihilate
\(P(\theta)+\lambda t\).

For the full exponential factor the explicit conjugation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G19-B-conjugation}
       e^B D_Z e^{-B}=D_Z-(D_ZB)\wedge.
\end{equation}
The right side belongs to the same spin Lie algebra. Equation
\eqref{eq:G19-B-conjugation} follows by differentiating the finite
exterior exponential and using the derivation rule. It gives an injective
Lie homomorphism of \(\mathfrak{su}(4,4)\) into the stabilizer of
every character \eqref{eq:G19-whole-character}.

\paragraph{The real-form obstruction.}
There is no injective real Lie homomorphism
\[
 \mathfrak{su}(4,4)\longrightarrow
 \mathfrak{sl}_8(\mathbb R)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_8(\mathbb R).
\]
Indeed \(\mathfrak{su}(4,4)\) is simple: its complexification is
\(\mathfrak{sl}_8(\mathbb C)\), whose simplicity follows from
the elementary-matrix commutator relations. At least one projection
of an injection to a factor would be nonzero, and its kernel is an
ideal. That projection would therefore be injective. Both its domain
and codomain have dimension 63, so it would be an isomorphism.

The Killing forms exclude this. For \(\mathfrak{sl}_8(\mathbb R)\)
the form is \(16\operatorname{tr}(XY)\). It is positive definite
on the 35-dimensional symmetric trace-zero space and negative definite
on the 28-dimensional skew-symmetric space. Its signature is \((35,28)\).
For \(\mathfrak{su}(4,4)\), use the Hermitian matrix
\(J=\operatorname{diag}(I_4,-I_4)\). Its compact block-diagonal
part has dimension \(16+16-1=31\), and the Killing form
\(16\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(XY)\) is negative there.
The off-diagonal matrices
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\T^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
form a 32-dimensional positive subspace. The parts are orthogonal,
giving signature \((32,31)\). An isomorphism preserves the Killing
form, because that form is the trace of the product of adjoint
operators. The two signatures differ, proving the claimed nonembedding.

\paragraph{The exact Fourier--Mukai intertwiner.}
For an abelian variety \(A\), write
\(\mathcal W_A=H^1(A,\mathbb R)\oplus H^1(A,\mathbb R)^*\).
The Poincar\'e pairing gives the explicit identification
\(\iota_A(v,\xi)=(\xi,v)\) with
\(H_1(A,\mathbb R)\oplus H_1(\widehat A,\mathbb R)\).
Let \(L=\Phi_E^H\) be the cohomological transform of a
given Fourier--Mukai equivalence \(D^b(A)\to D^b(B)\).
There is an actual linear isometry \(f:\mathcal W_A\to\mathcal W_B\)
for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G19-Clifford-intertwiner}
        L\,c_A(w)\,L^{-1}=c_B(fw)\qquad(w\in\mathcal W_A).
\end{equation}
Here is the precise construction, including the passage between the
two abelian varieties. The universal translation-and-flat-tensor kernel
\(S_A\) gives an equivalence from \(D^b(A\times\widehat A)\)
to the category of kernels on \(A\times A\). Conjugation of kernels
by \(E\), followed by \(S_B^{-1}\), is represented by a line bundle
on the graph of an isomorphism
\(f_E:A\times\widehat A\to B\times\widehat B\).
This is Orlov's Theorem 2.10; the graph is attached to the given kernel.
Set \(f=\iota_B^{-1}(f_E)_*\iota_A\).

The cohomological map of \(S_A\) sends the Poincar\'e dual of
\(w\in H_1(A\times\widehat A)\) to the correspondence \(c_A(w)\).
For a dual basis \(x_j\in H^1(A)\), \(\ell_j\in H_1(A)\),
the two basis identities are exterior multiplication by \(x_j\)
and contraction by \(\ell_j\). They follow by expanding the
Poincar\'e kernel \(\exp(\sum x_j\widehat x_j)\) and the addition
map in the explicit formula for \(S_A\); these are the complete
basis identities of Golyshev--Lunts--Orlov, Lemma 4.3.7.1.
The line bundle on the graph contributes no higher term on cohomology
of degree \(4\dim A-1\). Consequently the conjugation diagram for
the actual kernels restricts exactly to
\eqref{eq:G19-Clifford-intertwiner}. Taking anticommutators proves
that \(f\) preserves \(Q\). Taking commutators proves that \(L\)
conjugates the full spin Lie algebras and their stabilizers.
This proves the required map for equivalences between distinct abelian
varieties as well as for autoequivalences.

The published sources are D.\ Orlov, \emph{Derived categories of
coherent sheaves on abelian varieties and equivalences between them},
Izv. Math.\ 66 (2002), 569--594, Theorem 2.10 and diagram (2.3)
(author PDF pp.9--12); and V.\ Golyshev, V.\ Lunts, D.\ Orlov,
\emph{Mirror symmetry for abelian varieties}, J. Algebraic Geom.\ 10
(2001), 433--496, Sections 4.2.5, 4.3.6, Proposition 4.3.7 and
Lemma 4.3.7.1 (author PDF pp.15--19). The above argument uses their
explicit Clifford correspondence, rather than a comparison of unnamed
group actions.

\paragraph{Isogenies, line bundles and compositions.}
For an isogeny \(q:A\to B\), put \(u=q^*:H^1(B,\mathbb R)
\to H^1(A,\mathbb R)\). Its cohomological pullback is exactly
\(\bigwedge^*u\), and
\[
 (\bigwedge^*u)c_B(v+\xi)(\bigwedge^*u)^{-1}
          =c_A\bigl(uv+(u^{-1})^*\xi\bigr).
\]
The map in parentheses preserves \(Q\). Also
\(q_*=(\deg q)(q^*)^{-1}\) on cohomology, so pushforward has the
same conjugation property. The tangent bundles of abelian varieties
are trivial; hence Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch gives these exact maps
on Chern characters, without a Todd correction.
Tensoring by a line bundle multiplies the character by \(e^B\);
on Clifford generators its explicit shear is
\[
 e^Bc(v+\xi)e^{-B}=c\bigl(v-\iota_\xi B+\xi\bigr).
\]
Translations act trivially on cohomology, and a shift multiplies the
character by \(-1\). These covariant functors therefore give invertible
scalar multiples of Clifford intertwiners.
For derived duality the character map on degree \(2k\) is multiplication
by \((-1)^k\). Extend it to the exterior algebra by
\(\tau|_{\wedge^dV}=(-1)^{d(d-1)/2}\). Directly from the change
in degree by two, conjugation by \(\tau\) negates the exterior and
contraction bivectors in \eqref{eq:G19-spin-decomposition} and fixes
\(D_A-\operatorname{tr}(A)/2\). Thus duality also conjugates the
full spin Lie algebra by this explicitly given automorphism.
Every allowed composition consequently conjugates stabilizer Lie
algebras exactly, including compositions containing contravariant duals.

The source stabilizer is \eqref{eq:G19-stabilizer}. The stabilizer of
\eqref{eq:G19-whole-character} contains the explicitly embedded
\(\mathfrak{su}(4,4)\) of \eqref{eq:G19-B-conjugation}. Conjugating
that embedding back to the source contradicts the real-form
nonembedding just proved. This proves the assertion.
