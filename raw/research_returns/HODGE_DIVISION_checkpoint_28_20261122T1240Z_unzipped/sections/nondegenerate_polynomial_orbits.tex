\subsection{HD-R08: the exact remaining nondegenerate polynomial-character source}
\label{sec:nondegenerate-polynomial-orbits}

Fix an integer $n\ge3$. Consider actual perfect complexes on
complex abelian $2n$-folds with whole character
\[
                 e^{B_0}P_0(H),\qquad \deg P_0\le2n,          \tag{R08.1}
\]
where $B_0$ and $H$ are real $(1,1)$ classes, $H$ is
nondegenerate, and $P_0$ has real coefficients. Consider the
actual Fourier--Mukai, isogeny pullback and pushforward,
line-twist, translation, shift and derived-dual maps already
specified in HD-N18. Among (R08.1), every character whose orbit
contains a nonzero scalar multiple of
\[
                 e^B\bigl(P(\theta)+\lambda t\bigr),
       \quad0\ne\lambda t\in W_K(A),\quad\deg P\le2n,       \tag{R08.2}
\]
with $B$ real of type $(1,1)$ and $P$ real, on a polarized
Weil $2n$-fold of signature $(n,n)$ has the
following precise necessary form:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 P=0,\qquad
 P_0(H)=e^{rH}\bigl(\alpha e^{icH}+\overline\alpha e^{-icH}\bigr),
 \\
 r\in\mathbf R,\quad c>0,\quad\alpha\ne0,
 \qquad \operatorname{signature}(H)=(n,n).                   \tag{R08.3}
 \end{gathered}
\]
In particular every ample source (R08.1) is excluded for all
$n\ge3$, including the eightfold helper. Formula(R08.3) is a
proved restriction on actual transported characters. It supplies
no object, transport or semiregularity assertion for the remaining
form. The proof gives the exact maps and exhausts every alternative
in the finite coefficient equations.

\paragraph{1. Source and target Hochschild ranks in every dimension.}
The Hodge Clifford space, the isogeny and line-shear maps, the
Poincar\'e swap for an actual Fourier--Mukai equivalence, and
both duality signs of HD-N18, paragraph1, have no dimension-six
restriction. Use those same literal formulas with
$\dim_{\mathbf R}H^1=4n$. They preserve the rank of the action
of $HH^2=\bigwedge^2(H^{0,1}\oplus H^{1,0*})$ on the whole
character. The real $(1,1)$ factors displayed in(R08.1)--(R08.2)
are removed by the explicit shear
$(v,\xi)\mapsto(v-\iota_\xi B,\xi)$. Thus no fractional
geometric line twist is introduced in this calculation.

Write $P_0(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{2n}p_jz^j$, $m_j=j!p_j$, and set
\[
 M_0=(m_{k+j})_{0\le k\le2n-2,\ 0\le j\le2},
                       \rho=\operatorname{rank}M_0.         \tag{R08.4}
\]
Nondegeneracy gives complex bases $x_i\in H^{1,0}$ and
$y_i\in H^{0,1}$ with $H=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_i y_i$.
Each pair $i<j$ contributes the three columns
$y_i y_j P_0(H),y_i y_j P_0'(H),y_i y_j P_0''(H)$, with
the second column supplied twice up to the fixed exterior signs.
The equal-index mixed operator is zero. For every
$0\le k\le2n-2$, the map
$\bigwedge^2\langle y_i\rangle\to H^*(A)$ given by wedging
with $H^k$ is injective: to recover the coefficient of $y_i y_j$,
choose $k$ other paired indices and read the monomial with
precisely those holomorphic factors. Its coefficient is the
chosen coefficient multiplied by nonzero $k!$. Separate $k$
have separate bidegrees. The resulting source map is therefore
the tensor product of that coefficient space and the matrix in
(R08.4), and
\[
                \operatorname{rank}c^2_{P_0(H)}
                          =n(2n-1)\rho.                     \tag{R08.5}
\]

For the target, choose balanced bases
\[
 \theta=\sum_{i=1}^n(a_i y_i+b_i z_i),\quad
 \tau_+=a_1\cdots a_nz_1\cdots z_n,\quad
 \tau_-=b_1\cdots b_ny_1\cdots y_n.
\]
The nonzero rational Weil term is $u\tau_++u'\tau_-$ with
$uu'\ne0$. Give $a_i,z_i$ weight $1$, and $b_i,y_i$ weight
$-1$. The two $2n$-dimensional generator spaces
\[
 U_- =\langle\iota_{a_i},y_i\wedge\rangle,
       \qquad U_+=\langle\iota_{b_i},z_i\wedge\rangle
\]
have weights $-1$ and $1$. On their exterior squares the Weil
outputs have weights $2n-2$ and $-(2n-2)$; the polynomial
outputs have weights $-2$ and $2$. Since $n\ge3$, the Weil
weights have absolute value at least four. The double
contractions, contraction--wedge products and double wedges
give respectively $\binom n2,n^2,\binom n2$ distinct
monomials in each Weil output, so both exterior-square maps
are injective. Their images are mutually independent and
independent of the mixed block by these weights.

The mixed block $U_-\otimes U_+$ kills both Weil volumes.
For each $(i,j)$, its four outputs are, up to the actual exterior
signs, $y_i z_jP(\theta),y_i z_jP'(\theta)$ twice, and
$y_i z_jP''(\theta)$. The same monomial recovery, now
avoiding the $a_i y_i,b_j z_j$ pairs, proves injectivity for
each power through $2n-2$. Set $\rho'$ equal to the rank of
the target factorial Hankel matrix, with the same row and
column ranges as(R08.4). The exact target rank is consequently
\[
        \operatorname{rank}c^2_{P(\theta)+\lambda t}
                  =2n(2n-1)+n^2\rho'.                       \tag{R08.6}
\]
Equality of the transported ranks gives
\[
                 (2n-1)(\rho-2)=n\rho'.                     \tag{R08.7}
\]
Here $0\le\rho\le3$ and $\rho'\ge0$ are integers. Values
$\rho<2$ give a negative left side. Value $\rho=3$ would
give $\rho'=2-1/n$, which is not an integer for $n\ge3$.
Thus $\rho=2,\rho'=0$. Every target coefficient occurs in
its zero Hankel matrix, proving $P=0$.

\paragraph{2. All solutions of the finite source recurrence.}
The real rank-two matrix $M_0$ has a nonzero real kernel
vector $(q_0,q_1,q_2)$. Its equations are exactly
\[
          q_0m_k+q_1m_{k+1}+q_2m_{k+2}=0,
                               \quad0\le k\le2n-2.          \tag{R08.8}
\]
For $q_2\ne0$, the initial values $m_0,m_1$ determine every
moment through $m_{2n}$. Distinct roots $r_1,r_2$ of
$q_2z^2+q_1z+q_0$ give, by solving those two initial
equations and substituting in the recurrence,
\[
                   P_0(H)=\alpha e^{r_1H}+\beta e^{r_2H}.
                                                               \tag{R08.9}
\]
Both coefficients are nonzero, since a single exponential
has Hankel rank at most one. A repeated root gives
\[
                      P_0(H)=(a+bH)e^{rH},\quad b\ne0.       \tag{R08.10}
\]
For $r\ne0$ its moments are $ar^k+bk r^{k-1}$; for $r=0$
they are directly $m_0=a,m_1=b$ and all later moments zero.
These are all solutions of the second-order initial-value
recurrence, including the repeated-zero case.

For $q_2=0,q_1\ne0$, put $r=-q_0/q_1$. Equations(R08.8)
give $m_k=m_0r^k$ through $k=2n-1$, leaving $m_{2n}$ free.
The resulting character is
\[
                        P_0(H)=a e^{rH}+bH^{2n},\quad ab\ne0.
                                                               \tag{R08.11}
\]
Finally $q_2=q_1=0$ gives $m_0=\cdots=m_{2n-2}=0$, hence
\[
                        P_0(H)=aH^{2n-1}+bH^{2n},\quad a\ne0.
                                                               \tag{R08.12}
\]
The asserted nonzero coefficients in(R08.10)--(R08.12) follow
by direct inspection of rank two. Every exponential in these
formulas is its finite exterior expansion modulo $H^{2n+1}$.

\paragraph{3. Exact full stabilizers exclude the real secants.}
Put $V=H^1(A,\mathbf R)$, $\dim V=4n$, and use the full
spin action of $\mathcal W=V\oplus V^*$ with
$Q(v+\xi,w+\eta)=\xi(w)+\eta(v)$. Its Lie operators are
\[
          \beta\wedge+D_T-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}T
                          +\iota_\gamma.                    \tag{R08.13}
\]
For two distinct real roots in(R08.9), the annihilators are
the transverse real graphs
\[
     L_j=\{-r_jH^\sharp\xi+\xi:\xi\in V^*\}.
\]
They are isotropic by skewness of $H^\sharp$ and transverse
by its invertibility. Applying the operators of $L_2$ to
$e^{r_1H}$ gives the actual relative Fock map
$\bigwedge^*L_2\to\bigwedge^*V$. After the shear $e^{-r_1H}$,
its leading terms are the exterior powers of the invertible
map $(r_2-r_1)H^\sharp$, with the fixed exterior signs.
It is thus an invertible real map and sends the two axes to
the vacuum and top axes. For(R08.11), the shear $e^{-rH}$
gives $a+bH^{2n}$ directly, with annihilators $V^*$ and $V$.

In either Fock model the character is $a\cdot1+b\cdot\mathrm{vol}$,
$ab\ne0$. In(R08.13), its degree-two and degree-$(4n-2)$
outputs force $\beta=0$ and $\gamma=0$; its degree-zero and
top outputs force $\operatorname{tr}T=0$. Those degrees are
separate for $n\ge3$. Its full real stabilizer is therefore
exactly $\mathfrak{sl}_{4n}(\mathbf R)$.

The pure target Weil class has its explicit complex axes
$V_+\oplus V_-^*$ and $V_-\oplus V_+^*$, where
$V_+,V_-$ are the two field eigenspaces. Wedging in one
eigenspace and contracting the other gives its relative Fock
isomorphism. On the first axis the real structure induces the
Hermitian form $h(z,w)=Q(z,\overline w)$. A basis of $V_+$
and its dual in $V_-^*$ gives the exact Gram matrix
\[
                           \begin{pmatrix}0&I_{2n}\\I_{2n}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The trace-zero real form of the same complex stabilizer is
therefore exactly $\mathfrak{su}(2n,2n)$, through this displayed
Hermitian matrix and the Fock map. The signatures of their
Killing forms, calculated on symmetric/skew matrices and on
off-diagonal/compact block matrices respectively, are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \mathfrak{sl}_{4n}(\mathbf R)&
       (8n^2+2n-1,\ 8n^2-2n)\\
 \mathfrak{su}(2n,2n)&(8n^2,\ 8n^2-1).
 \end{array}                                                 \tag{R08.14}
\]
Every actual transport conjugates the full stabilizers by
HD-N18's literal maps; conjugation intertwines their adjoint
operators and hence preserves the Killing trace pairing.
The two signatures in(R08.14) are unequal. This excludes
both real-secant cases.

\paragraph{4. Exact tangent maps and their full stabilizer.}
Choose a real Darboux basis $H=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}a_i b_i$,
and put $L=H\wedge$, $\Lambda=\sum_i\iota_{b_i^*}\iota_{a_i^*}$.
These are actual spin Lie operators. Removing $e^{rH}$ from
(R08.10), the invertible operator
$\exp(-a\Lambda/(2nb))$ sends $a+bH$ to $bH$.
The explicit operator $\mathcal F_H=e^L e^{-\Lambda}e^L$
sends $1$ to $a_i b_i$ and $a_i b_i$ to $-1$ on each
two-dimensional pair space. The pair operators commute, giving
\[
       \mathcal F_H(H^k)=(-1)^{2n-k}
                         \frac{k!}{(2n-k)!}H^{2n-k}.          \tag{R08.15}
\]
It sends(R08.12) to
$-a(2n-1)!H+b(2n)!$, whose constant term is removed by
the preceding contraction. These operators compute stabilizers;
no assertion that they individually come from geometric
equivalences is needed or made.

For(R08.13) acting on $H$, the three exterior degrees are
four, two and zero. The degree-four condition $\beta H=0$
forces $\beta=0$: the exact Darboux commutator on degree two
is $[\Lambda,L]=2n-2$, giving
$(2n-2)\beta=-H\Lambda\beta$; wedging again and using
$H^2\ne0$ kills the scalar $\Lambda\beta$ and then $\beta$.
The degree-two condition is
$D_TH=\operatorname{tr}(T)H/2$. For $D_TH=kH$ the
matrix equation gives $2\operatorname{tr}T=4nk$.
Combining the two equations yields
$(n-1)\operatorname{tr}T=0$, so $T\in\mathfrak{sp}(V,H)$.
The degree-zero condition is exactly $\iota_\gamma H=0$.
Thus the full stabilizer is the explicit semidirect product
\[
 \mathfrak{sp}_{4n}(\mathbf R)\ltimes
       \{\gamma\in\bigwedge^2V^*:\iota_\gamma H=0\}.          \tag{R08.16}
\]
The second summand is a nonzero proper abelian Lie ideal,
of dimension $\binom{4n}{2}-1$; even contractions commute,
and the derivation commutator preserves its displayed equation.
The pure target stabilizer is simple: complexification of its
explicit Hermitian-adjoint matrix equation is
$\mathfrak{sl}_{4n}(\mathbf C)$, with the scalar-extension
inverse already written in HD-N17. The latter is simple by
its elementary-matrix brackets. Conjugation of the two full
stabilizers would send the displayed proper nonzero abelian
ideal to one in that simple algebra. This excludes both
tangent cases by the exact maps above.

\paragraph{5. Imaginary secants force precisely the middle index.}
The remaining roots of(R08.8) are $r\pm ic$, $c>0$.
Their coefficients in(R08.9) are conjugate because all
moments are real and the two distinct exponential columns
are independent already in degrees zero and two. Remove
$e^{rH}$ by its real $(1,1)$ shear. Put $C=H^\sharp$,
$\mathcal I=\operatorname{diag}(I,-I^*)$, and
\[
    \mathcal J_H=\begin{pmatrix}0&cC\\-c^{-1}C^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},
        \qquad G_H(x,y)=Q(x,\mathcal I\mathcal J_Hy).         \tag{R08.17}
\]
The $(1,1)$ identity $IC+CI^*=0$ shows that these two
orthogonal complex structures commute, so $G_H$ is a
nondegenerate symmetric real form. Its $\mathcal J_H$ axes
are the actual annihilators of $e^{\pm icH}$.

The unordered axes are recovered intrinsically from the whole
character. Its complex stabilizer is the vacuum--top
$\mathfrak{sl}_{4n}$ just computed. The fixed spinors of this
Lie algebra are exactly the vacuum--top plane: every intermediate
exterior monomial has a nonzero weight for a diagonal trace-zero
matrix, obtained by selecting one included and one omitted
index. The only pure lines in this plane are the two axes.
Indeed a mixture of both axes has zero annihilator, since its
degree-one outputs lie in distinct Fock degrees one and $4n-1$,
with each half injective. Each axis has its $4n$-dimensional
annihilator. The identical argument applies to the pure target.
Consequently the actual transport, which conjugates stabilizers
and carries annihilators, carries the unordered source axes
to the unordered target axes.

Diagonalize the real $(1,1)$ form with its Hodge structure:
\[
 H=\sum_{j=1}^{2n}h_j a_jb_j,\quad h_j\ne0,\quad
 Ia_j=-b_j,\quad Ib_j=a_j.
\]
Let $p$ of the $h_j$ be positive and $q$ negative, $p+q=2n$.
In the actual basis $a_j,b_j,a_j^*,b_j^*$, the matrix of $G_H$
in(R08.17) is diagonal with entries
$(ch_j)^{-1},(ch_j)^{-1},ch_j,ch_j$. Thus
\[
                          \operatorname{sig}(G_H)=(4p,4q).
                                                               \tag{R08.18}
\]
The target has
$\mathcal J_t=\operatorname{diag}(K_V,-K_V^*)$, where
$K_V$ is its normalized real field action. It commutes with
$\mathcal I$, and $G_t=Q(\ ,\mathcal I\mathcal J_t\ )$ is
nondegenerate with the whole $V$ isotropic. Since $V$ has
dimension $4n$ inside the $8n$-dimensional Clifford space,
its signature is exactly $(4n,4n)$.

Write the actual isometry or anti-isometry as $f$, so
$Q_t(fx,fy)=\epsilon Q_H(x,y)$ with $\epsilon=\pm1$.
Axis recovery gives
$f\mathcal J_Hf^{-1}=\varepsilon\mathcal J_t$,
$\varepsilon=\pm1$, and Hodge compatibility gives
$f\mathcal I_H=\mathcal I_t f$. The exact identity is
\[
                 G_t(fx,fy)=\epsilon\varepsilon G_H(x,y).
                                                               \tag{R08.19}
\]
The neutral target signature and(R08.18) therefore force
$p=q=n$. This is exactly the index assertion in(R08.3),
and completes the proof.
