\subsection{Condition A, condition I, and the correspondence inverse}

The project's condition-A interpretation uses the corrected factorization
in SIGN\_NEXT SN-C03/SN-C04. For a smooth hyperplane
$j:W\hookrightarrow T$, with $\dim T=2m$ and hyperplane operator $L_T$,
the exact map is
\[
 L_T^2=j_*L_Wj^*=L_Tj_*j^*,\qquad L_W=j^*L_T.
\]
SN-C03 identifies the critical algebraic inverse condition with
restriction descent and the \emph{weighted} Gysin equality
\[
 L_Tj_*\operatorname{Alg}^{m-1}(W)
       =\operatorname{Alg}^{m+1}(T).
\]
SN-C04 applies that identity to the square in the corrected proposition
of cote16, scan050, folio49, with Kleiman (1968), Theorem2.13.
Our integral cycles $Z^+,Z^-$ and multiplier $N$ provide explicit
uniform representatives for the particular Weil classes under study.
The supplied reduction identifies their boundedness with the project's
quantitative condition-A problem on a fixed family. This is the precise
scope of that interpretation.

The positivity step occurs after a detector is present. On its Weil
plane, the pairing $(-1)^n\int uv$ is positive. The exact inverse
calculation in HD-D01 is
\[
 \operatorname{Gram}(y,z)=\operatorname{diag}(d,sd),\qquad
 (s\delta d)t=sb_1y+b_2z.
\]
This is the condition-I step of cote16, scan049, and cote44,
Theorem4, applied to the supplied cycle classes. The upper estimates
come from its positive norm and the independently constructed effective
representatives of $y,z$.

The numerator $P([a]^*)=Re_W$ is a polynomial in actual graph
correspondences. Its Bezout identity with the complementary spectral
polynomial is the finite-dimensional Cayley--Hamilton inverse mechanism
of cote16, scans048--049, in the letter dated6 January1966.
The explicit unit correspondences in the Gaussian calculation obey
the same projector identity. Thus the relation between the arithmetic
numerator and the geometric projector is displayed as an equality of
operators, including its denominator $R$.

\subsection{Schemes, finite presentation, and bounded cycle spaces}

The cyclic sources are finite covers of curves and connected kernels
of relative norm maps. The reflection-group source is a finite Hurwitz
cover, followed by a rational group-algebra summand of its Jacobian.
The monomial source is related to its cyclic source by the specific
quotients $Z/\ker\chi\to Z/H$ and the pullback isogeny HD-I02.
The CM construction consists of a plane quartic, finite quotient
isogenies, ideals of embedded curves, Poincare kernels, and actual
idempotent retracts of perfect complexes.

Each finite collection of these equations, morphisms, line bundles and
coherences descends to a finitely generated $\mathbb Z$-algebra and then
to a suitable localization: EGA IV$_3$,8.8.2(i)--(ii).
Finite groups become etale after their orders are inverted; smoothness
and the required polarizations persist after a further shrinking.
For the explicit Fermat construction all its individual data are
defined over a finite number-field extension, including the finitely
many torsion points and character projectors.

The bounded-cycle reductions use the proper parameter spaces already
established in ATTACK R1 and TheoremD. Their scheme-theoretic setting is
FGA, Bourbaki Expose221, Theorems3.1 and3.2: the Quot functor and its
projective fixed-Hilbert-polynomial pieces; the Hilbert specialization
appears immediately after Proposition3.8. The constants in this paper
bound effective cycle degrees and denominator choices before those
closedness arguments are applied. The smoothing proofs use constant
cohomology dimensions and cohomology/base change in EGA III$_2$,
7.7.5 and7.8.4. Their geometric nodal gluings are written explicitly.

The effective CM argument also uses an analytic neighbourhood of an
algebraic polarized fibre. Its actual flat coherent sheaf satisfies
the six explicit regularity vanishings. Their openness is Grauert,
\S7, Satz3, printed pp.62--63, applied with the flatness hypothesis
proved in HD-N09. Each projective fibre and its degeneracy cycle are
algebraic by GAGA. The single fixed degree then enters the same
proper bounded-cycle space. This gives the explicit passage from the
analytic deformation neighbourhood to the whole algebraic Weil
component.

Riemann existence for the branch tuples is SGA1, ExposeXII,5.1.
The exact exceptional-fibre calculation in HD-G05 uses proper base
change, SGA4, ExposeV bis,4.1.1 in the topological setting and
ExposeXII,5.1 in the etale setting. The simply branched auxiliary
pencils of HD-I08 use SGA7 II, ExposeXVII, Definition2.2 and2.5.2.
These are the particular SGA operations used by the corresponding
geometric maps.

\subsection{K-theory and exterior operations}

Brauer induction in HD-G06 is an equality in
$K_0(\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb C}G)$. Applying the multiplicity-space
functor and its determinant-character homomorphism gives the stated
identity of one-dimensional characters, including negative coefficients.
On the chosen quadratic degree-one summand the Weil determinant is
$\lambda_K^{2n}H^1=\bigwedge_K^{2n}H^1$. The relevant exterior-power
formalism is SGA6, ExposeV,2.1--2.2, with its exact-sequence identities;
its gamma operations are in ExposeV,3. The geometric symmetric-group
correspondence includes the Koszul sign from odd degree, as explicitly
computed in HD-G and HD-I.

The CM construction supplies classes of actual perfect objects in
$K_0(A)$, with pullback, tensor product and direct-summand morphisms.
The Fourier--Mukai map on $K_0$ is the additive integral transform
\[
 x\longmapsto p_!\bigl(q^*x\otimes[\mathcal P^{-1}[3]]\bigr).
\]
Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch gives the character calculation; on an
abelian variety the Todd class equals one. Pullbacks preserve exterior
operations. For the character decomposition of HD-N06 the exact
$K_0$ identity is
\[
 [S^*\mathcal E]=\sum_\chi[F_\chi]
 =\sum_{c\in C/\operatorname{ann}(K)}[t_c^*F].
\]
After the Chern-character realization, translations act trivially and
this becomes $\operatorname{ch}(F)=S^*\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal E)/16$.
The ordinary $K_0$ identity retains the individual translated summands.
The trace/evaluation maps used to prove semiregularity are
Buchweitz--Flenner (2008), Theorem6.4.2 and Corollary6.3.2.
They relate the actual obstruction space to contraction with that
full character. The finite group action and its coherence are retained
through every equivalence and retract.

In HD-N09 the original deformation gerbe has the precise seed class
$\delta(\det(F)^{-1})\in H^2(A,\mu_r)$, and its tautological line
satisfies $\mathcal T^r=\det(F)^{-1}$. This is the Kummer boundary
of that actual line bundle. Multiplication by $r$ kills the finite
cohomology class and yields the ordinary seed
$B_0=[r]^*F\otimes\det(F)^{-r}$, up to a flat line bundle.
The resulting evaluation kernel has the exact $K_0$ relation
\[
 [V']=N[\mathcal O_A(7h)]-[B_0((R+7)h)].
\]
The Newton identity for its third Chern class gives a nonzero Weil
component. A general map
$\mathcal O_A^{\operatorname{rank}V'-2}\to V'$ supplies the effective
codimension-three degeneracy cycle. This relates the actual
$K$-theory object to the bounded integral cycle through the
displayed bundle presentation. The root-line and character comparison
is Hotchkiss, Definition4.5, Lemma4.6, Remark4.13 and
Definition4.16/Theorem4.17. The cycle formula is the
Thom--Porteous formula, Fulton, \S14.4.

\subsection{Realizations, weights, and motivic Euler factors}

All quotient pullbacks, norms, graph correspondences and exterior
projectors have Betti and etale realizations. For a smooth proper
spread $\pi$, the relevant local system is a direct summand of
$R^1\pi_*\mathbb Q_\ell$, of weight one at good finite-field points.
Its degree-$2n$ determinant has weight $2n$, and the Tate twist $(n)$
has weight zero. The purity input can be located exactly in Deligne,
\emph{Weil II}, Corollary3.3.9; the more general direct-image statement
is Theorem3.3.1.

For precision the motive here can be taken in the category of
homological motives with rational coefficients. The degree-$2n$
projector is algebraic: on cohomology use the Lagrange polynomial in
$[m]^*$ that is one at $m^{2n}$ and zero at $m^j$ for
$0\le j\le4n$, $j\ne2n$, with $m\ge2$.
Compose this with $P([a]^*)/R$.
The displayed spectral identities make the resulting correspondence
idempotent modulo homological equivalence, giving the object
$M_W=(A,e_W,n)$ and its realization $W_\ell(n)$.
The supplied effective detectors and the subsequent signed cycles
give explicit cycle morphisms into that summand.

At a good point $v$ with residue cardinality $q_v$, the local factor is
\[
 L_v(M_W,s)=
 \det\bigl(1-q_v^{-s}\operatorname{Frob}_v
       \mid W_\ell(n)\bigr)^{-1}.
\]
On a single $K$-embedding the untwisted determinant line has Euler
polynomial $1-\det(\operatorname{Frob}_v\mid V_\ell)T$.
These are the realizations of the same projector and determinant
operations. The degree bounds in this paper come from the supplier
cycles, intersections, isogeny maps and positive integral Gram inverse.
No analytic estimate for these Euler factors is an input to a cycle
bound.

\subsection{The exact E8 and Fermat correspondences in these realizations}

HD-S22--S24 starts with a genuine connected finite cover and a

HD-I17 supplies an actual analytic point of this algebraic covering

For HD-R01 and HD-N15 the geometric maps are finite quotient maps,
Abel maps, projective bundles, coordinate-power maps between Fermat
hypersurfaces, and closed join correspondences. Finite-cover comparison
is SGA1, Expose XII, Theorem5.1. The unique-fibre localization uses
proper base change, SGA4, Expose V bis,4.1.1. Their Chow classes and
intersection products are integral; only the explicitly named
character projectors and scalar comparisons use rational coefficients.
The surface input is the actual first Chern class of a line bundle,
made effective by the displayed Riemann--Roch calculation. The
projective-bundle and blowup maps then transport that class, with
their character-survival and degree formulas recorded in HD-N15.

These are finite-presentation constructions. Each chosen cover,
cycle and finite list of maps descends to a finitely generated
subring of $\mathbb C$, and localization makes the required curves
and Fermat factors smooth and their projective maps proper. The
finite-presentation descent locator is EGA IV$_3$,8.8.2. This supplies
schemes over an open part of a finite-type scheme over $\mathbb Z$;
no unproved numerical bound is obtained by this descent itself.

On the group-representation and de Rham sides, the supplier row is
the determinant of the explicit Frobenius-reciprocity multiplicity
module. Addition of rows is multiplication of determinant characters,
the top-exterior-power operation in the corresponding $\lambda$-ring.
On $K_0$ the surface line bundle gives $c_1$ through the Chern character,
and all later maps are the stated pullbacks, proper pushforwards and
intersection products. On Betti and etale realizations the very same

$(3)$ has weight zero; the product-Fermat summand of degree $S$ has
weight $S$, with the explicitly proved bundle shift $(-Rg)$.
The determinant Euler factor is consequently the one written above
for $n=3$. It is a realization of these maps, not an analytic input

The integral effective supplier degrees are the concrete representative
