\subsection{HD-S26: the natural Gaussian \(E_8\) eightfold route has rank at most 15}
\label{sec:e8-eightfold-cap}

Let \(G=W(E_8)\cdot\mu_4=W(E_8)\sqcup iW(E_8)\), with its
natural representation on \(K^8\), \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), and the
actual primitive Jacobian-factor construction of HD-S22. For every
connected \(G\)-cover of \(\mathbb P^1\) for which this factor is
an eightfold of Weil signature \((4,4)\), its period map has
differential rank at most 15. The same bound holds after any specified
\(K\)-linear isogeny and change of integral polarization lattice.
Thus no such Hurwitz component dominates a 16-dimensional Gaussian
Weil-eightfold moduli component. This assertion concerns this natural
representation and these sphere covers.

\paragraph{Proof of the universal bound.}
Write the nonidentity branch tuple as \((g_1,\ldots,g_b)\), with
product one and generated group \(G\), and put
\(c_j=\operatorname{rank}(1-g_j)\). Its image in
\(G/W(E_8)\simeq\mathbb Z/2\) has an even, positive number of
nontrivial entries. Hence at least two entries have the form
\(iw\), \(w\in W(E_8)\). Because \(w\) is real, its
\(+i\) and \(-i\) eigenspaces are complex conjugate and have
equal dimensions. The fixed space of \(iw\) is the \(-i\)
eigenspace of \(w\), so it has dimension at most 4. Each such
entry has \(c_j\ge4\); every other nonidentity entry has
\(c_j\ge1\).

For clarity, the dimension used here follows directly from the
parabolic cocycle quotient. Its variables lie in
\(\bigoplus_j\operatorname{im}(1-g_j)\), its relation is
\(\sum_jP_jc_j=0\), and its coboundaries have dimension 8,
where \(P_j=g_1\cdots g_{j-1}\). The relation map is surjective:
a vector orthogonal to its image has
\(P_j^{-1}v\in\ker(1-g_j)\) for every \(j\); induction makes
these vectors equal to \(v\) and fixed by every \(g_j\).
The natural representation has no invariants, so \(v=0\).
Consequently
\[
 \dim_K H^1_{\rm par}=\sum_jc_j-16.
\]
For the primitive eightfold this dimension is 8, by the exact
matrix-coefficient and Jacobian map of HD-S22--HD-S23. Therefore
\[
 24=\sum_jc_j\ \ge\ 4+4+(b-2)=b+6,
 \qquad b\le18.
\]

An ordered, marked Hurwitz component is locally a finite cover of
the configuration space of \(b\) distinct points on \(\mathbb P^1\).
The map sends a cover to its ordered branch points; its discrete fibre
is the finite set of generating product-one tuples in the finite group.
Moving the cut endpoints and retaining the sheet permutations supplies
the inverse local construction. After quotienting the free local action
of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\), or fixing three branch values, its
dimension is \(b-3\le15\). The primitive factor is obtained by
the actual fixed integral endomorphism \(De\) of the relative
Jacobian. Hence its period map factors through this space and has rank
at most 15.

Finally, in local homology markings a specified isogeny is a fixed
rational \(K\)-linear map \(F\). It sends a Hodge subspace to
its image under \(F\). Since \(F\) is invertible over
\(\mathbb Q\), this map of period domains is a local isomorphism,
with inverse induced by \(F^{-1}\); it preserves the rank of any
composed period map. The maps changing integral lattices in HD-S24
are precisely of this form. Thus they cannot remove the rank bound.
The Weil-eightfold domain has dimension \(4\cdot4=16\): its
tangent space is \(\operatorname{Hom}(F^1,H/F^1)\) with the two
four-dimensional \(K\)-embedding spaces. A map of rank at most 15
does not dominate that component. This last claim is made for each
actual family and specified isogeny; it is not an assertion about the
Zariski closure of an unspecified countable union of isogeny images.

\paragraph{The branch-parameter bound is attained.}
There are actual covers with \(b=18\) and signature \((4,4)\).
With the integral matrix \(a\) from HD-S22, put \(u=ia^5\).
The exact matrix equalities are \(a^{10}=-1\), \(u^2=1\), and
\(\operatorname{rank}(1-u)=4\). Let \(s_1,\ldots,s_8\) be
the eight simple reflections used in the same group certificate.
The tuple
\[
 (u,u,s_1,s_1,s_2,s_2,\ldots,s_8,s_8)
\]
has product one and generates \(G\): the \(s_j\) generate
\(W(E_8)\), and \(u(a^5)^{-1}=iI\). Its codimension sum is
24. The two \(u\)'s contribute age 4 in total, and the sixteen
reflections contribute age 8. The complementary age sum is also 12.
The unitary Riemann--Roch calculation of Dettweiler--Wewers,
Theorem 2.3, pp.155--157, gives the two Hodge dimensions
\(12-8=4\). The cut-and-sheet construction produces the connected
smooth compact cover for every choice of distinct branch points.
This gives a 15-dimensional Hurwitz component; no assertion that its
period rank equals 15 is needed or made.
