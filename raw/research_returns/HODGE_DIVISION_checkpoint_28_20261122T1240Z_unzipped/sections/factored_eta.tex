\section{HD-G24: the explicitly factored elliptic coupling family}
\label{sec:factored-eta-family}

\paragraph{The actual family and its scope.}
Retain the six packages of HD-N16. Distribute their graph sheaves in
three layers as follows: positive coefficients with an even number
of nonzero slope coordinates are in shift zero, negative coefficients
are in shift one, and positive coefficients with an odd number of
nonzero coordinates are in shift two. Put the single ample line
\(L\) in shift zero. The alternating whole character is again
\(e^\theta+96w_d\).

We now specify an actual family of couplings, rather than a rank
claim for arbitrary morphisms. Choose the scalar elliptic
cohomology class \(\eta\) from the tangent direction of the third
elliptic pair. Group the graph sheaves by their third slope
\(\epsilon_3\) and their third package exponent
\(a_3=1_{3\notin J}\). Within each group, use the exact product
model and the differential \(d+\eta H\) constructed below, where
\(H\) is any closed, strictly upper triangular degree-zero matrix
in the remaining factors. Its adjacent entries give the actual
degree-two graph maps, and its skip entries give degree-three maps.
Take zero cross-group higher entries. The line coupling is an
actual cone into the groups with \(a_3=0\), as described below;
it has no entry into the groups with \(a_3=1\).

These definitions give actual perfect three-layer objects, including
objects with nonzero consecutive graph couplings and nonzero line
coupling. They have no negative self-Ext and the stated whole
character. Every such object has a full degree-two trace kernel of
dimension at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G24-lower}
                   64(d+1)^6-1\ \ge\ 4095.
\end{equation}
The conclusion includes all closed strictly upper triangular \(H\)
in the specified product models. It does not assert that a new
cross-group higher morphism or a different coherent lift defines
the same object. The exact product quasi-isomorphism and an optional
gauge map within each product model are given below.

\paragraph{Why the specified product groups arise.}
For one elliptic pair the actual Ext polynomials are (N16.8).
Multiplication by the tangent \(H^1(\mathcal O)\) class on that
pair has the following literal effects. For different slopes,
the Ext is supported at finitely many transverse points and is
concentrated in degree one, so this multiplication is zero on Ext.
For the same slope and different twist exponents, the elliptic
line ratio has positive or negative degree. Its coherent cohomology
occurs in just one degree, and multiplication by \(H^1(\mathcal O)\)
is zero; the same remains true after the normal Ext factor.
Thus every nonzero graph entry obtained by this scalar multiplication
preserves the third slope and third exponent. We choose its product
representatives in precisely these groups and set other higher
entries to zero as part of the construction. This specifies the
coherent model; it does not replace a separately supplied morphism
by its Ext class without tracking its higher entries.

\paragraph{The actual common factor and its dg quasi-isomorphism.}
Focus on the group \(\epsilon_3=0,a_3=1\). Write
\(S_3=E_i\times E_i\) for the third pair and \(A_{\rm rest}\)
for the other three pairs. Its common third-factor object is
\[
 C_3=\mathcal O_{\Gamma_0}\otimes L_3
       =M^d\boxtimes\mathcal O_0\quad\text{on }S_3.
\]
All remaining data form a finite direct sum \(T\) of sheaves on
\(A_{\rm rest}\), each placed in its prescribed shift zero, one
or two. Let \(B=R\operatorname{Hom}(T,T)\) be an actual dg
endomorphism algebra, using complexes of sheaves to compute it.
The derived product Hom map gives the dg model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G24-product-model}
 R\operatorname{Hom}(C_3\boxtimes T,C_3\boxtimes T)
             \simeq\bigwedge(\eta,\varepsilon)\otimes B,
 \qquad |\eta|=|\varepsilon|=1.
\end{equation}
Here are explicit quasi-isomorphisms for the two factors. For
\(M^d\) on the tangent elliptic curve, its endomorphism complex
is \(R\Gamma(\mathcal O_{E_i})\). Inclusion of constant and
translation-invariant Dolbeault forms gives the dg algebra
\(\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\eta\), with \(\eta^2=0\),
and is a quasi-isomorphism since the two coherent cohomology
groups both have dimension one.

For the normal point, restriction to its local ring computes its
derived self-Hom: all sheaf Ext is supported at that point and has
no higher coherent cohomology. With a local parameter \(z\), use
the actual two-term Koszul resolution \(K=(R\xrightarrow{z}R)\)
in degrees minus one and zero. In its endomorphism dg algebra,
the identity and the degree-one map from the first copy of \(R\)
to the second given by multiplication by one define the subalgebra
\(\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\varepsilon\). The latter map is
closed and has square zero. It represents the nonzero residue
generator of \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(R/(z),R/(z))\).
The Koszul calculation gives just one copy of \(\mathbb C\)
in degrees zero and one and none otherwise, proving this dg
quasi-isomorphism. Tensoring these two explicit maps, followed by
the derived Kunneth map, proves \eqref{eq:G24-product-model},
with \(\eta\varepsilon=-\varepsilon\eta\).

Choose a closed matrix \(H\in B^0\), strictly upper triangular
when the layer blocks are ordered by decreasing shift \(2,1,0\).
The element \(\eta H\) has total degree one and satisfies the
literal Maurer--Cartan equation
\[
                  d(\eta H)+(\eta H)^2=0.
\]
The first term is zero because \(d_BH=0\); the second is zero
because \(\eta^2=0\). The finite twisted-complex construction
in this dg model therefore gives an actual perfect object \(C_H\).
Transport through \eqref{eq:G24-product-model} gives the same
actual finite iterated-cone construction on the abelian variety.
No further compatibility equation is omitted.

For precision, a normal-factor term \(\eta\varepsilon K\)
with closed \(K\in B^{-1}\), when it occurs in the same product
model, can also be accounted for by an explicit map. For this layer
distribution \(H^{-1}(B)=0\): between adjacent layers its only
possible groups are Hom between opposite-sign graph supports,
which vanish; between shifts zero and two its only possible group
is \(\operatorname{Ext}^1\) between the two positive parts, which
vanishes by the support separation proved below. All other groups
are negative Ext. Thus write \(K=d_BS\). The invertible degree-zero
operator \(g=1+\eta\varepsilon S\), whose inverse is
\(1-\eta\varepsilon S\), satisfies the literal equation
\[
 g\bigl(d+\eta H+\eta\varepsilon K\bigr)g^{-1}=d+\eta H.
\]
Indeed \([\eta\varepsilon S,d]=-\eta\varepsilon d_BS\),
and its products with \(\eta H\) vanish because they contain
\(\eta^2\). This is the literal gauge map within the specified
product model; no statement about cross-group higher terms follows.

\paragraph{The diagonal degree-zero endomorphisms.}
The algebra \(H^0(B)=\operatorname{End}(T)\) is upper triangular
in the same decreasing-shift order. A map in the opposite direction
would be negative Ext between sheaves. In a diagonal layer, distinct
rest-graph supports have zero Hom. On the same support, different
packages with \(a_3=1\) have exactly one positive and one negative
degree in their elliptic line ratio, since their two-element sets
\(J\) are distinct subsets of the other three indices. Such a
ratio has no global section. Therefore the diagonal quotient of
\(H^0(B)\) is exactly
\[
                 \prod_{\text{graph/package types }U}
                                      \operatorname{Mat}_{m_U}(\mathbb C).
\]
The class of \(H\) is strictly upper triangular, so every
commutator \([H,b]\), \(b\in H^0(B)\), has zero diagonal
blocks. This remains a literal assertion about the actual algebra,
not a choice of a semisimple approximation.

\paragraph{Explicit closed endomorphisms and their non-boundaries.}
Take any matrix \(a\) in one diagonal multiplicity block,
represented by the same constant matrix on the identical complexes
of that block and zero elsewhere. It is closed in \(B^0\).
The actual degree-two endomorphism
\[
                         f_a=\eta\varepsilon a
\]
is closed for the twisted differential
\(D=d_B+[\eta H,-]\), since every extra commutator contains
\(\eta^2\).

Suppose a linear combination of these endomorphisms is a boundary.
Write a total degree-one preimage uniquely in the exterior basis as
\[
 b=b_0+\eta b_\eta+\varepsilon b_\varepsilon
                  +\eta\varepsilon b_{\eta\varepsilon},
 \quad
 |b_0|=1,\quad |b_\eta|=|b_\varepsilon|=0,\quad
 |b_{\eta\varepsilon}|=-1
\]
in \(B\). The \(\varepsilon\)-coefficient of \(Db\) forces
\(d_Bb_\varepsilon=0\). The
\(\eta\varepsilon\)-coefficient then gives, in \(H^0(B)\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G24-diagonal-boundary}
                            [a]=[H,[b_\varepsilon]].
\end{equation}
With the displayed exterior order and the tensor differential
\(d(\alpha\otimes b)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha\otimes d_Bb\),
the cochain equation is exactly
\(a=d_Bb_{\eta\varepsilon}+[H,b_\varepsilon]\).
Its right side has zero
diagonal blocks. Hence a nonzero diagonal matrix cannot be a
boundary, and the maps \(a\mapsto[f_a]\) are injective on all
diagonal multiplicity blocks. This computation also treats arbitrary
\(H\), including simultaneous nonzero adjacent and skip maps.

\paragraph{The actual trace calculation.}
For a traceless matrix \(a\) in one multiplicity block, every
component of the full Atiyah trace of \(f_a\) is zero.
To see the map explicitly, represent the filtered perfect object
by the corresponding upper triangular complex of locally free
resolutions and choose block-diagonal local connections. The Atiyah
matrix is upper triangular, and its diagonal entries are the Atiyah
matrices of the underlying graph blocks. On the selected block all
copies and their connections are identical, so the diagonal entry
of \(f_a\operatorname{At}^r\) has multiplicity factor \(a\),
while all other diagonal entries vanish. The supertrace is therefore
\(\operatorname{tr}(a)\) times the common scalar trace, hence zero.
Strictly upper triangular terms never contribute a diagonal trace.

The actual product quasi-isomorphisms preserve this filtration.
Their maps on a diagonal multiplicity block are the same on every
copy; any higher correction is strictly upper triangular. Thus the
same trace computation applies to the transported perfect object.
Equivalently, in the categorical cyclic trace every term with an
upper triangular transition cannot return to its original block,
and the remaining terms have the displayed multiplicity trace.
This is the full categorical/Atiyah trace already identified in
HD-N16, not only its rank component.

In the component under consideration take
\(U=(J=\{1,2\},\epsilon=e_4)\). Its multiplicity is
\(m_U=8(d+1)^3\), and it belongs to the positive upper layer.
The traceless matrices in this block have dimension
\(m_U^2-1=64(d+1)^6-1\). The non-boundary and trace calculations
prove \eqref{eq:G24-lower} on \(C_H\).

\paragraph{The full whole-character object and its actual gluability.}
For the other graph groups use the same product construction, or
the zero upper triangular matrix. Add the line by any actual cone
into the groups with \(a_3=0\), keeping its entries into
\(a_3=1\) equal to zero. Thus \(C_H\) is an actual direct
summand of the full object. Its nonzero trace-kernel classes remain
nonzero and trace-zero in that object.

There are explicit nonzero consecutive and line couplings within
the complementary groups. Use \(J=\{3,4\}\) and the chain
\[
 B_{-e_1-e_2}\quad\longrightarrow\quad B_{-e_1}
                      \quad\longrightarrow\quad B_{e_1}.
\]
The first graph has positive coefficient and even support size,
the middle graph negative coefficient, and the last positive
coefficient and odd support size. Both edges change just one
coordinate. Multiply their nonzero transverse degree-one classes
by the same \(\eta\) in the shared third factor. Their product
is zero. A nonzero line-to-middle class comes from the two negative
cohomology factors three and four; multiplication by \(\eta\)
kills it. Exactly the Hom-sequence lift and cochain nullhomotopy
construction of HD-G23 gives two actual nonsplit cones. This
couples the line and both graph edges while leaving \(C_H\) as
the specified direct summand.

The parity distribution has no negative self-Ext between its
underlying shifted blocks. Hom between opposite-sign supports is
zero. For the positive parts in shifts zero and two, their graph
slopes never differ in only one coordinate. This follows from the
signs of the odd coefficient \(S_\epsilon\): positive support
sizes one and three have respectively a positive single slope
and a majority of positive slopes, while positive sizes two and
four have respectively two negative slopes and at most one positive
slope. Adding or removing one nonzero coordinate never joins these
two positive sets; changing its sign preserves parity. Therefore
the needed \(\operatorname{Ext}^{0,1}\) groups vanish by the
factor Ext formula. The line-to-graph index is at least two.
The exact two-cone negative-Ext argument of HD-G23 now applies
to the displayed actual constructions. Whole Chern characters are
unchanged by these cones and shifts, giving \(e^\theta+96w_d\).

All four positive single-coordinate supports occur in degree minus
two, and the line remains the degree-zero rank-one quotient. The
literal support equations therefore give trivial original point
stabilizer. Canonical inherited deck-subgroup actions and actual
Fourier--Mukai conjugates retain the nonzero trace kernel by the
same intermediate-quotient descent and injective pullback maps as
HD-N16. Arbitrary newly selected linearizations and separately
introduced cross-group higher entries require their own maps and
are not included in this assertion.
