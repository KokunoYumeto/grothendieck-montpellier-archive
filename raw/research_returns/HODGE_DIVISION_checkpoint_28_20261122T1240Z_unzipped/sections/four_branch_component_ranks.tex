\subsection{HD-I13: every four-branch reflection component}
\label{sec:HD-I13}

Keep the groups, signature and degree range of HD-I12.  Every
reflection-only Nielsen component whose intermediate curve has
$g=n-1$ has generic period rank
\begin{equation}
                         3n-2.                 \tag{I13.1}
\end{equation}
There are four cyclic branch points in this case.  In particular,
for $n=5$ every such component has period-image dimension $13$.

Write the four colors as $s_1,\ldots,s_4$, with
$0<s_j<d$ and $\sum_j s_j=2d$.  Let
\[
 L^d=\mathcal O_Y\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^4s_jP_j\Bigr),\quad
 R=P_1+\cdots+P_4,\quad A=\omega_YL,\quad
 B=\omega_YL^{-1}(R),\quad M=L^2(-R).
\]
Then $\deg L=2$, $\deg A=\deg B=2g$, and
$h^0(A)=h^0(B)=g+1=n$.  The line bundle $A$ is globally generated:
$H^1(A(-p))=0$ follows from degree $2g-1>2g-2$ for every $p$.
At every point with $M\not\simeq\mathcal O_Y$, Riemann--Roch gives
\[
 h^1(BA^{-1})=h^1(M^{-1})=g-1=n-2.
\]
The exact Green lemma used and sourced in HD-I12 therefore makes
the primitive multiplication map surjective.  Its target has dimension
$3g-3+4=3n-2$.

We prove that $M\not\simeq\mathcal O_Y$ holds on a nonempty open subset
of every cyclic-cover component with these colors.  This is a local
deformation assertion, so it does not require a connectedness theorem
for the global cyclic-cover moduli space.  Taking the $d$-th power gives
the actual line-bundle identity
\begin{equation}
 M^d=\mathcal O_Y\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^4(2s_j-d)P_j\Bigr).
                                                   \tag{I13.2}
\end{equation}
If one coefficient $c=2s_j-d$ is nonzero, fix $Y$ and the other three
points and vary $P_j$ in a small open set.  Roots lift this variation
locally because multiplication by $d$ on the Picard variety is etale.
The right side of (I13.2) varies nonconstantly: it is a constant translate
of the Abel map $P\mapsto\mathcal O_Y(cP-cP_0)$, which is nonconstant
since the Abel map is nonconstant for $g\geq1$ and multiplication by
$c\ne0$ on the Jacobian is finite.  Thus $M$ cannot be trivial on the
whole local component.  Triviality of a degree-zero line bundle is
closed in the relative Picard variety, proving the asserted nonempty
open subset in this case.

If all four coefficients in (I13.2) vanish, $d$ is even and all colors
equal $d/2$.  In this case $M\simeq\mathcal O_Y$ would give
$L^2\simeq\mathcal O_Y(R)$.  Let $t$ be the section of $L^2$ with
divisor $R$.  Under the cyclic root identification, the section of
$L^d$ defining the cover is a nonzero scalar multiple of $t^{d/2}$;
rescale $t$ to absorb this scalar.  The cover equation factors globally
as
\begin{equation}
 y^d-t^{d/2}
   =\prod_{\xi\in\mu_{d/2}}(y^2-\xi t).          \tag{I13.3}
\end{equation}
Over the complement of $R$ these are $d/2$ disjoint nonempty subcovers.
Normalization separates their function-field factors and therefore
has at least $d/2>1$ connected components.  The cyclic quotient in
HD-I02 is connected, so this is impossible.  Consequently $M$ is
nontrivial at every point of every connected cyclic-cover component
in this remaining color case.

The map-lifting proof (I12.4) applies unchanged at $g=n-1$, since
$m\geq n+4>g-1$.  Thus each original Nielsen component maps locally
smoothly to its cyclic-cover deformation space and meets the nonempty
open subset just proved.  On that subset its period differential has
rank $3n-2$, while HD-I04 bounds the rank everywhere by the same
number.  The polarized isogeny of HD-I02 identifies these with the
original multiplicity-factor periods, proving (I13.1).

Nonemptiness is also explicit: for every proper divisor $e$ of $d$,
take two colors $e$ and two colors $d-e$, assign one handle monodromy
to a generator of $\mu_d$, and apply the degree-$m$ pencil and
colored-transposition construction of HD-I12.  The resulting group
is exactly $G(d,e,m)$ and its intermediate genus is $n-1$.

The author, GPT-6 Astra Pro with inherited effort, read the complete

degree-$2g$ global generation, the nontrivial degree-zero cohomology,

color case.  Together, HD-I12 and HD-I13 determine all reflection-only
Nielsen components with $g\leq n-1$.
