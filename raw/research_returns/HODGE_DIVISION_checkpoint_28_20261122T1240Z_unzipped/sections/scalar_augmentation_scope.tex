\subsection{HD-S33: natural scalar \(A_m\) sixfold sources of rank nine are split}
\label{sec:scalar-augmentation-scope}

Let \(N\ge3\), \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), and let
\[
 G=\mu_4\times S_N,
 \qquad V=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in K^N:\ \sum x_j=0\},
 \qquad \rho(\zeta,\sigma)=\zeta P_\sigma|_V.
 \tag{S33.1}
\]
Consider a connected sphere cover with full monodromy \(G\), and
its actual natural primitive Jacobian factor of signature \((3,3)\).
Let \(r_0\) be the number of branch values with nontrivial scalar
phase. Its restricted-theta Hermitian determinant has exactly the
ordinary norm class
\[
                         [-N^{\,r_0-2}].                    \tag{S33.2}
\]
Every such source with odd \(r_0\) has at most ten true branch
values, and period rank at most seven. Consequently every rank-nine
family from (S33.1) is split. This includes arbitrary \(N\); the
coefficient discriminant \(N\) is retained throughout the proof.

\paragraph{1. The exact permutation splitting.}
Let \(\chi(\zeta,\sigma)=\zeta\). Define the twisted permutation
module \(M=K^N\) with action \(\zeta P_\sigma\), and its scalar
line \(K_\chi\). There are actual coefficient maps
\[
 j:K_\chi\longrightarrow M,\quad j(a)=(a,\ldots,a),
 \qquad p:M\longrightarrow K_\chi,\quad
                 p(x)=\frac1N\sum_jx_j.
 \tag{S33.3}
\]
They are equivariant and \(pj=1\). Their complement \(1-jp\)
has image exactly \(V\). For \(h_0(x,y)=\sum\bar x_jy_j\),
the decomposition is orthogonal and the pullback along \(j\)
is the scalar-line form multiplied by \(N\). In the standard
augmentation basis \(u_j-u_N\), \(1\le j<N\), the Gram matrix
is \(I_{N-1}+\boldsymbol1\boldsymbol1^t\), of determinant \(N\).
Thus the maps and their polarization scalar are explicit.

The standard augmentation representation is absolutely irreducible.
Indeed a nonzero invariant subspace contains a vector with two
unequal coordinates. Applying \(1-(ab)\) produces a nonzero
multiple of \(u_a-u_b\), and its permutation orbit spans \(V\).
The scalar twist preserves this argument. Its character field is
\(K\), since the central element \(z=(i,1)\) has trace
\((N-1)i\). The representation is faithful for \(N\ge3\): a
scalar permutation on the rational augmentation module has scalar
\(1\) or \(-1\); the latter would give permutation trace
\(2-N<0\) on \(K^N\), which is impossible. The former is
the identity permutation.

\paragraph{2. Boundary modules without division by cycle lengths.}
Put \(R=\mathbb Z[i,1/2]\). The coefficient lattice
\(M_R=R^N\), with Gram \(I_N\), and the scalar coefficient
lattice \(R_\chi\), with Gram \(1\), are both self-dual.
Take a local monodromy \(g=(\zeta,\sigma)\). On a permutation
cycle of length \(\ell\), use the unit basis change
\[
 w_j=\zeta^j u_{\sigma^j(a)},\quad0\le j<\ell.
\]
Then \(g w_j=w_{j+1}\) until the last step, and
\(g w_{\ell-1}=\zeta^\ell w_0\). Thus the coinvariant module
of this cycle is exactly
\[
                  R/(\zeta^\ell-1)R.                     \tag{S33.4}
\]
If \(\zeta^\ell=1\), it is free of rank one, and the invariant
module is generated by \(\sum_jw_j\). The coefficient pairing
of this invariant generator with the coinvariant generator
\([w_0]\) is one. The relations \(w_{j+1}-w_j\) explicitly
give the kernel of the map which sums the coefficients in this
basis.

If \(\zeta^\ell\ne1\), its difference from one is one of
\(-2,i-1,-i-1\), all units of \(R\). Both modules are zero;
the inverse to \(1-g\) on this cycle is the actual matrix
\[
            \frac{1+g+\cdots+g^{\ell-1}}{1-\zeta^\ell}.
\]
Consequently all boundary invariant and coinvariant modules for
\(M_R\) and \(R_\chi\) are free. This calculation does not
invert \(\ell\), \(N\), or \(N!\).

\paragraph{3. Perfect integral parabolic pairings.}
Let \(\Sigma\) be the punctured sphere with small open branch
discs removed. Full monodromy contains \(z\), and \(i-1\) is
a unit in \(R\). Hence absolute zeroth homology vanishes for
both coefficient systems and their duals. Absolute first homology
is free from the graph cellular complex. Poincar\'e--Lefschetz
cap product and the universal coefficient sequence give perfect
cross-pairings with relative first homology, which is also free.

For either lattice \(L\), the exact pair sequence identifies the
cokernel of
\(H_1(\Sigma,L)\to H_1(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;L)\)
with the free boundary zeroth homology. Its image \(P(L)\) is
therefore primitive. The two absolute-to-relative maps are adjoint.
Extend a functional on the primitive image to the relative module
and use the perfect cross-pairing: the resulting absolute class
maps to its unique dual parabolic class, with kernel identified by
adjointness. This proves that the induced parabolic pairing is
perfect over \(R\), as in the full argument of HD-S27,
paragraph 2. Only the explicitly proved freedom of the boundary
modules is needed here; no averaging over an odd-order inertia
group is used.

The parabolic image over \(K\) is the image of compact support
cohomology in ordinary cohomology, and identifies through these
cap products with \(H^1(\mathbb P^1,j_*L_K)\). These are the
actual restriction and cap-product maps, and preserve the cup
pairing. The integral images just constructed supply lattices in
this rational cohomology; their perfection does not require an
identification of integral invariant summands by an averaging
denominator. For the cap-product formulation see
Dettweiler--Wewers, \emph{Variation of parabolic cohomology and
Poincar\'e duality}, Proposition 2.1, printed pp.153--154; the
primitive-image argument proves the integral surjectivity here.

Write \(H_M=iS_M\) and \(H_\chi=iS_\chi\) for the Hermitian
forms obtained from these skew-Hermitian cup forms, with the
Gaussian action convention of HD-S23. Both have determinants in
\(R^\times\cap\mathbb Q=\{\pm2^a:a\in\mathbb Z\}\).
This is the place where self-duality is used. The augmentation
lattice, whose discriminant is \(N\), has not been asserted
self-dual.

\paragraph{4. Exact ranks and determinant.}
The scalar monodromy is full \(\mu_4\). Its parabolic rank is
\(r_0-2\), by the sphere Euler formula with its two zero global
invariant spaces. The natural sixfold hypothesis means that the
augmentation parabolic rank is six. Applying the actual coefficient
maps (S33.3) to the cellular complexes and to the absolute-to-relative
maps gives the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 (P(M)_K,H_M)
   \simeq (P(\chi)_K,NH_\chi)\ \perp\ (P(V),H_V),
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}_K P(M)=r_0+4.
 \tag{S33.5}
\]
In particular this is an isomorphism of paired spaces induced by
specified maps. It is not a decomposition deduced from equal ranks.

Choose bases of the two perfect integral parabolic lattices, and
any \(K\)-basis of \(P(V)\). The change of basis in (S33.5)
contributes the norm of its determinant. Thus
\[
 [\det H_V]=
  [\det H_M]\,[N^{\,r_0-2}\det H_\chi]^{-1}.
 \tag{S33.6}
\]
The quotient of the two perfect-lattice determinants is a signed
power of two. Since \(N>0\) and \(H_V\) has signature
\((3,3)\), its sign is negative. The identity \(2=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1+i)\)
removes every power of two. Finally \(N^{2(r_0-2)}\) is a norm
of a rational number. Equation (S33.6) is precisely
\([\det H_V]=[-N^{r_0-2}]\).

For a direct check of the ranks, let \(c_V(g)=\operatorname{rank}(1-\rho(g))\).
Then \(c_M(g)=c_V(g)+1\) at the \(r_0\) nontrivial scalar
values and equals \(c_V(g)\) elsewhere. The sixfold formula
\(\sum c_V=2(N-1)+6=2N+4\) consequently gives
\(\dim P(M)=\sum c_M-2N=r_0+4\), in agreement with the
actual splitting.

\paragraph{5. The fibre product and both actual Jacobian realizations.}
Let \(\widetilde C\to\mathbb P^1\) be the full \(G\)-cover,
and let \(S_{N-1}\) fix the first letter. Set
\[
 C_0=\widetilde C/S_N,\qquad
 Y=\widetilde C/(\mu_4\times S_{N-1}),\qquad
 X=\widetilde C/(1\times S_{N-1}).
\]
Their degrees over the sphere are \(4,N,4N\), respectively.
Over the branch complement, the map
\(X\to C_0\times_{\mathbb P^1}Y\) sends a coset to its two
quotient cosets and is the bijection
\(\mu_4\times\{1,\ldots,N\}\) on every fibre. Thus \(X\)
is the normalization of this fibre product. This proves the claimed
cover identification, including all ramified points by normalization.

Let \(p:X\to C_0\), of degree \(N\), and let \(\sigma\)
be the order-four deck transformation of \(X\to Y\).
Write \(a_\sigma\) for its covariant Jacobian action and
\(\epsilon=(1-a_\sigma^2)/2\). Pullback and norm satisfy
\(p_*p^*=[N]\); they are adjoint for the canonical principal
polarizations. They commute with the cyclic actions. Consequently
\[
 \pi_A=\epsilon\left(1-\frac1N p^*p_*\right),\qquad
 A_X=\operatorname{im}(2N\pi_A:J(X)\to J(X))
 \tag{S33.7}
\]
are a self-adjoint rational projector and an actual abelian
subvariety. The displayed endomorphism is integral, since
\(2N\pi_A=(1-a_\sigma^2)(N-p^*p_*)\).
The local permutation-sheet cochain map identifies the cyclic
primitive cohomology of \(X\) with the parabolic module \(M\)
and the pullback of that of \(C_0\) with the constant-coordinate
map \(j\). Integration along the degree-\(N\) map gives exactly
the factor \(N\) in (S33.5). The complementary rational homology
of (S33.7) is therefore the augmentation multiplicity space, so
\(A_X\) is the asserted sixfold.

Here is a second, explicit primitive idempotent on the full
Jacobian, fixing also its scalar relative to \(H_V\). Take
\(v=u_1-u_2\in V\), of norm two, and put
\[
 e_v=\frac{N-1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}
       \operatorname{Re}h_0(v,\rho(g^{-1})v)\,g,
 \qquad D=\frac{|G|}{N-1}=\frac{4N!}{N-1}.
 \tag{S33.8}
\]
Absolute irreducibility and matrix-coefficient orthogonality prove
\(e_v^2=e_v=e_v^*\) and \(e_v\mathbb Q[G]e_v=K\).
The inner products are Gaussian integers, so \(De_v\in\mathbb Z[G]\).
Use the actual left deck action \(a_g=L_{g,*}\), and put
\(B=\operatorname{im}(De_v)\), \(i_B=a_z^{-1}|_B\).
The coefficient map \(j_x(g)=h_0(v,\rho(g)x)/2\), followed by
\(\operatorname{PD}(j+\bar j)\), is the actual \(K\)-linear
map of HD-S23. Its complex Schur scalar is \(|G|/(2(N-1))\);
the two conjugate cross terms give the exact Hermitian identity
\[
                         h_B=D H_V.                       \tag{S33.9}
\]
The determinant multiplier \(D^6=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(D^3)\) is
a norm, proving (S33.2) for the restricted-theta factor itself.

For clarity, these two Jacobian realizations are related by an
actual isogeny with its scalar specified. Let
\(\alpha:\widetilde C\to X\), of degree \((N-1)!\), and put
\(w=Nu_1-\sum_ju_j\). The \(S_{N-1}\)-fixed augmentation
line is \(Kw\), with norm \(N(N-1)\), and
\(h_0(v,w)=N\). The map
\[
                  q=(De_v)\alpha^*|_{A_X}:A_X\to B
\]
is integral. On this coefficient line it sends \(w\) to
\(D(N/2)v\) and is nonzero; on the multiplicity space it is
the identity tensor this line map. Thus it induces a rational
homology isomorphism and is an isogeny. Pullback along \(\alpha\)
multiplies the curve Jacobian form by \((N-1)!\), and the
orthogonal rank-one projection has norm ratio
\(N/[2(N-1)]\). Hence, for the two restricted polarizations,
\[
 q^*\theta_B
     =D^2 (N-1)!\frac{N}{2(N-1)}\,\theta_X|_{A_X}
                   \quad\text{in rational cohomology}.     \tag{S33.10}
\]
This also proves directly that the fibre-product realization has
the same ordinary determinant class. The scalar cyclic deck actions commute
with \(q\), and the Gaussian action on each factor is the inverse
of the scalar deck action, so this isogeny is \(K\)-linear.

\paragraph{6. Odd scalar count forces low period rank.}
Let \((g_1,\ldots,g_b)\) be the generating product-one tuple of
true branch elements. Faithfulness proved in paragraph 1 gives
\(c_V(g_j)\ge1\). For a permutation \(\sigma\), let \(k(\sigma)\)
be its cycle count, and \(k_q(\sigma)\) the number of its cycle
lengths divisible by \(q\). Direct diagonalization of each cyclic
permutation block gives the exact formulas
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \zeta&c_V(\zeta,\sigma)\\\hline
 1&N-k(\sigma)\\
 -1&N-1-k_2(\sigma)\\
 i\text{ or }-i&N-1-k_4(\sigma).
\end{array}
 \tag{S33.11}
\]
Here the one removed permutation-trivial line has eigenvalue
\(\zeta\), so it reduces the fixed space only in the first row.

The scalar phases generate \(\mu_4\). Thus at least one is odd
in its exponent of \(i\). Their product is one, so their number
of odd exponents is even and at least two. If \(r_0\) is odd,
the number of phases equal to \(-1\) is then odd, so at least
one such phase also occurs. Select two odd phases and one \(-1\).
Using \(k_q(\sigma)\le\lfloor N/q\rfloor\), their total
codimension is at least
\[
 2\bigl(N-1-\lfloor N/4\rfloor\bigr)
   +N-1-\lfloor N/2\rfloor\ \ge\ 2N-3.
\]
Every other true branch contributes at least one. Since the sum
of codimensions is exactly \(2N+4\),
\[
 2N+4\ge(2N-3)+(b-3),\qquad b\le10.
 \tag{S33.12}
\]
The marked Hurwitz component is locally finite over the ordered
configuration space of its branch values modulo \(PGL_2\):
the finite group supplies finitely many generating tuples, and
topological continuation followed by filling the fixed local
branched discs gives the actual local cover family. Its dimension
is \(b-3\). The relative Jacobian and the fixed projector
(S33.8) give its polarized period map. Thus odd \(r_0\) forces
period rank at most seven.

An even \(r_0\) makes (S33.2) exactly \([-1]\), since
\(N^{r_0-2}\) is the norm of the rational number
\(N^{(r_0-2)/2}\). In signature \((3,3)\), the exact Meyer
reductions and final binary norm equation of HD-S03a identify this
ordinary determinant class with the hyperbolic space \(U^3\).
In particular every rank-nine source is split by that already
proved isometry construction.
For the adjacent case \(N=2\), the augmentation character is
\((\zeta,\sigma)\mapsto\zeta\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\).
The actual quotient cover by its kernel is cyclic of degree four.
A sixfold primitive multiplicity has eight nontrivial character
branch values, so its period map factors through their
five-dimensional configuration space. Extra kernel branch values
do not change this quotient or its factor. Hence that case does
not give rank nine either.

\paragraph{7. Exact scope and checks.}
The determinant is asserted for the restricted polarization and
the explicit isogeny above. A specified rational \(K\)-similitude
changes its rank-six determinant by a norm. On a rank-nine family,
any other compatible flat polarization transported by a fixed
isogeny has the same conclusion: pull it back and apply the
common-centralizer calculation of HD-S17 on the open unitary
domain. Its self-adjoint scalar is a positive rational number.
This statement does not replace the polarization at an isolated
special point by an arbitrary one.

The proof has been read in full by its author. The accompanying
finite certificate checks the displayed coefficient changes,
local cycle identities, and every permutation cycle partition up
to its stated finite cutoff; the universal formulas and the
rank bound are proved above without extrapolation. This result
restricts exactly the natural scalar-augmentation construction.
It does not assert a determinant restriction for other
representations, other groups, or unrelated geometric sources.
