\section{HD-R17: the rank-four tensor obstruction}
\label{sec:rank-four-tensor-obstruction}

Every finite absolutely irreducible matrix group in dimension
\(m>16\), generated by its actual moving-rank-four elements,
preserves no nontrivial complex tensor decomposition, including
permutations of tensor factors. This applies directly to the
absolutely irreducible normal subgroup \(N_4\) generated by
the actual moving-rank-four coefficient elements in the
remaining no-rank-three source branch, where \(m\ge21\).
All matrices, the original cover and its prescribed primitive
corner are retained in this application.

\paragraph{1. A rank-four generator fixes every tensor factor.}
Consider an actual linear isomorphism
\[
 \Phi:V_{\mathbf C}\xrightarrow{\sim}
            W_1\otimes\cdots\otimes W_t,
 \qquad t\ge2,\quad d_i=\dim W_i\ge2,
 \quad\prod_i d_i=m.                                    \tag{R17.1}
\]
The universal tensor-cycle calculation in HD-R14 applies to
each original finite-order operator. For a nontrivial cycle
of \(\ell\) factors of common dimension \(d\), transport
bases along its actual transition maps. Its cycle operator is
\[
 (u_1,\ldots,u_\ell)\longmapsto
               (P u_\ell,u_1,\ldots,u_{\ell-1}),         \tag{R17.2}
\]
where the notation denotes its action on pure tensors and
\(P\) is the literal product of those transition maps.
Finite order of the entire operator makes each relevant
tensor factor semisimple up to a scalar, as shown by the
pure-tensor functional argument in HD-R14. In an eigenbasis
of \(P\), cyclic words give weighted permutation orbits.
Every specified eigenspace has at most one dimension on
each such orbit. There are \(d\) constant words, and
each other orbit has at least two words. Hence the largest
eigenspace of this cycle has dimension at most
\[
       (d^\ell+d)/2\le3d^\ell/4.                        \tag{R17.3}
\]
Tensoring with the remaining factors preserves the bound
of three quarters of the full dimension for each specified
eigenvalue: sum over their semisimple eigenvalues with their
multiplicities. Thus a generator permuting tensor factors
would have moving rank at least \(m/4>4\), a contradiction.

\paragraph{2. Each generator has one active tensor factor.}
Now a generator acts as a product of operators on the fixed
factors. The other universal estimate from HD-R14 is the
following exact two-factor eigenvalue bound. For two
nonscalar semisimple operators on dimensions \(a,b\ge2\),
the multiplicity of any specified eigenvalue of their tensor
product is at most
\((a-1)(b-1)+1\). Indeed the matching eigenvalue blocks
form a partial bijection, and their sorted multiplicities
give the successive upper bounds
\[
 a_1b_1+(a-a_1)b_2
 \le(a-1)b_1+b_2
 \le(a-2)b_1+b
 \le(a-1)(b-1)+1.                                      \tag{R17.4}
\]
Both multiplicity lists have at least two nonzero parts;
these are exactly the inequalities proved with the actual
eigenspace decompositions in HD-R14. Thus the moving rank
is at least \(a+b-2\).

If two of the fixed tensor factors were nonscalar, take
one against the product of all others. Both resulting
operators are nonscalar: a tensor product which is scalar
has every factor scalar, by evaluation against nonzero
pure-tensor functionals. Their dimensions have product
\(m>16\), and
\(a+b-2\ge2\sqrt m-2>6\), contradicting moving rank four.
Consequently exactly one factor is nonscalar. Absorb all
other scalar factors into it, without changing the original
operator. For some index \(i\) this gives literally
\[
             \Phi x\Phi^{-1}=A_i'\otimes I,
 \qquad4=\operatorname{rank}(A_i'-I)q_i,
 \qquad q_i=\prod_{j\ne i}d_j\in\{2,4\}.              \tag{R17.5}
\]
An entirely scalar generator would move either zero or
all \(m>16\) dimensions and is excluded by its rank four.

\paragraph{3. The actual common invariant subspace.}
Two different factors cannot be active for two generators.
For distinct indices \(i,j\), formula (R17.5) would give
\[
 q_iq_j\le16,
 \qquad q_iq_j=m\prod_{k\ne i,j}d_k\ge m>16,            \tag{R17.6}
\]
a contradiction. All generators therefore have the same
active factor \(W_i\), acting identically on its nontrivial
complement \(U=\bigotimes_{j\ne i}W_j\), of dimension
two or four. Choose any nonzero \(u\in U\). The literal map
\[
            W_i\longrightarrow V_{\mathbf C},
                  w\longmapsto\Phi^{-1}(w\otimes u)      \tag{R17.7}
\]
is injective and has a proper nonzero image. Every original
generator preserves it by (R17.5), so the entire generated
group preserves it. This contradicts absolute irreducibility.

This proves the assertion for the original matrices. Applying
it to the actual subgroup \(N_4\) changes neither the
coefficient field action nor any curve, Jacobian operator,
polarization or period differential. It removes tensor
structures in that branch; the remaining actual rank-four
source analysis is not asserted to be complete.
