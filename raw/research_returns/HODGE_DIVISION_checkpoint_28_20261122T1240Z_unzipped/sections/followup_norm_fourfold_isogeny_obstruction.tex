\section[HD-F1-S30: isogeny transports and orbit sums retain the obstruction]{HD-F1-S30: the actual obstruction survives finite isogeny transport, its canonical finite point actions, and every finite point-orbit sum}
\label{sec:f1-norm-fourfold-isogeny-obstruction}

Fix any actual norm sheaf $E=\mathcal O_Y$ on $P$ in
the geometric open of S29, and retain its curve map $\mu_C$
and $r_C=\operatorname{rank}\mu_C$. Let
\[
                    f:P\longrightarrow A,\qquad
                    g:B\longrightarrow P
 \tag{F1S30.1}
\]
be actual isogenies of complex abelian varieties. Transport
the rational Weil action through these isogenies. On $A$
use the rational ample class $\theta_A$ characterized by
$f^*\theta_A=\beta$, and on $B$ use $\theta_B=g^*\beta$.
An integral positive multiple of $\theta_A$ is an actual
polarization and gives the same deformation tangent.
The sheaves
\[
                         F_+=f_*E,\qquad F_-=g^*E
 \tag{F1S30.2}
\]
are ordinary simple coherent sheaves. Each has an actual
$(16-r_C)$-dimensional subspace of its degree-two full
trace kernel, invariant for every specified coherent finite
point-action lift. In particular both fail weak
point-equivariant semiregularity.

Their point stabilizers are exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Stab}(F_+)
   &=\{0\}\times
       \ker\bigl(f^*:\operatorname{Pic}^0(A)
                              \to\operatorname{Pic}^0(P)\bigr),\\
 \operatorname{Stab}(F_-)&=\ker(g)\times\{\mathcal O_B\}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{F1S30.3}
\]
The full groups displayed here have actual coherent lifts,
constructed below. The same trace-kernel conclusion holds
for every nonempty finite direct sum of shifts of point
translates and degree-zero line twists of either $F_+$ or
$F_-$ on its indicated ambient variety. This includes every
actual finite point-orbit induction of one of these objects.

\paragraph{1. The pushforward is the actual normalization sheaf.}
Put $H=\ker f$ and let $Y_A=f(Y)$ with its reduced image
scheme. All distinct translates $Y+h$, $h\in H$, are
distinct irreducible subvarieties by S28's trivial
translation stabilizer. A generic fibre of $f|_Y$ has
one point. Indeed an additional point would give a
nonzero $h\in H$ with $Y\cap(Y+h)$ dominant over $Y$.
Since $H$ is finite, generic degree greater than one
would force one such intersection to have dimension four,
hence $Y=Y+h$, a contradiction. Thus
\[
                          f|_Y:Y\longrightarrow Y_A
 \tag{F1S30.4}
\]
is finite birational. The actual normal $Y$ is the
normalization of $Y_A$, and $F_+$ is its finite
normalization pushforward.

Locally, for the resulting integral ring inclusion
$R\subset S$ in their common function field, an
$R$-linear endomorphism of $S$ is multiplication by
its generic scalar. Evaluating at $1$ makes that
scalar an element of $S$, and conversely multiplication
by every element of $S$ preserves $S$. Hence
$\mathcal End_R(S)=S$. Globally,
\[
                         \operatorname{End}_A(F_+)
                                  =H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=\mathbf C.
 \tag{F1S30.5}
\]
Coherence and the smooth ambient variety also make
$F_+$ perfect; negative self-Ext vanishes because it
is an ordinary sheaf.

The reduced inverse image of $Y_A$ has the distinct
irreducible components $Y+h$, $h\in H$. If a translation
$x\in A$ stabilizes $Y_A$, lift it to $p\in P$.
Translation by $p$ preserves this inverse image and
therefore sends $Y$ to $Y+h$ for some $h\in H$.
S28 gives $p=h$, whence $x=0$. Thus $Y_A$ has
trivial translation stabilizer.

Finite flat base change and the literal fibre product
\[
 P\times_A P=\coprod_{h\in H}\{(p,p+h):p\in P\}
\]
give the functorial decomposition
\[
                       f^*f_*E\simeq\bigoplus_{h\in H}t_h^*E.
 \tag{F1S30.6}
\]
Suppose $F_+\otimes L\simeq F_+$, with
$L\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(A)$. Pulling this isomorphism
back and using (F1S30.6) forces an isomorphism on each
individual support summand. To see this, any Hom
between two distinct support summands is zero: its
image would be supported on their proper intersection,
hence would be a torsion subsheaf of the rank-one
torsion-free module on the integral target support.
The same argument applies after the line twist.
The identity summand therefore gives
$E\otimes f^*L\simeq E$. The actual S19 Picard injection
forces $f^*L$ trivial. Conversely this condition gives
$F_+\otimes L\simeq F_+$ by projection formula.
Together with the translation conclusion, this proves
the first formula of (F1S30.3).

\paragraph{2. The pullback and its exact point stabilizer.}
Put $K=\ker g$, $Y_B=B\times_PY$ and
$T_B=B\times_PT$. The source $T_B$ is a finite etale
cover of the smooth projective $T$. It is connected.
Here is the actual integral comparison proving this:
S21 identifies the Albanese homomorphism of $a$ with
an isomorphism $\operatorname{Alb}(T)\simeq P$.
By the period-lattice construction of the Albanese,
\[
                a_*:\pi_1(T)\longrightarrow\pi_1(P)=\Lambda_P
                               \quad\hbox{is surjective}.
 \tag{F1S30.7}
\]
The connected covering $B\to P$ corresponds to the
sublattice $\Lambda_B\subset\Lambda_P$. The pullback
cover is connected because the action of $\pi_1(T)$
on $\Lambda_P/\Lambda_B$ is transitive. Moreover its
fundamental group is the inverse image of
$\Lambda_B$, so
\[
                           \pi_1(T_B)\longrightarrow\Lambda_B
                                      \quad\hbox{is surjective}.
 \tag{F1S30.8}
\]
This argument uses the actual map of period lattices;
it does not infer connectedness from a numerical degree.

The finite etale pullback $Y_B$ is normal. It is
connected because $T_B\to Y_B$ is surjective, so it
is integral: the irreducible components of a normal
scheme are disjoint. Thus $F_-=\mathcal O_{Y_B}$ is
an ordinary simple sheaf. Equation (F1S30.8) also
shows that $\operatorname{Alb}(T_B)\to B$ is
surjective with connected kernel. Indeed for its
homomorphism of complex tori the period lattice
map is surjective; the kernel is the quotient of
the kernel vector space by its own period lattice.
Dualizing gives an injection
\[
                     \operatorname{Pic}^0(B)\hookrightarrow
                                            \operatorname{Pic}^0(T_B).
 \tag{F1S30.9}
\]
In particular a degree-zero line trivial on $Y_B$
is trivial on $B$.

A translation $b$ of $B$ stabilizes $Y_B$ exactly
when $g(b)$ stabilizes $Y$. Its necessity follows
by taking the image under $g$; its sufficiency
when $g(b)=0$ follows from the defining fibre
product. S28 therefore identifies this translation
stabilizer with $K$. For such a translation the
structure sheaf already has the canonical
isomorphism supplied by $g t_b=g$. Any additional
line twist fixing it must be trivial by (F1S30.9).
This proves the second formula in (F1S30.3).

\paragraph{3. Split injections on the actual self-Ext groups.}
For every integer $m$, finite pushforward gives a
map
\[
 P_f:\operatorname{Ext}^m_P(E,E)\longrightarrow
                          \operatorname{Ext}^m_A(F_+,F_+).
 \tag{F1S30.10}
\]
It has an explicit left inverse. Pull back a
target morphism by $f^*$, use (F1S30.6), then
precompose with the identity-summand inclusion
and postcompose with its projection. On $P_f(\alpha)$
this is exactly the identity component of
$\bigoplus_h t_h^*\alpha$, namely $\alpha$.
Thus (F1S30.10) is injective, with all summands
and maps specified by the actual fibre product.

Similarly flat pullback gives
\[
 Q_g:\operatorname{Ext}^m_P(E,E)\longrightarrow
                          \operatorname{Ext}^m_B(F_-,F_-).
 \tag{F1S30.11}
\]
To retract it, push a morphism forward, use
$g_*g^*E=E\otimes g_*\mathcal O_B$, precompose
with the algebra unit, and postcompose with
$1_E\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_g/\deg g$.
The trace of the unit is $\deg g$, so the
composite on $Q_g(\alpha)$ is $\alpha$.
This is another split injection.

\paragraph{4. The actual tangent and Atiyah comparisons.}
For an etale isogeny $f$, the differential gives
$f^*\Omega_A^1\simeq\Omega_P^1$. Combined with
$f^*:H^1(\mathcal O_A)\simeq H^1(\mathcal O_P)$
it gives the actual isomorphism
\[
 \Psi_f:H^1(A,T_A)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(P,T_P),
 \qquad
 \Psi_f(\kappa)=f^*\kappa\circ(df^*)^{-1}.
 \tag{F1S30.12}
\]
It restricts to the specified sixteen-dimensional
Weil tangent spaces: it intertwines the transported
rational field actions and pulls back $\theta_A$
to $\beta$. The analogous map for $g$ is
\[
                     \Psi_g:\mathfrak D_P\xrightarrow{\sim}
                                               \mathfrak D_B.
 \tag{F1S30.13}
\]
These are also the infinitesimal maps obtained by
lifting the original finite etale isogenies across
a nilpotent thickening. No map between unrelated
period components is being used.

The first-jet extensions commute with these
operations using the displayed cotangent
isomorphism and projection formula. One can
check pushforward directly on first infinitesimal
neighbourhoods: finite base change first replaces
the diagonal of $A$ by the graph of $f$ in
$P\times A$. The etale map $1\times f:P\times P
\to P\times A$ is identity on the corresponding
diagonal and graph, and formal etaleness identifies
their first infinitesimal neighbourhoods.
Pushing the resulting first-jet extension to
the output factor gives
\[
               J_A^1(f_*E)\simeq f_*J_P^1(E),
 \tag{F1S30.14}
\]
including its unit, quotient and cotangent terms.
Equivalently it is the same direct-sum first-jet
calculation on every etale local splitting of
the finite cover; the splitting changes are
the actual sheet permutations. Flat pullback
has the identical diagonal base-change comparison.
Thus the Atiyah classes, with S24's fixed diagonal
sign, give the exact formulas
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{ob}_{F_+}(\kappa)
     &=P_f\bigl(\operatorname{ob}_E(\Psi_f\kappa)\bigr),\\
 \operatorname{ob}_{F_-}(\Psi_g\kappa)
     &=Q_g\bigl(\operatorname{ob}_E(\kappa)\bigr).
 \end{split}
 \tag{F1S30.15}
\]
In particular their ranks on the transported Weil
tangents are exactly $16-r_C$, by S29 and the
split injections above.

The entire character also transports through
actual maps. Since both ambient tangent bundles
are trivial, GRR and flat pullback give
\[
           \operatorname{ch}(F_+)=f_*\operatorname{ch}(E),
        \qquad \operatorname{ch}(F_-)=g^*\operatorname{ch}(E).
 \tag{F1S30.16}
\]
For example $f_*(\beta^j)=(\deg f)\theta_A^j$,
and $f_*w$ lies in the transported determinant
space. Contraction commutes with these maps:
pull the pushforward identity back by $f$ and
use $f^*f_*=\sum_{h\in H}t_h^*$ on cohomology.
Every translation acts trivially there, and
$f^*$ is injective. This proves, including
the tangent identification (F1S30.12), that
\[
  \kappa\mathbin{\lrcorner}\operatorname{ch}(F_+)=0
        \quad(\kappa\in\mathfrak D_A),
 \qquad
  \Psi_g(\kappa)\mathbin{\lrcorner}\operatorname{ch}(F_-)=0
        \quad(\kappa\in\mathfrak D_P).
 \tag{F1S30.17}
\]
The full trace-characteristic identity now
places the nonzero images in (F1S30.15) in
their respective full trace kernels. Explicitly
the two injections from S29's original source are
\[
                P_f\Phi_C,\qquad Q_g\Phi_C:
             (\ker\mu_C)^*\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
                                 \ker\sigma^2_{F_\pm}.
 \tag{F1S30.18}
\]
Their source and dimension have not changed.

\paragraph{5. Actual canonical point actions and arbitrary chosen lifts.}
For pushforward, let $\widehat H=\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathbf C^*)$.
The torsor algebra has its actual eigenspace decomposition
\[
          f_*\mathcal O_P=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat H}L_\chi,
             \qquad L_\chi\otimes L_\psi
                          \xrightarrow{\sim}L_{\chi\psi}.
 \tag{F1S30.19}
\]
These maps are algebra multiplication. Etale locally
the torsor is the disjoint union indexed by $H$;
character functions form the displayed rank-one
summands, and their products prove every displayed
isomorphism. The lines are torsion and hence in
$\operatorname{Pic}^0(A)$. Restricting the algebra
counit gives compatible trivializations
$f^*L_\chi\simeq\mathcal O_P$.
Conversely a trivialized pullback of a line in
$\ker f^*$ carries a deck action by scalar constants,
which is a character of $H$. Thus these are exactly
the distinct lines in $\ker f^*$, with its full
group law.

The actual $f_*\mathcal O_P$-module structure on
$F_+$ restricts to isomorphisms
\[
                         L_\chi\otimes F_+\xrightarrow{\sim}F_+.
 \tag{F1S30.20}
\]
Associativity of the algebra gives exactly the
coherence for the point functors
$L_\chi\otimes(-)$. This constructs a faithful
coherent lift of the entire group in (F1S30.3).
Its equivariant coherent category is identified
with coherent modules over $f_*\mathcal O_P$:
the maps (F1S30.20) assemble to that module action,
and an algebra module has these isomorphisms
because every $L_\chi$ is invertible in the
strongly graded algebra. The actual affine
equivalence sends a module $\mathcal M$ back to
\[
           f^*\mathcal M\otimes_{f^*f_*\mathcal O_P}\mathcal O_P.
 \tag{F1S30.21}
\]
It identifies the original object with $E$ and
its self-Ext with the invariant self-Ext of
this canonical lift. These are exact functors;
over characteristic zero the invariant projector
is the average over the finite group.

For pullback, the equality $g t_k=g$ gives the
canonical coherent translation lift of $K$ on
$F_-$, with the group law inherited from the
literal fibre product. Finite etale descent
has the explicit inverse $\mathcal F\mapsto
(g_*\mathcal F)^K$, and its unit is checked on
the split torsor. It identifies the invariant
self-Ext of this canonical lift with the
self-Ext of $E$.

Finally take any specified coherent point lift
of either object, not necessarily these canonical
choices. Every point functor acts identically
on ambient $H^1(T)$, and a degree-zero line has
zero Atiyah class. Naturality under the chosen
lift therefore fixes each class
$\operatorname{ob}_{F_\pm}(\kappa)$ in (F1S30.15).
This argument requires no chosen decomposition
to be respected by that lift. R05's actual
identity-sector comparison puts the resulting
nonzero invariant trace kernel in the weak
semiregularity kernel. The existence of the
canonical actions above therefore gives no
exception to the asserted failure.

\paragraph{6. Every actual finite point-orbit direct sum.}
On either fixed ambient variety let $F$ denote
the corresponding object in (F1S30.2), and form
any actual nonempty finite direct sum
\[
                    V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^s
                           \bigl(t_{x_i}^*F\otimes M_i\bigr)[k_i],
            \qquad M_i\in\operatorname{Pic}^0,\quad s\ge1.
 \tag{F1S30.22}
\]
The Atiyah obstruction is the diagonal direct
sum of the transported obstructions of $F$.
Projection to any one diagonal summand, followed
by the inverse point functor and shift, shows
that its kernel on the transported Weil tangent
is exactly that of $\operatorname{ob}_F$.
Its image has dimension $16-r_C$.
The whole character is
$\operatorname{ch}(V)=(\sum_i(-1)^{k_i})\operatorname{ch}(F)$,
so all these tangents annihilate it, even when
the integer coefficient is zero. The full trace
identity again gives a nonzero kernel of that
same dimension. Atiyah naturality makes this
subspace invariant under every specified point
lift on $V$, regardless of mixing between its
summands.

For a given finite coherent point action,
$\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Forg}(F)$ is
the actual sum of its point translates with
the specified associators and induction lift.
It is a particular case of (F1S30.22). Thus
finite point-orbit induction cannot remove
the obstruction computed here. The proof
asserts this for these exact sums and maps;
it makes no claim about arbitrary coupled
extensions or a different perfect object.
