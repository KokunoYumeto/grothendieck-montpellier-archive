\section{A rank-nine G24 period map over \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$}{Q(sqrt(-7))}}

\paragraph{HD-G12 (an actual twelve-branch source and its exact period rank).}
Put $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, where
$\alpha^2+\alpha+2=0$ and $\bar\alpha=-1-\alpha$.
Let $G=G_{24}$ be the order-$336$ group generated by
\[
s_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&\alpha\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\quad
s_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&-1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\quad
s_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\-1-\alpha&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{G12.1}
\]
The exact group identification, irreducibility and character field are
proved in HD-S13. Its positive definite invariant Hermitian matrix is
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&-\alpha\\-1&2&-1\\1+\alpha&-1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define the six reflections by the following words, read from left to
right as matrix products:
\[
\begin{split}
r_1&=s_3s_1s_3,\\
r_2&=s_3s_1s_2s_3s_1s_3s_2s_1s_3,\\
r_3&=s_2,\\
r_4&=s_1s_3s_1s_2s_3s_2s_1s_3s_1,\\
r_5&=s_1,\qquad r_6=s_3.
\end{split}
\tag{G12.2}
\]
Then the tuple
\[
\boldsymbol g=(r_1,r_1,r_2,r_2,r_3,r_3,r_4,r_4,r_5,r_5,r_6,r_6)
\tag{G12.3}
\]
defines connected $G$-covers of $\mathbb P^1$ of genus $673$.
Their Hurwitz component has dimension nine. The associated primitive
Jacobian factor is a Weil sixfold of $K$-signature $(3,3)$; its
period map has differential rank exactly nine on a nonempty open
subset. Its rational Hermitian form is explicitly split.

\paragraph{Proof: covers and the precise multiplicity space.}
Each word in (G12.2) is checked to square to one, to preserve $H$,
and to have $\operatorname{rank}(1-r_j)=1$. The six words contain
$s_1,s_2,s_3$ at positions $5,3,6$. Thus they generate $G$, and the
doubled tuple has product one. Riemann existence gives the connected
covers; its precise algebraic form is SGA 1, Expose XII, Theorem 5.1,
applied off the branch points and followed by normalization.
Riemann--Hurwitz gives
\[
2g(C)-2=336\left(-2+12\left(1-\frac12\right)\right)=1344,
\]
so $g(C)=673$. Ordered distinct branch configurations modulo
$\operatorname{PGL}_2$ have dimension $12-3=9$, and the marked covers
form a finite local covering of this configuration space.

The reflection character has degree three and each of the twelve
inertia generators contributes $1/2$ to its holomorphic
Chevalley--Weil multiplicity. The two conjugate multiplicities are
therefore $-3+12/2=3$. The precise formula is
Frediani--Ghigi--Penegini, IMRN 2015, Theorem 2.10, also numbered
Theorem 2.10 on printed page 8 of their author preprint.
The primitive self-adjoint matrix idempotent constructed in HD-S13
selects a six-dimensional abelian factor with this signature.
All the period calculations below concern this actual rational factor;
the subsequent order saturation is a fixed isogeny and preserves its
period differential rank.

Here is the cocycle model and its exact relation to that factor.
For every $r_j$ choose a column $v_j$ and row $\beta_j$ with
\[
1-r_j=v_j\beta_j,\qquad \beta_jv_j=2.
\tag{G12.4}
\]
The choices are completely specified: take the first nonzero column
of $1-r_j$ for $v_j$, then divide the row at its first nonzero entry
by that entry to obtain $\beta_j$.
Put $v_i'=v_{\lceil i/2\rceil}$ and
$\beta_i'=\beta_{\lceil i/2\rceil}$ for $1\le i\le12$.
A parabolic cocycle has value $u_i=x_iv_i'$ on the $i$th standard
puncture loop. If $P_i=g_1\cdots g_{i-1}$, set
\[
C=(P_1v_1',\ldots,P_{12}v_{12}'),\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}-\beta_1'\\ \vdots\\-\beta_{12}'\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The parabolic cohomology is
\[
V=\ker(C:K^{12}\longrightarrow K^3)/\operatorname{im}(B:K^3\longrightarrow K^{12}).
\tag{G12.5}
\]
The cocycle relation follows by evaluating on the product of the
twelve loops, and the rows of $B$ are the scalar coboundaries
$(g_i-1)u=-v_i'\beta_i'u$. This also proves $CB=0$ directly.
The published cohomological formulation is Dettweiler--Wewers,
Israel J. Math. 156 (2006), Lemma 1.2 and its following quotient;
Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.5 treat variation and braid monodromy.

For $\pi:C\to\mathbb P^1$, finite proper base change identifies
$R\pi_*K$ with $\pi_*K$; a fibre is a finite set and has no higher
cohomology. Taking the exact representation summand of the regular
sheaf $\pi_*K$ identifies (G12.5) with the corresponding Jacobian
multiplicity space, up to the regular-action dual convention.
This dual convention is harmless by an explicit intertwiner: unitarity
gives
\[
\rho^\vee(g)=H^{\mathsf t}\overline{\rho(g)}(H^{\mathsf t})^{-1}.
\tag{G12.6}
\]
Thus coefficient conjugation $\alpha\mapsto-1-\alpha$, followed by
$H^{\mathsf t}$, transports our calculation to the dual calculation.
Both conjugate constituents lie in the same rational idempotent.
These maps give the rational Hodge-structure identification with the
abelian factor and preserve its polarization up to a nonzero rational
factor and coefficient conjugation.

\paragraph{Proof: all nine logarithms and their rank.}
Take the nine puncture intervals in this exact order:
\[
(1,2),\ (3,4),\ (1,4),\ (5,6),\ (1,6),\
(7,8),\ (9,10),\ (7,10),\ (7,11).
\tag{G12.7}
\]
Every interval has between two and ten elements. Every pair is
nested or disjoint. Thus their boundary curves form a maximal
system of nine disjoint essential curves on the twelve-pointed sphere.

For $[a,b]$ put
\[
c=g_a\cdots g_b,\qquad
D(x)=\sum_{i=a}^b g_a\cdots g_{i-1}v_i'x_i.
\]
The interval product is one for the first eight intervals and is
the involution $r_6=s_3$ for the last one. The first-return powers
are consequently
\[
(k_1,\ldots,k_9)=(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,2).
\tag{G12.8}
\]
For the last interval the certificate also checks that at least one
contained generator does not commute with $c$, so its first power
does not already fix the marked tuple.
Using the cocycle identity on
$\gamma^k\ell_i\gamma^{-k}$, where
$\gamma=\ell_a\cdots\ell_b$ and $c^k=1$, gives
\[
x_i\longmapsto x_i+
\beta_i'(1+c+\cdots+c^{k-1})D(x)\quad(a\le i\le b),
\tag{G12.9}
\]
with the other coordinates fixed. This is an explicit pullback
operator on cocycles, rather than an assumed period-matrix formula.

Here is the entire elimination procedure used for the exact certificate.
Reduce $C$ over $K$ from left to right and take the resulting standard
free-coordinate basis $Z$ of its kernel. Form $[B\ Z]$; take its
leftmost pivot columns after the first three as a six-column matrix
$Q$, and set $E=[B\ Q]$. Select the leftmost independent rows of
$E$, invert their square matrix, and extend by zero to a left inverse
$J$ of $E$. Its last six rows, denoted $P$, give the quotient
projection: $PB=0$ and $PQ=1_6$. Apply (G12.9) as a matrix $M_j$ on
$K^{12}$ and define
\[
N_j=PM_jQ-1_6.
\tag{G12.10}
\]
All arithmetic uses rational coefficient pairs in $1,\alpha$ with
$\alpha^2=-\alpha-2$; no floating-point comparisons are used.
The script verifies the relation, fixation of every coboundary,
and the following exact assertions:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{rank}C=3,\qquad \dim\ker C=9,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}B=3,\qquad\dim_KV=6,\\
N_jN_k=0\quad\hbox{for every }j,k,\\
\bigl(\operatorname{rank}N_1,\ldots,\operatorname{rank}N_9\bigr)
=(1,1,2,1,3,1,1,2,2).
\end{gathered}
\tag{G12.11}
\]
In the specified quotient basis every $N_j$ has support only in its
upper-right $3\times3$ block $A_j$. The sum of those blocks has
\[
\det\left(\sum_{j=1}^9 A_j\right)=-3160.
\tag{G12.12}
\]
Flatten each $A_j$ in row-major order and use these nine rows to
form a $9\times9$ matrix. Its exact determinant is
\[
\det\bigl(\operatorname{vec}(A_j)\bigr)_{j=1}^9
=1792+3584\alpha=1792\sqrt{-7}\ne0.
\tag{G12.13}
\]
Consequently, with $I=\sum_j\operatorname{im}N_j$,
\[
\dim_K I=3,\quad
\operatorname{im}\sum_jN_j=\ker\sum_jN_j=I,\quad
\langle N_1,\ldots,N_9\rangle_K
=\operatorname{Hom}_K(V/I,I).
\tag{G12.14}
\]
The nine logarithms are also independent over $\mathbb Q$.
Since $N_j^2=0$, their exponential is exactly $1+N_j$ and they
are the logarithms of the corresponding return operators.
The complete basis, projection, nine operators, all nine blocks
and determinant witness are retained in the JSON certificate.

\paragraph{Proof: passage from boundary logarithms to the period differential.}
Pinch the curves in (G12.7). The resulting stable pointed rational
curve has ten components with three special points on each and nine
nodes. Give each node the local smoothing $xy=q_j$ and plumb the
corresponding annuli. This produces nine independent analytic
parameters. Independence can also be checked infinitesimally:
on each component the tangent sheaf fixing its three special points
is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$ with vanishing $H^1$, so the
deformation directions are exactly the nine local node smoothings.
For nonzero $q_j$ this is a smooth twelve-pointed sphere.

Over the punctured polydisc the marked Hurwitz family is a finite
cover. A finite Kummer cover of the polydisc kills its permutation
monodromy. Marking a point over a fixed point outside the clusters
removes the deck ambiguity, or its finite order is killed by one
further power. The logarithms of the geometric weight-one variation
are therefore
\[
M_j=\epsilon c_jN_j,\qquad c_j\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
\quad \epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\text{ independent of }j.
\tag{G12.15}
\]
The common sign follows directly from the cluster coordinate
$z=q_jw$: a counterclockwise turn of every smoothing parameter
rotates its cluster in the same direction. Nested cluster coordinates
are products of these parameters, with the same orientation.

Choose positive integers $a_j$ for which $a_jc_j$ is constant and
restrict the Kummer coordinates to $u_j=t^{a_j}$. The resulting
one-variable logarithm is a nonzero common scalar multiple of
$\sum_jN_j$. Equation (G12.14) makes its monodromy weight filtration,
centred at one, equal to
\[
W_0=W_1=I,\qquad W_2=V.
\tag{G12.16}
\]
Schmid's Nilpotent Orbit Theorem (4.12), Invent. Math. 22 (1973),
printed page 233, extends the untwisted several-variable Hodge map
holomorphically to the polydisc. Write its value at the origin as
$F_0$. The restriction to the chosen diagonal has the same limiting
filtration. Schmid's Theorem (6.16), printed page 255, says that
this filtration and (G12.16) form a mixed Hodge structure.
These theorems are applied to rational first cohomology before
passing to either $K$-eigenspace.

Since $F_0^0=V_{\mathbb C}$ and $F_0^2=0$, the weight-zero
graded part has only type $(0,0)$ and the weight-two part only
type $(1,1)$. On the selected $K$-eigenspace it follows that
\[
F_0^1\cap I_{\mathbb C}=0,\qquad
\dim F_0^1=3,\qquad V_{\mathbb C}=I_{\mathbb C}\oplus F_0^1.
\tag{G12.17}
\]
Thus the actual inclusion and quotient maps give an isomorphism
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(V_{\mathbb C}/I_{\mathbb C},I_{\mathbb C})
&\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(F_0^1,V_{\mathbb C}/F_0^1),\\
X&\longmapsto
\bigl(F_0^1\hookrightarrow V/I\xrightarrow{X}I
\hookrightarrow V/F_0^1\bigr).
\end{split}
\tag{G12.18}
\]
The two outside maps are isomorphisms by (G12.17).
Equations (G12.13)--(G12.15) therefore give nine independent
tangent vectors $M_jF_0$.

Explicitly, the lifted period map has the form
\[
\Phi(z)=\exp\left(\sum_jz_jM_j\right)\psi(u),
\qquad u_j=e^{2\pi iz_j},\qquad\psi(0)=F_0.
\]
After translating the tangent space by the inverse exponential,
its $j$th derivative is
\[
M_j\psi(u)+2\pi i u_j\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial u_j}.
\]
Holomorphic extension makes its limit $M_jF_0$ as $u\to0$.
The determinant of the nine columns therefore remains nonzero
at nearby interior points. The signature-$(3,3)$ Weil domain has
complex dimension $3\cdot3=9$, so the rank is exactly nine.
Rank nine also makes the algebraic image Zariski dense in its
connected fixed polarized Weil moduli component: it contains an
analytic open subset of that component.

\paragraph{Proof: the rational form is split through an actual isometry.}
Geometric monodromy commutes with $K$ and preserves the polarization.
Hence its logarithms, and therefore the $N_j$, are skew-adjoint for
the multiplicity Hermitian form $h$. Equation (G12.11) gives
\[
h(N_jx,N_ky)=-h(x,N_jN_ky)=0.
\]
Thus $I$ is a rational totally isotropic $K$-subspace of dimension
three in the six-dimensional Hermitian space. To exhibit the
isometry explicitly, choose a basis $v_1,v_2,v_3$ of $I$ and
choose $w_1,w_2,w_3$ with $h(v_i,w_j)=\delta_{ij}$.
Set $A_{ij}=h(w_i,w_j)$ and replace
\[
w_j\quad\hbox{by}\quad
w_j-\frac12\sum_i v_iA_{ij}.
\]
Hermitian symmetry makes the new $w_j$ mutually orthogonal and
isotropic, while their pairings with the $v_i$ stay unchanged.
The resulting Gram matrix is
$\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1_3\\1_3&0\end{psmallmatrix}$,
which is $K$-isometric by a basis permutation to $U^3$.
The explicit cohomological comparison (G12.6) preserves this
rational Lagrangian, proving splitness for the actual Jacobian factor.
\hfill$\square$
