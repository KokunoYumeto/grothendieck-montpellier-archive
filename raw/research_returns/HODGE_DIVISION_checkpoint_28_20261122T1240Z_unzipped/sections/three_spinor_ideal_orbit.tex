\subsection{HD-N22: two actual three-spinor ideal orbits}
\label{sec:HD-N22}

Let $X,Y$ be abelian threefolds. Choose effective ample Cartier
divisors $D_X\subset X$, $D_Y\subset Y$, and a point $p\in Y$.
Consider either of the actual coherent sheaves on $X\times Y$
\begin{equation}
 I_{D_X\times D_Y},\qquad I_{D_X\times\{p\}}.       \tag{N22.1}
\end{equation}
Their orbits under finite compositions of actual Fourier--Mukai
equivalences, isogeny pullbacks and pushforwards, arbitrary line
twists, translations, shifts and derived duals contain no object
whose whole character is
\begin{equation}
 e^B\bigl(P(\theta)+\lambda t\bigr),\qquad
 0\ne\lambda t\in W_K(A),                            \tag{N22.2}
\end{equation}
on a polarized Weil sixfold of signature $(3,3)$.
Here $P$ is any real polynomial, and $B$ may be any real
degree-two cohomology class. The assertion therefore includes
the rational algebraic $B$-fields in the preceding constructions.
It also permits a nonzero scalar multiple of a transported
character. No assertion about an arbitrary different-character
retract, a new cone, or a new differential is made.

These are actual gluable objects: coherent sheaves have no
negative self-Ext. They exist, in particular, on the CM product
$E_i^3\times E_i^3$, by taking effective ample product divisors.
Smoothness of the divisors is unnecessary for the displayed
ideal sequences and their characters. The obstruction below uses
the entire character and holds irrespective of a coherent point
action or a separate semiregularity assertion.

\paragraph{1. The two exact ideal characters.}
Write $H_X=c_1\mathcal O_X(D_X)$ and
$H_Y=c_1\mathcal O_Y(D_Y)$, also for their pullbacks.
The actual divisor exact sequences and the external tensor
product of their structure sheaves give
\begin{align}
 v_{DD}&=\operatorname{ch}I_{D_X\times D_Y}
       =e^{-H_X}+e^{-H_Y}-e^{-H_X-H_Y},\nonumber\\
 v_{Dp}&=\operatorname{ch}I_{D_X\times\{p\}}
       =1-\mathrm{vol}_Y+e^{-H_X}\mathrm{vol}_Y,
 \qquad \mathrm{vol}_Y=[p].                          \tag{N22.3}
\end{align}
For the second formula $\operatorname{ch}\mathcal O_p=[p]$;
the tangent bundle of an abelian variety is trivial, so the
point Gysin formula has no Todd correction. Each formula follows
from the actual ideal triangle, with its specified signs.

Put $V=H^1(X\times Y,\mathbb R)=V_X\oplus V_Y$,
where both summands have real dimension six. On
$\mathcal W=V\oplus V^*$ use
\[
 Q(v+\xi,w+\eta)=\xi(w)+\eta(v),\qquad
 c(v+\xi)=v\wedge+\iota_\xi
                 \quad\text{on }S=\bigwedge^*V.
\]
Then $c(x)c(y)+c(y)c(x)=Q(x,y)$.
For a nonzero spinor $s$ let $L_s$ denote its annihilator
under this literal Clifford action. The annihilator of $e^H$ is
\begin{equation}
 L_{e^H}=\{-\iota_\xi H+\xi:\xi\in V^*\}.
                                                               \tag{N22.4}
\end{equation}
It follows directly by contracting the exponential; it has
dimension twelve and is maximal isotropic for $Q$.

For $v_{DD}$ take $s_1=e^{-H_X}$, $s_2=e^{-H_Y}$,
$s_3=e^{-H_X-H_Y}$. Since $H_X-H_Y$ is nondegenerate,
$L_{s_1}$ and $L_{s_2}$ are transverse. Equating the graph
coordinates in (N22.4) shows that
\begin{equation}
 \dim(L_{s_3}\cap L_{s_1})
   =\dim(L_{s_3}\cap L_{s_2})=6.                    \tag{N22.5}
\end{equation}
The two intersections are given respectively by contraction
variables in $V_X^*$ and $V_Y^*$.

For $v_{Dp}$ take $s_1=1$, $s_2=e^{-H_X}\mathrm{vol}_Y$,
$s_3=\mathrm{vol}_Y$. Here
\[
 L_{s_1}=V^*,\qquad L_{s_3}=V_Y\oplus V_X^*,\qquad
 L_{s_2}=V_Y\oplus
           \{\iota_\xi H_X+\xi:\xi\in V_X^*\}.
\]
Nondegeneracy of $H_X$ proves transversality of the first pair.
The intersections of $L_{s_3}$ with that pair are
$V_X^*$ and $V_Y$, respectively. Thus (N22.5) holds for
this actual character as well. All spaces and maps here are real.

\paragraph{2. An explicit real Fock change of coordinates.}
We give the exact intertwiner, rather than assume uniqueness of
a decomposition of a spinor into three summands.
Set $L_1=L_{s_1}$ and $L_2=L_{s_2}$. Their transversality
and isotropy identify $L_1$ with $L_2^*$ using $Q$.
Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F:\bigwedge^*L_2\longrightarrow S,\qquad
 \ell_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\ell_j\longmapsto
                  c(\ell_1)\cdots c(\ell_j)s_1.      \tag{N22.6}
\end{equation}
Clifford anticommutation makes $L_2$ act as exterior
multiplication and the paired $L_1$ as contraction in this
map. Choose a basis of $L_2$ and the $Q$-dual basis of $L_1$.
Applying successive dual contractions to a linear relation
between its exterior images proves their independence, starting
with maximal degree; their number is $2^{12}$. Thus
$\mathcal F$ is an actual real Clifford-module isomorphism.
Its vacuum is $s_1$. Its top exterior line is annihilated by
all of $L_2$, so it is the nonzero line $\mathbb R s_2$.
The uniqueness of these annihilated lines also follows immediately
from the same contraction/exterior basis calculation.

Put $U=L_{s_3}\cap L_2$, a real six-dimensional space.
The other intersection in (N22.5) is its annihilator in
$L_1=L_2^*$, because $L_{s_3}$ is isotropic. The two
intersections have total dimension twelve, so their sum is
exactly $L_{s_3}$. Choose a complement $W$ to $U$ in $L_2$.
In the exterior model (N22.6), being annihilated by exterior
multiplication by $U$ and by contraction by $W^*$ forces a
spinor to be a scalar multiple of $\mathrm{vol}_U$.
Consequently (N22.3) is carried to
\begin{equation}
 w=a\,1+b\,\mathrm{vol}_{U\oplus W}
                    +c\,\mathrm{vol}_U,
       \qquad a,b,c\in\mathbb R^*,\quad
       \dim U=\dim W=6.                              \tag{N22.7}
\end{equation}
This is a real linear change of Clifford coordinates used to
compute a stabilizer. We do not claim that this auxiliary change
is induced by an algebraic functor.

\paragraph{3. The complete real spin Lie stabilizer.}
In an exterior model on the real twelve-space $U\oplus W$,
the spin Lie algebra has the explicit action
\[
 \bigwedge^2(U\oplus W)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(U\oplus W)
          \oplus\bigwedge^2(U\oplus W)^*,
 \quad
 \beta\wedge,\quad D_A-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}A,
                       \quad\iota_\gamma.
\]
Suppose that such an element kills (N22.7).
Its degree-two component is $a\beta$, so $\beta=0$.
Its degree-ten component is
$b\iota_\gamma\mathrm{vol}_{U\oplus W}$, so $\gamma=0$;
contraction of a nonzero top volume is injective on bivectors.
Its degree-zero component gives $\operatorname{tr}A=0$.
The degree-six equation is then
$D_A\mathrm{vol}_U=0$. Relative to $U\oplus W$, the
components replacing a basis vector of $U$ by one in $W$
are independent. Therefore $A(U)\subset U$, and the
remaining coefficient gives $\operatorname{tr}(A|_U)=0$.
Conversely these conditions kill all three terms.
This proves the exact equality
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak s_w=
  \left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\0&D\end{pmatrix}:
          \operatorname{tr}A=\operatorname{tr}D=0\right\}
 =\bigl(\mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb R)\oplus
              \mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb R)\bigr)
                  \ltimes\operatorname{Hom}(W,U).   \tag{N22.8}
\end{equation}
Its last summand is an actual abelian ideal, and its quotient
is the displayed pair of split real Lie algebras. Its dimension
is $35+35+36=106$. The argument computes the full stabilizer;
it neither assumes a secant uniqueness statement nor discards
off-diagonal endomorphisms.

\paragraph{4. Actual functorial comparisons.}
For an actual Fourier--Mukai equivalence
$\Phi:D^b(A)\to D^b(A')$, the
cohomological transform $L=\Phi^H$ and its actual graph
isomorphism $f_\Phi:A\times\widehat A\to
A'\times\widehat A'$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
 Lc_A(w)L^{-1}=c_{A'}(fw),\qquad
 f=\iota_{A'}^{-1}(f_\Phi)_*\iota_A,
             \quad\iota_A(v,\xi)=(\xi,v).           \tag{N22.9}
\end{equation}
The swap uses the Poincar\'e identification of the Clifford
space with $H_1(A)\oplus H_1(\widehat A)$.
Exact published locators for this comparison are
Golyshev--Lunts--Orlov, \emph{Mirror symmetry for abelian varieties},
J.~Algebraic Geom. \textbf{10} (2001), 433--496,
Proposition4.3.7 and Lemma4.3.7.1 in its proof, together with
Orlov, \emph{Derived categories of coherent sheaves on abelian
varieties and equivalences between them}, Izv.~Math.
\textbf{66} (2002), 569--594, Theorem2.10.
Their precise maps and proofs were checked in HD-G19 and HD-N17.
Commutators in (N22.9) identify the real spin Lie stabilizers.

For an isogeny pullback, $T=p^*$ on $H^1$ gives the exact
Clifford map $(v,\xi)\mapsto(Tv,(T^{-1})^*\xi)$ and exterior
map $\bigwedge T$. Pushforward is $\deg(p)$ times the inverse
cohomological map. A line twist of class $D$ acts by $e^D$,
with explicit shear
\[
             e^D c(v+\xi)e^{-D}
                    =c(v-\iota_\xi D+\xi).
\]
Translations act identically, and a shift multiplies the
character by a sign. Derived duality on even cohomology extends
to $\tau|_{\wedge^j V}=(-1)^{j(j-1)/2}$; conjugation by
$\tau$ negates the wedge and contraction bivectors and fixes
$D_A-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}A$. It is therefore an explicit
real spin Lie automorphism. All operations in the assertion
identify the stabilizer with (N22.8). Multiplication of the
whole character by a nonzero scalar changes none of these kernels.

\paragraph{5. The target unitary algebra cannot embed.}
For the polarized target in (N22.2), its real Lie algebra
$\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}(3,3)$ acts faithfully on real
$H^1(A)$, preserves $\theta$, and fixes the two field determinant
lines containing the Weil classes. Its real trace is zero,
so its middle spin action annihilates $P(\theta)+\lambda t$.
Multiplication by $e^B$ gives the exact embedding
\[
       Z\longmapsto e^B D_Ze^{-B}
                    =D_Z-(D_ZB)\wedge
\]
into the stabilizer of (N22.2). This shear is a real Clifford
isometry for every real $B$; no claim of a line-bundle realization
of an arbitrary real $B$ is needed.

The already specified actual intertwiners would thus give an
injection of $\mathfrak g$ into (N22.8). Its composite with
the quotient by $\operatorname{Hom}(W,U)$ is nonzero: otherwise
the perfect nonabelian algebra $\mathfrak g$ would inject into
an abelian algebra. The real algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3,3)$ is
simple, since its complexification is $\mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb C)$.
At least one projection to a factor $\mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb R)$
is therefore injective. Both dimensions are 35, so it would be
an isomorphism.

Their Killing forms prevent that isomorphism. On
$\mathfrak{sl}_6(\mathbb R)$ the skew-symmetric subspace has
dimension 15 and negative Killing form, and the symmetric
trace-zero subspace has dimension 20 and positive Killing form.
On $\mathfrak{su}(3,3)$ the compact block algebra
$\mathfrak s(\mathfrak u_3\oplus\mathfrak u_3)$ has dimension
17 and negative Killing form, and its off-diagonal complement
has dimension 18 and positive Killing form. These signs follow
directly from the positive multiple of the real trace of $Z^2$.
The signatures $(20,15)$ and $(18,17)$ differ. This proves
the asserted whole-orbit exclusion for every imaginary quadratic
target field and every target Hermitian determinant.
