\section{HD-N23: a positive graph union in determinant class [-7]}
\label{sec:positive-graph-union}

There is an actual reduced union $Z$ of $9408$ translated abelian
threefolds in the Gaussian CM sixfold $A=E_i^3\times E_i^3$, with
only transverse double points and no triple points, for which
\[
 [Z]=32\theta^3+120T,\qquad
 \operatorname{ch}(I_Z)
   =1-32\theta^3-120T+\frac{3264}{7}\theta^6.                 \tag{N23.1}
\]
Here $T$ is an explicitly displayed nonzero integral Weil class,
and the actual polarized $\mathbf Q(i)$-space has ordinary
Hermitian determinant class $[-7]$. This class differs from both
$[-1]$ and $[-3]$. The ideal is a gluable coherent sheaf and
$\chi(I_Z,I_Z)=0$.

The construction retains positive multiplicities and its actual
restriction differential. Nevertheless, for every nodal translated
arrangement with the directions and multiplicities below, and every
actual coherent action of a faithful finite point subgroup on its
ideal, the invariant degree-two categorical trace has a kernel of
dimension at least $61693$. Thus this particular positive-cycle
construction does not give a weakly semiregular supplier. The same
failure holds after an actual isogeny pullback and after actual line
tensor and Fourier--Mukai equivalences, with their conjugated point
actions. Arbitrary new differentials or other cycle decompositions
are not included in this statement.

\paragraph{1. The actual field action and integral Weil generator.}
Let $E_i=\mathbf C/(\mathbf Z+i\mathbf Z)$, and write the two
threefold coordinates as $(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ and $(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)$.
Take integral degree-one forms $x_j,y_j,u_j,v_j$ with
$x_j+i y_j$ and $u_j+i v_j$ holomorphic, and put
\[
 h_j=x_jy_j,\quad k_j=u_jv_j,\quad
 \theta_1=h_1+k_1,\quad\theta_2=h_2+k_2,\quad
 \theta_3=7h_3+k_3,\quad\theta=\theta_1+\theta_2+\theta_3.
                                                               \tag{N23.2}
\]
These are the first Chern classes of the indicated external
products of degree-one and degree-seven line bundles, so $\theta$
is an actual ample integral polarization. Let $i\in K=\mathbf Q(i)$
act by the actual endomorphism $(X,Y)\mapsto(iX,-iY)$.
It has balanced signature $(3,3)$ and is skew-adjoint for this
polarization. On the $K$-basis of first real homology generators,
the associated Hermitian form
$h(v,w)=E(v,iw)+iE(v,w)$ has Gram matrix
\[
                  \operatorname{diag}(1,1,7,-1,-1,-1).       \tag{N23.3}
\]
Its determinant is $-7$. Every rational Gaussian norm has even
valuation at $7$: after clearing denominators, divisibility of
$a^2+b^2$ by the prime $7\equiv3\pmod4$ forces both $a,b$
to be divisible by $7$, and iteration gives the assertion.
Neither $7$ nor $7/3$ is therefore a norm. This proves the two
claimed distinctions in the norm quotient.

Set
\[
 \kappa_j=(x_j+i y_j)(u_j-i v_j),\qquad
 K_0=\kappa_1\kappa_2\kappa_3,\qquad
                  T=-\operatorname{Im}K_0
                    =\frac{i}{2}(K_0-\overline K_0).          \tag{N23.4}
\]
Each of the six one-forms in $K_0$ is an $i$-eigenvector for
the pullback field action. Thus $K_0$ and its conjugate are
the two top $K$-eigenvolumes, and $T$ is a rational Weil class
of type $(3,3)$. Its coefficients in the integral exterior basis
are $0,1,-1$, with nonzero coefficients, so it is integral and
nonzero. If $v_0=\prod_jh_jk_j$ is the positive point class, then
\[
 \theta T=0,\qquad T^2=-32v_0,\qquad\theta^6=5040v_0.
                                                               \tag{N23.5}
\]
For the middle identity use
$\kappa_j\overline\kappa_j=-4h_jk_j$; the last follows from
the six polarization weights $1,1,1,1,7,1$.

\paragraph{2. Positive graph interpolation with all multiplicities.}
For $z_j\in\mathbf Z[i]$, let $B_z$ be the actual graph
$Y_j=z_jX_j$. The quotient
$(X,Y)\mapsto(Y_j-z_jX_j)_j$ has connected kernel $B_z$.
Writing $z=a+ib$, its $j$th factor has fundamental class
\[
 g_{z,j}=(u_j-a x_j+b y_j)(v_j-b x_j-a y_j)
       =k_j+|z|^2h_j+z A_j+\overline z\,\overline A_j,
 \qquad A_j=-\frac{i}{2}\kappa_j.                            \tag{N23.6}
\]
This is the actual exterior Gysin pullback of a point on the
quotient elliptic curve. In particular $[B_z]=\prod_jg_{z_j,j}$.

Let $\mathcal P=\{(\zeta_1,\zeta_2,\zeta_3)\in\mu_4^3:
\zeta_1\zeta_2\zeta_3=1\}$, a set of sixteen triples.
Its character sum kills every exponent vector in
$\{-1,0,1\}^3$ except $(0,0,0),(1,1,1),(-1,-1,-1)$.
For an integer $R>0$ this gives
\[
 \sum_{\zeta\in\mathcal P}[B_{(\zeta_1,\zeta_2,R\zeta_3)}]
 =16(h_1+k_1)(h_2+k_2)(R^2h_3+k_3)+4R T.                  \tag{N23.7}
\]
The coefficient is $4R$ because
$A_1A_2A_3+\overline A_1\overline A_2\overline A_3=T/4$.
Take three copies of each $R=1$ graph and nine copies of each
$R=3$ graph. Their positive cycle is
\[
                       192\theta_1\theta_2\theta_3+120T.   \tag{N23.8}
\]
Copies will mean distinct parallel translates, not a nonreduced
multiple structure.

Order the six point classes as
$(h_1,k_1,h_2,k_2,h_3,k_3)$, with weights $(1,1,1,1,7,1)$.
For each three-element subset $S$ of these six coordinates
which does not choose exactly one coordinate from each pair,
take the coordinate threefold $C_S$ on which the coordinates
in $S$ are zero, with multiplicity
\[
                         192\prod_{j\in S}w_j.             \tag{N23.9}
\]
There are twelve such directions, with multiplicities $192$
or $1344$. Their cycle is exactly
\[
                 192\left(\frac{\theta^3}{6}
                                -\theta_1\theta_2\theta_3\right).
                                                               \tag{N23.10}
\]
All displayed coefficients are positive integers. The graph
part has $192$ components and the coordinate part has $9216$,
so their total is $N=9408$. Adding (N23.8) and (N23.10)
proves the first identity of (N23.1).

\paragraph{3. A finite torsion choice of the actual reduced arrangement.}
Each direction is a connected abelian threefold with a displayed
quotient to $E_i^3$. Two distinct directions whose tangent spaces
span $T_0A$ have finite transverse intersection. The intersection
number is the degree of the actual stacked quotient isogeny
$A\to E_i^6$, namely the Gaussian norm of the determinant of
its six-by-six matrix. Noncomplementary directions have sum a
proper connected abelian subvariety; translates outside that
sum are disjoint. Parallel directions are included in this case.

Every individual direction has self-intersection zero, either
from its trivial normal bundle or from its decomposable exterior
Gysin class. Consequently the sum of all proper pair-intersection
degrees, with the displayed multiplicities, is
\[
 q=\frac12\int_A(32\theta^3+120T)^2=2350080.                \tag{N23.11}
\]
The exact certificate also computes this number independently
from all $946$ stacked quotient matrices of the $44$ directions.

Here is a finite choice of translates which ensures exactly these
nodes and no triples. Order the directions and their copies in
any fixed way, and order the points of $A[101]$ lexicographically
in the basis $1/101,i/101$ on each elliptic factor. At each step
choose the first translation which is disjoint from every earlier
noncomplementary component and contains none of the earlier
pair-intersection points. A forbidden noncomplementary condition
is a coset of a subgroup of dimension at most five, so excludes
at most $101^{10}$ torsion points. An earlier intersection point
excludes at most $101^6$ points. There are at most $N-1$ earlier
components and at most $q$ earlier nodes. The strict inequality
\[
              (N-1)101^{10}+q101^6<101^{12}                 \tag{N23.12}
\]
proves that this finite rule always has a choice. Intersections
of a subgroup coset with $A[101]$ are either empty or a coset
of its $101$-torsion, which proves the counts without a generic
rank assertion over a finite field. The chosen points are
algebraic torsion points on the CM variety.

The resulting reduced union $Z$ has exactly $q$ transverse
double points and no triple points. If $Q$ denotes its node set
and $j_\nu:B_\nu+a_\nu\hookrightarrow A$ its components, the
actual normalization sequence is
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_Z\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_\nu j_{\nu,*}\mathcal O_{B_\nu+a_\nu}
 \xrightarrow{\text{difference of restrictions}}
 \bigoplus_{p\in Q}\mathcal O_p\longrightarrow0.            \tag{N23.13}
\]
At a node this is the exact fiber-product sequence for the two
coordinate three-planes. Thus the actual complex
\[
 \left[\mathcal O_A\longrightarrow
       \bigoplus_\nu\mathcal O_{B_\nu+a_\nu}
       \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in Q}\mathcal O_p\right]
                    \quad\text{in degrees }0,1,2           \tag{N23.14}
\]
is quasi-isomorphic to $I_Z$ in degree zero. All its restriction
maps are retained. This is a coherent sheaf and has no negative
self-Ext. Its local free resolutions below also show it is perfect.

The tangent bundles of every abelian component and of $A$ have
Todd class one. GRR for the actual embeddings (Fulton,
\emph{Intersection Theory}, Theorem15.2) gives
$\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal O_{B_\nu+a_\nu})=[B_\nu]$.
Applying (N23.13)--(N23.14) gives (N23.1), since
$qv_0=(3264/7)\theta^6$. Dualizing the whole character changes
the sign of its codimension-three term. Riemann--Roch therefore
gives
\[
              \chi(I_Z,I_Z)=2q-\int_A[Z]^2=0.               \tag{N23.15}
\]

\paragraph{4. Actual local Ext modules and their intrinsic gluing.}
The following calculation applies to any reduced union of $N$
translated abelian threefolds in an abelian sixfold with $q$
transverse double points and no triples. At a node the completed
local ring is
\[
 R=\mathbf C[[x_1,x_2,x_3,y_1,y_2,y_3]],\qquad
 I_Z=\mathfrak m_x\mathfrak m_y
       =\mathfrak m_x\widehat\boxtimes\mathfrak m_y.         \tag{N23.16}
\]
The multiplication isomorphism is the one on monomials in the
disjoint variable sets; tensoring the two truncated Koszul
resolutions gives an actual free resolution of length four.
For $S=\mathbf C[[x_1,x_2,x_3]]$, $\mathfrak m=(x_1,x_2,x_3)$
and $k=S/\mathfrak m$, the truncated resolution is
\[
 0\longrightarrow S\xrightarrow{(x_3,-x_2,x_1)^t}
 S^3\xrightarrow{
 \left(\begin{smallmatrix}-x_2&-x_3&0\\x_1&0&-x_3\\0&x_1&x_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)}
 S^3\longrightarrow\mathfrak m\longrightarrow0.             \tag{N23.17}
\]
The exact sequence $0\to\mathfrak m\to S\to k\to0$ and
this resolution give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m)&=S,\\
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_S(\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m)&=k^3,\\
 \operatorname{Ext}^2_S(\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m)&=k^3,
 \qquad\operatorname{Ext}^{>2}=0.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{N23.18}
\]
For the connecting maps, $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathfrak m,S)=0$.
The map on Hom is zero, and the map on the top Ext is the identity:
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak m,S)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak m,k),\qquad
 \operatorname{Ext}^2(\mathfrak m,S)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Ext}^2(\mathfrak m,k)=k.
\]
Hence the two nonzero positive groups in
(N23.18) are respectively
$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak m,k)$ and
$\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathfrak m,k)$, not an assumed formality
identification.

The tensor product of these actual resolutions and Hom complexes
gives the following completed local sheaf Ext modules:
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal E xt^1(I_Z,I_Z)^{\wedge}_p
       &\simeq \mathbf C[[y]]^3\oplus\mathbf C[[x]]^3,\\
 \mathcal E xt^2(I_Z,I_Z)^{\wedge}_p
       &\simeq \mathbf C[[y]]^3\oplus\mathbf C[[x]]^3\oplus k^9.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{N23.19}
\]
These statements follow first for polynomial rings by the ordinary
tensor-product Kunneth map over $\mathbf C$, and then by flat
completion. A finite free resolution computes Ext before and after
completion. Faithful flatness is Stacks Project, Lemma10.97.3,
\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MC}{Tag00MC}.

Here are intrinsic global maps, so no local splitting is silently
glued. We have $\mathcal H om(I_Z,\mathcal O_A)=\mathcal O_A$
and $\mathcal E xt^1(I_Z,\mathcal O_A)=0$, as the same local
resolutions show. The ideal sequence therefore identifies
\[
 \mathcal E xt^1(I_Z,I_Z)
                      \simeq\mathcal H om(I_Z,\mathcal O_Z).
                                                               \tag{N23.20}
\]
For a component $C$, compose $I_Z\to I_C/I_C^2$ with an actual
normal section in $N_{C/A}$. At a node with another component,
the local product $x_i y_j$ maps to the normal coefficient
times $y_j$, so its image vanishes at that node. It can therefore
be extended by zero on the other branches through (N23.13).
This gives the canonical morphism
\[
 \bigoplus_C j_{C,*}N_{C/A}
       \longrightarrow\mathcal H om(I_Z,\mathcal O_Z).
                                                               \tag{N23.21}
\]
Off the nodes it is the usual normal map. At each node the
first formula in (N23.19) makes it an isomorphism, using the
same connecting map in (N23.20). Faithful completion proves
it is an isomorphism of the actual sheaves. Every normal bundle
is the trivial rank-three bundle supplied by the quotient
abelian variety. Thus
\[
                 h^2\mathcal E xt^1(I_Z,I_Z)=9N.             \tag{N23.22}
\]

The maximal zero-dimensional subsheaf of
$\mathcal E xt^2(I_Z,I_Z)$ is intrinsic. Formula(N23.19)
identifies it at each node with exactly $k^9$, because neither
three-dimensional branch module has finite-length torsion.
Equivalently it is the cross Yoneda product of the two canonical
normal pieces in (N23.21); the tensor-resolution map identifies
that product with the middle $k^3\otimes k^3$ term. Consequently
there is an actual subsheaf $\mathcal T$ with
\[
 \mathcal T\hookrightarrow\mathcal E xt^2(I_Z,I_Z),\qquad
                     h^0(\mathcal T)=9q.                    \tag{N23.23}
\]
This construction is unchanged by coordinate changes at a node.

\paragraph{5. Survival through the actual local-to-global differential.}
Use the local-to-global Ext spectral sequence
\[
 E_2^{p,q}=H^p(A,\mathcal E xt^q(I_Z,I_Z))
                       \Longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{p+q}(I_Z,I_Z).
                                                               \tag{N23.24}
\]
The precise primary locator is Stacks Project, Section20.43,
\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BQP}{Tag0BQP};
its boundedness hypotheses hold for these actual sheaves.
No degeneration is assumed. From the $9q$-dimensional subspace
in $E_2^{0,2}$, the differential $d_2$ can remove at most $9N$
dimensions by (N23.22). The only further outgoing differential
is $d_3$ to a subquotient of $H^3(A,\mathcal O_A)$, of dimension
$20$, since $\mathcal H om(I_Z,I_Z)=\mathcal O_A$.
There are no incoming differentials at this position. Hence
\[
            \dim\operatorname{Ext}^2(I_Z,I_Z)\ge9q-9N-20.
                                                               \tag{N23.25}
\]
The full degree-two categorical trace has target
$\bigoplus_{p=0}^4H^{p+2}(A,\Omega_A^p)$, whose dimension is
\[
                  \sum_{p=0}^4\binom6p\binom6{p+2}=495.     \tag{N23.26}
\]
This is the full Atiyah--Chern trace used by
Buchweitz--Flenner, Proposition6.2.1 and Theorem6.4.2, at the
published locators in HD-N21. For the explicit numbers above,
the ordinary Ext dimension is at least $21066028$, and its
trace kernel has dimension at least $21065533$.

\paragraph{6. Every coherent point action on this direction list.}
First, the explicit $101$-torsion arrangement has trivial
translation stabilizer. For each coordinate direction $C_S$,
its distinct cosets form a set of $192$ or $1344$ points in
$(A/C_S)[101]$. Any translation preserving that set is itself
$101$-torsion, by taking the difference of a point and its image.
A nonzero such translation acts freely with orbits of length101,
which cannot divide either cardinality. Thus a stabilizing
translation lies in every $C_S$. Their intersection is zero,
because every coordinate occurs in one of their normal sets.

There is also a uniform bound for every nodal arrangement with
the displayed directions and multiplicities. Let $H$ be its
full translation stabilizer. The two amplitude-one graph
directions with slopes $(1,1,1)$ and $(i,i,-1)$ each have exactly
three parallel components. Translation preserves directions,
so $H$ maps to the translation stabilizers of these two sets
of three quotient points. Each of those finite groups has order
dividing three, since it acts freely on the set of points.
The kernel lies in the intersection of the two graph subgroups.
That intersection is the kernel of the actual isogeny with
diagonal entries $i-1,i-1,-2$, of degree $2\cdot2\cdot4=16$.
Therefore
\[
                              |H|\le144.                    \tag{N23.27}
\]
In particular $H$ is finite; its finiteness was not assumed.

Every isomorphism $T_a^*I_Z\otimes P\simeq I_Z$, with
$P\in\widehat A$, has $P\simeq\mathcal O_A$: taking double
duals gives this literal line-bundle isomorphism, since
$I_Z^{**}=\mathcal O_A$. After trivializing $P$, the isomorphism
of ideals extends to an automorphism of $\mathcal O_A$, which is
multiplication by a nonzero constant. Naturality of the inclusion
into the double dual therefore makes the two ideal subsheaves
equal. Their zero schemes agree, so $a\in H$.
Thus every faithful coherent finite point action is by a subgroup
$G\subset H$ of translations. Let $p:A\to A/G$ be the actual
finite etale quotient. The invariant reduced union descends to
a reduced union $Z'$ of translated abelian threefolds, with
\[
            p^*I_{Z'}=I_Z,\qquad q'=q/|G|\ge16320,
                                      \qquad N'\le9408.     \tag{N23.28}
\]
Etaleness preserves the two transverse local branches and the
absence of triples. Images of components are smooth translated
abelian subvarieties. Two components in one orbit cannot meet:
they are cosets of the same connected subgroup, and distinct
cosets are disjoint. Thus the local model and the Ext proof
apply to $Z'$ itself, rather than to a formal quotient class.

Equations(N23.25)--(N23.26) downstairs now give a trace kernel
of dimension at least
\[
                 9(16320-9408)-20-495=61693.                 \tag{N23.29}
\]
Finite-etale pullback on Ext is injective: its adjunction unit
is split by the sheaf trace divided by $\deg p$. The pulled
classes are invariant for the canonical deck action and have
zero categorical trace, since the finite-free resolutions,
Atiyah class and trace commute with etale pullback.
Moreover $\operatorname{Hom}(I_Z,I_Z)=\mathbf C$.
Every other coherent choice of the isomorphisms
$T_a^*I_Z\simeq I_Z$ differs from the canonical choice by
scalars. Those scalars cancel on conjugation of Ext, so these
same classes are invariant for the given action, including its
given scalar coherence. The identity-sector comparison in
HD-N12 identifies this ambient invariant trace kernel with
the weak equivariant trace kernel. This proves the assertion
for every coherent point action, including specially invariant
arrangements.

For an actual isogeny $f:A'\to A$, the ideal $f^*I_Z$ still
has double dual $\mathcal O_{A'}$. Its full point stabilizer
is the translation group $f^{-1}(H)$: equality of inverse-image
supports implies equality of their images in $A$. Apply the
previous descent to $H$ and pull its kernel through
$A'\to A\to A/H$. It remains injective and invariant for
$f^{-1}(H)$ and hence for every subgroup and every coherent
linearization, by the same rank-one scalar argument.
An actual line-tensor or Fourier--Mukai equivalence transports
Ext, categorical trace and the actual point action by the
intertwiners proved in HD-N12 and HD-N17. Thus these stated
transports retain the obstruction. No statement about a new
complex with a different differential or an unrelated point
action is inferred from the cycle identity alone.
