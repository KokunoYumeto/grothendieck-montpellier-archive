\section[HD-F1-T18: defining fields at most 32]{HD-F1-T18: the exact Jordan maps reduce every remaining almost simple Lie-type source to defining field size at most 32}
\label{sec:f1-refined-field-lie-source-exclusion}

Every remaining T2-OTHER source with almost simple projective
coefficient image of Lie type has defining field size \(q\le32\).
For the three largest remaining field sizes the original
coefficient rank satisfies the further bounds
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 q&29&31&32\\ \hline
 m\le&87&46&38.
 \end{array}
 \tag{F1T18.1}
\]
These conclusions include all central and outer lifts and
all multiplicities in restriction to the perfect central
layer. The conservative independently checked T16 exclusion
is retained; the sharper result below uses the two explicit
estimates of the same published source and the precise
central-scalar comparison.

\paragraph{1. Published estimates and the actual multiplicative Jordan maps.}
In Liebeck, \emph{Character ratios for finite groups of Lie
type, and applications}, \emph{Contemp. Math.}~694 (2017),
193--208, Theorem~2.4, published pp.~195--196 (author PDF
pp.~3--4), the unipotent estimate for \(q>4\) is
\(1/(\sqrt q-1)\), and the non-unipotent estimate for
\(q>9\) is \(9/q\). Here the group is the simply
connected fixed-point group of the notation in Section~2.
We use the first estimate on actual nonidentity unipotent
elements and the second only when the semisimple part is
noncentral. The following maps explain all central multiples.

For \(x\in\widetilde S\setminus Z(\widetilde S)\),
write its order as \(p^a t\), with \((p,t)=1\).
Choose integers \(v,w\) satisfying
\[
 tv\equiv1\pmod{p^a},\qquad
 p^aw\equiv1\pmod t,
 \qquad u=x^{tv},\quad s=x^{p^aw}.
 \tag{F1T18.2}
\]
Thus \(u\) has \(p\)-power order, \(s\) has order
prime to \(p\), and the two displayed powers commute
and multiply to \(x\). In a faithful algebraic matrix
representation in characteristic \(p\), these are its
unipotent and semisimple parts: the relevant annihilating
polynomials are \(X^{p^a}-1=(X-1)^{p^a}\) and
\(X^t-1\), the latter separable. In particular both
parts belong to the same fixed-point group, by these
explicit power maps.

For an irreducible complex character \(\chi\), a
central \(s\) acts by a scalar root of unity \(z_\chi\).
The exact representation equality is then
\[
 \rho_\chi(x)=z_\chi\rho_\chi(u),\qquad
 |\chi(x)|=|\chi(u)|.
 \tag{F1T18.3}
\]
The element \(u\) is noncentral: a central \(u\), together
with the central \(s\), would make their actual product
\(x=su\) central. Thus the unipotent estimate applies to
\(u\). When
\(s\) is noncentral, \(x\) is non-unipotent and the
stated second estimate applies directly to \(x\).
These two cases establish the single bound
\[
 \frac{|\chi(x)|}{\chi(1)}\le R(q),\qquad
 R(q)=\max\left\{\frac1{\sqrt q-1},\frac9q\right\},
 \qquad q>9.
 \tag{F1T18.4}
\]
In particular the \(9/q\) estimate has not been applied
to a central multiple of a nontrivial unipotent.

The named input is the inspected published Liebeck
statement. The full original Gluck proof remains
unavailable in this run, as documented for T16. No
independent reconstruction of that original character
estimate is asserted here.

\paragraph{2. Transport this estimate to every original inertia matrix.}
For every \(q\ge29\), the perfectness and universal-cover
exceptions in Malle--Testerman Theorem~24.17 and
Remark~24.19/Table~24.3 are absent: all their defining
field sizes are at most nine. The complete construction
of T16 therefore gives the actual maps
\[
 \widetilde S\xrightarrow{\beta}L\longrightarrow S,
 \qquad L=P'=[L,L],\quad \beta\text{ surjective},
 \quad V_{\mathbf C}^L=0.
 \tag{F1T18.5}
\]
As in (F1T16.5), sum the inflated nontrivial irreducible
characters with the actual restriction multiplicities.
Equation (F1T18.4) gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}_V(h)|\le R(q)m,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(h-1)\ge\frac{1-R(q)}2m
 \quad(h\in L\setminus Z(L)).
 \tag{F1T18.6}
\]
For every nonscalar original \(g\in G\), the triviality
of \(C_{\operatorname{Aut}(S)}(S)\) supplies an actual
\(e\in L\) with \([g,e]\notin Z(L)\). For every scalar
\(\lambda\), use the same matrix identity
\[
 [g,e]-1=
   ((g-\lambda)e-e(g-\lambda))g^{-1}e^{-1}.
 \tag{F1T18.7}
\]
It gives
\(\operatorname{rank}(g-\lambda)\ge(1-R(q))m/4\).
The nonidentity scalar inertia case has moving rank
exactly \(m\). Thus all the true original inertia
matrices obey this moving-rank bound, including the
outer and scalar lifts.

\paragraph{3. Exact rank bounds at the original cover.}
Use the original identities
\(2m+6=\sum_j\operatorname{rank}(g_j-1)\) and
\(b\ge12\), with the same full connected sphere cover.
For \(q\ge37\), both terms defining \(R(q)\) are at
most \(9/37\): indeed
\(1/(\sqrt q-1)<1/5<9/37\), and \(9/q\le9/37\).
Consequently
\[
 2m+6\ge12\frac{1-9/37}{4}m=\frac{84}{37}m,
 \qquad m\le\frac{111}{5},\qquad m\le22.
 \tag{F1T18.8}
\]
This contradicts \(m\ge27\). Since the field size is a
prime power, the largest possible size below 37 is 32,
proving the main assertion.

For \(q\in\{29,31,32\}\), the first term of \(R(q)\)
is less than \(1/4\), while \(9/q\ge9/32>1/4\).
Hence \(R(q)=9/q\), and the same branch budget gives
\[
                 (q-27)m\le6q.
 \tag{F1T18.9}
\]
The integral upper bounds are \(87,46,38\), respectively,
which proves (F1T18.1). This records these remaining
intervals without asserting any actual source exists
within them.
