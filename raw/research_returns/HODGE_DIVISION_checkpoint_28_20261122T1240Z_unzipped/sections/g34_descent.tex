\section{All determinant classes for Eisenstein sixfolds}

\paragraph{HD-G10 (an effective descent from eightfolds).}
Let $K=\mathbb Q(\omega)$, where $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$, and
$\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\omega]$. Let $(A,\theta_A)$ be a polarized
abelian sixfold with an integral $\mathcal O$-action of signature $(3,3)$
whose Rosati involution restricts to conjugation on $K$. Write
\[
\Delta=\frac1{6!}\int_A\theta_A^6
\]
for its polarization elementary-divisor product. All ordinary Hermitian
determinant classes are allowed. With the explicit integer function
$D_8$ proved in HD-G08, put
\[
\boxed{D_{6,\omega}^{\rm all}(\Delta)=D_8(\Delta^2).}
\]
There is a reduced irreducible effective three-cycle $Z\subset A$ with
nonzero Weil projection and
\[
\deg_{\theta_A}Z\le D_{6,\omega}^{\rm all}(\Delta).
\]
This bound holds on every fibre of every fixed polarized Weil sixfold
family with the displayed integral action. In particular, for a nonzero
rational Weil class $t$ with $\delta t$ integral and
$T=-\int_A t^2>0$, the proved Theorem W gives
\[
\boxed{
c_t\le\left\lceil
C_{3,3+\omega}(\delta,T)D_8(\Delta^2)^4
\right\rceil.}
\]
The checked small projector HD-D03 gives the further explicit estimate
\[
\boxed{c_t\le393697003\,\delta M_T D_8(\Delta^2)^2,
\qquad M_T=\lceil\sqrt T\rceil.}
\]

For completeness, the integer in this statement is entirely explicit.
Using the constants of HD-G07--HD-G08,
\[
\begin{gathered}
G=39191040,\qquad g=4G+1,\qquad
R=(2G+1)G!G^G,\qquad L=2gR,\\
S_0=G^{2g}(g!)^2,\qquad
S_B=(L/2)!S_0^R,\qquad k=L/2+4,\\
B_0=(40G)^k\binom{k}{4}S_B\,8!G^8,
\qquad D_8(q)=(4G^8q)^8B_0.
\end{gathered}
\]
Thus $D_{6,\omega}^{\rm all}(\Delta)=(4G^8\Delta^2)^8B_0$.
All cycle degrees here are unnormalized intersection degrees.

\paragraph{Proof: the exact determinant and polarization.}
Let $\Lambda=H_1(A,\mathbb Z)$ and let $E_A$ be the alternating
Riemann form of $\theta_A$. Since $\mathcal O$ is a PID, the
torsion-free $\mathcal O$-module $\Lambda$ is free of rank six.
Use the second-variable-linear Hermitian form
\[
h_A(x,y)=E_A(x,\omega y)-\bar\omega E_A(x,y).
\]
Its matrix $H_A$ in an $\mathcal O$-basis has entries in $\mathcal O$.
The identity Hermitian matrix induces a unimodular alternating form
on the underlying integral basis $(v_j,\omega v_j)$. Replacing the
identity matrix by $H_A$ multiplies its rational determinant by
\[
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\det H_A)=(\det H_A)^2.
\]
On the other hand, the determinant of the alternating form is
$\Delta^2$. Thus $|\det H_A|=\Delta$. Its signature is $(3,3)$,
so
\[
\det H_A=-\Delta.
\tag{G10.1}
\]
This computes both the ordinary determinant class and its normalization
against the given integral polarization.

Take $E_\omega=\mathbb C/\mathcal O$ with the degree-one divisor
class $\eta=[0]$, and put
\[
B=E_\omega\times E_\omega,\qquad
\rho_B(\omega)(x,y)=(\omega x,\bar\omega y),\qquad
\theta_B=\eta_1+\Delta\eta_2.
\]
This is an actual integral balanced action and an ample polarization
of type $(1,\Delta)$. In the two coordinate $\mathcal O$-generators,
the compatible Hermitian form is
\[
h_B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-\Delta\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{G10.2}
\]
Indeed $E(v,\omega v)=1$ on the first elliptic factor, while the
conjugate action gives $E(v,\bar\omega v)=-1$ on the second factor;
the second polarization is multiplied by $\Delta$.
Consequently $A\times B$, with
\[
\Theta=p_A^*\theta_A+p_B^*\theta_B,
\]
has signature $(4,4)$, ordinary determinant $\Delta^2$, and
polarization product
\[
\frac1{8!}\int_{A\times B}\Theta^8=\Delta^2.
\tag{G10.3}
\]

\paragraph{Proof: this rank-eight Hermitian space is split.}
Here is a direct verification of the precise splitting needed for
HD-G08. The rational quadratic form $x\mapsto h(x,x)$ associated
to a Hermitian space of signature $(r,r)$ has rational rank $4r$
and real signature $(2r,2r)$. Meyer's theorem supplies a nonzero
rational isotropic vector in the successive ranks $16,12,8$.
The exact published locator is Serre, \emph{A Course in Arithmetic},
Chapter IV, Section 3.2, Corollary 2 (Meyer) to Theorem 8,
printed page 43.
Each vector is also a nonzero $K$-vector with $h(v,v)=0$.
Choose $w$ with $h(v,w)=1$, and replace it by
$w-h(w,w)v/2$; the new vector is isotropic and still pairs to one
with $v$. Thus each step splits off the Hermitian hyperbolic plane
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
After three such steps the remaining plane has signature $(1,1)$
and ordinary determinant class $[-1]$, because the original class
is $[\Delta^2]=[1]$ and each $U$ has determinant $-1$.
Diagonalize the remaining plane over $K$ as $\langle a,b\rangle$,
with $a,b\in\mathbb Q^*$. The determinant class gives
$-ab=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(z)$ for some $z\in K^*$. The vector
$(z/a,1)$ is isotropic, since
\[
aN(z/a)+b=\frac{-ab}{a}+b=0.
\]
It splits the last hyperbolic plane by the same explicit adjustment.
Therefore the Hermitian space of $A\times B$ is $K$-isometric to
$U^4$.

HD-G08 now applies to this actual polarized eightfold, including its
decomposable fibres: its bound was extended to the whole fixed moduli
component before restriction to any family. It gives an effective
integral four-cycle $Y$ on $A\times B$ such that
\[
e_{W_{A\times B}}[Y]\ne0,
\qquad
\int_Y\Theta^4\le D_8(\Delta^2).
\tag{G10.4}
\]

\paragraph{Proof: four effective divisor classes on the CM surface.}
Consider the actual homomorphisms
\[
f_1(x,y)=x,\quad f_2(x,y)=y,\quad
f_3(x,y)=x+y,\quad f_4(x,y)=x+\omega y,
\]
and the integral classes $L_j=f_j^*\eta$, $D_j=2L_j$.
The line bundle $\mathcal O_{E_\omega}(2[0])$ is globally generated:
it has two sections, while its twist by the negative of any point
has one. Hence all four line bundles defining $D_j$ are globally
generated.

Relative to the product principal polarization on $E_\omega^2$,
the pullback polarization matrices of $L_j$ are obtained from their
row morphisms $r_j$ as $\bar r_j^{\mathsf t}r_j$. Explicitly they are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega\\\bar\omega&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{G10.5}
\]
The first two give the diagonal entries, while the last two, after
subtracting the diagonal entries, give the independent real and
imaginary off-diagonal directions. The intersection calculation is
also explicit. Each $L_j^2=0$, and for $j\ne k$ the intersection
$L_jL_k$ is the degree of the isogeny $(f_j,f_k)$, namely the norm
of its row determinant. Therefore
\[
\left(\int_B L_jL_k\right)_{j,k}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1&1\\
1&0&1&1\\
1&1&0&3\\
1&1&3&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=-3.
\tag{G10.6}
\]
For $D_j=2L_j$ the matrix is four times this matrix and has
determinant $-768$. The four classes are consequently linearly
independent over $\mathbb C$. They lie in $H^{1,1}(B)$, which
has dimension four, so they span it; their intersection pairing is
nondegenerate. In particular the whole rational $(1,1)$ space of
this CM surface is spanned by these explicit integral divisor
classes.

The needed nef bounds hold for these same divisors:
\[
0\le D_j\le4\theta_B\qquad (1\le j\le4).
\tag{G10.7}
\]
For $j=1,2$ this is immediate from $\Delta\ge1$.
For $j=3$, the theorem of the square gives
\[
(x+y)^*\eta+(x-y)^*\eta=2\eta_1+2\eta_2;
\]
both summands are nef. Multiplying by two and using
$\eta_1+\eta_2\le\eta_1+\Delta\eta_2$ proves the assertion.
For $j=4$ use the same identity with $y$ replaced by $\omega y$;
multiplication by $\omega$ preserves $\eta$.

\paragraph{Proof: nonvanishing survives an effective contraction.}
Write $V_A=H^1(A,\mathbb Q)$ and $V_B=H^1(B,\mathbb Q)$.
The exterior-product morphism
\[
\bigwedge_K^6V_A\otimes_K\bigwedge_K^2V_B
\longrightarrow \bigwedge_K^8(V_A\oplus V_B)
\tag{G10.8}
\]
is an isomorphism of one-dimensional $K$-spaces. Under the
rational cohomology embeddings, the complex Weil space of the
product is exactly
\[
\left(\bigwedge^6 V_{A,\sigma}\otimes
\bigwedge^2 V_{B,\sigma}\right)
\oplus
\left(\bigwedge^6 V_{A,\bar\sigma}\otimes
\bigwedge^2 V_{B,\bar\sigma}\right)
\subset W_{A,\mathbb C}\otimes H^2(B,\mathbb C).
\tag{G10.9}
\]
Thus its projector factors through the $(6,2)$ Kunneth component
and then through the canonical Weil projector on the $A$ factor.
This assertion follows directly from the displayed eigenspace
exterior powers and descends to $\mathbb Q$, since conjugation
interchanges the two summands.

Let $[Y]_{6,2}$ denote that Kunneth component and put
\[
T_Y=(e_{W_A}\otimes1)[Y]_{6,2}.
\]
Equations (G10.4) and (G10.9) give $T_Y\ne0$. The projector is a
rational morphism of Hodge structures. Since $[Y]$ has type $(4,4)$
and $W_A$ has pure type $(3,3)$,
\[
T_Y\in W_A\otimes
\bigl(H^2(B,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{1,1}(B)\bigr).
\tag{G10.10}
\]
The four functionals $v\mapsto\int_BvD_j$ separate this second
factor by (G10.6). Hence at least one of the contractions
\[
\left(1\otimes\int_B(-)D_j\right)T_Y
\tag{G10.11}
\]
is nonzero.

Choose a general actual divisor in the globally generated class
$D_j$. Its pullback meets each component of $Y$ properly or avoids
it. The intersection is an effective integral three-cycle.
Proper pushforward gives an effective integral three-cycle
\[
Z_j=(p_A)_*\bigl(Y\cdot p_B^*D_j\bigr).
\tag{G10.12}
\]
The cycle-class projection formula identifies $e_{W_A}[Z_j]$
with (G10.11), so it is nonzero for at least one $j$.
This also shows that a contributing image has dimension three.

Set $\alpha=p_A^*\theta_A$ and $\beta=p_B^*\theta_B$.
All mixed intersections of these nef classes on the effective
four-cycle $Y$ are nonnegative. By (G10.7) and projection formula,
\[
\begin{split}
\deg_{\theta_A}Z_j
&=\int_Y\alpha^3p_B^*D_j\\
&\le4\int_Y\alpha^3\beta\\
&\le\int_Y(\alpha+\beta)^4
\le D_8(\Delta^2).
\end{split}
\tag{G10.13}
\]
The factor four is exactly the corresponding binomial coefficient.
Among the reduced irreducible components of the detecting $Z_j$,
at least one has nonzero Weil projection. Its degree is no larger
than the degree of the effective pushforward, giving the required
$Z$ and bound. Theorem W, with the stated normalization, gives
the division estimate. Substitution of this same detector degree into
the Eisenstein sixfold row of HD-D03 gives the displayed quadratic
estimate. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Relation to the published contraction argument.}
Schoen, \emph{Addendum to: Hodge classes on self-products of a variety
with an automorphism}, Compositio Math. 114 (1998), 329--336,
Proposition 10, pp.~332--333, uses the product with a balanced
abelian surface and contraction by divisor classes to pass between
Weil dimensions. Equations (G10.5)--(G10.13) give the effective
integral degree calculation for the present actual CM surface.
The four full $(1,1)$ directions ensure that additional Kunneth
components of an effective detector cannot cancel every detecting
contraction. The proof uses the fully checked HD-G08, and no
theorem from the unrefereed Severn manuscript.

\paragraph{HD-G11 (all determinant classes and a fixed nonmaximal order).}
Let $(A,\theta)$ be a polarized Weil sixfold for $K=\mathbb Q(\omega)$
with integral action by $\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathbb Z[\omega]$,
Rosati involution equal to conjugation, and polarization product
$\Delta$. Then
\[
\boxed{\delta_W(A,\theta)\le f^{93}D_8(\Delta^2).}
\tag{G11.1}
\]
Every fixed polarized family with these data has this same bound
on every fibre. For $\delta t$ integral and
$T=-\int_A t^2>0$, one has
\[
\boxed{
c_t\le\left\lceil
C_{3,f(3+\omega)}(\delta,T)
\bigl(f^{93}D_8(\Delta^2)\bigr)^4
\right\rceil.}
\tag{G11.2}
\]
The corresponding small-projector bound, with
$M_T=\lceil\sqrt T\rceil$, is
\[
\boxed{c_t\le393697003\,\delta M_T f^{210}D_8(\Delta^2)^2.}
\tag{G11.5}
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Put $\Lambda'=\mathcal O\Lambda=\Lambda+\omega\Lambda$, where
$\Lambda=H_1(A,\mathbb Z)$. The integral $\mathcal O_f$-action
gives $f\Lambda'\subset\Lambda$. Hence $\Lambda'$ defines the
actual quotient isogeny
\[
q:A\longrightarrow A',\qquad
d=\deg q=[\Lambda':\Lambda].
\]
Equip $\Lambda'$ with $E'=fE$, where $E$ is the Riemann form
of $\theta$. This form is integral: for $x,y\in\Lambda$, both
$fE(x,\omega y)$ and $fE(\omega x,y)$ are integral because
$f\omega\Lambda\subset\Lambda$, while
$E(\omega x,\omega y)=E(x,y)$ by Rosati compatibility.
These identities cover all four terms after expansion on
$\Lambda+\omega\Lambda$. The form remains positive and compatible
with $\mathcal O$, defining a polarization $\theta'$ on $A'$ with
\[
q^*\theta'=f\theta,\qquad
\Delta'=\frac1{6!}\int_{A'}(\theta')^6
=\frac{f^6\Delta}{d}.
\tag{G11.3}
\]
Apply HD-G10 to $A'$. It yields an effective integral detecting
three-cycle $Z'$ with
\[
\deg_{\theta'}Z'\le D_8((\Delta')^2).
\]
Finite flat pullback gives an effective integral three-cycle
$q^*Z'$ on $A$. The $K$-linear isogeny induces an isomorphism
on the Weil spaces, commuting with their exterior projectors,
so this pullback has nonzero Weil projection. Its degree is
\[
\begin{split}
\deg_\theta q^*Z'
&=\frac d{f^3}\deg_{\theta'}Z'\\
&\le\frac d{f^3}D_8\left(
\left(\frac{f^6\Delta}{d}\right)^2\right)\\
&=\frac{f^{93}}{d^{15}}D_8(\Delta^2)
\le f^{93}D_8(\Delta^2).
\end{split}
\tag{G11.4}
\]
The equality uses the exact degree-sixteen homogeneity of
$D_8(x^2)$. A detecting reduced irreducible component has no larger
degree, proving (G11.1). Finally $a=f(3+\omega)$ belongs to
$\mathcal O_f$ and
\[
\frac a{\bar a}=\frac{8+5\omega}{7}
\]
is not a root of unity: it is outside $\mathcal O$, which contains
all roots of unity of $K$. The proved Theorem W therefore yields
(G11.2). HD-D03 multiplies its maximal-order sixfold coefficient by
$f^{24}$. Substitution of (G11.1) gives
$f^{24}(f^{93})^2=f^{210}$, proving (G11.5).
\hfill$\square$
