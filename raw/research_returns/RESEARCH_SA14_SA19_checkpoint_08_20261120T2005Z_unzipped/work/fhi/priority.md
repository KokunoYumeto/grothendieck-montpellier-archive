# SA19 H1–H2: mathematical identification and dating audit

## SA19 H1 — Component-group duality

**Mathematical identification: Known. Historical priority: Undetermined from the supplied evidence.**

The boxed statement on 43:117 says that the component groups of dual abelian varieties, viewed through their Néron models over the residue field, are dual with values in Q/Z. This matches the precise conjecture later stated in SGA 7 I, Exposé IX, §1.2.1 and Conjecture 1.3, printed p.323 (PDF p.328 in the opened SLMath scan). The original page constructs a canonical pairing and separately conjectures its perfectness. It does not prove perfectness in general. Section 1.3.1, pp.323–324, discusses special cases and the reduction to complete strictly local bases; its discussion of Artin–Mazur is explicitly about unpublished work.

The modern theorem matching the manuscript's complete discrete valuation setting is Takashi Suzuki, *Grothendieck's pairing on Néron component groups: Galois descent from the semistable case*, Kyoto J. Math. **60** (2020), no.2, 593–716, DOI 10.1215/21562261-2019-0045. The opened author version is arXiv:1410.3046v4, dated 22 March 2018. Its setup on p.2 assumes that K is a complete discretely valued field and its residue field k is perfect of characteristic p>0. Theorem C on p.4 proves that Grothendieck's pairing is perfect for every abelian variety over K. Algebraically closed residue fields satisfy its residue-field hypothesis. Theorem A on p.3 and Theorem B on p.4 formulate derived duality and its Galois descent, explaining the substantive correspondence with the programme on 43:116–120. These are identified as known theorems, not new proofs in this report.

The residue-field condition is meaningful. An opened primary counterexample outside the manuscript's algebraically closed hypothesis is Bosch–Lorenzini, *Grothendieck's pairing on component groups of Jacobians*, Invent. Math. **148** (2002), 353–396, Lemma 6.1 and Example 6.2, pp.393–395. It constructs Jacobians over a discrete valuation field with imperfect residue field whose pairing has a nonzero radical. This does not contradict the manuscript's stated scope.

Primary sources opened:

- SGA 7 I original scan: https://library.slmath.org/nonmsri/sga/sga/pdf/sga7-1.pdf . Exposé IX §1.2.1 and Conjecture 1.3 p.323 were visually checked from PDF p.328; local evidence is `output/sga7_p328.png`.
- Suzuki: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.3046 . Setup p.2, Theorems A–C pp.3–4; published metadata checked at https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.3046 .
- Bosch–Lorenzini author PDF: https://dinolorenzini.franklinresearch.uga.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/papers/LorenziniBosch.pdf . Lemma 6.1 and Example 6.2 were read; their explicit geometric realization is not reproduced as an independent new construction here.

## SA19 H2 — Three-variable Ext and biextensions

**Mathematical identification: Supported and matched to a known formalism. Historical priority: Undetermined.**

The boxed three-variable Ext on 43:16 is labelled the Lang pairing. Several inputs and decorations are uncertain in the supplied transcription. On 43:21, the decisive additional evidence is the explicit use of the tensor product of two resolutions and H¹ of a Hom complex, together with a Galois-equivariant cup product. This is substantial evidence for a biextension/derived-tensor interpretation; the isolated three-argument notation alone would not determine a precise category or construct a biextension.

The exact published comparison is SGA 7 I, Exposé VII, Corollary 3.6.5, printed p.205 (PDF p.210 in the opened scan):

    Biext¹(P,Q;G) ≅ Ext¹(P ⊗ᴸ Q,G).

The immediately preceding §3.6.4 describes the corresponding category of extensions of a tensor product of resolutions. Corollary 3.6.5 itself states degree one; no unqualified all-degree comparison is attributed to it. The printed formula and its proof were visually checked.

Mumford's primary paper *Bi-extensions of formal groups*, in *Algebraic Geometry: Papers presented at the Bombay Colloquium 1968*, Oxford University Press/TIFR, 1969, original pp.307–322, defines biextensions in §2, original pp.310–312, then treats group functors in §3, pp.312–314. A biextension is a torsor under a third group over the product of two groups, endowed with compatible extension laws in the two variables. The Poincaré bundle is among his examples. The official TIFR retypeset volume has new running pagination: Mumford's article is pp.321–337; the original pagination is retained in the margins. It must not be cited as if the new page numbers were the original ones.

Exposé VII's introduction, printed p.133 (PDF p.138), explicitly attributes introduction of biextensions in the formal-group context to Mumford. That contemporaneous attribution is evidence which a priority claim must address, rather than silently replacing it by a proposed archive date.

Primary sources opened:

- SGA 7 I as above: Exposé VII introduction p.133, §3.6.4 p.204, Corollary 3.6.5 p.205. Local visual evidence is `output/sga7_p138.png` and `output/sga7_p210.png`.
- Official TIFR volume: https://mathweb.tifr.res.in/Documents/Publications/Studies/SM_04-Algebraic-Geometry.pdf . The title and copyright pages establish publication in 1969 and the conference report establishes the meeting on 16–23 January 1968. The article's definitions and resolution discussion were read.

## The date inference does not follow

The SA19 hunch calls the dossier a 1962–64 dossier and treats three leaves as dating evidence. The transcriptions give more limited information:

| Leaf | Actual dated item | What it supports |
|---|---|---|
| 43:144 | A typed remark cites Shatz's Harvard thesis, June 1962 | A bibliographic date for the cited thesis; a possible lower bound for that typed text, not an upper bound for other leaves |
| 43:87 | A bibliography cites Lang's Bourbaki talk no.274, May 1964, and Néron's 1964 IHÉS paper | Dates of cited works; no date for 43:16, 43:21 or 43:117 |
| 43:118 | A cancelled administrative list mentions James and Bott with September 1963 | An administrative reference on that leaf, without an established physical or chronological implication for the scientific text on neighbouring leaves |

No explicit date occurs on the relevant supplied transcriptions 43:16, 43:21 or 43:117. The inspected cover sheets do not supply one. Reused paper and adjacency in an archival dossier do not by themselves bound the date at which a separate scientific leaf was written. In particular, a reference to a work published in 1964 establishes no terminus ante quem of 1964. The argument offered in HUNCHES therefore does not prove pre-SGA-7 priority.

The official University of Montpellier archival catalogue supplies a further primary check. Its cote 43 entry gives the tentative span **“[à partir de 1961-vers 1968]”**, with the notes themselves marked undated. Footnote 37 records a verso containing Bourbaki exposé 338 (1967–1968), alongside earlier bibliography and exposé 239. The catalogue separately warns that versos often have no connection with the mathematical notes. Thus its description does not support the proposed dossier-wide upper bound of 1964 or 1965. Equally, a late verso somewhere in the dossier does not date the three scientific leaves at issue. Cote 44 is separately catalogued as 1964 and must not supply an inherited date for cote 43. Source opened on 20 November 2026: https://grothendieck.umontpellier.fr/archives-grothendieck/ , entry for cote 43 and footnote 37.

This is a refutation of that dating inference, not a proof that the manuscript was written later. Both chronology claims remain **Undetermined**. The exact gate is an independently secure date or provenance for the relevant scientific leaves, together with a justified relation between that evidence and these particular texts. A dated letter quoting the formulas, archival collation evidence, or an independently dated draft containing them could close the gate. Merely repeating the dossier-wide date range cannot.
