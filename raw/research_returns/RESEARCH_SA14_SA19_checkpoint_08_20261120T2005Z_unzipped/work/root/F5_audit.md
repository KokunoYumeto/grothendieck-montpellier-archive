The Kummer divisor calculation gives B a = n d, and the adjugate kills each abelian tame inertia image by det(B). Each image is procyclic, so the subgroup generated by the finitely many images is finite. At a higher exceptional valuation the Kummer exponent is the sum of the component exponents weighted by valuations of their local equations; a local unit has valuation zero. Thus the same finite subgroup contains all such inertia images. An open subgroup disjoint from it gives one fixed finite cover. Restricting any abelian prime-to-p coefficient representation to that cover kills all exceptional inertia. Purity on a fixed regular resolution then extends the coefficient sheaf. Restriction on H1 is injective by prime-to-p corestriction.

For the extended bundle V, the isomorphism F*V=V and the projection formula identify the pole-graded quotient with B_Z tensor V|Z tensor J^(-m)|Z exactly as claimed in F5.2. Abelian prime-to-p monodromy splits V into torsion line bundles over the algebraically closed coefficient field. Their restrictions have degree zero on every exceptional component. The fixed filtration of the exceptional cycle and a fixed embedding of each vector bundle into positive line bundles give a cutoff independent of the order, number, or characters of these torsion summands.

Suzuki's negative-degree filtration remains negative after every torsion twist. It gives injectivity of the linear map on the finite low-pole H1 spaces. The Frobenius-fixed-space argument bounds the Fp-dimension by their k-dimension, and curve Riemann–Roch bounds the latter linearly in rank. The normalized nodal exceptional curve gives the same rank bound for its ordinary H1. The five-term nearby-cycle sequence then proves the stated uniform estimate.

Two harmless hypotheses should be made explicit in the final proof: a complete equal-characteristic local ring with perfect residue field has a coefficient field and is F-finite; if A is already regular, choose a blowup resolution so that the exceptional divisor is nonempty. These clarifications were sent to the owner before promotion. The source's unrestricted Frobenius-finiteness step is valid in this setting.

Inflation from the F3 quotient embeds its twisted H1 in geometric H1. Its cyclic odd-order modules in characteristic2 have unbounded dimension/rank ratio, so F5 excludes that specific quotient. Nothing here bounds nonabelian or wild coefficient monodromy.

## Extension audit: F4 and F6

At 17:40 UTC we also read `F_prosolvable_criterion.tex` and `F_pro_p_by_abelian.tex` in full. Both pass. For F4, a generating tuple in the quotient by a minimal elementary abelian normal subgroup has a nongenerating lift precisely when it lies in a complement. The intersection of its generated subgroup with the minimal normal subgroup is invariant under the whole group, since the normal subgroup is abelian. Complements are counted by crossed homomorphisms. The nonzero identity in End_H(M) gives the strict factor needed to ensure a generating lift among all |M|^D tuples. Compactness correctly passes the finite result to the prosolvable group.

For F6, a normal pro-p group acts trivially on any finite simple characteristic-p module; its invariants are nonzero and stable under the whole group. F5 thus applies to these modules. In other characteristics, continuous H1 of the pro-p subgroup vanishes by finite averaging, and inflation–restriction reduces to the finitely generated abelian tame quotient. These bounds are uniform in the quotient. A virtually abelian tame quotient reduces to a finite prime-to-p cover of the punctured spectrum; its normalized complete normal local surface is again within the proved hypotheses. This proves finite generation for the stated geometric nonabelian subclass, but still does not control alternating wild/tame normal factors or nonsolvable quotients.
