## The extension lemma

For a regular-sequence ideal J of length h, C_J = Hom(A/J,K) has only H^h = A/J, after the chosen Koszul orientation. Its degree-h summands at minimal primes of J identify with (A/J)_r; this uses the complete-intersection Gorenstein property and the localization of the chosen global Ext identification.

At a prime q of height j > h, all residual terms above degree j vanish after localization, and the only surviving degree-j summand is the q-summand. Exactness therefore gives a surjection (C_J^{j-1})_q -> C^j_{J,q}. At j=h+1, its kernel is exactly the localization of the augmentation A/J; the prescribed cycle condition kills it. At later stages the kernel is the preceding image, which is killed by the already-established chain identity and d_M^2=0. Thus each new component factors uniquely. The resulting map is diagonal among primes of a fixed height, so the direct sum is legitimate: every source element has finitely many such components. This addresses the potential finite-support concern.

For generation, Ann(v) is p-primary for nonzero v in a p-local p-power-torsion summand. A product of suitable elements outside p kills the finitely many differential components of v. Cohen--Macaulayness supplies a regular sequence of length ht(p) inside Ann(v). The extension then hits v by dividing its p-component by that product. No illicit global inverse is used: division takes place only in the p-local summand.
