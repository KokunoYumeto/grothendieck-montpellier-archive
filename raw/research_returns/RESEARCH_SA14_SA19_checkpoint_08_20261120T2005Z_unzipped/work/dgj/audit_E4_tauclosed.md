## Intersection-matrix argument

If \(Y/R'\) has generic dimension \(d\), the expressions \(Y_iY_jH^{d-1}\) and \(MY_iH^{d-1}\) are correctly top-dimensional intersection numbers on the \(d+1\)-dimensional total space. The fibre relation \(\sum m_iY_i=\operatorname{div}(\varpi')\) gives \(Am=0\). On the regular total space, distinct components are Cartier and their intersection is an effective cycle of the expected dimension \(d-1\). Its degree against the ample \(H^{d-1}\) is strictly positive precisely when the intersection is nonempty.

The special fibre is connected: the finite algebra \(H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\) is a domain over the complete henselian DVR, hence has only one local factor, and Stein factorization applies. Thus its component-intersection graph is connected.

For completeness, the weighted-Laplacian identity follows by substituting

\[
A_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}m_j/m_i
\]

into \(v^tAv\). Each unordered pair contributes

\[
-A_{ij}\bigl((m_j/m_i)v_i^2+(m_i/m_j)v_j^2-2v_iv_j\bigr)
=-A_{ij}m_im_j(v_i/m_i-v_j/m_j)^2.
\]

Connectedness therefore gives \(\ker A=\mathbf Qm\) and \(\operatorname{im}A=m^\perp\). Flat projective intersection constancy gives \(m\cdot b=0\), so \(Aa=-b\) has a rational solution.

For \(N=M+\sum a_iY_i\), its degree against \(H^{d-1}\) on every component is zero. When \(d=1\), this is numerical triviality. For \(d\ge2\), cutting to an integral surface gives nonpositivity of \(N^2H^{d-2}\). The flat-family sum of these nonpositive terms is zero, since \(N\) equals \(M\) generically and \(M\) is generically numerically trivial. Therefore each term vanishes. Kleiman Theorem 6.3(h), whose proof was checked rather than just its surface base case, gives full numerical triviality on each component. Clearing denominators makes the rational-line-bundle use legitimate. Passage to a nonclosed residue field is valid: the geometric components of an integral component form a single conjugacy orbit and their generic multiplicities agree; descended intersection numbers therefore have the same sign and vanish on every component if their sum vanishes.

## Galois averaging and normalization

Zheng Lemma 3.8 applies with trivial input group, dense open \(X_K\), and complete excellent trait. It yields a regular projective alteration over a finite trait extension and an action with purely inseparable invariant field. The open immersion in its general statement becomes an isomorphism here because its source is proper over the original trait and the target separated, so that the immersion is also closed and has dense image.

At the generic point \(\xi\) of the integral reduced special fibre of \(X\), the local ring is indeed a DVR: it is a one-dimensional local domain and its maximal ideal is generated by the original uniformizer. The normalization \(Z\) in the finite extension \(k(Y)\) is finite because \(X\) is excellent. The proper birational map \(Y\to Z\) is an isomorphism at the generic points of prime divisors of the normal target.

The extensions of the DVR valuation to the purely inseparable invariant field are unique. The remaining field extension is Galois, and its group acts transitively on the extensions of the valuation. These are exactly the vertical prime divisors of \(Z\), since finite maps preserve their dimension and the special fibre of \(X\) is integral. Their strict transforms on \(Y\) are therefore the single orbit of vertical components dominating \(X_k\).

The group acts over \(R\), possibly semilinearly over \(R'\). Consequently it fixes the original uniformizer, whose divisor is \(e(R'/R)\sum m_iY_i\), and hence preserves the multiplicities \(m_i\). Averaging the rational divisor makes the coefficients \(a_i\) constant on this orbit. Thus \(a_i/m_i=c\) there, and subtracting \(c\operatorname{div}(\varpi')\) removes every nonexceptional vertical component. Rational averaging requires no invertibility of the group order in the base field.

Numerical triviality is invariant under the group action, so averaging preserves the conclusion for \(M+D\); no invariant choice of the ample bundle \(H\) is needed after the correction has been constructed. The resulting \(D\) is exceptional and numerically trivial on every curve contracted by \(Y\to Z\): \(M\) is pulled back from \(X\), and \(M+D\) is numerically trivial on both generic and special fibres. This also covers fibres over nonclosed points, by invariance of numerical triviality under field extension.

Applying Kollár Lemma 11.60 to \(D\) and \(-D\), both with pushforward zero, gives both inequalities and hence \(D=0\). The hypotheses are satisfied without assuming that \(Z\) is flat over \(X\) or that the quotient by the finite group is étale. Finally, numerical triviality descends along the proper surjective alteration by taking a curve multisection above each integral curve and using the degree projection formula.
