For X=P_S(E), with S a smooth projective surface and E of rank three, a smooth ample relative plane curve Y is a possible dimension-four test. Since Y is regular, a line bundle on its generic curve over k(S), represented by a divisor over that field, extends by closing that divisor on Y. The generic restriction of Pic(X) is generated by O(1). Thus a genuine nontrivial degree-zero p-torsion line bundle on the generic curve would be a potential cokernel witness. A rational point of its Picard functor is not enough: its Brauer obstruction must vanish. No family satisfying all the required smoothness, ampleness and line-bundle conditions has been constructed.

The following simple models fail to supply such a witness.

1. The p-linear incidence equation s0*x0^p+s1*x1^p+s2*x2^p=0 in P2_s x P2_x is smooth and very ample of bidegree (1,p). Projection to the x-plane makes it a P1-bundle, and the two ambient Picard generators remain a basis. Its Picard restriction is an isomorphism. A direct coefficient-Jacobian check also excludes a non-étale finite base map S→P2 in this untwisted three-coefficient construction: at a rank drop the equation and its base derivatives have a common nonzero solution in the pth-power fibre coordinates.
2. Tanaka's characteristic-two conic in Example7.5, q=s*x^2+t*y^2+u*z^2+s*y*z, has coefficient vector (x^2+yz,y^2,z^2) with no common projective zero. Its reverse projection is again a P1-bundle; it does not give an index-two Picard class. The source was opened: arXiv:2308.08119v2, Example7.5 and Remark7.6, printed pp.52–53. The nonreduced discriminant must not be confused with a scheme-theoretically double fibre over its imperfect generic residue field.
3. A constant plane-curve family over S lacks ampleness in the base directions. The conventional Weierstrass cubic in the quotient convention P(O⊕L^-2⊕L^-3), of class 3H+6L, restricts on the zero-section quotient L^-3 to -3L. For positive L this model fails ampleness. This calculation is specific to that model; it is not a theorem excluding all elliptic fibrations.
4. Passing to a level-p cover of the curve parameter space might supply p-torsion, but singularities appear at discriminant ramification. A Frobenius pullback does not repair a singular fibre: the base derivatives disappear. A resolution of the base or total space has not been shown to preserve ampleness of the required divisor. No general impossibility claim is made.

## A2 construction variant under investigation

A separable Artin–Schreier cover could preserve a nontrivial p-torsion class at the generic point and kill its restriction on a ferociously ramified divisor. The local equation z^p-s^(p-1)z=b has purely inseparable residue extension at s=0. Turning this into the required smooth projective pair needs both consistent global line-bundle gluing and a smooth residual divisor; the local equation alone proves neither. The degree-p construction with a smooth ample full inverse-image divisor is now rigorously excluded by A2Z, including when the associated mu-p torsor is nonnormal. Its normalization conductor and the ambient ramification divisor give opposite canonical differences, contradicting positivity of intersection. The full proof is work/dgj/A2_fierce_cover.tex; the independent root audit is work/root/audits_08.md. This excludes one construction, without settling A2.

# No codimension purity for vanishing of torsion sections

## Finite flat proper target, every characteristic p

Let k be a field of characteristic p and R=k[s,t]. Set

G = Spec R[x,y]/(x^p-s^(p-1)x, y^p-t^(p-1)y).

Both defining polynomials are monic. Thus the monomials x^i y^j,
0<=i,j<p, are an R-basis, and G is finite locally free of rank p^2,
hence proper and separated. Give x and y primitive comultiplications.
In characteristic p each displayed polynomial is additive. Its
comultiplication is therefore F tensor 1 + 1 tensor F; the ideal is a
Hopf ideal. Counit and inverse are x,y ->0 and x,y ->-x,-y.

Define h:(Z/p)_R ->G by mapping the component indexed by i in F_p to
x=is, y=it. The defining equations hold because i^p=i; addition is
coordinatewise, so h is a genuine group homomorphism. At a geometric
point of Spec R, the map is nonzero, and indeed injective on the p
geometric points of its source, whenever at least one of s,t is nonzero.
It is the zero homomorphism exactly on V(s,t), a codimension-two locus
in the regular base.

The generic target is etale; its special fibre at the origin is
alpha_p^2. There is no flatness failure in G. The image of h need not be
a flat subgroup, which is precisely why reasoning from a hypothetical
finite flat image would be invalid.

For any N>=1 the same construction with coordinates s_1,...,s_N and
primitive x_i satisfying x_i^p-s_i^(p-1)x_i=0 gives a finite flat target
of rank p^N, with the homomorphism i |-> (is_1,...,is_N) vanishing exactly
in codimension N. There is therefore no uniform codimension bound from
properness, finite presentation, flatness, and separatedness of the
target alone.

## Smooth proper connected target, characteristic two

There is an equally explicit counterexample with an abelian scheme as
target. Over T=Spec F_2[u,1/(1+u^6)], put

E_u: Y^2 Z + u^2 X Y Z + Y Z^2 = X^3 in P^2_T,
O=[0:1:0], P_u=[u:1:u^3].

The section lies on the cubic, since substitution gives
u^3+u^6+u^6=u^3. The standard Weierstrass invariants give
Delta=1+u^6; the following elementary Jacobian check also verifies
smoothness directly. At Z=0, the unique point is O, where the Z
partial derivative equals Y^2 and is nonzero. On Z=1, a singular point
would satisfy

u^2 X+1=0,  u^2 Y+X^2=0,  Y^2+u^2XY=0.

Thus u is nonzero, X=u^(-2), Y=u^(-6), and Y(Y+1)=0 forces u^6=1,
which is excluded from T. Therefore E_u/T is a smooth proper genus-one
curve with identity O, hence an elliptic scheme.

The inverse involution is

[X:Y:Z] |-> [X:Y+u^2X+Z:Z].

It fixes P_u because u^2*u+u^3=0. Consequently 2P_u=O. The section P_u
equals O exactly when u=0.

Now take

S=Spec F_2[s,t,1/((1+s^6)(1+t^6))],  A=E_s x_S E_t.

A is an abelian scheme. The homomorphism (Z/2)_S -> A generated by
(P_s,P_t) is nonzero away from the origin and zero exactly at s=t=0.
The base is regular of dimension two. Products provide the analogous
codimension-N example with abelian targets of dimension N.

This also has a Picard-family interpretation: identify A with the dual
of its dual abelian scheme A^vee, and use the normalized Poincare
bundle. The section (P_s,P_t) defines a relative two-torsion line bundle
on A^vee which becomes trivial exactly at the origin. This is a varying
abelian-scheme family, not the smooth hyperplane sections of a fixed
ambient variety required by A2. It therefore rejects the abstract
purity argument but does not refute A2.
