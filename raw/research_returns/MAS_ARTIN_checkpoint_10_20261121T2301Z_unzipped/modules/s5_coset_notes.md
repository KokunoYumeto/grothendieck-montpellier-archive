# Coset-ring audit

## Result

`MA-FQ-002` in `s5_coset.tex` proves the exact lemma requested for the proposed S5 Fourier obstruction. The topology used for local finiteness is the usual real topology; the Fourier–Stieltjes algebra and its abstract coset ring use the discrete group `R_d`. No uniform discreteness is required.

The delicate Boolean step is proved directly. After translating an actual point of a nonempty atom to zero, choose a minimal dense subgroup among the finite intersection family generated by the ambient and exclusion subgroups. An excluded coset either misses this subgroup completely or meets it in a nowhere dense coset. A finite union of the latter cannot remove all points of this dense subgroup from an open interval. Thus such an atom is dense, contradicting local finiteness. This avoids assuming that a common stabilizer is dense, and it does not assume that the original abstract subgroups are closed.

Once every atom has discrete ambient subgroup, Boolean operations on a lattice give periodic residue classes modulo finitely many point exceptions. Sanders's finite integer coset representation is then used on each nonzero level set; integer-valued l1 coefficient functions have finite support. Inclusion–exclusion yields the weighted comb expansion.

## Remaining scope

The lemma itself is complete. It neither establishes positive definiteness of the S5 signed multiplicity function nor compares its Fourier transform with the exceptional-prime measure. Those are the distinct statements MA-FQ-001 and MA-FQ-003/004 assigned to the other agents. The argument introduces no zero-location hypothesis.
