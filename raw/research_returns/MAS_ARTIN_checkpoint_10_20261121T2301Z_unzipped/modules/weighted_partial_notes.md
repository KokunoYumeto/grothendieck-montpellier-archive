1. Convolution by every E function is bounded on the specified moderate weighted Hilbert space. Every element of the actual smooth ideal acts as zero on its Hilbert quotient, by density of E.
2. MT-M18's full block idempotent has a lift `T_n(1)` whose E seminorms grow only polynomially in height. This uses the holomorphic quotient-algebra norm and the uniform Gaussian interpolation proof, not unstable individual Hermite interpolation coefficients.
3. Convolution by this lift gives an actual bounded projection P_n on the Hilbert quotient. Its range is the image of the finite smooth block, hence is finite dimensional and closed. The rank is at most the full block dimension O(log |n|).
4. For a smooth test g, `T_n(R_n g)` has rapidly decreasing convolution norms. The corresponding finite-rank quotient operators sum in trace norm to the actual C_g, because `g−Σ T_n(R_n g)` belongs to the smooth ideal exactly.
5. Dense block ranges alone would not exclude completion ghosts in the simultaneous kernel of all P_n. The actual convolution identity does: if every P_n kills v, every C_g kills v; a compact smooth approximate identity then gives v=0.
6. Every functional on a finite block range pulls back to a continuous finite Mellin-jet combination. One bounded dual translation, chosen to have distinct eigenvalues at the finitely many different zeros, and finite Hermite spectral projectors separate the zero components. A polynomial times one exponential then has precisely the weighted integrability in MA-TR-103. Higher boundary jets cannot hide in a cancelling finite combination.
7. These duals identify each surviving algebra as the exact truncated jet algebra and identify the entire Hilbert closed ideal. Mellin-Plancherel gives the literal boundary Hardy–Sobolev norm of order s/2 and the same closed principal ideal generated by Xi(a+z).
