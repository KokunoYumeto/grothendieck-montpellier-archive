    After these steps, h is square-free. Equation (9.1) and its product description prove the
irreducibility test in Step 4. For Step 5, a nonscalar b has at least two different coordinates
in Frp . Taking a equal to one of its coordinate values makes gcd(h, b − a) nonconstant and
proper, so the scalar search succeeds. Step 6 succeeds by Lemma 9.1 and the two splitting
propositions. Both final recursive arguments again have smaller positive degree and product
h.
    Every call to a splitter has degree r ≤ deg h and requires only primes at most that degree.
Hence induction also proves the stated restriction on table entries. Along any recursion path
degrees decrease strictly. At a binary branch the child degrees sum to the parent degree; at
a unary branch the degree decreases. Consequently there are at most b + 1 levels and at
most O(b + 1) calls at each level, which gives the call bound.

9.3    Preprocessing and the original input
The table restriction in Proposition 9.2 closes the apparent dependency between factoring and
constructing the table. Enumerate primes q ≤ n in increasing order by trial division. At prime
q, carry out Proposition 3.2 with the supplied auxiliary prime ℓq . Its only factorization call,
for odd q, is on the cyclotomic polynomial Φq of degree q − 1. Proposition 9.2 therefore uses
only table entries already constructed. At q = 2 no factorization call is required. Induction
over these primes constructs the whole table before the final call on the input polynomial.
A recursive Factor call uses the available table and never restarts this preprocessing.
