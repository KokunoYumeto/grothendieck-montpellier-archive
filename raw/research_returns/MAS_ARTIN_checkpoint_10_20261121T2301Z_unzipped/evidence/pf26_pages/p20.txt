Proposition 5.1 (Effective divisor arithmetic). On the curve C above, the following
operations are deterministic and use a number of field operations polynomial in q, N , the
degrees occurring in the input representation, the absolute degrees of the divisors materialized
during the operation, and the parameter ℓ in the third operation:

1. divisor addition, negation, coefficientwise minimum and maximum, and the action of σ;
2. construction of the divisor of an explicitly represented nonzero function, and evaluation

3. computation of the Riemann–Roch space L(D + ℓ∞0 ) for ℓ ≥ 0;
4. replacement of a degree-zero divisor D by a linearly equivalent divisor D′ satisfying

5.1    Fractional ideals and finite linear algebra
Smoothness of the affine curve implies that O is a Dedekind domain. It is a free k[X]-module
with basis 1, Y, . . . , Y q−1 . The finite part of a divisor D is represented by the fractional ideal

                   I(D) = {a ∈ L : vP (a) ≥ vP (D) at every finite point P }.                   (5.1)

The zero function is included in this definition. The rest of the representation is the integer
vector (v∞0 (D), . . . , v∞q−1 (D)).
