## Results and source status

The supplied RH and Dirichlet GRH declarations are adopted as research theorems in this module. Newly recovered definitions are therefore cited with both physical and footer numbers. Original French text is preserved in the curated question register, including deleted passages and the transcription's `unsure` markers. A modern mathematical reading is provided in a separate column. In particular, “modulo torsion” on14/038 concerns the cohomological conclusion: the premise used in the proof is integral. Rationalizing that premise produces an explicit counterexample over an algebraic closure of a finite field.

The original projective Albanese relation is introduced in Definition1.1, official14.pdf physical24/footer23: a correspondence sends a degree-zero zero-cycle with vanishing Albanese sum to the cycle being declared equivalent to zero. Proposition1.2(i) gives the subgroup and Chow quotient;1.2(v), physical25/footer24, places its kernel inside algebraic equivalence. Corollary2.3, physical34/footer33, identifies P as the algebraically trivial part of that quotient. Theorem2.1, physical31/footer30, supplies the induced map from the parameter Albanese. This is sufficient to identify the quotient below without assuming that it is a universal finite-dimensional algebraic representative. The equality of relations is proved in characteristic zero by AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE; AQ-14-NONPROPER proves the forward implication for all smooth quasiprojective parameters in every characteristic; AQ-14-ALTERATION proves equality after inverting the characteristic and rationally, while the possible integral p-primary discrepancy remains explicit.

The bridges use exactly the adopted analytic statements. They do not upgrade a finite-order Dirichlet character to a nonzero-infinity-type CM Hecke character, do not recover extension classes from semisimple Frobenius polynomials, and do not identify a cellular surface with an arithmetic scheme. Where an implication is not established, the status is a remaining gate rather than a claim of formal logical nonimplication. Explicit counterexamples are proved in full where available.

## AQ-FINITE: finite Frobenius recognition of abelian Artin–Tate motives

#### Statement.

Adopt the Dirichlet GRH theorem supplied in `ALPOGE_Main.lean`, `LFunction_eq_zero_re`. Let $N\geq1$, put $$Q=\operatorname{lcm}(N,4),\qquad B_Q=(\varphi(Q)\log Q)^2,$$ and let $M,M'$ be finite direct sums of Tate twists of abelian Artin motives over $\mathbb Q$, whose Artin representations factor through $G_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$. Work initially with a coefficient number field $E\subset\mathbb C$ splitting $G_N$. Write $$M=\bigoplus_{\chi,n}(\chi\otimes E(n))^{m_{\chi,n}},
 \qquad
 M'=\bigoplus_{\chi,n}(\chi\otimes E(n))^{m'_{\chi,n}}.$$ Then $M\simeq M'$ if and only if $$\det(1-TF_p\mid M)=\det(1-TF_p\mid M')
 \quad\text{for every prime }p\leq B_Q,\ p\nmid Q.                 \tag{AQ.1}$$ Here $E(n)$ has Frobenius eigenvalue $p^{-n}$. These finitely many polynomials recover every multiplicity $m_{\chi,n}$. The same isomorphism criterion holds with rational coefficients. The characters are labelled by their Euler-factor values $\chi(p)$; changing the Artin-reciprocity convention inverts all character labels and leaves the criterion unchanged. One may use a single auxiliary $\ell>B_Q$ for all the displayed Frobenius factors, avoiding the prime $\ell$ among the tested primes.

#### Known input and exact locator.

Lamzouri, Li, and Soundararajan, *Conditional bounds for the least quadratic non-residue and related problems*, arXiv:1309.3595v3, Corollary 1.2, p. 3, proves under Dirichlet GRH that every reduced residue class $a\bmod Q$, $Q>3$, contains a prime $p_a\leq(\varphi(Q)\log Q)^2$. This result is cited, not reproved.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The forward direction is functoriality. Inflate all characters to $G_Q$. For each $a\in G_Q$ choose such a prime $p_a$. Every eigenvalue at $p_a$ is $\chi(a)p_a^{-n}$, of complex absolute value $p_a^{-n}$. Since $p_a>1$, different integers $n$ give different absolute values. Equality in (AQ.1) therefore gives equality, for every $a$ and $n$, of the sums of eigenvalues of absolute value $p_a^{-n}$. Multiplying this sum by $p_a^n$ gives $$t_n(a)=\sum_\chi m_{\chi,n}\chi(a).$$ Finite character orthogonality yields the reconstruction $$m_{\chi,n}=\frac1{|G_Q|}\sum_{a\in G_Q}
             t_n(a)\chi(a)^{-1}.                                \tag{AQ.2}$$ Indeed, translating $\sum_a\theta(a)$ by an element $b$ with $\theta(b)\ne1$ makes that sum zero for every nontrivial character $\theta$; it is $|G_Q|$ for the trivial character. Thus (AQ.2) gives the same multiplicities for $M'$. For rational coefficients, the semisimple commutative algebra $\mathbb Q[G_Q]$ is a product of cyclotomic fields. The multiplicity of each simple rational module is recovered from the common multiplicity of its characters over a splitting field. The rational representations, weight by weight, are therefore isomorphic as well. ◻

</div>

This is a finite stopping criterion for recognition within the abelian Artin–Tate part of the programme. It needs every residue class, or equivalent full character information: values on an arbitrary generating set do not determine a general character. It does not reconstruct extension classes, integral lattices, period comparisons, or nonabelian fundamental groups.

## AQ-14-FORWARD: the cohomological implication at cote 14, scan 38

#### Archive question.

The sheet labels *Théorème 3.1*, marks its proof *dém. pas faite*, and asks whether $u_Z=0$ implies, modulo torsion, the vanishing of $$a({}^tZ):H^{2(d-i)+1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(d-i))
                     \longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathbb Q_\ell).
                                                               \tag{AQ.3}$$ Here $d=\dim X$ and $A$ is an abelian parameter variety. Its margin asks *A-t-on une réciproque*. The lower display $H^{(n-i)+1}$ is inconsistent with the degree in the preceding paragraph; (AQ.3) uses the latter.

#### Precise modern formulation.

For a smooth projective geometrically connected $X$ over an algebraically closed field $k$, let $N^i(X)\subset CH^i(X)_{\rm alg}$ be generated by $$\Gamma_*z,\quad
 z\in\ker\bigl(CH_0(T)^0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alb}_T(k)\bigr),
 \quad\Gamma\in CH^i(T\times X),                                \tag{AQ.4}$$ where $T$ is smooth projective geometrically connected. Set $P^i(X)=CH^i(X)_{\rm alg}/N^i(X)$, the generated Albanese quotient. For $Z\in CH^i(A\times X)$ the map on degree-zero cycles descends to $u_Z:A(k)\to P^i(X)$, since two lifts of the same point of $\operatorname{Alb}_A=A$ differ by an Albanese-kernel class. If $u_Z=0$ integrally, then (AQ.3) is zero for every $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$. The sole initially supplied sheet 14/038 does not define $A^i_{\rm alb}(X)$, but the official full cote now resolves the definition. In its physical PDF page 24 (footer and manuscript page 23), Definition 1.1 declares a cycle Albanese-equivalent to zero when it has the form $Z(v)$ for a smooth connected projective parameter $T$, a degree-zero zero-cycle $v$ with $S_T(v)=0$, and a correspondence $Z$ for which the intersection is defined. The word *projectif* is bracketed in the sheet, between *un schéma* and *lisse connexe*. We first retain that projective-parameter formulation. AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE below proves equality with the quasiprojective-parameter relation in characteristic zero, with the nonproper Albanese and zero-cycle conventions explicit. Here $S_T$ is the Albanese summation map. Proposition 1.2(i), on the same page, says these cycles form a subgroup containing rational equivalences and defines the quotient of the Chow group by its image. Proposition 1.2(v), physical PDF page 25 (footer 24), says Albanese equivalence implies algebraic equivalence. Finally Corollary 2.3, physical PDF page 34 (footer 33), defines $P^i(X)$ itself as the algebraically trivial subgroup of $A^i_{\rm alb}(X)$. Thus this is the original quotient in the projective formulation, not a quotient defined by regular maps to abelian varieties. The Chow moving lemma makes the finitely many intersections in (AQ.4) defined; since rational equivalences are already killed, the cycle-level and Chow formulations agree. Theorem 2.1, physical PDF page 31 (footer 30), defines $u_Z$ through the Albanese summation map. Its image lies in $P^i(X)$, so vanishing in this subgroup and in the original total quotient are equivalent. The supplied `scan038` uses physical PDF numbering: it is footer/manuscript page 37, after the cover. Definition 1.1 itself says only that $k$ is a field. The preceding handwritten sheets inspected in the full cote concern other motive conventions and preparatory cycle calculations; no explicit algebraic-closure convention was found there. Accordingly the algebraically closed hypothesis in our theorem is retained as an explicit mathematical scope condition, rather than attributed to an unseen original convention.

#### Primary inputs.

Bloch (1979), *Torsion algebraic cycles and a theorem of Roitman*, Propositions 3.5 (correspondences), 3.8 (specialization), and Theorem 4.2 (prime-to-characteristic Roitman), provide the inputs. They are also explicitly recorded in Achter, Casalaina-Martin, and Vial, *On the image of the second $\ell$-adic Bloch map*, arXiv:2007.07180v2, Propositions A.23, A.24, A.26, and A.29. These appendix statements apply in every codimension.

#### Torsion lemma.

For Bloch’s map $$\lambda_X^i:CH^i(X)[\ell^\infty]\longrightarrow
                    H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(i)),$$ one has $$N^i(X)\cap CH^i(X)[\ell^\infty]\subseteq\ker\lambda_X^i.          \tag{AQ.5}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Choose $x$ in the intersection, a finite expression $x=\sum_j\Gamma_{j*}z_j$ of the form (AQ.4), and $r$ with $\ell^r x=0$. Spread out this entire expression, its rational-equivalence certificates, its varieties and correspondences, their Albanese maps, and the equalities $\operatorname{alb}(z_j)=0$, over an integral finitely generated ring over the prime ring. Shrink so that the varieties are smooth projective, the chosen generic Albanese varieties extend to abelian schemes $B_j$, and the chosen maps $T_j\to B_j$ extend. It is unnecessary to assert that these are universal relative Albanese maps. Here the base can be chosen regular: the finitely generated field of definition has a separating transcendence basis over its prime field, and a model becomes smooth over $\mathbb F_p$, or over $\mathbb Z[1/M]$ in characteristic zero, after a further shrinking. All the finitely many conditions just listed survive that shrinking. In characteristic zero invert $\ell$ as well. Choose a closed point with finite residue field of characteristic $p\ne\ell$. A regular system of parameters in its local ring gives a flag of prime ideals with regular successive quotients. Localizing at consecutive primes gives discrete valuation rings. Specialization along this flag, after choosing algebraic closures of the residue fields, gives geometric special fibres over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, denoted by subscripts $0$.

Every degree-zero zero-cycle on a smooth projective connected variety over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ is torsion: join its finite support by finitely many projective curves and lift the differences of points to their normalizations. Every degree-zero divisor class on those curves is a point of a Jacobian defined over a finite extension of a finite field, and has finite order.

The specialized map $T_{j,0}\to B_{j,0}$ factors through $h_j:\operatorname{Alb}(T_{j,0})\to B_{j,0}$. This map is an isomorphism on $\ell$-power torsion. Indeed Kummer theory gives $H^1(T,\mathbb Z_\ell(1))=T_\ell\operatorname{Pic}(T)
=T_\ell\operatorname{Pic}^0(T)_{\rm red}$: the Néron–Severi group is finitely generated and has zero Tate module. Duality of $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\rm red}$ and Albanese therefore gives $H^1(T,\mathbb Z_\ell)=\operatorname{Hom}(T_\ell
\operatorname{Alb}_T,\mathbb Z_\ell)$, compatibly with maps. This is also the codimension-one calculation in Achter–Casalaina-Martin–Vial, Propositions A.25 and A.28. The generic Albanese map thus induces an isomorphism on $H^1(-,\mathbb Z_\ell)$, and smooth proper base change preserves this isomorphism. The latter is obtained by taking inverse limits of the finite-coefficient base-change isomorphisms for each variety; no assertion that the Albanese map is an isomorphism on every finite-coefficient $H^1$ is needed. Equivalently $T_\ell h_j$ is an isomorphism. It follows that $h_j$ is an isogeny with no $\ell$-primary kernel, and hence an isomorphism on $\ell$-power torsion. Since the specialization of the equality $\operatorname{alb}(z_j)=0$ says that $z_{j,0}$ maps to zero in $B_{j,0}$, the $\ell$-primary part of its actual Albanese class is zero. Prime-to-$p$ Roitman implies that the $\ell$-primary part of $z_{j,0}$ itself is zero. Thus every $z_{j,0}$, and hence $x_0$, has order prime to $\ell$. The equality $\ell^r x_0=0$ also specializes, so $x_0=0$.

Bloch’s compatibility with specialization sends $\lambda_X^i(x)$ to $\lambda_{X_0}^i(x_0)=0$. The cohomological specialization map is an isomorphism by smooth proper base change, first for finite coefficients and then for their direct limit $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(i)$. Consequently $\lambda_X^i(x)=0$. Only finitely many data are spread for each $x$; no simultaneous model for all of $N^i(X)$ has been assumed. ◻

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof of AQ-14-FORWARD.* Put $g=\dim A$. Roitman identifies $CH_0(A)[\ell^\infty]$ with $A[\ell^\infty](k)$. Use the torsion zero-cycle lift of a torsion point under this isomorphism; it is unnecessary to identify it with the literal class $[a]-[0]$. For every such torsion cycle $z$, the premise gives $Z_*z\in N^i(X)\cap CH^i(X)[\ell^\infty]$. Thus (AQ.5) gives $\lambda_X^i(Z_*z)=0$. Correspondence compatibility, followed by taking Tate modules and tensoring with $\mathbb Q_\ell$, makes $$Z_*:H^{2g-1}(A,\mathbb Q_\ell(g))
             \longrightarrow H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$$ zero: the degree-zero Bloch map identifies the source with $V_\ell A$. Under the perfect Poincaré pairings its transpose is (AQ.3), so (AQ.3) is zero. ◻

</div>

This proves the stated generated-Albanese-quotient formulation in every characteristic, independently of RH.

#### AQ-14-RATIONAL-PREMISE.

The integral premise cannot be replaced by $u_Z\otimes\mathbb Q=0$. Take an elliptic curve $E/\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ and $X=A=E$, $Z=\Delta_E-E\times\{0\}$. Then $P^1(E)=\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$ and $u_Z(a)=[a]-[0]$. The group $E(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)$ is torsion, so the rationalized premise holds. The normalized diagonal acts as the identity on the nonzero two-dimensional $H^1(E,\mathbb Q_\ell)$, refuting the weakened assertion. The phrase *modulo torsion* in the original sheet qualifies its cohomological conclusion. For the asserted $P^1$ identification, a divisor correspondence on $T\times E$ defines a morphism $T\to\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$ after choosing a base point, and hence factors through $\operatorname{Alb}_T$. Every generator of $N^1(E)$ is therefore zero.

#### AQ-14-FIELD-SCOPE.

The algebraically closed base is also substantive. If the premise is tested only on $k$-rational points over an arbitrary field, it can fail to detect the cohomological map. Take $k=\mathbb F_5$ and $E:y^2=x^3+1$. The affine fibres over $x=0,1,2,3,4$ have respectively $2,0,2,0,1$ points, so $\#E(\mathbb F_5)=6$, including infinity. For $Z=6(\Delta_E-E\times\{0\})$, the map $u_Z$ on $E(\mathbb F_5)$ is zero by Lagrange’s theorem, while the induced rational $\ell$-adic map is $6\operatorname{id}\ne0$ for every $\ell\ne5$. The original context’s use of divisibility of $A_Y(k)$ in physical PDF page 34 (footer 33) is consistent with the algebraically closed convention used here. This counterexample prevents an unintended arbitrary-field reading.

## AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE: removing the parameter qualification in characteristic zero

Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X/k$ be smooth projective. For a smooth connected parameter variety $T$, write $Z_0(T)^0$ for the group of finite formal sums $v=\sum_jn_j[t_j]$ of $k$-points with $\sum_jn_j=0$. In this paragraph the Albanese of a nonproper $T$ means its *abelian Albanese*: the universal pointed morphism $a_T:T\to\operatorname{Alb}_{\mathrm{ab}}(T)$ to an abelian variety. Put $a_T(v)=\sum_jn_ja_T(t_j)$; this does not depend on the base point used for the pointed morphism.

For $\Gamma\in\mathrm{CH}^i(X\times T)$ define $$\Gamma(v)=\sum_j n_j\,\iota_{t_j}^{!}\Gamma
                         \in\mathrm{CH}^i(X),$$ where $\iota_t:X\times\{t\}\hookrightarrow X\times T$ is the regular immersion of the fibre. Thus this is a sum of fibre restrictions; no nonproper pushforward $X\times T\to X$ is being used. Let $N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$ be generated by all such $\Gamma(v)$ with $T$ smooth connected quasiprojective and $a_T(v)=0$. Let $N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}(X)$ use smooth connected projective $T$ instead.

If every permitted $T$ has a smooth projective compactification, then $$N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)=N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}(X).                 \tag{AQ.14.1}$$ In particular this equality holds in characteristic zero. It also holds if the convention for a nonproper parameter is the universal semiabelian Albanese, with the kernel imposed on $Z_0(T)^0$. This last convention must be formulated on zero-cycles, not automatically on the ordinary Chow group of the nonproper variety.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The inclusion from right to left is immediate, since a smooth projective parameter is quasiprojective. For the other inclusion take a generator $\Gamma(v)$ as above. Choose a smooth projective compactification $j:T\hookrightarrow\overline T$. Represent $\Gamma$ by a finite integral linear combination of integral codimension-$i$ subvarieties of $X\times T$, and take their closures in $X\times\overline T$. This gives a class $\overline\Gamma\in\mathrm{CH}^i(X\times\overline T)$ whose restriction is $\Gamma$.

The morphism $a_{\overline T}\circ j:T\to
\operatorname{Alb}(\overline T)$, normalized to send the chosen base point to zero, factors by universality through $a_T$. Therefore $a_{\overline T}(j_*v)=0$. Here $j_*v$ denotes the same finite formal zero-cycle regarded on $\overline T$; it does not assert the existence of a pushforward $j_*:\mathrm{CH}_0(T)\to\mathrm{CH}_0(\overline T)$. For each point in the support of $v$, refined fibre restriction commutes with restriction to the open subset $X\times T$; equivalently, both restrictions are computed in the same neighbourhood of $X\times\{t_j\}$. Hence $$\overline\Gamma(j_*v)=\Gamma(v).$$ The left side is a generator allowed in $N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}(X)$, proving the reverse inclusion. The same proof works for the semiabelian convention, because the target $\operatorname{Alb}(\overline T)$ is also semiabelian, and because the semiabelian Albanese of a smooth projective parameter is its ordinary abelian Albanese.

In characteristic zero, embed the quasiprojective $T$ in a projective variety and resolve that projective closure by a projective resolution that is an isomorphism over its smooth locus, in particular over $T$. The resulting $\overline T$ is smooth projective. This is the strong compactification consequence of Hironaka’s resolution theorem, stated in precisely this form in Kahn and Sujatha, *Birational geometry and localisation of categories*, May 22, 2008 author version, Theorem 1.6.3(a), p. 11. ◻

</div>

Thus AQ-14-FORWARD applies in characteristic zero to the quotient generated using all smooth quasiprojective parameters, and AQ-14-CONVERSE over $\mathbb C$ remains a counterexample for that larger parameter class as well. The algebraically closed base-field qualification remains. In positive characteristic this comparison applies to parameters admitting a smooth projective compactification; no existence of such compactifications in all dimensions is asserted here. The next theorem proves the rational cohomological forward implication without needing that positive-characteristic equality of relations. AQ-14-ALTERATION then proves that the discrepancy between those relations is $p$-primary torsion.

## AQ-14-NONPROPER: the rational cohomological implication for all smooth quasiprojective parameters

Retain the algebraically closed base $k$ and smooth projective $X$. Let $N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$ be the relation defined in AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE, using the *abelian* Albanese of a smooth connected quasiprojective parameter and free degree-zero zero-cycles. Put $P^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)=\mathrm{CH}^i(X)_{\mathrm{alg}}/
N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$. For every prime $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$, one has $$N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)\cap\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]
       \subseteq\ker\lambda_X^i.                            \tag{AQ.14.2}$$ Consequently the implication of original 14/038 holds for this quasiprojective-parameter quotient in every characteristic: if $A$ is abelian, $Z\in\mathrm{CH}^i(A\times X)$, and $u_Z:A(k)\to P^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)$ is zero integrally, then $$a({}^tZ):H^{2(\dim X-i)+1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(\dim X-i))
                       \longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathbb Q_\ell)$$ is zero. The same implication holds for the smaller relation using the semiabelian Albanese kernel. No equality $N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}=N^i_{\mathrm{proj}}$ in arbitrary positive characteristic is asserted.

#### Inputs and coefficient conventions.

Write $H_j^S(T,R)$ for Suslin homology: it is the homology of the complex generated in degree $n$ by integral closed subvarieties of $T\times\Delta^n$ finite and surjective over $\Delta^n$, tensored with $R$. Its degree-zero cycles are finite formal sums of closed points. Write $G_T$ for the pointed Serre semiabelian Albanese and $$0\longrightarrow S_T\longrightarrow G_T\longrightarrow B_T
       \longrightarrow0$$ for its torus and abelian quotient. Universality shows that $B_T$ is the abelian Albanese of $T$: a homomorphism from a torus to an abelian variety is zero. The notation $[\ell^\infty]$ means the subgroup annihilated by some power of $\ell$.

Geisser, *Rojtman’s theorem for normal schemes*, arXiv:1402.1831v2, Theorem 1.1, p. 2, gives, for normal $T$, $$H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)[\ell^\infty]
       \xrightarrow{\sim}G_T(k)[\ell^\infty],\qquad
 H_1^S(T,\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell=0.
                                                               \tag{AQ.14.3}$$ The degree-zero convention on the left is automatic for torsion. This prime-to-characteristic theorem does not require a smooth compactification. The finite-coefficient perfect pairing used below is Geisser–Schmidt (2016), Theorem 1.1, journal p. 92; their Theorem 7.1, p. 118, identifies it with the Suslin–Voevodsky comparison. We invoke its prime-to-characteristic case, where tame and ordinary abelian $\ell$-primary covers agree. Kahn (2014), *Algebraic tori as Nisnevich sheaves with transfers*, Lemma 4.1(b), journal p. 707 (July 4, 2013 author version, Lemma 3(b), p. 8), states $\operatorname{Alb}_{\mathrm{sa}}(G)=G$ for a semiabelian $G$.

The integral generic-base-change input is Sun, arXiv:1511.01393v1, Definition 2.2, p. 3, Lemma 2.3(iii), pp. 4–5, and Theorem 2.5(i), p. 7, with $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$. In Lemma 2.3(iii)’s proof, one dense open works for *all* finite coefficient levels, by the finite set of Jordan–Hölder constituents. The constant $\mathbb Z_\ell$ system has the single simple constituent $\mathbb F_\ell$. Constructibility and preservation of stratifiability are part of the stated setup and Remark 2.5.1(ii). We use this only over a finite-type $\mathbb F_p$-base, $p\ne\ell$, which satisfies the finite cohomological-dimension condition (LO).

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Characteristic zero follows already from AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE and AQ-14-FORWARD. Thus assume $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$ and $\ell\ne p$. We first give the three facts needed for specialization.

*1. A correspondence with proper target descends to Suslin zero-homology.* For $\Gamma\in\mathrm{CH}^i(X\times T)$, the fibre-evaluation map $Z_0(T)\to\mathrm{CH}^i(X)$ kills every Suslin boundary. Indeed, a generator of a Suslin one-chain is an integral $W\subset\mathbb A^1\times T$ finite and surjective over $\mathbb A^1$. Its normalization $C$ is a smooth curve, finite over $W$ and over $\mathbb A^1$. Let $\overline C$ be its smooth projective completion. Pull $\Gamma$ back along the morphism $X\times C\to X\times T$; pullback is well defined between smooth varieties by the graph/refined Gysin construction. Extend a representing cycle by closure to a class on $X\times\overline C$.

The function $t/(t-1)$ on $\overline C$ has divisor equal to the fibre over zero minus the fibre over one, including multiplicities, and no contribution over infinity. The normalization projection formula identifies their images in $Z_0(T)$ with the two faces of $W$. Applying the extended correspondence to this principal divisor gives zero in $\mathrm{CH}^i(X)$. Its evaluations at points of $C$ agree with the original fibre evaluations, since refined pullback is local around those fibres. This proves the claim and produces a homomorphism $$\Gamma_*:H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH}^i(X).
                                                               \tag{AQ.14.4}$$ The construction is compatible with extension of the base field and with automorphisms of that field. No pushforward along the nonproper projection $X\times T\to X$ was used.

*2. The integral $H^1$ of the semiabelian Albanese.* The homology coefficient sequence and (AQ.14.3) give a natural isomorphism $$H_1^S(T,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)
             \simeq G_T(k)[\ell^\infty].                    \tag{AQ.14.5}$$ For clarity, the coefficient sequence has left term $H_1^S(T,\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ and right term $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z),
\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)=H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)[\ell^\infty]$; the left term is zero by (AQ.14.3).

The prime-to-$p$ finite pairings of Geisser–Schmidt are natural and compatible with the coefficient homomorphisms. Dualizing the direct system $H_1^S(T,\mathbb Z/\ell^n)$, whose transition is induced by $\mathbb Z/\ell^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z/\ell^{n+1}$, identifies its dual with the inverse system $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(T,\mathbb Z/\ell^n)$ under reduction. The pairings are viewed in $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ via $1\bmod\ell^n\mapsto\ell^{-n}\bmod\mathbb Z_\ell$; this makes the transition compatibility explicit. Thus $$H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(T,\mathbb Z_\ell)
  \simeq\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(
       G_T(k)[\ell^\infty],\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell\bigr)
  \simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}
       (T_\ell G_T,\mathbb Z_\ell).                         \tag{AQ.14.6}$$ The left side is the inverse limit of the finite groups; there is no $\varprojlim^1 H^0$ obstruction, because the constant $H^0$ transition maps are surjective. All isomorphisms are functorial. Since $G_T$ is its own Serre Albanese, the Albanese morphism $a_T:T\to G_T$ induces an isomorphism on $H^1(-,\mathbb Z_\ell)$. This conclusion is deliberately integral-adic, not an assertion about $H^1(-,\mathbb Z/\ell^n)$ for each $n$.

*3. The finite-field vanishing.* Suppose for the moment that $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. Every class in $H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)^0$ is torsion. To see this, join the finitely many points in a representative by finitely many integral curves in the connected smooth quasiprojective variety $T$, and pass to their normalizations. Such curves can be chosen through any prescribed finite set by hyperplane sections of sufficiently high degree; only the resulting curves and their normalizations are used. Differences of points lift to degree-zero classes on these smooth open curves. For a smooth curve $C$, $H_0^S(C,\mathbb Z)^0$ is the group of points of its Rosenlicht generalized Jacobian, equivalently the curve case of Geisser, Theorem 1.3, p. 3. Each such Jacobian and each of its points are defined over some finite extension of a finite field, and its rational points over that finite field form a finite group. Each lifted class, and hence each original degree-zero class, is torsion.

Let $v\in H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)^0$ have zero image in $B_T(k)$, and let $v_\ell$ denote its $\ell$-primary component. Under (AQ.14.3), the class $v_\ell$ corresponds to an element of $S_T(k)[\ell^\infty]$. Consider the homomorphism $$S_T(k)[\ell^\infty]\longrightarrow
 H_0^S(T,\mathbb Z)[\ell^\infty]
 \xrightarrow{\Gamma_*}\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]
 \xrightarrow{\lambda_X^i}
 H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(i)).                \tag{AQ.14.7}$$ All varieties, maps and $\Gamma$ descend to a common finite field $\mathbb F_q$, after enlarging it if needed. Thus (AQ.14.7) is Frobenius-equivariant. On $V_\ell S_T=T_\ell S_T\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$ geometric Frobenius has eigenvalues $q^{-1}$ times roots of unity: a torus splits over a finite extension of $\mathbb F_q$. On $H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$ its eigenvalues have complex absolute value $q^{-1/2}$, by Deligne (1974), Theorem (1.6), printed p. 276, after the indicated Tate twist. These spectra are disjoint. A linear map commuting with Frobenius between these two spaces is zero: apply relatively prime characteristic polynomials and Bézout’s identity, or use generalized eigenspaces after extending scalars. Semisimplicity is not required for this step.

The rational Tate module of the last group in (AQ.14.7) is exactly $H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$. More explicitly, that group has maximal divisible subgroup $H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Z_\ell(i))_{\mathrm{free}}
 \otimes\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ and finite quotient contained in $H^{2i}(X,\mathbb Z_\ell(i))[\ell^\infty]$. The domain of (AQ.14.7) is divisible. Its image is therefore in the maximal divisible subgroup, and a homomorphism between these finite-corank divisible $\ell$-groups is zero if its map of rational Tate modules is zero. Consequently (AQ.14.7) is zero, and $\lambda_X^i(\Gamma_*v_\ell)=0$.

It follows that over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ the conclusion (AQ.14.2) holds: in a finite sum of generators each $H_0^S$ class is torsion; its prime-to-$\ell$ component maps to prime-to-$\ell$ torsion, whereas the $\ell$-primary component has zero Bloch image by the preceding argument. Taking the $\ell$-primary component of an expression for $x\in N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)\cap\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]$ gives the claimed vanishing.

*4. Spreading to the finite-field situation.* Now take arbitrary algebraically closed $k$ of characteristic $p>0$ and a fixed $x\in N^i_{\mathrm{qp}}(X)\cap\mathrm{CH}^i(X)[\ell^\infty]$. Choose a finite expression $x=\sum_j\Gamma_{j*}v_j$, with $v_j\in Z_0(T_j)^0$ mapping to zero in $B_{T_j}(k)$, and choose $r$ with $\ell^r x=0$. Spread all varieties, finite zero-cycles, correspondences, rational-equivalence witnesses for these equalities, and the pointed maps $T_j\to G_{T_j}\to B_{T_j}$ over an integral finite-type $\mathbb F_p$-scheme $S$. After shrinking, $S$ is regular, $X/S$ is smooth projective, the $T_j/S$ are smooth with geometrically connected fibres, and the $G_j/S$ are semiabelian schemes in exact sequences $0\to S_j\to G_j\to B_j\to0$ with tori $S_j$ and abelian schemes $B_j$. The sections representing the zero-cycles remain inside $T_j$, and the relations $B_j(v_j)=0$ persist. The spread maps need not be relative universal Albanese morphisms.

Apply Sun’s integral generic base change simultaneously to the finitely many $T_j\to S$ and $G_j\to S$. Their $R^1(-)_*\mathbb Z_\ell$ are constructible adic sheaves, and become lisse on a further dense open. By (AQ.14.6) the map induced by $T_j\to G_j$ is an isomorphism at a geometric generic point. Indeed its base extension to $k$ is the chosen Albanese map, and prime-to-$p$ étale cohomology is unchanged by extension of algebraically closed fields. Its kernel and cokernel vanish on a further dense open, so this is an isomorphism of integral adic sheaves there. Generic base change now identifies its stalk at *every* geometric point of that open with the actual map on $H^1(-,\mathbb Z_\ell)$ of the fibre. This uses one open for the full integral system, not separate opens for infinitely many coefficient levels.

Choose a closed point $s$ in this open; its residue field is finite. Over $\overline{k(s)}$, the map $T_{j,s}\to G_{j,s}$ factors by universality as $$T_{j,s}\longrightarrow G_{T_{j,s}}
                  \xrightarrow{h_j}G_{j,s}.$$ By (AQ.14.6), $T_\ell h_j$ is an isomorphism. A homomorphism of semiabelian varieties whose Tate module is an isomorphism is an isogeny with no $\ell$-primary kernel: a positive-dimensional connected kernel or quotient has a nonzero Tate module, and a nonzero finite $\ell$-primary kernel makes the Tate-module image a proper sublattice. The induced homomorphism of abelian quotients $$b_j:B_{T_{j,s}}\longrightarrow B_{j,s}$$ is also an isogeny with no $\ell$-primary kernel. Indeed an isogeny of semiabelian varieties maps the torus onto the torus; the kernel of its map of abelian quotients is a quotient of its finite kernel. The first assertion follows since the resulting quotient of the target by the image torus is a quotient of the proper source abelian variety, and hence has zero toric part.

The equality $B_{j,s}(v_{j,s})=0$ and injectivity of $b_j$ on $\ell$-primary torsion imply that the $\ell$-primary component of the actual abelian Albanese class of $v_{j,s}$ is zero. Although the whole class need not vanish, this is precisely what step 3 needs: the $\ell$-primary Suslin class of $v_{j,s}$ corresponds to the actual torus $S_{T_{j,s}}$. Thus the argument in step 3 gives $\lambda_{X_s}^i(x_s)=0$.

Finally specialize from a geometric generic point to $s$ along a regular flag through $s$, as in the complete flag construction in AQ-14-FORWARD. The finite cycle and rational-equivalence data make $x_s$ the specialization of $x$, preserve $\ell^r x_s=0$, and identify each specialized fibre evaluation with $\Gamma_{j,s*}v_{j,s}$. Refined Gysin functoriality provides this compatibility locally at the sections. Bloch’s specialization theorem sends $\lambda_X^i(x)$ to $\lambda_{X_s}^i(x_s)$, and smooth proper base change for $X/S$ makes the cohomological specialization an isomorphism. Cohomological invariance under algebraically closed extension also identifies the original $k$-fibre with the geometric generic fibre for this calculation. Therefore $\lambda_X^i(x)=0$, proving (AQ.14.2).

For the final cohomological implication, lift each $\ell$-power torsion point of $A$ to its torsion zero-cycle class by prime-to-$p$ Roitman. The integral premise puts its image under $Z$ in the left side of (AQ.14.2). Bloch correspondence compatibility, followed by Tate modules and tensoring with $\mathbb Q_\ell$, therefore kills $Z_*:H^{2\dim A-1}(A,\mathbb Q_\ell(\dim A))
\to H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(i))$. Poincaré duality gives exactly the displayed transpose. This is the same final argument as AQ-14-FORWARD, now with the broader relation. For the semiabelian-kernel convention, its relation is contained in the abelian-kernel relation, so the torsion vanishing and the implication follow as well. ◻

</div>

## AQ-14-ALTERATION: the two parameter relations agree away from the characteristic

#### Statement.

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $p>0$, and let $X/k$ be smooth projective. Define $N^i_{\rm proj}(X)\subset N^i_{\rm qp}(X)\subset CH^i(X)$ using the projective and smooth quasiprojective parameters, respectively, and the ordinary abelian Albanese, exactly as in AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE. Then $$N^i_{\rm qp}(X)/N^i_{\rm proj}(X)
 \quad\hbox{is a $p$-primary torsion group.}               \tag{AQ.ALT.1}$$ In particular the relations agree after tensoring with $\mathbb Z[1/p]$, hence also with $\mathbb Q$. The natural map between the two cycle quotients has $p$-primary kernel and induces an isomorphism on $\ell$-primary torsion for every $\ell\ne p$. The same assertions hold if the nonproper relation uses zero cycles annihilated by the semiabelian Albanese. Together with AQ-14-QUASIPROJECTIVE this gives equality with rational coefficients in every characteristic. No bounded exponent for the possible $p$-primary kernel is asserted.

#### Exact external inputs.

The separability refinement is needed here: Illusie and Temkin (2014), Exposé X, Theorem 3.5(iii), printed p. 202, gives a *separable* projective prime-to-$\ell$ alteration with regular source over a perfect field. Their Theorem 2.1 alone is not the separability statement; the distinction is explicit on p. 167 and in §3.5.4, p. 207. The other input is the extension of a rational map from a smooth variety to an abelian variety to a morphism on the whole source: Milne, *Abelian Varieties*, version 2.0 (March 16, 2008), Chapter I, Theorem 3.2, printed p. 17. We use these as cited classical inputs. The moving and trace arguments needed for their application are proved below.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* *1. Moving zero cycles inside a dense open using Suslin relations.* For a smooth connected quasiprojective $T$ and a nonempty open $U\subset T$, every class in $H^S_0(T,\mathbb Z)$ is represented by a zero cycle supported in $U$. The replacement can be chosen with the same degree. We prove this directly on point generators. There is nothing to prove for a point already in $U$, or if $\dim T=0$. Fix $x\in T\setminus U$. An affine neighborhood of $x$ embeds into affine space. Successive general hyperplanes through $x$ cut its dimension down to one and cut $T\setminus U$ down to dimension zero in that neighborhood. At each step one avoids finitely many positive-dimensional components; this is possible because $k$ is infinite and the linear system has only the point $x$ as its base locus. An irreducible one-dimensional component through $x$ therefore meets $U$. Its closure in $T$ is an integral curve $C_0$ through $x$, still meeting $U$. Let $C\to C_0$ be its normalization and choose $x'\in C$ above $x$. The normalization is finite, and $C$ is a smooth curve because $k$ is perfect. Let $\overline C$ be its smooth projective completion, $D=\overline C\setminus C$, and $F=C\setminus(C\times_T U)$. Both $D$ and $F$ are finite sets. Choose $y\in C\times_T U$.

For sufficiently large $n$, Riemann–Roch gives a rational function $f$ on $\overline C$, with poles only at $y$, whose first-order jet at $x'$ is a uniformizer and whose value at every point of $D\cup(F\setminus\{x'\})$ is $1$. Explicitly the required evaluation map is surjective because the first cohomology of $$\mathcal O_{\overline C}
 \left(ny-2x'-\sum_{z\in D\cup(F\setminus\{x'\})}z\right)$$ vanishes once its degree exceeds $2g(\overline C)-2$. Thus $f$ has a simple zero at $x'$ and no zero or pole at the other points of $D\cup F$. Its divisor is of the form $$\operatorname{div}(f)=[x']+z-a[y],
 \qquad \operatorname{supp}(z)\subset C\times_T U,$$ where $z$ is effective and $a>0$. This divisor is a Suslin boundary on $C$: the nonconstant morphism $g=f/(f-1):\overline C\to\mathbb P^1$ is finite, and $g^{-1}(\mathbb A^1)\subset C$ since $f=1$ on $D$. Its graph gives a finite correspondence from $\mathbb A^1$ to $C$; its fibres at $0$ and $1$ differ by $\operatorname{div}(f)$, up to the harmless choice of boundary sign. Push this correspondence to $T$. It shows that $[x]$ has a same-degree representative supported in $U$. Linearity proves the moving assertion.

Both maps used in our relation respect these Suslin boundaries. For the correspondence action $\Gamma_*:Z_0(T)\to CH^i(X)$, this is the proper-target Suslin descent proved in Step 1 of AQ-14-NONPROPER: pull to a normalized curve, close on its projective completion, and evaluate the principal divisor there. For the ordinary Albanese map it follows directly as well: the morphism $C\to\operatorname{Alb}_{\rm ab}(T)$ extends to $\overline C$ by properness, and any principal divisor has zero sum in that abelian variety. Consequently, if $v$ has degree zero and Albanese sum zero, we may replace it by a zero cycle $v_U$ supported in $U$ with $$\deg v_U=0,\qquad\operatorname{alb}_T(v_U)=0,
 \qquad \Gamma_*(v_U)=\Gamma_*(v).                \tag{AQ.ALT.2}$$

*2. The prime-to-$\ell$ transfer.* Fix a prime $\ell\ne p$ and a generator $\Gamma_*(v)$ of $N^i_{\rm qp}(X)$. Choose a projective integral compactification $T\subset\overline T$. The cited Theorem 3.5(iii), applied over the perfect field $k$, gives a regular projective source and a separable projective prime-to-$\ell$ alteration of $\overline T$. Choose a connected component that dominates $\overline T$; write it as $$f:S\longrightarrow\overline T,\qquad
 d=[k(S):k(T)],\qquad (d,\ell)=1.$$ It is smooth over $k$, projective and integral. There exists a nonempty open $U\subset T$ over which $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ is finite étale of degree $d$. Use Step 1 to obtain $v_U$ satisfying (AQ.ALT.2).

Fix a point of $S$ to normalize $a_S:S\to A_S=\operatorname{Alb}(S)$. The fibre sum $$\nu:U\longrightarrow A_S,\qquad
 u\longmapsto\sum_{s\in f^{-1}(u)}a_S(s)$$ is a morphism: on an étale cover of $U$ the finite étale cover splits into $d$ sections, their sum is regular, and these sums descend because they are invariant under permutations of the sections. It is a rational map from smooth $T$ to an abelian variety, so the cited extension theorem gives a morphism $\overline\nu:T\to A_S$. After subtracting its value at a base point, universality of $\operatorname{Alb}_{\rm ab}(T)$ makes this morphism factor through a homomorphism $\operatorname{Alb}_{\rm ab}(T)\to A_S$. Write $v_U=\sum_u n_u[u]$. Since it has degree and Albanese sum zero, $$w=f_U^*v_U\in Z_0(S)^0,
 \qquad \operatorname{alb}_S(w)
        =\sum_u n_u\nu(u)=0.                       \tag{AQ.ALT.3}$$

Restrict a cycle representing $\Gamma$ to $X\times U$, pull it back along the finite étale map $X\times f^{-1}(U)\to X\times U$, and take its cycle-theoretic closure on $X\times S$. Call the resulting codimension-$i$ cycle $\Gamma'$. Fibre Gysin evaluation commutes with this étale pullback and with restriction to the open set. Each of the $d$ points above $u\in U(k)$ therefore has the same fibre class $\Gamma_u\in CH^i(X)$. It follows that $$\Gamma'_*(w)=d\,\Gamma_*(v_U)
             =d\,\Gamma_*(v)
             \in N^i_{\rm proj}(X),               \tag{AQ.ALT.4}$$ where membership uses the projective parameter $S$ and (AQ.ALT.3).

*3. The quotient and its torsion.* Every element $c$ of $N^i_{\rm qp}/N^i_{\rm proj}$ is a finite sum of such generators. For each $\ell\ne p$, take the product of their degrees in (AQ.ALT.4); it is prime to $\ell$ and annihilates $c$. Taking any one $\ell\ne p$ first shows that $c$ is torsion, of some finite order $n$. For every $\ell\ne p$ a prime-to-$\ell$ annihilator forces $\ell\nmid n$. Thus $n$ is a power of $p$, proving (AQ.ALT.1).

Put $Q_{\rm proj}=CH^i(X)/N^i_{\rm proj}$ and $Q_{\rm qp}=CH^i(X)/N^i_{\rm qp}$, and let $K=N^i_{\rm qp}/N^i_{\rm proj}$. The exact sequence $0\to K\to Q_{\rm proj}\to Q_{\rm qp}\to0$ has $p$-primary torsion kernel. Multiplication by $\ell$ is an automorphism of $K$ when $\ell\ne p$: this holds on every finite cyclic subgroup and hence on their union. Therefore the induced map on $\ell$-primary torsion is injective. It is surjective as well: if $y\in Q_{\rm qp}$ is killed by $\ell^a$, lift it to $x\in Q_{\rm proj}$; then $\ell^a x\in K$, and choose $z\in K$ with $\ell^a z=\ell^a x$. The lift $x-z$ is killed by $\ell^a$. This proves the assertion about torsion. Localization is flat and kills $K$, proving the claimed equalities after inverting $p$.

Finally, a zero cycle in the semiabelian Albanese kernel is in the abelian Albanese kernel. Its relation is therefore between $N^i_{\rm proj}$ and the relation just treated. The same $p$-primary conclusion follows for that intermediate subgroup and quotient. ◻

</div>

#### Consequence for the original cohomological question.

This also gives a second route to the forward implication of 14/038. If an $\ell$-primary torsion cycle $x\in CH^i(X)$ belongs to $N^i_{\rm qp}$, (AQ.ALT.4), applied to a finite expression for $x$, supplies $d$ prime to $\ell$ with $dx\in N^i_{\rm proj}$. AQ-14-FORWARD gives $d\lambda_X^i(x)=0$. Since $\lambda_X^i(x)$ is $\ell$-primary and multiplication by $d$ is invertible on that group, $\lambda_X^i(x)=0$. The original integral-premise and rational-transpose argument then applies. This deduction uses the projective torsion lemma and the alteration theorem; the earlier direct Suslin-weight proof is retained as a separately checked argument.

## AQ-14-CONVERSE: the generated-quotient converse fails

For the generated quotient just defined, over $\mathbb C$ the converse fails, even rationally in its target. The following is an application of Walker (2007), Theorem 5.7(4), p. 930, and Theorems 6.1 and 6.2, pp. 933–934. Take a smooth split six-dimensional quadric $W$, a sufficiently general high-degree smooth hypersurface section $Y\subset W$, and the two-cycle $\gamma$ supplied by Theorem 6.1 with $r=2$: its singular homology class is zero, whereas $s\gamma\ne0$ in $L_1H_4(Y,\mathbb Q)$. The two families of maximal $\mathbb P^3$’s on $W$ give independent middle-dimensional algebraic homology classes, supplying the rank-at-least-two hypothesis of Theorem 6.1. Here $\dim Y=5$. For any complex elliptic curve $E$ put $X=Y\times E$, so $\dim X=6$, and $$Z=\gamma\times(\Delta_E-E\times\{0\})\in CH^4(E\times X).$$ Then (AQ.3) is zero but $u_Z$ is nonzero in $P^4(X)$, even after tensoring with $\mathbb Q$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The cycle class of $Z$ is zero because that of $\gamma$ is zero, so its cohomological correspondence vanishes. The needed factorization through the generated Albanese quotient follows explicitly from Walker’s Theorem 5.7(2),(4), p. 930, and Example 5.5, p. 928. If $d=\dim X$, $r=d-i$, and $\Gamma\in CH^i(T\times X)$, those statements give $$\Phi^{\rm mor}_{X,r}(\Gamma_*z)
    =\Gamma_{J,*}\Phi^{\rm mor}_{T,0}(z)
    =\Gamma_{J,*}\operatorname{alb}_T(z).$$ Theorem 5.7(4) applies to every Chow correspondence, not just an equidimensional representative. The displayed expression vanishes for each generator (AQ.4). Thus $\Phi^{\rm mor}_{X,r}$ descends to $P^i(X)$. The product construction in the proof of Walker’s Theorem 6.2 gives a nonzero cylinder $$\gamma\otimes H_1(E,\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow L_2H_5(X,\mathbb Q).$$ Pairing its image with a dual class on $E$ and pushing to $Y$ gives a nonzero multiple of $s\gamma$, verifying nonvanishing. This is a morphism of the (ind-)Hodge structures defining the morphic Jacobian. The source has type $(-1,0),(0,-1)$ after twisting. A nonzero rational morphism from this source has nonzero tangent map on the associated Jacobians: if the relevant complex Hodge summand were killed, its conjugate would also be killed, so the whole morphism would vanish. More explicitly, strictness of the Hodge filtration gives a nonzero complex linear map after quotient by $F^0$; if it were zero, the whole real image would lie in $F^0\cap\overline{F^0}$, which is zero in weight $-1$. #### AQ-ABELIAN-RECOGNITION.

Fix $k=\mathbb F_q$, $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$, and let $\mathcal A_q$ be the rational numerical category obtained from the motives of abelian varieties over $k$, finite étale $k$-schemes, and Tate motives by finite sums, tensor products, duals, and direct summands. Equivalently, its objects are direct summands of finite sums $$h^i(A)(n)\otimes h^0(S),\qquad
 A/k\text{ abelian},\quad S/k\text{ finite \'etale},
 \quad n\in\mathbb Z.$$ We use geometric Frobenius $F$ and $\mathbb Q_\ell(1)$ with eigenvalue $q^{-1}$. The summand above has weight $w=i-2n$ and parity $i\bmod2=w\bmod2$. Let $R_\ell(M)$ be its ordinary direct-sum realization, with the weight decomposition remembered. Then $$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_\ell
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q_\ell[F]}
       (R_\ell(M),R_\ell(N)).                         \tag{AQ.15}$$ Moreover, for $M,N\in\mathcal A_q$, $$M\simeq N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P_M(t)=P_N(t),\qquad
 P_M(t)=\det(1-tF\mid R_\ell(M)).                    \tag{AQ.16}$$ The same equivalence holds with the weight-signed Euler factor $$Z_M(t)=\prod_w
 \det(1-tF\mid R_\ell(M)_w)^{(-1)^{w+1}}            \tag{AQ.17}$$ in place of $P_M$. These statements are in the rational numerical category, and concern actual objects with their full multiplicities.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* First we explain the scalar extension in (AQ.15). For an abelian variety $X/k$, numerical and $\ell$-adic homological equivalence agree by the cited all-abelian theorem. Let $A^r_{\rm num}(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ denote the space of rational cycles modulo numerical equivalence. The numerical intersection pairing with $A^{\dim X-r}_{\rm num}(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is nondegenerate. For any finite linearly independent collection $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s$ of numerical classes, one can choose complementary classes $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_s$ making $(\deg(\alpha_i\beta_j))_{i,j}$ invertible over $\mathbb Q$. Their cohomology classes are then linearly independent over $\mathbb Q_\ell$, by pairing against the $\beta_j$. This also bounds $s$ by the finite Betti number, proving finiteness and injectivity after extension to $\mathbb Q_\ell$. The full Tate theorem supplies surjectivity onto the Frobenius-invariant subspace. Hence $$A^r_{\rm num}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell
 \simeq H^{2r}(X_{\bar k},\mathbb Q_\ell(r))^{F=1}.
                                                               \tag{AQ.18}$$

The cohomological projectors of an abelian variety are algebraic here without a further conjecture. For any integer $a\geq2$, multiplication by $a$ acts on $H^j(A)$ as $a^j$. The polynomials $$e_i=\prod_{\substack{0\leq j\leq2\dim A\\j\ne i}}
       \frac{[a]^*-a^j}{a^i-a^j}$$ give its projectors in numerical correspondences: their required identities hold in cohomology, hence numerically by the established equivalence on $A\times A$. Tensor products are realized using $A\times B$, and Poincaré duality expresses duals as complementary cohomological degrees with Tate twists. This proves the stated description of $\mathcal A_q$.

Consider first $M=h^i(A)(n)$ and $N=h^j(B)(m)$, and set $a=\dim A$. A correspondence between them has codimension $a+m-n$ on $A\times B$. Its relevant Künneth component, by Poincaré duality, is $$H^{2a-i}(A)\otimes H^j(B)(a+m-n)
 \simeq\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(H^i(A)(n),H^j(B)(m)\bigr).$$ It lies in the required cohomological degree precisely when $j-i=2(m-n)$, equivalently when the weights agree. In that case (AQ.18) for $A\times B$, followed by the algebraic projectors, gives exactly (AQ.15). For unequal weights every Frobenius-equivariant map is zero: the eigenvalues have complex absolute values $q^{(i-2n)/2}$ and $q^{(j-2m)/2}$, so their spectra are disjoint. The numerical correspondence group is also zero, since its cohomological component has the wrong degree and homological equals numerical equivalence.

For finite étale factors $S,T$, the schemes $A\times B\times S\times T$ are disjoint unions of abelian varieties viewed over finite extensions of $k$. A Frobenius-invariant class on a component orbit is determined by a class invariant under the appropriate power of Frobenius on one component. The all-abelian Tate theorem over that finite extension supplies the cycle. Intersection pairings are multiplied by the nonzero extension degree, which is invertible over $\mathbb Q$. Thus the same injection and surjection apply to these factors. Passing to finite sums and applying source and target idempotents proves (AQ.15) for every object of $\mathcal A_q$.

Frobenius acts semisimply on all these realizations. For $H^1(A)$ this follows from Tate (1966), Theorem 2(a), printed p. 140: $\mathbb Q[\pi_A]$ is the centre of the semisimple algebra $\operatorname{End}_k(A)\otimes\mathbb Q$. It is a product of number fields, so its Frobenius element has square-free minimal polynomial; dualizing or inverting preserves this property. The identity $H^i(A)=\bigwedge^i H^1(A)$ shows semisimplicity in every degree. Finite étale Frobenius has finite order, and tensor products, Tate twists and invariant direct summands of semisimple single-operator representations remain semisimple in characteristic zero.

Equality in (AQ.16) therefore gives an isomorphism $R_\ell(M)\simeq R_\ell(N)$ as $\mathbb Q_\ell[F]$-modules: each irreducible polynomial in a semisimple operator’s characteristic polynomial records the multiplicity of its simple $\mathbb Q_\ell[F]$-module. By (AQ.15) this is an invertible element of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(M,N)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$. Choose a rational basis $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ of this Hom space. The polynomial $$D(x_1,\ldots,x_s)=
 \det R_\ell\!\left(\sum_\nu x_\nu f_\nu\right)$$ over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is not zero. Since $\mathbb Q$ is infinite, some rational tuple has $D\ne0$; this elementary fact follows by induction on the number of variables, using the finite number of roots of a nonzero polynomial in one variable. It gives a rational morphism $f:M\to N$ with invertible realization. By full faithfulness its realization’s inverse comes from a morphism in $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_q}(N,M)\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$. The equations $gf=1_M$ and $fg=1_N$ form a finite linear system over $\mathbb Q$ in the coefficients of $g$. A rational linear system solvable after extension to $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is solvable over $\mathbb Q$, by row reduction. Thus $f$ has a rational inverse. The converse in (AQ.16) is immediate.

Finally a reciprocal root contributed by weight $w$ has complex absolute value $q^{-w/2}$. Weights of different parity cannot contribute the same root. Hence the numerator and denominator of (AQ.17) are coprime, each with constant term one. Equality of those rational functions recovers their numerator and denominator separately, and their product is $P_M(t)$. This reduces (AQ.17) to (AQ.16) and completes the proof. ◻

</div>

No essential-surjectivity claim onto all Frobenius representations is needed here. In particular, this recognition theorem does not identify an arbitrary smooth projective motive with an object of $\mathcal A_q$, does not retain integral lattices, and does not classify mixed extensions. It is a genuine strengthened answer to the abelian sector of cote 15, supplied by the new cycle-algebraicity theorem rather than by analytic zero location.

#### AQ-RATIONAL-FIBRE: an explicit neutralization obstruction.

Even this now-established abelian category need not admit a fibre functor with values in rational vector spaces. This is a concrete restriction on any reading of the archive’s motivic Galois group as a neutral group over $\mathbb Q$. It is not a refutation of a group defined after scalar extension or of a motivic gerbe formulation.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Consider $E:y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbb F_3$, then base-change it to $k=\mathbb F_9$. It is smooth: the derivative of $x^3-x$ is $-1$ in characteristic three. For each of $x=0,1,2$, the right side is zero, so there is one affine point; with infinity, $\#E(\mathbb F_3)=4$. Thus its $3$-Frobenius polynomial on $H^1$ is $T^2+3$. The square of Frobenius is $-3$ on $H^1$, and injectivity of the Tate-module action of endomorphisms implies that the $9$-Frobenius endomorphism is $[-3]$. Tate (1966), Theorem 2(d), p. 140, identifies $$D=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_9}(E)\otimes\mathbb Q
   =D_{3,\infty},$$ the quaternion division algebra ramified at $3$ and infinity. Alternatively, the main theorem on p. 134 gives $\dim_{\mathbb Q}D=4$, since Frobenius is scalar on a two-dimensional Tate module; every nonzero endomorphism of an elliptic curve is an isogeny, so this algebra is a division algebra.

Put $M=h^1(E)$ in $\mathcal A_9$. Pullback endomorphisms give an injective algebra map $D^{\rm op}\to\operatorname{End}(M)$. By (AQ.15), the target has rational dimension four, since its scalar extension is $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. This map is therefore an isomorphism, so $\operatorname{End}(M)$ is a quaternion division algebra.

Use the usual parity-corrected symmetry on numerical motives, for which the ordinary $\ell$-adic realization is a symmetric tensor functor. The categorical trace of $1_M$ is then $2$: its image is the ordinary trace of the identity on $H^1(E,\mathbb Q_\ell)$, and the scalar map $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q_\ell$ is injective. A rational fibre functor would preserve this trace, hence send $M$ to a two-dimensional rational vector space. Its faithfulness would embed the four-dimensional division algebra $\operatorname{End}(M)$ into $M_2(\mathbb Q)$. Equal dimensions force this embedding to be an isomorphism. That is impossible because $M_2(\mathbb Q)$ has nonzero noninvertible elements. This proves the asserted obstruction.

It also exhibits an object restriction invisible in a list of eigenvalues alone. Here $P_M(t)=(1+3t)^2$. There cannot be an object $L\in\mathcal A_9$ with $P_L(t)=1+3t$: otherwise (AQ.16) would give $L\oplus L\simeq M$, but the former has a nontrivial idempotent endomorphism and the latter’s division algebra has none. The eigenvalue $-3$ occurs, as AQ-WEIL-EXIST predicts, but its multiplicity cannot be discarded in a rational motivic classification. ◻

In particular, a nonzero CM period and $\pi$ are algebraically independent. The non-CM case remains the full period-conjecture gate. Question (c) asks whether the Hodge-splitting scalar can be algebraic without CM. In the normalization $y^2=4x^3-g_2x-g_3$, put $\omega=dx/y$, $\xi=x\,dx/y$, take full periods $\omega_1,\omega_2$ with $\operatorname{Im}
(\omega_2/\omega_1)>0$, and write the periods of $\xi$ as $-\eta_1,-\eta_2$. The opposite Hodge line is generated by $\xi+h\omega$. Its periods must be proportional to $\overline{\omega_1},
\overline{\omega_2}$, which gives $$h=\frac{\eta_1\overline{\omega_2}-\eta_2\overline{\omega_1}}
          {\omega_1\overline{\omega_2}-\omega_2\overline{\omega_1}}
   =\frac{\pi^2}{3\omega_1^2}E_2^*(\tau).$$ Here $\tau=\omega_2/\omega_1$ and $E_2^*(\tau)=E_2(\tau)-3/(\pi\operatorname{Im}\tau)$. The second equality uses $\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=2\pi i$ and $\eta_1\omega_1=\pi^2E_2(\tau)/3$. The CM conclusions in (a) and (c) can be separated from the remaining non-CM problem.

<div class="proof">

*The CM period field and algebraicity of the splitting.* Take a nonreal CM endomorphism $u$, defined over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. In the algebraic de Rham basis its action has the form $u^*\omega=u\omega$ and $u^*\xi=a\omega+\overline u\xi$, with $a\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$. The opposite Hodge line is the $\overline u$-eigenline, so $$a+hu=\overline u h,\qquad
 h=\frac{a}{\overline u-u}\in\overline{\mathbb Q}.$$ The period ratio $\tau$ is imaginary quadratic. The preceding formula for $h$ and the Legendre relation give $$\omega_2=\tau\omega_1,\qquad
 \eta_1=h\omega_1+\frac{\pi}{\omega_1\operatorname{Im}\tau},
 \qquad \eta_2=\tau\eta_1-\frac{2\pi i}{\omega_1}.$$ Thus the full period and quasi-period field is $\overline{\mathbb Q}(\omega_1,\pi)$; the reverse inclusion follows from the Legendre relation. Chudnovsky’s cited Corollary 2 makes its transcendence degree exactly two. ◻

</div>

The remaining non-CM assertion is the nonholomorphic weight-two Eisenstein-transcendence question attributed to Katz in Waldschmidt (2004), p. 277, immediately after Conjecture 3.22. That passage states the same remaining condition for a non-CM lattice with algebraic invariants. Its non-CM transcendence is not established by the supplied proofs. AQ-FINITE cannot supply that missing algebraic-independence assertion.

## AQ-COCYCLE: the image-density statement at 18/059

Let $P$ be an affine torsor under an algebraic group $G$ over $F\subset\mathbb C$, and $p\in P(\mathbb C)$. The closure of the image of its automorphism cocycle is the translated $F$-locus of $p$. Thus the image is Zariski dense in $G_{\mathbb C}$ exactly when $p$ is generic in $P$ over $F$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Embed $P$ in affine space over $F$, and let $I$ be the ideal over $\mathbb C$ of the $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/F)$-orbit of $p$. Each finite-dimensional degree-bounded part of $I$ is stable under the coefficientwise semilinear action. Its unique reduced-row-echelon basis, relative to an ordered monomial basis, must therefore have entries fixed by every automorphism. The fixed field of $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/F)$ is $F$: any nontrivial embedding of $F(a)$ over $F$, for $a\notin F$, extends first across a transcendence basis and then an algebraic closure to an automorphism of $\mathbb C$. Consequently every degree-bounded part has a basis over $F$, and $I=(I\cap F[\mathbf x])\mathbb C[\mathbf x]$. An $F$-polynomial vanishes on the orbit exactly when it vanishes at $p$. Thus $I$ is precisely the ideal of $Z_F(p)_{\mathbb C}$, proving the density assertion. The torsor difference map $D_p:P_{\mathbb C}\to G_{\mathbb C}$ is an algebraic isomorphism and the cocycle is $D_p(\sigma p)$, up to inversion according to convention. Its closure is consequently $D_p(Z_F(p)_{\mathbb C})$. ◻

</div>

The generated-subgroup version is false for general torsors. In $G=\mathbb G_m^2$ take $F=\mathbb Q$ and $p=(t,t+1)$ with $t$ transcendental. Its locus is a curve. The translated cocycle curve is $u\mapsto(u/t,(u+1)/(t+1))$. A character $x^a y^b$ identically one there would have orders zero at $u=0$ and $u=-1$, giving $a=b=0$. Thus this proper curve generates the whole torus. This refutes an abstract torsor inference, without claiming an arithmetic-motivic counterexample to the period conjecture.

## AQ-CM: the hexagonal elliptic curve and a genuine GRH consequence

The hexagonal real-elliptic classification recorded in SA22, §2(A.7), concerns the $j=0$ period lattice and its real forms. It is not the Grothendieck–Teichmüller hexagon. For example, the latter is the identity $$f(z,x)z^m f(y,z)y^m f(x,y)x^m=1,\qquad
 z=(xy)^{-1},\quad m=(\lambda-1)/2,$$ in a profinite free group, as in Boavida de Brito, Horel, and Robertson (2019), Definition 7.2(II). The two uses of *hexagonal* name different mathematical data.

#### Hodge obstruction.

For $E:y^2=x^3+1$, the motive $h^1(E)$ is not Artin–Tate, even after adjoining its CM field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* An Artin motive has Hodge type $(0,0)$, and $\mathbb Q(n)$ has type $(-n,-n)$ in our convention. Tensor products, sums, subobjects, and quotients of such Hodge structures have only diagonal types. But $H^1(E)$ has nonzero types $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Base extension to an algebraic number field does not change these complex Hodge types. This proves the claim. ◻

</div>

Thus the fact that the CM field is abelian does not turn its weight-one Hecke character into a finite-order Dirichlet character with an integral Tate twist. No conclusion about the zeros of $L(h^1(E),s)$ is obtained merely by substituting that field in the supplied Dirichlet theorem.

#### Effective distribution of trace-zero primes.

Let $a_p=p+1-\#E(\mathbb F_p)$, for $p>3$, and put $N_0(x)=\#\{3<p\leq x:a_p=0\}$. Then $$a_p=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ p\equiv2\pmod3,\qquad
 N_0(x)=\tfrac12\operatorname{Li}(x)+O(\sqrt x\log x)            \tag{AQ.7}$$ under the adopted Dirichlet GRH. The implied constant is effective.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* With the quadratic character extended by $\chi_p(0)=0$, $$a_p=-\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi_p(x^3+1).$$ If $p\equiv2\bmod3$, cubing permutes $\mathbb F_p$, so the sum is $\sum_u\chi_p(u+1)=0$. If $p\equiv1\bmod3$, set $m=(p-1)/2$. Euler’s criterion and the binomial theorem show $$a_p\equiv-\sum_{j=0}^m {m\choose j}
                 \sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}x^{3j}
       ={m\choose (p-1)/3}\pmod p.$$ The $j=0$ sum is $p=0$ modulo $p$. Among the positive exponents $3j\leq3(p-1)/2$, the only multiple of $p-1$ is $p-1$, and its power sum is $-1$ modulo $p$. The displayed binomial coefficient is nonzero modulo $p$ because all its factorial arguments are less than $p$. This proves the equivalence.

Now $\zeta_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})}(s)=
\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{-3})$, so the adopted theorem gives GRH for this quadratic Dedekind zeta function. Lagarias–Odlyzko (1977), Theorem 1.1, equation (1.3), applied to its nontrivial Frobenius class, gives $$\left|\pi(x;3,2)-\tfrac12\operatorname{Li}(x)\right|
 \leq c_1\left(\tfrac12\sqrt x\log(3x^2)+\log3\right),
 \qquad x>2.$$ Here $c_1$ is their effective absolute constant. For $x\geq3$, $N_0(x)=\pi(x;3,2)-1$ because $p=2$ is a bad prime of $E$. This proves (AQ.7). ◻

</div>

This gives an effective arithmetic statement about the hexagonal curve using the quadratic splitting character. It does not replace that curve’s weight-one Hecke $L$-function by a Dirichlet $L$-function.

## AQ-67-RANGE: the global isomorphism in 67/078 is false

The literal question is *Ces flèches sont elles des iso — et si oui, comment expliciter un iso en sens inverse ?* In the stable nodal $(0,4)$ case, normalize both three-pointed rational components with the node at $0$ and the other two marked points at $1,\infty$. In the tangent-tensor normalization of the capping construction, a tensor $q$ in the image has absolute value equal to the product of the two node-cap conformal radii. Consequently $$|q|\leq16,\qquad q=17\ \hbox{has no preimage}.                  \tag{AQ.8}$$ If the target is written with dual tangent tensors, the equivalent statement uses the inverse tensor. This normalization is fixed by the original sheets: 67/075 calls the propagation datum a nonzero basis of $T_{x'}\otimes T_{x''}$; 67/078 writes its inverse as a dual pairing and asks for mutually polar disks in the two tangent lines. In coordinates, polarity is $|q^{-1}|r'r''=1$, hence $r'r''=|q|$. The target in 67/076–077 imposes no bound on this nonzero tensor. We use the map without additional marked-point disks in 67/077.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* For completeness, the Koebe bound needed here follows from the area argument. If $F(z)=z+b_0+\sum_{n\geq1}b_nz^{-n}$ is univalent for $|z|>1$, Green’s formula gives the area inside $F(|z|=r)$ as $\pi(r^2-\sum_{n\geq1}n|b_n|^2r^{-2n})\geq0$. Letting $r\downarrow1$ proves $\sum_{n\geq1}n|b_n|^2\leq1$. For a univalent $f(z)=z+a_2z^2+\cdots$ in the unit disk, $g(z)=\sqrt{f(z^2)}=z+(a_2/2)z^3+\cdots$ is an odd univalent function there. The branch exists because $f(z^2)/z^2$ never vanishes in the disk. Equality of two $g$-values forces equality of the squared arguments under $f$, and oddness then proves injectivity. Its reciprocal $1/g(1/z)$ is univalent outside the disk, so the area inequality gives $|a_2|\leq2$. If $w\ne0$ is omitted by $f$, the normalized univalent function $f/(1-f/w)$ has second coefficient $a_2+1/w$. Thus $|1/w|\leq|a_2|+|a_2+1/w|\leq4$. This is the Koebe quarter theorem (Duren, 1983, Theorem 2.3, p. 31).

A node cap is the image of a univalent map $h$ from the unit disk with $h(0)=0$ and conformal radius $r=|h'(0)|$. It omits $1$ and $\infty$. Apply the preceding conclusion to $h/h'(0)$ and the omitted value $1/h'(0)$ to get $r\leq4$. Both caps therefore have radii at most four. The tensor is the product of the two derivative vectors, up to the unit-modulus boundary identification, so its norm is at most sixteen. The nonzero tensor $17$ belongs to the stated unrestricted target, establishing failure of surjectivity. ◻

</div>

The intake SA22 reports the sharper exact image $0<|q|<16$, tangent invertibility, and a $\pi_1$-isomorphism. Its full prior proofs are not in the supplied packet. The next result independently supplies complete proofs for the labelled geodesic-cut $(0,4)$ sector, while AQ-67-NONHOLO separates the real and complex tangent claims. ## AQ-67-EXACT: the canonical radius and the exact labelled $(0,4)$ image

#### Scope and normalization.

Consider the geodesic-cut interpretation of the map without marked-point disks in cote 67/077. Fix a partition of four labelled punctures into two pairs and remember the separating geodesic. Its length is $\ell>0$. Cutting produces two hyperbolic pairs of pants of boundary lengths $(\ell,0,0)$, where zero denotes a cusp. Cap the geodesic boundary by the unit disk using its normalized hyperbolic arclength, as prescribed in 67/074. On each resulting three-pointed sphere use the unique coordinate with cap centre $0$ and the two labelled cusps $1,\infty$. Let $r(\ell)>0$ be the conformal radius of the cap at zero in that coordinate. A cap coordinate $w$ has $|w|=1$ at the seam and $z=r(\ell)w+O(w^2)$ after a rotation of $w$. The tensor coordinate $q$ of 67/075 then has modulus $r(\ell)^2$.

Put $$x=\frac{\ell}{4\pi},\qquad
 \phi(x)=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\log\Gamma(1+ix)
                       -\log\Gamma(\tfrac12+ix)\bigr),$$ where the analytic logarithm of $\Gamma$ on the right half-plane is real on the positive real axis. Then $$\boxed{\quad r(\ell)=4\exp\!\left(\frac{\phi(x)-\pi/4}{x}\right).\quad}
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.1}$$ The function $r:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,4)$ is a real-analytic diffeomorphism. With the orientation of the normalized twist angle $\theta\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$ chosen so that its increase multiplies the tangent tensor by $e^{i\theta}$, the capping map is $$(\ell,\theta)\longmapsto r(\ell)^2e^{i\theta}.
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.2}$$ It is a real-analytic diffeomorphism onto $0<|q|<16$. Its real tangent map is invertible everywhere, and its composite with the inclusion $\{0<|q|<16\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb C^\times$ induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups. These are statements about this labelled geodesic-cut sector. They do not assert complex holomorphicity or algebraic *étaleness* of the map in 67/077–082, nor do they settle the full multi-node question.

#### Geometric inputs and source locators.

The hyperbolic pair of pants with specified nonnegative boundary lengths is unique, with zero lengths interpreted as cusps. Its Fenchel–Nielsen length and twist parametrization is recalled in Mondello (2011), §2.2, p. 640. The canonical attachment here is the infinite flat-cylinder grafting of Mondello, §5.4, Theorem 5.4, p. 660; a punctured flat cylinder is conformally the punctured disk with the arclength parametrization used above. For an explicit independent source for the projective-connection calculation below, see Fırat (2021), equations (3.11), (3.13), (3.14a), (3.23), and (3.30)–(3.34), arXiv:2102.03936v2, pp. 14–17 and 24–25. We specialize the two other ends to cusps and determine the branch directly below; we do not infer the branch from that paper’s integer-valued numerical prescription (3.27).

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Write $\lambda=\ell/(2\pi)$, so $x=\lambda/2$. The developing map $D$ of the hyperbolic metric is a local isometry to a half-plane. Near a geodesic of length $2\pi\lambda$, diagonalize its hyperbolic monodromy, with fixed points $0,\infty$. In the arclength cap coordinate its analytic continuation is, up to a nonzero constant, $w^{i\lambda}$. Indeed, increasing $\arg w$ by $2\pi$ multiplies this function by $e^{-2\pi\lambda}$, and along $|w|=1$ its hyperbolic arclength is $\lambda\,d\arg w$. The same expression supplies the analytic continuation across the seam. Its Schwarzian derivative therefore has a double pole at the cap centre with leading coefficient $(1+\lambda^2)/2$. At a cusp its leading coefficient is $1/2$, as follows from the local developing coordinate $\log(z-z_0)$. There are no other poles. On the sphere with centres and cusps $0,1,\infty$, the resulting Schwarzian is consequently $$T(z)=\frac{1+\lambda^2}{2z^2}
       +\frac1{2(z-1)^2}
       -\frac{1+\lambda^2}{2z(z-1)}.                           \tag{AQ.67.3}$$ To see uniqueness, any two such projective connections differ by a quadratic differential with at most simple poles at three points; this space is $H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-1))=0$.

Set $a=(1+i\lambda)/2$. Direct substitution in $y''+\tfrac12T(z)y=0$ gives the two solutions $$y_\pm(z)=z^{(1\pm i\lambda)/2}(1-z)^{1/2}
     {}_2F_1\!\left(\frac{1\pm i\lambda}{2},
                    \frac{1\pm i\lambda}{2};
                    1\pm i\lambda;z\right).$$ The ratio, normalized by its leading term at zero, is $$D(z)=\frac{y_+(z)}{y_-(z)}
     =z^{i\lambda}
       \frac{{}_2F_1(a,a;2a;z)}
            {{}_2F_1(\bar a,\bar a;2\bar a;z)}.                \tag{AQ.67.4}$$ All developing maps with the prescribed Schwarzian differ by a Möbius transformation. The ratio in (AQ.67.4) has the same diagonal monodromy at zero; hence it is a valid developing map after changing the target half-plane by that transformation. Its boundary still contains the two fixed points $0,\infty$; thus it is a straight half-plane bounded by a line through zero, rather than a disk with a different boundary circle.

We now fix the phase without an unspecified multiple of $\pi$. On the real interval $0<z<1$, $y_- =\overline{y_+}$. Their Wronskian, computed at zero, is $y_+'y_- -y_+y_-'=i\lambda$. In particular neither solution vanishes there, and the continuously lifted argument $\Theta(z)$ of $D(z)$, normalized by $\Theta(z)-\lambda\log z\longrightarrow0$ as $z\downarrow0$, satisfies $$\Theta'(z)=\frac{\lambda}{|y_+(z)|^2}>0.
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.5}$$ The zero-balanced hypergeometric asymptotic $${}_2F_1(a,a;2a;z)
   \sim\frac{\Gamma(2a)}{\Gamma(a)^2}[-\log(1-z)]
       \qquad (z\uparrow1)$$ is DLMF (15.4.21). Thus $$\lim_{z\uparrow1}\Theta(z)=\vartheta(\lambda),\qquad
 \vartheta(\lambda)=2\operatorname{Im}
       \left(\log\Gamma(1+i\lambda)
                    -2\log\Gamma(\tfrac12+i\lambda/2)\right).
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.6}$$ This is an equality of lifted arguments. For clarity, the asymptotic is locally uniform in $\lambda\geq0$. The hypergeometric factor on $0<z<1$ has no zeros when $\lambda>0$ by the Wronskian, and at $\lambda=0$ it is positive. Its continuous logarithm, zero at $z=0$, therefore has limiting phase equal to the indicated continuous gamma phase, rather than that phase plus a nonzero multiple of $2\pi$. Equivalently that possible integer is locally constant in $\lambda$ and is zero at $\lambda=0$.

The pair of pants has its reflection fixing the perpendicular seams. After capping, this antiholomorphic involution fixes $0,1,\infty$, so it is complex conjugation. The perpendicular seam from the cusp at $1$ to the geodesic boundary is the terminal segment of the real interval $(0,1)$. Its developing image lies on the unit circle, by (AQ.67.4). The cusp endpoint has phase $\vartheta$. By (AQ.67.5), approaching that cusp from the core uses phases less than $\vartheta$. Hence the developing half-plane of the core is the half-plane with argument interval $(\vartheta-\pi,\vartheta)$. The axis of the boundary monodromy, whose fixed points are $0,\infty$, is its ray of argument $\vartheta-\pi/2$. The seam meets that axis perpendicularly. Starting from the cusp, this is the first intersection of the seam with the axis; the further formal intersections obtained after crossing the boundary line of that half-plane are not in the hyperbolic core. This selects the lifted phase $\vartheta-\pi/2$, with no integer ambiguity.

Choose $w=1$ at this perpendicular foot. On a collar of the geodesic, and thence by analytic continuation into its cap, $$w=\exp\left(\frac{\log D-i(\vartheta-\pi/2)}{i\lambda}\right).$$ Here the logarithm has the lifted phase just specified. Its boundary modulus is one and its boundary argument has the normalized arclength, so this is precisely the prescribed cap coordinate. At zero, (AQ.67.4) gives $$w=\exp\left(-\frac{\vartheta-\pi/2}{\lambda}\right)z+O(z^2),
 \qquad
 r(\ell)=\exp\left(\frac{\vartheta-\pi/2}{\lambda}\right).
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.7}$$ The duplication formula, DLMF (5.5.5), yields $\vartheta=2\lambda\log2+2\phi(\lambda/2)$, proving (AQ.67.1).

It remains to establish the exact range and the nonvanishing derivative, rather than only an upper bound. The digamma integral DLMF (5.9.16) gives, for $x\geq0$, $$\phi'(x)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\psi(1+ix)
                            -\psi(\tfrac12+ix)\bigr)
           =\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos(xt)}{e^{t/2}+1}\,dt.
                                                               \tag{AQ.67.8}$$ Let $f(t)=(e^{t/2}+1)^{-1}$. Then $$f''(t)=\frac{e^{t/2}(e^{t/2}-1)}{4(e^{t/2}+1)^3}>0
               \quad(t>0).$$ The function and its derivatives decay exponentially. Two integrations by parts, with their endpoint terms included, give $$\phi'(x)=\frac1{x^2}\int_0^\infty f''(t)(1-\cos xt)\,dt>0
                  \quad(x>0).                                \tag{AQ.67.9}$$ Stirling’s expansion for the same analytic logarithms, DLMF (5.11.1), or the corresponding gamma-ratio expansion (5.11.13), gives $\phi(x)\longrightarrow\pi/4$ as $x\to\infty$. Since $\phi(0)=0$, it follows that $0<\phi(x)<\pi/4$ for $x>0$. Therefore $$\frac{d}{dx}\log r(4\pi x)
       =\frac{x\phi'(x)+\pi/4-\phi(x)}{x^2}>0.                 \tag{AQ.67.10}$$ At zero, $\phi'(0)=2\log2$, so $$r(\ell)=16\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{\ell}+O(\ell^2)\right)
              \quad(\ell\downarrow0),
 \qquad r(\ell)\longrightarrow4\quad(\ell\to\infty).$$ Real analyticity and (AQ.67.10) prove that $r$ is the asserted diffeomorphism.

Finally Fenchel–Nielsen gluing of the two labelled pairs of pants has exactly one length $\ell>0$ and one twist modulo $\ell$, equivalently an angle $\theta$ modulo $2\pi$. The two derivative vectors each have norm $r(\ell)$, and the twist contributes their tensor’s unit phase. This proves (AQ.67.2). Its inverse is $\ell=r^{-1}(\sqrt{|q|})$, $\theta=\arg q$ with the latter viewed as a circle-valued coordinate. In logarithmic polar coordinates the real Jacobian is diagonal, with entries $2r'(\ell)/r(\ell)>0$ and $1$. Both the punctured disk and $\mathbb C^\times$ deformation retract to the same circle $|q|=1$; the inclusion therefore induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$, taking one full twist to the positive generator. ◻

</div>

## AQ-67-NONHOLO: the natural complex-analytic formulation fails

Give the labelled geodesic-cut $(0,4)$ space its natural complex structure, namely the covering $$\mathcal C=\mathcal T(S_{0,4})/\langle D_\alpha\rangle$$ of the moduli space of four labelled punctures, where $\alpha$ is the chosen separating curve and $D_\alpha$ is its Dehn twist. Equivalently, this is the component of the cover that remembers the isotopy class of the cut. The two sides and all punctures remain labelled. Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates upstairs are $(\ell,\tau)\in\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb R$, with $D_\alpha(\ell,\tau)=(\ell,\tau+\ell)$; downstairs the normalized angle is $\theta=2\pi\tau/\ell$ modulo $2\pi$. The canonical capping map of AQ-67-EXACT is not holomorphic from this complex manifold to $\mathbb C^\times$. Thus its everywhere invertible real tangent map does not make it a complex-analytic or $\mathbb C$-algebraic *étale* map. This refutes that stronger interpretation of 67/077–082 for the natural Teichmüller complex structure already in the labelled $(0,4)$ sector.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Suppose it were holomorphic. By AQ-67-EXACT it is a bijection with nonzero real Jacobian onto the punctured disk $\mathcal D=\{0<|q|<16\}$. The holomorphic inverse function theorem then makes it a biholomorphism with $\mathcal D$. Conjugating the rotations $q\mapsto e^{is}q$ by it gives holomorphic automorphisms of $\mathcal C$. Their lifts to $\mathcal T(S_{0,4})$, continuously chosen to equal the identity at $s=0$, are the explicit global diffeomorphisms $$H_s(\ell,\tau)=
       \left(\ell,\tau+\frac{s\ell}{2\pi}\right),
                      \qquad s\in\mathbb R.                  \tag{AQ.67.11}$$ This follows directly from the tensor’s unit twist phase; any choice of zero-angle section changes $\theta$ by a function of $\ell$ and leaves (AQ.67.11) unchanged. Since the quotient projection is a holomorphic covering, every $H_s$ is holomorphic as well.

Let $\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}$ and $g_{\mathrm{WP}}$ be the Weil–Petersson Kähler form and metric. Wolpert (1983), Lemma 4.1, p. 229, says that the arclength twist vector field $t_\alpha=\partial/\partial\tau$ has Hamiltonian potential $-\ell$, in his normalization: $$\iota_{t_\alpha}\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=-d\ell.$$ The vector field of (AQ.67.11) is $V=(\ell/2\pi)t_\alpha$, so $$\iota_V\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}
   =-\frac{\ell}{2\pi}d\ell
   =-d\!\left(\frac{\ell^2}{4\pi}\right).$$ Cartan’s formula and $d\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=0$ imply $\mathcal L_V\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=0$; hence every global $H_s$ preserves $\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}$. A different fixed normalization of the Weil–Petersson form only multiplies these identities by a nonzero constant and gives the same conclusion. A holomorphic map preserving the Kähler form preserves the metric, since that metric is recovered from the form and the complex structure. Thus every $H_s$ would be a Weil–Petersson isometry of $\mathcal T(S_{0,4})$.

Brock and Margalit, *Weil–Petersson isometries via the pants complex*, Main Theorem, p. 2 of arXiv:math/0412499v1, prove that every such isometry is induced by an element of the extended mapping class group; their theorem explicitly includes $S_{0,4}$. That group is countable. On the other hand (AQ.67.11) gives distinct maps for every two distinct real numbers $s$, because $\ell>0$ and the upstairs twist coordinate is real, not taken modulo $\ell$. This is an uncountable family of isometries, a contradiction. ◻

</div>

## AQ-67-INVOLUTION: the proposition holds; its corollary fails

The proposition of 67/091 gives an equivalence between two-regular modules with involution and triples $(P,Q,m)$, where $P,Q$ are two-regular and $m\subset P/2P\oplus Q/2Q$ meets each summand trivially. This proposition is correct, with morphisms of triples preserving $m$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* For an involution on $M$, let $P=M_+$ and $Q=M_-$. Two-regularity gives $P\cap Q=0$ and $2M\subset P\oplus Q\subset M$. Let $m=2M/(2P\oplus2Q)$. If an element of $2M$ has zero $Q$-coordinate, say $2x\in P$, then $2(\sigma x-x)=0$, so $x\in P$ and $2x\in2P$. This proves $m\cap(P/2P)=0$, and the other summand is identical.

Conversely take the inverse image $L\subset P\oplus Q$ of $m$, with involution $(p,q)\mapsto(p,-q)$. It is stable because modulo two this involution is the identity. It is two-regular, and the intersection conditions give $L_+=2P\oplus0$, $L_-=0\oplus2Q$. Multiplication by two canonically identifies these eigenspaces with $P,Q$ and recovers the original $m$ from $2L/(4P\oplus4Q)$. Starting with $M$, the reconstructed $L$ is $2M$, canonically isomorphic to $M$ by multiplication by two. Maps restrict to the eigenspaces, and maps of triples preserve the indicated inverse images. These constructions are inverse on maps as well as objects, proving the equivalence. ◻

</div>

The corollary claiming invertible rank-one eigenspaces for a nontrivial involution on a rank-two projective module over a commutative two-regular ring is false even when the ring is an integral domain. Set $$R=\mathbb Z[x,y,z]/(x^2+yz-1),\quad M=R^2,\qquad
 \sigma=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\z&-x\end{pmatrix}.                   \tag{AQ.9}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The polynomial $f=x^2+yz-1$, viewed in $\mathbb Z[x,y][z]$, is primitive and linear over the fraction field; hence it is irreducible in the unique factorization domain $\mathbb Z[x,y,z]$. Thus $R$ is a domain, in particular two-regular. Clearly $\sigma^2=1$ and $\sigma\ne\pm1$.

In $S=\mathbb Z[x,y,z]$ set $A=\sigma-I$ and $B=\sigma+I$. Then $AB=BA=fI$. If $\bar A\bar v=0$ over $R$, lift $v$ to $S^2$ and write $Av=fw$. Multiplication by $B$ gives $fv=fBw$; since $f$ is a non-zero-divisor, $v=Bw$. Therefore $\ker\bar A=\operatorname{im}\bar B$. Interchanging $A,B$ proves $\ker\bar B=\operatorname{im}\bar A$, and hence $$P=\ker(\sigma-I)\simeq\operatorname{coker}(\sigma-I).$$ At the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(2,x-1,y,z)$ every entry of $A$ vanishes, so $P/\mathfrak mP\simeq\mathbb F_2^2$. Generically $A$ has rank one, giving $P$ rank one. An invertible module would have a one-dimensional fibre at $\mathfrak m$, a contradiction. The same argument applies to $Q$. ◻

</div>

Connectedness of $\operatorname{Spec}R$ alone cannot repair the corollary. This algebraic counterexample does not involve RH, and cannot be excluded by it.

## AQ-154-COUNT: what can actually be counted over finite fields

The supplied 154/120 says $$\chi(\mathcal X)=s_0-s_1+s_2=1-g,\qquad
 2s_1=4s_0,\qquad \chi(\mathcal X)=s_2-s_0.$$ Its $\mathcal X_2$ is a cellular surface of relative positions of pseudolines. Neither that sheet nor SA11 supplies it with a scheme over a finite field. The original Euler-characteristic calculation is therefore not an assertion about $\#\mathcal X_2(\mathbb F_q)$.

There is a genuine arithmetic consequence for a different, explicitly specified object. Let $L_1,\dots,L_n$ be distinct rational projective lines in $\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb Q}$, let $U$ be their complement, and let $S$ contain the finitely many primes where lines coincide or their incidence lattice changes. If $v$ runs through their intersection points and $m_v$ lines meet at $v$, set $$b_2=\sum_v(m_v-1)-n+1.$$ For every $p\notin S$ and $r\geq1$, $$\#U(\mathbb F_{p^r})=p^{2r}-(n-1)p^r+b_2,\qquad
 Z(U_{\mathbb F_p},t)
  =\frac{(1-pt)^{n-1}}{(1-p^2t)(1-t)^{b_2}}.                  \tag{AQ.10}$$ Consequently its incomplete arithmetic zeta function is $$\zeta^S(U,s)=
 \frac{\zeta^S(s-2)\zeta^S(s)^{b_2}}
      {\zeta^S(s-1)^{n-1}},\qquad
 \zeta^S(s)=\prod_{p\notin S}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}.                 \tag{AQ.11}$$

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Every intersection point is rational because the lines are rational. Over $\mathbb F_{p^r}$ the sum of the $n$ line counts is $n(p^r+1)$. It overcounts an intersection $v$ exactly $m_v-1$ times. Subtracting the corrected union count from $p^{2r}+p^r+1$ proves the first formula. Insert it in $Z(U_{\mathbb F_p},t)=
\exp(\sum_{r\geq1}\#U(\mathbb F_{p^r})t^r/r)$ and use $-\log(1-z)=\sum_{r\geq1}z^r/r$ to get the second. Multiplying its Euler factors gives (AQ.11), initially for $\operatorname{Re}s>3$ and then by meromorphic continuation. ◻

</div>

Under the adopted RH, the nontrivial-zero contribution to the divisor of (AQ.11) is on $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52$: the middle line gives poles when $n>1$, while the other two give zeros when their exponents are positive. Trivial zeros, the zeta pole, and the explicit omitted Euler factors remain separately specified by (AQ.11). These shifted divisors do not give a formula for the pseudoline counts or the topology of $\mathcal X_2$.

## AQ-SA08-LIFT: a concrete divided-power lifting obstruction

The supplied SA08 intake concerns the uniqueness problem of cote 136/117–121 and a proposed lifting input. The original cote-136 sheets are absent from this packet. The auxiliary lifting claim can nevertheless be checked directly in its primary source: Caradot–Lin (2026), arXiv:2511.22614v4, §2.1, pp. 5–6, and Proposition 3.10, pp. 19–20. There *free complex* means free in each degree, and surjectivity of the PD ideals is degreewise. Under these hypotheses the asserted lift need not exist, over any nonzero commutative ring $k$.

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Use cochain degrees and the square-zero PD dg algebras $$R'=k\oplus(ku\xrightarrow{\,d\,}kv),\qquad
 |u|=-2,\quad |v|=-1,\quad du=v,
 \qquad R=k\oplus k\epsilon,\quad|\epsilon|=-2,\quad d\epsilon=0.$$ Their augmentation ideals have zero multiplication, and $\gamma_n=0$ for $n\geq2$ defines divided powers. All divided-power identities hold: products of two ideal elements vanish, and the differential identity $d\gamma_n(u)=\gamma_{n-1}(u)\,du$ is zero for $n\geq2$, including $u\,du=uv=0$ when $n=2$. Define $\pi(u)=\epsilon$, $\pi(v)=0$, and $\pi|_k=1$. It is a PD dg map surjective on the ideals. Take the termwise free complex $X=k\epsilon$, concentrated in degree $-2$, and the identity chain map $X\to k\epsilon\subset R$. A PD dg algebra lift from the free PD dg algebra on $X$ would restrict on its generator to a chain lift $\epsilon\mapsto cu$. Compatibility with $\pi$ forces $c=1$, whereas compatibility with the differential forces $cv=0$, hence $c=0$ in $k$. This contradicts $k\ne0$. ◻

</div>

The notation $X$ here denotes just $k$ placed in degree $-2$; no resolution or projectivity in the category of complexes is being assumed. The failed implication is precisely from a graded lift to a chain lift. Theorem 3.15 of the same version, pp. 23–24, assumes a quasi-isomorphism. Our $\pi$ is not one: $H^*(R')=k$ but $H^*(R)=k\oplus k\epsilon$. Consequently this counterexample does not refute that later theorem.

The unresolved SA08 statements are the actual integral common-source assertion A-CS, terminality of the all-arrow localization A-TL, and application of the SA09 comparison structures A-SA09. The intake states them but does not supply their complete definitions and 15,000-line proof context. Neither a weight grading on a divided-power algebra nor the adopted analytic zero theorem supplies a chain lift in the displayed counterexample. That is a specific false lifting route; it is not a disproof of the remaining existence or localization conjectures.
