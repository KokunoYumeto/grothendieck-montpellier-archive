## Scope and mathematical reconstruction

No existence assertion is made for the fields left over.

The normal central product restricts as a tensor product with equal factor degrees, because the ambient group permutes the quasisimple factors transitively. Each factor representation is faithful as a representation of the actual matrix subgroup. Thus the projective lower degree applies to every factor and the total degree is a power of that factor degree. Applying the character-ratio inequality to the total degree is legitimate; a tensor-factor degree has not been substituted for it.

The exact ceiling inequality leaves upper bounds 87, 45, 33, 21, 15, 15 for the six prime powers 29, 31, 32, 37, 41, 43. All non-A1 classical types exceed these bounds already at the least permitted rank. The stated exceptional parameter pairs have field size at most four. The simply connected exceptional tables also exceed the upper bounds, and the complete exceptional-cover list supplies the required justification for using those tables for arbitrary quasisimple covers. The Suzuki and Ree parameter conventions have been checked explicitly. In particular the Suzuki degree at field size 32 is 124, not a value obtained by confusing the square-root parameter with field size.

For A1, the projective lower degrees squared exceed the total upper bounds, forcing a single quasisimple factor. Fields 41 and 43 are then excluded by their factor lower degrees. The exact possible special-linear degrees at 37 are disjoint from the exact ceiling set; no self-duality assumption enters that step.

I checked the full special-linear conjugacy argument, including determinant correction in the semisimple centralizer, both signs of the odd-characteristic unipotents, and the even-characteristic involutions. It proves inverse conjugacy in the full central cover at 29 and 32. A real character gives a unique invariant bilinear form up to scalar, so the entire normalizer is essentially self-dual and D-BI-18a applies.

At field size 31, perfectness makes the map from automorphisms of the special-linear cover into automorphisms of its central quotient injective. The nonsquare diagonal automorphism is class-inverting on the cover, including central and negative unipotent elements. For a nonreal irreducible character it exchanges the character and its complex conjugate, and therefore cannot be induced by conjugation in the fixed representation. The ambient normalizer is consequently scalar times the irreducible core, to which the stronger scalar-only bound applies. This handles both possible character types without assuming that the inducing field equals the original imaginary quadratic field.
