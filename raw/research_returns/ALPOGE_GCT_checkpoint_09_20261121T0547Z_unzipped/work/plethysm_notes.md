## Approaches tried and their exact stopping points

- Universal lambda identities: yield correct operation identities, but ψ²(s1)=s2−s11 refutes preservation of the positive cone by arbitrary Adams operations.
- Effective G-sets: subset identity fails on C2; pre-lambda structure is insufficient for universal plethysm; sign cannot be an effective permutation character. This does not rule out richer categories or signed cancellation followed by positivity.
- Tensor-power Cauchy expansion: fully proved in characteristic zero. Its coefficients in the chosen basis are virtual; replacing the expansion by Schur–Weyl only recovers already-known ordinary positivity, not the flip's quantitative requirements.
- Modular transfer: exact G0 relation over F2 prevents naive freeness. Arbitrary local coefficient rings require a precise category and exact/split convention; no classification was established.
- Orbit-to-closure transfer: exact rank formula PL-06 reduces the missing datum to restriction maps. Positivity of all ambient coefficients does not establish their ranks.
- Positive Hilbert series: supplied an actual invariant ring and verified root's shifted-polytope construction. This improves the one-ray model but does not construct uniform positive rational forms of polynomial complexity or separate permanent/determinant.

## Higher-ambient repair of the minimal-dimension proof (independent check)

There is a genuine subtlety in citing Kahle–Michałek's one-variable interval exclusion for a one-dimensional polytope presented in a higher-dimensional ambient lattice. The following supplies the missing reduction, and was sent to root.

Suppose P_s={x∈R^D:Ax≤sb+c} is bounded, has eventual dimension one, and has φ(s)>0 integer points for every sufficiently large integer s. Every vertex is among finitely many affine functions x_B(s)=A_B^{-1}(sb_B+c_B), where B indexes D linearly independent tight rows. Feasibility of each candidate, and whether each row is tight on it, is determined by finitely many affine functions in s. Thus both stabilize on a real tail. The rows tight at every feasible candidate vertex form a fixed set J. Their equations give the affine hull: if a relative interior point had an additional constraint not strict, that row would also be globally tight; directions in the common nullspace can be moved slightly in both signs without violating the other strict constraints. Thus aff(P_s)={Mx=sb'+c'}, where ker M is a fixed rational line.

Choose integer points x_N∈P_N and x_{N+1}∈P_{N+1} on that tail. Then w=x_{N+1}−x_N and v0=x_N−Nw are integral, with Mw=b' and Mv0=c'. Consequently v0+sw is an integral point of the affine support for every integer s. Let v be a primitive integral generator of ker M. All integral points of the affine support are exactly v0+sw+t v, t∈Z. Substitution in the original inequalities gives lower and upper bounds for t by finitely many rational affine functions of s. After passing further along the tail, the maximum lower bound and minimum upper bound each have one fixed affine formula. The interval length has slope 1/3 because its count φ(s) has slope 1/3 and count differs from length by a bounded amount.

Translate t by an integer multiple of s to make the lower endpoint's slope a belong to [0,1); the upper slope is a+1/3. At every large s≡5 mod6 the count jumps by2. Since the lower endpoint does not move left, this forces a+1/3>1: an interval moving its right endpoint by at most1 can add at most1 integer. But then at every large step at least1 integer enters from the right, while the lower endpoint moves by less than1 and removes at most1 integer. (The intervals overlap for large s, since their lengths tend to infinity.) The count must be eventually nondecreasing, contradicting its drop by1 at every large s≡0 mod6. Hence no higher-ambient one-dimensional shifted model exists either.

## PLE-F8: the binary-dihedral ring is the actual cubic-plethysm algebra
