## 2. Formal groups, crystals, and integral differential operators

Over a finite extension where the Jacobian has good reduction, its
\(p\)-divisible group and Cartier dual determine dual Dieudonné
objects. The accepted DP_MULT DP30–34 and DP49 distinguish the
one-dimensional height-three factor from its two-dimensional dual.
The three labelled tangent directions are not three independent
height-three formal lines. Frobenius permutes the CM labels and
Verschiebung controls the restricted derivations. This is why the
restricted nilpotence exponents in AM-K013 are direction dependent.

The exact finite-level framework is SGA 3, Exposé VII_A, §§6.2–6.4,

and the formal-biextension injection is Proposition 121 and equation

The new theta statements concern the completed local rings and actual
sections of a polarized abelian scheme. In AM-K012 the explicit ordered
divisor chart is étale on a dense open and supplies a ring stable under
the invariant derivations. AM-K017 extends a logarithmic Hessian across
the theta divisor by checking every horizontal and vertical divisor and
using normality. It then uses properness to turn a global regular
function on the special fibre into a constant. This is a scheme-theoretic
argument on the actual integral model. It does not replace a missing
integral reduced theta frame by a complex analytic one.

The resulting Hurwitz series use the ordinary invariant operators
(D_i^n), with factorials in the multiplication law. Their reductions
and restricted-power identities are explicit. A Chern connection on an
ample theta line is not thereby made into a global flat algebraic
connection or a Dieudonné crystal. To make that additional assertion
one would have to construct the relevant stratification, integrable
connection, and Frobenius structure. The present proof does not do so.

For the oscillator, the same distinction has an exact EGA locator:
EGA IV_4, Theorem 16.11.2, p. 54, and Remarks 16.11.3(ii), p. 55.
Over an integral base the full sheaf of differential operators contains
divided derivatives and is generally larger than the algebra generated
by coordinates and ordinary derivations. AM-DO-003 constructs an
integral Weyl-algebra module, becoming the familiar delta
\(D\)-module after rationalization. It does not claim an integral
action of the full EGA differential-operator sheaf.
