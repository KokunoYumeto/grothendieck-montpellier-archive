# Regular-coherent coefficient rings: Schur resolution route

AI-produced research note, GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI, ChatGPT Pro mode), RESEARCH_SA17_FINISH/c1_regular_coherent. Working proof, not yet a compiled or released claim. Created 2026-11-19 after18:54UTC. Root owns the checkpoint10 compiled-source freeze.

## Proposed statements and exact scope

1. **Schur-perfect generation lemma.** Let k be any commutative ring, n>=1, B=k[S_n], V=k^n and S=End_B(V^{tensor n}) with the usual right place-permutation action. Let e in S project onto the multilinear weight space of V^{tensor n}. For a B-module M which is finite projective over k, put F=S e tensor_B M. If the underlying k-module F is perfect, then [M] in the admissible lattice Grothendieck group R_k(S_n) belongs to the K_0(k)-submodule generated by the Young permutation modules k[S_n/S_mu] for partitions mu of n. Here the K_0(k) action tensors with a projective k-module carrying the trivial action. This is a sufficient lemma, not an intrinsic all-ring criterion.

2. **Regular-coherent criterion.** If k is a nonzero commutative regular-coherent ring (coherent, and every finitely presented k-module has a finite resolution by finite projective k-modules), then the polynomial induction map theta_k is an isomorphism if and only if char(k)=0 and the unit Z -> K_0(k) is an isomorphism. This extends the existing regular-Noetherian theorem without any finite-dimensionality assumption.

3. **Valuation-domain family.** For every valuation domain V, of arbitrary rank and with arbitrary value group, theta_V is an isomorphism if and only if char(V)=0. In particular all nondiscrete mixed-characteristic valuation domains are removed from the remaining C.1 gate. The theorem is not claimed for valuation rings with zero divisors; the convention here is the usual domain definition.

## Full argument for statement1

We use two opened exact structural inputs, explained below: S_k(n,n) is split quasi-hereditary over every commutative ring; it is homologically smooth over k, and its standard modules form a full exceptional collection with components Perf(k). All these assertions explicitly allow non-Noetherian k. The divided-power projectives P_mu=Gamma^{mu_1}(V) tensor ... tensor Gamma^{mu_l}(V), for partitions mu of n, have standard filtrations with multiplicities K_{lambda,mu}, the unitriangular integral Kostka matrix. We do not need to identify a standard module's Schur image with a choice of Specht or dual Specht convention.

### The corner and the Schur lift

Write T=V^{tensor n}. Its standard tensor-word basis is permuted on the right by S_n. The multilinear weight summand T_omega has basis the words which use every basis letter exactly once. This is the regular right B-module: choose one word and send g to its right translate. The projection e:T->T_omega commutes with every place permutation, so e belongs to S. Extension by zero from this summand identifies eSe with End_B(T_omega)=End_B(B_B), hence with B by left multiplication. This also fixes all sides in F=S e tensor_B M. Applying e gives canonically eF=(eSe) tensor_B M=M.

The algebra S is finite free over k. Indeed End_k(T) has the basis indexed by pairs of tensor words, and commuting with all simultaneous place permutations means that matrix entries are constant on each orbit of this finite permutation action. Orbit indicator matrices form a k-basis. This identifies S with the integral Schur algebra and commutes with arbitrary base change. Its direct summands S e and eS are finite projective over k.

Because M is finite projective over k, N=S e tensor_k M is finite projective over k. The module F is its quotient by the image of the finite projective k-module S e tensor_k B tensor_k M under

    x tensor b tensor m |-> x b tensor m - x tensor b m.

Consequently F is finitely presented as a k-module. This observation uses only the finite k-basis of B and does not assume k coherent.

### Why k-perfect implies S-perfect

Homological smoothness means that the diagonal S-bimodule is perfect over S tensor_k S^op (ordinary tensor here since S is k-projective). If X is an S-module whose underlying k-complex is perfect, apply the derived tensor functor - tensor_S^L X to the finite construction of this diagonal from finite free bimodules, shifts, cones and direct summands. A free bimodule is sent to S tensor_k^L X, which is a perfect left S-complex because X is k-perfect. Every operation in this finite construction preserves perfectness. Its value on the diagonal is X itself. Thus X is S-perfect, with no coherence or Noetherian hypothesis. Apply this to F.

A module concentrated in degree0 which is perfect has a finite resolution by finite projective modules. For completeness, begin with a bounded finite-projective complex quasi-isomorphic to it. Split off the positive-degree acyclic tail starting at its last nonzero term; the resulting complex is concentrated in nonpositive degrees. Its augmentation onto H^0=F is the desired finite resolution. Thus choose

    0 -> Q_r -> ... -> Q_0 -> F ->0

with Q_i finite projective over S. The exact functor e(-) gives the same resolution ending in M, and each eQ_i is finite projective over k. Since M is finite projective over k, the last surjection onto M splits as k-modules. Its kernel is finite projective over k. Inducting upward makes every short exact sequence in this resolution admissible in the lattice category. Therefore

    [M] = sum_i (-1)^i [eQ_i] in R_k(S_n).

### Standard factors and polynomial generators

Let K_0(S) mean the projective Grothendieck group, equivalently K_0(Perf(S)). The full exceptional standard collection gives a semiorthogonal decomposition with one Perf(k) component for each partition lambda of n. It follows by the finite successive projection triangles that every element of K_0(S) is a sum of [Delta_lambda tensor_k a_lambda] for a_lambda in K_0(k). More explicitly, the triangle projections successively write a perfect object as an extension of Delta_lambda tensor_k C_lambda for perfect k-complexes C_lambda; taking their Euler classes gives the stated formula. Thus this use is generation, not a numerical character argument and not a supposition that idempotent completion preserves a previously computed K_0.

The integral Cauchy filtrations give, in this group,

    [P_mu] = sum_lambda K_{lambda,mu} [Delta_lambda],

where K_{lambda,mu}=0 unless lambda dominates mu and K_{mu,mu}=1. Their successive quotients and all P_mu,Delta_lambda are finite free over k, so these relations hold in the exact k-projective category as well. The integer unitriangular matrix has an integer inverse. Therefore K_0(S) is generated as a K_0(k)-module by the [P_mu].

The exact functor Q |-> eQ from finite projective S-modules to k-projective B-modules gives a map K_0(S)->R_k(S_n). To see its value on Delta_lambda without ambiguity, apply e to a finite S-projective resolution of Delta_lambda and use the same k-splitting argument. Its value is [eDelta_lambda]. The map is K_0(k)-linear, since e commutes with tensoring a projective scalar module. The Euler class of F maps to [M]. Consequently [M] is a K_0(k)-linear combination of [eP_mu].

Finally eP_mu is exactly the Young permutation module. A basis of Gamma^{mu_j}(V) is given by the divided-power monomials in the basis letters. Multilinear weight in their tensor product means that the n letters are distributed among ordered blocks of sizes mu_j, each occurring once. These ordered set partitions form a basis, and permutation of the basis letters acts transitively, with stabilizer S_{mu_1} x ... x S_{mu_l}. On the multilinear space the corner's B-action is exactly this permutation action: its generators extend the permutation matrices on V, and on T_omega these are left regular multiplication after fixing the base word. Thus eP_mu is k[S_n/S_mu], with no factorial division and in every characteristic. This proves statement1.

## Full argument for statement2

For regular-coherent k, the k-module F just constructed is finitely presented and hence perfect by the definition. Statement1 therefore applies to every lattice M in every degree. If Z -> K_0(k) is an isomorphism, every coefficient a_lambda is an integer times [k]. Thus every [M] is an integer combination of Young permutation modules. The module k[S_n/S_mu] is precisely the induction product rho_{mu_1} ... rho_{mu_l} of the trivial modules in those degrees. This proves surjectivity of theta_k directly, without passing through G_0(k[S_n]), noetherian support induction, or Specht conventions.

If char(k)=0, then k tensor Q is nonzero: if1 died in that localization, some nonzero integer would vanish in k. Choose a maximal ideal in k tensor Q and its characteristic-zero residue field L. Base change to L sends every rho_i to the same generator and is exact on the admissible lattice category, because its short exact sequences split on the underlying k-modules. The composite polynomial map -> R_k(S_*) -> R_L(S_*) is the characteristic-zero field isomorphism already proved in C:Qalgebras. Therefore theta_k is injective.

Conversely, the degree-zero part of theta_k is the unit Z -> K_0(k), so its being an isomorphism is necessary. A nonzero ring of positive characteristic is excluded by the existing C:positive_characteristic theorem, whose hypotheses are arbitrary commutative positive-characteristic coefficient rings. These are exactly the two claimed necessary conditions. This proves statement2.

## Full argument for statement3

A valuation domain is local, so every finite projective module is finite free and rank gives K_0(V)=Z. Every finite presentation matrix over V can be diagonalized by invertible elementary row and column operations: among its finitely many nonzero entries choose one dividing all the others, possible because the principal ideals in a valuation domain are linearly ordered. Move it to the upper-left corner, subtract its multiples to clear the rest of its row and column, and repeat on the smaller matrix. Division is used only when divisibility is already known. Thus every finitely presented module is a finite direct sum of free modules and V/(a) for nonzero a. Each V/(a) has the finite free resolution 0->V --a--> V ->V/(a)->0, since V is a domain. It follows that every finitely presented V-module is perfect. Also V is coherent: every finitely generated ideal is principal, and a nonzero principal ideal is free. These facts prove regular coherence directly, with no discreteness, rank or finite-dimensionality assumption. Apply statement2.
