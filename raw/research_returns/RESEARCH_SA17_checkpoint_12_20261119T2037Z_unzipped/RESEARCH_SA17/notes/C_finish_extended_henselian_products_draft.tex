\subsection{Products including strict and non-Noetherian henselian factors}
\label{C:extended-henselian-products-subsection}

\begin{theorem}[Proved: exact bounded coordinates for the enlarged local families]
\label{C:extended-henselian-products-WG}
For each $i$ let $R_i$ be any one of the local rings in
Theorems~\ref{C:effective-henselian-DVR-WG},
\ref{C:finite-field-henselization-WG},
\ref{C:arithmetic-henselization-WG},
\ref{C:arithmetic-strict-henselization-WG}, or
\ref{C:effective-henselian-flat-unions}, with the finite
input access specified there.  The family may mix these
types, different odd residue characteristics, and
Noetherian and non-Noetherian factors.  Put $R=\prod_iR_i$.

Use each factor's proved canonical indecomposable indices.
For a symmetric index write its coordinate as $(a_j,b_j)$,
where $a_j\in\mathbf Z$ and $b_j\in\mathbf Z/2$ in
finite residue, and put $b_j=0$ in algebraically closed
residue.  All other indices have one integer coordinate.
Define $r_+,r_-,c$ by (C.3.HP2), with the convention
just given.  Then
\[
 WG_{\rm sp}(BO_{m,R})\hookrightarrow
             \prod_i WG_{\rm sp}(BO_{m,R_i})             \tag{C.3.XP1}
\]
is injective, and its image consists exactly of the
families with uniformly bounded $r_+$, $r_-$ and $c$.
At each coordinate these three integers are computable
from its finite canonical data and are the simultaneous
minimum positive rank, negative rank and common
coefficient degree of a difference of actual forms.

Products and every prescribed exterior operation in
(C.3.XP1) are coordinatewise.  Each requested coordinate
is computed by the ordinary terminating local algorithm.
No finite decision algorithm for equality or boundedness
of an arbitrary infinitely supplied family is asserted.
In particular the result gives literal bounded
coordinates for products of the four infinite-dimensional
local domain families in
Corollary~\ref{C:infinite-variable-henselian-WG}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First consider one factor.  In finite residue its
actual-form monoids and all coordinates are exactly
those of (C.3.HD2), also for a flat union by
Theorem~\ref{C:effective-henselian-flat-unions}.
Proposition~\ref{C:henselian-minimal-form-bounds}
therefore gives the three simultaneous minima: its
proof uses the stated monoid classification, free
underlying modules, and constant basis changes in
$C_d$, all of which hold for these union rings.
In algebraically closed residue, (C.3.SH4) says that
the symmetric block monoid is just $\mathbf N$, with
generator rank $n_j$.  The alternating and paired
block monoids are $\mathbf N$ with generator rank
$2n_j$, and there is no unitary block.
For an integer $a_j$ the simultaneous least positive
and negative multiplicities are
$\max(a_j,0)$ and $\max(-a_j,0)$.
Thus exactly the same formulas hold with every $b_j=0$.

For completeness, the degree assertion does not
require Noetherianity.  A direct summand of a free
representation over a local ring is finite free.
Choose bases for the two complementary summands.
Conjugating the original coaction by this constant
invertible matrix shows that every block remains
in $C_D$.  A supported indecomposable therefore has
minimal degree at most the degree of any form in
which it occurs.  Duality preserves minimal degree
because the antipode preserves the filtration and
is an involution.  The explicit minimal-rank forms
use only the supported indecomposables, so their
common degree is exactly the largest supported
degree, with degree zero for the zero class.
This proves all local minimum assertions, including
the constructive nature of their attainment.
Each local monoid is cancellative, by its block
description.

Every difference of actual forms over $R$ has bounded
ambient idempotent sizes and a finite coefficient
degree.  Its coordinate family satisfies the three
bounds just proved.
If a difference $[P,q]-[Q,h]$ has zero coordinates,
local cancellation gives an actual isometry
$(P_i,q_i)\simeq(Q_i,h_i)$ for every $i$.
Write $P=eR^a$ and $Q=fR^b$.  Represent these local
isometries and their inverses by matrices of sizes
$b\times a$ and $a\times b$, satisfying the corner
conditions imposed by $e_i,f_i$.
Their entries assemble in $R$ to inverse corner
maps.  The Gram and equivariance identities are
finite coefficient identities.  Since each $C_D$
is finite free over the integral base, its map over
$R$ to the product of its maps over $R_i$ is
injective.  Thus these assembled maps are an
actual equivariant isometry.  This proves
injectivity in (C.3.XP1).

Conversely let a family have common bounds $B,D$.
The proved local minima construct positive and
negative actual forms of rank at most $B$ and
degree at most $D$.  Pad their free presentations
to size $B$ using the idempotent
$\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)$ for the local rank $r$.
Pad their coactions, Gram matrices and inverse
Gram matrices with zeros on the complement.
The coaction counit is the idempotent, not $I_B$.
All entries, including the finitely many coefficient
positions in the fixed integral basis of $C_D$,
assemble in the product ring.  The finite
idempotent, coaction, invariance and inverse-Gram
identities hold because they hold at every factor.
They give actual forms over $R$ whose difference
has exactly the requested coordinates.  This
proves the asserted image.

Tensor products and exterior minors commute with
base change.  Multiplication of two differences
with bounds $(B,D)$ and $(B',D')$ is represented
with rank bound $2BB'$ and degree bound $D+D'$.
The coefficient of $t^r$ in
$\lambda_t(P)\lambda_t(Q)^{-1}$ is a finite
integral combination of tensor products of
exterior powers.  Its ranks are bounded in
terms of $B,r$ and its degree by $rD$.
The image is therefore closed under all claimed
operations.  The factor algorithms compute the
coordinates of each such finite construction.
Their use requires no decision about an
arbitrary infinite family, precisely as stated.
\end{proof}
