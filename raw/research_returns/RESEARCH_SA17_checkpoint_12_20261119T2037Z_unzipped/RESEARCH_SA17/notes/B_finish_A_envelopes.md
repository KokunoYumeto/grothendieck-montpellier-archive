## 1. The global ind-etale shortcut is false

Opened Stacks09AN (Section110.46) and04QK (Section110.45). Their ring
extension A -> B^twist(A) is Zariski locally a filtered colimit of finite
products of A, hence integral and faithfully flat and weakly etale. Its
spectrum is connected, and it has a retraction rho:B->A with nonzero
kernel. That kernel is not generated by idempotents.

Every b in B has an equality relation containing an open neighborhood of
the diagonal. Indeed, for a weakly etale algebra the ideal I of the
diagonal in B tensor_A B is pure. For x=b tensor1-1tensorb, purity gives
y in I with x=xy; then e=1-y satisfies xe=0 and mu(e)=1, so D(e) contains
the diagonal and lies in the equality relation.

Yet every map from an etale A-algebra C to B has image in A. Compose it
with rho to obtain C->A. This is a surjective etale map (a morphism between
etale A-algebras), so its kernel is generated by an idempotent f. The
image of f in connected B is0 or1; its image in A is0, so it is0. The
map C->B therefore factors through C->A. The same holds for an ind-etale
C by passing to its finite stages. Thus any b outside A is an integral
element with the open-diagonal equality property which is not in an
ind-etale envelope inside B, or even in the image of an ind-etale
A-algebra mapping to B.

The additional henselian closed-fibre condition in the P datum is NOT
satisfied by such a b: the retraction rho would send an annihilator
1+t*c to a unit and force b into A. Therefore this is only a counterexample
to the attempted general envelope lemma.

## 2. Even an affine weakly-etale envelope can be too much

Here is a direct example over a strictly henselian complete noetherian
base in every characteristic p. Let k be algebraically closed of
characteristic p and put

    A=k[[t]][epsilon]/(epsilon^2,t*epsilon),
    B=A[u,v,1/(u+v)]/(u*v-t),
    w=u/(u+v),
    b=epsilon*w.

The algebra B/A is smooth: the partial derivatives v,u of u*v-t generate
the unit ideal after inverting u+v. It is faithfully flat since each of
the two fibres over the primes of A_red=k[[t]] is nonempty. The ring
B_red=k[[t]][u,v,1/(u+v)]/(u*v-t) is a domain, as is seen by its embedding
in k((t))[u,u^(-1)] with v=t/u. Hence B is connected.

We have b^2=0. Modulo t, the two comaximal factors u=0 and v=0 give

    B/tB = (k[epsilon]/epsilon^2)[u,u^(-1)]
            times (k[epsilon]/epsilon^2)[v,v^(-1)],

and b has components epsilon and0 (in the corresponding ordering).
Thus b is not in A.

The equality relation of b contains a neighborhood of the diagonal.
Indeed D(u),D(v) cover Spec B; on D(u) we have b=epsilon, and on D(v)
we have b=0, because epsilon*u*v=epsilon*t=0. Consequently the equality
relation contains D(u_1*u_2) union D(v_1*v_2), which contains the diagonal.
There is even an explicit normalized annihilator. Write w_1=w tensor1,
w_2=1tensorw and set

    e=w_1*w_2+(1-w_1)*(1-w_2)+2*w_1*(1-w_1).

Its multiplication image is1. Since epsilon*w_i^2=epsilon*w_i and
w_1*(1-w_1)=t/(u_1+v_1)^2, each of its three summands annihilates
b tensor1-1tensorb. These identities include p=2.

We claim that every map D->B from a weakly etale A-algebra has image in A.
First, D is ind-etale over A. The reduction D/epsilon*D is weakly etale
over the excellent regular domain k[[t]], so it is ind-etale by de Jong--
Olander, On weakly etale morphisms, Theorem3 (printedp2; proofpp8--9),
opened at https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/230886016/On_weakly_tale_morphisms.pdf.
Stacks61.7.7 (097H), opened in full, lifts it to an ind-etale A-algebra E.
Formal etaleness gives a compatible map E->D lifting the specified
isomorphism modulo epsilon: write E as a filtered colimit of etale
algebras and use uniqueness of nilpotent lifting to ensure compatibility.
This map is an isomorphism. For its cokernel M as A-modules, M/epsilon*M=0
implies M=0 since epsilon^2=0. Its kernel K has K/epsilon*K=0 because
D is A-flat, and therefore K=0 as well. Thus D is ind-etale.

Now let E0/A be etale of finite presentation and let E0->B be a map.
Choose a point of Spec B above the closed point of A. Its image in
Spec E0 gives a k-valued point of the closed fibre, since k is separably
closed and E0/A is etale. Henselian lifting produces an A-section of
Spec E0 through that point. Such a section is open and closed because
E0/A is affine etale: algebraically the retraction E0->A has kernel
generated by an idempotent. Its pullback to connected Spec B is a
nonempty open-and-closed subset, hence all of Spec B. Thus E0->B factors
through E0->A. The same conclusion follows for D by passing to its
etale stages. In particular b is not in the image of ANY affine weakly
etale envelope mapping to B.

A nonaffine weakly etale scheme does carry b. Glue two copies of Spec A
along D(t), and define its global function by epsilon on one copy and0
on the other; these agree on D(t) because t*epsilon=0. On D(u) and D(v)
the maps Spec B->the two copies glue on D(u*v)=D(t), giving a map from
Spec B to this doubled scheme which pulls the function back to b.
This makes the distinction between an affine envelope and a scheme or
space envelope concrete.

Again this example is NOT a counterexample to P descent. Its equality
relation fails the requirement of containing the entire t=0 pair fibre:
b has values epsilon and0 on its two branches there. After t-henselization
the branches split, and the actual henselian cocycle condition is absent.
The example shows why a proof must retain that full closed-fibre condition
or permit a nonaffine weakly etale quotient; open-diagonal equality alone
cannot produce an affine envelope, even over this complete local base.
