\subsection{Complete split groups over henselian global-field coefficient bases}
\label{C:henselian-global-WG-subsection}

\begin{theorem}[Proposed: actual radical and division-factor algorithms]
\label{C:henselian-global-finite-algebra-algorithms}
Let $H$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-field-arithmetic},
and let $k/H$ be any supplied finite field tower.
Every finite-dimensional associative unital
$k$-algebra $E$, supplied by a multiplication
table, has ordinary terminating algorithms
computing its Jacobson radical and an actual
isomorphism
\[
 E/\operatorname{rad}E
       \simeq\prod_j M_{r_j}(D_j).                       \tag{C.3.HW1}
\]
The centers of the division algebras are
computed finite extensions of $k$, including
inseparable ones when they occur.
The output includes their multiplication
tables, all quotient and matrix-unit maps,
and a complete family of actual orthogonal
primitive idempotents of $E$ lifting the
diagonal matrix idempotents in (C.3.HW1).
No division index is left undecided.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The zero algebra is handled separately,
with empty semisimple product.
For positive dimension the radical
certificate argument of
Theorem~\ref{C:global-function-finite-algebra-algorithms}
uses only effective field arithmetic,
factorization in finite towers, and
actual central-simple-algebra algorithms.
We give its ingredients for the present
fields to identify every terminating step.

Enumerate finite towers $L_j/k$,
positive-dimensional associative unital
$L_j$-algebra tables $C_j$, and
$k$-linear maps
\[
              \rho:E\longrightarrow\prod_j C_j.
\]
Tower validity is decidable by
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-field-arithmetic}.
Certify each $C_j$ as central simple
by checking invertibility of its
two-sided action map
\[
 C_j\otimes_{L_j}C_j^{\rm op}
                 \longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{L_j}(C_j).
                                                               \tag{C.3.HW2}
\]
This is a finite determinant test.
Its surjectivity makes every two-sided
ideal invariant under all linear
endomorphisms, hence zero or the
whole algebra; a central element
commutes with all such endomorphisms
and is therefore a scalar.
Conversely a central simple algebra
passes the test, as proved in the
referenced finite-algebra argument.

Verify that $\rho$ is unital,
multiplicative and surjective.
Compute its kernel $J$ and its
powers by finite $k$-linear algebra,
and require $J^{\dim_kE+1}=0$.
Then $J$ is a nilpotent ideal,
so $J\subseteq\operatorname{rad}E$;
the quotient is semisimple, so
$\operatorname{rad}E\subseteq J$.
This certificate therefore computes
the exact radical.
Artinian structure guarantees one
such certificate: the actual simple
quotients have finite centers over
$k$, whether separable or inseparable,
and their radical kernel is nilpotent
with the stated dimension bound.
Consequently the enumeration terminates.
It does not assert that a semisimple
complement exists inside $E$.

For each certified $C_j$, the center
$L_j$ has the effective henselian
valuation data supplied by
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-field-arithmetic}.
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-central-simple-algorithms}
computes its actual matrix-over-division
decomposition, giving (C.3.HW1).
All maps are compositions of the
computed linear maps and matrix-unit
maps, rather than merely abstract
Wedderburn invariants.

Finally diagonal primitive idempotents
lift through the computed nilpotent
ideal.  One elementary proof starts
with $a^2-a\in J$ and replaces $a$ by
\[
              a-(2a-1)^{-1}(a^2-a).
\]
The inverse exists because
$(2a-1)^2=1+4(a^2-a)$; all these
elements lie in the commutative
subalgebra generated by $a$.
The new idempotence error is a
multiple of the square of the old
one, so iteration terminates through
the nilpotent powers.
Successive complementary corners
give an orthogonal family summing
to one.  Alternatively enumerate
finite families with the prescribed
quotient images and test the finite
idempotent, orthogonality and sum
identities.  The lifting argument
proves that this exact search halts.
Primitivity follows from primitivity
modulo the radical.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Proposed: canonical split groups over henselian global-field coefficient rings]
\label{C:henselian-global-coefficient-WG}
Let $H$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-field-arithmetic},
and let $k/H$ be a supplied finite field tower.
For every $m\geq0$ the following coefficient
rings have complete ordinary terminating
classification and split-$WG$ operation
algorithms for rational $O_{m,R}$-representations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every finite-dimensional commutative
$k$-algebra $R$ given by its multiplication
table;
\item $R=B^h$, where $B$ is a countable
commutative local ring with exact decidable
finite names, computable arithmetic and
residue map, and an ordinary algorithm
computing finite generators of every
finite matrix kernel over $B$, whose
residue field is a supplied finite
extension of $k$.
\end{enumerate}
In each case all finite projective
indecomposable rational representations
have a decidable literal canonical
index set $\mathscr I_R\subseteq\mathbf N$.
Module isomorphism, indecomposability,
duality, existence of either invariant
nonsingular form sign, actual equivariant
isometry, and equality in
$WG_{\rm sp}(BO_{m,R})$ are decidable.

For each self-dual index $i$, one computes
a reference form $q_i$ of sign
$\varepsilon_i$, the actual residue
division algebra $D_i$ of its
endomorphism ring, and the induced
adjoint involution $\sigma_i$.
There is a canonical additive description
\[
 WG_{\rm sp}(BO_{m,R})\simeq
 \bigoplus_{i=i^*}\mathcal G(D_i,\sigma_i,\varepsilon_i)
 \ \oplus\
 \bigoplus_{\{i,i^*\},\ i\ne i^*}\mathbf Z,              \tag{C.3.HW3}
\]
where $\mathcal G$ is the literal
decidable least-Gram-pair group of
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-hermitian-isometry}.
Every finite coordinate vector has
computable actual positive and negative
representatives.
Products and every specified exterior
coefficient of finite virtual inputs
are computed in finite time.
No unresolved division-algebra or
hermitian-isometry quotient is part
of the output.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We specify how the previously proved
representation algorithms apply with
the new arithmetic; in particular
all actual Hom spaces and lifts
are retained.

\emph{Finite coefficient algebras.}
The zero coefficient ring has only
the zero module and the zero group;
return those outputs immediately.
Zero modules are recognized and
omitted from indecomposable lists.
Represent a finite $R$-module by a
finite $k$-vector space and its
$R$-action matrices.  The unital
and multiplication equations are
decidable.  A rational coaction
uses a finite coaction-degree
piece $C_d(k)$ and satisfies the
finite coefficient identities
already proved for these pieces.
Commutation with the $R$-action
is another finite linear system.

Projectivity is a finite affine
linear test over $k$: the canonical
surjection $R^s\to M$ defined by
a $k$-basis of $M$ has an
$R$-linear section exactly when
the finite equations for a section
are consistent.  A solution is
an actual projective presentation.
Hom modules, invariant forms, and
the dual $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$
are finite $k$-linear kernels;
the dual is not replaced by
the $k$-linear dual.

The field $k$ is infinite.
On any finite-dimensional linear
space of matrices, invertibility
is nonvanishing of a determinant
polynomial.  If the matrices have
size $s$, a grid of $s+1$ distinct
$k$-elements in each variable
decides whether this polynomial
vanishes identically and produces
an invertible matrix when one
exists.  This decides actual
module isomorphism and both
nonsingular form signs.

The actual endomorphism algebra
is finite dimensional over $k$.
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-finite-algebra-algorithms}
computes its radical quotient,
actual division factors, and
lifted primitive idempotents.
A module is indecomposable
exactly when this quotient
is one division factor of
matrix size one.  Otherwise
the lifted idempotents split
actual projective submodules,
whose positive $k$-dimensions
decrease.  Thus the complete
Krull--Schmidt decomposition
algorithm terminates.

\emph{Henselian local coefficient rings.}
The exact-access proof in
Theorem~\ref{C:effective-coherent-henselization-WG}
applies to $B^h$ over the present
residue field without any $C_1$
assumption.  Indeed its equality
and kernel proof uses only the
given finite $B$-matrix kernels,
finite free monic algebras,
simple residue roots, and the
faithfully flat pointed local
henselization.  It computes
exact names, equality, residues,
residue lifts and all full
finite matrix kernels over $R$.

Finite projectives over $R$
are free.  Their actual Hom
and form modules are exactly
the finite coefficient kernels
just computed.  Reduce their
computed generators to obtain
their actual images over the
residue field $\kappa$; these
need not be all intertwiners
of the residue representations.
The same determinant grid over
the infinite field $\kappa$
decides invertibility in those
images, and lifting its scalar
coefficients gives actual
isomorphisms or sign forms.

Let $E$ be an actual finite
endomorphism ring and let $E_0$
be its residue image in the
matrix algebra over $\kappa$.
Its kernel $I$ lies in
$\operatorname{rad}E$: for
$u\in I$ and $a\in E$,
$1-au$ has unit determinant
and its inverse is still
equivariant.  Therefore
\[
 E/\operatorname{rad}E
            \simeq E_0/\operatorname{rad}E_0.          \tag{C.3.HW4}
\]
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-finite-algebra-algorithms}
computes the right side over
$\kappa$, including all actual
division tables and quotient maps.
The finite kernel generators
make $E$ finite over $R$.
Lemma~\ref{C:nonnoetherian-henselian-lifting}
then gives semiperfectness and
idempotent lifting.  Enumerating
actual endomorphisms with the
computed residual idempotent
finds an exact lift; its
existence has just been proved.
Independent residue columns
give bases of the finite free
image and complementary image.
Their ranks decrease at each
split, so the actual decomposition
terminates here as well.

In both coefficient cases, encode
all the finite module data by
natural numbers and retain an
indecomposable code exactly when
no smaller valid code is isomorphic
to it.  All tests are decidable
and there are finitely many
predecessors.  This constructs
$\mathscr I_R$ and the canonical
index of each input summand.
Computing the actual $R$-dual
and canonicalizing gives $i^*$.

\emph{Residual forms and their actual lifts.}
For a self-dual indecomposable,
choose an isomorphism $g:M\to M^*$.
The endomorphisms
$g^{-1}(g+g^{\mathsf t})$ and
$g^{-1}(g-g^{\mathsf t})$ sum
to $2$ in its local endomorphism
ring.  They cannot both be
nonunits.  Hence one sign
admits a nonsingular form.
The preceding determinant
tests decide both signs.
Choose the positive sign if
possible and otherwise the
negative sign; the least
valid form code of that sign
is found by a finite search
bounded by the constructed
form.  This defines
$q_i,\varepsilon_i$.
The adjoint on the actual
endomorphism ring descends to
the computed division quotient
$D_i$ and defines $\sigma_i$.

Let $L_i$ be the computed
center of $D_i$, and let
$F_i=L_i^{\sigma_i}$.
The involution fixes $k$ in
the finite-algebra case and
$\kappa$ in the local case.
Compute $F_i$ as the linear
kernel of $\sigma_i-1$ on
$L_i$.  It is a finite field
extension of that ground field.
An explicit tower presentation
is obtained by adjoining
successive basis elements:
their minimal polynomials and
all coordinate maps are finite
linear algebra, and each step
is a field because it is a
subalgebra of the known field.
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-field-arithmetic}
supplies the exact normalized
valuation, finite residue,
and regular completion for
every such fixed center $F_i$.
The hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-hermitian-isometry}
are therefore all verified,
including inseparable ground
extensions.  It decides every
actual residual multiplicity-form
isometry and constructs the
literal groups in (C.3.HW3).

For finite-dimensional actual
endomorphism algebras, nilpotent
radical lifting and the QSS
transfer argument apply.
For the local coefficient case,
Lemma~\ref{C:nonnoetherian-henselian-lifting}
supplies the same exact transfer
without a completeness or
Noetherian assumption.
The resulting semisimple
endomorphism-category blocks
are the computed $D_i$.
Duality exchanges nonself-dual
blocks, giving their hyperbolic
integer generators, and the
reference adjoint on a self-dual
block gives precisely the
$\varepsilon_i$-hermitian
multiplicity forms in (C.3.HW3).

These statements construct
actual representatives.
Lift residual Gram coefficients
through the computed quotient
maps and actual endomorphism
generators.  In the local case
this first uses $\kappa$-linear
coefficient expressions and
then arbitrary supplied residue
lifts; a coefficient-field
section is unnecessary.
Average a lift with its
$\varepsilon_i$-adjoint using
$1/2$, and compose it with
the direct sum of copies of
$q_i$.
It is nonsingular because
its residual Gram matrix is
invertible modulo the radical.
Residual isometries lift to
actual ones by the proved
radical lifting statement;
an enumeration of exact
intertwining isometry matrices
finds them when required.
Least residual Gram-pair
coordinates, with the actual
positive and negative forms
they contain, now decide
all group equalities and
realize every finite coordinate
vector.  No unidentified
isometry relation remains.

\emph{Tensor and exterior operations.}
For finite coefficient algebras,
balanced tensor products and
exterior powers are finite
$k$-linear quotient constructions
with their induced $R$-actions
and finite coactions.
For the local coefficient case,
use their finite free matrices.
The tensor pairing and the
determinant exterior pairing
are exact finite expressions.
They are nonsingular because
the input modules are finite
projective and this is checked
on free localizations.
No factorial is inverted.
Apply the already terminating
decomposition and form algorithm
to these actual outputs.

Virtual products use bilinearity.
For a virtual difference $x-y$,
compute a requested finite
coefficient of
$\lambda_t(x)\lambda_t(y)^{-1}$
by the finite recurrence for
inverting a series with constant
coefficient one.
Only the coefficients up to
the requested degree are used.
This gives all claimed finite
ordinary operation algorithms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proposed: explicit new fields and nonfield bases]
\label{C:henselian-global-new-families}
For every odd prime power $q$, the field
\[
 H_q=\operatorname{Frac}
       \bigl(\mathbf F_q[t]_{(t)}^h\bigr)               \tag{C.3.HW5}
\]
and every supplied finite extension of it
have the preceding ordinary algorithms.
They give complete answers over all
finite-dimensional commutative algebras
over these fields, and over the
henselizations of every finitely presented
algebra localized at a supplied point
with finite residue extension.
Singular and nonreduced local bases
are included.
They also apply to henselizations
of countable valuation domains
with exact decidable finite names,
computable arithmetic and residue
maps to these fields, decidable
divisibility, and computable division
when allowed.  In particular they
apply to
the explicit lexicographic valuation
domains in $k(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$ with
value group $\mathbf Z^r_{\rm lex}$
and residue $k$, for every finite $r\ge1$.

Already $H_q$ is not a $C_1$ field
and is not a global function field.
Thus these are new effective coefficient
families beyond the earlier $C_1$,
finite-global-field and $\mathbf Q$-algebra
cases.  The assertion concerns finite
input data and does not assert an
ordinary algorithm for an infinitely
supplied product of coefficient rings.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The field (C.3.HW5) is the special
case $K=\mathbf F_q(t)$ and the
$t$-adic place of
Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-field-arithmetic}.
Apply Theorem~\ref{C:henselian-global-coefficient-WG}.

For a finitely presented algebra
over a supplied finite extension
$k/H_q$, ordinary polynomial
syzygies over the effective field
$k$, followed by localization,
give all finite matrix kernels.
The full pointed-neighborhood
argument of
Lemma~\ref{C:C1-henselization-access}
uses no $C_1$ hypothesis in this
access step: field arithmetic,
the supplied point and residue
field, and the finite polynomial
systems suffice.  It therefore
verifies the local input hypotheses
even for singular or nonreduced
algebras.
For effective valuation domains
with decidable divisibility and
division when allowed, finite
Smith elimination gives these
kernels, as in
Corollary~\ref{C:lexicographic-valuation-henselization-WG}.
In the stated lexicographic
examples, the least nonzero
monomial of a polynomial determines
its value; exact coefficient
arithmetic over $k$ decides it,
and subtraction of values and
exact field division decide all
the allowed divisions.
The residue is $k$.

To verify the scope assertion,
choose $a\in\mathbf F_q^\times$
nonsquare.  Over $H_q$ the
quaternion norm form is
\[
       X_1^2-aX_2^2-t(X_3^2-aX_4^2).                  \tag{C.3.HW6}
\]
For a nonzero pair $(x,y)$,
$v(x^2-ay^2)=2\min(v(x),v(y))$:
if the two valuations are equal,
cancellation of the leading
residue would make $a$ a square
in $\mathbf F_q$.
The first binary term in
(C.3.HW6), if nonzero, has
even valuation, and the second
has odd valuation.
They cannot cancel.
Thus the four-variable quadratic
form is anisotropic, proving
that the quaternion algebra
$(a,t)$ is division and that
$H_q$ is not $C_1$.

For every $n$ prime to the
characteristic, the polynomial
$X^n-(1+t)$ has simple residue
root $1$ at $t=0$, so its root
belongs to $H_q$ by Hensel's
lemma.  It is irreducible over
$\mathbf F_q(t)$ by Eisenstein
at the prime $t+1$ of
$\mathbf F_q[t]$.
Consequently $H_q$ contains
finite extensions of
$\mathbf F_q(t)$ of unbounded
degree.  It is algebraic over
$\mathbf F_q(t)$ by the
henselization construction,
so it is not finitely generated
as a field over $\mathbf F_q$
and cannot be a global function
field.  Its positive characteristic
also excludes the $\mathbf Q$-algebra
theorem.

As a ring $H_q$ has maximal
ideal zero and residue field
$H_q$ itself.  Thus the earlier
local-ring theorems with finite
or $C_1$ residue do not classify
this field or the new examples
$H_q[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^s)$,
$s\ge2$, and
$H_q[z]_{(z)}^h$.
The ordinary decisions here use
the new regular-completion and
projective-point arguments.
\end{proof}
