\subsection{Complete split groups over global function-field coefficient bases}
\label{C:global-function-WG-subsection}

\begin{theorem}[Proved: canonical split groups and operations over global function fields]
\label{C:global-function-coefficient-WG}
Let $k$ be a supplied finite field tower over
$\mathbf F_q(t)$, where $q$ is odd.
The following coefficient rings $R$ have complete
ordinary terminating classification and operation
algorithms for every split $O_{m,R}$, $m\geq0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every finite-dimensional commutative $k$-algebra
given by its multiplication table;
\item $B^h$, where $B$ is a countable local ring
with exact decidable finite names, computable
arithmetic and residue map, a terminating finite
matrix-kernel algorithm, and residue field a
supplied finite tower over $k$.
\end{enumerate}
There is a decidable literal index set
$\mathscr I_R\subseteq\mathbf N$ for all
indecomposable finite projective rational
$O_{m,R}$-modules.
Isomorphism, indecomposability, duality,
invariant nonsingular form sign, actual
equivariant isometry, and equality in
$WG_{\rm sp}(BO_{m,R})$ are decidable.

For each self-dual index $i$ we compute a
reference form of sign $\varepsilon_i$,
a residue division algebra $D_i$ by its
multiplication table over an explicit
global center, and its adjoint involution
$\sigma_i$.
Then
\[
 WG_{\rm sp}(BO_{m,R})\simeq
   \bigoplus_{i=i^*}
       \mathcal G(D_i,\sigma_i,\varepsilon_i)
   \ \oplus\
   \bigoplus_{\{i,i^*\},\ i\ne i^*}\mathbf Z .             \tag{C.3.GW1}
\]
Here $\mathcal G(D,\sigma,\varepsilon)$ is
the literal decidable set of least finite
pairs of Gram-matrix codes constructed in
Theorem~\ref{C:global-function-hermitian-isometry},
with its computed abelian-group operations.
Thus (C.3.GW1) contains no undecided
division index or hermitian-isometry quotient.
Actual representatives of every finite
coordinate vector are computable.
All products and every specified
exterior coefficient of finite virtual
inputs are computed in finite time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Finite coefficient algebras.}
Let first $R$ be finite dimensional over $k$.
Represent an $R$-module by its finite
$k$-vector space and the matrices of the
action of a supplied $k$-basis of $R$.
These satisfy explicit multiplication
and identity equations.
An $O_m$-coaction uses finitely many
coefficients in the explicitly based
modules $C_d(k)$; its identities and
commutation with the $R$-action are
finite decidable equations.
All finite projective rational
representations have such data.

Projectivity is decided exactly as
in Theorem~\ref{C:artinian_effective_C1}:
from a $k$-basis of the module construct
the canonical $R$-linear surjection
from a finite free $R$-module.
An $R$-linear section is a solution
of a finite affine linear system
over $k$.
The system is solvable if and only
if the module is projective, and
a solution gives a projective
presentation.
Hom modules and invariant forms
are finite $k$-linear kernels.
Invertibility on any such linear
space is the nonvanishing of its
determinant polynomial.
If the underlying $k$-dimension
is $n$, a grid of $n+1$ distinct
elements of the infinite field
$k$ in each coordinate decides
this nonvanishing and constructs
an invertible solution when one
exists.
This decides actual module
isomorphism and each nonsingular
form sign.

The endomorphism algebra $E$
is finite dimensional over $k$.
Lemma~\ref{C:global-function-finite-algebra-algorithms}
computes its radical and an actual
matrix-over-division decomposition
of its semisimple quotient,
including primitive idempotents
and their nilpotent-radical lifts.
Thus indecomposability is decidable:
the quotient must be one division
factor, with matrix size one.
Otherwise its lifted idempotents
split the actual representation.
The summands remain projective,
and their positive $k$-dimensions
decrease.
This is a finite Krull--Schmidt
decomposition algorithm.
It uses no perfectness assumption
on $k$ or on a residue center.

Code all the finite data by
natural numbers.  Retain an
indecomposable exactly when no
smaller valid code is isomorphic
to it.
Every test is now decidable,
and the predecessor set is
finite.
This constructs $\mathscr I_R$
and computes the least index
of every given indecomposable.
The dual is $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,R)$,
computed by its $k$-linear
equations and the antipode;
finite projectivity gives the
usual biduality.
Canonicalization therefore
computes the dual index.

\emph{Henselian coefficient rings.}
Now let $R=B^h$ as in the
second case.
The exact access proof of
Theorem~\ref{C:effective-coherent-henselization-WG}
uses no $C_1$ property:
a pointed standard \'{e}tale
neighborhood is a localization
of a finite free monic algebra
over $B$.
Multiplication in this algebra
and every matrix over it are
finite $B$-matrices.
The supplied kernel algorithm
computes their actual kernels
and annihilators.
A named element is zero in
$R$ exactly when one of its
annihilator generators has
nonzero residue at the chosen
point.
This follows from faithful
flatness of the pointed
local henselization.
Tensor neighborhoods compare
names; flat base change gives
the full kernel over $R$.
These arguments provide the
same exact finite names,
residue lifts and kernel
algorithms for the present
global residue fields.

Finite projective $R$-modules
are free.
Their actual Hom and form
modules are the finite
coefficient-system kernels
just computed.
Their actual residue images
are finite vector spaces over
the residue field $\kappa$.
Finite determinant grids over
the infinite field $\kappa$
decide invertible intertwiners
and nonsingular sign forms;
lifting the successful scalar
coefficients constructs actual
matrices over $R$.
Let $E$ be an actual
endomorphism ring and let
$E_0$ be its image in
$\operatorname{End}_\kappa(M/\mathfrak m_RM)$.
The kernel of $E\to E_0$
lies in $\operatorname{rad}E$,
because every matrix $1-vu$
with $u$ in that kernel
has unit determinant and
an equivariant inverse.
Consequently
\[
       E/\operatorname{rad}E
                  \simeq E_0/\operatorname{rad}E_0 .
                                                               \tag{C.3.GW2}
\]
The finite-algebra algorithm
over the global field $\kappa$
computes the right side with
all its actual division factors.
Lemma~\ref{C:nonnoetherian-henselian-lifting}
applies because the kernel
algorithm makes $E$ finite
over $R$.
It gives semiperfectness
and idempotent lifting through
the kernel of $E\to E_0$.
After a nontrivial residual
idempotent has been computed,
enumeration of actual endomorphisms
finds an idempotent with exactly
that residue.
This is a positive search with
existence proved by the lemma.
Independent residue columns
split its finite free image
and complement.
Rank decreases.
The same finite predecessor
tests as above now give
canonical free indecomposable
indices over $R$.

\emph{Actual forms and their coordinates.}
In either case an indecomposable
has a local endomorphism ring.
If it is self-dual and
$g:M\to M^*$ is an isomorphism,
the endomorphisms obtained from
$g+g^{\mathsf t}$ and
$g-g^{\mathsf t}$ sum, after
multiplication by $g^{-1}$,
to $2$.
They cannot both lie in the
radical.
Thus at least one sign has a
nonsingular invariant form.
The determinant tests decide
both signs.
Choose the positive sign if
available, otherwise the negative
sign, and the least valid form
code of that sign.
A previously constructed form
bounds this last finite search.
This gives $q_i,\varepsilon_i$.
The adjoint on its endomorphisms
induces a computable involution
$\sigma_i$ on the computed
division quotient $D_i$.
It fixes the ground global
field in the finite-algebra
case, and the central residue
field in the henselian case.
Its center is an explicit finite
extension of that global field.

Radical reduction and hermitian
transfer give the factors in
(C.3.GW1).
For a finite coefficient algebra
this is the nilpotent-radical
argument in the checked QSS
theorem
\cite[Propositions 2.4--2.5 and
Theorems 3.2--3.3, pp.\ 271--273]
 {C-QSS1979}.
For $B^h$ it is precisely
Lemma~\ref{C:nonnoetherian-henselian-lifting}.
The hypotheses in each case
have just been verified.
In a self-dual isotypic block
the residual Gram entries are
the images of $q_i^{-1}B_{uv}$
in $D_i$.
Reducing the whole nonsingular
form to the semisimple
endomorphism category proves
these diagonal blocks remain
nonsingular.
Nonisomorphic blocks have
only radical off-diagonal maps.
A dual pair contributes its
common multiplicity and the
hyperbolic generator.

Theorem~\ref{C:global-function-hermitian-isometry}
now decides every residual
form isometry and computes
its canonical finite group
coordinate.
Together with Krull--Schmidt
uniqueness and exact radical
lifting, this decides actual
equivariant isometry and
group equality.
For a requested actual
isometry matrix enumerate
intertwiners and check its
Gram and inverse identities;
the positive decision proves
that this search terminates.

Conversely every residual
hermitian Gram matrix lifts
through the computed quotient:
solve for its coefficients in
the finite actual Hom generators
and lift the residue scalars.
Symmetrizing with $1/2$
enforces its sign.
Its reduction is invertible,
so the lifted matrix is
invertible.
Transfer by $q_i$ produces
an actual symmetric form on
the corresponding isotypic
representation.
The canonical positive and
negative pairs therefore give
actual representatives of all
finite coordinates in (C.3.GW1).

\emph{Ring and exterior operations.}
Tensor and exterior operations
are performed on these actual
representatives, then classified
by the algorithms just proved.
In the finite-algebra case the
balanced tensor product and
the alternating quotient are
finite $k$-linear quotients;
their Gram forms are the
tensor pairing and the
determinant pairing.
In the local case use free
bases and the usual tensor
matrices and exterior minors.
Finite projectivity guarantees
nonsingularity in both cases.
Coefficient degrees are at
most $d+d'$ and $rd$,
respectively.
No factorial is inverted.
Products of virtual inputs
extend bilinearly, and each
specified coefficient of a
negative exterior series is
computed by
$\lambda_t(-x)=\lambda_t(x)^{-1}$.
Every calculation is finite
and every positive search
has its stated existence proof.
This proves the complete
ordinary algorithms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: actual affine and valuation families over global fields]
\label{C:global-function-henselization-WG}
Let $k$ be any supplied finite tower over
$\mathbf F_q(t)$ with $q$ odd.
For every finitely presented commutative
$k$-algebra $S$ and supplied surjection
$S\twoheadrightarrow\kappa$ to a finite
tower field over $k$, all conclusions
of Theorem~\ref{C:global-function-coefficient-WG}
hold over $(S_{\ker\rho})^h$.
They also hold over $V^h$ for every
effective valuation domain $V$ whose
residue field is a supplied such
global function field and whose
residue map and exact divisions
are computable.
This includes arbitrary finite
lexicographic rank in
$k(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Global-field factorization and
exact field arithmetic are
ordinary algorithms by
Lemma~\ref{C:global-function-field-arithmetic}.
The proof of
Lemma~\ref{C:C1-henselization-access}
uses the $C_1$ hypothesis nowhere
in its polynomial syzygy,
annihilator, pointed-neighborhood
or faithful-flatness steps.
Apply exactly those steps over
the present effective field:
they provide finite names and
the full actual matrix kernels
for $(S_{\ker\rho})^h$.
Its residue is the supplied
global field $\kappa$.
The henselian proof of the
preceding theorem therefore
applies directly.
This includes singular and
nonreduced positive-dimensional
$S$.

For $V$, the finite Smith
elimination in
Corollary~\ref{C:lexicographic-valuation-henselization-WG}
uses only total divisibility,
exact divisions and the
domain hypothesis.
It computes every matrix
kernel.
The second case of the
preceding theorem therefore
applies with $B=V$.
For the stated concrete
lexicographic examples,
leading terms compute
valuations, divisibility,
quotients and residues exactly
as in (C.3.NH4), with the
finite constant field
replaced by $k$.
The residue field is $k$,
so no unspecified arithmetic
oracle remains.
\end{proof}
