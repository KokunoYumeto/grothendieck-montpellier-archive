% Independently audited constant-lattice classification; square-zero gate remains open.
\begin{proposition}[Proved: nine lattices at the minimal cubic cyclotomic prime]
\label{C:cyclotomic_C3_lattices}
Let $A=\mathbb Z_3[\zeta]$, where $\zeta$ is a primitive cube
root of unity, put $\pi=\zeta-1$, and let $\Lambda=A[C_3]$.
Every finite $A$-free $\Lambda$-module is a direct sum of exactly
the following nine indecomposable types (with arbitrary
multiplicities):
\begin{enumerate}
\item for each nonempty $S\subseteq\{0,1,2\}$, the lattice
\[
 L_S=\{(a_i)\in A^S: a_i\bmod\pi\text{ are all equal}\},
\]
with generator acting diagonally by $(\zeta^i)_{i\in S}$;
\item the lattice
\[
 Q=\{(a_0,a_1,a_2)\in A^3:
                    a_0+a_1+a_2\equiv0\pmod\pi\},
\]
with the same diagonal action;
\item the regular lattice $\Lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
This is a classification of the constant integral lattices, not
of their square-zero deformations or of the Swan group over
$A\ltimes\mathbb F_3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $B=A^3$, with the group generator acting by
$(1,\zeta,\zeta^2)$. Evaluation embeds $\Lambda$ in $B$.
The three Lagrange numerator polynomials show that the conductor
contains $\pi^2B$, since every difference of two distinct cube
roots is $\pi$ times a unit. Modulo $\pi^2B$, the image is
\[
 \overline\Lambda
 =\mathbb F_3\cdot(1,1,1)
   +\pi\mathbb F_3\cdot(1,1,1)
   +\pi\mathbb F_3\cdot(0,1,-1).
                                                        \tag{C.1.31}
\]
Here $A/\pi^2\simeq\mathbb F_3[\pi]/\pi^2$ because
$3=-\pi^2/(1+\pi)$. The last two vectors span the plane with
coordinate sum zero. No vector $\pi e_i$ is in this image, so
the conductor is exactly $\pi^2B$.

For a lattice $M$, its normalization $BM$ is a direct sum
$\bigoplus_{i=0}^2 A^{r_i}$, and
$\pi^2BM\subseteq M\subseteq BM$. Put
$E=(BM)/\pi BM=E_0\oplus E_1\oplus E_2$ over $\mathbb F_3$.
The image of $M$ modulo $\pi^2BM$ is described by subspaces
\[
 U\subseteq V\subseteq E,\qquad \alpha U\subseteq V,
 \quad \alpha=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,-1),
\]
and a linear graph map $f:U\to E/V$.
The projections $U\to E_i$ are surjective, since $BM$ is the
normalization generated by $M$. Explicitly, $U$ is the image
modulo $\pi$, $\pi V$ is the intersection with $\pi BM$, and a
choice of lifts of $U$ gives $f$. Conversely these data specify
a lattice, by taking the inverse image in $BM$.
A normalization-basis change $1+\pi H$, with $H$ block diagonal
on the three $E_i$, changes $f$ by $H|_U$ modulo $V$.

Suppose $V\ne E$. Choose a nonzero linear functional
$\lambda:E\to\mathbb F_3$ vanishing on $V$, and write its three
components as $\lambda_i$. The equations
$\lambda(U)=\lambda(\alpha U)=0$ imply
\[
 \lambda_0(u_0)=\lambda_1(u_1)=\lambda_2(u_2)=h(u)
 \quad\text{for every }u\in U.
\]
The common functional $h$ is nonzero: otherwise surjectivity of
each projection would make every $\lambda_i$ zero.
Choose $u\in U$ with $h(u)=1$. Split
$E_i=\mathbb F_3u_i\oplus\ker\lambda_i$.
Relative to $E=E^{\circ}\oplus E'$, where
$E^{\circ}=\bigoplus_i\mathbb F_3u_i$, we have
\[
 U=\mathbb F_3u\oplus U',\qquad
 V=\langle u,\alpha u\rangle\oplus V'.
\]
Indeed the projection of $U$ to $E^{\circ}$ is the diagonal line,
and the projection of $V$ is contained in the coordinate-sum-zero
plane, already spanned by $u,\alpha u$.
Moreover $U'$ still surjects onto every $\ker\lambda_i$:
given $v_i\in\ker\lambda_i$, choose $w\in U$ projecting to
$v_i$; then $h(w)=\lambda_i(v_i)=0$, so $w\in U'$.

Use block-diagonal $H$ to remove the cross terms of $f$ for this
splitting. The maps $\mathbb F_3u_i\to\ker\lambda_i$ remove the
$E'/V'$ component of $f(u)$; the maps
$\ker\lambda_i\to\mathbb F_3u_i$ remove the
$E^{\circ}/\langle u,\alpha u\rangle$ component of $f(U')$,
because a functional on $U'\subseteq E'$ extends to $E'$.
Diagonal scalar changes remove the remaining one-dimensional
component of $f(u)$. Thus the data split off the lattice with
$U^{\circ}=\langle(1,1,1)\rangle$,
$V^{\circ}=\langle(1,1,1),(0,1,-1)\rangle$, and zero graph map.
By (C.1.31) this is one regular summand $\Lambda$.
Repeating leaves only the case $V=E$.

Then the graph map vanishes and $M$ is the inverse image of
$U\subseteq E$. This is the elementary three-subspace problem:
put $K_i=\ker(U\to E_i)$, so $\bigcap_iK_i=0$.
Here is its complete decomposition. Split off each pairwise
intersection $K_i\cap K_j$ by a projection whose kernel contains
the remaining $K_l$; its image is contained in both $K_i,K_j$,
so this is a direct-summand projection of the configuration.
When the pairwise intersections are zero, split off the parts
of each $K_i$ outside $K_j+K_l$, using a projection with kernel
containing $K_j+K_l$. What remains satisfies
$K_i\subseteq K_j+K_l$ for all three indices. Then
$K_1\cap K_2=0$, and $K_3\subseteq K_1\oplus K_2$ projects
isomorphically onto each factor. It is the graph of an
isomorphism $K_1\simeq K_2$. Choosing a basis splits this part
into two-dimensional configurations with three distinct kernel
lines. A complement of $K_1+K_2$ has all kernels zero.

Thus the indecomposable configurations are the seven
one-dimensional configurations with at least one zero kernel,
and one two-dimensional configuration with three distinct lines.
The former are exactly the $L_S$; the latter is $Q$.
The one-dimensional configurations are indecomposable, and three
distinct lines cannot all be coordinate summands of a decomposition
of a two-dimensional space, so $Q$ is indecomposable.
The regular module is indecomposable because $\Lambda$ is local:
its reduction modulo $\pi$ is
$\mathbb F_3[t]/(t-1)^3$. It is not one of the other types,
which satisfy $\pi BM\subseteq M$, whereas $\Lambda$ does not.
The seven supports and the dimensions of $U$ distinguish the
remaining types. Finally the endomorphism ring of any lattice
is finite over the complete DVR $A$, hence semiperfect; the usual
idempotent-lifting proof of Krull--Schmidt gives uniqueness of the
indecomposable multiplicities.
\end{proof}

This agrees with the finite-type criterion in
\cite[Proposition~1.2, printed p.~340]{C-Wiegand1991}:
the Artinian normalization pair has three generators and
$(\mathfrak mB+\Lambda)/\Lambda$ has length one.
That proposition applies to mixed characteristic; its later
classification of complete curve equations is not being imported.
The explicit nine-type decomposition above has been proved directly.
