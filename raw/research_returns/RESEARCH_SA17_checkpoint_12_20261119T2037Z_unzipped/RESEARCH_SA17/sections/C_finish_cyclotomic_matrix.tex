\begin{proposition}[Proved: all diagonal-character infinitesimal cyclic actions]
\label{C:cyclotomic_matrix_cancellation}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{C:cyclotomic_torsion_cancellation},
let $D=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{a_1},\ldots,\zeta^{a_d})$
and let $U\in M_d(I)$. The matrix $D+U$ defines a free
$k[C_p]$-module $M_{D,U}$, and
\[
 [M_{D,U}]-[M_{D,0}]=\delta(\operatorname{tr}U).
\]
In particular its induced relative class in $R_k(\mathfrak S_p)$
is zero. Thus this cancellation applies to every infinitesimal
cyclic action whose constant integral representation is a direct
sum of the characters $\zeta^a$; it does not assert such a
splitting for an arbitrary integral lattice.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every matrix with entries in $I$ is fixed by multiplication by
$D$ on either side, and products of two such matrices are zero.
Consequently $(D+U)^p=D^p+pU=1$.
Put $\delta_D(U)=[M_{D,U}]-[M_{D,0}]$ and retain the constant
line class $z=[Z]$ from the previous proposition.
For arbitrary $S,T\in M_d(I)$, consider
\[
 \mathcal T_S=\begin{pmatrix}D+S&1\\0&\zeta D\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_{S,T}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\T-S&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All blocks have size $d$. The upper-right block of
$\mathcal T_S^p$ is
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(D+S)^i(\zeta D)^{p-1-i}
 =D^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\zeta^{p-1-i}
        +S\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}i=0,
\]
and the diagonal blocks are one. The exact conjugation identity is
\[
 \mathcal P_{S,T}^{-1}\mathcal T_S\mathcal P_{S,T}
 =\begin{pmatrix}D+T&1\\0&\zeta D+S-T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The lower-left block vanishes because $D(T-S)=(T-S)D=T-S$
and $(\zeta-1)(T-S)=0$. Both triangular presentations give
admissible free-submodule sequences, so
\[
 \delta_D(S)=\delta_D(T)+z\delta_D(S-T).
\]
The same odd-order argument as before gives
$z\delta_D(U)=\delta_D(U)$ and additivity of $\delta_D$ on
$M_d(I)$.

For $i\ne j$, the representation $D+xE_{ij}$ has a free invariant
flag with the same diagonal character factors as $D$, by ordering
basis vector $i$ before basis vector $j$. Hence
$\delta_D(xE_{ij})=0$.
Splitting $U$ into its matrix units and then splitting its diagonal
representation as a direct sum gives
\[
 \delta_D(U)=\sum_i z^{a_i}\delta(U_{ii})
             =\delta(\operatorname{tr}U).
\]
Here the last equality uses additivity and $z\delta=\delta$
from Proposition~\ref{C:cyclotomic_torsion_cancellation}.
The induced vanishing follows from that proposition as well.
\end{proof}
