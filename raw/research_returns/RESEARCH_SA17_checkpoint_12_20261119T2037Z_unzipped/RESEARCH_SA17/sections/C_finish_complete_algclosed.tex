\subsection{Complete local coefficients with algebraically closed residue field}
\label{C:complete-algclosed-subsection}

We give a second positive-dimensional coefficient family.  The answer is
an explicit set of compatible coefficient sequences and eventual
finite-level formulas.  It does not assert an ordinary terminating
algorithm on arbitrary infinite formal series.

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a complete noetherian local algebra over a
countable algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p>2$, and
assume that its residue map identifies $R/\mathfrak m$ with $k$ through
the specified coefficient field.  Write $R_a=R/\mathfrak m^a$ for
$a\geq1$.  Choose compatible $k$-bases: a basis of $R_a$ is the initial
part of the coordinates for $R_{a+1}$, with the remaining coordinates a
basis of $\mathfrak m^a/\mathfrak m^{a+1}$.  Such choices can be made by
linear splittings.  Every $R_a$ is a finite-dimensional local $k$-algebra.
For the finite computations below its multiplication table, quotient maps
and these splittings are supplied.  Fix a repetition-free enumeration of
$k$, and order its elements by their positions in that enumeration.
Exact arithmetic, equality and univariate factorization over $k$ are
assumed for those computations.  In particular these data exist for
$k=\overline{\mathbf F}_p$ and the usual explicitly presented formal
power-series quotients, with their finite truncations supplied.

We use the finite free coefficient coalgebras of
Lemma~\ref{C:integral-orthogonal-coalgebras}, base changed to $R$.
Their bases make an $R$-free rational representation of rank $r$ and
coefficient degree at most $d$ an actual finite tuple of $r$-square
matrices, satisfying the unit and multiplication equations for $C_d^*$.
All congruences of tuples below mean these coefficient coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Proved: finite detection of morphisms and decompositions]
\label{C:complete_Hom_detection}
Let $R$ be any complete noetherian local ring with $2$ invertible.
For two finite free rational $O_{m,R}$-modules $M,N$, there is an
integer $c\geq0$ such that, for every $b\geq1$, every intertwiner
modulo $\mathfrak m^{b+c}$ agrees modulo $\mathfrak m^b$ with an
actual intertwiner over $R$.  Consequently
\[
 M\simeq N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 M\otimes R_a\simeq N\otimes R_a\quad\hbox{for all }a.
                                                               \tag{C.3.AC1}
\]
Moreover, for any fixed finite list of modules, its indecomposable
types, their distinctness and their multiplicities in every module
on the list agree with those at every sufficiently large finite
level.  Every global indecomposable has indecomposable reduction
at all sufficiently large levels.  If its reduction at one level
is indecomposable, then it is globally indecomposable.
No uniform bound for the sufficient level is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose one coefficient coalgebra containing all coefficients in
question.  The intertwiner condition is the kernel of a map of
finite free $R$-modules
\[
 \Phi:\operatorname{Mat}_{s\times r}(R)
       \longrightarrow\operatorname{Mat}_{s\times r}(R)^h,
       \qquad X\longmapsto(T_iX-XS_i)_i.
\]
The Artin--Rees lemma applied to the image of $\Phi$ gives $c$ with
\[
 \Phi^{-1}(\mathfrak m^{b+c}\operatorname{Mat}(R)^h)
       \subseteq\ker\Phi+\mathfrak m^b\operatorname{Mat}(R).
                                                               \tag{C.3.AC2}
\]
Indeed, an element of the left side has the same image as an
element of $\mathfrak m^b\operatorname{Mat}(R)$; their difference
is in the kernel.  This is exactly the finite-module form recorded
in \cite[Section 15.4, Tag 07VD, introductory Artin--Rees
inclusion]{C-CompleteArtinRees}.
Lift a matrix over $R_{b+c}$ arbitrarily to $R$ and apply
(C.3.AC2).  This proves the assertion about intertwiners.
For $b=1$, an invertible approximate intertwiner becomes an
actual intertwiner with the same invertible residue matrix,
hence an actual isomorphism.  This proves (C.3.AC1).

Here are the ring-theoretic details needed for the final assertions.
For a finite direct sum $V$ of the modules under consideration, put
$E=\operatorname{End}(V)$ and
$I=E\cap\mathfrak m\operatorname{Mat}(R)$.
The ring $E$ is a finite $R$-module and is complete for the
$\mathfrak m$-adic topology.  The ideal $I$ lies in its Jacobson
radical: if $x\in I$ and $y\in E$, the matrix $1-yx$ is invertible,
and its inverse commutes with every action matrix, so belongs to
$E$.  The $I$-adic and $\mathfrak m$-adic topologies agree.  One
inclusion follows from $\mathfrak mE\subseteq I$; for the other,
\[
 I^a\subseteq E\cap\mathfrak m^a\operatorname{Mat}(R)
                  \subseteq\mathfrak m^{a-c'}E
\]
for large $a$, by Artin--Rees for the inclusion of $E$.
Thus idempotents lift from $E/I$ to $E$.  Explicitly, for an
approximate idempotent $e$ the Newton correction
$e-(2e-1)^{-1}(e^2-e)$ squares the error ideal; its iterations
converge in $E$.  The inverse exists since
$(2e-1)^2=1+4(e^2-e)$.

The quotient $F=E/I$ is the image of $E$ in
$\operatorname{End}_{R/\mathfrak m}(V/\mathfrak mV)$,
a finite-dimensional algebra.  In particular $E$ is semiperfect:
first lift primitive idempotents across the nilpotent radical of
$F$, and then across $I$.  They give the Krull--Schmidt
decomposition, with local endomorphism rings and unique
indecomposable multiplicities.

Let $E_a=\operatorname{End}_{R_a}(V\otimes R_a)$ and let
$F_a$ be its image in the same residue matrix ring.  Equation
(C.3.AC2), with $b=1$, proves $F_a=F$ for all sufficiently
large $a$.  The kernel of $E_a\to F_a$ is nilpotent, whereas
$I\subseteq\operatorname{rad}E$.  Hence scalar extension
induces, for those levels, an isomorphism
\[
       E/\operatorname{rad}E
          \ \simeq\ E_a/\operatorname{rad}E_a.          \tag{C.3.AC3}
\]
Apply the same assertion to $V$ enlarged by its finitely many
global indecomposable summands.  Primitive factors in (C.3.AC3)
then show that each summand stays indecomposable, distinct ones
stay distinct, and all their displayed multiplicities are
unchanged.  Finally a nontrivial global idempotent cannot reduce
to $0$ or $1$ over the local ring: its two projective summands
are free of positive rank.  Thus an indecomposable reduction at
any level implies global indecomposability.  This proves all claims.
\end{proof}

For context, uniform finite-level comparison for lattices over
a DVR order with separable generic algebra is the classical
Maranda theorem, stated in
\cite[Introduction, pp.\ 1--2]{C-CompleteMarandaContext}.
The preceding proof uses the displayed Artin--Rees inclusion
with an object-dependent constant; it makes no separability
assumption on a bounded coefficient algebra.  The explicit
example below shows that uniformity fails in the present
modular rational-representation setting.

For fixed $(r,d)$ and $a$, order the coefficient coordinates
lexicographically, giving all earlier $\mathfrak m$-adic digits
priority over later digits, and within a digit using a fixed order
of the matrix entries and coalgebra basis.  A finite tuple of
elements of the enumerated field is well ordered by this order.
For an actual tuple $T_a$ over $R_a$, let $\gamma_a(T_a)$ be
the least tuple in its $\operatorname{GL}_r(R_a)$-orbit.
This is an explicit finite procedure: for each successive
coordinate enumerate possible field values in order and test
whether an invertible conjugating matrix with that specified
prefix exists.  Its equations over $R_a$ are finitely many
polynomial equations and one determinant inequation over $k$.
Lemma~\ref{C:canonical-polynomial-elimination} decides each test.
A value is eventually found because the current prefix has an
extension in the nonempty orbit.  There are only finitely many
coordinates at this level.

\begin{theorem}[Proved: canonical coefficient sequences over the complete local base]
\label{C:complete_algclosed_indices}
In the stated coefficient and field-access model, all
indecomposable finite projective rational $O_{m,R}$-modules have
the following explicit parameter set, with no isomorphism
quotient remaining.  For every $r\geq1,d\geq0$, take compatible
sequences of coefficient tuples $(T_a)_{a\geq1}$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the finite coalgebra equations hold at every level;
\item $T_a=\gamma_a(T_a)$ at every level;
\item the minimal coefficient degree is $d$: if $d>0$, some
      level has a nonzero coefficient outside $C_{d-1}$;
\item the module defined by $T_a$ is indecomposable at some level.
\end{enumerate}
Each finite-level condition is an explicit polynomial test over
$k$, with the indicated finite enumeration for $\gamma_a$.
The union of these sets, denoted $\mathscr I_R$, has exactly
one member for every global indecomposable.  Its representative
is the entrywise inverse limit of the tuples.
Every finite prefix of the canonical sequence of a given
representation is computable from that finite truncation.
The full parameter set consists of infinite sequences, and is
not asserted to have a natural-number enumeration.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The coalgebra is finite free and $R=\varprojlim R_a$.
Consequently a compatible sequence satisfying its equations
gives a unique actual coaction over $R$.  The minimal-degree
condition follows from the separation of the $\mathfrak m$-adic
topology.  Condition (4) is exactly indecomposability, by
Lemma~\ref{C:complete_Hom_detection}; the finite test is the
absence of a nontrivial commuting idempotent, as in
Theorem~\ref{C:artinian_algclosed_templates}.

Reduction maps each orbit over $R_{a+1}$ onto the orbit over
$R_a$, because every invertible matrix over $R_a$ lifts to an
invertible matrix over $R_{a+1}$.  Since earlier digits have
priority, the least tuples $\gamma_a(T\bmod\mathfrak m^a)$
are therefore compatible.  Their inverse limit $T^{\rm can}$
is a representation isomorphic to $T$ at every finite level.
Equation (C.3.AC1) proves that it is actually isomorphic to $T$.
Thus every global isomorphism class has a member in the stated
set.  If two canonical sequences give isomorphic modules,
their truncations are in the same finite orbit and hence are
equal, level by level.  They are the same sequence.

All finite projective modules over the local ring $R$ are
free.  Direct summands of representations are again rational
and have coefficient degree at most that of the original
module, because their coactions are obtained by constant
$R$-linear inclusions and projections.  Thus the construction
covers every object and every indecomposable summand in the
required category.  The finite-prefix procedure was proved
above, completing the theorem.
\end{proof}

Duality acts on $\mathscr I_R$ by applying the antipode and
matrix transpose, then taking the canonical sequence.  A
self-dual index has symmetric or alternating type.  Let
$\mathscr I_s,\mathscr I_a$ denote these two sets, and choose
one element of each nonself-dual pair to form $\mathscr I_h$.
The latter choice can be the least of the two coefficient
sequences in the first-differing-digit order.  If dimensions
or degrees differ, compare them first.  Equality of sequences,
self-duality and these types have their exact finite-level
meaning from the proofs below; they are not silently assumed
to have uniform terminating decision procedures.

\begin{theorem}[Proved: complete split orthogonal group and eventual operation formulas]
\label{C:complete_algclosed_WG}
For every complete local coefficient ring $R$ in this
subsection and every $m\geq0$, the split group is free abelian
on the following explicitly indexed generators:
\[
 W_G(BO_{m,R})=
   \bigoplus_{i\in\mathscr I_s}\mathbf Z[U_i,q_i]
   \ \oplus\!
   \bigoplus_{i\in\mathscr I_a\cup\mathscr I_h}
                         \mathbf Z[H(U_i)].             \tag{C.3.AC4}
\]
Here $q_i$ is any invariant nonsingular symmetric form on
$U_i$; its isometry class is unique.  Forgetting the forms
is injective, with images $[U_i]$, $2[U_i]$, and
$[U_i]+[U_i^*]$, respectively.

All tensor products and exterior operations in (C.3.AC4)
are determined by eventual finite-level computations as
follows.  Form the actual coaction of the tensor product or
exterior power.  For each candidate global indecomposable
$U_j$, its multiplicity is the eventual constant multiplicity
of $(U_j)_a$ in the resulting module over $R_a$, at the
levels where $(U_j)_a$ is indecomposable.  These finite-level
multiplicities are computed by the finite coefficient-algebra
algorithm already proved.  For an orthogonal generator of
alternating type divide that multiplicity by $2$; for a
symmetric or paired generator take it unchanged.  The
resulting coefficient function has finite support and is
exactly the requested product or exterior coefficient.
Virtual exterior powers follow from
$\lambda_t(x-y)=\lambda_t(x)/\lambda_t(y)$.

This is an explicit inverse-limit classification with
eventually constant formulas, not a claim that their
stabilization level can be recognized from arbitrary formal
series input.  It applies, for example, to every
$\overline{\mathbf F}_p[[x_1,\ldots,x_s]]/I$ with its
finite truncations supplied, including singular and
nonreduced rings of positive dimension.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The semiperfectness argument in
Lemma~\ref{C:complete_Hom_detection} supplies Krull--Schmidt
decomposition in the additive category of finite free
rational representations.  All idempotents split in that
category, and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,R)$ is a strong duality.
For $E=\operatorname{End}(M)$, the ideal $\mathfrak mE$ is
contained in $J=\operatorname{rad}E$; since $E/\mathfrak mE$
is finite-dimensional, some power $J^b$ is contained in
$\mathfrak mE$.  Hence $E$ is also $J$-adically complete.
These verify all categorical hypotheses of
\cite[Theorems 2.2, 3.2 and 3.3, pp.\ 269--273]{C-QSS1979}.

For an indecomposable, its endomorphism residue division
algebra is finite-dimensional over $k$, since it is a
quotient of $E/\mathfrak mE$.  It is $k$ itself: each
element has a minimal polynomial over the algebraically
closed central field, which splits; a product of linear
factors vanishing in a division ring forces one factor
to vanish.  An induced adjoint involution fixes this
coefficient field and is therefore the identity.
Every self-dual indecomposable has an invariant nonsingular
form of one of the two signs.  In fact if $g:U\to U^*$
is an isomorphism, the two endomorphisms obtained from
$g+g^*$ and $g-g^*$ sum to twice a unit in its local
endomorphism ring, so one of them is a unit.  They cannot
both be nonsingular because the residue involution is
trivial and $2$ is invertible; this is also
\cite[Proposition 2.5, p.\ 271]{C-QSS1979}.

Hermitian reduction and transfer in the cited theorems
now leave only symmetric and alternating forms over $k$.
The former are classified by dimension and the latter
by half-dimension.  Nonself-dual pairs are hyperbolic.
Thus the three kinds of generators in (C.3.AC4) give all
isometry classes with the stated multiplicities.  They
also show cancellation and injectivity of forgetting
forms, proving the complete additive assertion.  This
uses the orthogonal-sum group, without imposing any
additional metabolic relation.

For clarity, the types themselves can be specified entirely
by finite-level data.  An index is self-dual exactly when
its finite-level modules are self-dual for all $a$, by
(C.3.AC1).  At all sufficiently large $a$ they are
indecomposable.  The reduction of a global symmetric or
alternating reference form has the same sign there, and
the preceding uniqueness of sign over $R_a$ proves that
its type is the eventual finite-level type.  If an index
is not self-dual, its dual is a distinct global module and
the two are distinct at all sufficiently large levels by
Lemma~\ref{C:complete_Hom_detection}.

Let $T$ be any actual tensor product or exterior power
appearing in the assertion.  It has a finite global
Krull--Schmidt decomposition.  Apply the finite-list part
of Lemma~\ref{C:complete_Hom_detection} to $T$, its
summands, and the particular candidate $U_j$.  Its
multiplicity at every sufficiently large finite level
is then exactly its global multiplicity.  This proves
existence and the value of the asserted eventual constant,
including value zero for a candidate absent globally.
Only finitely many of these values are nonzero, by the
global decomposition.  Injectivity of forgetting forms
and the displayed images of the generators give precisely
the coefficient divisions in the theorem; those divisions
are integral because the actual form already exists.

Tensor coactions multiply coefficients in $C_dC_e$, and
the $r$-th exterior coaction has coefficients in $C_{rd}$.
Exterior powers use determinant minors on the underlying
free modules, so do not require $r!$ to be invertible.
The Gram matrices are the tensor-product and determinant
Gram matrices.  All finite-level reductions are the actual
balanced tensor and exterior operations over $R_a$.
Their canonical decompositions are supplied by
Theorems~\ref{C:artinian_algclosed_templates}
and~\ref{C:artinian_algclosed_WG}.  Bilinearity and formal
power-series inversion treat virtual inputs, with finitely
many operations needed for each specified exterior degree.
This proves the full ring and exterior-operation formulas
and also their stated limits of effectivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Proved: no fixed truncation bound even in rank five]
\label{C:complete_unbounded_detection}
Put $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_3$ and $R=k[[t]]$.
There is a fixed finite coefficient degree $d$ such that, for
every $b\geq1$, a rational $O_{3,R}$-module $E_b$ has rank five
and coefficient degree at most $d$, is indecomposable over $R$,
but has decomposable reduction modulo $t^a$ for every $a\leq b$.
Thus no stabilization level depending only on rank, coefficient
degree and this fixed ring can replace the object-dependent
level of Lemma~\ref{C:complete_Hom_detection}.

Moreover there is no uniform algorithm deciding equality in
$W_G(BO_{3,R})$ from coefficient-stream presentations of
rational coaction and Gram matrices alone.  Here a coefficient
stream returns any requested coefficient of a formal series;
an oracle deciding equality of entire formal series is not
part of that input model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $V=k^2$ be the standard $SL_2$-module, with polynomial
coordinates $x,y$.  In $E=\operatorname{Sym}^4V$ the subspace
\[
       W=\langle x^4,x^3y,xy^3,y^4\rangle
\]
is stable, because it is the image of the natural map
$V\otimes V^{(1)}\to\operatorname{Sym}^4V$.
We verify directly that $W$ is simple.  Its torus weights
$4,2,-2,-4$ are distinct characters, and each weight space
has dimension one.  Every nonzero submodule contains a
weight vector.  The substitution $y\mapsto y+s x$ carries
any one of the last three weight vectors to a polynomial
with a nonzero $x^4$ coefficient for some $s$, whereas
the opposite substitution on $x^4$ has all four weight
vectors as its coefficients:
\[
       (x+s y)^4=x^4+s x^3y+s^3xy^3+s^4y^4.
\]
Torus weight projection and the infinitude of $k$ therefore
give the whole of $W$ from any nonzero submodule.

The quotient $E/W$ is trivial.  Indeed the coefficient
of $x^2y^2$ in $(a x+b y)^2(c x+d y)^2$ is
$a^2d^2+abcd+b^2c^2=(ad-bc)^2=1$ in characteristic three.
The extension
$0\to W\to E\to\mathbf1\to0$ is nonsplit.
A torus-invariant lift of its quotient vector would have
to be $x^2y^2$, because the other four weights are nonzero.
But $y\mapsto y+s x$ changes this vector by
$2s x^3y+s^2x^4$, which is not identically zero.
Since the two composition factors are simple and distinct,
this nonsplit extension is indecomposable.

In the decomposition of its underlying vector space as
$W\oplus kx^2y^2$, write the action as
\[
          \rho(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_W(g)&c(g)\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The identity for $c$ is the usual linear cocycle identity.
For any $h\in R$ it therefore remains an action after
replacing $c(g)$ by $h c(g)$.  Call this module $E_h$.
The central $\mu_2$ acts trivially on it, so the action
descends to $PGL_2\simeq SO_3$ and then extends to $O_3$
along $g\mapsto\det(g)g$, as in the explicit construction
of Theorem~\ref{C:BO_field_lambda}.
The coaction coefficient functions lie in one fixed finite
coalgebra, independently of $h$.

If $h\ne0$, after extension to the fraction field of $R$
the module is isomorphic to $E$ by scaling the last basis
vector by $h$.  The same elementary weight proof proves
indecomposability there; hence $E_h$ is indecomposable
over $R$, since a nonzero free summand cannot disappear
in the fraction field.  For $h=t^b$, every reduction
modulo $t^a$ with $a\leq b$ is $W\oplus\mathbf1$.
This proves the first assertion.

For the algorithmic assertion, given a Turing machine
$e$, define a coefficient stream $h_e$ as follows:
if its first halt occurs at step $b\geq1$, set $h_e=t^b$;
if it never halts, set $h_e=0$.
The coefficient of $t^b$ is computed by simulating just
$b$ steps and checking whether that is the first halt.
Thus these streams and the associated coactions $E_{h_e}$
are uniformly computable.  Their inverse coactions and
hyperbolic Gram matrices are computable streams as well.

Consider the actual difference
\[
    \delta_e=[H(E_{h_e})]-[H(W\oplus\mathbf1)]
                      \quad\hbox{in }W_G(BO_{3,R}).
\]
It is zero if the machine does not halt, because $h_e=0$.
If the machine halts, extension to the fraction field
and then its algebraic closure gives the hyperbolic class
of an indecomposable nonsplit extension, minus the
hyperbolic class of the two simple summands.
Forgetting forms distinguishes them by Krull--Schmidt:
the indecomposables $E,E^*$ have dimension five, whereas
the summands of $W\oplus W^*\oplus\mathbf1^2$ have
dimensions four and one.  Therefore $\delta_e\ne0$.
A uniform equality decision procedure in the stated
coefficient-stream model would decide the halting problem,
contrary to the same diagonal argument used in
Theorem~\ref{C:locally-finite-equality-obstruction}.
This proves the precise obstruction claimed.  It neither
rules out additional exact-ring oracles nor changes the
canonical sequence classification above.
The exact polynomial certificate
\texttt{code/C\_finish\_complete\_detection.py} verifies all
25 entries of the symmetric-fourth-power action identity,
all 25 entries after scaling the cocycle, and the 25-entry
generic intertwiner.  It also checks all 36 pairs
$1\leq a\leq b\leq8$ of the stated truncation comparison.
Its JSON output records 0.696514 seconds.  The all-$b$
and undecidability assertions follow from the written
proof and are not inferred from these finite checks.
\end{proof}
