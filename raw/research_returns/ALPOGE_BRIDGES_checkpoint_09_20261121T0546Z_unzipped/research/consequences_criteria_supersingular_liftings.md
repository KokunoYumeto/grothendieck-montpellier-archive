# Supersingular abelian generators and the precisely scoped 2026 lifting theorem

## CC-XC22 — Finite-field consequences of geometric supersingular abelian Chow type

Let \(X/\mathbf F_q\) be smooth projective of pure dimension. Suppose that \(h(X_{\bar{\mathbf F}_q})\) belongs to the rational Chow category generated by supersingular abelian varieties and Tate motives, closed under sums, tensor products, duals and direct summands. Then:

1. \(h(X)\) belongs to the rational Chow abelian--Artin category over \(\mathbf F_q\), so its fixed-target forward-cycle search terminates.
2. For \(X\), every finite product of varieties satisfying this same hypothesis, and every finite extension of the base field, rational, \(\ell\)-adic homological and numerical equivalence agree in every codimension.
3. The rational cycle-class map, tensored with \(\mathbf Q_\ell\), is an isomorphism onto Frobenius-invariant even cohomology with the corresponding Tate twist, for every \(\ell\ne p\).
4. Frobenius acts semisimply in every cohomological degree.

These conclusions do not require the general or all-abelian Tate conjecture. If odd cohomology is nonzero, the conclusion is not that \(h(X)\) is Artin--Tate.

**Complete deduction.** The geometric Chow hypothesis supplies a finite presentation as a summand of a finite sum of twisted abelian-variety motives: expand tensor products as products of abelian varieties, and use their usual Chow duality for the dual factors. The projection, inclusion and their rational-equivalence witnesses descend to a common finite field extension. The finite-field induction and trace-retraction construction of CC-XC16 then puts \(h(X)\) in the actual Chow abelian--Artin category over \(\mathbf F_q\). This proves the asserted cycle existence and search termination.

For the stronger comparison assertions, use the more precise published result of Fu--Li (2021, Corollary 2.12(i), (ii), (iv), p. 160; proof pp. 160--161). Over the algebraic closure, for a variety \(Y\) satisfying the hypothesis, it gives

\[
 h(Y_{\bar k})\simeq
 \bigoplus_i\mathbf1(-i)^{b_{2i}(Y)}
 \oplus
 \bigoplus_i h^1(E)(-i)^{b_{2i+1}(Y)/2},
\tag{CC-XC.U1}
\]

where \(E/\bar k\) is a supersingular elliptic curve. This is a Chow-motive decomposition, and the cited corollary identifies the algebraically trivial part of each rational Chow group with a finite sum of copies of \(E(\bar k)\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Q\).

An elliptic curve over \(\bar{\mathbf F}_q\) is defined over a finite extension of \(\mathbf F_q\), and each of its geometric points is rational over a further finite extension. Every such point therefore lies in a finite group. Hence

\[
               E(\bar{\mathbf F}_q)\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Q=0.
\tag{CC-XC.U2}
\]

It follows from (CC-XC.U1) and the cited corollary that

\[
 \mathrm{CH}^j(Y_{\bar k})_{\mathbf Q}\otimes\mathbf Q_\ell
          \simeq H^{2j}(Y_{\bar k},\mathbf Q_\ell(j)).
\tag{CC-XC.U3}
\]

In particular, the geometric rational Chow group is finite-dimensional and has a finite-image Galois action: finitely many cycles forming a basis are defined over one finite extension.

The rational restriction--corestriction argument in CC-XC20 gives
\[
 \mathrm{CH}^j(Y)_{\mathbf Q}
     \simeq \mathrm{CH}^j(Y_{\bar k})_{\mathbf Q}^{\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)}.
\tag{CC-XC.U4}
\]
Taking invariants commutes with tensoring by \(\mathbf Q_\ell\) for this finite group action. Applying it to (CC-XC.U3) proves assertion 3. The even-degree intersection pairing is perfect on geometric rational Chow groups by (CC-XC.U3) and Poincaré duality. Averaging a complementary class over the finite Galois group proves that it remains perfect on invariants. This makes the numerical kernel of the rational Chow group zero; the rational--homological--numerical implications give assertion 2. Tensor products and finite base extensions preserve the geometric supersingular abelian Chow hypothesis, so this argument applies to all the products specified in the statement.

Finally the Chow presentation over \(\mathbf F_q\) from the first paragraph realizes \(H^*(X)\) as a direct summand of a finite sum of tensor products of abelian-variety cohomology, Artin representations and Tate twists. Frobenius is semisimple on \(H^1\) of an abelian variety: Tate (1966, Theorem 2(a), p. 140) identifies \(\mathbf Q[\pi]\) with the centre of its semisimple rational endomorphism algebra, so the minimal polynomial of \(\pi\) is squarefree. Abelian cohomology is the exterior algebra on \(H^1\), and exterior products of a diagonalizable operator are diagonalizable. Finite-order Artin actions, Tate twists, tensor products, sums and direct summands preserve semisimplicity. This proves assertion 4.

The fixed-target algorithm can also be run entirely in this supersingular sector. In degree \(i>0\), every Frobenius eigenvalue has all normalized crystalline slopes \(i/2\), by the geometric Chow presentation. Each weight-one Honda root used in CC-XC11/14 therefore has all slopes \(1/2\); its Honda abelian variety is supersingular. Degree zero uses only Artin factors. The target construction, its duality, and the required numerical Hom calculations thus use only products of supersingular abelian varieties and finite étale schemes. Assertions 2 and 3 just proved supply the required Tate surjectivity and numerical--homological equality on these products. This verifies search termination without invoking the all-abelian input. \(\square\)

**Source-consistency note.** The explicit multiplicities printed in Fu--Li's Lemma 2.8, p. 158, contain an error: its claimed rank ten for \(\operatorname{Sym}^4 h^1(E)\) cannot be a direct summand of the stated rank-nine \((\operatorname{Sym}^2 h^1(E))^{\otimes2}\). In the source's convention, in which \(\operatorname{Sym}^2 h^1(E)\) has rank three, the correct even and odd multiplicities are \(2j+1\) and \(j+1\), respectively. Its qualitative argument still gives a sum of Tate motives in even degree and a sum of twists of \(h^1(E)\) in odd degree. Theorem 2.9 and Corollary 2.12 then obtain the Betti-number multiplicities used in (CC-XC.U1) directly from realization; the incorrect Lemma 2.8 counts are not used here.

## CC-XC23 — Actual cycle generators from the 2026 lifting theorem

**External input and version.** Fu--Huang--Li's *Supersingular Tate conjecture for irreducible symplectic varieties of known types: I*, arXiv:2609.38801v2, was submitted on **20 October 2026 at 07:58:38 UTC**, after v1 of 30 September 2026. The theorem cited here is **Theorem 1.9, p. 5** of v2. The downloaded v2 text agrees with the author-hosted 59-page version after removing only the arXiv header and whitespace.

Let \(X/\mathbf F_q\) be smooth projective of dimension \(d\). Apply the following hypotheses to \(X_{\bar k}\):

- It is irreducible symplectic in the source's sense: its geometric étale fundamental group is trivial and its global two-forms are spanned by a closed nowhere-degenerate two-form (Definition 2.1, p. 7).
- It is **excellent**: there is a smooth projective lifting over a finite extension DVR of \(W(\bar k)\), with irreducible-symplectic geometric generic fibre, all Hodge numbers unchanged, and a relatively ample line bundle whose restriction is a primitive polarization of \(X_{\bar k}\). This is Definitions 1.6, p. 4, and 2.2/2.6, pp. 8--9.
- It is Artin-supersingular, meaning that its formal Brauer group is the formal additive group. In this excellent setting the condition is equivalent to slope one for its second crystalline cohomology (Definition 2.14 and Proposition 2.15, p. 11).
- \(p>d\).

Under these hypotheses the inspected preprint gives the following geometric Chow generators:

| Deformation type of the excellent lift | Additional condition | Conclusion of Theorem 1.9 over \(\bar k\) | Finite-field consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(K3^{[n]}\) | None beyond the displayed hypotheses | Tate Chow motive | CC-XC20: actual Artin--Tate motive over \(\mathbf F_q\), with all-products cycle comparison and Tate surjectivity. |
| \(\mathrm{OG10}\) | \(\sigma_0\ne12\) | Tate Chow motive | The same conclusion from CC-XC20. |
| \(\mathrm{OG6}\) | \(\sigma_0\ne4\) | Tate Chow motive | The same conclusion from CC-XC20. |
| \(\mathrm{Kum}^{n}\) | \(\sigma_0\ne3\) | Supersingular abelian Chow motive | CC-XC22: actual abelian--Artin Chow membership, all-products cycle comparison, Tate surjectivity and semisimple Frobenius. Odd cohomology need not vanish. |

Here \(\sigma_0\) is the source's Artin invariant. It is specified by the \(p\)-primary discriminant group \((\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z)^{2\sigma_0}\) of the crystalline Tate lattice with its Beauville--Bogomolov form; see §5.1, Proposition 5.1, p. 26. It is not being replaced by the Artin invariant of an unspecified auxiliary surface.

**Complete deduction of the finite-field column.** In the first three rows, the preprint supplies exactly the geometric Tate Chow input of CC-XC20. In the last row it supplies exactly the geometric supersingular abelian Chow input of CC-XC22. Apply those theorems. Their proofs descend finite cycle data and actual splitting identities; consequently they construct the required motive inclusion over \(\mathbf F_q\) even when the lifting or the geometric generating abelian varieties were initially given only over \(\bar k\). All four rows therefore discharge the forward-cycle existence condition, and CC-XC15's search terminates. Products and finite base extensions are covered by the same preceding theorems. \(\square\)

The new geometric content invoked here is the preprint's generator theorem; the finite-field deductions do not reprove it. Its final motivic proofs have been inspected: Theorem 7.13, p. 44, for \(K3^{[n]}\), \(\mathrm{Kum}^n\) and \(\mathrm{OG10}\); and Theorem 8.9, p. 49, for \(\mathrm{OG6}\). Their route uses a modular lift, supersingular good reduction of the underlying K3 or abelian surface, and specialization of an actual split Chow-motive inclusion. The specialization statement is Lemma 6.12, pp. 39--40, which explicitly allows smooth proper algebraic-space models with smooth projective generic and special fibres. We inspected these statements and final proofs and the stated dependency chain; an independent reconstruction of all 59 pages is not claimed.

The lifting conditions and excluded Artin invariants remain genuine boundaries of this application. Mere equality of the second crystalline Newton slopes does not supply an excellent lifting. Likewise a variety of one of the four named deformation types without the displayed lifting and characteristic hypotheses is not covered by this theorem. The three excluded maximal invariants are stated limitations of the source's method, not asserted counterexamples (Remark 1.13, p. 6).

This gives actual cycle generators for the original finite-field variety in the listed cases, including varieties not initially presented as moduli spaces. It neither assumes nor proves that every smooth projective variety belongs to the abelian--Artin sector.
