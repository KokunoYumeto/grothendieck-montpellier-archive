## 1. Statement

Let \(X\) be a complex abelian fourfold with the balanced RM data of SF-E819--E821. Let \(i:S\hookrightarrow X\) be a smooth integral projective surface, and put \(L=\det N_{S/X}\). Projection of the two invariant RM bivectors to the normal bundle gives sections

\[
 P_+,P_-\in H^0(S,L).
 \tag{E822.1}
\]

In local equations \(I_S=(f,g)\), the coefficient of \(P_\pm\) in the corresponding normal determinant frame is \(\pi_\pm(df,dg)\bmod I_S\).

Suppose a coherent sheaf \(M\) has a locally free hull \(H=M^{\vee\vee}\), and its canonical hull map fits into

\[
 0\longrightarrow i_*T\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow H
       \longrightarrow i_*Q\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{E822.2}
\]

where \(T,Q\) are vector bundles on \(S\) of the same positive rank \(m\), satisfying

\[
 \int_S c_1(T)\,\theta|_S
     =\int_S c_1(Q)\,\theta|_S.
 \tag{E822.3}
\]

Assume \(e^B\operatorname{ch}(M)=r v_q\), with \(r,q>0\) and \(B\) an algebraic rational divisor. If \(M\) is weakly semiregular for a specified effective coherent finite translation/tensor action, then the two sections in(E822.1) span a vector space of dimension at most one.

Consequently no such weakly semiregular \(M\) exists when \([S]=k^2\theta^2\), for \(k>0\). The statement applies to actual nonsplit local extensions; it does not assume that \(i_*T\) is a local direct summand.

## 2. Intrinsic extension class and the actual local resolution

Write \(K=\ker(H\to i_*Q)\). The exact sequence
\(0\to i_*T\to M\to K\to0\) has a local extension class

\[
 \mathcal E\in H^0\bigl(S,\mathcal H om(Q,T)\otimes L\bigr).
 \tag{E822.4}
\]

This is intrinsic: locally freeness of \(H\) identifies
\(\mathcal E xt^1_X(K,i_*T)\) with
\(\mathcal E xt^2_X(i_*Q,i_*T)\), and the two-element Koszul resolution identifies the latter with \(i_*(\mathcal H om(Q,T)\otimes\det N_{S/X})\). The edge map from the actual global extension gives(E822.4). Extra global extension data do not change this local class.

Choose a small affine neighborhood where \(S\) has regular-sequence equations \(f,g\), where \(H,T,Q\) are trivialized as needed, and where the quotient \(H\to i_*Q\) is reduction modulo \((f,g)\) on the first \(m\) coordinates. Then
\(K=(f,g)^{\oplus m}\oplus\mathcal O^{\operatorname{rk}H-m}\).
The free summand splits from the extension locally. The remaining module is the quotient of the three generator blocks \(R^m\oplus R^m\oplus R^m\) by the submodule generated by the following relation vectors, for \(w\in R^m\):

\[
 (-gw,fw,-\widetilde{\mathcal E}w),
 \qquad (0,0,fw),\qquad (0,0,gw).
 \tag{E822.5}
\]

where \(\widetilde{\mathcal E}\) is a matrix lift of(E822.4). In column-map convention its finite free resolution is

\[
 0\longrightarrow R^m\xrightarrow{d_2}R^{3m}
       \xrightarrow{d_1}R^{3m}\longrightarrow M_{\rm loc}
       \longrightarrow0,
 \tag{E822.6}
\]

with

\[
 d_1=\begin{pmatrix}
 -gI_m&0&0\\
 fI_m&0&0\\
 -\widetilde{\mathcal E}&fI_m&gI_m
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 d_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-gI_m\\fI_m\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{E822.7}
\]

The first block of columns is the first relation vector in(E822.5). In particular, the extension matrix maps the quotient-normal relation into the torsion generators; no transpose convention is left implicit.

Here is the exactness check. If \(d_1(a,b,c)=0\), then \(fa=ga=0\), hence \(a=0\) since \(f\) is a nonzerodivisor. The remaining equation is \(fb+gc=0\). The two-element Koszul complex gives \((b,c)=(-gh,fh)\), which is precisely \(\operatorname{im}d_2\). The same regular-sequence calculation shows \(d_2\) is injective. The cokernel has the stated extension by \((R/(f,g))^m\), since the first relation reduces modulo the torsion generators to the presentation of \((f,g)^m\), and no nonzero element of the torsion quotient is killed by an additional relation. Thus(E822.6) resolves the actual module, including at zeros of \(\det\mathcal E\).

## 3. The characteristic obstruction forces a right inverse up to the normal section

Let \(\pi\) be either RM bivector. For this matrix calculation use right modules with column vectors, so maps are left multiplication by the displayed matrices. This entails no extra vanishing assumption: a left-module deformation for the opposite product is a right-module deformation for the stated product, and changing to the opposite first-order product replaces \(\pi\) by \(-\pi\). Vanishing is linear in the Hochschild class, so the two balanced vanishings also give these sign-changed vanishings. Relabel the sign if necessary; it has no effect on linear dependence of \(P_+,P_-\).

If this characteristic obstruction on \(M_{\rm loc}\) vanishes, the right-module version of the identical bar/resolution argument of SF-E819, section3, gives a flat first-order module deformation. Lifting the finite free resolution, its differentials are the displayed matrices with the first-order star product, corrected by matrices \(\varepsilon h_1,\varepsilon h_2\).

The uncorrected star-square is

\[
 d_1*d_2
   =\varepsilon\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\-\pi(df,dg)I_m\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{E822.8}
\]

In particular no derivative of \(\widetilde{\mathcal E}\) occurs: its block is multiplied by the zero block of \(d_2\). The correction equation is

\[
 d_1h_2+h_1d_2
   =\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\\pi(df,dg)I_m\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{E822.9}
\]

Reduce modulo \((f,g)\). The second summand vanishes because \(d_2\) reduces to zero. Only the \(-\mathcal E\) block of \(d_1\) remains. Absorbing its sign into the first block of \(h_2\), we obtain a local morphism

\[
 A_\pi:T\longrightarrow Q,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E A_\pi=P_\pi I_T
       \quad\text{as maps }T\to T\otimes L.
 \tag{E822.10}
\]

The type of \(A_\pi\) follows also from the resolution twists: both the domain of \(d_2\) and the first column block of \(d_1\) carry the same factor \(L^{-1}\), leaving \(\mathcal H om(T,Q)\) after reduction. Changes of normal equations multiply \(\mathcal E\) and \(P_\pi\) by the same determinant-frame unit; changes of the two bundle bases give the usual source/target transformations. Thus(E822.10) is intrinsic.

Balanced weak semiregularity supplies vanishing of both ordinary local characteristic obstructions by exactly the published BF2008 and supplied invariant-evaluation bridge of SF-E819. Therefore(E822.10) holds locally for both signs.

## 4. Equal degree makes the local inverse global and invertible

If both sections \(P_+,P_-\) are zero, the asserted conclusion is already true. Otherwise interchange the signs so that \(P_+\ne0\). On the dense open set where \(P_+\) is nonzero, equation(E822.10) makes \(\mathcal E\) an isomorphism, since both bundles have rank \(m\). Its kernel as a sheaf on the integral surface is zero: it is a subsheaf of a locally free sheaf with zero generic stalk.

It follows that a local solution \(A_\pi\) of(E822.10) is unique. Two solutions differ by a map into that zero kernel. The local solutions therefore glue to global morphisms \(A_\pm:T\to Q\).

The determinant of \(A_+\) is a nonzero section of
\(\det Q\otimes(\det T)^{-1}\). By(E822.3), this line bundle has degree zero against the ample class \(\theta|_S\). Its zero divisor would be an effective curve of degree zero, which is impossible unless it is empty. Hence \(A_+\) is everywhere invertible.

Consequently

\[
 \mathcal E=P_+A_+^{-1},
 \qquad
 P_+ A_+^{-1}A_-=P_-I_T.
 \tag{E822.11}
\]

The trace of the global endomorphism \(A_+^{-1}A_-\) is a global regular function on the integral projective surface, and is therefore constant. Taking its trace in(E822.11) shows that \(P_-=cP_+\) for a constant \(c\in\mathbf C\). This proves the first assertion, without any assertion that the hull map itself deforms. Such a hull-map lifting would generally be unjustified for codimension-two defects.

## 5. Independence on an ample complete-intersection surface

Fix an invariant holomorphic four-form on \(X\). Adjunction identifies \(L\) with \(K_S\). Under this identification, the normal projection of an invariant bivector \(\pi\) is the restriction to \(S\) of its contraction with that four-form. In local coordinates this is exactly the coefficient \(\pi(df,dg)\) defining(E822.1).

For \(\pi_+,\pi_-\), these contracted forms are nonzero scalar multiples of the holomorphic volume forms \(\Omega_-,\Omega_+\) on the opposite RM planes. Suppose \([S]=k^2\theta^2\), and take any nonzero linear combination \(\Omega=a\Omega_++b\Omega_-\). With the compatible positive Hermitian forms, each \(\Omega_\pm\wedge\overline{\Omega_\pm}\) is a positive constant times \(\theta_\pm^2\). The mixed terms have zero pairing with \(\theta^2\), by their separate holomorphic and antiholomorphic degrees in the two planes. Therefore

\[
 \int_S\Omega\wedge\overline\Omega
  =k^2\bigl(c_+|a|^2+c_-|b|^2\bigr)
       \int_X\theta_+^2\theta_-^2>0
 \tag{E822.12}
\]

for positive constants \(c_\pm\). No nonzero combination restricts to zero on \(S\). Thus \(P_+,P_-\) are linearly independent, contradicting section4.

## 6. Same-character surface repairs satisfy the numerical hypotheses

Suppose in(E822.2) that \(\operatorname{ch}(M)=\operatorname{ch}(H)\), with \(T,Q\) nonzero vector bundles on the smooth integral surface. Then
\(\operatorname{ch}(i_*T)=\operatorname{ch}(i_*Q)\).
Their leading components are \(\operatorname{rk}(T)[S]\) and \(\operatorname{rk}(Q)[S]\). Pairing with \(\theta^2\) proves the two ranks are equal. The next component of the embedding GRR formula is

\[
 \operatorname{ch}_3(i_*T)
   =i_*\left(c_1(T)-\tfrac{\operatorname{rk}T}{2}c_1(N_{S/X})\right),
 \tag{E822.13}
\]

and similarly for \(Q\). The equal ranks cancel the normal term. Pairing the resulting equality with \(\theta\) gives(E822.3). This is the smooth-embedding specialization of the already checked Fulton, *Intersection Theory*, second edition, Corollary18.3.1(c), p354; expand \(\operatorname{td}(N_{S/X})^{-1}\) through degree one. No additional Riemann--Roch assertion is assumed.

Thus a balanced locally free hull cannot be repaired, while retaining its entire character, by equal-character vector-bundle torsion and defect on such an ample complete-intersection surface. This conclusion includes choices of \(T,Q\) that are not initially isomorphic.

## 7. Actual nonsplit local balanced examples to which the obstruction applies

On the explicit CM fourfold \(E_i^4\), take the actual rank32 bundle \(H\) of SF-E815, with \(\operatorname{ch}(H)=32v_{1/2}\). Choose a smooth integral complete intersection \(S=D_0\cap D_1\) of two divisors in \(|3\theta|\). Such surfaces exist: the previously checked Bauer--Szemberg1996 Theorem1.1 gives very ampleness of three ample factors; Bertini is applied twice to that embedding. Exact additional locator: Stacks Lemma33.47.3 and Remark37.32.4, tags0FD4/0G4G. The smoothness proof and the smooth-irreducible hyperplane statement were freshly read for this use; no independent audit of every earlier Bertini dependency is claimed.

The ideal sheaf has the actual Koszul resolution

\[
 0\to\mathcal O_X(-D_0-D_1)
       \to\mathcal O_X(-D_0)\oplus\mathcal O_X(-D_1)
       \to I_S\to0.
\]

Applying sheaf Hom into \(\mathcal O_S\) makes its differential zero. Hence each section of \(\mathcal O_S(D_0+D_1)=K_S\) gives an actual class in
\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(I_S,\mathcal O_S)\): it is the degree-one direct summand of the resulting zero-differential Hom complex. In particular choose the nonzero section \(e=P_+\) from(E822.1), and form the actual coherent extension

\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S\longrightarrow M_e
      \longrightarrow I_S\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{E822.14}
\]

Its class in \(K_0(X)\) equals \([\mathcal O_X]\), since
\([I_S]+[\mathcal O_S]=[\mathcal O_X]\). Set

\[
 E=H\otimes M_e.
 \tag{E822.15}
\]

This is an actual coherent, hence ordinarily gluable, perfect sheaf. Its hull is \(H\), its torsion and hull-map cokernel are both \(i_*(H|_S)\), and its complete character is \(32v_{1/2}\). The intrinsic local extension matrix is \(e I_{32}\). On the dense open subset of \(S\) where \(e\ne0\), that matrix is invertible. The local extension is therefore nonsplit: the Koszul class is nonzero, and projecting its torsion to any positive-rank direct summand still gives a nonzero extension class. The surface torsion is genuinely coupled there, so the local-direct-summand hypothesis of SF-E821 is not being used.

SF-E822 excludes weak semiregularity for every specified coherent finite action that might exist. No such action, simplicity, or a semiregular object is asserted to have been constructed.

## 8. Remaining scope

The proof requires a smooth integral support and vector bundles on that support, equal ranks, and equal degree as in(E822.3). Unequal degrees allow determinant zero divisors and destroy the global-inverse step. Nonreduced support, singular support with non-locally-free modules, different codimensions, and more general derived local couplings are not settled. Supports for which the two normal bivector sections are proportional are also not excluded by this criterion alone.
