2.7 The converse of the 156-6 Théorème–Scholie
Theorem 2.21 (Proved: the corrected representation, in both directions) . The groupoid of
prime ateliers with At 4–At 7 is equivalent to the groupoid of pairs (E, H ⊆ Fig(E)) with the
following five properties:
1. ∅ ∈ H , and H is closed under unions bounded by a member of H .
2. If A ∈ Φ ∩ Ψ, the principal subfigures ΦA = {B ∈ Φ : B ⊆ A} and ΨA agree, and their
common value belongs to H . Denote it by ˆA.
3. For occurring strata A ⊆ B, ˆA ≪ ˆB as set-theoretic figures.
4. If ˆA ∪ ˆB ∈ H for every A ∈ Φ, B ∈ Ψ, then Φ ∪ Ψ ∈ H .
5. U = ⋃Φ∈H Φ is an admissible family of support E, and it separates the points of E.
The italicized membership clause is present at scan 32, but omitted from the shortened final At ∗2
list at scan 34. With that clause retained, the converse is valid. Without it, if all displayed
refinements are interpreted in the ambient Fig(E), the final list is insufficient. Without (B.P) on
the atelier side, the forward equivalence is already false by Counterexample 2.3.
Proof. First start from (E, H ). Every point e ∈ E has a least member Ae ∈ U containing it,
by admissibility. The residual of each A ∈ U is nonempty. If two points have the same least
member, they belong to exactly the same members of U ; separation makes them equal. Thus
every residual is a singleton eA, and e ↦→ Ae is a bijection E → U .
Transfer inclusion of these sets to a refinement order on E: eA ≪ eB iff A ⊆ B. Transfer
occurrence in canonical principal figures to incidence: eA ≤ eB iff A ∈ ˆB. This is a partial order:
membership in a principal subfigure is transitive, and antisymmetry follows from set inclusion.
Property 2 makes every Φ ∈ H a downset for this incidence. Properties 1 and 2 admit every
closed subfamily, since it is the bounded union of its principal subfigures. The principal members
are exactly the completely join-prime irreducibles, and property 4 is compatibility by strata.
This establishes the prime incidence core.
For a point eA refining Φ, the least member of Φ containing eA is the least member containing
A: A is already the least member of the entire U containing that point. This gives the least-
carrier axiom. If ˆA, ˆB are compatible and A ⊆ B, their admitted union has A as a substratum
of B; property 2 forces A ∈ ˆB. Thus compatible refinement is incidence.
Refinement of two members Φ, Ψ is equivalent to every A ∈ Φ being contained in some B ∈ Ψ.
Necessity is the monotone-carrier property of set-figure refinement. For sufficiency, property 3
gives ˆA ≪ ˆB ≤ Ψ. The open stratum of A in Φ is its open stratum in ˆA, so these refinements
supply a unique target cell for every source cell. The carrier maps agree on principal subfigures
and are monotone; therefore Φ ≪ Ψ. This is At 4. If eAρeBρeC, the corresponding principal
refinements carry the top open cell first into the top cell of ˆB, then into that of ˆC. Composition
of cell containment gives eAρeC, proving At 7.
Conversely start from a prime atelier. Set E = M , AX = {Z ∈ M : Z ≪ X}, and
ΦF = {AX : X ∈ ~F }. The principal ≪-ideals AX form an admissible separating cover of M ,
with residual singleton {X}, giving property 5. Least carriers make every ΦF admissible. For
strata inside one figure, AX ⊆ AY means X ≪ Y and hence X ≤ Y ; therefore the canonical
subfigure at AX is exactly ΦX , giving property 2. Bounded union preservation and stratum
compatibility give properties 1 and 4. Carrier composition gives set-figure refinement ΦX ≪ ΦY
whenever X ≪ Y , hence property 3. The point-to-least-stratum bijection constructed in the first
half recovers X ↦→ AX ; the figures, incidence and refinement are recovered exactly. Isomorphisms
transfer through the same bijections. This proves equivalence of groupoids, without asserting an
unproved equivalence for any broader category of morphisms.
