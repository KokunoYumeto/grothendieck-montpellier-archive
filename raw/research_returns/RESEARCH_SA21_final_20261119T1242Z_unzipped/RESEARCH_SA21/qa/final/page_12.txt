Theorem 1.19 (Proved: the exact relation with orthomodularity) . In a complete orthomodular
lattice, every mutually orthogonal family of nonzero elements satisfies the lattice clauses (a)–(c)
of Theorem 1.18. Conversely, if the relative-complement formula holds for every two-element
orthogonal family of nonzero lattice elements, the lattice is orthomodular. Thus 156-9, scan 117,
applied to the universal spatial figures of a spatios, is equivalent to orthomodularity. The axiom
for the actual figures of a given magasin is weaker, even under Mag 1–4, divisibility and Mag L2.
Proof. For orthogonal u, v in an orthomodular lattice the dual orthomodular law gives (u ∨
v) ∧ u⊥ = v. For any subset A of an orthogonal family, let u = ⋁i∈A ei and v = ⋁i /∈A ei.
Completeness and order reversal of orthocomplement give u ⊥ v; the displayed identity gives
the relative-complement formula for SI = u ∨ v. This argument treats infinite families directly.
Theorem 1.18 supplies the other lattice conclusions. It is also a direct form of the commuting-
element calculus usually derived from the Foulis–Holland theorem. Conversely, for a ≤ b apply
the assumed identity to u = b⊥ and v = a; zero terms may be omitted. It yields (b⊥ ∨ a) ∧ b = a.
Taking orthocomplements gives a⊥ = ( b ∧ a⊥) ∨ b⊥, the orthomodular law for b⊥ ≤ a⊥. Thus
it holds for every comparable pair. For strictness, use the graph of Counterexample 1.8 with
identity incidence and identity interior refinement, so all eight vertices are lieux. Theorem 1.14
verifies every magasin hypothesis. Theorem 1.9 proves Axiome supp 1 for every figure, whereas
its twenty-element support lattice is not orthomodular. Actual figures do not realize every
orthogonal pair of abstract supports, which is exactly the missing quantifier.
