Theorem 3.3 (Proved: the correct connectedness of each figure) . Let Φ be any admissible family
on L, and let π : |Φ| → Φ send a point to its least stratum. Give |Φ| the Alexandrov topology
whose closed sets are π−1(D) for downsets D ⊆ Φ. Its connected components are exactly the
inverse images of the comparability components of the stratum poset Φ. No finiteness hypothesis
is needed.
For a finite frontier stratification by definably connected cells, with each closed stratum the
closure of its cell, these same components are exactly the definable connected components of its
actual support. In particular this applies to the o-minimal cell atelier of Part B.4.
Proof. Every residual fiber π−1(A) is nonempty and has the indiscrete subspace topology. A
comparability component of Φ is both a downset and an upset, so its inverse image is clopen.
Any clopen subset must be constant on each fiber, and cannot separate comparable fibers. Finite
comparability zigzags therefore cannot be separated. The stated clopen pieces are connected and
maximal.
In the definable model, a poset component is a finite union of closed strata; its complement
in the support is another such union. Hence it is definably clopen. Conversely a definably clopen
subset cannot split a definably connected cell. If it contains a cell, closedness makes it contain
that cell’s entire closure, hence every incident lower cell. Applying the same reasoning to its
complement forces it to be a union of comparability components. This proves equality of the two
component decompositions.
The finite-poset part is the standard finite-space connectedness criterion, which we checked
in Barmak (2011, Proposition 1.2.4, p. 5); our proof also gives the possibly infinite admissible-
family version and explicitly handles nonsingleton residual fibers. Over a non-Archimedean real
closed field, the geometric assertion concerns definable connectedness, not arbitrary topological
connectedness.
Theorem 3.4 (Proved: the global refinement replacement for C4) . Let A be a set-theoretic
atelier on L, with inherited set-figure refinement, containing every point figure and closed under
disjoint unions. Make a hypergraph on L whose hyperedges are the supports of the multistrates.
The comparability components of (M, ≪) are exactly the components of that support hypergraph,
with a multistrate assigned to the component containing its support. Multistrates in different
refinement components are compatible.
For the full Fig(L), the refinement graph is connected whenever L ⁄= ∅. For the closed
o-minimal cell atelier of Part B.4, its refinement components are exactly the definable connected
components of the ambient definable set X.
Proof. Every p ∈ | A| supplies the refinement {p} ≪ A. Thus all points of one hyperedge are
joined through its multistrate. Conversely A ≪ B implies |A| ⊆ | B|, so a refinement edge
never crosses a hypergraph component. These two observations prove equality of the component
relations. Distinct components have disjoint supports, and closure under disjoint unions gives
compatibility. For the full set-theoretic atelier, any two points lie in the one-stratum multistrate
{{p, q}}, so there is one component.
In the o-minimal model, every multistrate support is the closure of a definably connected cell,
hence is definably connected. It lies in one definable component of X, so refinement components
cannot be larger. If p, q lie in one definable component, Coste (1999, Theorem 3.9, p. 28) supplies
a definable continuous path γ : [0 , 1] → X between them. Its image P is closed and bounded:
coordinate functions are bounded, and for a putative missing limit point v, the positive function
t ↦→ ‖ γ(t) − v‖ attains a positive minimum, contradicting that v is a limit point. These uses
of definable compactness follow from Coste (1999, Theorem 3.4, pp. 26–27). Triangulate P
compatibly with {p}, {q}, using Coste (1999, Theorem 4.4, p. 42). The resulting finite closed
cell figure has connected support, so its incidence graph is connected by Theorem 3.3. Incidence
implies refinement, and its endpoint point figures therefore lie in one refinement component.
This proves the converse. No injective-arc theorem is needed.
