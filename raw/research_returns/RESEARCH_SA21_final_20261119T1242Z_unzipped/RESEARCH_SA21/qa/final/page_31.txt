2.6 At 14 bis: deployment uniqueness and object uniqueness
Counterexample 2.19 (Refuted: unique extension in At 14 bis and ter) . Let M = {a, x}, let
incidence be equality, let refinement have a ≪ x, and let internal disjointness be empty. The
full figures are 0, a, x; two nonempty figures are compatible only when equal, and no nonempty
figures are disjoint. Mag 1–4 hold: every principal deployment is a singleton, carriers are unique,
and all orthogonality clauses are vacuous. The sole lieu is a, and Ua = Ux = {a}. Thus Mog L2,
the local axioms, the prime core, C, D, all gluing and At supp hold. Every nonempty refinement
has the same cosupport, so a ≼ x.
Take F = x, G = G′ = 0 . Both choices F ′ = x and F ′ = a are subdivisions. Their
deployment maps are isomorphisms of one-point posets, their restrictions over G are empty, and
the deployment squares are cartesian. The complementary deployment map is an isomorphism.
The mixed incidence condition is vacuous. Hence both satisfy At 14 bis, but they are different
figures. Their boundaries are both empty, so At 14 ter’s uniqueness also fails. This model satisfies
At 14 bis existence for every possible diagram : the finite certificate enumerates all six extension
diagrams and all twelve gluing assignments.
Proposition 2.20 (Proved: the exact uniqueness supplied by the diagram) . Fix posets ~G ⊆ ~F ,
with ~G a downset, and a monotone map r : ~G′ → ~G. A deployment square with inverse image
~G′, an isomorphism over ~F \ ~G, and the intended mixed incidence rule is unique up to unique
isomorphism over these data. Its poset is
E = ~G′ ∐ ( ~F \ ~G),
with the original orders on each piece, no exterior-to-interior relations, and
ξ ≤ η ⇐ ⇒ r(ξ) ≤ η (ξ ∈ ~G′, η ∈ ~F \ ~G).
This is a uniqueness theorem for an abstract ordered deployment. An atelier can have several
objects with this same deployment and carrier map, as Counterexample 2.19 shows. Requiring
a coarsest extension is an additional property, not a consequence of the displayed deployment
diagram.
Proof. Every element belongs either to the prescribed inverse image or to the identified
complementary poset. The within-piece orders and mixed rule prescribe every possible ordered
pair. There are no relations from the exterior to the interior because the latter is a downset.
The displayed relation is transitive: the only mixed transitivity checks use monotonicity of r
or transitivity in ~F . Antisymmetry follows because no reverse mixed pair exists. Thus it is a
poset, and the prescribed identifications give the unique isomorphism between any two such
diagrams.
