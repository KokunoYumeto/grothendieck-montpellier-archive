Proposition 5.8 (Proved: the correct connectedness statement) . The undirected refinement
components of (M, ≪) are exactly the definably connected components of X: a multistrate is
assigned the unique component containing its support, and two multistrates are in the same
refinement component exactly when those assignments agree. Different refinement components
are compatible. For an individual finite figure, its incidence components are exactly the definably
connected components of its support.
Proof. Generalized cells are definably connected, since open boxes are definably path connected.
A closure of a definably connected set is definably connected: a definable clopen partition would
restrict to the dense set, and density excludes an unused nonempty open part. Thus |m| lies in
one component. Refinement implies support inclusion, so the assigned component is constant on
refinement edges. Each p ∈ |m| also gives {p} ≪ m.
Conversely Coste (1999, Theorem 3.9, p. 28) gives finitely many definably connected
components, each definably arcwise connected. Join p, q in one component by a continuous
definable γ : [0, 1] → X; no injectivity is needed. Its image P is bounded by the extreme-value
property applied to its coordinates, and closed by the same property applied to distance from a
proposed missing closure point. These follow from Coste (1999, Theorem 3.4, pp. 26–27). The
image is definably connected as a continuous image of [0, 1].
Triangulate P compatibly with its endpoints. The resulting finite cell figure in X has p, q as
vertices and connected incidence graph: otherwise an incidence component and its complement
would be finite unions of closed strata, giving a nontrivial definable clopen partition. Incidence
implies refinement, so this graph connects the points and then the original multistrates. Distinct
components have disjoint supports, hence compatibility.
For an individual figure, incidence components are definably clopen by the same finite-union
argument. They are connected because each closed stratum is connected and incident strata
intersect. Their connected finite incidence graph gives connected union, successively along a
spanning tree. These are therefore exactly its support components.
Proposition 5.9 (Refuted stronger assertions and proved scope limits) . Global incidence C4
and C4 bis, the polyhedral restrictions and an infinite-family strengthening of moderation fail in
general. Allowing arbitrary nonclosed supports with closures taken only relative to themselves
also destroys the disjoint-support compatibility axiom.
Proof. For C4 let X = R. The one-cell figure on R has no proper incidence substratum. It
cannot occur properly below another elementary figure since its support is already all of R. It is
isolated in the global incidence poset. The singleton at 0 lies in a different component and is
incompatible with it, since the open cells intersect. C4 fails, and so does its strengthening C4 bis.
For the polyhedral restrictions, partition the semialgebraic unit circle into two points p, q and
two complementary open arcs. The arc closures intersect in the two-point figure, neither empty
nor elementary. This refutes At pol 1 and the biconditional At 9′′. It does not refute CL(pol)
or At CD2 ′, which are sufficient criteria. Partitioning the circle instead into one point and its
complement gives a top stratum with exactly one immediate predecessor, refuting At simp 1.
The cells are generalized one-cells: stereographic projection identifies a punctured circle with
R, then with (0, 1); the two arcs are semialgebraically homeomorphic to intervals. The finite
incidence tests are in the program; these geometric realizations put the checked posets in the
specified model.
For arbitrary nonclosed supports, take the one-cell partition of (0, 1) ⊂ R and the singleton
at 1. Their supports are disjoint, but they cannot both be closed subfigures of a common frontier
stratification: (0, 1) is not closed in (0, 1], so its cell acquires the new boundary. This is the
obstruction removed by fixed ambient closures and closed supports.
