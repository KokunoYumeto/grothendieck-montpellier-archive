Lieux and disjunction. Every singleton exists since parameters are allowed. They are exactly
the refinement-minimal nonempty multistrates: every nonsingleton one has a proper singleton
refinement. Full and open shadows are |m| and m◦, respectively. Every open shadow has a lieu;
distinct lieux are disjoint. The identity
m◦ ∩ n◦ = ∅ ⇐ ⇒ (∀p ∈ m◦)(∀q ∈ n◦) p ⁄= q
and its whole-support analogue prove Mag L1–L2, the displayed divisible-magasin conditions, At
8–10, At L1–L3 and the lieu detection clauses of the Cont systems. Support inclusion makes
disjointness hereditary under refinement.
Compatibility and gluing. For figures F, G, intersect their sets of elementary subfigures to
obtain their greatest common incidence subfigure K. Then
F ⊿ ◁ G ⇐ ⇒ (|F | \ |K|) ∩ (|G| \ |K|) = ∅.
The forward direction uses their common partition. For the reverse, outside cells are disjoint by
the formula, inside cells are identical, and outside cells cannot meet an inside cell. The union
criterion applies. This proves CL(spac), At 9′, and At 9Σ with all finite common posets allowed.
Suppose F ⊿ ◁ G, F ′ ≪ F , G′ ≪ G, and F ′|K ⊿ ◁ G′|K. Any intersection of a refining F ′-cell
and G′-cell lies in |F | ∩ |G| = |K|. A refining cell meeting |K| has its carrier in K, so belongs to
the appropriate restriction. All possible intersections are thus governed by compatible restrictions.
The union criterion proves F ′ ⊿ ◁ G′: this is At 12 and At C. The same restriction identity with
empty intersection proves At D and D 0. For absolute At CD2, suppose all refinements of F, G
whose restrictions to K are empty are pairwise disjoint. Singleton points outside |K| are among
these refinements. Hence |F | \ |K| and |G| \ |K| are disjoint, and the displayed formula gives
F ⊿ ◁ G. This proves At CD2; the combined At CD formulation follows from it and the relative
criterion. CL(pol) and At CD2 ′ add a hypothesis that K be empty or elementary to a sufficient
criterion, so follow from CL(spac) and At CD2. They differ from the biconditional At 9′′.
Refinements agreeing on overlaps glue by union, with precisely the prescribed restrictions,
proving At 11 and At 11′. For subdivisions the supports cover the original support, proving
C8. Any incidence cover of a finite figure has a finite subcover. The data on any further cover
member agree with the finite gluing on its intersections with this subcover, which cover it. Thus
the arbitrary-cover version holds. For At 11 bis, a directed family with an existing incidence
supremum on one stratum is stationary there: the supremum has finitely many strata, included
at one directed index. Finitely many original strata give one global stabilization index. The
eventual figure is the required supremum. Starting instead with stratumwise stationarity gives
At Fil.
Supports and induction. If F ′ ≪ F and some figure is disjoint from F ′ but not F , choose
p ∈ |F | \ |F ′|. Its singleton refines F , is disjoint from F ′, and is not disjoint from F . This proves
At supp, At 13 and Cond 7 in their exterior-lieu forms. It also proves that, for a refinement,
equal annihilators are equivalent to equal supports, giving C5. The restriction formula gives
C6, including uniqueness and commutation with bounded joins. A refinement missing a target
point is strictly enlarged by adjoining that singleton; with equal supports no enlargement is
possible. Hence subdivisions are exactly incidence-maximal refinements. Antisymmetry gives
C80. Extensions are proved separately.
