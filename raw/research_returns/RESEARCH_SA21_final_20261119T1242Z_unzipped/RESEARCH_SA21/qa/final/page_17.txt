For B ⊆ U , set
E(B) = {q ∈ Q+(S(L)) : D(q) ⊆ B}.
We identify U with M (L) via the embedding. Equation (7) gives B⊥Q = E(B⊥U ). More
generally,
E(B)⊥Q = E(B⊥U ).
Indeed testing orthogonality to E(B) includes testing every b ∈ B because D(b) = {b}; conversely
the pieces of every element of E(B) belong to B, so the same test suffices. Hence E(B)⊥Q⊥Q =
E(B⊥U ⊥U ). In particular E takes closed sets to closed sets, commutes with complement, and its
trace on U is B.
Conversely let A ⊆ Q+(S(L)) be closed. If q ∈ A, then every piece of q is orthogonal to A⊥Q
by (7), and hence belongs to A. Conversely if every piece lies in A, that same equation gives
q ∈ A. Therefore A = E(A ∩ U ). The preceding polar formula then shows that A ∩ U is closed
in U . Thus trace and E are mutually inverse order isomorphisms preserving orthocomplement;
consequently they preserve all meets and joins. This proves the complete-ortholattice comparison,
including the empty case.
We call P a conditional lattice when every pair having a common upper bound has a least
upper bound and every pair having a common lower bound has a greatest lower bound. We do
not require a global least or greatest element.
Lemma 1.29 (Proved: finite conditional lattices suffice for quotient spaces) . If P is a finite
conditional lattice, then C(Q+(P )) is orthomodular.
Proof. Adjoin two new strict bounds ˆ0, ˆ1 to form ˆP . This is a finite lattice: joins or meets already
present in P are retained, and a pair with no upper, respectively lower, bound in P has join ˆ1,
respectively meet ˆ0. Let X be closed in Q+(P ) and put ˆX = X ⊥⊥ in Q+( ˆP ). Then
ˆX ∩ Q+(P ) = X. (8)
To verify the possibly delicate inclusion, a point of the left side is orthogonal to every element
of X ⊥ in the larger space, hence to every element of its trace, which is exactly the polar in
Q+(P ). Since X is closed in the smaller space, the point belongs to X. The reverse inclusion is
extensivity of closure.
