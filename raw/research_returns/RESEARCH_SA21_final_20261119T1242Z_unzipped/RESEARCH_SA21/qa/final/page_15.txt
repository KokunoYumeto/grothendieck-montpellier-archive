Theorem 1.25 (Proved: the typed spatial reconstruction) . Let (E, ≼, ⊥) have a partial incidence
order and an orthoset relation. For each x label its principal incidence ideal by y ↦→ cl{y},
transporting the incidence order. Assume that these labels form an elementary ordered spatial
figure: the labels are nonzero, distinct, and pairwise orthogonal. Assume also that the resulting
ordered deployment Dx determines x injectively. There is then a unique spatial interior refinement
making these data a strong magasin. It is given by the existence of an order-preserving carrier
h : Dx − →Dy, S ⊆ h(S), h (cl{x}) = cl{y}.
This is the fully typed interpretation of At spat 1–3 in 156-9, scan 50, using the spatial target
explicitly defined on scan 48. If that target is read merely as disjoint subsets of E, the assertion
is false and strong At 4 must be added.
Proof. The carrier, when it exists, is unique: a nonzero closed set cannot lie below two different
orthogonal labels in Dy. Identities and composition prove reflexivity and transitivity. If carriers
exist in both directions, their composites send each label S to a label U of the same deployment
with S ⊆ U . Distinct labels there are orthogonal, so S = U ; the intermediate inclusion is also
equality. The deployments and their transported orders coincide, and injectivity gives x = y.
Thus the relation is a partial order. If z ≼ x, restricting a carrier to Dz lands in the principal ideal
below its value on the top label of Dz; that ideal is the deployment of the corresponding stratum
of y. This proves incidence lifting and uniqueness. Allowing a carrier without the top-preserving
condition gives exactly its composite with incidence: factor through the principal ideal below
the top image. Internal orthogonality is monotone because the top labels are included, and
two distinct strata under one incidence top are orthogonal by the typed deployment hypothesis.
These are strong Mag 1–4. The carrier description is precisely the sufficient “Raff” condition
defining spatiality on scans 46–47, and it also proves uniqueness. For the weaker interpretation
use the five-cycle orthoset with incidence 0, 2 ≺ 1 and all other incidences identities. Its singleton
closures are singletons. All deployments are distinct ordered families of disjoint nonempty subsets,
and comparable distinct strata are orthogonal. But the two strata 0, 2 below 1 are not orthogonal,
violating strong At 4. This finite example is checked by the program. Scan 47’s margin and scan
49 explicitly retain At 4, consistent with the corrected typed formulation.
