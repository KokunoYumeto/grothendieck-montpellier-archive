1 Supports and the orthomodular law
We write x ⊥ y for the primitive internal disjunction of 156-8 and 156-9. In the earlier theory
we apply the same construction to the explicitly specified figure-disjointness relation. These two
relations must not be identified without a hypothesis. A figure may be finite, or belong to an
explicitly chosen larger class; every assertion below states which quantifier is intended.
1.1 Two exact tests, including infinite ordered sets
Definition 1.1 (Known: orthoset definitions) . An orthoset is a set E with a symmetric irreflexive
relation ⊥. For A ⊆ E put
N (A) = {x ∈ E : (∀a ∈ A) x ⊥ a}, cl A = N (N (A)), Σ(E) = {A ⊆ E : A = cl A}.
We abbreviate N ({x}) to N (x). Orthogonality between subsets means orthogonality of every
pair of their elements. A basis of A means a maximal, under inclusion, pairwise orthogonal
subset of A; it need not be finite. For a poset L, NL(A) denotes the points strictly comparable
with every member of A.

Theorem 1.3 (Known criterion, explicitly specialized and proved) . Let E be an arbitrary
orthoset. Its support lattice is orthomodular if and only if
for every S ⊆ E and every basis D ⊆ N (S), N (D) ⊆ N (N (S)). (1)
In particular, the exact answer for the comparability disjoncteur of a poset L is the following
condition expressed entirely in its order: for every S ⊆ L and every maximal chain D of NL(S),
every point strictly comparable with all of D is strictly comparable with every point of NL(S).
Proof. Every closed set has the form A = N (S). Condition (1), together with D ⊆ A, is
equivalent to N (D) = N (A), or cl D = A. Suppose the lattice is orthomodular. For a basis D
of A, let B = cl D ≤ A. Maximality implies A ∩ N (D) = ∅: a point in this intersection could
be adjoined to D. Hence A ∧ B⊥ = 0, and the orthomodular law gives A = B ∨ (A ∧ B⊥) = B.
Conversely let every basis generate its closed set. Given closed B ≤ A, take a basis D of B and
extend it to a basis H of A. Existence and extension follow from Zorn’s lemma; we work in ZFC.
For h ∈ H \ D we have h ∈ A ∩ N (D) = A ∩ B⊥. Therefore
A = cl H ≤ B ∨ (A ∧ B⊥) ≤ A.
