B = Q, hence Q ≼ P . Antisymmetry of ≼ gives P = Q.

Counterexample 2.26 (Refuted: even prime C1–C8 0 need not give At 5) . Let M = {d, a, y, z},
with d < a, d < y, d < z . Every pair is compatible except a, y and a, z. The figures, written by
their deployments, are
0, d, da, dy, dz, dyz.
Primitive subdivision is equality together with
da ≼ dy, da ≼ dz, da ≼ dyz.
This is a partial order. All nonempty figures contain d, so no two are disjoint and C5 holds.
C1–C3, including (B.P), are the explicit clique-downset construction. The incidence graph of M
is connected, so C4 and C4 bis impose no additional condition.
For da ≼ dy or da ≼ dz, restriction sends the bottom, the subfigure d, and the whole parent
respectively to 0, d, da. For da ≼ dyz, it sends 0, d, dy, dz, dyz respectively to 0, d, da, da, da.
These are the unique subfigures that subdivide the prescribed subfigure; they have exactly the strata
refining it, and preserve all joins. Identity subdivisions restrict identically. This checks C6(a,b,c)
completely. A subdivision da ≼ dy ≤ dyz extends by da ≼ dyz, and likewise for dz; all other
extension cases are identities. Hence C7 0 holds, as does C8 0.
Nevertheless a ≪ dyz, and the strata refined by a in dyz are y, z, with no least member. Thus
the later At 5 fails. Final C8, the full gluing axiom for subdivisions, is a different assertion and
fails here: the subdivisions da ≼ dy and dz ≼ dz agree on d but cannot glue, because a, z are
incompatible. The script legacy_subdivision.py exhaustively checks all bounded joins, every
C6 restriction and every C7 0 extension.
