for the lower-open convention, is a weak homotopy equivalence: McCord (1966, Theorem 2,

Proof. For completeness, we verify the hypotheses of McCord’s local theorem in this application.
Put Ux = L≤x. The preimage µ−1L (Ux) consists of points having a positive coordinate at some
vertex of Ux, and is the complement of the full subcomplex on L \ Ux; it is open. Renormalizing
the coordinates on Ux gives a deformation retraction onto |∆(Ux)|, a cone with apex x. Each
Ux is contractible as an Alexandrov space and these sets form a basis. Thus all restricted maps
are weak equivalences, and McCord’s Theorem 6, p. 467, applies. This argument needs no local
finiteness. To see concretely why a homotopy equivalence need not result, take the four-element
poset with two incomparable minima below two incomparable maxima. Its order complex is a
circle. Every continuous map from this connected finite poset to a Hausdorff space is constant:
comparable points must have the same image, and the comparability graph is connected. A
homotopy inverse into the circle would therefore make the circle’s identity null-homotopic, an
impossibility. The checker verifies that the four-element order complex is the four-cycle.
The subdivision homeomorphism for a regular CW realization and the weak equivalence to
an Alexandrov poset have different types. They do not identify the Alexandrov and Hausdorff
topologies.
