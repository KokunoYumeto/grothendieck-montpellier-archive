We claim that X ⊥ = Y and Y ⊥ = X. Every base element of I is strictly below every base element

ideal, both endpoint bases belong to U , so q ∈ Y . The dual reasoning gives Y ⊥ = X. Hence X
is orthoclosed.

Remark 1.31 (Refuted and corrected: an unbounded source claim) . We use the bounded
theorem of Jenča, not the unrestricted wording of his intermediate Corollary 4.5 in the opened
2022 arXiv version. As printed on p. 8 of that version, the corollary asserts q⊥⊥ = q↓ for every
poset. Two disjoint two-element chains provide a finite counterexample: their quotient space
has two elements and no orthogonality, so the double polar of either singleton is both elements,
whereas its interval ideal is a singleton. For a bounded P , the stated singleton formula is correct.
If [r < s ] is not contained in [x < y ], then either x ⁄≤ r or s ⁄≤ y. In the first case [0 < x ] is a
proper quotient orthogonal to [x < y ] and not to [r < s ]; in the second case [y < 1] does the
same. The relevant quotient is proper because the failed inequality excludes x = 0 or y = 1 ,
respectively. Contained quotients are always in the double polar by monotonicity. This proves the
exact bounded replacement. The preceding Lemma 4.3 and Corollary 4.4 explicitly impose the
relevant boundedness. Our transfer and trace-block arguments do not use the invalid unbounded
extension. No assertion here extends the finite conditional-lattice characterization to arbitrary
infinite posets; Theorem 1.27 itself is unrestricted.
2 B.1. A typed comparison of the successive axiom systems
We distinguish the written axioms from the stronger hypotheses tacitly used in several of their
purported consequences. In particular, complete join-irreducibility is not complete join-primality.
This distinction changes the comparison already at C1–C3, before any refinement is introduced.
The symbols C4 and C8 also change their meanings during 156-4. We therefore attach a scan
locator whenever we use a chronological label.
