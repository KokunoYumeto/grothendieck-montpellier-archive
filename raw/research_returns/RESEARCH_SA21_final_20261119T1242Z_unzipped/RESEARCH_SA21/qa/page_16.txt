Proposition 1.27 (Proved: the cograph sufficient condition is strict) . Every finite cograph has
an orthomodular support lattice. The converse fails even for a five-element comparability poset
with a Boolean support lattice.
Proof. We use the recursive definition of cographs: start with one vertex and close under disjoint
union and graph join. For graph join, the polarity acts coordinatewise on the vertex parts; the
support ortholattice is the product of their support ortholattices. For a disjoint union of nonempty
graphs, every proper nonzero closed set lies in a single component, and is a proper nonzero closed
set of that component. Its orthocomplement is the corresponding component complement. Thus
the support lattice is their horizontal sum, identifying all zeros and all units; a two-element factor
contributes no proper element. Both products and horizontal sums of orthomodular lattices are
orthomodular: a comparable proper pair in a horizontal sum lies in the same factor, and the
pairs involving zero or one satisfy the law directly. Induction proves the claim. For strictness,
let the five points represent the nonempty subsets {1}, {2}, {3}, {1, 2}, {2, 3}, orthogonal when
disjoint. The three singletons separate all subsets of {1, 2, 3}, so the closed sets are exactly the
intersections of these five points with the principal ideals of P({1, 2, 3}); the lattice is Boolean
with eight elements. Write the singleton points as a, b, c and the other two as x, y. The graph has
edges ab, ac, bc, cx, ay and contains the induced path x − c − a − y. It is a comparability graph:
orient its edges by a < b < c , x < c , a < y . These comparisons are transitive and introduce no
extra edges. The explicit graph and its eight closed sets are checked in the certificate. This
verifies the substance of R14’s strictness without relying on its earlier enumeration counts.
1.8 The split-poset comparison and the finite classification
For a poset P , write
Q+(P ) = {[u < v ] : u, v ∈ P, u < v }, [u < v ] ⊥ [r < s ] ⇐ ⇒ v ≤ r or s ≤ u.
This is the orthogonality space of proper quotients in Jenča (2023, §4, p. 6). Its order by interval
inclusion is a different relation. We write C(O) for the complete ortholattice of double-polar
closed subsets of an orthogonality space O; complements and polars below are always taken in
the indicated ambient space.
