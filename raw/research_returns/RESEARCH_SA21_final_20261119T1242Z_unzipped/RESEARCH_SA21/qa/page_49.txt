6 Oriented maquettes and regular cell structures
We distinguish the oriented set M , its quotient P = M /⟨a⟩ by the fixed-point-free reversal a,
and the order complex ∆(P ). The quotient map q : M → P forgets an orientation; it does not
identify points in a geometric cell. All order complexes have the weak topology. The empty
complex is denoted S−1.
6.1 A necessary audit of the literal axioms
The transcription and our inspection of 156-8, scan 4, write Magor 1′(c) as X | ◦ | Y ⇒ X − | ◦ | Y ;
scan 6 writes X | ◦ | Y ⇐ ⇒ X ⁄= Y . These formulas cannot both hold on a nonempty
oriented set. We inspected both original scan images. The inequality glyph on scan 6 has no
separate sign identifying orientation orbits. We retain the literal deduction and the explicitly
corrected interpretation separately. The transcription also places a prime on the antecedent Y of
Magor 1′(b), while the consequent uses Y −. We read the same bound variable on both sides. If
the prime instead denotes an independently quantified variable, identity refinement would force
every X to equal every Y , again leaving only the empty case under free reversal.
Theorem 6.1 (Proved: literal incompatibility) . Let M have a fixed-point-free involution. If an
irreflexive relation ⊥ ⊆ M × M satisfies x ⊥ y ⇒ a(x) ⊥ y, then it cannot equal inequality unless
M = ∅.
Proof. For x ∈ M , put y = a(x). Inequality gives x ⊥ a(x), and reversal invariance gives
a(x) ⊥ a(x), contradicting irreflexivity. The empty case satisfies every condition vacuously.
The geometric correction used below is X | ◦ | Y ⇐ ⇒q(X) ⁄= q(Y ). Changing Magor 1′(c) to
simultaneous reversal is another consistent correction; in the maquette case it gives the same
incidence classification, but it is a different general axiom. Likewise, the doubtful superscript in
Magor 2(b) is treated explicitly. The original image has a small epsilon-like superscript on the
source X, and no corresponding superscript on the target Y . We do not identify this doubtful
mark with an unprinted target sign.
Proposition 6.2 (Proved: the two readings of Magor 2(b)) . Suppose interior refinement is
identity and disjunction is corrected to orbit inequality. If Magor 2(b) permits X ′ ◁ X ϵ for either
sign ϵ but concludes X ′ ◁ Y with Y = X, then Magor 1, 1′, 5 and 6 permit precisely the graphs
whose edges are all loops; there are no cells of dimension at least two. If the target is given the
same sign, or the source superscript is absent, Magor 2(b) is automatic and the general graph
dictionary below applies.
Proof. In the first reading, write DX for the set of immediate predecessors of X. Taking ϵ = −1,
first at X and then at X −, gives DX = DX −. Simultaneous reversal then gives a(DX ) = DX .
If Z ◁ Y ◁ X has rank difference two, both Y and Y − lie between Z and X, contradicting the
uniqueness in Magor 6(a). Any element of dimension at least two has an initial length-two
portion of a longest incidence chain; its last element has dimension two. Thus such elements are
impossible. For a one-cell, Magor 6(b) and reversal-stability of DX force exactly the two signs of
a single vertex below it, so the associated edge is a loop. Conversely arbitrary graphs consisting
of loops and isolated vertices satisfy all these axioms: rank-two conditions are empty and every
one-cell has precisely those two signed endpoints. In the same-sign reading the asserted incidence
is the assumed incidence because X = Y ; the reading without the source sign has the same
property.
