Corollary 1.16 (Proved: the exact atom class for interval magasins) . For every poset L, the
lieux of M (L) are exactly the point symbols Px. All these lieux have atomic supports if and only
if L is an antichain or L has no isolated points. Thus every lieu has an atomic support for every
nonempty connected poset, including the singleton. For L = ∅ the assertion is vacuous.
Proof. We use the manuscript’s full-refinement definition of a lieu: minimality for ≪, not merely
for interior refinement ρ (156-8, scan 15, J156-8-02_transcription.tex:277). In the primed
formulation it is minimality for both incidence and ρ (J156-8-04_transcription.tex:282,296).
Only Px refines Px, since a factorization U ρZ ≤ Px forces Z = Px and then U = Px. Every
interval Iab has the distinct incidence predecessor Pa, which also refines it. Thus the lieux are
exactly the points, even when a ⋖ b makes Iab minimal for ρ alone. In particular we make no
unsupported divisibility claim for intervals having no point strictly between their endpoints.
Theorem 1.7 proves that every nonisolated x has cl{Px} = {Px}, a nonzero atom. For an
isolated x, the symbol Px has no neighbour, so cl{Px} = M (L). If L has a comparison a < b ,
the singleton support of Pa is a proper nonzero closed set, and this top support is not an atom.
If L is a nonempty antichain, there are no interval symbols and no disjunction edges; its support
lattice has just zero and one, so every lieu has that single nonzero atom as support. These
alternatives prove both directions of the criterion. A connected comparability graph with more
than one vertex has no isolated point, and the singleton case is an antichain. The empty case
follows from the universal quantifier over no lieux.
Counterexample 1.17 (Refuted inference; R2 and L3 precisely qualified) . Two lieux with
no edge have the two-element Boolean support lattice; their supports are the same atom. Thus
nondisjoint distinct lieux do not, by themselves, measure failure of distributivity. Under Mag L2
and divisibility, the following are equivalent: faithfulness; Booleanity with lieux represented by
distinct atoms; and p ⊥ q for every pair of distinct lieux. In the additional point-separating case
cl{p} = {p} for all lieux, Booleanity alone is equivalent to pairwise disjointness of distinct lieux.
Without point separation the exact replacement is Theorem 1.4. For interval magasins the exact
replacement is the nonisolated-core criterion of Theorem 1.7. In particular, for a connected poset
L, Booleanity of ΣM (L) is equivalent to totality of L, hence to disjointness of distinct lieux. Thus
this precise connected interpretation of the qualitative claim in L3 is true. The unqualified claim
fails even in a divisible interval magasin with genuine interval strata: take L = Q ∐ {c}, with c
incomparable to every rational.
Proof. The two-vertex graph has N (∅) = L and N (A) = ∅ for every nonempty A, proving the
first claim and also showing why the atom question is weaker than separation. Faithfulness on
distinct lieux forces orthogonality, since they have no common interior lower bound. Conversely
if distinct lieux are orthogonal, detection says X ⁄⊥ Y precisely when UX ∩ UY ⁄= ∅. This is
equivalent to a common interior refinement: a common lieu is one, and any common refinement
contains a lieu. Thus the magasin is faithful. Its support lattice is P(L) under Theorem 1.14,
with distinct singleton atoms. Conversely distinct atoms of a Boolean algebra are orthogonal,
so the corresponding lieux are orthogonal. The same argument proves the last assertion when
singleton separation is assumed. The four-lieu graph consisting of two disjoint edges gives M O2:
its supports are zero, the four singletons, and one; it is orthomodular but not distributive. This
is also checked in the code.
For the connected assertion, a connected comparability graph with at least two vertices has
no isolated vertex, so its nonisolated core is all of L. The empty and singleton cases cause no
exception. Apply Theorem 1.7; the lieux of an interval magasin are its point symbols, whose
disjunction is strict comparability.
For the final example the nonisolated core is the chain Q, so Theorem 1.7 proves Booleanity.
