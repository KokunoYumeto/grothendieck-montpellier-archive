Counterexample 2.28 (Refuted: C4 does not force C4 bis in restricted atlases) . Take an
infinite set of independent point objects, both orders equal to identity, and internal disjointness
equal to inequality. Admit only finite figures. All the strong local axioms, finite gluing and At Fil
hold; the latter holds because its parent has only finitely many strata, so local stationarity is joint
stationarity. Every incidence component is one point and distinct components are compatible, so
C4 holds. The family containing one point figure from every component has no figure union, so
C4 bis fails. This failure concerns an unbounded family and is consistent with At Fil.
Counterexample 2.29 (Refuted: cosupport preservation does not characterize primitive
subdivision). There is a second reason not to identify primitive subdivision with (B.S) without an
extra axiom. For the two-element incidence chain a < x , with all downsets as figures and primitive
subdivision equal to identity, C5, C6, C7 0 and C8 hold tautologically. Derived refinement is
incidence. Both nonempty figures have cosupport {0}, so (B.S) would additionally declare a ≼ x.
C5 says that subdivisions preserve cosupport; it does not say every same-support refinement is
one. The complete two-stratum structure, including every C6 restriction, C7 extension and C8
gluing assignment, is checked in legacy_subdivision.py.
2.9 The 156-9 spatial presentation and the comparison ledger
The typed spatial reconstruction is proved in Theorem 1.24. Its use in the axiom comparison
is precise. At spat 1 gives incidence and symmetric irreflexive internal disjointness. At spat
2 is the comparable-pair implication. At spat 3 places each deployment, injectively, in the
space of ordered spatial figures. On scan 48 that target has two explicit conditions: every label
is nonzero, and distinct labels are orthogonal. For principal labels, orthogonality is exactly
internal disjointness of their generators. Consequently the target membership supplies (B.M4); it
cannot be replaced merely by injectivity into ordered families of disjoint sets. The source itself
requests the additional At 4 in the margins of scans 47 and 49: J156-9-06_transcription.tex,
scans 47–49, and J156-9-07_transcription.tex:11--39 , scan 50. The five-cycle example in
Theorem 1.24 verifies the failure of that weaker untyped reading. The change from “magasin” to
“atelier” on scan 41 changes terminology, not these relations.
Counterexample 2.30 (Refuted: spatiality and divisibility are automatic) . Strong Mag 1–4,
even together with Mog L2 and all the extension existence axioms, do not force At spat 3. In the
two-object model of Counterexample 2.18, internal orthogonality is empty, so the orthoclosure of
either singleton is M . The two elementary deployments have the same single label M and the
same one-point order. They therefore fail the required injectivity.
Conversely the typed spatial axioms do not force divisibility. Take incidence a < t , ρ = id,
and δ equal to inequality on {a, t}. Orthoclosures are singletons. The two deployments are the
labelled singleton {{a}} and the labelled chain {a} < {t}; labels within a deployment are nonzero
and orthogonal, and the deployments are distinct. A top-preserving carrier by inclusion of these
singleton labels exists exactly for an identical source and target, recovering ρ = id. Thus the
fully typed spatial axioms hold. The only lieu is a, but Ut = ∅, so divisibility fails. The finite
certificate checks the orthoclosures and both deployment assertions.
