If “every cell link is connected” is interpreted to include a zero-dimensional spherical link,
the proposed condition is not even necessary for regular CW complexes: such a link is S0.
The preceding counterexample addresses the usual nontrivial interpretations that exclude that
unavoidable exception. Higher-dimensional link connectedness cannot replace spherical lower
intervals.
Counterexample 6.7 (Refuted: connected global links are necessary) . Connectedness of
positive-dimensional global links is not necessary for a poset to be a regular CW face poset.
Proof. Take the abstract simplicial complex with facets {0, 1, 2} and {0, 3, 4}. This is a regular
CW complex with five vertices, six edges and two triangles. The link of the common vertex 0
consists of the two disjoint edges {1, 2} and {3, 4}, so it is one-dimensional and disconnected. Its
link in the barycentric order complex likewise has two components. The finite checker verifies
these assertions. The lower interval below a cell, an upper interval of cofaces, and a global link
must therefore not be treated as interchangeable.
6.4 An exact characterization in all dimensions
For the signed condition, choose one lift of each orientation orbit, using the distinguished positive
lift at vertices. For a cover y < x , write ϵ(y, x) = +1 when these chosen lifts are incident and
ϵ(y, x) = −1 when the opposite lower lift is incident to the chosen upper lift. This notation is
defined by the non-repetition condition below. Changing a chosen lift changes the adjoining signs
but preserves the signed-diamond equations.
Theorem 6.8 (Proved; the unoriented classification is known) . Let M carry free reversal and
an immediate incidence relation invariant under simultaneous reversal. Suppose the induced
relation on orientation orbits generates a poset P . These data arise from a regular CW complex
with oriented cells and distinguished positive zero-cell orientations if and only if the following
conditions hold:
1. Every principal ideal of P is finite.
2. Immediate incidences project exactly to covers of P ; for each cover u < v and each
orientation of v, exactly one orientation of u is incident to it.
3. For every x ∈ P , |∆(P<x)| is homeomorphic to Sd(x)−1 for some integer d(x) ≥ 0.
4. The products of the two incidence signs around every rank-two diamond are opposite; at
an edge the two vertex coefficients have opposite signs relative to the distinguished vertex
orientations.
The spherical condition implies that the poset is graded and that d(x) is its dimension rank. The
theorem allows infinitely many cells, unbounded dimension, and failure of local finiteness. The
empty case gives the empty CW complex.
