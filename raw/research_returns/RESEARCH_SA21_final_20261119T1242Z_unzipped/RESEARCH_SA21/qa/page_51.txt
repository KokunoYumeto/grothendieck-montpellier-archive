Counterexample 6.5 (Refuted: excluding loops alone is insufficient) . Even in finite dimension
two, connected boundaries and the exclusion of loops do not make an oriented maquette regular.
With finite faces per cell, the exact dimension-at-most-two regular class additionally excludes
using both orientations of an underlying edge in one two-cell boundary.
Proof. Take two vertices v0, v1, one non-loop edge e between them, and one formal two-cell f
with both e+ and e− incident to f +. Include simultaneous reversals. More explicitly the signed
vertex incidences are
v+0 ◁ e+, v −1 ◁ e+, v −0 ◁ e−, v +1 ◁ e−.
Each sign of e is incident to each sign of f . For either sign of either vertex there is exactly one
two-step incidence path to each sign of f , and likewise one from the opposite vertex orientation.
Thus Magor 6 holds. The unoriented boundary poset of f has two vertices below a single edge;
its order complex is an interval and cannot decompose the boundary circle of a regular two-cell.
This eight-element signed structure is checked exhaustively by the supplied program.
For the corrected positive statement, a finite connected directed boundary with one incoming
and one outgoing edge per vertex is a cycle. When it has no loop and uses no underlying edge
twice, it embeds as a circle in the underlying graph. Attach a disk along that homeomorphism.
Doing this for every two-cell yields a CW complex with all closed cells embedded. Conversely
regularity gives exactly these boundary embeddings and finite face sets. The number of cells and
the valence at a vertex may be infinite.
