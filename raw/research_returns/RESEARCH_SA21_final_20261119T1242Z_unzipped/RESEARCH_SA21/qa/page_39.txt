Counterexample 4.2 (Refuted: R12 and the literal form of L1) . The literal finite-figure axioms
C1–C3 do not imply representation by finite downward-closed compatibility cliques. The failure
persists after adding C4, C4 bis, and the C5–C8 subdivision axioms, including C8 0, as stated in
156-4, scans 65–87.
Proof. Take the six-element lattice
F = {0, d, a, b, c,1}, 0 < d < a, b, c < 1,
where a, b, c are pairwise incomparable. All omitted comparisons follow by reflexivity or
transitivity. Distinct members of {a, b, c} have join 1 and meet d. All families have suprema, so
C1 holds. The equality-based definition gives
M = {d, a, b, c}, δ (0) = ∅, δ (d) = {d}, δ (u) = {d, u} (u = a, b, c), δ (1) = M.
These sets generate their figures, proving C2. Every pair of figures has upper bound 1;
compatibility is universal and C3 holds. The incidence poset M is connected, since d < a, b, c.
Consequently C4 is vacuous and C4 bis is the identity decomposition into one component.
Define subdivision by equality. The refinement induced by taking a subfigure of a subdivision
is then ≤. C5, invariance of disjointness under subdivision, is immediate. In C6, the subdivision
of G ≤ F induced from F ′ = F is uniquely G′ = G. Induction is the identity on the principal
ideal of F , so preserves every bounded supremum and satisfies the multistrate formula. For C7 0,
G′ = G extends by F ′ = F ; the stronger canonical choice is equally forced. A compatible family
of subdivisions in C8 consists of the original cover members, whose given supremum is its own
subdivision. Both zigzag conditions of C8 0 hold because equality in either subdivision position
forces equality throughout. Moderation holds since each figure has only its identity subdivision.
These verifications agree with the observation in 156-4, scan 81, that C5–C7 are tautological for
equality subdivisions.
