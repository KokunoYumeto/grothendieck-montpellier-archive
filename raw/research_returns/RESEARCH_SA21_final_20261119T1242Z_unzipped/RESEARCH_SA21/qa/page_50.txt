We require an immediate incidence to project to a cover relation of the orbit poset. A merely
acyclic generating relation need not have this property: adding a redundant arrow across two
ranks does not change the generated order. The covering condition is therefore part of the precise
characterization below, unless already included in the definition of immediate incidence. The
orbit relation is a partial order: any cycle of orbits can be lifted, using simultaneous reversal, to
a strict chain from X to either X or X −. The first is impossible, and the second, followed by its
reversed-sign copy, is also a cycle. Thus the quotient order used below is well defined.
6.2 Connected boundaries and the low-dimensional dictionary
Theorem 6.3 (Proved: the connected-boundary axiom) . Let P be any poset and Bx = P<x.
The following are equivalent:
1. Bx is nonempty and connected for either Alexandrov topology;
2. its undirected comparability graph is connected;
3. |∆(Bx)| is nonempty and connected.
If principal ideals are finite, the comparability graph can be replaced by the Hasse graph. Thus
the exact additional incidence axiom for connected cell boundaries is that any two proper faces of
each cell of dimension at least two can be joined by a finite incidence zigzag among its proper
faces. Dimensions zero and one must be excepted.
Proof. A comparability component is both upward and downward closed, hence clopen for either
Alexandrov topology. Conversely a clopen separation cannot separate comparable elements: a
lower-open set containing the greater one contains the smaller one, and its lower-open complement
gives the reverse exclusion. The upper-open proof is dual. This proves the first equivalence. Edges
of the order complex are precisely comparable pairs. Components of its edge graph partition
all simplices and therefore give disjoint open and closed subcomplexes in the weak topology; a
finite edge path is a topological path. This proves the second equivalence. In a finite principal
ideal every comparable pair is joined by a saturated chain. Finally, a regular zero-cell has empty
boundary and a regular one-cell has boundary S0, which is disconnected.
Theorem 6.4 (Proved under the stated corrected incidence conventions) . In dimension at most
one, oriented maquettes are precisely graphs given by vertices V , oriented edges E, a free reversal
e ↦→ e− on E, and an origin map s : E → V . In dimension at most two, the oriented edges below
each selected two-cell orientation form a disjoint union of finite directed cycles and two-sided
infinite directed paths, with each incident vertex used once as origin and once as terminus. If
each cell has finitely many faces, only finite cycles occur.
Proof. Put V = M +0 and E = M1. Magor 6(b) gives the unique origin s(e); applying it to e−
and then reversing gives the unique terminal vertex t(e) = s(e−). The incidences are exactly
s(e)+ ◁ e and t(e)− ◁ e, with their simultaneous reversals. Conversely these formulas recover every
incidence from the graph data and verify the dimension-one axioms. This is Serre’s convention
(Serre, 1977, Chapter I, §2.1, p. 22); the attached study attributes the identification to Michel
Hua’s project, which we credit while giving the independent derivation.
