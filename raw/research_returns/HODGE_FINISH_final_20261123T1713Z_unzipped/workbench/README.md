# Algebraic cycles and motives workbench

This workbench contains two research papers about algebraic cycles on abelian varieties. Paper A proves an equivalence: the Hodge conjecture for all complex abelian varieties is exactly a familywise uniform bound on degrees of cycles at finite-field reductions of CM points. Paper B tests concrete constructions and gives sufficient geometric criteria for the Hodge conjecture on all powers of the stated Mumford fourfolds. Neither paper proves the general Hodge conjecture.

## Follow a statement to its evidence

[STATEMENTS.tsv](STATEMENTS.tsv) lists all 113 preserved and new statement/research tags. [STATEMENT_TAG_MAP.tsv](STATEMENT_TAG_MAP.tsv) connects tags with current labels and printed numbers. The first 89 inherited suffixes are preserved, including historical refuted or superseded assertions; new suffixes 90–113 record their refinements and additions. Printed numbering is not an identifier.

Each version has the three canonical tables `STATEMENTS.tsv`, `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv` and `SOURCES.tsv`, a manifest, its deposit sheet and its relevant checks. Shared [checks](checks/README.md) provide a second navigation route. Historical records are expressly marked; they are not represented as checks of the present PDFs.

An independent re-proof means that a separate worker received the mathematical statement before seeing the manuscript proof. All current workers used the same GPT-6 Astra model family. A targeted check is proof-aware. A computational certificate establishes the specified finite equality or bound, not every geometric claim surrounding it. No human has checked these papers.

## Scope of the conclusions

The family equivalence uses published geometric and arithmetic foundations, not the recent unrefereed CM algebraicity theorems. Some explicit sixfold/eightfold estimates retain precisely identified unrefereed inputs. The positive Gaussian and Eisenstein networks solve diagonal trace conditions; their full deformation and strong-semiregularity problems remain open. The formal half-twist-to-Mumford inference is refuted for the specified operations; the refined quadratic-block criterion and the distinct consequence of the published canonical Kuga–Satake statement are recorded separately.
