\subsection*{H9-T23. Degree-three families of every genus at least two}

\begin{theorem}[H9-T23]
Let $A/k$, $n=2m\ge4$, and its Lefschetz pencil be as in
H9-T19. For every $g\ge2$ and every characteristic,
there is an explicit one-parameter family of degree-three
cyclic covers $C_s\to\mathbf P^1$, with $C_s$ smooth
projective of genus $g$ and the covers unramified above
the critical set $\Sigma$, whose geometric generic
function fields satisfy
\[
 h_L(z,w)=\frac13 h_K(\operatorname{cor}z,
                             \operatorname{cor}w),\qquad
 \operatorname{rad}h_L
       =\operatorname{cor}^{-1}(\operatorname{rad}h_K).
                                                               \tag{H9.126}
\]
All homologically trivial rational codimension-$m$
cycles are admissible. The full homological height
group is positive semidefinite for an abelian fourfold
under 032. The constants are the algebraic closure of
the stated parameter function field. No assertion
about every component of a triple-cover Hurwitz space
is included.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
                    g=2a+3b,\qquad a,b\ge0.
\]
For instance use $b=0$ when $g$ is even, and $b=1$ when
$g$ is odd. The latter is possible since an odd
$g\ge2$ is at least three. Write $q=|\Sigma|$,
let $V$ be the variable pencil system, and let
$r=\operatorname{rank}V>0$ and $H=H^{n-1}(A)$.
As before, $V$ is tame and absolutely irreducible,
including after finite-index restriction, and its
nonzero local monodromies are rank-one transvections.

\paragraph{Characteristic different from three.}
Choose $g$ distinct finite points outside $\Sigma$.
Assign their exponents in blocks consisting of
$a$ pairs $(1,2)$ and $b$ triples $(1,1,1)$.
Thus every exponent $e_i$ is either one or two and
\[
          \sum_{i=1}^{g}e_i=3(a+b).
\]
Choose infinity smooth, set
\[
         f(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{g}(t-b_i)^{e_i},
 \qquad C_s:\ u^3=(t-s)f(t),
 \qquad s\notin\Sigma\cup\{b_i\}.
                                                               \tag{H9.127}
\]
Take the smooth projective normalization. Its branch
points are the $g$ fixed points, the moving point
$s$, and infinity, all totally tamely ramified.
The exponent at infinity is two modulo three.
The moving valuation one makes the extension
connected of degree three.

Smoothness of the normalized family is explicit:
after removing a unit by an \'etale cube-root
extension, every local branch equation is
$u^3=z$ or $u^3=z^2$. Their normalizations are
$z=v^3,u=v$ and $z=v^3,u=v^2$, respectively.
The latter chart also applies at infinity after
the usual line-bundle rescaling. The branch
sections are disjoint, so the normalized curve
family is smooth and proper, with a finite flat
degree-three map. Riemann--Hurwitz gives
\[
             2g(C_s)-2=-6+2(g+2)=2g-2.
\]

For either nontrivial cubic character $\chi$,
put $M_\chi=\mathcal L_\chi(f(t))$. Since
$\sum e_i$ is divisible by three, $M_\chi$
is unramified at infinity. Every one of its
$g\ge2$ finite singularities is nontrivial,
including those of exponent two. The Leray and
middle-extension calculation of H9-T20 gives
the exact nontrivial middle-cohomology piece
\[
       H\otimes MC_\chi(M_\chi)
                    \oplus MC_\chi(V\otimes M_\chi).
                                                               \tag{H9.128}
\]
Both inputs are irreducible and are in Katz's
middle-convolution category. Their finite-drop
sums are $g$ and $q+gr$ respectively, and their
infinity representations are trivial of ranks
one and $r$. Consequently the two transforms
have ranks $g$ and $q+gr$ and infinity forms
\[
 \begin{split}
       J(\chi,2)\oplus J(\chi,1)^{\oplus(g-2)},\\
       J(\chi,2)^{\oplus r}
                \oplus J(\chi,1)^{\oplus(q+(g-2)r)}.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{H9.129}
\]
Katz irreducibility and these nontrivial
unipotent powers show that both transforms
have no finite-orbit vector. Thus the whole
nontrivial deck part, including the fixed
ambient block, has none.

\paragraph{Characteristic three.}
Choose $0$ and infinity smooth, and choose
$a+b$ distinct nonzero finite points outside
$\Sigma$. At $a$ of them put a nonzero simple
pole, and at the remaining $b$ put a nonzero
double pole. For example take
\[
 f(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\frac{c_i}{t-b_i}
          +\sum_{j=1}^{b}\frac{d_j}{(t-c_j')^2},
 \qquad c_i,d_j\ne0,
\]
and use the family
\[
                   C_s:\ w^3-w=f(t)+\frac{s}{t},
                           \qquad s\in\mathbf G_m.
                                                               \tag{H9.130}
\]
All its branch points avoid $\Sigma$. The
simple pole at zero proves degree three and
connectedness for every $s\ne0$.

The simple-pole normalization is (H9.119).
At a double pole with local coordinate $z$,
write the right side as
$c/z^2+d/z+e(z,s)$, with $c$ a unit. The
coordinate $v=z w$ gives the equation
\[
          v^3-z^2v=c z+d z^2+e(z,s)z^3.
                                                               \tag{H9.131}
\]
Its derivative with respect to $z$ at
$z=v=0$ is $-c\ne0$. The pole valuation
forces ramification index three, with
$\operatorname{ord}(z)=3$ and
$\operatorname{ord}(w)=-2$, so $v$ is a
uniformizer. Thus (H9.131) is the smooth
normalized chart, also when the lower
coefficients and the regular term vary.
Away from the poles the cover is \'etale.
These explicit charts prove a smooth proper
curve family and finite flatness of its
degree-three map.

At a simple pole the different exponent is
four. At a double pole a nonidentity deck
translation gives $v\mapsto v+\alpha z$,
whose difference from $v$ has valuation
three. Hence its lower ramification groups
have order three in levels $0,1,2$, and the
different exponent is six. There are $a+1$
simple poles, including zero, and $b$ double
poles. Riemann--Hurwitz therefore gives
\[
       2g(C_s)-2=-6+4(a+1)+6b=2(2a+3b)-2.
\]
In particular the genus is the prescribed $g$.

For a nontrivial additive character indexed
by $\alpha\in\mathbf F_3^\times$, put
$x=1/t$ and define the middle extensions
\[
 \mathcal G_\alpha=\mathcal L_\psi(\alpha f(1/x)),
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_\alpha=(\operatorname{inv}^*V)
                                      \otimes\mathcal G_\alpha.
\]
They are unramified at infinity because
$f$ is regular at zero. The rank-one input
has finite wild poles and hence is an
irreducible Fourier sheaf; the rank-$r$
input is irreducible as a rank-one twist
of $\operatorname{inv}^*V$. The exact
character contribution is
\[
 [s\mapsto\alpha s]^*
   \left(H\otimes\operatorname{NFT}_\psi(\mathcal G_\alpha)
          \oplus\operatorname{NFT}_\psi(\mathcal F_\alpha)\right)
                                      \bigm|_{\mathbf G_m}.
                                                               \tag{H9.132}
\]
The justification is the same compact-support
and middle-extension calculation as (H9.121):
the parameter factor is
$\mathcal L_\psi(\alpha sx)$, wild at
$x=\infty$ when $s\ne0$.

A simple pole has Swan-plus-drop two, and
a double pole has Swan-plus-drop three.
Their sum is $2a+3b=g$. Thus the two
Fourier ranks are $g$ and $q+gr$.
Katz's local rule gives at parameter zero
\[
 \begin{split}
       J(1,2)\oplus J(1,1)^{\oplus(g-2)},\\
       J(1,2)^{\oplus r}
                  \oplus J(1,1)^{\oplus(q+(g-2)r)}.
 \end{split}                                                  \tag{H9.133}
\]
Both transforms are irreducible with
infinite image and have no finite-orbit
vectors. This proves the same conclusion
for the entire nontrivial deck part as
in characteristic different from three.

\paragraph{The common height conclusion.}
In either case put $F=\overline{k(s)}$,
$K=F(t)$, and $L=F(C_s)$ at the geometric
generic parameter.
Both families are separable and unramified
above the old critical set. Their pullback
pencil models are smooth, and all
homologically trivial generic cycles are
admissible by H9-T12(iii). With constants
the algebraic closure of the parameter
function field, every algebraic middle
class has finite monodromy orbit. The
nontrivial deck parts must be zero.
Therefore for an actual Chow closure $Z$
the cycle $Z-3^{-1}Q^*Q_*Z$ is globally
homologically trivial. The height
projection formula gives (H9.126);
surjectivity of corestriction over
$\mathbf Q$ gives its exact radical.
H9-T8 supplies semipositivity in abelian
dimension four under 032.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[H9-T23a: composite families in many positive-genus degrees]
For every $M\ge1$ and $g\ge1$, in every
characteristic there is a family of degree-$2M$
covers of genus $g$ with the same exact
norm-height formula, with factor $1/(2M)$.
For every $M\ge1$ and $g\ge2$, the analogous
assertion holds in degree $3M$.
The families are geometric generic composite
families, not a claim about every Hurwitz
component of that degree and genus.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Start with a degree-$M$ rational-cover
family whose new middle trace kernel has
no finite-orbit vector, supplied by H9-T21
(or the identity for $M=1$).
After shrinking its smooth irreducible
parameter base, require a chosen finite
set of source points, including those
used as branch points below, to avoid
the pulled-back critical set. The old
kernel still has no finite-orbit vector:
restriction to a nonempty open base is
surjective on fundamental groups.

Append either the hyperelliptic
degree-two family of H9-T20 or H9-T22,
or the degree-three family just proved,
over the rational source of the first
cover. Their Fourier or middle-convolution
proofs still apply. Indeed the new pencil
system is the pullback of the original
$V$ and remains absolutely irreducible
by finite-index irreducibility; the
constant sheaves and condition~A are
preserved because all critical values
are unramified. These are exactly the
persistence hypotheses checked in
H9-T21.

For the Artin--Schreier second steps,
the residue parameter runs over
$\mathbf G_m$, so the combined base is
a product with a section over the first
parameter base. For the tame moving
branch point, remove the pulled-back
critical locus and the fixed branch
points from the parameter line. Choose
one additional constant point and
shrink the first base so that it is
always allowed; it supplies a section
of that projection as well. All
resulting parameter spaces are smooth
and irreducible. The middle trace
kernel of the composite decomposes
into the pullback of the first kernel
and the second-step kernel. The first
still has no finite-orbit vector by
section-surjectivity on fundamental
groups. The second has none after
restriction to the geometric generic
fibre of the second parameter, by the
proved Fourier or middle-convolution
calculation. Thus the whole composite
kernel has none. Actual global Chow
projection and the projection formula
give the factor $1/(2M)$ or $1/(3M)$
and the same corestriction radical.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The next parameter gate remains separate.}
The constructions above strictly enlarge
the degrees and genera for which an
explicit actual cover family has the
complete norm-height theorem. They do
not prove irreducibility of a full
triple-cover or general Hurwitz space,
nor that every such component contains
one of these families. Genus one is
deliberately absent from the cubic
construction: a three-branch-point
tame cubic elliptic family, and its
single-double-pole characteristic-three
analogue, can be isotrivial and do not
provide the nontrivial size-two blocks
used here. No genus-one assertion is
inferred from this route.
