The theorem Aut(A_m)=S_m for m>=5, m!=6 is Known. The source leaves
Lemma11.4 as an exercise and its printed overlap table in Theorem11.7
has transposed/inaccurate cases. This note verifies precisely the input
used in D_non_natural_alternating_primitive_models.tex, without treating
that printed table or the unwritten exercise as a correct written proof.

1. Preserving the set of3-cycles.
Let x have cycle type3^a 1^(m-3a), a>=1, m>=5. Its symmetric centralizer
has order3^a a! (m-3a)!. Its alternating centralizer has half that order:
for a=1 the at least two fixed letters give a commuting odd transposition;
for a>=2 the interchange of two3-cycles uses three transpositions and is
an odd centralizer element. Consequently equality of its alternating
centralizer size with that of one3-cycle is equivalent to

 (m-3)!/(m-3a)! = 3^(a-1) a! .

For a=2 the left side is (m-3)(m-4)(m-5), strictly increasing for m>=6,
and equals6 precisely at m=6. For a>=3 it is at least(3a-3)!.
At a=3,6!=720>3^2*3!=54. On replacing a by a+1, the ratio of the left
factorial to the right expression is multiplied by

 (3a)(3a-1)(3a-2)/(3(a+1)) > 1 .

Induction proves strict inequality for every a>=3. Hence when m!=6
an automorphism preserves3-cycles, because it preserves element orders
and centralizer orders. This explicitly supplies the needed exercise.

2. An orientation-independent replacement for the printed overlap table.
For3-cycles x,y, consider P=<x>, Q=<y>. If P=Q, the generated group has
order3 and their supports agree. Otherwise each P has one three-letter
support, and the following are exact:

 support intersection0: <P,Q>=C3 x C3, order9;
 support intersection2: <P,Q>=A4 on their four-letter union, order12;
 support intersection1: <P,Q>=A5 on their five-letter union, order60.

The first assertion follows from disjoint supports. In the second, the
generated subgroup is transitive on four letters and contains an element
of order3; its order divides12 and is divisible by both4 and3, hence is12.
In the third it is transitive on five letters, so its order is a divisor
of60 divisible by15. Order30 would give an index-two subgroup of the
simple group A5. Order15 is impossible: the Sylow3 and Sylow5 subgroups
would both be normal (their possible numbers are forced to1), making
the group cyclic and giving an element of order15, which S5 does not
contain. Its order is therefore60. This proves every case without a
choice of orientation or a product-of-two-cycles case convention.

Thus any automorphism preserving3-cycles preserves support-intersection
cardinality. The order3/9/12/60 test corrects all the table ambiguities.

3. Recovering point stabilizers and completing the known argument.
Let G_i be the point-i stabilizer in A_m. For i=m, it is generated by
the consecutive3-cycles(123),(234),...,(m-3,m-2,m-1). These generate
A_(m-1): the A3 and A4 cases are immediate and follow from step2;
inductively A_(s-1) on the first s-1 letters, together with(s-2,s-1,s),
contains every(i,j,s) by the two-transitivity of A_(s-1) for s-1>=4,
and hence contains all3-cycles. Adjacent generators have support
intersection2. Their automorphic images therefore have a union of
supports of size at most3+(m-4)=m-1. The image of G_m is contained in
some G_j and has the same order, so is equal to G_j. Conjugating this
argument treats each i; the m distinct G_i are permuted.

For completeness, once an automorphism alpha fixes every G_i setwise,
it is the identity without an induction through A4. For every h in A_m,
the identity alpha(h G_i h^-1)=alpha(h) G_i alpha(h)^-1 shows that h
and alpha(h) induce the same permutation on{G_i}. The conjugation action
of A_m on its point stabilizers is its faithful natural action. Hence
alpha(h)=h for every h. An arbitrary alpha can first be adjusted by
conjugation with a permutation in S_m to fix all G_i. This proves that
every automorphism is induced by S_m. The S_m action by conjugation is
faithful, since a permutation commuting with all3-cycles preserves every
three-point support and consequently every point. The resulting map is
therefore exactly Aut(A_m)=S_m for m!=6.

Nothing here needs the structure of Aut(A6): its tautological action on
the360 elements of A6 is faithful and is the sole input at that one
degree. Thus no claim of having independently verified the exceptional
outer-automorphism construction or its exercise is needed.
