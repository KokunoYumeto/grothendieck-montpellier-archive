Local interpretation of the bounded-period anchor comparison
2026-11-20. Unnumbered working note; no new theorem or review movement.

1. A fixed root-chart conductor.

Use the P-stable anchor A1, not the character-saturated generator.
On a strictly local common-M root chart at a node, write the coarse
parameters as x,y and the root parameters as s,t, with s^M=x,t^M=y.
A local equation of the inherited cycle is z=x^c y^d, c,d>=1.
At a single component omit the second root variable.

End(A1) is an S-free module of rank h^2. Scalar inertia acts trivially,
so its equivariance factors through the abelian root quotient. Exact
weight pieces and homogeneous Nakayama give an S-basis of homogeneous
elements. A basis element of character (i,j) becomes invariant after
multiplication by s^a t^b, with 0<=a,b<M. The element z is divisible by
every such monomial. Therefore

  z End_S(A1) subset image(S tensor_R B1 -> End_S(A1)),
  where B1=q_*End(A1).

The proved comparison has z B1 subset P and P acts regularly on A1.
After a local frame this yields actual fixed inclusions

  z^2 Mat_h(S) subset P tensor_R S subset Mat_h(S).

The right map is injective because it is generically an isomorphism
between S-free modules of equal rank. The displayed inclusions and
their witnesses survive arbitrary extension of the coefficient field
and principal Cartier-adic completion. This uses no relative reflexive
hull and no new choice of a root lattice in a parameter family.

2. Why the analogous saturation of a varying module is insufficient.

Here is an exact local algebra diagnostic. It is not asserted that this
particular order is the canonical full pullback of an order in the
surface-cover family. It disproves the unrestricted inference from an
exact order action on a coefficient-flat ordinary bundle to a flat
matrix-saturated lattice.

Let T=k[epsilon]/(epsilon^2), S=T[[z]], and take h>=2 with h invertible
in k if that additional hypothesis is desired. Put

  P=S I_h + z Mat_h(S),       g=I_h+(epsilon/z)E_12.

The inverse is I_h-(epsilon/z)E_12. Conjugation defines an integral
unital action of P on the free ordinary module E=S^h: for a=cI_h+zB,

  g a g^-1 = cI_h+zB+epsilon(E_12 B-B E_12).

In particular no z denominator remains in the action of P. For h=2,
rho(zE_21)=zE_21+epsilon(E_11-E_22), and the formula is valid in every
characteristic. The order admits the explicit scaled separability
tensor h^-1 sum_(i,j) (zE_ij) tensor (zE_ji), with product z^2 I_h,
whenever h is invertible.

After inverting z this action extends to Mat_h(S[1/z]). Its stable
matrix lattice generated by E is

  Mat_h(S) dot E = S^h + (epsilon/z) S^h,

where matrix action means the conjugated action. To verify equality,
g^-1 E is generated by the ordinary basis together with an epsilon/z
term; applying ordinary matrix units spreads that term to every
coordinate. Multiplication by g leaves the resulting module unchanged
because epsilon^2=0.

This lattice is not T-flat. The element epsilon z^-1 e_1 is killed by
epsilon, but it is not in epsilon times the lattice: that image is
epsilon k[[z]]^h. Flat T-modules satisfy ker(epsilon)=im(epsilon), by
tensoring the usual exact periodic sequence for the dual numbers.

Thus a fixed bounded conductor and exact action do not justify relative
root-stack local freeness via saturation. The safe alternative is to
construct/algebraize the ordinary locally free module with its exact
order action, and use Azumaya Morita on the puncture U only. There the
matrix algebra is genuinely Azumaya and the underlying module is
locally free, so its Morita inverse is a genuine vector bundle. This
note does not supply global Frobenius/unit/cocycle effectivity.
