\subsection*{A precise duality reduction of the finite-support generation defect}

Let $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a proper regular resolution of a complete
normal local surface, and let $Z>0$ have full exceptional support.  Let
$\mathcal B$ be an ordinary locally free algebra on $Y$, and let $E$ be an
ordinary locally free right $\mathcal B$-module.  Assume that an actual map
\[
             \mathcal B^{n}\longrightarrow E(-Z)
\]
has cokernel of dimension zero.  The integer $n$ is unrestricted.  This is
the precise generic-generation conclusion supplied by the canonical
saturation identity $E=(Q\mathcal B)^{\vee\vee}$ and the actual ordinary
generating quotient of $Q(-Z)$; it does not say that $n$ can be replaced
by the generic order rank plus one.

\paragraph{The conditional vanishing input.}
Fix an integer $a\geq0$.  Suppose that, for every closed exceptional point
$P$ and every sufficiently large integer $b$, one has
\[
 H^1\bigl(bZ,\,\mathcal I_{P,bZ}\otimes
                    \mathcal B|_{bZ}(-aZ)\bigr)=0.       \tag{B.GD.1}
\]
Here $\mathcal I_{P,bZ}$ is the actual ideal of the reduced closed point
in the nonreduced Cartier curve $bZ$.  The threshold in $b$ may depend on
$P$; the integer $a$ is the quantitative input whose uniformity would be
needed in the application.  This note does not prove (B.GD.1).

Under this hypothesis the underlying ordinary bundle $E(-(a+1)Z)$ is
globally generated.  In particular, a separate numerical bound for the
zero-dimensional saturation quotient is unnecessary if the actual
order-valued point-ideal vanishing (B.GD.1) can be proved.

\paragraph{Exact proof on a nonreduced cycle.}
Put $D=bZ$.  Tensor the displayed generic generating map first by
$\mathcal O_D(-aZ)$ and then by $\mathcal I_{P,D}$.  The resulting map
\[
 (\mathcal I_{P,D}\otimes\mathcal B_D(-aZ))^n
       \longrightarrow
       \mathcal I_{P,D}\otimes E_D(-(a+1)Z)              \tag{B.GD.2}
\]
has zero-dimensional cokernel.  This follows by localizing at each
generic point of $D$: the original map was surjective there, and
tensoring is right exact, including over the full Artinian generic ring
of a nonreduced component.

The curve $D$ is Cohen--Macaulay and Gorenstein because it is Cartier
on a regular surface.  Its dualizing sheaf $\omega_D$ has no nonzero
zero-dimensional subsheaf.  Therefore applying
$\operatorname{Hom}_D(-,\omega_D)$ to (B.GD.2) gives an injection from
the Hom of its target into the Hom of its source.  Indeed, a morphism
from the target which vanishes on the image factors through the
zero-dimensional cokernel and is zero.  Serre duality for this proper
Cohen--Macaulay curve and (B.GD.1) consequently give
\[
 H^1\bigl(D,\mathcal I_{P,D}\otimes
                         E_D(-(a+1)Z)\bigr)=0.         \tag{B.GD.3}
\]
No inference from a divisor sandwich or from the dimension of an Euler
characteristic is used here.  The argument is valid for the actual
point ideal, not merely for a line bundle which approximates it.

\paragraph{Passage back to the proper surface.}
Set $F=\mathcal I_{P,Y}\otimes E(-(a+1)Z)$.  Restriction to $D=bZ$
has a natural surjection
\[
 F|_D\longrightarrow
        \mathcal I_{P,D}\otimes E_D(-(a+1)Z).
\]
Its kernel is supported at $P$: away from $P$ both point ideals are the
unit ideal.  Hence its first and second cohomology vanish, and (B.GD.3)
implies $H^1(D,F|_D)=0$.  This explicitly retains the possible
zero-dimensional Tor term from restricting $\mathcal I_{P,Y}$.

The powers of the ideal of $Z$ and the powers of
$\mathfrak m_A\mathcal O_Y$ define cofinal topologies, since their
radicals have the same support.  The theorem on formal functions thus
identifies the completion of the finite $A$-module $H^1(Y,F)$ with the
inverse limit of these zero groups.  Since $A$ is complete, it follows
that $H^1(Y,F)=0$.  The point-ideal sequence now makes
\[
 H^0(Y,E(-(a+1)Z))\longrightarrow E(-(a+1)Z)\otimes k(P)
\]
surjective.  This holds for every closed point of the exceptional
fibre.  Choose finitely many global sections generating along that
proper fibre.  The cokernel of their evaluation is coherent with
proper support over the local base.  If nonempty, its image would be
a nonempty closed subset of $\operatorname{Spec}A$, hence would contain
the closed point, a contradiction.  Thus the chosen sections generate
on all of $Y$.

\paragraph{What this reduction does and does not remove.}
The argument absorbs an unbounded finite-support saturation defect;
it does not create the vanishing (B.GD.1).  In particular neither
$P\subset\mathcal B\subset P(C)$ nor generic order generation implies
that vanishing.  The separate nonminimal-anchor diagnostics show that
fixed conductor bounds can coexist with arbitrarily large endomorphism
cohomology.  The root-normal examples also show that balanced reduced
spectral data need not give ordinary generation on a zero-intersection
$(-2)$-component.  Thus (B.GD.1) must be proved for a suitably chosen
actual anchor, with all root-normal and intercomponent contributions
retained, or replaced by a different actual small-source construction.

If (B.GD.1) were uniform, the already checked incidence calculation
would then give $s+1$ order generators for $E(-(a+1)Z)$ when $E$ is
locally $\mathcal B^s$.  Uniformly controlled rank-one order syzygies,
their whole-cycle coefficient complexes, and compatibility with the
fixed object Frobenius poles would still be needed for the
$O(ftr^2)$ atlas.  No all-coefficient family saturation, new
representation count, or main-gate reduction is claimed here.
