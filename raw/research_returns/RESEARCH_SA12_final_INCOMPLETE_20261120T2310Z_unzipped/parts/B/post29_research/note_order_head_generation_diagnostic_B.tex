\subsection*{Semisimple-head evaluation and actual order generation}

Retain the setting of the generic-order-generation duality note.  On a
proper nonreduced Cartier curve $D$, let $B$ be an ordinary locally free
algebra and let $E$ be locally $B^s$ as a right module.  For a closed
point $P$, define the actual two-sided ideal sheaf
\[
 J_{B,P}=\ker\bigl[B\longrightarrow
                   B(P)/\operatorname{rad}B(P)\bigr].    \tag{B.HD.1}
\]
It equals $B$ away from $P$.  In particular $EJ_{B,P}$ is locally
$J_{B,P}^s$, is Cohen--Macaulay as an ordinary module on $D$, and its
quotient in $E$ is the actual semisimple head at $P$.

\paragraph{A valid conditional weakening of the point-ideal criterion.}
Suppose $B^n\to E(-Z)$ has zero-dimensional cokernel.  Multiplying its
image by the two-sided ideal (B.HD.1) gives
\[
 J_{B,P}(-aZ)^n\longrightarrow EJ_{B,P}(-(a+1)Z)         \tag{B.HD.2}
\]
with zero-dimensional cokernel.  At every full Artinian generic ring
of $D$, the ideal is the unit ideal and the original map was
surjective.  A map from the target of (B.HD.2) to $\omega_D$ which
vanishes on the image factors through a zero-dimensional module and
is zero.  Nonreduced curve duality therefore proves the implication
\[
 H^1(D,J_{B,P}(-aZ))=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 H^1(D,EJ_{B,P}(-(a+1)Z))=0.                            \tag{B.HD.3}
\]
The latter makes global evaluation surjective, as a map of
$k$-vector spaces, onto
\[
             E(-(a+1)Z)/EJ_{B,P}(-(a+1)Z).             \tag{B.HD.4}
\]
If the vanishing holds for every $P$ on all cofinal thickenings,
the same formal-functions argument as in the previous note applies
on the proper surface.  The possible kernel on restricting the
surface ideal (B.HD.1) to $D$ is supported at $P$ and has no positive
cohomology; it is not discarded as though restriction were exact.

Surjectivity onto (B.HD.4) is sufficient for the earlier incidence
argument.  Write $B(P)/\operatorname{rad}B(P)=\prod_i\operatorname{Mat}_{d_i}(k)$.
For $b$ candidate sections the failure locus in each block has
codimension $d_i(b-s)+1$, because evaluation onto the whole head is
surjective.  Thus $s+1$ general global sections generate $E$ over $B$
on the proper curve.  This criterion is weaker than ordinary fibre
evaluation-surjectivity, but it remains a sufficient condition, not
a necessary condition for order generation.

\paragraph{The exact distinction from generation.}
Let $W$ be the image of the global sections in the head.  Generation
requires
\[
           W\cdot\bigl(B(P)/\operatorname{rad}B(P)\bigr)
                         =E(P)/E(P)\operatorname{rad}B(P). \tag{B.HD.5}
\]
Equivalently, every actual nonzero simple quotient of the head must
receive a nonzero image from $W$.  The quantifier is over all simple
quotient maps, not merely over their isomorphism classes; for a matrix
block the various quotient maps encode the required rank condition.
Equation (B.HD.5) does not require $W$ itself to be the whole head as
a $k$-vector space.  For the regular module over $k^h$, the single
vector $(1,\ldots,1)$ generates the module, although its scalar span
has dimension one.

\paragraph{An exact normalized toric obstruction to head-vector surjectivity.}
Take the specified toric $A_1$ model with common root order $h=2$,
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from
$2$.  Let $A_2$ be the rank-two Heisenberg anchor and let
$B=q_*\mathcal End(A_2)$ denote its coarse endomorphism order.
As an ordinary module it is the character sum of the toric double
cover.  Write the three rays as
\[
                         (1,0),\quad(1,1),\quad(1,2).
\]
For the character $(a,b)/2$, $a,b\in\{0,1\}$, the corresponding line
summand on the exceptional $E\simeq\mathbb P^1$ has degree
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac a2\right\rfloor
 +\left\lfloor\frac{a+2b}{2}\right\rfloor
 -2\left\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
Consequently the four degrees, in order $00,10,01,11$, are
\[
                             0,\quad0,\quad1,\quad-1. \tag{B.HD.6}
\]
This is a calculation of the full coarse order restricted to $E$,
not just the reduced spectral line of the anchor.

At a smooth point of $E$ away from the two toric ends, the normal
inertia character is $a+b\pmod2$.  The two odd-character summands
map into the radical of $B(P)$.  The semisimple quotient retains
precisely characters $00$ and $11$ and is $k^2$.  Equivalently the
degree-two spectral cover is etale at $P$, and its two geometric
points give these two simple quotients.  The nontrivial normal-zero
summand is $\mathcal O_E(-1)$ and has no global sections.  Thus
\[
       \operatorname{im}\bigl[H^0(E,B|_E)\to
                       B(P)/\operatorname{rad}B(P)\bigr]
                         =k\cdot(1,1)\subsetneq k^2.   \tag{B.HD.7}
\]
The unit $1$ nevertheless generates $B$ as a right $B$-module.
There is no failure of order generation in this example.

Since all four line summands in (B.HD.6) have vanishing $H^1$,
the exact sequence defining $J_{B|_E,P}$ shows
\[
                         h^1(E,J_{B|_E,P})=1.          \tag{B.HD.8}
\]
If an inherited cycle $Z$ has $Z\cdot E=0$, all its line twists
restrict trivially to $E$, so (B.HD.7)--(B.HD.8) persist under every
fixed twist by $-aZ$.  More generally, on any proper thickening
containing this $E$ and retaining this order restriction, global
sections restrict through $H^0(E,B|_E)$.  They therefore cannot
surject onto the head at $P$; the corresponding $H^1$ of the
head-kernel ideal is nonzero by its exact sequence.

Thus the conditional reduction (B.HD.3) is correct, but its uniform
vanishing hypothesis cannot be deduced merely from the specified
full toric normalization.  An effective hybrid argument must allow
the order-generated regular module in this example and keep the
discrete full block category, rather than requiring vector-space
surjectivity onto every head.  This does not refute an optimized
global order-module presentation or the requested hereditary count.
