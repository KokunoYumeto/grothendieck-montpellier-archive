# Exact limitation on killing a degree-zero first obstruction

Bhatt, B. (2012). Derived splinters in positive characteristic. *Compositio Mathematica, 148*(6), 1757–1786. https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X12000309

The published primary PDF was opened in full at https://compositio.nl/Content/prize2016_bhatt.pdf. The exact relevant locators are Lemma7.8, printed1774–1775, and Example7.9, printed1775–1776. The older22-page author manuscript has the corresponding construction in Examples6.7–6.8, printed14–16. The published genus hypothesis is g≥2; a web parser misreads the inequality as >.

For each positive characteristic and genus g≥2, the proof constructs suitable smooth projective curves C and degree-zero line bundles M over a sufficiently large ground field. The line bundle has injective Frobenius pullback on H1 for every iterate and avoids the finitely many degree-zero composition factors from each finite etale cover. The proof works in the abelian category of semistable bundles of degree zero; its subquotient language should be understood in that category. The resulting Example7.9 proves that H1(C,M) maps injectively under EVERY finite surjective curve cover, including inseparable covers. Since M is nontrivial of degree zero, Riemann–Roch gives h1=g−1>0.

The proof factors a dominating finite cover into Frobenius and a generically separable cover. A kernel class would inject M^(-p^e) into the quotient of the latter pushforward by O_C. The degree-zero part of its Harder–Narasimhan filtration forms an algebra and corresponds to an etale intermediate cover, contradicting the chosen degree-zero composition factors. This is the exact obstruction to replacing our torsion hypothesis by arbitrary degree zero in the class-killing lemma.

No realization of such a class as the first infinitesimal neighborhood of a contractible negative curve is established here. No failure of finite exceptional wild image, and no counterexample to D.1/D.2(ii), follows from the curve statement alone. The note records a genuine limitation of one proof method, not closure of the remaining geometric problem.
