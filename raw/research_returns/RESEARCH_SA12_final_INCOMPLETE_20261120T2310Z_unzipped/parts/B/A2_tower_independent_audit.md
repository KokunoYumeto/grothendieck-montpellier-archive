## Finite group and local construction

We checked the quotient of the regular module independently in coordinates `(b,u;v)`, with multiplication

`(b,u;v)(b',u';v') = (b+b', u+u'+b'v; v+v')`.

The subgroup `H={b=0}` is elementary abelian of rank twice `dim V`; the involution conjugates `(u,v)` to `(u+v,v)`. Changing coordinates to `(u,u+v)` gives the two swapped factors in the written proof. The transfer is `(0,v;0)` in these coordinates, or `(v,v)` in the swapped coordinates. This holds both for elements in H and for elements in its other coset. Thus the map detected on ideal classes is the diagonal injection of V, not multiplication by two on V.

The ray modulus can be chosen V-invariant without changing the prescribed open local squareclasses. Its ray class field E is Galois over F. Choosing a Frobenius conjugacy class containing the specified element of Gal(E/M), and then a prime with that precise representative, gives a prime of F splitting completely in M. Its selected M-prime has the required ray class of the principal fractional ideal `(b)`. Consequently it is `(eta)` with `eta/b` congruent to one at the modulus; integrality of eta follows from its integral principal ideal. No extra inverse-Galois theorem is needed.

Distinct conjugate prime divisors make all conjugate radicands independent modulo squares. The presentation by these independent square roots permits an actual V-action permuting labeled roots, so the Galois group is the stated split semidirect product. At the one new prime, inertia is generated by one basis element; its image is an involution outside H, hence its intersection with H is trivial. At all other odd primes the radicands are units. At the dyadic primes the imposed unramified-or-square classes control the otherwise delicate ramification.

For a prescribed old prime with nonzero Frobenius v in V, exactly the two conjugate radicands indexed by `g,g+v` have the nontrivial unramified class at a fixed M-prime. The Frobenius square maps to `(0,v)`. The group square formula `(b,u;v)^2=(0,bv;0)` therefore forces b=1. For v=0, one basis element is the Frobenius, again with b=1 and now of order two. This proves inertness in L/F at every prescribed prime and complete splitting in N/L at every dyadic prime. The transfer/Artin diagram then proves injectivity after reduction modulo two, which is stronger than injectivity before reduction or merely increasing finite-stage class ranks.

## Infinite tower and all coefficients

Every fixed rational prime is eventually inert and unramified at every later quadratic step. It therefore has finitely many prolongations and bounded ramification. The local rings of the full integral closure R are discrete valuation rings, and each nonzero element has finitely many prime divisors. The written finite-generation argument for an arbitrary nonzero ideal, using one element and a minimum-valuation witness at each of its finitely many prime divisors, proves noetherianity without assuming it. Thus R is Dedekind.

For every finite extension T/K, the Brauer argument is correctly performed after choosing a finite number-field stage E. After adjoining F_m, the completion-degree tower identity gives

`v_2([(EF_m)_{w_m}:E_w]) >= m-n-v_2([E_w:(F_n)_v])`.

The bound is uniform over all prolongations of each fixed w and grows without bound. A sufficiently large common stage kills the finitely many nonzero local invariants of any descended two-torsion Brauer class. Every archimedean place is complex. Brauer--Hasse--Noether therefore proves Br(T)[2]=0 for every finite T/K, not merely for K itself.

The subsequent passage to arbitrary finite F_2-Galois modules is sound: induced permutation modules surject onto the module, their second cohomology vanishes by Shapiro, and the kernel's third cohomology vanishes by the preliminary cd_2<=2 bound. Coefficient dévissage and filtered colimits give the full cd_2<=1 statement. A DVR uniformizer supplies a nonsquare and the lower bound one.

Picard groups commute with the filtered ring colimit. The proved injections modulo two and doubling of dimensions give an infinite-dimensional limit. Any nonempty open of the noetherian integral curve removes only finitely many closed points, so its Picard group modulo two remains infinite dimensional. Kummer gives a nonzero degree-two class on every such open; the independently proved curve bound gives exact dimension two on affine opens. This refutes all eventual-equality quantifiers of A.2.
