      Abstract. We give a detailed account of the proof of a purity theorem for finite flat torsors due to Laurent
      Moret-Bailly. In addition, we show that a part of this result, namely the full faithfulness, holds also under
      weaker assumptions.

      Keywords. Torsors, finite group schemes, purity.

Introduction

The purpose of this text is to give a more concise and convenient form to some contents of the
author’s Master thesis [7], together with an additional result which is surely known to the experts,
but was left as an open question in loc. cit. More specifically, we give an account of the proof
of a purity theorem for finite flat torsors due to Laurent Moret-Bailly (Theorem 6), which was
originally published without proof in [8]. The statement is analogous to the Zariski–Nagata purity
theorem for finite étale coverings (see [5, X, §3]). The proof is based on Auslander’s method
from [1] and relies on a crucial result from loc. cit. (Theorem 5). As hinted above, this proof
follows an argument suggested by Moret-Bailly. We remark that a slightly more general theorem
was recently obtained in [2, Theorem 7.1.3] using a similar strategy. The goal of this note is to
provide a fully detailed proof of Moret-Bailly’s result and to show that a part of it, namely the full
faithfulness, holds also under weaker assumptions (Proposition 3).

Notation. For any scheme S, we denote by O S its structure sheaf. Let S be a scheme, G an affine
S-group-scheme. We denote by Sch/S the category of S-schemes and by Tors(S,G) the category
of fppf G-torsors over S. If T → S is a morphism of schemes and X is any S-scheme, we write X T
for the pullback X ×S T ; we will consider G T as a T -group-scheme and, if X is an fppf G-torsor
over S, we will consider X T as a G T -torsor over T . If X and Y are two S-schemes, we denote by
HomS (X , Y ) the set of homomorphisms of S-schemes from X to Y ; if X and Y are fppf G-torsors
over S, we write Hom(S,G) (X , Y ) for the set of homomorphisms from X to Y in Tors(S,G). Regular
schemes are assumed to be locally Noetherian.

  We first address the full faithfulness statement, which is an easy consequence of the following
two lemmas; the first one is simply recalled from [3].

Lemma 1 ([3, Corollaire 6.1.14]). Let S be a locally Noetherian normal scheme, T → S an integral
morphism of schemes, U ⊆ S a dense open subscheme. The restriction map
                                   HomS (S, T ) −→ HomS (U , T )
is bijective.

Lemma 2. Let S be a scheme, G an affine S-group-scheme, X and Y two fppf G-torsors over S. The
following fppf sheaf of sets (with the obvious restriction maps):
                              HomG (X , Y ) : Sch/S −→ Sets
                                         (T → S) 7−→ Hom(T,G T ) (X T , YT )
is representable by an affine S-scheme, which is fppf locally isomorphic to G. In particular, if G is
finite over S, then HomG (X , Y ) is representable by a finite S-scheme.

Proof. We may argue in a similar way as for the representability of fppf torsors with respect to
an affine group scheme. Namely, the question is fppf local on S by effective descent of affine
schemes (see e.g. [9, Tag 0245]). Thus, we are reduced to the situation where X and Y are trivial
G-torsors, in which case HomG (X , Y ) is isomorphic to G as a presheaf on Sch/S and the claim is
clear. The final statement of the lemma follows because the property of being finite is fppf local
on the base.                                                                                    □
Proposition 3. Let S be a locally Noetherian normal scheme, U ⊆ S a dense open subscheme, G a
finite S-group-scheme. The pullback functor
                                     Tors(S,G) −→ Tors(U ,GU )
is fully faithful.

Proof. Let X , Y be fppf G-torsors over S. We have to show that the restriction map
                              Hom(S,G) (X , Y ) −→ Hom(U ,GU ) (XU , YU )                        (∗)
is bijective. Let Z be the finite S-scheme representing HomG (X , Y ) by Lemma 2. The map (∗)
may then be identified with the restriction map:
                                   HomS (S, Z ) −→ HomS (U , Z ),
which is bijective by Lemma 1 because Z is integral over S.                                        □
Remark 4. The proposition could also be proved bypassing Lemma 2. Indeed, the bijectivity
of (∗) may be checked fppf locally, thus reducing the matter to the bijectivity of the restriction
map HomS (S,G) → HomS (U ,G), which holds by Lemma 1.

   Regarding the essential surjectivity of the pullback functor above, we now report a result due
to Laurent Moret-Bailly giving a sufficient condition. The statement is analogous to the Zariski–
Nagata purity theorem for finite étale coverings (see [5, X, §3]). We provide here a proof, omitted
in the original publication [8], based on a sketch which the author of loc. cit. communicated to
us. The argument relies on a crucial result by Maurice Auslander, which we recall for the reader’s
convenience.

Theorem 5 ([1, Theorem 1.3]). Let R be a regular local ring, M a finitely generated reflexive R-
module. Suppose that the R-module of endomorphisms EndR (M ) is isomorphic to a direct sum of
copies of M . Then, M is a free R-module.

   We now come to Moret-Bailly’s purity theorem for finite flat torsors.

Theorem 6 ([8, Lemme 2]). Let S be a regular scheme, U ⊆ S an open subscheme, G a finite flat
S-group-scheme. Suppose that the complement of U in S has codimension at least 2. Then, the
pullback functor
                                 Tors(S,G) −→ Tors(U ,GU )
is an equivalence of categories.

Proof. Full faithfulness follows from Proposition 3. To show that we have an equivalence of
categories, we will exhibit, for any fppf GU -torsor over U , an object of Tors(S,G) which restricts
to the given one over U .
     Let j : U → S denote the inclusion morphism, let X be an fppf GU -torsor over U and denote
by ρ : GU ×U X → X the GU -action on X . Because X → U is finite flat, it corresponds to a
coherent OU -algebra P , which is locally free as an OU -module. Consider the quasi-coherent O S -
algebra Q := j ∗ P , which satisfies Q|U = P . Then, Q corresponds to an affine S-scheme Xe such
that Xe U = X . Let now G denote the coherent O S -algebra corresponding to G → S. Then, G |U
is the coherent OU -algebra corresponding to GU → U and ρ corresponds to a homomorphism
of OU -algebras ρ # : P → P ⊗OU G |U . We set ρe# := j ∗ ρ # : Q → Q ⊗OS G , where j ∗ (P ⊗OU G |U ) ∼  =
Q ⊗OS G because G is finite locally free as an O S -module. We have that ρe# is a homomorphism
of O S -algebras satisfying ρe# |U = ρ # , hence it corresponds to a homomorphism of S-schemes
ρe : G ×S Xe → Xe whose pullback along j is ρ. By functoriality of j ∗ (and of the identification used
in the definition of ρe# ), ρe verifies the rules of a G-action on Xe . It remains to see that ρe makes Xe
an fppf G-torsor over S.
    We first observe that Q is a coherent O S -module by [4, Corollaire 5.11.4], because S is regular,
P is finite locally free and the codimension of S \U in S is at least 2. In other words, Xe is a finite
S-scheme. Next, we claim that Q is a locally free O S -module. In order to prove this, consider
the O S -modules of homomorphisms (respectively endomorphisms) F := HomOS (G , Q), H :=
EndOS (Q). We define a homomorphism of O S -modules θ : F → H by setting, for V ⊆ S open and
u : G |V → Q|V a homomorphism of OV -modules:
                                           ρ̃ # |V                  (id,u)
                             θV (u) : Q|V −−−→ Q|V ⊗OV G |V −−−→ Q|V ,
where the last map uses the OV -algebra structure of Q|V . We argue that θ|U is an isomorphism.
Indeed, let Ω# : P ⊗OU P → P ⊗OU G |U be the homomorphism of OU -algebras given by the
canonical map P → P ⊗OU G |U on the first component and ρ # on the second component.
Because X is a GU -torsor over U (with respect to ρ), we have that Ω# is an isomorphism. This
allows to find an inverse (θ|U )−1 : H |U →→ F |U of θ|U , namely, for V ⊆ U open and v an
endomorphism of the OV -module Q|V = P |V :

       (θ|U )V−1 (v) : G |V −−−−−→ P |V −−−−−→ ⊗OV G |V −−−−−→ P |V −−−−−→ ⊗OV P |V −−−→ P |V ,
where the first arrow is the canonical map of OV -algebras. Note now that, because Q is the
pushforward of an OU -module along j and it is coherent, then, by [4, Théorème 5.10.5], Q has
depth at least 2 at all points of S \ U . Therefore, the same holds for F and H . But then, by
loc. cit. (now used in the opposite direction than above), the fact that θ is an isomorphism over U
is enough to deduce that θ is an isomorphism of O S -modules. In particular, for every point s ∈ S,
we have an isomorphism of the stalks EndOS,s (Qs ) = H s ∼     = Fs = HomOS,s (G s , Qs ). Because G is
locally free, it follows that EndOS,s (Qs ) is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of Qs . Moreover,
Qs is a finitely generated reflexive O S,s -module, because Q is coherent, locally free over U and, as
observed above, it has depth at least 2 on S \U (then one uses the criterion [9, Tag 0AVA]). Thus,
for every s ∈ S, we have that Qs is a free O S,s -module by Auslander’s result (Theorem 5), since S
is regular. This proves that Q is a locally free O S -module, hence that Xe is a flat S-scheme. In fact,
Xe → S is an fppf covering. Indeed, it only remains to see that it is surjective, but this follows from

   A consequence of the previous theorem is that, given a regular base scheme, the obstruction
to extend a finite flat torsor from a dense open subscheme lies exclusively in codimension 1.
A slightly more general version of this result, based on a similar proof, is given in [2, Theo-
rem 7.1.3]. For a non-Noetherian analogue (in the context of algebraic spaces over Prüfer bases),
see [6, Theorem 6.8].
