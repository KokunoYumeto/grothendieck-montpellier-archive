                                                   Abstract. Let AK be an Abelian variety over the quotient field of a Dedekind
                                                   domain R. We show that the identity component of the Néron model of AK
                                                   acts regularly on any minimal model of AK over Spec R, and discuss when the
                                                   Néron model is an open subset of a minimal model.
                                                       The main technical result is the rigidity of small modifications, leading to
                                                   a regularity criterion for birational group actions.



                                              Let S be a 1-dimensional, irreducible, regular scheme with function field K, and
                                           AK an Abelian variety over K.
                                              Finding a model of AK over S attracted considerable attention. Kodaira de-
                                           scribed minimal, elliptic surfaces over C [Kod60, Kod63]; this was extended to
                                           arbitrary Dedekind domains in [Nér64, Chap.III]. For higher dimensional Abelian
                                           varieties, the optimal smooth group scheme Nér(AK )—called the Néron model—is
                                           constructed in [Nér64, Chap.II]; see Section 2 for a summary.
                                              Minimal model theory—also known as Mori’s program—aims to provide a (non-
                                           unique) projective, minimal model of any variety or scheme; see (6.4) and (16).
                                           For Abelian generic fibers, minimal models give higher dimensional analogs of the
                                           minimal, elliptic surfaces of Kodaira and Néron.
                                              The aim of this note is to study actions of group schemes on minimal models,
                                           and then derive connections between the Néron model and the minimal models.
                                           The main result is Theorem 7, which gives the following for the Néron model.

                         1. Main technical theorems
   Theorem 1 is a special case of the following regularity criterion, whose proof is
given in (38). It can be viewed as a generalization of [Han87, 3.3] and of [Kim24,
Thm.1.3].
Theorem 7. Let S be a normal scheme with function field K, GS → S a smooth
group scheme with connected fibers, and π : XS → S a dominant, projective mor-
phism. Assume that XS has terminal singularities and KXS /S is π-nef.
   Then any rational GK -action µK : GK ×K XK 99K XK on the generic fiber XK
extends to a regular GS -action µS : GS ×S XS → XS .
   Note that the statement tacitly assumes that KXS /S exists. This holds if S is
excellent and has a dualizing complex.
   Theorem 7 is derived from a more general regularity criterion on rational group
actions (37). The full scheme theoretic version is a little technical, the following
variant for varieties, to be proved in (32), gives the right idea.
Theorem 8. Let X be a normal, projective variety over an algebraically closed
field, and G a connected, smooth algebraic group. Let µ : G × X 99K X be a
rational action. Assume that, for general g ∈ G, the map µg : X 99K X is an
isomorphism in codimension 1. Then µ : G × X → X is a regular action.
  Note that we do not assume that there is an open subset U ⊂ X where all the
µg are defined.
9 (Small modifications). Let Xi be normal, proper schemes over S. A birational
map φ : X1 99K X2 is a small modification, if there are closed subsets Zi ⊂ Xi of
codimension ≥ 2 such that φ restricts to an isomorphism φ : (X1 \ Z1 ) ∼
                                                                       = (X2 \ Z2 ).
   The best known examples are flips and flops, much studied when the Xi have
log terminal singularities [KM98]. The case of arbitrary normal varieties attracted
much less attention [Kol91, Sec.2].

                                2. Néron models
   We summarize the properties of Néron models that we use; see [Art86, BLR90]
for complete treatments, and [Lor17] for futher results.
Notation 11. As explained in [BLR90, p.13], the theory of Néron models is local
on the base. Thus, from now on, R denotes a DVR with quotient field K, residue
field k and T := Spec R. For a morphism π : XT → T , we use XK (resp. Xk ) to
denote the generic (resp. closed or central) fiber.
Definition 12. A smooth morphism XT → T satisfies the Néron mapping property
iff the following holds.
 (12.1) Let YT → T be any smooth morphism. Then every morphism φK : YK →
    XK extends to a morphism φT : YT → XT .
A morphism XT → T is a Néron model of its generic fiber XK , if it is smooth, of
finite type, and satisfies the Néron mapping property. We use Nér(XK ) to denote
the Néron model of XK .
    It is clear that XK uniquely determines its Néron model Nér(XK ). Néron models
commute with étale base changes T ′ → T [BLR90, p.13]. Thus many questions
about Néron models can be treaded over the strict Henselisation of T .
    However, ramification usually changes the Néron model drastically.
   [Nér62, Nér64] construct Néron models when XK is a torsor (or principal homo-
geneous space) under an Abelian variety over K. Note that [Art86] and [BLR90]
discuss mainly (13.1).
Theorem 13 (Néron models). Let AK be an Abelian variety over K, and XK an
AK -torsor. Then the following hold.
(13.1) Nér(AK ) exists, and it is a smooth, finite type group scheme over T .
(13.2) Nér(XK ) exists.
(13.3) Nér(XK ) is either XK or a Nér(AK )-torsor.                           
Definition 14. The union of AK and the identity component of Nér(AK )k is the
identity component of Nér(AK ), denoted by Nér◦ (AK ). Note that Nér◦ (AK ) is open
                       NÉRON MODELS AND MINIMAL MODELS                              5


and dense in Nér(AK ), and Nér◦ (AK ) → T is smooth group scheme with connected
fibers.
   The central fiber Nér(XK )k is usually disconnected. Its group of connected com-
ponents is a finite abelian group.
15 (Pseudo-Abelian varieties). When K is not perfact, it is natural to also consider
pseudo-Abelian varieties PK as defined in [Tot13]. These also have Néron models
Nér(PK ) by [BLR90, Sec.10.2]; see also [Ove24] for further results. Theorem 7 also

                    3. Minimal models and Néron models
    An introduction to minimal models is in [KM98], and a summary of later results
is in [Kol23, Secs.11.1–2]. For the purposes of Theorem 1 we need only the following
special setting.
16 (Minimal models of Abelian torsors). Let S be a 1-dimensional, irreducible,
regular scheme with function field K. Let XK be a proper K-variety with terminal
singularities, whose canonical class is numerically trivial.
    A proper morphism π : XS → S is a minimal model of XK if
 (16.1) XS has terminal singularities [KM98, 2.34],
 (16.2) KXS is numerically π-trivial, and
 (16.3) the generic fiber is isomorphic to XK .
Numerically π-trivial means that (KXS · C) = 0 for every curve contracted by π.
    Being terminal is preserved by étale base change [Kol13, 2.15], so being a minimal
model is also preserved by étale base change. Note that minimal model theory
frequently works with Q-factorial schemes, that is, some multiple of any Weil divisor
is required to be Cartier. However, being Q-factorial is not an étale-local property,
so, as in [Kol21], we work without it.
   Minimal models of XK need not be unique, but the following is easy to see
directly; see [KM98, 3.52].
   Claim 16.4. Let B be a normal scheme and πi : Xi → B proper morphisms.
Assume that Xi is irreducible, has terminal singularities, and KXi /B is πi -nef for
i = 1, 2. Let φ : X1 99K X2 be a birational map.
   Then there are closed Zi ⊂ Xi of codimension ≥ 2, such that φ induces an
isomorphism X1 \ Z1 ∼  = X2 \ Z 2 .                                               
   For the existence of minimal models, the main references are [HM07, BCHM10],
with additional steps in [Fuj11, Lai11, LM22].
   Theorem 16.5. Assume that S is over a field of characteristic 0. Let XK be a pro-
jective K-variety with terminal singularities, whose canonical class is numerically
trivial. Then XK has a Q-factorial, projective, minimal model π : XS → S.
   In particular, every Abelian torsor over K has a minimal model.                

                             4. Small modifications
  We work over a Noetherian base scheme. To avoid some possible problems with
dimension, let us assume that it is universally catenary.
Definition 24 (Families of birational maps). A birational map φ : X 99K Y be-
tween (reduced, separated) schemes X, Y is an (equivalence class of) isomorphisms
between dense, open subsets X ⊃ UX ∼     = UY ⊂ Y . The largest such is called the
domain of isomorphy of φ, denoted by domi(φ), and usually viewed as a subset of
X.
   Let pi : Xi → S be equidimenisonal, proper morphisms. A birational map
φ : X1 99K X2 over S is a family of birational maps if domi(φ) contains the generic
points of each fiber. Thus the restrictions φs : red(X1 )s 99K red(X2 )s are birational
for every s ∈ S.
   Warning 24.1. If each φs is an isomorphism, then φ is an isomorphism over a
dense, open subset of S by (25.2), but it can happen that φ is not an isomorphism,
see (27.2) for such an example.
25 (Restriction of birational maps). Let φ : X 99K Y be a birational map, and
WX ⊂ X and WY ⊂ Y closed subsets such that φ restricts to a birational map
φW : WX 99K WY . Then domi(φ) ∩ WX ⊂ domi(φW ), but they need not be equal.
That is, it can happen that φW is an isomorphism at a point x ∈ WX , but φ is not
defined at x.
   It is useful to know that this does not happen if WX is in general position.
   Let S be a normal scheme and π : X → S an irreducible, normal scheme, proper
over S, with geometrically irreducible generic fiber. There is a dense, open S ◦ ⊂ S
over which the fibers are geometrically irreducible. Set S1 := S \ S ◦ . For i ≥ 2 let
Si ⊂ S be the closed subset over which the fiber dimension is ≥ i−1+dim X −dim S.
By a dimension count we see the following.

mension m. If codim(W ∩ Si , Si ) ≥ m for every i, then π −1 (W ) is irreducible. 

Definition 26 (Small modification). An open subset U ⊂ X is called large if X \ U
has codimension ≥ 2 in X. A birational map φ : X1 99K X2 is a small modification
if domi(φ) is large in both X1 and X2 .
    The composite of small modifications is a small modification.
    A family of birational maps φ : X1 99K X2 over S is a family of small modifica-
tions if φs : (X1 )s 99K (X2 )s is a small modification for every s ∈ S.
Remark 27. Given Xi → S, let φ : X1 99K X2 be a small modification. By defini-

φU : U1 ∼= U2 . There is a dense, open subset S ◦ ⊂ S such that (Ui )s ⊂ (Xi )s are
large open subsets for s ∈ S ◦ .

following example illustrates some of the possibilities.
