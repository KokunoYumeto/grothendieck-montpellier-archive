                                                                                      1. Introduction
                                              In [Xu14], Chenyang Xu proved that the algebraic local fundamental group (the profinite
                                           completion of the topological fundamental group of the link) of a complex klt singularity is
                                           finite. This result was then used by Greb–Kebekus–Peternell [GKP13] to show that for a
                                           complex quasi-projective variety X with klt singularities, any sequence of finite quasi-étale
                                           (i.e. étale in codimension one) and generically Galois covers eventually becomes étale. In
                                           particular, for any quasi-projective klt variety X/C there exists a finite quasi-étale generically
                                           Galois cover ρ : X e−→ X such that any further quasi-étale cover Y −   →X  e is actually étale.
                                              Inspired by the relation between klt singularities and strongly F -regular singularities in
                                           characteristic p > 0 [HW02], a subset of the authors showed that if (R, m) is a strictly
                                           Henselian strongly F -regular local ring and U ⊆ Spec R is the regular locus, then π1ét (U) is
                                           finite [CST16], a variant of Xu’s result in characteristic p > 0. In this paper we use this
                                           result, a theorem of Gabber [Tra14], and recent work on the non-local behavior of F -signature
                                           [DSPY16], to prove a variant of the result of Greb–Kebekus–Peternell in characteristic p > 0.
                                              Philosophically, both these results are studying the obstructions of extending finite étale
                                           covers of the regular locus Xreg of a scheme X to the whole scheme. The expected general
                                           answer is that for more severe singularities on X, there are more obstructions. Analogous to
                                           [GKP13], we show that schemes with strongly F -regular singularities are mild in this sense.
                                           Main Theorem (Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.2). Suppose X is an F -finite Noetherian integral
                                           strongly F -regular scheme. Suppose we are given a sequence of finite surjective quasi-étale
                                           (étale in codimension one) morphisms of normal integral schemes
                                                                                         γ1          γ2         γ3
                                                                            X = X0 ←−− X1 ←−− X2 ←−− · · ·
                                           such that each Xi /X is generically Galois. Then all but finitely many of the γi are étale.

                               2. Notation and conventions
Convention 2.1. All rings and schemes considered will be Noetherian. We will typically also
be working with normal connected and hence integral schemes.
Notation 2.2. Let ρ : Y −  → X be a morphism of normal schemes.
  (a) We say that ρ is quasi-étale if it is quasi-finite and there exists a closed subset Z ⊆ Y
      of codimension ≥ 2 such that ρ|Y \Z : Y \ Z −     → X is étale. Notice that if ρ is further
      finite, then Y coincides with the integral closure of X in K(Y ).
  (b) Assume that ρ is finite and surjective. Let W ⊆ X be the largest Zariski-open subset
      such that ρ−1 (W ) − → W is étale. The branch locus of ρ is defined to be X \ W . It
      is worth to remark that by Purity of the Branch Locus, if ρ is quasi-étale then its
      branch locus is contained in the non-regular locus of X.
  (c) If X and Y are connected, we say that ρ finite and surjective is generically Galois
      if the fraction fields extension K(Y )/K(X) is Galois. In this case, it follows that
      ρ−1 (W ) −→ W is Galois, where W is as in (b).
  (d) By a geometric point x̄ ∈ X of a scheme X we mean a morphism Spec L −          → X, where
      L is a separably closed field. Note that x̄ −     → X factors through Spec k(x) −     → X,
      where x ∈ X is the scheme-theoretic image point.
  (e) Suppose that s̄, t̄ ∈ X are geometric points of a scheme X. A specialization of
      geometric points s̄     t̄ is a factorization
