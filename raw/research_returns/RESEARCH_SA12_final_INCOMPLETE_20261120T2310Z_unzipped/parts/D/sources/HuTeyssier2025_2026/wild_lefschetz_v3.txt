                                                  CHARACTERISTIC CYCLE AND WILD LEFSCHETZ THEOREMS

                                                                     HAOYU HU AND JEAN-BAPTISTE TEYSSIER

                                                  A BSTRACT. By relying on a new approach to Lefschetz type questions based on Beilin-
                                                  son’s singular support and Saito’s characteristic cycle, we prove an instance of the wild
                                                  Lefschetz theorem envisioned by Deligne. Our main tool are new finiteness results for
                                                  the characteristic cycles of perverse sheaves.

Beyond the tame case, wild ramification needs to be bounded. A natural way to do
this is by means of an effective Cartier divisor R supported on D.
Definition 1.1. We say that L ∈ Loc(U, Qℓ ) has log conductors bounded by R if for every
morphism f : C → X over k where C is a smooth curve over k not mapped inside D
and for every x ∈ f−1 (D), the higher logarithmic ramification slope of L|C×X U at x as
in [Ser68] is smaller than the multiplicity of f∗ R at x.
   Note that Definition 1.1 makes sense even for X singular and Qℓ replaced by a finite
local ring Λ of residue characteristic ℓ ̸= p. The following remark ensures that when X
is smooth, checking log conductors boundedness by R is easy.
Remark 1.2 ([HT25b, Proposition 5.8]). If X is smooth over k, the sheaf L ∈ Loc(U, Qℓ )
has log conductors bounded by R if for every irreducible component Z of D, the
logarithmic conductor of L at the generic point of Z is smaller than the multiplicity of
Z in R − D.

    As a replacement for surjectivity, Deligne asked [ES21, Introduction] the following
Question 1.3. Let X ⊂ Pk be a normal projective variety and let U be the complement of an
effective Cartier divisor D of X. Given r ≥ 0 and an effective Cartier divisor R supported on D,
can one find a hypersurface H ⊂ Pk such that for every L ∈ Loc(U, Qℓ ) with rank ≤ r and
log conductors bounded by R, the sheaves L and L|U∩H have the same monodromy groups?
  Question 1.3 was answered positively in the rank 1 case in [KS14] when the com-
pactification X is smooth and D is a strict normal crossing divisor and in [ES21] in the
rank 1 case when X is normal and D is an effective Cartier divisor.

Firstly, Theorem 1.4 applies to finite coefficients while Deligne asks for Qℓ -coefficients.

To explain what makes the finite coefficient case already meaningful, observe that the
objects of Loctf (U, Λ) with log conductors bounded by R are the representations of a
quotient π1 (U, R) of π1 (U) classifying finite étale covers with ramification bounded
by R (see [Hir17]). Artin-Schreier’s theory implies that the pro-p abelian quotient
of π1 (U, R) is not topologically finitely generated in general. Thus, π1 (U, R) is not
topologically finitely generated in general. Hence, even in the finite coefficient case,
defining an object of Loctf (U, Λ) requires to specify an infinite number of data. What
Theorem 1.4 says is that these data are captured by a finite number of hyperplane
sections. Secondly, Deligne’s question is about the existence of a single hypersurface
of possibly high degree depending on the divisor R, whereas Theorem 1.4 sticks to
hyperplane sections for an embedding independent of R. Also, we allow some flexibility
in the choice of these hyperplanes. The price to pay for using only hyperplanes is that
several are needed.
Remark 1.6. If one is interested by cutting down the dimension further with higher
codimension projective subspaces, one can construct a moduli of multi-flags containing
a dense open subset of points realizing the Lefschetz theorem. This moduli provides
some flexibility useful in practice. Using it, one can indeed show that the projective
subspaces realizing the Lefschetz theorem can be taken in some prescribed dense open
subset of the Grassmannian. See Theorem 7.24 for the full statement.
   The proofs of Theorem 1.4 rely on a new approach to questions of Lefschetz type
based on Beilinson’s singular support and Saito’s characteristic cycle. Namely given
a quasi-projective variety U ⊂ Pk , a hyperplane H and L ∈ Loctf (U, Λ), we give
a criterion for L and L|U∩H to have the same monodromy group in terms of the
transversality of H ,→ Pk with respect to the singular support of some auxiliary
sheaf constructed from L (see Lemma 5.10 and Proposition 5.11). The whole point
of Theorem 1.4 is then to produce enough hyperplanes satisfying this transversality
criterion, or equivalently to show that the singular supports under scrutiny can be
controlled in some sense.
   To explain how to do this, let us recall Beilinson’s singular support construction in al-
gebraic geometry. For a transcendental construction predating Beilinson’s construction
see [KS90]. For a smooth scheme of finite type X over a perfect field k of characteristic
p > 0 and for a finite field Λ of characteristic ℓ ̸= p, Beilinson associates to every object
K ∈ Dbc (X, Λ) a closed conical subset SS(K) of the cotangent bundle T∗ X of X, called
the singular support of K and recording the hyperplanes through which the étale local
sections of K don’t extend [Bei16]. In particular SS(K) measures how far K is from
being locally constant. The propagation defect of sections through a hyperplane H is
reflected by the non triviality of some monodromy action on the complex of vanishing
cycles of K. The characteristic cycle CC(K) of K is a cycle supported on SS(K) mea-
suring the wild ramification of this monodromy action through the so-called Milnor
formula [Sai17b, Theorem 5.9].

that for a sufficiently nice closed immersion i : X ,→ Pk in some projective space (see

Linear Overview. Section 2 provides an account of Abbes and Saito theory for the
logarithmic conductor. In section 3, we introduce Beilinson’s singular support and
Saito’s characteristic cycle for étale sheaves and recall some terminology from [HT25b]
to state the cohomological boundedness result from [HT25c] we need. In section 4, we
give the cohomological interpretation of the total degree for the characteristic cycle
(Theorem 1.7) and deduce Theorem 1.9. A consequence is the existence of some dense
open set of hyperplanes achieving transversality for perverse sheaves with bounded
rank and log conductors (Theorem 4.19). In section 5, we give a criterion for the
restriction to a hyperplane section to distinguish two sheaves in terms of the singular
support of their Hom-sheaf (Proposition 5.11). The wild Lefschetz theorem for finite
coefficients (Theorem 1.4) is then deduced. Section 6 provides the basic language
for moduli of multi-flags used to formulate Lefschetz type results in codimension
> 1, thus giving some flexibility for future applications. In section 7, we derive the
existence of some dense open subset of a moduli of multi-flags whose points achieve
simultaneous recognition for all pair of perverse sheaves with bounded rank and
log conductors (Theorem 7.18) and extend Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 1.9 in higher
codimension. See Theorem 7.24 and Theorem 7.11 respectively.

to show by Lemma 7.23 that F• admits a j! Λ[L, u]-transversal branch. Since j! L has
log conductors bounded by E , Lemma 5.8 implies that j! Λ[L, u] has log conductors
bounded by E as well. Since j : U ,→ X is affine, we deduce
                                  j! Λ[L, u][n] ∈ PervΣ,tf (X, E , Λ) .
Since j! Λ[L, u][n] has rank smaller than | GLr (Λ)|, the existence of the sought-after
branch follows by construction of V.
