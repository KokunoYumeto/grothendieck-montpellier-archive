\subsection{D.7: a candidate reconstruction theorem and its exact gap}
The candidate below is not used as a checked theorem. The proposed
covering argument extends partial sections by a baseline map. It does
not yet prove that the transition isomorphisms agree on an overlap
which is a proper clopen summand: that summand need not be an object
of the sieve, whose objects are required to map epimorphically to the
final object. Common refinements only test globally equal arrows.
One must either prove that these tests force equality on every such
summand or add an explicit descent-compatibility condition. The finite
realization corollary depends on resolving this step. These candidates
are preserved here to expose the precise proof obligation.

\begin{proposition}[Unchecked candidate: the exact essential image]\label{D:alpha-image}
Retain the hypotheses of 29/202--204, including the margin hypothesis
that $\varphi(M)\to e$ is an epimorphism. Interpret ``locally filtering''
as providing common refinements after an \'etale covering of the base.
For an admissible sieve $C_0$, let $\mathcal P(C_0)$ be the full
subcategory of cartesian sections $Z$ of $\mathcal E|C_0$ such that,
for every $M\in C_0$, the finite \'etale cover $Z(M)\to s(M)$ is
constant on each of its degree strata. Then
\[
 \mathscr R_S^{C_0}\xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ }\mathcal P(C_0)
\]
is an equivalence. Consequently, in the filtered-colimit statement,
the exact essential image consists of sections which acquire this
property after an admissible sieve refinement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We give the descent details that the assertion requires. First, for
any $P\in\mathscr R_T$, realization gives an equivalence between the
objects of $\mathscr R_T/P$ which are constant on their degree strata
and the analogous finite covers of $r(P)$. For a fixed finite degree,
a map between constant objects is specified by a finite clopen
partition of the base and a map of finite sets on each part. R2 lifts
that partition uniquely. R1 lifts disjoint sums. If the degree is not
globally bounded, the degree strata cover by R4, and R3 glues the
objects and maps. This proves the equivalence without assuming that
$r$ preserves arbitrary fibre products.

Here is the covering argument needed to apply descent to $C_0$.
Work \'etale locally on $S$ and choose $M\in C_0$. For each finite
product $Q$ of objects $\varphi(M_i)$, with $M_i\in C_0$, there
is, after further base localization, an object $N\in C_0$ such that
$Q$ is subordinate to $\varphi(N)$ and there is a map
$\varphi(N)\to Q$. Indeed, first use the generating condition to
subordinate $Q$ to an object of $C$, then use a common refinement
with the finitely many $M_i$. Membership in the sieve is preserved
under this refinement. The common-refinement arrows supply the
map to $Q$.

The split cover $Q\times\varphi(N)\to\varphi(N)$ is covered by
its sections over clopen degree pieces. Each such partial section,
viewed as a map from a summand of $\varphi(N)$ to $Q$, extends to
all of $\varphi(N)$ by the already chosen map on the complementary
summand. These maps $\varphi(N)\to Q$ cover $Q$: their images
cover $Q\times\varphi(N)$, and the latter maps epimorphically
to $Q$. Their images are summands. R1 sends the intervening
epimorphisms to surjections, and R3 makes the family effective for
descent. Full faithfulness of $\varphi$ says that each component
map $\varphi(N)\to\varphi(M_i)$ comes from a map in $C_0$.
The same argument applies to overlaps: their fibre products are
summands of the corresponding finite products. Equalities and
isomorphisms may therefore be checked after these refinements.

Now let $Z\in\mathcal P(C_0)$. The first paragraph lifts $Z(M)$
uniquely to an object $A_M$ over $\varphi(M)$ which is constant
on degree strata. Cartesian transition maps of $Z$ lift uniquely to
cartesian transition isomorphisms among the $A_M$. Over
$\varphi(M)\times\varphi(M)$, use the covering family just
constructed. The two pullbacks of $A_M$ are identified on each
member by the transition isomorphisms of $Z$. On overlaps these
identifications agree, because a further covering refinement reduces
the assertion to the cartesian cocycle identity for $Z$. They thus
glue, and the same check on triple products proves the descent
cocycle. Epimorphisms in a multigaloisian category are effective
descent morphisms. Hence $A_M$ descends uniquely to $X\in\mathscr R_S$.
Applying the argument simultaneously to $M$ and any other object
of $C_0$ shows $X\times\varphi(M')\simeq A_{M'}$.
Thus $X\in\mathscr R_S^{C_0}$ and $\alpha(X)\simeq Z$.
The construction and uniqueness of descended morphisms prove full
faithfulness as well. Finally, the same statement at each refinement
gives exactly the asserted essential image in the filtered colimit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unchecked candidate: reconstruction after refinement for finite realizations]
\label{D:alpha-finite}
If, in addition, every $s(M)\to T$ is finite for $M\in C_T$, then
\[
 \mathscr R_S\simeq
 \mathop{\mathrm{2\!\operatorname{colim}}}_{C_0}
 \operatorname{Cart}(C_0,\mathcal E)
\]
where the transition functors are restriction to admissible refinements.
This does not assert the erroneous equivalence for each individual
$C_0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A finite \'etale cover of a finite $T$-scheme becomes piecewise
constant after an \'etale cover of $T$. At a geometric point of $T$,
base change to its strict henselization makes the finite scheme a
finite disjoint union of strictly henselian local schemes, over each
of which every finite \'etale cover is constant. Finite presentation
descends the resulting finitely many idempotents and isomorphisms to
an \'etale neighborhood. This proves the assertion locally at every
geometric point.

Given a cartesian section $Z$ on $C_0$, choose objects $M_i\in C_0$
over an \'etale covering $T_i\to S$. Refine that covering until
$Z(M_i)$ is piecewise constant. Restrict $C_0$ to the sieve generated
by these base-changed $M_i$. It is admissible. Every value of $Z$
on this sieve is a pullback of one of the piecewise constant covers,
so is itself piecewise constant. Proposition~\ref{D:alpha-image}
now supplies the object of $\mathscr R_S$. Full faithfulness in the
colimit follows from the same proposition after intersecting sieves.
\end{proof}
