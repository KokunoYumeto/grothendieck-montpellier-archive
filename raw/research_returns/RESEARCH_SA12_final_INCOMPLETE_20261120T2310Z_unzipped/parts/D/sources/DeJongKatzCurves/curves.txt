1 Introduction.
Let Fq be a ﬁnite ﬁeld having q elements and let Mg be the algebraic stack classifying
smooth proper curves of genus g ≥ 2. We would like to know the number of points of Mg
over Fq . Recall that for a stack this number is deﬁned as
                                                ∑      1
                                 #Mg (Fq ) =                 ,
                                                     #Aut(C)

where the sum is over the isomorphism classes of smooth projective genus g curves C over
Fq and Aut(C) denotes the automorphism group of C over Fq .
    To give a precise formula for this number seems out of reach at the moment. On the
other hand it is known that for g ﬁxed and q → ∞ one has #Mg (Fq ) ∼ q 3g−3 . However,
Nick Katz asked [1] what the behaviour is of #Mg (Fq ) when q is ﬁxed and g grows.
    In this note we show that there exist positive constants c1 , c2 such that

                                 #Mg (Fq ) ≤ q c1 g ec2 g log(g) .

The constants c1 , c2 , . . . that appear in this paper are positive absolute constants. We give
two proofs of this result. The ﬁrst method bounds the number of isomorphism classes of
degree d morpisms to P1 with a ﬁxed discriminant. The main term in the resulting bound
comes from a Hurewitz number. The second method uses the Lefschetz ﬁxed point formula
for Frobenius and proceeds by estimating the betti numbers of Mg ⊗ C.
     It is perhaps interesting to note that neither method can be used to obtain better
bounds (see 3.1 and 5.2). Thus the question arises whether a better bound exists. For
example, does there exist a function φ(q) and a constant c3 > 0 such that

                                   #Mg (Fq ) ≤ φ(q)q c3 g ?

3.2 Lemma. Let k be an algebraically closed ﬁeld of characteristic p. Let D ⊂ P1k be
a divisor of degree 2g − 2 + 2d. Then the number of isomorphism classes of nonconstant
morphisms f : X → P1k of smooth projective curves over k of degree d which are tamely
ramiﬁed and have discriminant D is at most H(g, d).
     The proof is by lifting to characteristic zero. We omit it here since we prove a similar
result later (see Proposition 4.2).

5.1 Lemma.      The number of ribbon graphs of genus g with 1 boundary component is at
most (12g)!.
Proof. Let Γ be such a ribbon graph. We ﬁrst bound the number of vertices v in terms
of the number of edges e: we have 2e ≥ 3v. Furthermore, the formula for the euler
characteristic is 2 − 2g − 1 = v − e.

X is a set having 2e elements, s1 is a free involution of X, and S1 is any permutation of
X. (These triples do not all give rise to connected ribbon graphs, or if connected do not

switches 2j and 2j − 1 for all j = 1. . . . , #X/2. Since we know that e ≤ 6g − 3 we can
bound the number of possible graphs by counting the number of possible permutations s0 .
This gives the desired bound.
