                                                 Abstract
     Let S be a KLT threefold singularity over an algebraically closed field of positive
     characteristic p > 5. We prove that its local étale fundamental group is tame and
     finite. Further, we show that every finite unipotent torsor over a big open subset of S
     is realized as the restriction of a finite unipotent torsor over S.

                                             1. Introduction

Consider the following setup.

Setup 1.1. Let (X, ∆) be a Kawamata log terminal (KLT for short) log variety of dimension
d ⩾ 2 defined over a field k of characteristic p ⩾ 0. Let x̄ → X be a geometric closed point. Set
R := OX,x̄ sh and denote its field of fractions by K. Let Z ⊂ S := Spec R be a closed subscheme of

codimension at least 2, and set S ◦ := S ∖ Z. In other words, S ◦ is an arbitrary big open subset
of S. We choose and fix a geometric generic point η̄ → S ◦ (that is, a separable closure of K) and
let π1ét (S ◦ ) = π1ét (S ◦ , η̄) denote the étale fundamental group of S ◦ with base point η̄. If Z cuts
out the singular locus, we may refer to it as the (étale) fundamental group of the singularity.

     In this paper, we are concerned with the problem of determining the finiteness of π1ét (S ◦ ).
In [Xu14], Xu proved that if k = C, then π1ét (S ∖ {x}) is finite; cf. [GKP16, TX17, Bra21].
Inspired by this result, Schwede, Tucker, and the first-named author proved that if p > 0 and S
is strongly F -regular, then π1ét (S ◦ ) is finite, and its order is at most 1/s(R) and prime-to-p; see
[CST18], cf. [Car22]. (Here, s(R) denotes the F -signature of R.) It is worth recalling that Bhatt–
Gabber–Olsson later introduced in [BGO17] a spreading-out technique to prove Xu’s results
from that in [CST18]. We also recommend to see [JS22], where a characteristic-free approach
is discussed in terms of differential signatures. However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge,
little is known about the finiteness of π1ét (S ◦ ) if S is a non-strongly-F -regular KLT singularity
in positive characteristic—not even in sufficiently large characteristic. In this work, we attempt

to address this case in dimension 3 with p > 5. In positive characteristic, there is another aspect
regarding π1ét (S ◦ ) that is worth investigating, namely, its tameness. For strongly F -regular S,
we know that this group is very tame as p does not divide its order [CST18, Corollary 2.11]; cf.
[Car22, Theorem F]. The same cannot be true for general KLT S by the (independent) examples
of Yasuda and Totaro; see [Yas19, Tot19], respectively.
    It is worth observing that in dimension 2 and characteristics p > 5, KLT singularities and
strongly F -regular singularities coincide; see [HX15, Har98]. Therefore, if the characteristic is
large enough (p > 5), the aforementioned problem is solved by [CST18]; cf. [Art77, Art75].1 This
is no longer the case in dimension 3 as there exist non-strongly-F -regular but KLT threefold
singularities; see [CTW18].
    In dimension 3 and characteristic p > 5, we recall that Xu and Zhang have demonstrated in
[XZ19, Theorem 3.4] that a certain tame quotient of π1ét (S ∖ {x}) is finite. Their methods are
for the most part identical to the ones in [Xu14], which can only handle the tame part. In this
article, we complement Xu–Zhang’s work on the matter by proving that π1ét (S ◦ ) = π1ét (S ∖ Z) is
finite and tame for arbitrary Z. If G is a profinite group, we denote by G(p) the prime-to-p part
of G, which is the inverse limit over all finite quotients of G whose order is not divisible by p.
Then, our main result is the following.

Theorem A (Corollary 4.5, Theorem 4.3). Working in Setup 1.1, assume d = 3 and k to be
algebraically closed of characteristic p > 5. Then, the canonical quotient π1ét (S ◦ ) ↠ π1ét (S ◦ )(p) is
an isomorphism between finite groups.

Remark 1.2. By the purity of the branch locus theorem, we may always assume that Z cuts
out the singular locus of S. Further, since S ◦ is a 2-dimensional KLT scheme (of characteristic
p > 5), it has finitely many singular points which all have finite fundamental group. Hence, we
may find a Galois quasi-étale cover S ′ = Spec R′ → S such that every quasi-étale cover over the
punctured spectrum of R′ is étale. In other words, we may also assume Z = {x}. However, we
do not do this here as we want our methods to generalize to higher dimensions.

    Just as in [BCG+ 19], we obtain the following application.

Theorem B (Corollaries 4.7 and 4.8). Working in Setup 1.1, assume d = 3 and k to be alge-
braically closed of characteristic p > 5. Then, every quasi-étale cover over X whose degree is
a power of p is étale. Moreover, there exists a quasi-étale Galois cover X̃ → X with prime-to-p
degree such that every quasi-étale cover over X̃ is étale. Moreover, if (X, ∆) is log Fano and
U ⊂ X is a big open, then π1ét (U ) is finite and its order is prime-to-p.

     The problem of the rationality of KLT singularities in positive characteristics has been of
great interest in recent years, where several examples of non-rational KLT singularities have
been found.2 There are essentially two known ways to construct such counterexamples. On one
hand, we have the examples obtained from counterexamples of Kodaira or Kawamata–Viehweg
vanishing theorems (by taking affine cones over them); see, for example, [GNT19, CT19, Kov18,
Ber21, Tot19]. On the other hand, we have examples constructed by taking quasi-étale Z/pZ-
quotients of regular germs; see [Yas19, Yas14, Tot19]. In all these examples, either the dimension
is large, or the characteristic is small, which is predicted by [HW19], where it is shown that KLT
threefold singularities are rational in sufficiently large characteristics. Further, in Yasuda’s con-
struction of certain quasi-étale Z/pZ-quotients Âdk → S, we see that S cannot be simultaneously
rational (that is, Cohen–Macaulay) and KLT.3 In those examples, π1ét (S) = Z/pZ as Âdk → S is a
universal Galois cover. Our theorem goes further by establishing that KLT threefold singularities
in characteristic p > 5 cannot be realized as wild étale quotients of regular germs. We also make
the following observation.
Theorem D (Corollary 2.17). Working in Setup 1.1, assume d = 3 and k to be algebraically
closed of characteristic p > 5. If S is F -injective, then it is rational.
Remark 1.3. When a first version of this work was made public, [ABL22] was not available. In
that paper, rationality of KLT threefold singularities is established in characteristic p > 5. This
makes, for example, Theorem D obsolete and together with Theorem C answers Question 2.22;
see [ABL22]. However, we have decided to preserve our original paper as much as possible in
this second version. In particular, we keep emphasizing what type of results need rationality and
which need not. On the other hand, by using Appendix B, kindly provided by János Kollár, we
are able to remove the use of rationality from our original arguments regarding the finiteness of
the fundamental group.

1.1. Outline of the paper. Throughout this work, we follow the ideas in [HW19] to study
KLT threefold singularities in characteristic p > 5 by strongly F -regular ones via the so-called
generalized pure log terminal (PLT for short) blowups. Note that all theorems we have claimed
in this introduction are true for strongly F -regular singularities. What we do for the most part
of this paper is to prove that the same properties can be transferred across generalized PLT
blowups which, loosely speaking, is what Hacon–Witaszek do in [HW19] for rationality.
    In Section 2, we investigate the problem of (unipotent) tameness. This problem has to do
with the action of the Frobenius morphism on (local) cohomology. Therefore, what we do in
Section 2 is to compare the action of the Frobenius morphism on cohomology with supports
along a generalized PLT blowup.
    With tameness in place, we prove the finiteness of the fundamental group in Section 4. Our
proof starts identically to Xu’s argument in [Xu14] (which was later mimicked in [XZ19, § 3]).

                         2. Generalized PLT blowups and tameness
We begin by recalling the definition of Kollár components and PLT blowups; see [LX20, § 1.1],
[Xu14, Lemma 1]. In dimension 3 and positive characteristic p > 5, we also recall the refined
notion of generalized PLT blowups, which, roughly speaking, can be used to approximate KLT
threefold singularities by strongly F -regular ones. After this, we explain why this approximation
is good enough to impose tameness conditions on those KLT singularities.

2.1. Generalized PLT blowups. Consider the following definition.
Definition 2.1 (PLT blowups and Kollár components). Let X be a variety, and let x ∈ X be
a closed point. One says that a proper birational morphism f : Y → X from a log pair (Y, ∆Y )
is a PLT blowup at x if the following conditions hold:
(a) The morphism f induces an isomorphism over X ∖ {x}.
(b) The divisor E := f −1 (x) on Y is normal and prime, and (Y, E + ∆Y ) is a PLT log pair.
 (c) The divisor −(KY + E + ∆Y ) is Q-Cartier and f -ample.
(d) The divisor −E is Q-Cartier and f -nef.
The exceptional divisor E of a PLT blowup f : Y → X is called a Kollár component of X at the
closed point x.
Remark 2.2. Let X be variety, x ∈ X be a closed point, and f : Y → X be a PLT blowup
extracting a Kollár component E. Further, if we set an equality

2.2. Tameness. We apply the above results to obtain tameness conditions for KLT threefold
singularities in characteristic p > 5. We need, however, some standard results on fundamental
groups for log del Pezzo surfaces.

   2.2.1 Étale fundamental groups of big opens of log del Pezzo surfaces. We remind the reader
that a weak log del Pezzo surface is a KLT surface (X, ∆) for which −(KX + ∆) is nef and big.
We assume that the following result is well known to experts; see [Tak00, Zha06, GZ94, Xu09].
However, we include a proof for the sake of completeness. It will play a fundamental role in our
study of tameness.

of positive characteristic p (note that X is in particular projective over k). Then, π1ét (X) = 1,
and further, if p > 5, then π1ét (U ) is finite for all big opens U ⊂ X.

morphism of generic degree n, we have that (Y, f ∗ ∆) is a weak log del Pezzo surface and so by

   2.2.2 τ -modules. We shall see that tameness can be understood in terms of the action of the
Frobenius morphism on local cohomology modules so that we need to study modules equipped
with a Frobenius action. Following [BP09], one has the following definition.
Definition 2.8. Let X be an Fp -scheme. A τ -module on X is a quasi-coherent OX -module F
equipped with an OX -linear map ϕ : F → F∗ F. Equivalently, letting OX [F ] denote the sheaf of
non-commutative rings given by OX {F }/(F · a − ap · F ), where OX {F } denotes the OX -algebra
obtained by adding a free non-commutative variable, a τ -module is nothing but a left OX [F ]-
module (that is quasi-coherent as a sheaf of OX -modules).

    As the reader may expect, the category of coherent τ -modules over a field k (of positive
characteristic) should resemble the category of finitely generated modules over the principal
ideal domain k[t]. Indeed, if k is perfect, a right division algorithm holds for k[F ]. Consequently,
every left ideal of k[F ] is principal, and every submodule of a finite rank free module is free. In
particular, a τ -module over k is a finite direct sum of cyclic modules k[F ]/k[F ]f with f = 0
or a power of an irreducible polynomial in k[F ]. See [Mil17, § 14.e] for details. In particular, we
have the following well-known result (see for instance [Mil80, Lemma III.4.13] and the references
therein).
Theorem 2.9. Let k be a perfect field of characteristic p > 0. Let V be a coherent τ -module
and ϕ : V → F∗ V be the k-linear map realizing the action of F on V (that is, ϕ(v) = F · v).
Then, V admits a decomposition V = V nil ⊕ V ss of τ -modules such that ϕ is nilpotent on V nil
and ϕ is an isomorphism on V ss . Moreover, if k is algebraically closed, then V ss admits a k-basis
{v1 , . . . , vn } such that ϕ(vi ) = vi and further V F = ⟨v1 , . . . , vn ⟩Fp .
