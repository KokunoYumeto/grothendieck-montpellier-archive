Only the complement of the common open on the **projective** model has to have
codimension at least two. The common open in the original proper normal scheme
merely has to be dense. Every codimension-one boundary DVR on the projective
side then belongs to that common open and is an original boundary DVR. Removing
more divisors on the original side does not create any extra DVR to test on the
projective side. Connected covers remain connected on a dense open of a normal
integral scheme. These two facts give the stated tame quotient surjection and
full finite generation from the already proved projective case.

The toric theorem supplies common opens whose complements have codimension at
least two on **both** sides. In fan language, the common subfan is the zero cone
together with all rays. The boundary in our application is arbitrary and need
not be torus invariant.
