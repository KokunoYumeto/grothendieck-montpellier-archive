## Maire–Mináč–Ramakrishna–Tân

Primary source: the publisher's HTML for DOI 10.1007/s00208-026-03518-1, saved under evidence/main_gate/literature/massey_scope. I read Definitions 1.1–1.3, the intervening explanation of individual defining systems, Theorem 1.1, Remark 1.1, Theorem 1.2, and the final introduction coefficient convention in their entirety. The extracted local text places this material at lines 64–102.

The strong vanishing assertion is an existence statement for a lift of the tuple of first-order characters. The paragraph following Definition 1.1 explicitly distinguishes it from lifting every preassigned quotient representation. Theorem 1.1 concerns the absolute and maximal tame Galois groups of a number field without a primitive pth root of unity. Remark 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 explicitly allow additional tame ramification. The coefficients in the stated cohomology are trivial.

Our ordinary obstruction retains a fixed ramification set and a specified defining cochain. Trivializing its coefficient characters does not establish the required descent or preservation of those local conditions. The final draft correctly avoids suggesting that coefficient trivialization necessarily destroys the roots-of-unity hypothesis: for the present even characters a smaller splitting field can retain it. I did not read the complete proof of the global embedding theorem for this audit.

## Ray–Sujatha

Primary source: arXiv:2508.17156v1. I read Proposition 4.6 and its complete displayed proof on printed page 14; the complete ordinary setup on page 15; Theorem 6.10, Corollary 6.11 and their displayed proofs on page 23; and Theorem 7.1 with its full proof on page 24.

Proposition 4.6 is conditional on weak Leopoldt and concerns the infinite extension. Theorem 7.1 equates the fine-Selmer cotorsion and zero-mu condition with spanning by proper products made from repeated copies of the extension character and varying permitted defining systems. Theorem 6.10 and Corollary 6.11 explicitly identify those systems. This does not evaluate our fixed alternating-weight triple or establish finite-level vanishing.

The ordinary application additionally assumes weight two, determinant equal to the cyclotomic character, irreducibility after restriction to K(mu_p), and local absolute indecomposability. The present three-character residual system does not meet that package. The brief proofs on page 23 invoke LLS+23; this audit records those actual citation proofs and does not claim a new reconstruction of LLS+23.

## Ahlqvist–Carlson

https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/crelle-2025-0006/pdf

I directly read published Corollary 4.10 on page 88, Theorem 4.12 and its complete short proof on page 90, and Remark 4.14 on page 93. These agree with the local arXiv v7 statements on pages 26, 28, and 31. The publisher PDF has 52 pages, so the corresponding PDF indices are 27, 29, and 32. Corollary 4.10 has the stated cup-product and indeterminacy hypotheses. Theorem 4.12 uses an unramified cyclic degree-p extension and an explicit norm/ideal pair to evaluate the repeated product as a functional modulo its indeterminacy. Its short proof modifies the cohomology representative and checks the two norm/ideal relations; the preceding resolution calculations remain imported.

Remark 4.14 already sketches the comparison with Sharifi's Theorem 5.4 after removing the primes above p and assuming the roots of unity are in the field. The final reviewed draft explicitly acknowledges this. The remaining missing steps are the precise relative ordinary defining-system comparison and the actual norm-data evaluation. Merely identifying the class-group character supplies neither.
