## A.14: passage to a cuspidal quotient does not lose the same-prime quotient of an open subgroup

Let F be profinite, K closed normal, N the kernel of K to its maximal pro-r quotient, and I an open subgroup of F containing N. Given a finite r-quotient I/J, set I0 equal to the core of I in F and M equal to the core of J intersect I0 in F.

The crucial checks are:

- N is normal in F because it is characteristic in K, so N lies in I0.
- I0/M embeds in a finite product of conjugates of I0/(J intersect I0), hence is an r-group.
- The image of K in F/I0 is an r-group because it is a quotient of K/N.
- The image of K in F/M is consequently an r-group. The defining universal property of N forces N into M and therefore into J.

Thus every finite r-quotient of I kills N, proving the asserted natural isomorphism. The proof does not require I to be normal, of r-power index, or finitely generated. These absences are justified by the core construction, not assumed.

## A.15: the compactified r-adic representation is genuinely retained

For a geometric cover associated with I/N, the preimage I is the full prime-to-p geometric fundamental group of the actual punctured cover. A finite quotient of that group's prime-to-p fundamental group is seen in the ambient group by intersecting its finitely many conjugate kernels: the resulting extension and product still have order prime to p. This is the topology assertion needed before applying A.14.

Let T be the closed normal subgroup generated by actual cusp inertias. The actual compactification has group I/T. A.14 says every pro-r quotient of I kills N; hence taking the pro-r quotient after killing the image of T in I/N gives precisely the pro-r quotient of I/T. All these maps arise from the actual curve maps and are functorial for arithmetic automorphisms preserving the cover.

Consequently the comparison preserves the complete r-adic H1 representation with its arithmetic action. It is not merely an equality of dimensions or of characteristic polynomials modulo r. The equality of integral Weil polynomials follows from equality over Z_r, using injectivity of Z into Z_r. The trace formula gives compactified counts over every finite constant extension. Cusp double cosets and the images of their decomposition groups in the full arithmetic quotient determine the missing-point residue degrees, so subtracting the boundary counts gives equality for the open covers as well.

The geometric Galois closure is prime to p; the arithmetic cover may contain constant extensions whose degrees are divisible by p. Nothing in the argument incorrectly requires the latter degree to be prime to p.

## A.17: the normalizer statement is over the residue field

Saïdi–Tamagawa Lemma 1.7 assumes that the subgroup is closed, its arithmetic projection is open, and this projection is injective. It proves that the commensurator projects injectively to the arithmetic group and equals the normalizer and centralizer. For a section over the full G_L, working in the arithmetic group over L, this forces the normalizer to equal the section itself.

This does not justify replacing the normalizer of every closed-point group in the original arithmetic group by that group without changing constants. A.19 correctly works over L = k(R); the cleaned proof should explicitly write the normalizer in Pi_{C_L}.

The weight argument in the recovered proof also checks: a nontrivial geometric commensurator produces a nonzero abelian class fixed by an open Frobenius group on an actual cover. The relevant Tate modules are torsion-free and have no Frobenius eigenvalue one, giving a contradiction.

## A.18: two compactness steps and recognition in the whole quotient

Fix the image S of an actual point section over a finite field L. Consider all open subgroups H containing S, ordered by reverse inclusion. They all have full arithmetic projection, so the corresponding connected covers are geometrically connected over L. Their source counterparts have an L-point, and A.15 therefore makes each finite set X_H(L) nonempty.

Individual transition maps of rational-point sets need not be surjective. The argument does not assume that they are. For any finite list of compatibility conditions, intersect the finitely many subgroups involved, take an L-point on its cover, and project. This proves the finite-intersection property and supplies an inverse-limit family.

Its base point T is L-rational on the target open curve. The compatible geometric lifts of T to the profinite fibre form a second inverse-limit problem. The same finite-intersection argument gives a lift with all prescribed projections. Its decomposition group D maps isomorphically to G_L, because T is unramified and the full arithmetic quotient is retained. Rationality of the point on every H-cover gives surjectivity of D intersect H to G_L. Since D itself projects injectively, D is contained in H. Intersecting all such H gives D contained in S; both groups project isomorphically to G_L, so they are equal.

Thus the result is a decomposition group in the complete cuspidal quotient. No finite quotient has been identified with that inverse limit. The source and target proper images agree because beta is over the identity. Away from the uniform exceptional set, A.16 then identifies the point label with the original one.

## A.19: orientation, cocycle, descent, and evaluation

### Principal p-primary difference

The cusp-preserving isomorphism gives equal proper point-section classes. For every prime different from p, their abelian Kummer difference vanishes. The Jacobian rational-point group over the finite field is finite, so the difference has p-power order. A principal divisor p^e(P-Q) admits a function over the ground field: the scalar Galois cocycle of a geometric generator is removed by Hilbert 90.

### The integral orientation multiplier

In the geometrically cuspidally central quotient, the cusp kernel is procyclic and is exactly Z_r(1). The Heisenberg test sends the boundary word to a central element of infinite r-adic order, so the kernel is not a finite cyclic quotient. The geometric central extension is the degree-one orientation class, with the same residue convention at both points. A quotient-preserving isomorphism multiplying the kernel by u in Z_r^× must therefore satisfy u times this generator equals the same generator, hence u = 1. This comparison uses integral H2, not its reduction modulo r.

The group/curve cohomology identification used here can be checked by the tower of geometric prime-to-p covers. Cyclic torsors kill H1 classes, and covers of degree divisible by r^a kill the degree class in H2 with mu_{r^a} coefficients. Such covers exist for positive genus. Higher curve cohomology vanishes; finite coefficient systems satisfy the inverse-limit conditions. The tame punctured surface presentation gives the same orientation normalization.

### Difference cocycle and descent

On E_P times_{Pi_C} E_Q, chi(u,v) = beta_bar(u) v^{-1} lies in M = Z_r(1). Multiplication gives a crossed homomorphism for the common arithmetic action. After pullback to C minus {P,Q}, the residues are +1 and -1. Hence kappa_r(f) minus p^e chi has zero residues.

Geometric inertia acts trivially on M. A coboundary restricts to zero on it, and a cocycle vanishing on the inertia generators vanishes on their closed normal closure. This is the kernel of the map to the proper group. Thus the difference genuinely descends to H1(Pi_C,M).

Inflation from H1(G_k,M) is an isomorphism because H1(Delta_C,M) has no G_k-invariant elements. In the Tate-module description, the Frobenius eigenvalues on Hom(T_r J_C,Z_r(1)) have complex absolute value sqrt(#k); none is one. The integral module is torsion-free. A constant a in k^× therefore represents the difference class.

### Evaluation and final contradiction

For a good closed point R, work over L = k(R). Recognition gives conjugate target point sections. Their proper images are the same section. By A.17 in Pi_{C_L}, the projected conjugator belongs to that section; correcting by an element of the point decomposition group leaves a conjugator in the cuspidal kernel. Its image in E_Q is in M. Therefore chi evaluates to a coboundary, and the full r-adic Kummer class of f(R)/a is zero.

If P differs from Q, f is nonconstant and defines a finite surjective map over the algebraic closure. Only finitely many geometric values are excluded. Choose a nontrivial r-power root of unity zeta with a zeta not excluded and a geometric point over that value. Its finite residue field contains zeta = f(R)/a. The full r-adic completion of a finite cyclic group retains its r-primary subgroup, so zeta has a nonzero Kummer class, a contradiction.

A root becoming an r-th power in some finite extension does not invalidate this step: the proof uses the entire r-adic Kummer class, not only its first finite layer.
