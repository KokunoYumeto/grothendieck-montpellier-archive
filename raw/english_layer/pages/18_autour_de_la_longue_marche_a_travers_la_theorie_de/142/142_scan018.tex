In all this, there remains however an
important type of \deleted{\unsure{homomorphisms}} morphisms, between
compact closed \inserted{topological} surfaces or between schemes in
curves, which has not been taken into
account --- namely the morphisms
of open inclusion
\diagram{\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
(23)\quad X=Y\setminus T\arrow[r,hook,no head]\&Y
\end{tikzcd}}
corresponding to an operation of \uline{filling
holes}. But one is confronted, in order to enlarge the
category accordingly, by the imperative of
being able to compose finite étale morphisms,
and open inclusion morphisms (``filling holes'' type)
with considering morphisms $X\longrightarrow Y$ which
fit into a commutative diagram
\diagram{\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
X\arrow[r,hook,no head]\arrow[d]\&\widehat X\arrow[d]\\
(24)\quad Y\arrow[r,hook,no head]\&\widehat Y
\end{tikzcd}}
with $\widehat X\longrightarrow\widehat Y$ finite ramified, $X\mathbin{-}Y$ itself étale.
Setting $S=\widehat X\setminus X$, $T=\widehat Y-T$, so that
\[
(25)\qquad S\supset\widehat X|T
\]
the technical troubles come from the fact that
one is obliged to admit that $\widehat X/\widehat Y$ may have
points of actual ramification not only at
points of $\widehat X|T$, but also at points of $C$ which
are \uline{not} in $\widehat X|T$.
