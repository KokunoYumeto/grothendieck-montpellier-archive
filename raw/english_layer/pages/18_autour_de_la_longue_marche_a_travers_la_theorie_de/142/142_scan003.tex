Let $X$ and $Y$ be two oriented topological surfaces \inserted{(connected for simplicity)} ``of finite type'' (i.e. obtained from compact surfaces by removing a finite number of points). We consider
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1)\qquad \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{isot}}(X,Y)
&=\text{set of \inserted{path-}connected components}\\
&\quad\text{of the space of finite maps}\\
&\quad\text{étale from $X$ to $Y$ \deleted{respecting}}\\
&\quad\deleted{\text{the orientation}}
\end{aligned}
\]
If $f$ and $g$ are two finite étale maps from $X$ to $Y$, then $f$ and $g$ \inserted{are isotopic i.e.} define the same \deleted{ma} map from $X$ to $Y$ in the isotopy category, if and only if we can find a continuous finite étale map
\deleted{$F$ from $X\times I$}
\[
(2)\qquad F:X\times I\longrightarrow Y\times I\qquad I=[0,1]
\]
commuting with the projections to $I$, and such that
\[
(3)\qquad F(x,0)=(f(x),0)\qquad F(x,1)=(g(x),1)\qquad\forall x\in X
\]
Since the functor
\[
Y'\longmapsto Y'\times I
\]
from the finite étale coverings of $Y$ to the finite étale coverings of $Y\times I$ is fully faithful, we see that \deleted{there exi} for a given $F$, there must exist a finite étale covering $Y'$ of $Y$, unique up to unique isomorphism, and a homeomorphism
\[
(4)\qquad \alpha:X\times I\xrightarrow{\sim}Y'\times I
\]
making the commutative diagram commutative
\diagram{
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
(5)\quad X\times I \arrow[rr,"\alpha", "\sim"'] \arrow[dr,"F"'] \&\& Y'\times I \arrow[dl,"p\times\operatorname{id}_I"]\\
\&Y\times I\&
\end{tikzcd}
}
\margin{where $p:Y'\longrightarrow Y$ the projection}
