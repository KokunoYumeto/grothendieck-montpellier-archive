\margin{\uline{Version II}}
\noindent $n$-ary decompositions of a topological space\unsure{.}

1.1. Let $X$ be a topological space. A \uline{binary decomposition} of $X$ is a pair (\unsure{written|written} with indices)
\[
\unsure{\pi|\Pi}=(\Sigma^\varepsilon)_{\varepsilon\in\omega}\qquad(\omega\ \text{set \unsure{:|with} two elements})
\]
\unsure{of|of|of} \deleted{sets} \uline{closed} (distinct) subsets of $X$, with union $X$
\[
X=\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in\omega}\Sigma^\varepsilon.\tag*{(1)}
\]

We assume that
\[
\Sigma^\varepsilon\ne\varnothing\qquad(\forall\varepsilon\in\omega)\tag*{(2)}
\]
which also means that
\[
\pi\ne\{\varnothing,X\}\tag*{$(2')$}
\]
and implies that $X\ne\varnothing$, and even $\mathrm{card}\,X\geq 2$, since we have assumed
\[
\unsure{\deleted{X|\Sigma}}\Sigma^\varepsilon\unsure{\deleted{=|\ne}}\ne\Sigma^{\varepsilon'}\qquad\text{if }\varepsilon,\varepsilon'\in\omega,\quad\varepsilon\ne\varepsilon'.\tag*{(\unsure{3|s|$\wedge$})}
\]

We do not exclude the case where one would have an inclusion
\[
\Sigma^{\varepsilon'}\subset\Sigma^\varepsilon
\]
which is equivalent, by (1), to the relation
\[
\Sigma^\varepsilon=X.
\]

The $\Sigma^\varepsilon$ $(\varepsilon\in\omega)$ are called the \uline{\unsure{hemispheres|hemispheres}} of the binary decomposition, \deleted{the} and the set
\[
E=\bigcap_{\deleted{\omega}\varepsilon\in\omega}\Sigma^\omega\tag*{(4)}
\]
is called the \uline{equator} of the binary decomposition.

We do not exclude the case where one would have $E=\varnothing$, which indicates that the $\Sigma^\varepsilon$ \inserted{$(\varepsilon\in\omega)$} are \inserted{complementary to one another, \unsure{hence}} both closed and open -- hence that $X$ is disconnected.
