Proof by induction, it is trivial if $\operatorname{card} I=0,1$ The case $\operatorname{card} I=2$ \unsure{has} been seen. General case \unsure{with} $\operatorname{card} I\geq 3$, one chooses $J$ as above. It suffices to see that the sets
\[
\unsure{U|\deleted{=}U}_{J,i}\cap\Sigma_i^+,\ U_{J,i}\cap\Sigma_i^-
\]
have the preceding form, it suffices to see this for \unsure{one|one} of the two.

By the induction hypothesis, \unsure{one has}
\[
U_{J,i}=X\setminus\coprod_{\alpha\in \deleted{\unsure{J}}K_0}\widehat{\Sigma}_\alpha^+
\]

where
\[
\left\{\begin{aligned}
&\deleted{\Sigma_{\unsure{\alpha|0}}}K_0\subset J\\
&\Sigma_\alpha^+\in\unsure{\pi_0|\Pi_0}(X\setminus E_\alpha)\qquad (\forall\,\alpha\in K_0)\\
&\widehat{\Sigma}_\alpha^+=\Sigma_\alpha^+\cup E_\alpha.
\end{aligned}\right.
\]
\deleted{\unsure{D|O}}\unsure{One has} therefore
\[
U=U_{J,i}\cap\Sigma_i^-=X\setminus\left(\coprod_{\alpha\in K_0}\widehat{\Sigma}_\alpha^+\ \cup\ \widehat{\Sigma}_i^+\right)
\]
