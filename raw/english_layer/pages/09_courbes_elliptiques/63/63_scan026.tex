6. \quad \uline{Applications}.

\uline{Proposition} 6.1. \quad \uline{If} $E$ \uline{is a curve of genus one over an algebraically closed field}, $E$ \uline{is of elliptic type} (\uline{resp. multiplicative}, \uline{resp. additive}) \uline{if and only if} $\Delta\ne 0$ (\uline{resp.} $\Delta=0$ and $c_4\ne 0$, \uline{resp}. $\Delta=c_4=0$).

a) \quad When the characteristic $p\ne 2$, the first assertion follows from the third formula (3.2). When $p=2$ and $c_4\ne 0$, one verifies at once that the differential of (4.1) vanishes only at $(0,0)$, and $\Delta$ is elliptic if and only if $\Delta=a_6\ne 0$. For $p=2$ and $c_4=0$, one argues in the same way using (6.2).

b) \quad When $p\ne 2$, it follows from (3.2) that $\Delta=c_4=0$ if and only if the right-hand side of (3.1) has all its roots equal, which characterizes the additive case.

c) \quad When $p=2$, $\Delta=0$, $c_4\ne 0$, the principal part of (4.1) at $(0,0)$ is $y^2+xy+a_2x^2=0$, which corresponds to a double point with distinct tangents.

d) \quad When $p=2$, $\Delta=0$, $c_{4.}=0$, the principal part of (6.2) at $(0,0)$ is $y^2+a_4x+a_6=0$, which corresponds to a cuspidal singularity.
