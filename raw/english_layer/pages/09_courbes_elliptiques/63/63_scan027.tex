Let $E$ and $E'$ be given by equations (1.3) and $(1.3)'$. To give an isomorphism between $E$ and $E'$ amounts to giving $u$, $r$, $s$ and $t$, satisfying equations (1.6), hence also (1.7) and (1.8), $u$ having moreover to be invertible. By hypothesis, locally on $S$, $c_4$ or $\Delta$ is invertible (6.1), and likewise $c'_4$ or $\Delta'$ is. Locally on $S$, equations (1.8) are therefore equations of integral dependence for $u$ and $u^{-1}$, equations $(1.7)_6$ or $(1.7)_8$ equations of integral dependence for $r$ and finally $(1.6)_6$ (resp. $(1.6)_2$) is an equation of integral dependence for $t$ (resp. $s$). This proves that $\text{\uline{Isom}}(E,E')$ is representable by an affine scheme finite over $S$ ; it is even \tikz[baseline=(nrtwo.base)]{\node[inner sep=0pt] (nrtwo) {unramified};\handwritten{\draw[thin,gray] (nrtwo.north east) .. controls ([xshift=-1em,yshift=0.8em]nrtwo.north east) and ([xshift=-0.6em,yshift=0.7em]nrtwo.north west) ..
