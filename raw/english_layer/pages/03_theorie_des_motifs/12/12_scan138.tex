\begin{center}\S\ 3. \uuline{Groups generated by reflections}.\end{center}

\uline{In this} \S, \uline{we denote by} $E$ \uline{a real Hilbert space of finite dimension} $n$, \uline{by} $(x\handwritten{|}y)$ \uline{its bilinear form}, \uline{and by} $\handwritten{\lVert}x\handwritten{\rVert}$ \uline{its norm}. \uline{For any hyperplane} (linear or affine) $H$ \uline{of} $E$, \uline{we denote by} $s_H$ \uline{the reflection} (\uline{or orthogonal symmetry}) \uline{with respect to} $H$ (cf. \S\ 2, number 2).

1. \uline{Finite groups generated by linear reflections}.

\uline{In this} number, $G$ \uline{denotes a finite subgroup of the orthogonal group of} $E$, \uline{generated by reflections} (with respect to linear hyperplanes).

Let $\underline{H}$ be a finite set of hyperplanes of $E$ such that the $s_H$ $(H\in\underline{H})$ generate $G$. The set $W'$ of $x\in E$ invariant under $G$ is also the set of $x\in E$ invariant under the $s_H$ $(H\in\underline{H})$ ; thus $\displaystyle W'=\handwritten{\bigcap}_{H\in\underline{H}}H$. The subspace $W$ of $E$ orthogonal to $W'$ is stable under $G$ ; it is called the \uline{essential} subspace of $E$ (for $G$). The map which assigns to each $g\in G$ its restriction to $W$ is an isomorphism of $G$ onto a finite subgroup $G'$ of the orthogonal group of $W$, generated by the reflections $s_{H\handwritten{\cap}W}$ $(H\in\underline{H})$. We say that $G$ is \uline{essential} if $W=E$ ; in any case $G'$ is essential, which most often makes it possible to reduce to the case where $G$ is essential.

We shall henceforth fix a finite set $\underline{H}$ of hyperplanes of $E$ such that

1) the reflections $s_H$ $(H\in\underline{H})$ generate $G$,

2) the hyperplanes of $\underline{H}$ are permuted by $G$.

Let us note, in this connection, that, for $g\in G$, we have
\[
\begin{array}{l@{\qquad}l}(1)&s_{g(H)}=gs_Hg^{-1}.\end{array}
\]
In principle, all the constructions which follow depend on the choice of $\underline{H}$ ; in fact, we shall refrain from recalling this in our notations and our statements, for corollary 5 of theorem 1 will reveal that the set of the $s_H$ is necessarily the set of \uline{all} the reflections of $G$.
