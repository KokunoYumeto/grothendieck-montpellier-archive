## Central theorem and dependency repair

Théorème 12, scans 16–17, is the main question. Its finite-value hypothesis
(iv) repairs the incorrect converse in Remarque 5 bis. For an integral
quotient B, constant fibre rank n on an open and local finite
representability imply that the local representing module has generic rank
n and n generators, hence is free. Since F(B) is finite by (iv), it is a
vector bundle on a smaller open, and the natural map F(B) tensor M -> F(M)
is an isomorphism there. Thus generic representability follows without the
normality assumption in 5 bis. Lemme 5 then supplies a strict pro-object;
Corollaire 11 applies with the strictness and finiteness actually supplied
by that lemma, although those hypotheses disappeared in its rewritten
standalone statement.

## Counterexample to 5 bis without (iv)

A=Z. Let L be the subgroup of Q generated by 1/p for every prime p; these
are precisely the rationals with squarefree denominators. Let F=L tensor -.
L is flat. Its localization at (p) is p^{-1} Z_(p), and at (0) is Q.
Consequently all local functors are represented by rank-one finite free
modules, all local-completion comparisons hold, and finite-length values
are finite. Every fibre has dimension one. All integral quotients of Z
are normal. Nevertheless L_f is not finite for any nonzero f: a finite
set of denominators misses a prime q not dividing f, while 1/q belongs to
L_f. If F_f were represented, preservation of filtered colimits would
make the representing module finitely presented, and exactness would make
it projective; evaluation at A_f would make L_f finite, a contradiction.
The manuscript proof invokes finiteness of F(N), which i–iv of Lemme 5
do not supply. Literal Lemme 5(v) with f=0 allowed is vacuous and has the
same counterexample; the intended nonzero-f reconstruction is separate.

## Two-term Tor criterion

For A=k[x,y]/(xy), M=A/(x), and K=[A --y--> A] in degrees 0,1,
H^0(K)=(x), H^1(K)=A/(y), Z^1(K)=A. The periodic resolution of M has
differentials x,y,x,y,...; Tor_1(M,A/(y))=0. Also Tor_1(M,A)=0.
But H^0(K) tensor M=(x)/(x^2)=k, whereas H^0(K tensor M)=0.
Thus the written criterion with Tor_1(M,H^1)=Tor_1(M,Z^1)=0 is false.
Correct sufficient hypotheses are Tor_1(M,B^1)=Tor_1(M,H^1)=0, or
Tor_1(M,K^1)=Tor_1(M,H^1)=Tor_2(M,H^1)=0. The actual regular-sequence
application has all positive Tor vanishing and therefore remains valid.
The preceding Hom complex must have Q, whereas the scan writes P.

## CMU alone does not provide a generic canonical module

Let k be an infinite field. In k[t_i : i>=1] put
B=k[t_i^3,t_i^4,t_i^5 : i>=1], m_i=(t_i^3,t_i^4,t_i^5)B,
S=B minus union_i m_i, and A=S^{-1}B. Every nonzero element belongs
to only finitely many m_i. Prime avoidance over the infinite field shows
the maximal ideals of A are exactly m_i A. At such a maximal ideal,
A_(m_i)=K_i[t_i^3,t_i^4,t_i^5]_(t_i^3,t_i^4,t_i^5), where
K_i=k(t_j:j!=i). These are one-dimensional noetherian CM domains.
For any nonzero ideal I of A, choose x!=0 in I. Only finitely many
maximal ideals contain x. Choose finitely many generators of I locally
at each of these, lift them into I and add x. The resulting finite ideal
has the same localization at every maximal ideal, so equals I. Thus A
is noetherian. Its integral closed subschemes are A and residue fields,
so it satisfies EGA IV 6.11.8's CMU condition.

Every nonempty principal open contains all but finitely many m_i. Each
local parameter quotient by t_i^3 has basis 1,t_i^4,t_i^5 and square-zero
maximal ideal of dimension two. Its socle has dimension two, so the
quotient is not its own injective hull. A finite Omega with the asserted
canonical quotient property has generic rank one, hence is free rank
one on some nonempty principal open, producing a contradiction at one
of these m_i. This refutes the CMU marginal extension of Corollaire 10.
The quotient-of-regular and excellent versions are separate and valid.

## Corollaire 11 and Lemma 17 literal omissions

Scan 15 rewrites Corollaire 11 without strictness or finite terms.
Over a field the system P_n=k with zero transitions prorepresents F=0
and satisfies its displayed conditions, but its transition maps never
become isomorphisms. The manuscript's explicit meaning of stationarity
is therefore false literally. The strict finite version is proved by
finite generation of the increasing union Hom(P_i,Omega) and detection
by all parameter quotients. Pro-isomorphism to a constant object must
not be confused with the explicit eventual-transition assertion.

Lemma 17 writes all s in S after introducing a dense U. For a literal
counterexample take A=k[t], X=P^1_A and the extension
0 -> O(-2) -> E -> O -> 0 with class t. Then H^0(E)=0,
H^1(E)=A/t, but the fibre at t=0 splits and has H^0=k.
The intended statement with s in U follows by generic Tor vanishing
and the derived projection formula.

## Canonical-module and regular-sequence groundwork

For a nilpotent ideal J with gr_J A and gr_J R flat over A/J, the
additional freeness/faithfulness of gr_J A makes every A-regular
sequence regular on R after the generic shrinking used in Lemmas 6
and 16. Induct on a sequence: its first element is regular on A/J by
testing the last nonzero J-power; it is then regular on every graded
piece of R. Filtrations are strict under quotient, and the argument
repeats. Koszul resolutions give all positive Tor vanishing.

Canonical modules descend modulo regular sequences. An artinian
canonical module is the injective hull of the residue field. All
parameter-quotient Hom tests detect an isomorphism of finite modules:
artinian injective duality detects each quotient, and the ideals
(t_1^n,...,t_d^n) lie in m^n, so Krull intersection kills the kernel.
One fixed parameter ideal does not suffice.

## Proper Hom and affine restriction of scalars

EGA III_2 7.7.8 assumes a two-term presentation of F by finite locally
free sheaves; 7.7.9(iii) promises removal. Therefore it is not an exact
reference for the full Théorème 14. Hall, arXiv:1206.4179v2, Theorems
C,D and Example 3.10 supply an exact stronger modern result.

For f proper flat fp, affine Z=Spec_X B, linear maps B -> O_X are
represented by V(P_B) by the quasi-coherent extension of Théorème 14.
Unit and multiplication impose closed linear-map vanishing conditions,
giving affine restriction of scalars. For a closed subscheme Y of X
with ideal I, the inclusion I -> O_X corresponds to P_I -> O_S;
its image ideal cuts out exactly the bases where Y becomes all of X.
This also proves preservation of closed immersions. Finite presentation
is established by noetherian approximation of fp data; finite type by
writing a finite-type algebra as a quotient of a fixed fp algebra and
using closed immersions, with no unwarranted fp conclusion.

## Remarque 18 — unresolved scope, not silently narrowed

The ink says '(additif ?)' and proposes a not-yet-defined general notion
Qcoh(Y)->Qcoh(X). Unqualified R^q f_* cannot preserve coherent values:
A^1_k -> Spec k already fails for q=0. Modern coherent functors are a
rigorous positive realization and are closed under composition, but
this must not be asserted to prove closure of the entire affine class
defined by the displayed axioms without an argument. Over a complete
noetherian local ring composition preserves the completion condition:
for finite M, continuity of G and Chevalley's lemma make the tower
G(M/m^nM) pro-isomorphic to G(M)/m^nG(M), then apply F. For arbitrary
finite modules over a completion of a noncomplete ring, it remains to
justify finite-valuedness after extension; the hypotheses as written
only assert finite-valuedness over the original ring. Continue attacking
this point or make the definition/programme distinction explicit.
