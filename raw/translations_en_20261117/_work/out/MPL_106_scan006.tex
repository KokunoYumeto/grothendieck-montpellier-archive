In summary, one finds

\uline{Prop.} For $\Psi:W_{\mathrm{cof}}^{-1}C_{\mathrm{cof}}\longrightarrow W^{-1}C$ to be an equiv. of cat., it is n. and s. that the following condition be satisfied:

$(\unsure{*})$ For \deleted{every} diagram
\diagram{\begin{tikzcd}x \arrow[r,shift left=.65ex,"\varphi"] \arrow[r,shift right=.65ex,"\psi"'] & y \arrow[r,"f","\sim"'] & y'\end{tikzcd}}
where $f\varphi=f\psi$, $f\in W$, $x,y\in C_{\mathrm{cof}}$ \inserted{(whence $[\varphi]=[\psi]$ in $W^{-1}C$)}, one has $[\varphi]=[\psi]$ in $W_{\mathrm{cof}}^{-1}C_{\mathrm{cof}}$.

(condition implied by the condition that $\Psi$ be \uline{faithful})

We shall now work with $C_{\mathrm{cof}}$ instead of $C$. -- thus, changing notation if need be, we shall suppose that ev. object of $C$ is cofibrant.

[\uline{NB} $C_{\mathrm{cof}}$ with the notions induced by $W$ and cof is again a category \unsure{C1 : C3}], the inclusion of $C_{\mathrm{cof}}$ in $C$ commutes with pushouts \deleted{\unsure{which}} associated with diagrams
\diagram{\begin{tikzcd}a \arrow[r,"f"] \arrow[d,"g"'] & b\\ c &\end{tikzcd}}
such that $f$ or $g$ is in cof (the only sums whose existence is postulated!).]
