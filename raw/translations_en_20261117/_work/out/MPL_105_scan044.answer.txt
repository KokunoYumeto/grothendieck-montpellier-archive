\textit{sex is everywhere in the psyche} (and that the latter denies it and closes its eyes where it is changes nothing), and more than anywhere else, it is in its power to create. And on the other hand, \textit{the psyche and the soul are everywhere in reality.}

This is so, at least, and in an obvious way, when it is a question of reality as it is perceived or conceived by the psyche, that is to say of the representation it forms for itself of \deleted{\unsure{this}}\inserted{this} reality, of its relation to it. For the image of reality that the soul constructs for itself at the various conscious and unconscious levels is a creation of the soul just as much as, and more than, a mere more or less faithful reflection of an ``objective reality'' which would exist independently of it. (Reality whose existence there is no question here of denying). That is to say that \textit{the state of maturity and the general dispositions of the soul determine its image of reality as much as and more than this reality itself determines it.}
\margin{$\cdot$}

This seen, one can conceive that a state of fear of the soul before itself, and of flight from the reality of itself, can and must entail a fear and a flight before \textit{all} reality. We can also say that in our relation to it, \textit{all reality acts in the manner of a \textnormal{mirror} in which the psyche sees reflected an image of itself.} (And if this image is rarely perceived at the conscious lev\inserted{e}l, it always is at least in the deep unconscious, including in the medium-deep unconscious, where primordial fear is situated.) Indeed, this reality cannot be distinguished by the psyche from the image it forms for itself of it, that is to say also from its relation to it ; and necessarily and whether it wishes it or not, this image and this relation reflect it.
\margin{$\cdot$}

This image of ourselves which reality sends back to us involves us in an all the more neuralgic way, and it triggers reactions of fear and flight that are all the more energetic or violent, as this reality appears to us as ``closer'' to us. Most often, these reactions are the most vehement or the most implacable when the reality concerns our relation either to ourselves, or to someone close : to a parent, \deleted{\unsure{q}}\inserted{t}o the spouse, to \deleted{\unsure{a}}\inserted{our} child - and this all the more so the stronger the relation is, the more considerable the energy invested in it is. But it is above all \textit{the strength of our relation to this or that reality, the intensity of interest and the energy that we invest in it, which are decisive here.} Thus most often, high-flying philosophical speculations \figure{\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=-.3ex]\draw[line width=.7pt] (0,0)--(.15,.3);\end{tikzpicture}} w\deleted{a}\inserted{i}ll in vain concern the most crucial (and burning indeed !) questions that the human mind has ever asked itself about itself, about God and about the soul, these noble flights will send back to the soul only a pale or even imperceptible image of itself, which will have no power either to challenge it or to frighten it. But even in a science as abstract and as far removed in appearance from all human reality as mathematics, provided only that our relation to the beautiful lady is ardent and immediately it becomes neuralgic for us, and the image it sends back to us of ourselves strong and vehement.
