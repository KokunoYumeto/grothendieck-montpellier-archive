The maps are \uline{injective}.
\deleted{16}

\uline{Proof}. It suffices to prove it for the second map, and one is reduced to the case where $X$ is \inserted{it-$\unsure{\overline m}$} an algebraic group \inserted{$G$}, extension of an abelian variety by a torus. Then the map is a group homomorphism
\[
(16)\qquad \begin{gathered}G(K)\\\Vert\\H^0(K,G)\end{gathered}\longrightarrow H^1(K,H_1(\overline G))
\]
obtained as follows. For every integer $n$ one considers the Kummer exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow {}_nG\longrightarrow G\xrightarrow{n\operatorname{id}_G}G\longrightarrow1
\]
whence exact cohomology sequence, which gives
\[
0\longrightarrow H^0(K,G)_n\longrightarrow H^1(K,{}_nG)\longrightarrow {}_nH^1(K,G)\longrightarrow0
\]
and passing to the limit, one finds
\[
0\longrightarrow\varprojlim_n H^0(K,G)_n\longrightarrow\varprojlim_n H^1(K,{}_nG)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_n {}_nH^1(K,G)
\]
The composite of (16) with the \deleted{\unsure{composite}} \inserted{canonical} homomorphism
\[
H^1(K,H_1(\overline G))-\varprojlim_n H^1(K,{}_nG)
\]
in view of
\[
H_1(\overline G)=\varprojlim_n {}_nG
\]
is none other than the homomorphism induced by
\[
H^0(K)\longrightarrow\varprojlim H^0(K,G)_n\hookrightarrow\varprojlim H^1(K,{}_nG)
\]
