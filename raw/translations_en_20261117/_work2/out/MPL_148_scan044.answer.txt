\uline{Teichmüller groupoids.}

\textcircled{1} Topological case.

The simplest variant is
\[
\mathcal T^+_{\mathrm{top}}\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal T_{\mathrm{top}}
\]
(\uline{\uuline{oriented} topological Teichmüller groupoid})

\uline{Objects} : compact oriented surfaces \uline{with boundary} $(\Sigma)$.

\uline{arrows} : isotopy classes of isomorphisms (preserving orientation)

\margin{In the end, as long as one is not working from the
arithmetic point of view, it seems that it is the \unsure{groupoid} $\mathcal T^\pm$ that \unsure{will be|offers|is}
the most \unsure{used}; \deleted{\unsure{all|hence}} it is thus this one that
\unsure{deserves} the simplest notation $\mathcal T$ here,
and the oriented \unsure{oriented\ groupoid|groupoid|group} to be called $\mathcal T^+$.}

Immediate variant
\[
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{top}}^\pm
\]
(\uline{\uuline{bioriented} topological Teichmüller groupoid}) :
same objects, arrows are the isotopy classes of homeomorphisms
(not necessarily preserving orientation).

Let $[\pm1]$ be the groupoid having a single object $e$ (for example $e=\varnothing$)
and with automorphism group $\pm1$. We have a signature morphism
\diagram{\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
\mathcal T^\pm_{\mathrm{top}}\arrow[r,"\chi"]\&{[\pm1]}
\end{tikzcd}}
whose \uline{fibre} \deleted{is} (or kernel) is
$\mathcal T_{\mathrm{top}}^+=\mathcal T_{\mathrm{top}}$.
\margin{\unsure{Cannulated|Caveat}, \unsure{if one does not restrict oneself|henceforth restrict|has no meaning} to connected surfaces!}

The most complex variant is
\[
S_*\mathcal TD_{\mathrm{top}}^+\quad\text{or}\quad
S_*\mathcal TD_{\mathrm{top}},
\]
``\uline{special decomposed \inserted{oriented} topological Teichmüller groupoid}''
