## 1. The symmetric level-four pinning scale

**Origins:** SA16-283, SA16-286–288, SA16-293, SA16-295–299, SA16-506, SA16-516, SA16-538, SA16-560–561; C-SA16-286, C-SA16-293, C-SA16-295–298, C-SA16-504–505, C-SA16-509–511, C-SA16-513–515, C-SA16-535, C-SA16-537–538; MP-SA16-97, MP-SA16-161, MP-SA16-164–165. These origins use surviving canonical identifiers; retired identifiers remain traceable through `SA16_id_aliases.tsv`.

The target is the exact symmetric, `Q0`-twisted pinning attached to sum-zero halves of the three nonzero two-torsion points, over a base with two invertible. The geometric branch-data/tangent-square-root equivalence is already proved and independently reviewed. The ordinary level-four construction in Conrad, Proposition 3.2, also exists. Neither statement fixes the scalar postponed in scan 100, equation 73, and scan 121, condition 83c. [AntieauMeier2020 §4; ConradLevel §§2–3.]

**First concrete step:** on one ordered split Legendre chart, choose a temporary primitive fourth root and write both differentials in the same tangent/cotangent convention. Compute their ratio, then test it under two generating branch permutations and the change of fourth root. A successful run ends with a descent-compatible formula or an explicit failed identity. The finite group alone cannot identify this scalar.

## 2. Correct tangential Kummer data

**Origins:** SA16-289–293; C-SA16-289–293, C-SA16-391; MP-SA16-95.

The recorded unrestricted category allows an inertia embedding independently of the torsor class. The saved `C2²` example satisfies the written hypotheses and refutes essential surjectivity. The target is a theorem specifying actual tangent inertia, pointed descent data, and morphisms, initially with fixed group and fixed ramification index. The quadratic Legendre case is established; it does not settle the varying-group statement. [SGA1 XIII §2; StacksKummer.]

**First concrete step:** write the source and target categories for one finite étale group `G` and one invertible index `n`, and calculate their automorphism sheaves before testing essential surjectivity. Only after that comparison should morphisms changing the group or index be admitted.

## 3. The algebraic de Rham splitting converse

**Origins:** no Pro item exists for this Codex-only passage; C-SA16-843–845; MP-SA16-265–266; K63-01, scans 38–39 and 66–71.

Katz proves that CM yields the specified algebraic de Rham splitting and asks the converse. Bertrand and Waldschmidt preserve that question; Spence's special-subvariety theorem does not settle all individual algebraic pairs. This run located no verified resolution through the status date. Its independent proof of the zero set of `E2*` resolves a narrower analytic question. [Katz1976, Lemma 4.0.7 and Question 4.0.8; Bertrand1978, Conjecture 3; Waldschmidt2004 p.277; Spence2018, Theorem 5.8.]

**First concrete step:** state the exact period and quasiperiod criterion over an algebraic Weierstrass model with a fixed algebraic differential, and match it to the algebraicity of the relevant almost holomorphic modular value. List explicitly which known transcendence statements imply the desired converse and which do not. This is a formulation and source-boundary task; a few-hour run should not promise to solve a transcendence problem.

## 4. Exceptional trisections with nilpotents and arithmetic bands

**Origins:** SA16-32–33, SA16-60; C-SA16-15, C-SA16-22, C-SA16-25–26; MP-SA16-11, MP-SA16-16.

**First concrete step:** write completed local invariant maps at one harmonic and one cyclotomic point, including stabilizer actions and descent. The result should say exactly which reduced-base statement survives and how its full scheme-theoretic fibre differs.

## 5. Extension across singular normal bases

**Origins:** SA16-56–59; C-SA16-23–24; MP-SA16-17.

The target separates extension of a cubic algebra, its full ordering torsor, and the disjoint marked section. Full étale extension of the ordering torsor makes the map extend by integrality. Divisorial tame conditions suffice in the appropriate regular setting; the checked `A1` singularity shows why they do not suffice on an arbitrary normal base. [SGA1 X, Theorem 3.1; StacksNormalFinite.]

**First concrete step:** formulate the obstruction on the punctured spectrum of a normal local ring and name the exact finite étale torsor that must extend. Compare regular and singular examples before proposing a purity or root-stack formulation.

## 6. Anharmonic symmetry in characteristics two and three
