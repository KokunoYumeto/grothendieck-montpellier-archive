182: Lemma 3.5: Let $S$ be a local algebra over an integrally closed local domain $R$. If $S$ contains a prime ideal $\underline p$ such that $\underline p\cap R=0$, then $\dim S=\dim R$ and, for each prime ideal $\underline p$ in $S$, we have that $\dim S/\underline p=\dim S$ if and only if $\underline p\cap R=0$.
183: 
184: \uline{Proof:} Let $\underline p$ be a prime ideal in $S$ such that $\underline p\cap R=0$. Then $\dim S/\underline p=\dim R$ by ``going down theorem'' since $R\subset S/\underline p$ is a local algebra and $R$ is integrally closed. In particular, we have $\dim S\geq\dim R$ and consequently $\dim S=\dim R$ since $S$ is a local $R$-algebra. For a prime ideal $\underline p$ in $S$, $\dim S/\underline p\leq\dim R/\underline p\cap R$ since $S/\underline p$ is a local algebra over $R/\underline p\cap R$. Therefore $\dim S/\underline p=\dim S(=\dim R)$ entails that $\underline p\cap R=0$ since $R$ is a domain. Conversely, if $\underline p\cap R=0$, we have already observed that $\dim S/\underline p=\dim R$, i.e. $\dim S/\underline p=\dim S$.

286: The main purpose of this section is to prove Theorem 3.8, which provides
287: a decomposition of the ramification index into the sum of certain integers.
288: 
289: Given a local $R$-algebra $S$ over a local ring $R$, we have defined
290: $e(S/R)=\dfrac{e_S(mS)}{e_R(m)}$ and called it the multiplicity of the local $R$-algebra
291: $S$, where $m$ is the maximal ideal of $R$. As we have seen, the basic object
292: in the theory of ramification index is the multiplicity of local algebras.
293: This number $e(S/R)$ is not in general an integer ([9]) and a natural
294: question is to ask under what circumstances this number is an integer.
295: We already know two cases:
296: 
297: 1) If $R$ is a regular local ring, then $e(S/R)$ is an integer since
298: $e_R(\underline m)=1$ where $\underline m$ is the maximal ideal of $R$.
299: 
300: 2) If $R\subset S$ is a local algebra of normal local rings such that $\overline S$ is
301: separably algebraic over $\overline R$, then $e(S/R)$ is equal to $r(S/R)$ by Theorem 2.7
302: and hence is an integer.

304: The following propositions enable us to relax the hypothesis in the
305: above two cases.
306: 
307: Lemma 3.1: If $S$ is a local $R$-algebra over a local ring $R$ such that
308: $\operatorname{w.hd}_R S<\infty$, then $\widehat S$ is a finite local $\widehat R$-algebra such that $\operatorname{hd}_{\widehat R}\widehat S<\infty$.
309: 
310: \uline{Proof}: By the definition of a local algebra, $S=A_{\underline p}$ for a finite
311: $R$-algebra $A$ where $\underline p$ is a maximal ideal in $A$. It follows easily that
312: $\widehat S=(A\otimes_R\widehat R)_{\widehat{\underline p}}=(S\otimes_R\widehat R)_{\underline p'}$ where $\widehat{\underline p}=\underline p\otimes_R\widehat R$ and $\underline p'$ is the maximal ideal
313: in $S\otimes_R\widehat R$ generated by $\widehat{\underline p}$. Therefore $\widehat S$ is flat over $S\otimes_R\widehat R$ and consequently
314: $\operatorname{w.hd}_{\widehat R}\widehat S\leq\operatorname{w.hd}_{\widehat R}S\otimes_R\widehat R\leq\operatorname{w.hd}_R S$.
315: Thus if $\operatorname{w.hd}_R S<\infty$, then $\operatorname{w.hd}_{\widehat R}\widehat S<\infty$.
316: However $\widehat S$ is finitely generated as $\widehat R$-module since $\overline S$ is a finitely generated
