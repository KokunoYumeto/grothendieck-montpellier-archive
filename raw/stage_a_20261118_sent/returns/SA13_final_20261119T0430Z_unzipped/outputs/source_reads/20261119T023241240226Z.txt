### N49. Normalization does not need to be finite as a module

**File:** cote 33. **Scan(s):** 129, 127. **Approx. source line(s):** 129:Lemma 4; 127:continuation. **Kind:** reading question.

The assertion that the normalization of a one-dimensional local domain is a semilocal PID is correct under the usual Noetherian convention: normalization is semilocal Dedekind, and its invertible ideals are principal (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., [Tags 09IG](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09IG), [09N2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09N2), [02M9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02M9)). Thus absence of module-finite normalization or excellence is not by itself a gap in this lemma. What remains to recover is the reused source's convention for “local domain,” which is not spelled out in the surviving statement; a non-Noetherian reading would be a materially different claim.

ENTRY MPL_33_098-146#53 (notes source line 473)
### N51. Equal characteristic alone need not give a coefficient field

**File:** cote 33. **Scan(s):** 135. **Approx. source line(s):** 7. **Kind:** own idea.

The separate implication “same characteristic so that $R$ has a coefficient field” also needs qualification: $\mathbb Q[x,y]_{(x^2-2,y)}$ is equicharacteristic regular local of dimension two with residue field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, but its fraction field $\mathbb Q(x,y)$ contains no square root of $2$. Thus no coefficient field can map isomorphically to that residue field, while the complete-local Cohen theorem provides one in its own hypotheses (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., [Tag 032A](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/032A)). This is our second conditional scope test, distinct from N50, not an assertion that the unseen preceding hypotheses were absent.

**Confidence:** high — the residue field and the obstruction inside the rational function field are explicit.

### N54. The literal étale criterion admits a dual-number counterexample

**File:** cote 33. **Scan(s):** 145. **Approx. source line(s):** 23–36. **Kind:** own idea.

In condition (c), finite separability of $\kappa(y)/\kappa(x)$ alone does not imply the subsequent equality $m_y=m_xO_y$: $\operatorname{Spec}(k[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2)\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is finite, flat and finitely presented, with residue extension $k/k$, but has $m_y=(\varepsilon)\ne0$. This is a counterexample to the isolated residue-field-only wording as transcribed, not to the stronger condition printed immediately afterward or to an unseen theorem elsewhere.
