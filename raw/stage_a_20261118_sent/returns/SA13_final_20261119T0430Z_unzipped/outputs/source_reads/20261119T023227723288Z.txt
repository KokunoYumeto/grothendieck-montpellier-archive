### N23. Why reduction to a finite field is rejected

**File:** cote 33. **Scan(s):** 126. **Approx. source line(s):** 4–8. **Kind:** unpursued implication.

“Il ne serait pas admissible de se limiter à des corps” makes the arithmetic objective explicit: the author wants information finer than the fixed-point count modulo $\ell$. The obstruction is that cohomology modules over $\mathbb Z/\ell^\nu\mathbb Z$ need not be free, so the usual degreewise finite-projective trace cannot simply be applied to each of them. ENTRY MPL_33_098-146#27 (notes source line 245)
### N25. A perfect complex with nonprojective cohomology

**File:** cote 33. **Scan(s):** 126–132. **Approx. source line(s):** 126:6–8; 128:4; 130:2–6; 132:5–10. **Kind:** own idea.

Let $A=\mathbb Z/\ell^2\mathbb Z$ and take the two-term complex $P=[A\xrightarrow{\ell}A]$ in degrees $0,1$. It is perfect, with $H^0(P)\cong H^1(P)\cong A/\ell A$ nonprojective, while the trace of its identity is unambiguously $1-1=0$ in $A$ by the finite-projective supertrace formula (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., [Tag 03TI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03TI)). This gives a concrete reason for the manuscript's replacement of the system of cohomology groups by its complex, and differs from N10 because the **object** here is perfect even though its cohomology modules are not.

**Confidence:** high — all modules, differential, cohomology and traces are explicit.

### N32. The compact-support amplitude estimate

**File:** cote 33. **Scan(s):** 140, 142. **Approx. source line(s):** 140:22–25; 142:2–18. **Kind:** unproved claim.

The inserted hypothesis “l'Anneau de base soit de torsion” belongs to the proposition, whose proof is described as “Résulte … de Künneth.” For compactifiable $f$ with geometric fibres of dimension at most $d$, Deligne (1973, XVII, Corollary 5.2.8.1 and Proposition 5.2.9) gives $\mathrm{cd}(Rf_!)\leq2d$ for torsion sheaves and the projection comparison. Our inference sends Tor-amplitude $[a,b]$ in cohomological indexing into $[a,b+2d]$; setting $a=-M,b=-N$ then yields the displayed homological $[N-2d,M]$. This supports the direction of the shift without imposing a prime-to-characteristic hypothesis absent from this compact-support argument.
