## The order of the work in the packet

The first sheets combine reused invariant-theory material with biextension calculations. The Reynolds identity depends on linear reductivity. The quotient discussion uses a geometric notion of freeness stronger than merely trivial stabilizers. Follow-up research identified the reused source in Mumford’s Geometric Invariant Theory, through the checked1982 Mumford–Fogarty second edition. Its revised footnote settles the finite-type quotient question in its precise setting; the manuscript margin about replacing smoothness by flatness remains underdetermined.

The biextension sheets match SGA7 VII2.9 and VIII1.1–1.4: internal Hom vanishing, rather than global Hom vanishing, is the relevant hypothesis. Birigidification supplies a unique biextension structure in the specified abelian-scheme situation. The Picard calculation has a decisive sign alarm: the quadratic parallelogram identity uses a plus, whereas the transcribed minus fails even on an ample NS class. The doubled-extension construction is retained as conditional on the unfinished homomorphic lift.

The reused Haar sheets are identified more precisely than in the earlier notes. They belong to the source family of Bourbaki, Intégration VIII§5, not to an unidentified new Grothendieck theorem. The published discussion supplies the support-limit, normalized compactness, uniform-discreteness and covolume results. One literal avoidance-set formula is false; a set-theoretic correction or the weaker inclusion needed by the proof repairs it. Compact generation is an actual hypothesis, as the direct-sum discrete-group counterexample shows.

The degree-one invariant-cycle sheets are more delicate. Raynaud1970 identifies the Cartier-divisor and Picard ingredients, and SGA7 IX12.1 supplies a scoped curve comparison. The kernel in the manuscript is a cyclic subgroup of vertical torsion, not automatically the entire torsion group in arbitrary dimension. Raynaud’s P^τ is not silently replaced by P^0. The arithmetic tensor order is l^{v_l(e)}, and the added p-primary cases require an explicit flat-cohomology interpretation. The Brauer inverse-limit step becomes correct once its multiplication-by-l transitions are supplied; bounded exponent alone would not justify it. The exact short1967 announcement numbering and the full amended all-n statement remain honestly only partly identified.

The orthogonality pages lead to a particularly useful correction. The finite-field weight argument kills a specified pairing and does not require semisimplicity: disjoint annihilating polynomials suffice. SGA7 IX2.6(a) identifies precisely this arithmetic inclusion. It does not identify the entire orthogonal complement over the broader adic base written in the manuscript. The full equality is the separate henselian-trait theorem IX2.4. The question of a single finite-type model for all torsion levels remains distinct from finite-level descent.

The relative-curve lattice uses residue weights. Symmetry belongs to D u, not necessarily u, and an integral perfect pairing cannot be asserted when those weights are nonunits without a comparison. The abstract discriminant form is checked directly. Its geometric identification is matched with Bosch–Lorenzini Theorem4.6 and the qualifications in Corollary4.7, not with an unconditional perfectness claim.

Cote39 develops selected families of formal functions. The two drafts change both the coefficient ring and the axiom labels. Inverse closure H gives a Jacobson augmentation ideal; it does not by itself reflect every ambient unit. The complete counterfamily A_n=Z+(t)Q[[t]], A_0=Z, satisfies H and separates these assertions. H plus divided-derivative closure D repairs full unit reflection by coefficient extraction. This also explains why the self-rejected evaluation at1 cannot be reused. The faithful-flatness question depends on whether its letter d means H or D: the polynomial family refutes faithfulness under D alone and does not refute H+D.

Formal flows, BCH, Pfaff integration, projective-module coordinates and integral quotient effectivity remain distinct proposed closure properties. Ambient formal existence theorems do not show that solutions stay in an arbitrary A-family. Modern Weierstrass and analytic structures are useful comparisons with stronger hypotheses, not blanket resolutions of every question on these pages.

The final preparation theorem has the broad scope its wording suggests: a separated complete linear topology suffices, without a noetherian or local assumption. Strickland’s FPFP Theorem33.1 and Corollary33.2 match that scope. A proof by discrete quotients and a finite nilpotent filtration is supplied. The final scalar α in the manuscript must be checked against the earlier full unit series; the exact Z/8 computation shows why dropping its higher coefficients breaks the argument.

## Completed Codex review: K34-05 through K34-08

The central convention is now explicit. The manuscript complex Φ is the usual vanishing-cycle complex shifted by−1. Thus ordinary vanishing cycles concentrated in degree n appear here in degree n+1. This is forced by both displayed exact sequences, and has been checked against the original SGA7 construction and Illusie’s modern conventions. Local cycle triangles require no properness. The identification of their global hypercohomology with the whole-fibre specialization cone uses X/S proper. The smooth family P¹_S with one point removed from its closed fibre gives a concrete counterexample if that global hypothesis is dropped: the special fibre is A¹, the generic fibre is P¹, and their H² groups differ. These conventions control the later Codex depth sheets reviewed by the parent. [C-SA13-802,804,809,812,815,816; P4_SGA7IIntro; P4_Illusie2021; proof_audit.md §§10,20.]

The smooth-comparison proof contains a separate ramification issue. Pullback on a codimension-one fundamental class multiplies by the ramification index, not always by the field degree. Over a strictly henselian DVR with imperfect residue field there are finite separable extensions with e=1 and residue degree p. The audit supplies the explicit extension u^p−πu−a and checks all of these assertions. Further tame ℓ-power ramification still kills every class in the direct limit, so the smooth comparison theorem survives this repair. [C-SA13-807,808; MP-SA13-230; P4_Riou2014; proof_audit.md §11.]

The normal-crossing induction has been read as an actual proof, including its cancelled cover argument, the Gysin squares and the admitted residue compatibility. Removing a union of divisors gives the intersection of their complements, so the deleted union cannot be restored. Gabber’s absolute purity theorem, in Riou’s checked exposition, removes the historical purity hypothesis for regular noetherian pairs with invertible coefficients; no excellence assumption is added to that precise theorem. The residue square is valid after fixing the cup order and Kummer sign. A stronger coefficient reading is false: the Artin–Schreier local system given by z^p−z=y/x on D(x) has one global invariant section but p independent sections after restriction to y=0. Requiring the coefficient to extend across the ambient strict-local X repairs the induction. The intended carried coefficient convention remains a reading question, not an unresolved modern purity theorem. [C-SA13-824–834; MP-SA13-233–235; P4_Riou2014; proof_audit.md §12.]

The tame tower calculations preserve the finite and integral qualifications. Simultaneously adjoining r independent roots has group μ_m^r. In the cohomological kernel assertion, n must be greater than1; the zero coefficient ring detects no coset. A finite-index multiplicity map requires at least one positive multiplicity, and a least common multiple used for the vertical components excludes zero horizontal multiplicities. The exterior-algebra calculation, the weighted multiplicity map and the finite filtration give the expected nilpotence bound. None implies an automatic splitting or saturation of every integral filtration step. The latter adjective and the incomplete Griffiths reference remain distinct unresolved identification problems. [C-SA13-835–849,819,820; MP-SA13-232,238; proof_audit.md §§13–14.]

The original published SGA7 I3.4 has itself been checked against its image. Its q-prime formula is off by one, so it cannot be used to certify that sharper exponent. Illusie2002 formula(8.4.4.1) gives min(q+1,logarithmic rank), and Illusie2021 Theorem2.3 gives the general i+1 theorem. The full nearby-cycle constructibility result is Deligne’s SGA4½ Finitude3.2, whose original page was inspected; the earlier SGA7 XIII2.3.1 is restricted to equal characteristic zero. These later results settle the nearby-cycle consequences independently of the manuscript’s general resolution programme. The open positive-characteristic resolution problem is not declared solved by the later finiteness theorem. [C-SA13-818,850–854; P4_SGA7IIntro; P4_SGA7XIII; P4_DeligneFinitude1977; P4_IllusieLog2002; P4_Illusie2021; P4_Temkin2024.]

The normal-crossing tame-inertia claim is matched with exact later statements in Rapoport–Zink, Nakayama and Illusie’s logarithmic exposition. Trivial wild inertia is separated from possibly nontrivial tame unipotence. The nonvertical/open-stratum qualifications are retained. General constructible ℓ-adic systems are likewise distinguished from a naive inverse limit of torsion sheaves. [C-SA13-805,855,856; MP-SA13-231,237; P4_RapoportZink1982; P4_Nakayama1998; P4_IllusieLog2002.]

K34-08’s general infinite-root-tower dimension question has a negative answer in its written noetherian generality. Orgogozo’s Exposé XVIII_B constructs a regular strictly local nonexcellent ring of dimension2 with H³(D(t),Z/ℓ) nonzero. The prime-to-p root tower has group of ℓ-cohomological dimension1. Its Cartan–Leray spectral sequence therefore rules out the proposed upper bound1 on the tower: otherwise the base H³ would vanish. The original counterexample and this additional deduction are separately attributed. This does not decide every narrower excellent or geometric version. It supplies the leading Codex direction SA13-499 / C-SA13-868 / MP-SA13-239. [P4_OrgogozoDimension2014 §§2.1–2.4; P4_IllusieDimension2014; proof_audit.md §17.]

The depth and Lefschetz pages have two concrete repairs. First, the stronger lci consequences are now available over general traits through Illusie2003, beyond the characteristic-zero antecedent in SGA7 I4.5–4.7. The audit explicitly translates perversity to the manuscript’s degree convention and preserves the support-dimension premise. Second, vanishing only at the omitted point x does not justify vanishing on a punctured special fibre. The needed hypothesis is uniform vanishing throughout that fibre; isolated nonsmoothness is a sufficient geometric condition. The first localization triangle uses support cohomology in the ambient strict localization, while the second uses extension by zero. Interchanging them would change the argument. [C-SA13-872,874,878–882; MP-SA13-240,242; P4_Illusie2003; proof_audit.md §§18–19.]

The pinching example is also made concrete. The local fibre-product algebra has an embedded square-zero element in its special fibre. Its generic fibre has two connected components while the special fibre is connected, giving a nonzero manuscript Φ¹. Suppressing that embedded structure would falsely turn the example into a counterexample to the lci theorem. The construction, a proper projective version and the H⁰ computation are written out. [SA13-501; C-SA13-873; MP-SA13-241; proof_audit.md §18.]
