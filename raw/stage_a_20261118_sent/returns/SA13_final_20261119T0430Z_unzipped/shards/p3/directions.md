# Ranked follow-up directions from the p3 review

These are bounded next tasks emerging from the consolidation. They are not assertions of novelty, and this review does not begin a new research programme under their names. Ranking weighs mathematical consequence, how precisely the remaining question is stated, and whether an initial step has a clear stopping condition.

## 1. Repair the specialization lemma at the exact quantifier

**Records:** SA13-367; C-SA13-355 and C-SA13-363; MP-SA13-149; A36-02, scans 36:026–027, global lines 111–118.

The literal arbitrary-point assertion is false, even on a smooth ambient scheme with an invertible rank-one semilinear map. The generic-point case has a complete proof. The useful next step is to inspect the original scan for the scope of “t un point de S” and the later inserted generic-point qualification, then determine which version the preceding reduction 7.5 actually needs. A more general valid statement could refer to the normalization of the closure together with the resulting special decomposition group, but the residue-field change must be explicit. The nodal counterexample and repaired theorem give a sharp test for any proposed formulation.

## 2. Separate the Hasse–Witt trace theorem from the nonconiveau conclusion

**Records:** SA13-356–366; C-SA13-342–345, 350, 353, 359–360; MP-SA13-142, 145–147; A36, scans 36:017–022 and 025–026.

The weakened invariant-subspace trace statement does not imply the original whole-space statement, its every-residue corollary, or the deleted non-p-group consequence. Katz's published nonnilpotence and Koblitz's later stable-rank growth supply different results. The first next step is to fix one family, one base field and the whole-space quantifier, then trace that exact assertion through Katz's and Koblitz's bibliographies. In parallel, a fully written finite-field zeta-factor proof should state the fixed ambient factors and distinguish its nonconiveau conclusion from the stronger trace assertion. No search failure in this review establishes that the strong theorem is false or new.

## 3. Finish the categorical gluing theorem for biextensions

**Records:** SA13-317–322; C-SA13-291–295; MP-SA13-132–133; scans 36:048–050; comparison SGA7 VII2.7 and VIII1–2.

The manuscript gives restriction and finite pairing data for one and then two quotient variables. The tensor vanishing is proved, but the full equivalence of categories at the stated generality has not been matched to an exact printed proposition or independently completed. The next step is to write the long exact Biext sequence with its automorphisms, identify the obstruction and ambiguity maps, and compare the resulting groupoid with the proposed data. The bounded-torsion example shows why replacing all finite pairings by a Tate pairing changes the problem. Completion should be judged by an explicit quasi-inverse and a verification of morphisms, not by agreement of isomorphism classes alone.

## 4. Identify the precise pairing equality and sign in the editorial questions

**Records:** SA13-254, 275, 286–292, 316; C-SA13-265–268, 290; MP-SA13-63, 76, 81–82; scans 36:004, 007, 030–034, 046; p4 scans 36:082–084.

A finite perfect pairing and an inverse-limit perfect pairing can still differ by sign or normalization. The manuscript explicitly asks whether two constructions coincide. The first step is to recover both cited formulas in the relevant draft, including the transposition convention, the Tate twist, and one-factor versus two-factor transition. An elliptic-curve computation then gives a controlled normalization test. The weighted curve-discriminant formula in p4 is a related later geometric question, with extra integral-lattice issues; it should not be treated as the answer before those maps are compared.

## 5. Make the ordinary-factor counterexample a fully specified family

**Records:** SA13-269; C-SA13-254; MP-SA13-155; scan 36:006, global lines 214–216.

A geometrically simple generic fibre would indeed prevent a positive-dimensional proper abelian subvariety lifting the special ordinary factor. The remaining task is to produce the exact algebraic family and to define what “lifting” means in the disputed statement. The first step is to distinguish an abelian subscheme from a formal or p-divisible subgroup, then find a primary deformation theorem giving the required ordinary/nonordinary product and generic simplicity. This review does not certify the sketched refutation before those conditions are checked.

## 6. Determine the legitimate singular Picard–torsor formula

**Records:** SA13-296–305; C-SA13-272–281; MP-SA13-84–87; scans 36:039–041.

The nodal infinite-order abelian torsor and the real Brauer class rule out two unrestricted interpretations. Raynaud's finite locally free relative Picard formula is precise and proved, but it is not the manuscript's unspecified absolute abelian-torsor/Ext identity. The next step is to choose the relative site, distinguish the Picard functor from its identity component, and write the derived comparison with all base terms. A version for a single nodal curve would test exactly where normalization and local extension data enter. This is worth doing only after those conventions are fixed.

## 7. Keep rigidity, primitive classes and the NS quotient in one coherent continuation

**Records:** SA13-324–329; C-SA13-296–300; MP-SA13-134; p4 SA13-405–409 and MP-SA13-92; scans 36:052, 054, 056, 058.

Universal pointed-map rigidity proves the primitive torsor/extension result and the formal defect-kernel injection. The continuation adds biextensions, transpose compatibility, a quadratic pullback identity and a possible doubled splitting. The next step is an annotated joined statement across the package boundary, with the coefficient group and rigidity hypothesis stated once. The doubled splitting remains a separate assertion and the sign in the quadratic identity must be resolved before it is used. The cross-package audit records which objects should merge and which claims should remain distinct.

## 8. Resolve the Néron limit hypotheses before reusing the arithmetic orthogonality argument

**Records:** SA13-250, 257, 258, 267, 274, 277–278; C-SA13-251, 256–257, 310; MP-SA13-60, 64–65, 72, 75, 78; p4 SA13-432–439; scans 36:003–008 and 073–080.

Completion base change, arbitrary limits, torsion subgroup closure, and the adic orthogonality proof have different hypotheses. The next step is a line-by-line comparison of the editorial draft's numbered assertions with original SGA7 IX2.4–2.6 and 5.12–5.13, using the original print rather than an inferred numbering correspondence. At ell=p, inertia semisimplicity must not be inferred from potential good reduction. A clean table of which step uses normality, henselianity, completeness or finite residue fields would resolve several editorial obligations without asserting a stronger theorem.

## 9. Audit the rational-point interpolation bound separately from existence

**Records:** SA13-368–372; C-SA13-356–358, 361; MP-SA13-150–152; scans 36:023–026.

The broad existence theorem follows by interpolation after a finite field extension, and the norm-form construction is valid. The manuscript's explicit threshold and the two-product improvement require a same-degree interpolation argument and disjoint finite zero sets. The first step is to reconstruct those exact finite sets and correct only the logical inequality in the commentary, preserving the ambiguous written signs. A successful bound should state how the field extension, the degree and the chosen number N depend on one another.

## 10. Identify the reused Lie and cohomology typescripts

**Records:** SA13-331–335; C-SA13-301–308; MP-SA13-135–136; scans 36:038, 049, 051.

The visible independent identifiers are “p98, no316” on the Lie leaf and pages 15 and 13 on the Čech leaves. The mathematics checked here does not determine their bibliographic source or authorship. The next step is to search exact surrounding phrases and the local numbering in candidate editions, then inspect the actual printed pages. Godement and Bourbaki remain candidates, not verified exact sources. The internal order 051 before 049 should be preserved as a source observation, without inferring the extent of the missing typescript.

## 11. Recover the missing functorial definitions in the higher-duality programme

**Records:** SA13-295, 304–310; C-SA13-271, 280–285; MP-SA13-87–89, 154; scans 36:037, 041–044; related 33:166–167.

The current formulas leave the site, dualizing complex, variance and degree conventions underdetermined. The first task is documentary: locate the definitions of the intended f_! and shifted duality in the adjacent archive material, then state one finite bounded comparison with its edge maps. The elementary finite-free exchange and split evaluation formulas already checked should be retained as special cases. Broad derived-category language alone does not settle the programme.
