## 1. What these leaves contain, in their own order

The opening leaves, 36:003–008, are comments on a draft of SGA7 IX. Their objects are Néron models, kernels, inertia, finite and toric parts, ordinary factors, and biextensions. Much of their mathematical content is expressed as a request for an omitted hypothesis or proof in a separately numbered draft. A question about draft 6.6 is not automatically a theorem numbered 6.6 in the published book. The three recurring obligations are to distinguish the generic characteristic from the residue characteristic; to specify the category of a group, pro-group, or limiting module; and to compare actual pairing constructions, including their sign.

Scans 010–027, absent from the earlier Pro selection but present in Codex A36, develop a different line of work. They begin with the Leray class of a primitive cohomology class and its functoriality under correspondences. This becomes an algebraic proof of the Griffiths argument, conditional on an algebraic Lefschetz inverse and absence of a curve-level vanishing summand. The later pages try to remove the latter condition for high-degree hypersurfaces in positive characteristic through coherent Frobenius, point counts, and norm forms. The revision from a trace on the entire representation to a trace on an invariant subspace is decisive: the manuscript itself marks the stronger consequence unproved. These pages have precise continuations in SGA7 II XVIII, XX, XXI, and XXII, but those continuations preserve important qualifications.

Scans 028–034 assemble lattices, tori, abelian quotients, compatible trivializations, and two-term complexes. They anticipate the familiar duality of 1-motives. The finite realization is derived from `RHom(Z/n, [M→G])`, and its pairing is built before any Tate limit. The retained typescript pieces occur in reverse logical order: §2.1 on 034, §2.2 on 032, and §2.3 on 030. This is evidence of reused pages and a textual sequence; it does not date the handwriting.

The cover at 035 introduces Picard and duality. The subsequent pages move between Kummer sequences, Albanese maps, torsors under abelian and affine groups, higher Picard objects, and a shriek-adjunction programme. Several displayed comparisons have question marks, and an argument about extending a divisor across a birational morphism is cancelled in full. The reused Lie-algebra page at 038 is an independent printed source. It contains a valid exponential calculation once its omitted characteristic-zero setting is restored; neither its author nor its exact edition has been identified.

Scans 045–046 concern Artin–Schreier–Witt, stable and fixed parts, fundamental groups, and Weil pairings. Scans 048–050 return to the reconstruction of a biextension from a restriction and finite torsion pairings. The author explicitly distinguishes a system of finite pairings from its inverse-limit pairing. ## 3. Editorial monodromy leaves: what can and cannot be checked

### Semiabelian filtration and the isogeny test

C-SA13-250 is a statement about an existing morphism, not the Tate theorem asserting that every equivariant map is algebraic. Let `f:G→H` be a homomorphism of semiabelian varieties over a field and let `ell` be invertible there. A torus maps trivially to an abelian variety, so `f` preserves the torus/abelian filtration. The image of a semiabelian variety is semiabelian. The connected part of the inverse image of the target torus maps to a subgroup of the source abelian quotient which is both affine and proper, hence finite; rationalizing removes this finite discrepancy. This proves strictness up to isogeny. Integral strictness of arbitrary lattices is a separate claim.

If `f` is an isogeny, its finite kernel and cokernel disappear on rational Tate modules. Conversely the identity components of its kernel and cokernel have zero rational Tate modules if `V_ell(f)` is an isomorphism. A positive-dimensional semiabelian variety has nonzero torus or abelian Tate module, so those components vanish. The remaining kernel is finite and the image has full dimension, hence is the whole connected target. This establishes the stated test. The abelian part is also recorded, with a complete proof, in Gajda–Petersen, Lemma 2.2 (arXiv:2207.09345); it must not be confused with their deeper Theorem 2.3.

For C-SA13-251 the kernel identity follows simply by intersecting the equations `f(x)=0` and `nx=0`. A kernel between finite étale group schemes is finite étale, as can be checked after an étale cover where both are constant finite groups. Neither argument proves the further special/generic-fibre inclusion on 005: that statement lacks its full hypotheses and carries an eta/s reading doubt (C-SA13-310).

### Inertia, the two characteristics, and BT good reduction

A valuation on `K` must be prolonged to a separable closure before inertia is an embedded subgroup of `G_K`; different choices conjugate it. This settles the categorical part of the objection on 005, not the missing draft proof. The associated quotient by inertia for a henselian valuation explains the proposed morphism of étale classifying topoi, but the ringed-topos construction and the direction of its base ring remain underdetermined (C-SA13-253).

The original SGA7 I IX is now available and the relevant passages were inspected: 3.5, 5.12.1, and 5.13. Printed page 387, 5.12.1, restricts the translation between BT notions and the usual inertia statements to `ell` different from the residue characteristic. Page 388, 5.13, permits the mixed-characteristic `ell=p` case for its BT extension statements. It does not license replacing good reduction by semisimplicity of the p-adic inertia representation. This is the precise published distinction needed by the separate generic-characteristic and residue-characteristic comments on 006–007. The comparison with K34-04, scan 067, was checked on both supplied texts; the latter explicitly allows the residue prime in a semisimplicity iff potentially-good assertion, and p2 records its counterexample.

The ordinary-factor objection on 006 has a sound conditional core: if an abelian surface has simple geometric generic fibre, no positive-dimensional proper abelian subvariety of that fibre can lift a special elliptic factor. It does not exclude lifting a formal subgroup or a p-divisible subgroup. Nor does the sentence itself prove that a family with all the required deformation properties exists. C-SA13-254 therefore retains the family-existence and interpretation obligations. The modern BT and reduction comparators are not a substitute for that particular deformation.

The completion question has a precise modern answer in Robert, Proposition 3.1.5, which states the relevant Néron base change with ramification index one and separable residue extension and includes completion. BLR's publisher record was opened, but its complete proof was not inspected. Questions about arbitrary inverse limits, excellence in an absent paragraph, the operation `R`, and a sign in draft 9.5.8 remain exact editorial obligations, with bibliographic and mathematical limits recorded in MP-SA13-60–78.

## 4. Two-term complexes, torsion, and biextension pairings

### Internal Ext and compatible trivializations

The vanishing of internal `Ext^1(M,G_m)` for a locally constant finite free lattice is local: after trivializing `M=Z^r`, internal `Hom(M,-)` is the exact functor `(-)^r`. It says nothing by itself about global `Ext^1`, which can contain base cohomology. For an abelian scheme `B/S`, a homomorphism `B→G_m` is pulled back from a function on `S` because proper connected fibres give the universal equality `f_*O_B=O_S`; preserving the identity forces this function to be one. These are two different vanishings with different jobs in the construction on 029.

The two lifts on the leaf induce two trivializations over `M×M′`; requiring them to agree is exactly the extra compatibility needed for a biextension of complexes. Deligne's *Hodge III* 10.2.1 identifies such biextensions for complexes in degrees −1 and 0 with the appropriate derived `Ext` of their tensor product. The precise source was opened, and the January 1972 preprint cover was distinguished from the 1974 publication. Deligne 10.2.5's explicit torsion construction is over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero; that hypothesis is preserved when it is used. The full generality of another paper or chapter is not inferred from the vocabulary “1-motive.” [Deligne1974HodgeIII; Deligne1972HodgePreprint.]

### Finite realization and inverse limits

Resolve `Z/n` by `[Z --n→ Z]` and use the chosen cone sign. For `A=[M --u→ G]`, the degree-zero group is

\[
V_n(A)=\{(m,g):u(m)=ng\}/\{(nm',u(m'))\}.
\]

Changing the sign of the second coordinate gives the equivalent opposite cone convention; mixing conventions in a pairing does not. If `M[n]=0` and multiplication by `n` on `G` is an epimorphism of sheaves, the map to `M/nM` is locally surjective. Its kernel is `G[n]`: subtract `(nm′,u(m′))` when the first coordinate is divisible by `n`, and use `M[n]=0` for injectivity. This proves C-SA13-262–263 as sheaf statements. The code enumerates exact finite models with `M=Z`, `G=Q/Z`, and `u(1)=1/d`; the symbolic argument, not that enumeration, proves the general result.

For geometric realizations of a 1-motive and `ell` invertible, the standard transition maps are surjective on the needed finite systems. The Mittag–Leffler condition removes `lim^1`, yielding the exact Tate sequence in C-SA13-264. An arbitrary inverse limit of sheaves or an exchange with global sections requires additional justification. No such unrestricted assertion is certified here.

### Pairing transitions and filtered perfectness

A biextension supplies two canonical `n`-fold trivializations on a pair of `n`-torsion points. Their ratio is a root of unity. Changing a local lift changes both trivializations by the same power, so the ratio descends. The transition with both inputs multiplied is

\[
e_n(mx,my)=e_{mn}(x,y)^m.
\]

The power is essential. The code checks it exactly on every pair in the standard symplectic modules for the specified small levels; Milne, Lemma 16.1, was opened for the general compatibility. Interchanging the two complex factors carries the derived sign of Deligne 10.2.4. Perfectness alone cannot determine this sign, and it cannot identify the editorial `A. M.` pairing with the author's pairing.

For the filtered argument, the pairing gives a map from one filtered finite locally free group to the Cartier dual of the other with opposite filtration. Suppose each graded map is an isomorphism. Induct on the filtration length, comparing the two short exact sequences consisting of one successive piece and the preceding filtration step. Exact duality and the five lemma give an isomorphism at each stage. This proves C-SA13-268 only after the orthogonality and graded identifications in C-SA13-267 are established. Equality of total ranks would not exclude a nontrivial finite kernel.

### Tor boundary

Original SGA7 VIII 1.1.3–1.1.4 and 1.3, printed pages 220–225, were inspected in the original book, not accepted from a later retypesetting. The manuscript's typed proof invokes these same equation numbers. If `nP=P` and `nQ=0`, then `P⊗Q=0`: locally write `p=np′` and move the `n` to `Q`. The Tor boundary gives the stated `Tor_1(P,Q)=P[n]⊗Q` comparison in this setting. Applying the derived biextension formalism makes the finite pairing an edge map through `Hom(Tor_1(P,Q),G)`. The text's final compatibility sentence is cut off, so agreement with an established formalism does not turn the retained leaf into a complete proof. The specific chapter reference is VIII here; a broad citation to VII is insufficient for this numbered identity.

## 5. Picard and duality: exact safe statements and diagnostics

### Curve Kummer and characteristic-p coefficients

For a smooth proper geometrically connected curve over algebraically closed `k` and invertible `n`, the units are `k^*` and `n:k^*→k^*` is surjective. Kummer therefore gives `H^1(X,mu_n)=Pic(X)[n]=J[n](k)`; its degree term is `H^2(X,mu_n)=Z/n`. Omitting the degree term changes the duality diagram. At the residue characteristic, three distinct coefficient sequences must be kept apart: fppf Kummer for `mu_p`, étale Artin–Schreier for `Z/p`, and fppf Frobenius on `G_a` for `alpha_p`. Their finite group schemes and topologies are not interchangeable. [Stacks03PK; Stacks0A3J; SGA4XVIII.]

The original SGA4 XVIII PDF was eventually obtained despite the first web fetch limit. Section 1.2.9(b), printed page 497, gives the smooth-base torsion statement for abelian torsors and cites Raynaud XIII 2.6. That precise theorem supports the use on 036–039. Raynaud's original book theorem was not directly opened, so the bibliography retains that distinction. For pointed smooth proper connected `X` over algebraically closed `k`, maps to an abelian variety split into a constant part and `Hom(Alb_X,A)`. Divisibility of `A(k)` removes the constants in the Kummer quotient, yielding C-SA13-273.

The standard direct-system maps `H/nH→H/mnH` send `h` to `mh`. The map to `H⊗Q/Z` sends that class to `h⊗1/n`; compatibility is immediate. For `H=Z`, every element of the colimit is torsion, whereas `Q` is torsion free. This proves the Q/QZ alarm independently of any uncertain ink. It is a reading diagnostic, not permission to change the transcription.

### Singular and arithmetic counterexamples

C-SA13-276 refutes only the proposed absolute interpretation that every smooth proper `X` has `H^i(X,G_m)=0` for `i≥2`. Take `X=Spec R`, which is smooth and proper over `R`. The Hamilton quaternion algebra is a central division algebra: its norm is a sum of four real squares, so every nonzero element is invertible. Its Brauer class is nonzero. Equivalently `R^*/N(C^*)` has two classes, because the norm is positive and every positive real is a norm. This does not refute a geometric statement over an algebraically closed field or an assertion about a sheafified relative cohomology object.

For C-SA13-277 take the nodal rational proper curve over `C`, with normalization `nu:P^1→C` identifying 0 and infinity, and an abelian variety `A` with a point `a` of infinite order. The normalization comparison of étale sheaves has the difference map at the node

\[
0\longrightarrow A_C\longrightarrow \nu_*A_{P^1}
\longrightarrow i_*A_{\{\mathrm{node}\}}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Exactness can be checked on strict henselian neighbourhoods: a pair of maps on the two branches descends precisely when its values at the node agree. Local constants on the two normalized branches make the difference map surjective. Globally every morphism `P^1→A` is constant, so the map on global sections is zero. The connecting map injects `A(C)` into `H^1_et(C,A)`, and sends `a` to the torsor obtained by gluing the trivial torsor through translation by `a`. Its multiples correspond to `ma`, so its class is not torsion. The torsor is understood as an étale sheaf torsor, representable by an algebraic space; scheme representability is not needed or silently assumed. This diagnoses the singular extension, not the smooth theorem.

### Affine coefficients and derived exchange obligations

Raynaud's *Spécialisation du foncteur de Picard* 6.2.1, printed page 50, gives a precise finite locally free relative comparison with the Cartier dual under proper flat finite presentation and universal `f_*O=O`. It does not identify arbitrary absolute `H^1(X,G)` with a Hom group for every affine `G`. The dual of `G_a` also cannot be dismissed by looking only at field-valued polynomial characters: over `k[epsilon]/epsilon^2`, `x↦1+epsilon x` is a nontrivial character because the product is `1+epsilon(x+y)`. The computation is recorded exactly.

For a finite free constant lattice `gamma=Z^r`, `Hom(gamma,G)=G^r`; finite products commute with the cohomology construction, proving the narrow exchange claim. A general internal Hom or Ext exchange requires derived adjunction and vanishing of all competing edge terms. The usual local-to-global Ext spectral sequence can organize those obligations, but it cannot supply the missing site, coefficient object, or abutment. In particular the degree-two display on 043 actually denies a direct sum. A filtration with terms involving `Ext^1` and `Hom` does not split merely because those two terms have recognizable names. [Stacks0BQP; Deligne1974HodgeIII.]

### Cancelled divisor argument

At codimension one of a normal integral target, the local ring is a DVR. Properness lets the rational inverse of a birational morphism extend at such points. The closure of a divisor from the resulting open is consequently a Weil divisor. A Weil divisor need not be Cartier on a normal scheme; local factoriality is a sufficient additional hypothesis. This pinpoints one possible mathematical failure in the cancelled argument. The entire paragraph is struck out, and the intended theorem and full setup are absent. The review therefore does not assert that this was the author's reason for cancelling it, nor manufacture a completed Picard-invariance theorem from the discarded proof.

### Artin–Schreier–Witt and fundamental groups

For proper geometrically connected `A/k`, the beginning of the Artin–Schreier long exact sequence includes `k/(F−1)k`. It vanishes over algebraically closed `k`, but for `k=F_p` the map `F−1` is zero and the cokernel is `F_p`. The origin splits the arithmetic contribution. Consequently the equality with fixed points in `H^1(O_A)` needs the geometric convention. The stable part of a semilinear Frobenius module is a vector space over the ground field; its fixed points form an `F_p`-space. They are related under the appropriate separable closure and scalar extension, not identical by notation. The undefined `W_infinity` transitions on the leaf prevent certifying an unrestricted inverse-limit statement. [SGA7XXII §1; Stacks0A3J; Oda1969, introduction only.]

Milne 15.3 verifies the pointed geometric finite étale cover/isogeny statement. At `ell=p`, geometric points see only the étale part of the p-power torsion. An arithmetic fundamental group also has a Galois quotient. The adjacent literal statement that a positive-dimensional abelian variety has no torsion is impossible for any invertible prime: its geometric `ell`-torsion has `ell^{2g}` points. `NS(A)` is a plausible intended torsion-free object, but no ink decision has been made.

## 6. Reconstruction, finite systems, and primitive torsors

### Reconstruction from restriction and finite pairings

The one-variable proposition on 048 assumes an exact sequence `P′→P→P″`, divisibility `nQ=Q`, and surjectivity `P[n]→P″`. It seeks an equivalence between biextensions and a restricted biextension plus compatible finite pairing data, including a condition on morphisms. The cancelled proof and unfinished morphism clause are real limitations. The elementary consequence `nP″=0` follows by lifting locally to `P[n]`, and then `P″⊗Q=0` follows by divisibility. These facts are independently proved; they are not a proof of the categorical equivalence.

SGA7 VII's long exact biextension framework and VIII's Tor and finite pairing results are exact components of a possible proof. No exact printed theorem at the entire handwritten generality has been certified. The all-level corollary and the two-variable iteration are accordingly class C, with their asymmetric divisibility assumptions retained. This is a researched delimitation, not an unfinished literature search disguised as a theorem.

The warning against passing immediately to Tate modules has a complete elementary diagnostic. In ordinary abelian groups put

\[
P=\mathbf Z/\ell,\qquad Q=G=\mathbf Q_\ell/\mathbf Z_\ell.
\]

The transition on `P[ell^r]=P` is multiplication by `ell`, hence zero, so its inverse limit is zero. A free resolution of `P` gives `P⊗^LQ≃Q[ell][1]`, because multiplication by `ell` on `Q` is surjective. Thus `Biext^1(P,Q;G)=Hom(Q[ell],G)` is nonzero. Explicitly the compatible finite pairing is `phi_r(a,b/ell^r)=ab/ell` modulo `Z`. It is bilinear and nonzero. The finite-level inclusion and multiplication compatibilities hold, while the simultaneous Tate pairing is zero because its first factor is zero. The code verifies these formulas for several primes and levels; the resolution proves the general counterexample. This belongs to the earlier run's idea, rechecked here, not to a hidden manuscript calculation.

### Rigidity and primitive torsors

Three hypotheses must be distinguished. Vanishing of internal group-homomorphisms gives the edge isomorphism `Ext^1(A,G)=H^0(internal Ext^1(A,G))`, because the outer `H^1` and `H^2` terms vanish. Vanishing of **all pointed maps**, universally after base change, is stronger. It makes every map `A_T→G_T` constant by subtracting its value at zero, so `f_*G_A=G`. This universal hypothesis also controls products: view a pointed map on `A^r` in one variable at a time over the other factors, and apply the assumption after that base change.

The Leray five-term sequence has a potential transgression to `H^2(S,G)`. The zero section splits pullback on `H^2`, so that map is injective and the transgression vanishes. In degree one the section splits off `H^1(S,G)`, leaving the kernel of evaluation at zero as `H^0(S,R^1f_*G_A)`. This proves C-SA13-298, including the reason the splitting exists.

Now let `L` be a `G`-torsor on `A`, rigidified at zero, whose class is primitive. Primitivity provides a multiplication isomorphism between its two pullbacks and its pullback by addition. The discrepancy between two such isomorphisms is a map `A×A→G`; normalize along the axes using the chosen rigidification. Universal pointed-map rigidity makes the normalized discrepancy zero, proving uniqueness. The defects in associativity on `A^3`, symmetry on `A^2`, and the unit identities are normalized maps into `G`; they vanish by the same argument. The inverse is obtained from the multiplication and torsor property over `a+(-a)=0`, and its coherence is again forced. A normalized map of underlying torsors respects these unique operations. Automorphisms are normalized maps `A→G`, hence zero. This proves full faithfulness and the primitive essential image, not merely an injection of isomorphism classes.

Finally `L↦m^*L-pr_1^*L-pr_2^*L` has precisely those primitive classes as kernel. The quotient therefore injects into the relevant birigidified symmetric torsors. This formal kernel argument is established here, but calling the quotient the classical Néron–Severi sheaf requires a separate geometric identification. The following package's 054–058 was read to verify the direct continuation; its diagonal and doubling formulas are not silently used to repair doubtful labels on 052.

## 7. Reused Lie leaf and Čech leaves

For a nilpotent derivation `u` of an algebra over `Q`, induction gives the higher Leibniz rule

\[
u^n(xy)=\sum_{r+s=n}\binom nr u^r(x)u^s(y).
\]

Divide by `n!` and sum. Nilpotence makes the sums finite, and `binom(n,r)/n!=1/(r!s!)` gives multiplicativity of `exp(u)`. Multiplying the finite series for `exp(u)` and `exp(-u)` gives the identity. This is the proof printed on 038. The code checks factorial coefficients and all 81 products of matrix units for `u=ad(N)` on `M_3(Q)`, with `N` a three-step nilpotent Jordan block.

Characteristic zero matters even when a derivation is nilpotent. On `F_p[x]`, `D=d/dx` satisfies `D^p=0`. Its truncated exponential sends `x` to `x+1` but sends `x^p` to `x^p`; multiplicativity would require `x^p+1`. This is an exact counterexample, also computed for p=2,3,5. For the Lie assertions, if `x` lies in the nilradical, `ad(x)` sends the whole Lie algebra into that nilpotent ideal and is nilpotent on the ideal, hence on the whole algebra. Every power preserves every ideal, so the exponential does too. Under a surjection in finite-dimensional characteristic-zero Lie algebras, the radical maps onto the radical and a Levi factor maps to a semisimple complement, proving the quotient claim. The printed source remains unidentified; the Bourbaki reference is a candidate, not verified authorship.

On the Čech leaves, exact sheafification is left adjoint to the inclusion of abelian sheaves into presheaves. A right adjoint to an exact functor preserves injectives, which proves the written injectivity assertion. In an injective resolution, a positive-degree cocycle is locally a boundary because the complex is exact as a complex of sheaves. Hence the positive-degree cohomology presheaf has zero associated sheaf. Degree zero sheafifies back to the original sheaf. This supplies the actual comparison, not just a resemblance to a spectral sequence.

The typescript changes “right exact” to “left exact.” Finite limits defining the degree-zero Čech construction preserve kernels; a surjective map of presheaves need not give a surjection on every matching-family construction. Right derived functors consequently belong to the surviving left-exact formulation. The warning about the empty object remains substantive: a constant presheaf need not have zero value there, whereas a sheaf must satisfy the empty-cover convention. Stacks 21.10 was opened as a modern comparator. The exact `T.F. II §5, Theorem 10.2` edition was not identified, so no exact convention-equivalence beyond the printed conditional wording is certified.

## 8. A36: primitive classes and the Griffiths argument

For proper smooth `f:Y→S` with connected regular base and invertible ell, the edge map to `H^0(S,R^mf_*F)` detects restriction to a geometric fibre: the higher direct image is lisse, so vanishing at one point is vanishing everywhere. A primitive class lies in the first Leray filtration. Its quotient in `E_infinity^{1,m−1}` embeds into `E_2^{1,m−1}` because no differential can enter that column from a negative column. This constructs the canonical `u` without assuming degeneration of the entire Leray spectral sequence. A cohomological correspondence compatible with the shifts and twists induces a filtered morphism and therefore commutes with `u`. For the difference of two curve sections, its degree-zero fibre restriction vanishes; if the induced map on the lisse H1 vanishes on one fibre, functoriality kills its Leray class. These are the exact local mechanisms of C-SA13-325–327 and SGA7 XX §1.

Condition (a) asks for an algebraic correspondence acting as an inverse to the relevant Lefschetz map on its image. Hard Lefschetz supplies an inverse linear map, not an algebraic cycle producing it. The parenthesis about lifting with a polarization to characteristic zero therefore cannot be certified from hard Lefschetz. The Lefschetz standard conjecture remains separate; the opened 2025 Ancona–Cavicchi–Laterveer–Saccà paper proves special geometric cases, not the general assertion. No counterexample to the conjecture is claimed. [SGA7XX 3.3–3.4; AnconaEtAl2025.]

Under (a) and (b), write an algebraically trivial cycle on `Y` as a difference of fibres of a correspondence `Z` from a curve. Apply the chosen algebraic inverse to construct a correspondence `Z′` on `X`, and put `Z″=Z-i^*Z′`. Condition (b) and the inverse property make the H1 action of `Z″` zero. Taking fibre differences gives the claimed decomposition in rational Chow groups. Rational coefficients accommodate the denominators in the inverse correspondence. In a family the curve, points, and correspondence may need a finite extension and a nonempty base open; trace returns the rational relation to the original field. The suggestion that no shrinking is necessary is an additional problem, not part of this proof.

The precise Griffiths theorem in SGA7 XX 4.1 retains the pencil and Lefschetz hypotheses, the algebraic inverse, and the non-curve-level vanishing condition. XVIII 5.8.7 supplies the injectivity modulo the fixed part, including the finite-cover/open restriction used in the argument. The resulting lower bound for the rational Griffiths group is naturally a **quotient of a subgroup** mapping onto primitive algebraic cohomology. A noncanonical vector-space injection may be chosen from a dimension inequality; no canonical injection in that direction is constructed. This repairs the interpretation of the manuscript's “contains as a subspace” wording without changing it. [SGA7XVIII 5.8.4–5.8.7; SGA7XX 4.1, 5.1.]

For odd-degree vanishing cohomology a Picard–Lefschetz transformation is `1+N` with `N^2=0`; its r-th power is `1+rN`. Over `Q_ell`, a nonzero `N` stays nonzero for every positive integer r. Thus passing to an open subgroup does not remove the transvections needed in the irreducibility argument. In characteristic zero, high-degree sections have growing extreme Hodge pieces. These lie outside the two Hodge types of a twisted curve H1, and irreducibility excludes a nonzero curve-generated summand. This verifies the mechanism, while keeping the ambiguous indices and phrase on scan 016 in the reading table.

## 9. A36: the strong trace gap and its published afterlife

### Two different trace statements

The strong form of 7.1 requires every residue in `F_p` to occur as the trace of some group element on the **whole** Frobenius-fixed representation. The inserted version permits an invariant subspace depending on the element and the requested residue. The latter does not imply the former. For the trivial group on `F_p^{p−1}`, invariant subspaces have every dimension from 0 through p−1, hence every residue occurs as a subspace trace; the only whole-space trace is p−1. The group is a p-group, and for p=2 the space has dimension one. This also explains why the p-group and dimension consequences were struck out. The exact finite calculation is in `computations.py` and its captured output.

Chebotarev can realize a conjugacy class and its trace on a fixed representation. It cannot turn a trace on a chosen proper invariant subspace into the trace of that element on the whole space. Corollary 7.2's every-residue statement in every prescribed parameter open therefore still needs the strong theorem, exactly as the manuscript margin says. A specialization lemma embedding the special fixed representation into a generic one after an open subgroup does not fill this gap. The review neither declares the strong claim false nor calls it proved from a modern general framework. Its precise fate was searched and the closest primary results are recorded.

### A counterexample to the arbitrary-point specialization lemma

The additional hypothesis in Lemma 7.8 matters independently of the trace gap. The assertion on A36-02, scan 36:026, global line 111 allows **any point t of a normal scheme S**, with s a specialization of t. Its proof at line 118 reduces to t generic in a normal integral henselian S. That reduction is invalid: the closure of an arbitrary point in a normal scheme need not be normal. This yields an explicit counterexample to the assertion as transcribed (C-SA13-355), and a precise repaired statement (C-SA13-363).

Work over k = F3 and take the smooth, integral, normal affine scheme S = Spec k[x,x⁻¹,y]. On the free rank-one module put the p-linear endomorphism F(v) = x v³. Since x is a unit, the semilinear map is invertible on every geometric fibre. Let C be the closed curve y² = x(x−2)², let t be its generic point, and let s = (2,0). The polynomial defining C is irreducible: x is not a square in k(x), and multiplying by the square (x−2)² does not change its square class. Thus C is integral, and s belongs to its closure, so t specializes to s exactly as required. At s its quadratic tangent cone is y²−2(x−2)², with two distinct conjugate branches over F9. It is an ordinary nonsplit node.

The parametrization x = u², y = u(u²−2) identifies k(C) with k(u), since u = y/(x−2) in the function field. The generic fixed vectors satisfy v = x v³, hence are 0 and ±1/u. They are already rational over k(t). The generic fixed representation is therefore the trivial one-dimensional F3 representation; restriction to any open subgroup remains trivial. At s the equation becomes v = 2v³. Its nonzero solutions satisfy v² = 2 and lie in F9. Arithmetic Frobenius sends v to v³ = 2v = −v, so the special representation has image C2. No subrepresentation of the trivial generic line can have that image, after any finite extension of k(t). The exact F9 and rational-function checks are executed in `code/specialization_counterexample.py`, with output retained in `outputs/specialization_counterexample.txt`.

Normalizing C makes the two geometric branches above the node into a closed point with residue field F9. Its residue extension kills precisely the special C2 action. Thus normalization does not repair the original quantifier while preserving the given special representation. Normality of the **ambient** S is fully satisfied in this counterexample and is not the missing assumption.

There is a valid generic-point repair, proved here as a reviewer refinement. Suppose S is normal, integral and noetherian, t is its generic point, s is any point, and V is a finite-rank locally free module with a p-linear endomorphism. The fixed scheme F(v)−v=0 is affine and étale: its Jacobian in the vector coordinates is −I. Over the henselization of the local ring at s, the open-and-closed part meeting the closed fibre is finite étale. It is a subgroup: addition and scalar multiplication preserve it because their domains are finite over the local base and every nonempty finite component meets the closed fibre. Its special fibre is the full special fixed representation. Finite étale schemes over a henselian local ring are equivalent to those over its residue field. Choose the finite connected splitting torsor for this representation, and descend both the finite subgroup and that torsor to an elementary étale neighbourhood of s. This is possible because the data are finitely presented. The neighbourhood is normal; connected finite étale covers of a normal integral scheme remain connected over its generic point. Its generic point gives a finite separable extension of k(t), and the descended splitting torsor proves that the image is exactly the special image, rather than an unspecified larger group. The finite subgroup supplies the required subrepresentation. The same repair applies within a normal integral closure of t only if its special residue data remain the data originally requested.

The opened primary sources are Stacks Lemma 41.20.5 (tag 0BTN), Lemma 10.153.7 (tag 04GK), and Lemma 37.41.6 (tag 02LP); their precise conclusions supply the finite-part decomposition, residue-field equivalence, and elementary étale neighbourhood. This proof verifies the generic-point case used at the end of the manuscript proof. It does not validate the preceding arbitrary-point statement, and no ink correction is made. [SGA7XXII; Stacks0BTN; Stacks04GK; Stacks02LP.]

### Coherent trace, stable ranks, and the dimension check

SGA7 XXII's coherent trace congruence gives the alternating trace of the q-power Frobenius on coherent cohomology modulo p for a proper scheme over `F_q`. Over a finite field this is a linear operator. A p-linear operator over a general field has no ordinary basis-independent trace in that sense, so those two situations cannot be mixed. After a finite extension, the finitely many nonzero eigenvalues of the relevant finite-field operators become one; zeros remain zero. The traces then equal stable ranks and remain unchanged on further extensions.

For projective equidimensional Cohen–Macaulay `X` of dimension n, Serre duality and Serre vanishing give `H^i(X,O_X(-d))=0` for i<n and d sufficiently large. The exact sequence of an effective Cartier section then gives the isomorphisms in degrees at most n−2 and the injection in degree n−1. In the universal proper-section open, the resulting constant dimensions and cohomology/base-change theorem supply the locally free sheaves used in the argument. A Frobenius-stable subbundle may require further shrinking; it is not automatically a subbundle across every rank-jumping point.

The trace decomposition on 021 has a separate dimension alarm. If `dim X=n` and `dim Y=n−1`, the high-degree term paired by Lefschetz with `H^i(X)` has twist `q^{n−1−i}`. The paired contribution is therefore `Tr(F|H^iX)(1+q^{n−1−i})` for i≤n−2, together with an **ambient middle term** and a vanishing middle term. A formula with exponent 2n−1−i under those dimensions cannot be accepted without resolving the notation. Likewise a bound obtained by listing only the lower terms cannot silently drop the ambient middle contribution.

### Norm forms and arbitrary point counts

Let d≥n+1 and choose n+1 linearly independent elements of `F_(q^d)` over `F_q`. Their linear combination defines an injective map `F_q^{n+1}→F_(q^d)`. Its field norm is homogeneous of degree d and vanishes only at zero. Over an algebraic closure it is the product of the d conjugate linear forms. This proves the norm-form mechanism; original SGA7 XXI 1.2 was checked, and small exact F2 examples are computed.

The broad existence statement with an arbitrary number N of points also has an elementary proof, explicitly supplied here as a reviewer reconstruction. Enlarge the finite field until X has at least N rational points and at least one additional point on each positive-dimensional irreducible component outside a chosen set of N points. On the finite set X(k′), prescribe value zero at the selected points and value one elsewhere, after choosing trivializations of `O(d)` at those points. For all large d the evaluation map from homogeneous forms is surjective by the ideal-sheaf exact sequence and Serre vanishing. A resulting form contains exactly the prescribed rational points and no component, so its intersection has the expected dimension. Smoothness is not claimed. The field extension and degree threshold may depend on X and N.

This proof does not certify the manuscript's sharper numerical threshold or same-degree interpolation construction. Selecting N points from a finite set of size `r d_1^n` requires `N≤r d_1^n`, and the final rational zero set is the selected subset, not automatically all of that finite set. Both remain scan alarms. The later coprime-degree arithmetic is valid once the requisite geometric constructions exist: by choosing an appropriate residue class modulo a, every sufficiently large integer is `a u+b v` with u,v above any fixed lower bound. It supplies no geometric disjointness by itself.

### What was published, and what it does not settle

Original SGA7 XXI 1.4, printed pages 374–376, proves generic nonnilpotence of Frobenius on the coherent quotient for sufficiently high-degree sections of a projective equidimensional Cohen–Macaulay scheme. Its finite-field argument uses special sections with zero or one rational point and then openness of nonnilpotence. It is not the every-residue whole-space trace theorem. SGA7 XX 4.3 and XXI's introduction still distinguish the broad heuristic from the proved complete-intersection geometric conclusion; XXI 4.2 gives that conclusion with its own hypotheses.

Koblitz's 1975 paper strengthens the coherent side. In I §5, Theorem 2, printed page 141, the nilpotent rank of the quotient is bounded by a constant times `d^{n−1}`. Together with the leading term of its total rank, the stable rank grows without bound. His I §1 explicitly points back to Katz's earlier nonnilpotence theorem. The original Numdam PDF and the theorem's page were opened. This is a later mathematical development, not evidence that the cancelled trace argument was already complete. [Koblitz1975.]

A useful **reviewer inference** explains the relevance without overstating the source. For a fixed smooth X over a finite field, weak Lefschetz fixes the low-degree ambient factors of the zeta function as d varies; the high-degree factors are congruent to one modulo p after the relevant twists. Coherent cohomology fixes analogous ambient factors, leaving a determinant on the growing quotient. If all the variable middle ell-adic cohomology had positive correspondence coniveau, its eigenvalues would be divisible by q and its determinant would also be one modulo p. The coherent quotient's determinant would then equal a fixed rational function of bounded degree. Its degree is the stable rank, which Koblitz makes unbounded: a contradiction for large d. The fixed ambient factors are indispensable; mere nonnilpotence alone would not rule out cancellation against a fixed factor. This deduction gives a route to the desired nonconiveau conclusion in the stated finite-field setting. It does not prove the much stronger whole-space trace assertion, all its quantifiers after arbitrary spreading, or an undefined equivalence of motivic categories.

Finally, a correspondence quotient of `H^{n−3}(Z)(−1)` has q-divisible eigenvalues by integrality and the twist, so a unit eigenvalue excludes it. The converse divisibility-to-correspondence statement remains a distinct generalized Tate/coniveau question. Support and correspondence filtrations also require coefficient and properness conventions: Benoist–Ottem's opened original proves the rational proper smooth complex equality while exhibiting failures for other coefficient or open settings. The final crystalline heading on 027 states no theorem; no later comparison theorem is assigned to it by resemblance.
