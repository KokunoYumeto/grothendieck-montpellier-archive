## 6.5: recovery of the profinite group

With the usual forgetful fibre functor, the continuous rational representation category determines its affine proalgebraic Tannaka group. For the construction on 185–186 its maximal pro-étale quotient is the original profinite group: every component quotient of a finite-dimensional algebraic envelope is a finite continuous quotient of pi, and every finite quotient of pi is faithfully represented by its regular representation over Q_l. Taking all of them gives pi. Thus the assertion on 185 that even the tensor category cannot recover pi conflicts with the explicit component-quotient identity on 186 if the usual fibre functor is understood. Without a fibre functor, canonical neutral reconstruction needs a choice; that qualification does not negate the component-quotient identity. The manuscript's warning should be recorded as an unresolved distinction, not repeated as a correct theorem saying no recovery is possible.

For the analytic pro-Lie construction, every finite-dimensional continuous representation factors through its compact analytic image. Representations of a finite-dimensional Q_l-Lie algebra integrate on a sufficiently small open subgroup; morphisms commuting with the Lie action commute with a sufficiently small open subgroup by the local exponential. This explains the direct-limit representation equivalence in 6.4, with finite-dimensional and local hypotheses retained. Root has opened Lazard and the modern compact-linearity comparator; this audit does not claim to have reopened those sources independently.

## 6.6: a literal rational-points counterexample

The implication “all points of Gamma/Gamma^0 are rational, hence Gamma(Q_l) is Zariski dense” is false for arbitrary affine Gamma. Let K=Q_3 and L=K(sqrt(3)). Embed the norm-one torus T in SL_2 through multiplication on L and put Gamma=N_SL2(T). Its component quotient is the constant group C2. Multiplication by a in L has determinant N(a); conjugation on L has determinant −1. Therefore a rational point in the other normalizer component would be multiplication by a followed by conjugation, with N(a)=−1.

No such a exists. In this totally ramified quadratic extension the valuation of N(a) equals the normalized L-valuation of a. Norm −1 would make a a unit; its norm in the residue field F_3 is then a square, hence 1. The residue of −1 is 2. Thus Gamma(K)=T(K) and is not dense in the other component. This fully checks the proposed counterexample. In the actual construction Gamma is the Zariski closure of the given image of pi, so density follows from its definition and the intended application is unaffected.

The Lie criterion on 188 is sound when “algebraic” means that the embedded Lie subalgebra Lie(G_E) inside gl(E) is algebraic. It then equals the Lie algebra of the Zariski envelope, equivalently G_E is open in that envelope's Q_l-points. Conversely, an isomorphism of the natural strict pro-Lie systems forces equality at each stage: the transition map from a sufficiently large algebraic Lie algebra onto the chosen algebraic stage factors through the analytic Lie subalgebra, and is surjective. This is different from equality of the analytic groups G_E and all Q_l-points of their envelopes, which is much stronger.

## 6.15: analytic dimension one, algebraic dimension unbounded

Take X=Spec(Fbar_p), with l different from p. Its finite-field models are cofinal. Their fundamental groups and the transition maps are the open subgroups of Zhat. A compact analytic quotient of Zhat has an open pro-l subgroup whose procyclic image has Lie dimension at most one; projection to Z_l attains one. The open-index transition maps act on the Lie algebra Q_l by nonzero integer multiplication, hence by isomorphisms. The analytic pro-Lie algebra is therefore the constant one-dimensional Q_l, not an infinite-dimensional pro-Lie algebra. This refutes the literal “these dimensions are always infinite.” It does not refute positivity.

The algebraic dimension can nevertheless be arbitrarily large, which explains the proof actually written on 196. Given m, choose Q-linearly independent elements r_1,...,r_m in l^2 Z_l. The characters of Z_l given by x↦exp(r_i x) define a map to G_m^m. A character of this torus vanishes on the image only if an integer relation sum n_i r_i=0 holds, so the Zariski closure is the whole torus. Arbitrarily large m occur. Restriction to a finite-index subgroup replaces the r_i by a common nonzero integer multiple and preserves their independence. Thus the arithmetic algebraic pro-Lie algebra has quotients of unbounded dimension, whereas its analytic counterpart in this example has dimension one.

## 6.16: fate of the Tate endomorphism assertion

For a finitely generated field K over its prime field and l different from char(K), the integral Hom theorem is established: Tate over finite fields (1966); Zarhin in characteristic greater than two (1975 and 1976 papers, with a 1974 received date for the 1975 article); Faltings in characteristic zero (1983 with 1984 correction/complements); Mori's characteristic-two finiteness argument as published in Moret-Bailly, Astérisque129 (1985). The original Moret-Bailly text, XII2.4, p244, explicitly calls Mori's contribution previously unpublished; no guessed first-circulation date is assigned.

The exact primary positive-characteristic statement is Moret-Bailly XII2.5, pp244–245, under the function-field conventions of XII0.1, p239. It gives the integral Hom isomorphism and rational semisimplicity with k finite and l≠p. The whole original Numdam PDF is `work/p3_primary/moretbailly1985.pdf`; PDF pages247–248 give printed244–245. Zarhin's author preprint arXiv:1012.5083, p2 and bibliography p10, independently records the characteristic split and references. These are distinct from that paper's own theorems over geometric fields, which carry additional hypotheses.

For abelian schemes over a noetherian normal integral base, extension of generic-fibre homomorphisms is stated precisely in Achter–Casalaina-Martin–Vial2023, Proposition4.5(ii), citing Faltings–ChaiI.2.7. Gajda–Petersen, arXiv:2207.09345, Lemma3.4 gives the equality of Hom over the base and generic point and includes the limit argument; their Theorem2.3 states the Tate theorem over every finitely generated field. These exact passages were opened and read. Consequently, after descent of A and its endomorphisms to finite-type normal models, the manuscript's End(A)⊗Q_l=End_Pi(V_l A) follows by applying the finite-field/number-field/function-field theorem at a stage and passing to the direct-limit representation category. The normality and finite-presentation descent inputs should be explicit. No assertion is made for l equal to the characteristic of the generic field.

For X=Spec of an algebraically closed field this remains an arithmetic pro-group assertion. Replacing Pi by the ordinary geometric fundamental group, which is trivial, would make the right-hand side every linear endomorphism and would generally be false. This is exactly why the manuscript retains the system of arithmetic models.

At 199, centralizers of a subgroup and its Zariski closure agree because the equations defining a centralizer are algebraic. To identify them with the Lie centralizers when the ordinary fundamental group of X is trivial, pass far enough in the model system to kill the finite component quotient of the particular representation. This is allowed by descent of finite étale covers. The algebraic monodromy group at that later stage is connected, so its representation invariants equal Lie invariants in characteristic zero. It is not necessarily valid at an arbitrary initial model. The algebraic envelope of the analytic Lie algebra then has the same centralizer as that Lie algebra; this does not assert the two Lie algebras have equal dimensions.
