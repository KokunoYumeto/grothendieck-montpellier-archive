# SA27 — Ranked tasks to cut from the consolidation

These are future tasks. The counterexamples and proof checks already recorded in the dossier discharge its verification work; they are not proposed for repetition. No new direction is developed here. “One run” means a focused session of a few hours. The ranking weighs mathematical interest, the effect on a reliable companion, and the chance of obtaining a concrete result in that time. An unresolved identification is not automatically a currently open mathematical problem.

Source problem IDs can refer to the authorial problem register or to the separately labelled historical/editorial and modern-audit supplements in `research/`. This is deliberate: an editorial task can arise from an earlier run's question, while its associated manuscript assertion remains separately identified. The source lists do not attribute every proposed task to Grothendieck.

## 1. Recover the localizer and class definitions in 118

**Sources:** SA27-418–SA27-425, SA27-451–SA27-462; SA27-C433–SA27-C440, SA27-C442–SA27-C453; MP-SA27-206–MP-SA27-213. The modern audit counterexample SA27-C456 is in `research/modern_refinements.json`.

**What the task would establish:** a theorem-by-theorem account of which class inclusions use W∞, which survive a general fundamental localizer, and which depend on undecided notation. The W0 two-object-groupoid example already rules out an unrestricted smooth-and-proper-to-Serre implication. Individual transport existence must also be separated from composition-stable (op)fibration data.

**Known boundary:** SGA1 VI Def6.1 and §§7–10 give the classical distinctions. Maltsiniotis gives exact proper/smooth results under his definitions. Rezk supplies the ordinary sharp-map comparison. Neither shared terminology nor a diagram edge proves that all their quantifiers agree.

**First concrete step:** inspect the scans for the symbols W, S, T, F, C, WSC, WSF, WSB and the negative λ annotation; record a definition table with its dependencies. Then choose one uncertain implication and test only that exact formulation. A successful first run should leave a small list of actual mathematical questions after the reading and scope issues have been removed.

## 2. Type the linearization and Ext questions

**Sources:** SA27-32–SA27-35, SA27-37–SA27-42, SA27-44; SA27-C026–SA27-C034; MP-SA27-23–MP-SA27-31.

**What the task would establish:** the precise source, target, coefficient category, weak maps and boundedness of the proposed equivalence and Ext comparison. This would separate an application of existing derived-category results from any remaining stronger statement about all coefficients.

**Known boundary:** the opened EGA and Tôhoku results specify convergence, exactness and acyclicity hypotheses; they do not identify the manuscript's B. The Δ case marked positively is not a proof of every A/B comparison. Modern locally constant models are useful only after their weak-equivalence notions are matched.

**First concrete step:** make a typed version of112/003 with every symbol assigned a domain and codomain, using the image to resolve the B, q and superscript doubts. Fix one bounded coefficient setting and compare its positive Ext groups with the exact opened theorem. Defer the removal of smoothness until that first comparison is settled.

## 5. The Isbell envelope and its different subcategories

**Sources:** SA27-113–SA27-117, SA27-125–SA27-126, SA27-130, SA27-133–SA27-136, SA27-147; SA27-C090, SA27-C094–SA27-C096, SA27-C103–SA27-C111; MP-SA27-67–MP-SA27-69, MP-SA27-73–MP-SA27-76.

**What the task would establish:** a single variance-correct account of the triple envelope, its two fully faithful sides, the reflexive intersection, and the separate Cauchy-completion construction. It should identify which later E(A) diagram has a defensible interpretation.

**Known boundary:** Garner and Avery–Leinster supply exact modern comparisons. The dossier already refutes representability of every double-conjugacy fixed point, universal weakness of the units, and conversion of all weak equivalences to isomorphisms. Those examples need not be recomputed.

**First concrete step:** resolve the incoming/outgoing Hom order and the later vee/opposite markings on the scans. Draw the resulting functors with typed domains, then match each node to the opened definition or proposition. Restrict any new full-faithfulness question to a precise density or codensity hypothesis; do not use the vague word “generating” as a substitute.

## 6. The strong-asphericity converse for gluing topoi

**Sources:** SA27-205–SA27-214, SA27-146; SA27-C201–SA27-C210, SA27-C405; MP-SA27-101–MP-SA27-102.

**What the task would establish:** whether the questioned converse is true for one explicitly chosen meaning of strong asphericity and one class of gluing functors. All-coefficient cohomology, local-system equivalence and shape equivalence must first be treated as separate predicates.

**Known boundary:** the triple construction, cohomology comparison, local-constancy description and presheaf gluing criterion are checked. SGA4 IV9.5.9–10 provides the classical framework. Dropping left exactness of the adjoint changes the question.

**First concrete step:** formulate the converse for presheaf topoi through a crossing bifunctor and write both stratum inclusions explicitly. Compute one finite example for the chosen coefficient predicate. The goal is a sharp restricted converse or a counterexample, not an assertion that all three notions of equivalence agree.

## 8. Additive coefficient hypotheses

**Sources:** SA27-48–SA27-59, SA27-63–SA27-70, SA27-138–SA27-139, SA27-233–SA27-234; SA27-C038–SA27-C056, SA27-C113–SA27-C114, SA27-C236, SA27-C247; MP-SA27-34–MP-SA27-39, MP-SA27-78, MP-SA27-113.

**What the task would establish:** a useful corrected theorem package for the additive companion: recognition by small projectives, completed tensor universal properties, exact ordinary Kan extensions in a specified class, and the range in which projective or flat resolutions give balanced derived pairings.

**Known boundary:** the noncompact-projective-generator issue, finite-rank obstruction, coinvariant counterexample and unbounded flat-complex obstruction are already recorded. A valid bounded theorem does not settle arbitrary unbounded coefficient categories.

Apply the pointwise Kan formula to characterize one manageable class of indexing functors for which underived colimits are exact. Extend to unbounded complexes only after choosing an appropriate resolution theory.

## 10. Common targets and infinite synchronization

**Sources:** SA27-225, SA27-437–SA27-438, SA27-475; SA27-C221, SA27-C427–SA27-C429; MP-SA27-109, MP-SA27-216–MP-SA27-217.

**What the task would establish:** sufficient hypotheses for representing a family of localized maps and equalities with the particular common weak target and strict homotopy conventions desired on the pages.

**Known boundary:** arbitrary products of localizations of arbitrary relative categories fail. The infinite fence proves a genuine uniformity obstruction. Arbitrary coproducts of Cat→Hot(W), for a modern fundamental localizer, are nevertheless settled by the accessible-localizer finite-witness reduction. This does not automatically validate the manuscript's target-only roof construction.

**First concrete step:** specify the handedness of roofs and choose a relative category with a calculable fraction system. Formulate one uniform representative condition and check whether a model replacement supplies it without changing the source objects. Keep the naive homotopy quotient, ordinary localization and an enhanced localization distinct.

## 11. Filtrations beyond minimum stages

**Sources:** SA27-406, SA27-416, SA27-443; SA27-C406, SA27-C416–SA27-C417; MP-SA27-223.

**What the task would establish:** how far the corrected filtration theorem extends when minimum stages or arbitrary nonempty infima are replaced by weaker conditions on the indexing categories.

**Known boundary:** the stated sieve/cosieve theorem is repaired here using Grothendieck constructions. Filtration preservation yields a lax comparison, and connectedness alone does not give the aspherical corollary. Removing the sieve/cosieve or minimum-stage assumptions changes the theorem.

**First concrete step:** replace the minimum-stage section by the comma categories of the projection ∫X_i→X. Formulate one exact asphericity condition on these fibres and compare it with the ordinary union and intersection conditions. Test a small poset lacking a minimum before seeking a general statement. Do not repeat the already verified lax-triangle example.

## 14. Geometric and categorical base change

**Sources:** SA27-45, SA27-216, SA27-235–SA27-236, SA27-420–SA27-421; SA27-C211, SA27-C237–SA27-C239, SA27-C434–SA27-C437; MP-SA27-32, MP-SA27-103, MP-SA27-114, MP-SA27-210–MP-SA27-211.

**What the task would establish:** which fibre/comma and base-change statements are formal mate identities, which require a lifting property, and which depend on the coefficient universe. It would also separate the intended historical meaning of fibrant/cofibrant from modern model terminology.

**Known boundary:** the formal transposition is checked; it does not prove invertibility of a base-change map. SGA4's proper and smooth geometric theorems have explicit torsion and geometric hypotheses, while the categorical theorems have their own asphericity assumptions.

**First concrete step:** draw the two historical squares with typed maps and a chosen variance convention. Identify one exact coefficient category and compare its derived base-change morphism with the opened theorem. Only then test either bracketed alternative or a converse; similar names are not enough to transfer a theorem.
