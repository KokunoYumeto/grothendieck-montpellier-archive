# SA23 — Alexander Grothendieck's Montpellier papers

## Scope, result, and how to read the dossier

**Completed assessment as of 19 November 2026.** This dossier consolidates the 326 indexed Pro-note entries and the mathematical material in all 45 supplied transcriptions. Each indexed entry was compared with its cited passage before receiving a verdict. The additional Codex notes and API texts contribute source subjects, claims, problems and reading alarms, but no fictitious indexed-entry verdicts. The files record what the transcriptions say, what opened publications establish, and what this run reconstructs as three separate kinds of evidence.

The collection contains several lines of work, rather than a single unfinished book. Its order runs from tensor norms and Riemann–Roch through local algebra, differential and infinitesimal constructions, hyperplane sections, representability, categorical foundations, topoi, p-adic comparison, and the later work on orientations, polyhedra and graphs. The detailed sections retain the order of the arguments within each dossier. In particular, the categorical and homotopical sheets are read through their own definitions and questions; no general connection to *La Longue Marche* or *Les Dérivateurs* is assumed.

The main result is a change in the granularity of the evidence. Some doubts have exact answers in Grothendieck's published work; others belong to named later authors. Several purported open questions are completed on a subsequent leaf, and several attractive blanket statements fail in examples satisfying their actual hypotheses. A smaller set remains only partially identified because a definition, quantifier, coefficient convention or comparison map is missing. These are differentiated in the claim and problem registers. No assertion of novelty follows from an unsuccessful search.

The required registers are the authoritative detailed deliverables. `SA23_items.json` connects note entries and source subjects to passages; `SA23_claims.json` gives the statements, page status, computations and searched fate; `SA23_problems.json` separates the strongest source-supported question from a plausible modern formulation. `SA23_editorial_tasks.json` keeps translator requests and present-run repairs outside the count of the author's questions. The narrative explains the main findings and their consequences. Bracketed claim, problem and bibliography identifiers refer to those registers and `SA23_references.bib`. Exact theorem numbers, page numbers, access limits and date distinctions are recorded there and in the corresponding individual reference annotations, primary-source check records and `search_log.jsonl` files.

The folders also contain letters and printed material by other authors. Their questions remain attributed to their named writers, including Deligne and Serre; “authorial” does not silently make them Grothendieck’s words. The recovered 1966 hard-Lefschetz proof request is Deligne’s, and the precise later theorem is his 1980 result. The register keeps the historical abbreviated request separate from the smooth-projective rational l-adic hypotheses supplied by that publication.

## Main mathematical and historical findings

### Tensor norms and Riemann–Roch

The functional-analysis sequence contains an extensive route from exterior powers and determinant inequalities to Schatten estimates and eigenvalue continuity. Exact counterparts occur in Grothendieck's 1955 and 1956 publications. The current analysis separates the older trace-class meaning of *opérateur de Fredholm* from the modern Fredholm property. It also separates uniform convexity from the weak-operator Kadec–Klee property, and a matching topology on eigenvalue lists from an unspecified ordering of them. Several proposed strengthenings fail: the unrestricted power-monotonicity conjecture has a certified positive 2-by-2 matrix counterexample, and the proposed fixed-fraction singular-value estimate fails on weighted cyclic matrices. The valid inequalities and their parameter restrictions are retained. [C-SA23-analysis_curves-031, C-SA23-analysis_curves-032, C-SA23-analysis_curves-033, C-SA23-analysis_curves-034, C-SA23-analysis_curves-035, C-SA23-analysis_curves-036, C-SA23-analysis_curves-037, C-SA23-analysis_curves-038, C-SA23-analysis_curves-076, C-SA23-analysis_curves-077, C-SA23-analysis_curves-078, C-SA23-analysis_curves-079, C-SA23-analysis_curves-080, C-SA23-analysis_curves-081, C-SA23-analysis_curves-082, C-SA23-analysis_curves-083; AC_GrothPTT1955, II pp.13,20–21; AC_GrothFredholm1956; AC_Dodds1995, Theorem2.7 and Corollary2.8; AC_Ricard2018, Introduction and Theorem3.2.]

The Riemann–Roch leaves distinguish a universal Chern ring with a **chosen geometric family** from later constructions imposing conditions on all vector bundles. That choice changes the universal object and its integral torsion. The projective-space example and the two different factorials in the Chern computation have been checked integrally; rational Chern characters alone would not prove the torsion conclusion. The nonzero 2-torsion example survives after discarding the cancelled effective-hypersurface setup. The supported theory has established rational descendants, including localized Chern characters and singular Riemann–Roch, while the exact integral nonunital filtration remains a separate formulation problem. The final surface formula also distinguishes the structure sheaf of a singular curve from the pushforward of its normalization. [C-SA23-root-011, C-SA23-root-014, C-SA23-root-015, -025–038, -042–058, -071 and -071R; GrothendieckRRR1957; SGA6V; Mackall2020; FultonGillet1983.]

Cotes 22 and 23 repeatedly turn on the difference between an algebraic condition and its topological or finite-presentation counterpart. Adic formal smoothness does not mean discrete formal smoothness; finite-presentation openness theorems do not settle arbitrary noetherian maps. The characteristic-two differential-kernel examples and the ordinary differential module of a power-series ring have been checked under their exact hypotheses. ## What the earlier notes got right and wrong

The earlier notes are often useful at locating a genuine doubt, a missing proof or a consequential sign. Their main recurring weakness is loss of scope: dropping an inserted nilpotence condition, replacing the chosen geometric family by all geometric elements, interpreting a cancelled draft as live, or using a nearby modern theorem with different quantifiers. Imported project commentary sometimes adds an attractive bridge without a supplied endpoint. Such links remain explicitly attributed leads in `SA23_project_leads.tsv`; they are not counted as verified results of the other projects.

This consolidation also corrects its own hypotheses where the source comparison demands it. The finite checks are accompanied by general arguments where a universal assertion is used. A numerical matrix result is not advertised as a theorem for all ranks. Conversely, a deep published theorem carried as background is not labelled an independent re-proof. `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv` records those different actions, including the refined target of a refutation where a separate refinement record is present.

## Register conventions and the present state of the topic

The six literature classes have the requested meanings: **A**, a counterpart in Grothendieck's own published work; **B**, developed by others into established mathematics; **C**, an exact partial development with the remaining scope stated; **D**, not developed after recorded searches; **E**, known before the relevant work; **F**, not sufficiently identifiable from the surviving text. A class is not a truth value. If a literal formula is false and a repaired theorem has a published counterpart, the page status remains false and the fate explains which repaired statement the publication proves. For undated sheets, classA records an own-publication counterpart without asserting an unproved temporal priority. The bibliography's `note = {verified}` certifies opening the recorded link; its annotation specifies whether metadata, a theorem statement, a proof or an entire relevant passage was checked. Unopened originals remain `unverified` even when an opened later paper cites them.

An exact authored or jointly authored publication supports class A; editorship of a seminar volume alone does not. The final authorship audit therefore distinguishes Grothendieck’s SGA6 Exposé0 from Berthelot’s ExposésV andVII, and Grothendieck–Verdier’s SGA4 ExposéIV from chapters written by other participants. The Borel–Serre account retains Grothendieck’s mathematical attribution while carrying its actual publication authorship. A class B match to a checked later treatment does not claim that its author first discovered the result; unverified first-circulation and priority dates remain explicit gaps.

Every final claim has an explicit search-log route. The searches include the requested source families, exact-text and hypothesis searches, author bibliographies and backward references; access failures and absent exact matches are recorded. `SA23_fate_coverage.tsv` exposes the route from each canonical claim to those records. The problem index records an opened-source state of the art even for a question now solved, an authorial request deferred in the text, or a formulation too incomplete to identify. Translator requests and present-run repairs have their own separate register. When a source gives no reason why work stopped, `why_open` says so. An inference about a mathematical obstruction is not represented as a historical explanation.

Final identifiers are stable for items and claims. Cross-folder consolidations preserve every original witness in `source_records` and record their reasons in `integration/consolidation_plan.json`. The problem index is renumbered consecutively as `MP-SA23-001`, and so on; `SA23_id_aliases.json` resolves every former owner-qualified problem identifier and every merged subject or claim. Owner files retain their earlier identifiers for auditability. The final counts below, rather than intermediate local counts, describe the integrated dossier.

The claim-class and page-status counts use the canonical assessment of each final record. They do not erase the distinct drafting states retained in its source witnesses. Separate refinement records are labelled as this run's work and counted separately by origin. A source-provenance audit also distinguishes an authorial question or explicit unfinished step from a reader's request to check an asserted theorem. Answered questions and withdrawn unfinished proposals remain in the problem index; verification, translation and attribution tasks without an authorial question are kept in the editorial register.

The ranked directions are bounded specifications for later work. They state the originating item, claim and problem IDs, the exact prospective result, the established input and a first concrete step. Interest and plausible progress in one several-hour run are scored separately. Some high-value tasks are editorial concordances or hypothesis repairs; others concern a residual mathematical construction. None is developed into a separate research project in this dossier.

Follow-ups whose provenance is entirely a reader’s verification or a documentary identification are preserved separately in `SA23_editorial_directions.md` and its JSON companion. They are not assigned an invented source problem merely to enter the Stage6 ranking. A mixed direction can cite both a genuine authorial question and explicitly labelled editorial tasks.


## Cote 1: tensor norms, trace ideals and analytic spectral theory

Cote1 contains a sustained functional-analytic working sequence, beginning with isolated fragments on C*-algebra multipliers, rearrangements and traces, and continuing through Hilbert tensor products, Hilbert–Schmidt and trace-class operators, exterior powers, determinant inequalities, symmetric gauges, entire functions and Schatten convergence. ### Questions and asserted formulas with definite answers

The most consequential literal failure is the proposed monotonicity of

\[
F_p(r)=\operatorname{Tr}|H^r-K^r|^{p/r}
\]

for arbitrary p>0. It is stronger than the operator-norm Hölder bound on the adjacent lines. For H=[[2,1],[1,5]], K=diag(1,4), and p=1/10, both matrices are strictly positive and

\[
F_p(1/2)=1.3201298807\ldots>1.0717734625\ldots=F_p(1).
\]

The conclusion does not rest on decimals. The program certifies rational lower bounds for the two eigenvalues of sqrt(H)−sqrt(K), proving F_p(1/2)>1.29>1.1>F_p(1). Squaring H,K and halving p gives a failure with both power parameters strictly below1. The surviving published constant-one inequality gives F_p(s)≤F_p(r) when0<s<r and p≥s; thus it covers the interval0<r≤1 if p≥1. Ricard’s theorem gives all-p Hölder continuity with a constant, and cannot be used to erase that restriction. [C077–080; MP-SA23-013; computation `fractional_power_monotonicity_counterexample`; AC_Ricard2018, introduction and Theorem3.2.]

The weak-convergence question has a broader positive answer than the uniform-convexity route suggested on the leaves. Dodds and coauthors prove weak-operator Kadec–Klee for every Schatten exponent0<p<∞: weak-operator convergence together with convergence of the p-quasi-norm implies convergence in S_p. Uniform convexity is a different assertion, valid for1<p<∞. S1 has flat unit-sphere segments, and the infinite-dimensional p<1 spaces are not locally convex. These separate answers prevent the open margin from being left artificially unresolved. [C076–078; MP-SA23-012; AC_Dodds1995, Theorem2.7, Corollary2.8 and following paragraph; AC_FackKosaki1986, Theorems5.2–5.3.]

The request for a pointwise eigenvalue bound by a fixed fraction of the singular-value index also has a negative answer. Weighted cyclic matrices can have all eigenvalue moduli1 while every singular value except the first is arbitrarily small. The valid Weyl bound is the geometric mean of the first n singular values. A separately constructed compact trace-class sum of2×2 blocks has |λ_(2k)|/s_(2k)=k+1, proving the asserted unbounded same-index ratios in one operator. Tam’s checked proof of the Weyl–Horn theorem explains the finite feasible data; the explicit constructions answer the actual margin. [C034, C082–083; MP-SA23-014; AC_Tam2010, Theorem1.1.]

Several nearby proposed strengthenings fail in equally small examples. Orthogonal rank-one projections refute the aligned concave-power sum, while the separate-sums version remains valid. Symmetry cannot be deleted from the finite log-convex comparisons, even where equality of total products permits dropping monotonicity. A pair of rational rank-one2×2 maps refutes the one-sided mixed determinant estimate; the correct geometric-mean estimate includes both domain and range Gram factors and is attained by that example. Finally, the normal Fredholm adjugate has alternating signs. For diag(2,1), the proposed descending positive summands already occur in the wrong order, while the correct exclusion-of-one-eigenvalue formula proves the desired positivity and domination. The next unit distinguishes vector-bundle K-theory from coherent G-theory on a noetherian curve. ### Torsion with trivial total Chern class

The counterexample unit contains a reliable nonvanishing argument: projection after the constant-section pushforward is the identity. Its integral Chern calculation has two different factorials. For `ξ=1−[O(−1)]` on projective space, the leading term is

`c(ξ^r)=1+(−1)^(r−1)(r−1)! h^r`.

For a rank-zero line difference on a smooth curve, with first class `α`, the section pushforward instead has

`c(i_!x)=1+(−1)^r r! α h^r`.

The exact program checks finite coefficients; `proofs.md` R1 proves the identities universally over the integers before specializing to torsion. This avoids using a rational Chern character to prove an integral torsion assertion. Grothendieck’s 1957 report, reprinted in SGA 6, contains the Chern-ring formulas needed for the comparison (C-SA23-root-011).

The live example is a nontrivial 2-torsion line class on an elliptic curve, pushed into its product with P3. The effective-hypersurface setup above it is cancelled. The earlier imported register had retained that abandoned description. The example survives, but its attribution and hypotheses need correction. The earlier notes’ improvement to P2 is also valid: the exact rank-zero coefficient is already even there. Neither an effective torsion divisor on a proper curve nor a claim that P3 is the minimal possible dimension should be attributed to the surviving page (SA23-14, SA23-15, SA23-16, SA23-17, SA23-18).

Two smaller distinctions matter. A fixed factorial kills only torsion of appropriate order; the bare existence of torsion does not establish the page’s unrestricted fixed-dimension inference. Also, additive zero in the augmented Chern ring is `(0,1)`. A written zero need not mean that the total Chern series itself is the zero series. These are mathematical convention checks, not permission to change the transcript.

### 1. Differential criteria and formal simplicity

The beginning of cote22 moves from differential criteria for regular local rings to completion, separability and analytic reducedness. Jouanolou’s differential module is the universal Kähler module, but his quasi-separable terminology and residual hypotheses must be read in their historical formulation. Several annotations have real mathematical content: the residue-field inclusion has a direction, a quotient differential module must actually be a module over the quotient ring, and a finite algebra need not contain its base ring injectively. The annotation “faux” near a localization argument cannot be reassigned to a different sentence merely because that produces an easier objection. Claims001–013 retain these distinctions; MP-SA23-029 and MP-SA23-030 are the actual marginal questions, while ED-SA23-ega-01 and ED-SA23-ega-03 preserve the separate reader checks.

The regular group-scheme application raises a scope question. Modern finite-type smooth group schemes over a perfect field have the expected regularity properties, but this does not certify every unrestricted Hopf-algebra formulation suggested by a margin. Similarly, the source’s credit to Nagata is evidence of acknowledged prior knowledge, while the exact general normalization application has not been reconstructed from an original Nagata proof. Jouanolou’s alleged gap in Lang’s C1 argument is a specific historical verification question: it concerns separability of the fraction field of a completion. The searches identify Lang’s1952 paper and a later Nagata improvement concerning normic forms, but do not identify that improvement with the particular analytic-completion gap. These are bounded limitations in the claim map, not declarations of modern open C1 problems. [Lang1952; Nagata1962; Stacks0BF6]

The subsequent formal-simplicity and Cohen-algebra draft is organized around lifting, associated graded modules, completeness and residue-field extensions. Its literal comparison theorem4.6(ii) repeats hypotheses on the source algebra where the comparison evidently needs information on the target. The example
\[
A=B=\mathbf Q[e]/(e^2),\qquad C=\mathbf Q
\]
meets the literal source conditions and gives an isomorphism modulo the nilpotent ideal, but its quotient map is not an isomorphism. This is a mathematical reading alarm; replacing every B by C would still require an ink check and a complete theorem-level hypothesis audit. The modern local Cohen-factorization theorems are more precise than the draft’s broad nonlocal questions. [C-SA23-ega-017, C-SA23-ega-018, C-SA23-ega-019, C-SA23-ega-020; Stacks07NN; Stacks09Q9]

The most productive distinction in these pages is **adic formal smoothness versus discrete formal smoothness**. The power-series examples are not minor changes of notation. In characteristic zero, the ordinary differential module of a positive-dimensional power-series ring has a finite-dimensional residue quotient but a larger generic rank, detected by the transcendence of the formal exponential. It cannot be projective. When the coefficient field has infinitely many independent differentials, the manuscript’s unfinished finite-support argument can also be completed: a derivation into a direct sum cannot accommodate the infinitely many coefficient directions of one power series. The proof notes make both arguments explicit and distinguish the manuscript’s nonsplitting proposition from the earlier run’s stronger nonprojectivity idea. Finite truncations alone would not prove either result. [SA23-140, SA23-141, SA23-142; C-SA23-ega-021, C-SA23-ega-022, C-SA23-ega-023; MP-SA23-032, MP-SA23-035; Stacks00TH; Stacks0593]

The questions about the openness of geometric regularity and flatness require an equally careful scope distinction. The finite-presentation flat-locus theorem does not settle an arbitrary noetherian ring map. The Frobenius criterion connects a bad regular locus to a bad flat locus, and the literature search traces the example route through Brodmann–Rotthaus, Nishimura and the explicit modern MathOverflow application. ### 2. Differential kernels, adjunction and infinitesimal groups

The characteristic-two kernel examples in cote22 are stronger than the assertion that an inseparable extension kills a differential. They show that the kernel of the map on absolute differentials need not descend to the smaller field even when $k\cap K^p=k^p$. The rational-function and finite-coefficient variants have been checked with all their field hypotheses. A separate original-print comparison found a real defect in EGAIV,0.22.5.10(i): the displayed larger field does not adjoin the independent variable Z. Read literally, its stated generator is transcendental and cannot produce the advertised differential relation. Adjoining Z repairs the example; that repair is present reconstruction, not a change to the manuscript or printed source. Conversely, several map-label and Hom-variance errors alleged by the earlier run are absent from the original EGA print. The dossier therefore rejects a blanket claim that the comparison edition’s errors were EGA’s errors. [SA23-143, SA23-144, SA23-145; C-SA23-ega-026; ED-SA23-ega-10; EGAIV1]

The projective-bundle leaves in cote23 compute canonical bundles by Euler and conormal sequences. Their relative base-line factors matter: a degree-sum condition can make the canonical line a pullback from the base without making it globally trivial. The elliptic calculation gives equation weight6 and is independent of the discriminant mistake on the next leaf. The source and coordinate-change characters have been compared directly with Busé–Jouanolou’s propositions. The logarithm/exponential discussion contains two distinct literal failures. For commuting elements near1, the logarithm of a product is the **sum** of logarithms. In a Hopf algebra, the exponential of a nilpotent primitive is **group-like**; subtracting1 does not generally produce another primitive. The saved certificate over $\mathbf Q[t]/(t^3)$ shows the nonzero reduced coproduct directly. These corrections should not be collapsed into one vague “sign problem.” [C-SA23-ega-032, C-SA23-ega-033; ED-SA23-ega-14]

The shortened Cartier checklist also omits an essential connectedness condition. ### 3. Deligne’s corrections and their published fate

The Deligne letters form a sustained examination of definitions and proofs, especially outside the noetherian setting. A successive regular sequence need not be permutable in a nonnoetherian local ring. The idealization example in the proof notes checks this directly. Koszul acyclicity is permutation-invariant and is the right condition for the later geometric definition. SGA6VII explicitly replaces the earlier EGA formulation and records positive-degree acyclicity; a transcription saying that the complex is zero in degree0 is consequently a decisive alarm. [C-SA23-ega-049; MP-SA23-041; SGA6VII]

Several apparent refutations become more informative when compared with the final publication. Deligne’s disjoint-union map is not an immersion, while the published EGAIV16.1.5–6 assumes an immersion. It does not refute that final statement. His separate question about kernels of finitely presented algebras does have a negative answer: an idealization followed by localization gives a finitely presented algebra with an infinitely generated kernel from the base. Likewise, arbitrary A-subquotients of a B-module need not be adically separated, even when the finite B-module version has the usual noetherian separation theorem. The letters’ changes of position and their December resolution are retained. [C-SA23-ega-050, C-SA23-ega-051; MP-SA23-042; EGAIV4]

The omission of separation in the implication b⇒c of EGAIV19.5.5 is an exact success story. The final theorem gives a⇒b⇒c without separation and reserves the stronger separation hypothesis for the reverse implication. The footnote on printedp207 explicitly credits Deligne and says that his proof is reproduced. This is a dated manuscript-to-publication connection, not an unresolved theorem left behind by the correspondence. [C-SA23-ega-052; ED-SA23-ega-25-context; EGAIV4]

The most decisive editorial match concerns **EGA0.19.3.12**. Deligne objects that solution sets of multiplicative lifting equations are not linear varieties. The original printed proof makes exactly that mistake. EGAIV4’s1967 errata then expressly **withdraw the proposition** for this reason. The present polynomial-system example demonstrates the failure of the compactness inference; it does not pretend to be a counterexample satisfying every hypothesis of the withdrawn proposition. The published withdrawal is the established historical fate. [C-SA23-ega-053; ED-SA23-ega-26; EGAIV1; EGAIV4, Liste3 IVno.8]

The relative pro-Tor comparison is reconstructed at the level actually required by the letter. After a faithfully flat completion and a flat noetherian cover, one works with a complex of finite modules. Artin–Rees makes pro-completion exact and identifies completed homology with pro-homology. This proves an isomorphism of pro-systems, which is stronger than equality of their inverse limits. The proof also keeps the relative depth convention: depth is measured with respect to the maximal ideal of A, not silently replaced by depth over B. [C-SA23-ega-054, C-SA23-ega-055, C-SA23-ega-056, C-SA23-ega-057, C-SA23-ega-058, C-SA23-ega-059, C-SA23-ega-060; ED-SA23-ega-27, ED-SA23-ega-28, MP-SA23-044, ED-SA23-ega-30; Stacks0911; Stacks09Q9; StacksAuslanderBuchsbaum]

The sign correction has its own printed old/new-label transposition, which is recorded instead of concealed. ### The Tapis: homotopy types and higher algebra

The nerve, category-of-simplices and local-coefficient constructions are established. The presheaf construction has a different fullness issue: on the ordered set of integers, filtered colimit defines a geometric point that is not evaluation at any object. This supplies a negative answer to the manuscript’s explicit doubt without confusing it with the fully faithful simplicial nerve. The manuscript’s own product correction in category cochains is necessary for infinite categories, and its “Si!” margin retracts the denial of a nonlocal coefficient interpretation. Its derived tensor correction from bounded to bounded-above complexes is also mathematically necessary; dual numbers provide an exact periodic Tor example. [forms_SGA4I;forms_QuillenK;forms_Thomason;C-SA23-forms-034, C-SA23-forms-035, C-SA23-forms-036, C-SA23-forms-037, C-SA23-forms-085]

Two coefficient tests must be separated. A finite acyclic noncontractible complex passes all abelian coefficients pulled back from a point, so those tests alone do not detect the fundamental group. Moreover, the converse derived-local-system criterion cannot hold over every fixed nonzero ring: a simply connected Moore space M(Z/p,n), n≥2, is rationally acyclic but not weakly contractible. Its bounded rational locally constant derived category is the one of a point. Thus the coefficient ring and the equivalence of local-system categories must be specified. [forms_Hatcher,Examples2.38–2.40;C-SA23-forms-038, C-SA23-forms-080]

The strict higher-category discussions need their globularity conditions. Iterated internal categories give multicomplexes under normalization; globularity removes the extra terms to produce ordinary length-n complexes. Strictly commutative Picard models also omit a real invariant: the self-commutor. Superlines show why the deleted strict-commutative strengthening is false. Higher mapping objects require derived Hom and resolutions, not only the ordinary Hom complex. The bare homotopy-group-object question already has a negative example in Stasheff’s1963 Theorem17: taking p=5,n=3 gives an A4 space with no A5 structure and hence no loop-space homotopy type. ### 161-2, first and second files: envelopes, quotients, and elementary classifiers

The first major construction seeks a universal category by adjoining prescribed limits and colimits, imposing equations, and inverting comparison maps. Its appeal is clear: a single construction could organize many varieties of “algebraic” structure. The difficulty lies in what is required at each stage. The six elementary quotient theories are examples in a much larger lattice, not an exhaustive classification. ### Third file: ring properties, zero dimension, and characteristic

The ring sheets are an extended attempt to classify properties by their behavior under topological and algebraic operations. The resulting lattice is useful only if one keeps apart an external property of a ring, an internal geometric condition in a topos, and a category of rings equipped with chosen additional data. The differences matter throughout claims 038–058.

The noetherian “ultralocal” point test fails for the fppf topology. Schröer's exact classification says that a noetherian fppf-local ring is an algebraically closed field. Such tests cannot detect a nonzero nilpotent in the dual numbers. The reduced closed subscheme therefore passes all those tests although its sieve is not fppf covering: a faithfully flat map cannot kill that nilpotent. This gives a concrete answer to problem 19. Nonnoetherian local objects remain essential. The source comparison is [Schroer2016, Propositions 4.3–4.4 and Corollary 4.5](https://www.math.uni-duesseldorf.de/~schroeer/publications_pdf/points_fppf.pdf), published in 2017; the separate [Gabber–Kelly2015, Theorems 2.3 and 2.6, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5782) retains its own base and topology hypotheses.

Several ring predicates admit clean constructions. Reduction is compatible with the appropriate cartesian model interpretation. Irreducibility needs a careful distinction between a domain and a ring with prime nilradical. An ultraproduct of the rings (k[x,y]/(xy,x^n,y^n)) shows that the latter class is not coherently axiomatized in the unchanged ring signature: every finite stage has irreducible spectrum, while the two limiting nonnilpotent elements have product zero. This conclusion is confined to that signature; it is not promoted into a claim about every Morita-equivalent presentation. Connectedness also requires an explicit convention about the empty scheme.

The zero-dimensional calculation is stronger and fully checkable. Every element separates into a unit on one idempotent component and a nilpotent on the complementary component. The equations for that decomposition have at most one solution. The program enumerates finite rings to check the equations, and the algebraic proof supplies unrestricted uniqueness. The affine scheme represented by those equations is a disjoint union of a unit piece and a nilpotent piece. Its representable presheaf need not be the presheaf coproduct of the two pieces, because a disconnected test affine can meet both. That distinction is one source of the misleading factorization on the page.

The absolutely flat reflection gives a second exact comparison. Adjoining the unique quasi-inverse of each element produces the universal absolutely flat ring; its spectrum has the constructible topology and the same residue fields. The matched sources are [Olivier1967, Proposition 5](https://www.numdam.org/item/SAC_1967-1968__2__A6_0.pdf) and [Stevenson2014, Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.0399). The source's unrestricted factorization is corrected explicitly, rather than attributed to those theorems. The actual margin question asks whether the zero-dimensional ring topology has enough points. It does: finite presentations over the integers form a countable finite-limit site, and the countable families D_n and their pullbacks generate a countable coverage. [MakkaiReyes1977, Theorem 6.2.4](https://reyes-reyes.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/makkai-reyes-book.pdf) supplies enough points for that separable site; its exact definition and proof were opened. Claim 115 records the application. The displayed D_n family has no finite subcover, but this alone is not a theorem excluding every alternative coherent presentation. Internal local nilpotence and nilpotence of a global section are also separated: a product of truncated polynomial rings gives unbounded nilpotence orders.

The characteristic pages contain a particularly instructive correction. Inverting every positive integer defines a $\mathbf Q$-algebra; it does not merely assert characteristic zero. The ring $\mathbf Z$ distinguishes those conditions. An infinite intersection of open classifying subtopoi need not be open, and the proposed limit of affine open representables does not recover the $\mathbf Q$-algebra classifier. On finite-type $\mathbf Z$-tests, no nonzero test algebra can contain $\mathbf Q$, by the Jacobson residue-field result. Thus the presheaf-limit shortcut loses the intended models. SGA IV 9.2.6 guarantees finite intersections, not arbitrary intersections; [StacksJacobson, Lemma 10.35.19](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00GB) gives the exact algebraic test.

The later characteristic gluing has a valid more specific interpretation: local choices of prime-field algebra structures, with the zero-ring points identified. It is not a decomposition theorem for every ring. The wider proposal to glue arbitrary subtopoi through largest stable open factorization loci still needs existence and inverse-image stability of those loci. The author's “OK” for the explicit characteristic construction is therefore retained without closing the broader question.

The connected-component calculations have a similar boundary. A bound on finite partitions into nonzero orthogonal idempotents forces a finite Boolean idempotent algebra with at most that many atoms. Finiteness cannot be omitted: an atomless Boolean algebra has no atoms. Exact component rank fails filtered-colimit closure under unrestricted ring maps; repeated projection of $k^n$ onto its diagonal copy has colimit $k$. Orderings of a given decomposition form a symmetric-group torsor, and that torsor pulls back. What remains unproved is that the source's construction of the sheaf of all decompositions, through its proposed largest locus, itself commutes with every inverse image.

### Fourth file: geometric algebra properties and accessible categories

The module list provides several useful negative answers. Flatness has finite relation witnesses and a geometric formulation. Faithful flatness of modules is not preserved by arbitrary filtered colimits: the system $\mathbf Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbf Z\xrightarrow{2}\cdots$ has faithfully flat free stages and colimit $\mathbf Z[1/2]$, which kills $\mathbf Z/2$ under tensor product. The same example separates finite generation and projectivity. Ordinary injectivity is not preserved by arbitrary geometric inverse image: a constant rank-one local system on a circle embeds in a unipotent rank-two local system without an invariant retraction. The proof and its two-by-two calculation are recorded. These answers concern ordinary categorical properties, not an unspecified internal substitute.

The canceled tensor-invertible-module argument also has a precise repair. If evaluation $M\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(M,A)\to A$ is an isomorphism, a finite expression for the preimage of 1 gives a finite unit-ideal cover. On each piece a functional splits off a free summand; the remaining summand lies in the kernel of evaluation and must vanish. This proves local rank one without presupposing that Hom commutes with localization. It does not imply global cyclicity. The invertible nonprincipal ideal $(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$ gives the counterexample. The local proof was independently checked by the EGA owner; [StacksInvertible, Lemma 15.119.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B8I) supplies the standard comparison.

Several relative algebra properties have finite-equation geometric descriptions, but formal smoothness and formal étaleness cannot be grouped with smoothness and étaleness after dropping finite presentation. The register states the exact radicial, spectrum-surjectivity, flatness, and formal-unramifiedness criteria, then leaves the broader formal-property formulation conditional. Internal fraction fields require decidable zero as well as the domain condition. These are explicit hypothesis boundaries, not an assertion that every internal domain embeds in a geometric field.

The accessibility sheets introduce a second set of recurring difficulties: regularity, directedness, and changing cardinal ranks. The functor $X\mapsto X^{\mathbf N}$ preserves $\aleph_1$-filtered colimits, yet fails to preserve presentability at arbitrarily large successor ranks arising from singular cardinals of countable cofinality. A separate diagonal argument refutes the proposed small cofinal family of countable subsets. Ordinary filtered presentations cannot be substituted for $\aleph_1$-directed ones. These examples explain exactly why the source's cardinal bounds need sharpening; the modern sharply-larger relation has different quantifiers.

It is not recorded as an unqualified still-open elementary theorem. Verification of the asserted accessible-category bicolimit theorem is a separate editorial task. The checked flexible-limit theorem proves bilimits under its actual convention; reversing arrows does not prove a bicolimit result. The tensor product of locally presentable categories is also established, whereas a formulation for arbitrary accessible categories with unspecified exactness is not supplied by it. Exact comparisons include [AdamekRosicky1996](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/118845/CommentatMathUnivCarolRetro_37-1996-2_20.pdf), [ColimitDense2019](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc1904/abrocaporo.pdf), Lack–Tendas §5, and [CavigliaHorel2016, §2](https://geoffroy.horel.org/Rigidification.pdf).
