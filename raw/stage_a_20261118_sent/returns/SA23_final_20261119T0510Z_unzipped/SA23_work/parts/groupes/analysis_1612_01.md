## Scans 4–10, lines 5–263

1. Lines 5–21 state the small universal completion of a small category with prescribed cones, under a small family of limit and colimit shapes. The universal property is an equivalence of functor categories, not equality of strict choices. The construction alternately adjoins limits/colimits, identifies parallel maps forced equal by uniqueness, and adjoins factorization arrows. A small term construction is plausible and standard for bounded sketches, but this particular transfinite argument has the regularity gap below.
2. Lines 23–100 describe successor operations and a colimit at a limit ordinal. The printed colimit in line 32 has C_i where C_j is required. A projective cone in line 47 is drawn in the direction incompatible with p_lambda u; its arrows must go from the cone vertex to the diagram. Primes on the projective/inductive markings are inconsistent in lines 139–150 and require ink inspection.
3. Lines 102–109, repeated at 253–258, choose a cardinal pi merely larger than the diagram cardinalities, and then its initial ordinal. The proof needs cofinality greater than every indexing size. A singular pi=aleph_omega and a countable family of stages aleph_n show that cardinal inequality alone does not give a common stage. Repair: choose a sufficiently large regular kappa; then every <kappa-sized diagram, relation, natural transformation and equality witness is captured at some stage. This repairs the method, not the literal sentence.
4. Lines 117–124 claim the limit category has all required cones and retains the original exact cones. The usual finite/regular-small factorization proof works after that repair. Equality of the tracked cone family with literally all exact cones additionally requires closure under isomorphism/adding all equivalent choices; the universal property only needs existence and preservation, so the equality of families is stronger bookkeeping than necessary.
5. Lines 139–166 describe the six operations and say their order may be permuted. Fair repeated iteration reaches a category with the same universal property regardless of the order; the individual successor categories need not be isomorphic. This distinction should be explicit.
6. Lines 174–258 generalize to a small family f:R→R' in the completion class, requiring restriction of exact-functor categories to be faithful, fully faithful, or an equivalence. Stage operations identify two transformations, adjoin transformations, and adjoin extensions up to isomorphism. The equivalence of target-functor categories must be proved for each operation. The source explicitly leaves the construction of K for this second theorem unwritten at line 263. Do not use the first construction as if this missing proof had been supplied.
7. The requested filtered-colimit lemma at 258 is the regular-small factorization lemma for categories, functors and natural transformations. A diagram with <kappa arrows factors through one stage of a kappa-filtered category colimit; equality of such data is also eventually witnessed. This is more precise than cardinal size alone.

## Scans 11–15, lines 268–430

8. Lines 278–309 encode unconditional or conditional existence of limits and colimits, and implications between cone-exactness conditions, by restrictions between functor categories with marked cones. The question whether surjectivity/faithfulness on Hom can be expressed as essential-surjectivity conditions has an explicit diagram-category construction. For fullness, use the inclusion
   S=(R'⊔_R(R×[1])⊔_R R') → R'×[1].
   A map out of S specifies two extensions and a transformation between their restrictions; extending to the cylinder specifies a lift. Carry the marked cones on the two endpoints. For faithfulness take two copies of the cylinder glued along S and require the resulting pair of transformations to factor through the fold to one cylinder. This is a present reconstruction, not a proof written on the sheet. The whole formulation is invariant under isomorphism, so essential rather than literal surjectivity suffices.
9. Lines 327–347 flag this encoding and ask for composition stability of exact functors. If every delta-exact diagram in the admitted categories is lambda-exact and lambda contains delta, the condition reduces to preserving every lambda-exact test diagram. Equivalences satisfy it, composition does, and arbitrary small intersections are represented by the union of the test families. Lines 361–365 are correct with the omitted assumption F≅G in 361 restored; without that assumption the displayed implication for arbitrary F,G is false.
10. Lines 369–395 define object classes by essential-image inclusions and a locally full 2-subcategory by cone-preserving functors. Equivalence invariance and intersection stability follow directly. The margin at 398 adds compatibility between the exactness tests and existence of their limits. It should not be read as a theorem that every arbitrary locally full 2-subcategory is distinguished.
