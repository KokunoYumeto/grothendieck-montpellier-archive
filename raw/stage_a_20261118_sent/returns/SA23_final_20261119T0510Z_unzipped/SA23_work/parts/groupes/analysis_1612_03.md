## Ring classifiers, scans39–44

6. Rings are classified by PSh(R), R=(finitely presented commutative Z-algebras)^op, equivalently affine finite-type Z-schemes. Over Z finite type equals finite presentation. Lex(R,E) is the category of internal rings. Zariski continuous flat functors classify nonzero local rings (the empty covering excludes the zero ring); the local axiom is a+b=1⇒a unit or b unit. Étale continuous flat functors classify strictly henselian local rings. Henselian-local points correspond to Nisnevich rather than arbitrary étale covers. The sheet's henselian topology is defined by these point tests; match its finite-type conventions explicitly.
7. Ultralocal/fppf-local is asked about at268–275. The noetherian-testing converse at273 is false under the displayed fppf-local interpretation. An fppf-local noetherian ring is an algebraically closed field. Proof: every a in its maximal ideal has n-th roots for all n, hence a∈m^n for every n; Krull intersection gives a=0. The finite-flat polynomial tests then give algebraic closure. Thus Spec(F_p)→Spec(F_p[ε]/ε²) is surjective on all such test points but is not an fppf covering, even as a covering sieve: a faithfully flat extension cannot kill ε. Opened primary source: Stefan Schröer, Points in the fppf topology, final2016, §4 contains this exact noetherian classification. Need record exact proposition and construction theorem. The positive repaired statement uses a conservative family of possibly very nonnoetherian fppf-local rings; the proposed ind-faithfully-flat construction is a separate question and must retain its hypotheses.
8. Reduced rings form a cartesian theory (x^n=0⇒x=0 for every n), with classifier PSh(R_red). The finite limits of reduced affine schemes are obtained by reducing the ordinary fibre products. A family is covering for the induced quotient when a member has a section after reduction. The zero ring is allowed unless an additional nontriviality axiom is imposed. Lines281–286 express the nilradical as the filtered union of the kernels of power maps; the indexing must use divisibility or the increasing annihilation filtration, not assume x↦x^n is an additive map.
9. Integral domains: nontriviality and ab=0⇒a=0 or b=0, expressed by an epi from the two axes onto the zero-product locus. On finite-type affine schemes the cover by reduced irreducible components gives the closed topology; fibre functors are integral domains. The displayed descent equalizer is the usual sheaf condition for this finite cover and its overlaps. It does not say the components are disjoint.
10. Irreducible rings: nilradical prime and proper, equivalently A_red a domain. The formula for nilpotence of ab gives a geometric infinitary disjunction. The displayed topological cover in355–357 has competing induced-structure/reduced readings; taking reduced components would instead impose reducedness and classify domains. This is a high-weight reading alarm. The inserted word 'coherent' at359 and non-quasi-compact-site statement361 must not be conflated: failure of this presentation to be coherent does not alone decide intrinsic coherence of the topos. Ultraproducts of k[x,y]/(xy,x^n,y^n) demonstrate that the naive irreducible-ring axiom is not equivalent to a coherent theory in its given ring signature: x,y become nonnilpotent zero divisors.
11. Connected spectrum is the geometric idempotent condition e²=e⇒e=0 or e=1; nonempty connected spectrum additionally excludes the zero ring. Finite-type affine schemes split into finitely many connected components. The presheaf description on connected schemes and the sheaf condition 'finite coproducts become products' must say whether the empty scheme is included. The source itself changes this convention at382–389; keep both variants. The condition of preserving nonempty finite coproducts permits an unconstrained value at the empty object, whereas preserving the empty coproduct forces value1.
12. Local zero-dimensional rings satisfy every element unit or nilpotent. The predicate is geometric with a countable disjunction over nilpotence exponents. Scan42's test on local artinian rings is not automatically equivalent to testing all zero-dimensional local rings; approximation and flatness need proof. The whole comparison paragraph445–458 is cancelled, including 'all have enough points', the intersection warning, and the proposed induction on dimension.
13. Scan43 defines D_n by e²=e, f^n(1−e)=0, g(1−e)=0, fg=e. The projection to f is a monomorphism: the idempotent is determined by the clopen locus D(f), and g by the inverse on that component and zero elsewhere. D_n is the affine-scheme coproduct G_m ⊔ Spec Z[t]/t^n, not the coproduct of the two represented presheaves. Its covering family imposes the internal zero-dimensional condition. The source explicitly stresses this distinction at479–480.
14. The literal 'for every U, A(U) has compact spectrum' at466 is stronger than the geometric local condition just encoded. In E=Set^N take A_n=k[ε]/ε^{n+1}; internally each element is locally unit/nilpotent. Global sections form ∏A_n, where f=(ε_n) is neither nilpotent nor invertible on any nonzero idempotent part. Hence the global-sections reading fails. The precise internal formulation allows locally varying exponents. No ink is changed.
15. Reduced zero-dimensional rings are absolutely flat / commutative von Neumann regular rings, with a unique quasi-inverse. The D_1→A condition is cartesian. Formula489 has e(1−f)=0 where f(1−e)=0 is needed: f=2 in Q forces e=1 and refutes the printed equation. The scalar decomposition A≅A_f×A/fA is valid. The constructible/absolute-flat reflection and the asserted presheaf classifier require exact source matching; 'a point in every fibre' is a different coverage from a singleton map that is a bijection with trivial residue extensions.
16. Separably closed fields are strictly henselian fields; algebraically closed fields are fppf-local fields. Their specified residue-field lifting conditions correspond respectively to finite separable and arbitrary finite algebraic extensions. The char-independent classifier for fields uses the nontriviality axiom. The passage from a family to a single cover must use finite coproducts when warranted, rather than silently replacing every covering family by one of its members.

## Characteristic and pro-objects, scans44–47

17. n·1=0 and n invertible are cartesian ring quotients, classified by finite-type Z/n and Z[1/n] algebras. Their classifying subtoposes are open because these data are already subterminal objects of the finite-type affine syntactic category. For n=1 the only ring is zero and its classifier is Set, not the degenerate topos. The inversion-class description uses isomorphisms after base change; line541's word 'bijections' is too weak if it means only underlying sets of points.
18. Q-algebras are rings in which all positive integers are invertible, the intersection of the finite localization subtheories. They are not all characteristic-zero rings (Z is a counterexample); line600's X_car0 needs this explicit convention. The local theory is represented by PSh(R_Q). The parenthetical question about uniqueness of a Lambda-algebra structure is answered only for an epimorphism Z→Lambda in commutative rings, not arbitrary Lambda. Over a general Lambda one also needs finitely presented rather than merely finite-type algebras to identify the finite-limit syntactic category.
19. The assertion Pro(R)↪PSh(R) at608 is false in general with the naive limit-of-representables map. For R=FinSet, Pro(R) is profinite sets; restricting ordinary representables along finite discrete test sets forgets the topology, so arbitrary discontinuous maps appear as natural transformations. A particularly relevant failure: as presheaves on finite-type affine Z-schemes, the inverse limit of Spec Z[1/n] has no value on any nonempty finite-type Z-scheme (there is no nonzero finite-type Z-algebra containing Q), so it agrees with the representable empty scheme. The Q-algebra point theory is nevertheless nontrivial. Therefore one cannot replace intersection of open subtoposes by the presheaf open of this inverse limit.
20. For an actual mono S→1 in small finitely complete R, PSh(R/S)≃PSh(R)/yS, with i_! forgetful, i^* product with S, and i_* internal Hom(S,-), as the source writes. For a decreasing filtered pro-family S_alpha, the correct theory of inverting all S_alpha→1 is classified by PSh(colim_alpha R/S_alpha) under the stated smallness conditions. This uses the finite-limit localization of the syntactic category and does not make the resulting subtopos open. Lines662–670 explicitly warn to use i_* rather than i_!. The later scan48 line753 explicitly says the rational locus is not open, correcting the earlier extension attempt.
21. Perfect characteristic-p rings: p1=0 and Frobenius invertible; perfection implies reducedness. The finite-presentation objects in the perfect-ring theory are perfections of finitely presented F_p-algebras, not necessarily finitely presented ordinary rings. The classifier is the corresponding presheaf topos. Perfect absolutely flat rings add quasi-inverses; perfect fields add locality/nontriviality. The 'perfect in characteristic0 means reduced?' margin is a proposed convention, not a theorem or standard general definition of perfect ring.
