# Manual reading ledger: K161-2-05, physical lines 1–340

All lines read. This ledger records mathematical judgments from the transcription; no scans were consulted. Distinguish later corrections in 06/07 from the first wording here.

1. Scans 66–69, lines 1–83: for a doctrine with specified limits and colimits, limits of models are pointwise whenever those limits exist in the target and commute with the specified colimits. Evaluation preserves them. A change of target must preserve those limits as well. The omitted hypothesis in this first wording is restored explicitly in 07, scan 104, line 415. The apparent d/δ substitution also needs an ink check. For a limits-only doctrine, every limit that exists in the target exists in its model category; existence of all limits in every target is not asserted by the corrected version.

2. Functor categories and the exponential law give T(Fun(J,C)) ≃ Fun(J,T(C)), with pointwise doctrine operations and the stated universe bounds. Full faithfulness and faithfulness of C→C′ pass to the functor categories of models. The square of models on fixed underlying objects is a 2-pullback when those objects generate the representing category by the specified operations; mere faithfulness of the basis is weaker, as the later question 07:309–315 makes explicit.

3. For a limits-only doctrine the Yoneda embedding C→PSh(C) preserves the operations. A model in C is equivalently an S=T(Set)-valued presheaf on C for which the underlying constructions are representable. It suffices to check a genuinely operation-generating basis. Representables in the model category identify R^op; evaluation is represented by those objects. The canceled 79–83 paragraph remains an authorial withdrawn attempt, not a theorem silently restored.

4. Scans 70–74: the doctrine with no operations is represented by small categories; all arrows inverted give the groupoid completion. The passage to preorders identifies parallel arrows; passage to posets also identifies mutually comparable objects. These are distinct quotients. The free complete category on a small I is Fun(I,Set)^op, with the universe enlargement inherent in all small limits; the formula near line 114 has a target-variable ambiguity.

5. Localization of a complete category by a pullback- and composition-stable multiplicative system preserves finite limits under the calculus-of-fractions hypotheses. Arbitrary product preservation additionally needs stability under those products. The manuscript's questions about local smallness, transitivity of a strict cogenerator, filtered transfinite stages, and adjoining arrows are genuine gaps; none follows just from forming a Cat colimit. Line 127 explicitly withdraws a tempting transitivity assertion.

6. Lines 132–184: the proposed all-limit representation theorem requires an exact cogenerator notion and a solution-set theorem. A small cogenerator alone is not the full special adjoint functor theorem: completeness and well-poweredness (or an appropriate stronger cogenerator criterion that supplies it) must be tracked. Claims that every continuous Set-valued functor is represented, and every continuous functor has a left adjoint, are conditional on those hypotheses. Do not replace the text's changing word 'strict' by a modern theorem without saying so. Localization corresponds contravariantly to a fully faithful adjoint when the adjunction exists. Internal free-model constructions in arbitrary complete categories are a separate issue.

7. Scans 75–77: closure under a bounded set of limit shapes is obtained by transfinite iteration inside a sufficiently large regular universe bound. One step is enough for finite products but is not a general argument for arbitrary shapes. The auxiliary embedding C→Hom_Δ(C,Set)^op is fully faithful by Yoneda for a small limits sketch, and the text answers its question affirmatively at line 213. Localizing this envelope and taking the operation-closed image supplies a conditional construction; its local-smallness premise must remain visible. In line 220 the reference to Σ containing diagrams appears to mean the designated family of shapes. The smallness question at 253 is canceled; earlier bounded-shape construction addresses it with a regular bound.

8. Scans 78–79: for small finitely complete R, S=Lex(R,Set) ≃ Ind(R^op), and R^op is precisely S_fp. Finite colimits in R^op split idempotents, so a finitely presentable model, being a retract of a representable, is representable. This is the Gabriel–Ulmer correspondence. The formula around 278 confuses S with the small category Σ; the 'small colimits' closure around 286 must be finite colimits if the subsequent argument is intended. A small category with all small colimits is necessarily a preorder, so the literal unrestricted wording cannot describe general algebraic examples.

9. Lines 326–336: the restriction from theories on finitely complete categories to theories on complete categories is stated **1-faithful**, explicitly meaning a fully faithful functor on Hom categories, not an equivalence on those categories. This distinction matters: the underlying-object theory acquires infinite-power constructions on complete targets. The proposed essential-image criterion puts preservation of finitely presentable objects on the displayed model functor S→S′. Literally this is false: the underlying-set functor Groups→Sets belongs to the finite-limit theory but takes the finitely presented group Z to an infinite, hence non-finitely-presentable, set. The correct criterion is on its left adjoint; the manuscript **does make that correction** in 06, scan 91, physical 379. Record both passages rather than treating the first wording as the final claim.

10. Line 339 asks whether a finite-product theory is recoverable from its Set-model category and how all its presentations are found. In general the selected finite-coproduct subcategory is additional data, up to Cauchy/Morita equivalence. For Set, FinSet and its full subcategory of finite even-cardinality sets both are closed under finite coproducts and have the same idempotent completion; every finite set retracts from an even finite set. Their opposite finite-product theories have equivalent Set-model categories. In general the appropriate generators are strongly finitely presentable projectives, not every finitely presentable object. This is a precise research-fate target, not an identification by terminology alone.
