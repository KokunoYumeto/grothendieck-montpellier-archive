# Manual reading ledger: K161-2-06, physical lines 1–473

All lines read, with 341–473 re-read in smaller chunks after a truncated output. This ledger preserves canceled questions and later corrections.

1. Scan 81 inventories doctrines: finite limits, specified shapes, finite-meet posets, finite bicompleteness, pretopoi, additive/semiadditive/abelian categories, exact filtered colimits, frames, topoi, all limits and colimits, and essential topoi. The abelian functor exactness question and universe-exit warning at 45 are authorial flags. The presheaf characterization by sufficient essential points needs idempotent completion and the exact SGA IV Exercise 7.6 formulation; the phrase 'all exactness properties of Set' is not a complete axiom list.

2. Scans 82–85: ρ(T)=Nat(T,τ) and σ(R)=Hom_Δ(R,-) are contravariant in their arguments. The displayed diagram suppresses the opposite. The 2-Yoneda lemma gives Nat(σR,T)≃T(R) and ρσR≃R. Evaluation is a map T→σρT; the reversed map is canceled near 80. The proposed universal-reflection statement at 88 is canceled. It follows by currying only after proving that ρT lies in the chosen universe and doctrine and that evaluation is exact; the local-smallness doubt at 173 is substantive.

3. Envelopes and doctrine-localizations, if they exist, produce bicolimits by first forming a Cat bicolimit and then forcing exactness comparison maps to be invertible. The comparison collection can be too large for large categories (line 136). A pseudocolimit of an opfibration is the localization of its Grothendieck construction at cocartesian arrows; the source's 'cartésiens' at 138 requires checking against its variance convention.

4. Scans 85–87: empty theory, adjoining operations, adjoining invertibility/equality axioms, products, and fixed-basis 2-pullbacks are the construction rules. Equality is not automatically expressible by inverting arrows for a doctrine without equalizers; line 185 flags this. Forgetting newly adjoined operations is faithful. Independent products of theories correspond to coproducts of representing categories; only the fixed-basis construction at 231–236 is an amalgamated coproduct. The word at 218 therefore has a base-dependence alarm. The broad inverse-limit and small-basis claims retain the unresolved uniform-bound hypothesis.

5. A small representing category admits a presentation by its objects, arrows, composition relations, and operation-comparison maps (273). For a large R with a faithful basis, the attempted transfinite construction at 295–314 does not prove stabilization: the author questions full faithfulness, replacing stages by their full images, conservativity at limits, and a universe bound. A conservative equalizer-preserving functor is faithful: if Ff=Fg, its preserved equalizer becomes an isomorphism and hence already was one. This does not make the functor full. The manuscript strikes that stronger claim.

6. Scan 90: a small sketch of finite limit shapes has a locally finitely presentable category S of Set models. Its finitely presentable objects are the finite-colimit/retract closure of the representable models. The text's S0 stable under finite *projective* limits at 337 has the wrong variance in general; finitely presented modules over a noncoherent ring need not be closed under kernels. The dual closure is finite colimits. The Lazard 'analyseurs' question at 339 is a historical comparison lead, not an established equivalence without checking the actual definition.

7. Scans 91–92 assemble a dictionary among types, representing categories, model categories with a chosen small generating subcategory, and presheaf topoi. The corrected line 379 puts the finitely presentable-object condition on the **left adjoint** of the displayed model functor, repairing 05:332–336. The dictionary is valid in the finite-limit case, with coherent opposite conventions. For arbitrary limit families it is not literally valid without extra structure: for the finite-product theory of abelian groups, all Set models are abelian groups whereas points of the presheaf topos on the Lawvere category give filtered colimits of finite free groups, hence torsion-free groups. Thus the point-category column and C=Ind(S) cannot be used for arbitrary λ. Likewise essential-point categories recover a Cauchy completion, not an arbitrary non-idempotent-complete presentation. The 'faithful theory iff f* faithful' row at 395–399 requires independent testing, not an inference from the fully faithful row. The unresolved '2-fid ??' is preserved.

8. Scans 93–95: fields are not a limits-defined full ring subtheory because products of nonzero fields are not fields. The limits-envelope obtained by making the partial-inverse map invertible is the category of commutative von Neumann regular rings (the manuscript's pseudocorps), including the zero ring. The algebra Z[t,t^-1]×Z ≅ Z[x,y]/((xy−1)x,(xy−1)y) is checked by the unique quasi-inverse equations; it is an algebra coproduct on the affine-scheme side, not the presheaf coproduct. A map Z[t]→this algebra induces a monomorphism of functors because a quasi-inverse is unique. The broad orthogonality to maps inducing bijections of spectra and residue-field isomorphisms needs the exact absolute-flat reflection theorem.

9. Units are definable by finite limits: pairs (x,y) with xy=1, projected to x; inverse uniqueness makes this a subobject. The text answers its own units question at 94:426. A geometric field has the map 1⊔A×→A, given by zero and inclusion, invertible; this simultaneously says every element is locally zero or invertible and 0≠1. The deleted 1⊔ at 428/435 conflicts with its restoration at 453 and 462, so no transcription is silently changed.

10. The field classifier is obtained in two steps: first force the unique quasi-inverse condition (absolutely flat rings), then force the zero/invertible decomposition to be a disjoint cover, including the empty-cover condition on the zero ring. The deleted assertion that the field classifier is simply a presheaf topos is corrected in 07:1–9 to a sheaf topos for the disjoint-decomposition/Zariski topology. The general final claim 471 for every finitely complete finitely cocomplete balanced category is too weakly hypothesized: balancedness alone does not give disjoint stable coproducts or exactness of the field decomposition. Its 'finite sums suffice?' question stays separate from the valid Grothendieck-topos construction.
