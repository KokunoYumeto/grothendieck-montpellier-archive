4. Scan 102, 309–315 asks whether determination of natural transformations on S forces λ-generation of objects. It is false for λ empty: take the idempotent-splitting completion of the one-object monoid {1,e}, e²=e≠1, and let S be the original object. Every transformation is determined at that object because the new object is a retract; it is not in the empty-operation closure up to isomorphism. It is false even for finite products: take R=FinSet^op, S={2}; components at 2 determine the component at its retract 1 and hence every finite power of 1, but the product closure of 2 has even cardinalities 2m (including 0) and misses 1 and 3. This directly answers the author's proposed converse without claiming a stronger failed finite-limit result, since retracts are finite limits.
