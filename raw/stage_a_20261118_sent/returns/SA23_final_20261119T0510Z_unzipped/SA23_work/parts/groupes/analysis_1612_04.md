# Manual source analysis: K161-2-04

All 538 physical lines read. Later corrections to the accessibility definitions must be linked to their earlier formulations. In SGA's cardinal convention, 'grand devant pi' means bounds for subsets of cardinal at most pi, so modern regular-cardinal language often uses kappa=pi^+.

## Scans51–53: ring decompositions and module properties

1. The largest locus Pi_nj on which a factor ring is connected is defined by a universal condition; its formation is not automatically preserved by inverse image. The author asks this precisely at4. For a given decomposition into n connected nonzero factors, uniqueness up to permutation follows from the Boolean algebra of idempotents, so the sheaf of ordered choices is an S_n-torsor. The transitivity/uniqueness lemma is flagged at7. A pseudotorsor as an already given G-object is defined by G×P≅P×P, and its pullback remains a pseudotorsor; adding P→1 epi gives a torsor. This answers the property-level question at10, but does not itself prove the sheaf-of-all-decompositions construction commutes with base change.
2. Exact rank n with all ring homomorphisms is not a geometric full subtheory for n≥2: the sequence A=k^n with repeated idempotent endomorphism (a_1,...,a_n)↦(a_1,...,a_1) has filtered colimit k. Each stage has n components and the colimit one. This does not refute a theory retaining the torsor/decomposition as extra structure with compatible morphisms. For rank≤n the ordinary Set condition is a coherent bound on idempotents, but an equivalence with the author's local decomposition in every topos requires a constructive proof; pointwise agreement alone is insufficient. Do not report that stronger equivalence as proved.
3. Flat modules: the ideal criterion (I⊗M→M injective for finitely generated ideals I) is valid for commutative rings and internalizes by finite-equation witnesses. The universal-section formulation over A^n is correct. The equational flatness criterion is geometric (relations factor through finite matrices of relations), so flatness is stable under geometric inverse image and filtered colimits. Exact primary flatness/Lazard sources must be opened.
4. Faithfully flat modules as a property with all module maps fail filtered-colimit closure: Z --2→ Z --2→ ... has faithfully flat stages and colimit Z[1/2], whose tensor with Z/2 vanishes. Thus the circled 'typique?' question at54 has a negative answer in that interpretation. This example does not apply to unital algebra maps. Faithfully flat algebras require a separate geometric surjectivity condition.
5. Finite type/presentation, free/projective, rank-n locally free, cyclic and invertible module properties likewise need morphism conventions. The same Z-localization system refutes finite generation/presentation, cyclicity, projectivity and invertibility as geometric full module subtheories. A theory carrying a chosen basis or duality has different morphisms and is not refuted by this example. The source's injectivity question remains ring-dependent; injective modules over a noetherian ring are closed under direct limits, so a general negative needs either a nonnoetherian example or a base-change obstruction, not the Z example.
6. The relative-algebra list at70–87 contains explicit questions, not established answers. Integrality has the geometric finite-polynomial witness; radiciality can be expressed by nilpotence of the kernel of B⊗_A B→B; universal homeomorphism combines integral, radicial and surjective. Scheme-surjectivity admits radical-contraction tests, distinct from injectivity of A→B. Formal unramifiedness corresponds to Omega_B/A=0; formal smoothness/étaleness and the question of imposing flatness on a given finitely presented algebra need exact hypotheses and reference matches. Do not interpret 'smooth' without its finite-presentation condition.
7. Internal domains embedding in internal fields: domain alone does not suffice constructively. A subring of a geometric field has decidable zero (indeed equality); for a domain with decidable zero the nonzero elements form a multiplicative subobject and its fraction ring is a field, giving sufficiency. The generic domain has no universally nonzero elements except units because a nonunit may become zero under a domain-valued quotient. Thus its total fraction object may equal itself. This is the point of the source's universal-domain argument; it is not a denial that an ordinary Set-valued domain has a fraction field.
8. M⊗Hom_A(M,A)→A invertible implies M is tensor-invertible, hence finite projective of rank1 in ordinary modules (locally so for sheaves). The deleted argument132 sketches finite generation from the inverse image of1 and exactness of tensoring by an inverse. Its final 'M is monogenic' is too strong globally: an invertible nonprincipal ideal is rank1 projective but not cyclic. Only local monogenicity follows. All this block remains cancelled. Hom base change is valid after finite projectivity has been established, not before.

## Scans55–56: accessibility and cardinal ranks

9. A category is kappa-accessible iff it is Ind_kappa(C) for a small C, with its kappa-presentable objects the retract closure of C. A small dense family of kappa-presentables plus kappa-filtered colimits alone is not the complete formulation written initially at137–143; scan56 margin202 explicitly notices the distinction, and scan60 adds the filteredness of E_kappa/X. With all small colimits the density condition gives local presentability. Merge these as an authorial refinement, not independent contradictory theorems.
10. An accessible functor is a left Kan extension from a small presentable subcategory at a sufficiently suitable rank; at a rank where it also preserves presentable objects it is Ind_kappa(f_kappa). Restriction of kappa-filtered-colimit-preserving functors from Ind_kappa(C) has the free-completion universal property. The source's 'every sufficiently large pi' preservation claim160–163 is false without cardinal restrictions. Explicit example: F:Set→Set, F(X)=X^N, preserves aleph1-filtered colimits. For arbitrarily large singular lambda of cofinality omega, X of size lambda is lambda^+-presentable while |X^N|>lambda by König's theorem, so F(X) is not lambda^+-presentable. Modern repair uses sharply larger cardinals / suitable cardinal-closed ranks, not all large cardinals.
11. For locally presentable C, a Set-valued contravariant functor taking small colimits to limits is representable; a covariant accessible finite-limit-preserving functor is prorepresentable, and a covariant accessible all-limit-preserving functor is representable. The adjoint criteria need locally small codomain and the relevant solution-set/accessibility hypotheses; the unqualified C' in174 cannot silently supply them. The representability and prorepresentability statements are different.
12. Small pseudolimits and section categories of accessible categories with accessible transition functors are accessible (standard accessible-category limit theorem). The separately flagged bicolimit claim184 is to be checked separately; it is not a consequence by duality, since accessibility is not self-dual.
13. Pointwise colimits of accessible functors remain accessible when computed in the target. Pointwise small limits require enough exactness / sufficiently large rank so those limits commute with the directed colimits. Closure of presentable objects under suitably bounded colimits and eventual presentability of every object are valid with precise cardinal inequalities. The source at217 uses ≤pi in its own shifted convention; do not import <kappa without translating.
14. The generalized criterion using kernel pairs/equalizers, fibre products and a small family of universal strict epimorphic generators is explicitly being modified at224–252. Topoi and Grothendieck abelian categories are locally presentable; the deleted abelian hypothesis at255 cannot justify saying every arbitrary category with a generator and exact filtered colimits is locally presentable. Set^op and Ab^op are not accessible. The explanation via nonexact inverse limits needs a real counterexample; arbitrary products of surjections in Set or Ab are surjective under choice, so the loose 'coker does not commute with products' is not the right general argument.

## Scans57–59: density and boundedness

15. For small phi:C→E and cocomplete locally small E, its colimit extension PSh(C)→E is left adjoint to the nerve; the nerve is fully faithful iff the canonical density cocones have colimit X. Thus E is a reflective full subcategory of a small presheaf category iff it is cocomplete and has a small dense subcategory. Essential surjectivity of the extension alone is not equivalent to density; the bracket at298 is an overstatement.
16. Line300 claims C/X is filtered when C has finite limits and phi preserves them. This is the wrong variance: take C={1}→Set. C is finitely complete, phi is lex and dense, yet C/2 is a discrete two-object category. Finite colimits in C preserved by phi give the filteredness condition. Record a high-weight limit-direction alarm.
17. The universal cocontinuous-functor restriction property follows from density. The converses at314–324 need exactly what 'generating by strict epimorphisms' means, and the condition on existing colimits must be stated with its result lying in C. These claims overlap161-1 scans18–20 and are not resolved by the false cofinality lemma there. For a topos and lex dense C, the extension is lex, hence presents the topos as a subtopos of PSh(C).
18. The question351 asks whether every cocomplete bounded category is accessible. This is a genuine set-theoretic issue, related to Vopěnka's principle; it is not disposed of by the source's margin353. Its proposed Set^op example with a strict cogenerator2 does not prove the required small density. In fact maps between power-set functors preserving complement/constants need not preserve intersections; the single object2 is not codense in Set. Nonprincipal sufficiently complete ultrafilters show why larger small test subcategories involve measurable-cardinal assumptions. Exact primary sources on boundedness/Vopěnka have been located for opening. The source's 'only singleton objects accessible in Set^op' also needs the empty-object edge case checked.
19. The conclusion363 about free change-of-structure functors is valid for accessible limit-preserving model functors between locally presentable categories. A merely accessible category need not be complete. The separately continuous bifunctor/tensor-product statement365–369 matches the tensor product of locally presentable categories, under those hypotheses; it does not follow for arbitrary accessible categories with unspecified limits.

## Scans60–62: corrected Ind theorem and a concrete filtration counterexample

20. The precise Ind_pi lemma373–392 is sound: pi-directed colimits exist, pi-presentables are retracts of C, C is dense, and C/X is pi-directed. The converse includes all three conditions and explicitly notes that cocompleteness implies the third. This supersedes the earlier abbreviated equivalence.
21. An object represented by a pi-directed diagram with a cofinal set of cardinal≤pi' is pi'-presentable. For pi'=2^c with c≥pi, closure under pi-small subsets gives the reverse inclusion. The open question402 has a ZFC counterexample even with E=Set: take pi=aleph0, C a skeleton of countable sets, E=Ind_aleph1(C)=Set, and pi'=lambda>aleph0 singular of cofinality omega. A set X of size lambda is lambda^+-presentable, but no aleph1-directed presentation by countable sets has a cofinal indexing set of size≤lambda. Otherwise their images give a cofinal family of≤lambda countable subsets of X. Enumerate it in lambda stages and choose x_n outside the union of its first lambda_n members, for lambda_n increasing to lambda. The countable set {x_n} lies in none of the listed subsets, contradiction. Thus Fil^lambda(E)≠E_lambda, contrary to the unconditional equality suggested at412.
22. The proof427–433 changes 'pi-directed' subdiagrams to merely filtered subdiagrams. Those colimits need not lie in Ind_pi(C), and even when E is cocomplete they need not have a bounded pi-directed presentation. The preceding counterexample pinpoints this gap. The cardinal bound |J|≤(pi'')^(pi') is valid as a count of subsets; it does not imply ≤pi'' without cardinal arithmetic. The handwritten '=(pi''?)' is an actual unresolved question, not an identity.
23. The proof448–460 that presentables are retracts of bounded ordinary filtered colimits is a standard smallness argument when C is finite-colimit closed and E cocomplete. Splitting a retract can be implemented by a countable idempotent chain, giving a bounded ordinary filtered presentation. It does not repair the stronger pi-directed bound just refuted. The local-presentability criterion443 is valid after translating cardinal conventions.
