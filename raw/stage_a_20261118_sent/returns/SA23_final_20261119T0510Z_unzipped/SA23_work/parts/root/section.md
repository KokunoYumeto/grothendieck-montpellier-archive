## Cote 2 — Curves, Chern rings, symmetric tensors, supports, and Riemann–Roch

This is an assembled dossier, not one continuously dated argument. Its retained transcription has 41 scan markers within scans 2–45, with 6, 27 and 31 absent. The title leaves divide it into curve calculations, counterexamples, lambda-ring filtrations, covariant characters with supports, and Riemann–Roch for surfaces. Typed leaves on sesquilinear forms, integral closure and étale cohomology are interleaved with those units. The catalogue’s pre-1958 description cannot date every textual stratum. In particular, the typed étale passages match SGA 4 VII and VIII and refer to EGA IV; those publication dates do not by themselves date circulated drafts or physical reuse. These distinctions are recorded in SA23-46, SA23-80, and the exact published-work links.

### Curves and the preliminary class group

The opening starts with a preliminary sheaf-class ring and relation subgroups. Its notation must not be replaced immediately by the finished K-group. The word “quotient” attached to `Phi_N` conflicts with its later use as a subgroup, and the product convention is not completely determined by the fragment. The assertion that point classes annihilate reduced classes fits alternating Tor multiplication in the final theory: on a smooth curve the two Tor contributions to the product of a point with itself cancel. Ordinary tensor product would give the point sheaf again. This is a diagnostic, not a reconstruction of missing definitions (SA23-2–3, SA23-9; C-SA23-root-002).

The next unit distinguishes vector-bundle K-theory from coherent G-theory on a noetherian curve. With an ample line bundle and dimension at most one, rank and determinant describe K0 by locally constant integers and the Picard group. Its reduced product is square zero. The explicit modern comparison in Pedrini–Weibel, Appendix A p. 404, confirms the dimension-one description; its backward reference is recorded without pretending that the whole earlier reference was read. The generic-length invariants belong to a different coherent description. For `k[ε]/ε^m`, both abstract groups are infinite cyclic but the Cartan map multiplies by `m`. The earlier notes correctly recognize that this map carries information the abstract group names lose (C-SA23-root-005–006).

The splitting-dependent quantity `δ` changes by the weighted sum of changes in the chosen lifts. Its residue modulo the subgroup generated by those weights is independent of the splitting, but this is an earlier-run deduction, not a new canonical construction asserted by Grothendieck. The proposed homological character on the next page is deleted. The reason for deletion is not given; nonnaturality is a possible explanation, not evidence of his motive (MP-SA23-root-14).

### Torsion with trivial total Chern class

The counterexample unit contains a reliable nonvanishing argument: projection after the constant-section pushforward is the identity. Its integral Chern calculation has two different factorials. For `ξ=1−[O(−1)]` on projective space, the leading term is

`c(ξ^r)=1+(−1)^(r−1)(r−1)! h^r`.

For a rank-zero line difference on a smooth curve, with first class `α`, the section pushforward instead has

`c(i_!x)=1+(−1)^r r! α h^r`.

The exact program checks finite coefficients; `proofs.md` R1 proves the identities universally over the integers before specializing to torsion. This avoids using a rational Chern character to prove an integral torsion assertion. Grothendieck’s 1957 report, reprinted in SGA 6, contains the Chern-ring formulas needed for the comparison (C-SA23-root-011).

The live example is a nontrivial 2-torsion line class on an elliptic curve, pushed into its product with P3. The effective-hypersurface setup above it is cancelled. The earlier imported register had retained that abandoned description. The example survives, but its attribution and hypotheses need correction. The earlier notes’ improvement to P2 is also valid: the exact rank-zero coefficient is already even there. Neither an effective torsion divisor on a proper curve nor a claim that P3 is the minimal possible dimension should be attributed to the surviving page (SA23-14–18).

Two smaller distinctions matter. A fixed factorial kills only torsion of appropriate order; the bare existence of torsion does not establish the page’s unrestricted fixed-dimension inference. Also, additive zero in the augmented Chern ring is `(0,1)`. A written zero need not mean that the total Chern series itself is the zero series. These are mathematical convention checks, not permission to change the transcript.

### Equivariant modules and symmetric tensor powers

The next pages organize tensor powers with symmetric-group actions and induction products. The projection formula, associativity and block-swap symmetry are valid; explicit maps and balancing checks appear in `proofs.md` R5. The wording “ring” at the categorical level refers to coherent natural isomorphisms, while the induced Grothendieck-group product is an ordinary commutative product.

Two statements need narrower interpretations. Restriction after induction is not generally a direct sum of identical subgroup representations: for C3 inside S3, a nontrivial character gives the sum of it and its inverse. Serre’s Proposition 22 supplies the exact double-coset statement. Restriction preserves finite freeness of group-algebra modules only with the relevant finite-index hypothesis. These are explicit counterexamples to specified readings, not claims that the abbreviated functor notation has only one possible meaning.

The revised admissibility condition uses trivial locally free modules for Young stabilizers. A local tensor basis decomposes into the corresponding permutation orbits, validating that replacement. The former group-algebra-free condition is too strong even in rank one. The deleted locality wording and the promised completion of exterior operations leave a genuine editorial and proof obligation. Atiyah’s 1966 tensor-power construction is a precise complex topological comparison; it does not settle every integral ringed-topos convention in these leaves (MP-SA23-root-03).

### The chosen geometric family and the gamma filtration

The family of designated geometric elements is part of the input. Enlarging it can shrink the universal Chern ring. This is stated on the pages and tested by the projective-space example. It must remain visible in any comparison with later universal constructions.

The full definition asks for an augmented lambda-ring map. The short relation list on scan 29 is insufficient if treated as exhaustive: the explicit rank-two countermodel satisfies the ring and selected rank data but fails exterior-operation compatibility. Berthelot’s SGA 6 V6.14 visibly includes an additional family of operation relations. This corrects an overly short presentation, while preserving the fuller definition on scan 28.

The factorial comparison is sound under those full hypotheses. Its degree-i kernel is killed by `(i−1)!` for positive `i`, yielding integral isomorphisms in degrees one and two and rational isomorphisms in every degree. Degree zero is separate. The proposition and its proof disagree by a sign; degree one and the universal calculation support the proof’s sign, but the earlier exponent is not visibly cancelled in the supplied text. It remains a scan-review question.

The return map also changes multiplication. For positive homogeneous degrees `i,j`, its coefficient ring has product

`a⋆b=−(i+j−1)! ab / ((i−1)!(j−1)!)`.

The exact coefficient occurs in the printed 1957 report and SGA 6 V6.5.3. Calling the return map a homomorphism into the ordinary graded ring loses the manuscript’s explicit additive qualification.

The free-case proof is supplied integrally in R8 through symmetric Laurent coordinates and weighted gamma generators. It also answers the determinant and filtration-generation questions under their precise hypotheses. The cancelled unshifted gamma expression is different: it fails already on a line class. The shifted expression belongs to the ideal generated by the exterior powers above the rank, directly from the generating series. That mathematical repair does not restore the deleted shift in the transcription.

The question “is the comparison always bijective?” is answered by “Non” on the same sheet. For `K=Z[L]/(L−1)^n` and the chosen family generated by `L`, the degree-n universal class has order `(n−1)!`. At `n=3`, the exact ring is `Z[ξ]/(2ξ³,ξ⁴)`. Adding the geometric class `nL−1` imposes `ξ^n=0`. The program and R2 verify both claims. Mackall’s 2020 Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 4.1 concern a functor with rank conditions for all vector bundles and a projective-bundle pullback axiom. Its factorial theorem is a useful comparison, not an identification of these two universal inputs (MP-SA23-root-04, -06, -07).

### Supports, relative perfection, and covariant characters

The support unit explicitly starts with relative perfection. A complex on a singular infinitesimal neighbourhood may be perfect over the ambient scheme and fail to be intrinsically perfect on that neighbourhood. The periodic resolution of `k` over `k[t]/t^n` makes the difference concrete.

The assertion of full faithfulness is about a filtered limit over all thickenings. Failure at a finite stage does not refute it. R4 proves a precise affine noetherian version using the derived torsion equivalence and finite Yoneda diagrams. The Stacks comparison lemma is applied to finite modules inside the category of all torsion modules; applying it to torsion modules inside all modules would violate its hypotheses. The unqualified prescheme formulation still needs exact category and transition conventions (C-SA23-root-043).

The proposed supported K-group can be nonunital, and the intended Chow target is not determined merely by its image in ordinary Chow. A point in A1 gives a simple distinction. The manuscript’s question about a filtration on this pseudo-ring is therefore a programme requiring definitions, not an already constructed supported theory.

Several parts of the programme have exact later realizations. Alonso–Jeremías–Sancho’s Proposition 3.5 gives proper relative-perfect pushforward without a finite-Tor hypothesis on the intervening proper map. Their Proposition 3.11 requires a Tor-independent classical square for relative base change; arbitrary derived pullback of absolute perfect complexes is a separate statement. Their orientation `[O_X]` is a plausible comparison for the unfinished “canonical element”, but does not identify it.

The localized Chern character multiplied by the ambient Todd class has a particularly close match: Fulton–Gillet’s 1983 Riemann–Roch theorem, property (3) on p. 288. It extends the earlier Baum–Fulton–MacPherson construction to separated finite-type schemes over a field without quasi-projectivity. Thus singular rational Riemann–Roch is established. The still-unspecified integral supported filtration should not be mistaken for that settled theorem (MP-SA23-root-08–13).

The interleaved typed étale fragments have exact matches in SGA 4 VII5.11–5.13 and VIII1.1–1.2. The full invariance theorem includes integrality, absent from the fragment’s surviving antecedent. The continuity statement’s projection-letter typing issue also occurs in the original printed source. The ambiguity between deletion and editorial ruling remains an ink question.

### Curves and surfaces under Riemann–Roch

The final unit starts from the rational GRR diagram and specializes it to immersions, fibrations, finite maps of curves and a curve mapping into a surface. The integral immersion fraction must be interpreted as a universal polynomial quotient before evaluation; the top normal Chern class need not be invertible after evaluation.

Publication authorship is recorded separately from mathematical attribution. The exact Borel–Serre1958 treatments and applications are class B; their authors explicitly present Grothendieck’s theorem. The main rational formula itself also has an exact own-publication match in Grothendieck’s SGA6 Exposé0, formula1.1, printed p1, so that claim remains class A. A chapter written by another seminar participant does not become Grothendieck’s own publication merely because the volume bears his name [C-SA23-root-010,062,066–070; BorelSerre1958; SGA6].

The vector-bundle description of higher fibre cohomology needs additional hypotheses. The Poincaré bundle on an elliptic curve times its Picard variety has jumping fibre cohomology. Relative Serre vanishing supplies higher-direct-image vanishing for large twists, while flatness and base change supply local freeness. The exponential GRR computation gives rational polynomiality of the j-th Chern-character component, of degree at most the relative dimension plus j; it does not imply integral polynomial control of torsion.

For finite maps of smooth curves, the half-canonical expression is a rational Picard-class identity. The determinant/norm formulation retains the integral line-bundle information. Inseparable maps are included; relative Frobenius gives degree `(p−1)(g−1)`. The curve-to-surface formula on scan 44 agrees with the direct degree-two expansion.

The following specialization is wrong as written if it names `O_C` while using the genus of the normalization. It computes the normalization pushforward. The exact sequence with the normalization defect adds the missing zero-cycle. A rational nodal plane cubic gives 8 in the displayed expression and 9 for `c2(O_C)=C²`. This is a precise mathematical alarm with every geometric hypothesis satisfied; whether the symbol was misread or the source itself is wrong awaits the scan (C-SA23-root-071 and -071R).

### Reused algebra leaves

The adjoint target and matrix dimensions are elementary type checks. The scalar polarization identities are valid; their unrestricted bimodule extension cannot be decided without the absent axioms and can fail under a standard convention. The UFD-normality and valuation/localization arguments are standard algebra, checked directly and against exact Stacks statements. None should be absorbed into the lambda-ring narrative merely because it occupies the same physical cote.

The earlier notes are generally careful about these distinctions. The main corrections in this consolidation concern the chronology/deletion labels, the source-entry mapping for the two étale notes, the full universal-property hypotheses, and the fact that unavailable research appendices are not evidence. All calculations used here were rerun. External project links remain reported leads with unavailable endpoints; they are separated from verified archival and published comparisons.
