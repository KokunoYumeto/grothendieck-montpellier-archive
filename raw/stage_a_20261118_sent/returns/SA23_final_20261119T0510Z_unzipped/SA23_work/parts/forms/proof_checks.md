These are present-run arguments and source comparisons, not silently restored manuscript proofs. Claim IDs refer to the owner register. Exact finite calculations are in compute_checks.py and compute_checks.output.json. They verify the displayed identities or counterexamples; the general arguments below supply the wider scope where justified. External references have opened-source locators in references.bib. ## 1. Incidence, generic sections and the invisible component

**C-SA23-forms-001–008; F09.** With the quotient convention, a point of P(E) is a quotient E→L. A hyperplane equation comes from a line in E, hence a quotient of E∨. The universal pairing has bidegree (1,1). Its vanishing locus has a projective-bundle description over either projective space: fix one quotient, and the compatible quotients on the opposite factor come from the kernel of the corresponding evaluation. This local free-module calculation also proves arbitrary base-change compatibility. In degree d the line of equations lies in Sym^d E, so its parameter space is P((Sym^d E)∨); checking d=1 detects the printed missing dual. A relative divisor statement must additionally retain the effective-Cartier and fiberwise-regular hypotheses.

On an affine incidence chart, the equation is a_0+Σa_iS_i=0. On D(a_j), solve for S_j. Thus one variable is eliminated, and the signs of the remaining ratios are negative. The numerical certificate checks this elementary substitution; it is not a proof of geometric irreducibility. For an irreducible X, the total incidence over X is irreducible. Its generic parameter fiber is empty precisely when the image of X is a point; otherwise it is a localization of a domain and dominates X. The geometric assertion requires the primary-field lemma, whose exact modern hypotheses were checked in Stacks 37.32.1–37.32.3. The latter theorem's base-locus and image-dimension assumptions cannot be dropped.

The image in P of the generic hyperplane consists of the nonclosed points. Indeed a closed point has algebraic residue coordinates and is avoided by the generic parameter, whereas the positive-dimensional closure of a nonclosed point meets the generic hyperplane and the incidence calculation dominates that closure. A constructible subset whose image contains only closed points has finite image: a positive-dimensional irreducible component of its closure would contribute its generic point to a dense constructible open. This explains the finite-image criterion without replacing the manuscript's arbitrary-subset version by a stronger constructibility assumption.

The equality assertion in Corollary 2.4 is false if only Z is irreducible. Let f be the identity of P², let Z be a projective line, and let Z′ be that line together with a point outside it. Both have the required positive-dimensional image, and Z is irreducible. Their generic hyperplane pullbacks coincide because the isolated point is avoided. Nevertheless Z≠Z′. If both are irreducible, dominance of the corresponding incidence components recovers containment in both directions. These are separate literal and corrected statements.

The smoothness proof's generic-linear-combination shortcut also fails by itself. On A¹, the sections 1 and x generate a nonzero line bundle, but S_1+S_2x vanishes after extending the coefficient field. The correct Bertini proof imposes both the zero equation and vanishing of its first derivative. For a smooth d-dimensional immersed X, these give d+1 independent linear conditions on hyperplanes at a fixed point. The total singular incidence therefore has dimension one less than the parameter space. Stacks 33.47.3 and SGA7 II, XVII3.1–3.3 give the checked geometric formulations. This refutes the shortcut, not Bertini smoothness.

For connectedness, take the finite graph of irreducible components, joining two components when their intersection has positive-dimensional image. If this graph disconnected, remove the union of intersections across the partition; its image is finite, and the remaining X disconnects. Thus the manuscript's deletion hypothesis forces a connected graph. Each surviving component has a geometrically irreducible generic section and each edge survives, so their union is geometrically connected. The exceptional family must be defined by f(Z), not by the fixed set f(X).

## 2. Depth, parameter loci and finite étale choices

**C-SA23-forms-005,009–018.** The map from the generic incidence to X is a localization of a smooth incidence projection. The flat local depth formula applies, with the finite-type/noetherian support-dimension hypotheses of the setting. Regular fibers add the same amount to depth and local dimension. This explains preservation of codepth and the corresponding Serre/Cohen–Macaulay comparisons. Stacks 31.3.1 gives the exact associated-point formula for a base-flat quasi-coherent sheaf, with equality when the base is locally noetherian; 31.7.3 gives the relative-assassin base-change version. Neither result identifies an undefined historical phrase such as a “separable module” with a modern scheme property.

For a nonzerodivisor t on a finite module M over a noetherian local ring, depth(M/tM)=depth(M)−1. In the relevant support-dimension setting its dimension also drops by one. Consequently a depth-one surface point yields an embedded point in a regular curve section through it, and a depth-two normal threefold point yields a depth-one surface section, failing S_2. Hyperplanes through a fixed closed point form a divisor in the dual projective space. By contrast, hyperplanes containing a positive-dimensional associated support form a linear subspace of codimension at least two. This is a lower bound, not an equality in general.

The same-S_k threshold is also forced numerically: if depth M_x=k and dim M_x≥k+1, every regular section through x has depth k−1 and dimension at least k, so fails S_k. A codimension-two bad-parameter bound must exclude this divisorial family. The manuscript's missing Z_n definitions and damaged iff clause prevent certification of the full general criterion. Benoist 2011, Theorems 1.4–1.6, gives precise later depth obstructions and bounds; those results are partial matches to the damaged formulation.

The unqualified C_(m+1)⇒C_m claim fails when intrinsic dimensions are used for an arbitrary f. Let Z be two projective planes meeting in one point and X=Z×P², projected to Z⊂P^r. Each four-dimensional component stays connected after deleting a subset of dimension at most one; their intersection is P² and is not removed by such a deletion. Thus X has the relevant C_1 property. A generic hyperplane of P^r avoids the common image point, so its pullback is the disjoint union of two nonempty pieces. Requiring quasi-finiteness, or replacing dimensions by image dimensions as the source proposes, addresses this obstruction.

A constructible subset of an irreducible noetherian parameter space contains the generic point exactly when it contains a nonempty open. Over a Jacobson parameter scheme, a constructible set containing every closed point of an open contains the whole open. EGA IV3, 9.7.7, printed p79, is the precise checked finite-presentation constructibility theorem for geometric irreducibility, connectedness, reducedness and integrality. EGA IV3, 12.2.1–12.2.4, pp179–183, supplies the proper-flat openness statements and their additional hypotheses. The elementary proper-image argument explains why proper support turns an open pointwise good locus into an open parameter locus.

For the fiberwise-regularity assertion, tensor the sequence M→M→M/tM with residue fields. Base-flatness of M and fiberwise injectivity make Tor_1 of the cokernel vanish; the local flatness criterion gives flatness of the quotient. The property is open under the finite-presentation assumptions. The letter denoting the flat object matters: flatness of ambient projective space alone says nothing about an arbitrary module F.

For C012, properness and the component hypotheses promote generic geometric connectedness to every fiber over the normal dual projective space. Factor the proper incidence morphism through its finite Stein factor. Each component of the incidence dominates the parameter space, so each component of the Stein factor does too. The geometrically connected generic fiber makes the reduced generic finite algebra a single purely inseparable field extension. Its degree need not be one in positive characteristic. The reduced Stein factor is consequently integral. On a normal affine base A, a finite integral domain B with purely inseparable fraction-field extension has this property: for each b in B some p-power of b belongs to Frac(A); that power is integral over A, hence lies in A. Thus Spec(B)→Spec(A) is radicial, finite and surjective, hence a universal homeomorphism. Nilpotents do not alter geometric connectedness. The geometrically connected fibers of the Stein map now prove the assertion. In characteristic zero the generic extension is trivial. This is a present-run reconstruction; it does not infer degree one from geometric connectedness.

Over an infinite field, a nonempty open of a finite-dimensional affine chart has a rational point by nonvanishing of a finite product of nonzero polynomials. A residue-field point in a Grassmann chart lifts by lifting its finitely many matrix entries and shrinking the base. Over a finite residue field, first choose a closed point of the good open. Its residue extension is finite separable. Choose a monic irreducible separable residue polynomial, lift its coefficients after localizing the base, and invert its discriminant. The resulting finite free algebra is finite étale and has precisely the desired residue field. The lifted parameter remains in the good open after further shrinking. This is stronger than the general étale-neighborhood statement of Stacks 37.35.2, and the explicit monic argument is why it is available here.

Successive regular hyperplanes in a projective family can cut to a finite geometrically reduced residue fiber in the chosen smooth locus. Properness and quasi-finiteness near that fiber make the resulting morphism finite after shrinking; flatness and unramifiedness then make it finite étale. The proof uses projectivity to make the cuts. It does not prove the unrestricted proper statement in 7.4; the adjacent 7.5 warns of nonprojective failures. The intended historical counterexample was not identified, and the cancellation extent remains a scan question.

## 3. Duality, quadratic points, special systems and pencils

**C-SA23-forms-019–031,082–084; F06–F10,F23,F25.** The universal singular-section scheme is cut out by the universal equation and its first derivatives. For smooth X and unramified f, a hyperplane is singular at x exactly when its covector annihilates T_xX. The Euler sequence identifies this incidence with the corresponding projectivized conormal data. The exact normal/conormal dual depends on whether P denotes lines or quotients; the geometry is unchanged, but the displayed bundles must have the right variance. Its dimension for a d-dimensional X in P^r is r−1.

The differential of the conormal projection is controlled by the polar Hessian of the hyperplane section. Its nonsingularity is not equivalent to the ordinary quadratic condition in every characteristic. Over F₂, q=x²+yz defines a smooth projective conic: the gradient forces y=z=0, and q=0 then forces x=0, impossible projectively. Its polar form has radical the x-axis and rank two. The exact certificate displays the rank. Thus the projective tangent quadric is smooth even though the odd-rank polar Hessian is singular. SGA7 II, XVII1.1–1.2 and 3.3 make this distinction explicit. Cote25's early definitions at scans139–141 and later correction at158–160 are kept as separate occurrences.

For an embedded smooth X, differentiating the incidence relation shows that the tangent hyperplane to the dual recovers the original point. In the generically unramified case this gives the birational reflexivity result, with the smooth-locus statement checked in SGA7 XVII3.5. Merely unramified f is insufficient: compose a plane embedding of an elliptic curve with an étale isogeny of degree greater than one in characteristic zero. The pullback tangent hyperplanes still have nondegenerate local tangency, but several points over the same embedded point give the same conormal data; the projection has higher degree. This verifies every hypothesis of the wider unramified formulation and isolates the missing immersion condition.

In characteristic p, the smooth hypersurface ΣX_i^(p+1)=0 has partial derivatives X_i^p. They cannot all vanish projectively, while their differential is zero. The Gauss map is the Frobenius-type map in Katz's Example3.4. F07 checks the formulas for p=2,3,5; the identity for every p follows directly from characteristic p. A smooth quadric surface is classically ruled but has a hypersurface dual, so the draft's temporary word “ruled” cannot be adopted as standard terminology for dual defect.

For special linear systems, removing the base locus and applying generic-section irreducibility controls the moving part only. A fixed divisor remains in every member. For example, pull back plane lines through a point to the blowup of P² at that point: the complete pulled-back divisor has the exceptional curve as a fixed component. A base-locus codimension condition is therefore necessary. The polynomial ratios x²/x=x and xy/x=y (and degree-three analogues) check the prescribed-point/tangent construction's image dimension, but do not supply missing global base-locus assumptions.

The stronger arbitrary-morphism conjecture 8.17(a), now C082, fails even for a smooth proper geometrically integral threefold. Take f:Bl_p(P³)→P³. Every hyperplane through p has pullback equal to the strict transform plus the exceptional divisor. Such parameters form a hyperplane in the dual P³. In one blowup chart x=u,y=uv,z=uw, the pullback of ax+by+cz is u(a+bv+cw); F25 verifies the factorization. Poonen–Slavov Example1.7 records this exact obstruction. Their Theorem1.5 requires equal dimension for all nonempty fibers, while Theorem1.8 separates the exceptional divisors coming from fiber-dimension jumps. Neither theorem verifies the draft with that hypothesis simply omitted.

For C083, a connected reduced proper curve smooth away from at most one ordinary node has at most two irreducible components. Normalization separates its components, and a single node can identify at most two branches. More than two components could not be connected through that point. A tangent plane section of the smooth quadric x0x3−x1x2=0 at [1:0:0:0] is x1x2=0, two lines, showing that divisorial reducible sections can occur for surfaces. The unreadable separability/dual-defect qualifier is not restored from this argument.

For C084, the legible normal-case argument in 8.18 uses an open parameter locus U of integral sections. Away from a subset of codimension at least two, the candidate bad divisors are the hyperplanes parametrizing sections through the finitely many exceptional points and, when it is a divisor, the conormal image. For each such irreducible divisor D, its generic point lies in U exactly when D meets U. This explains why an irreducible member through each exceptional point and an irreducible singular member on the dual divisor are the proposed tests. The adjective singular/nonsingular is damaged, and the normalization step for nonnormal X is not proved by this argument. The source also writes dimension1 where a general section has dimension d−1. These are retained as separate reading and formulation problems.

In the fixed-base projection calculation, the image field is k(a_1,…,a_(p−1)), not K(a_1,…)=K. Projection from a q-dimensional linear subspace has fibers of dimension q+1, giving image dimension at least dim f(X)−q−1. A hypothesis of image dimension exactly2 is too restrictive for the displayed general criterion; the required lower bound is at least2. No missing inequality is silently rewritten in the transcription.

Likewise a line avoiding a merely constructible Z does not force codim Z≥2. In P² take Z=D\{p}, where D is a line, and take a different line through p. It avoids Z although closure(Z)=D has codimension one. The usual projective dimension argument applies to closed Z, or to avoidance of its closure.

For a regular sequence a,b, the relation module of the two generators is generated by (b,−a). The same regular-sequence argument proves that I=(a,b) is of linear type, so its Rees algebra is R[U,V]/(bU−aV). Taking Proj gives the pencil incidence, hence the blowup. Without regularity, the symmetric and Rees algebras can differ. F10 verifies the coordinate substitution; the regular-sequence argument supplies the general statement.

Katz's SGA7 XVII2.2 and 2.5.1–2.5.2 give the exact degree-at-least-two Veronese Lefschetz-pencil theorem. The singularities are ordinary quadratic, with the characteristic-two qualification above; the publication does not supply an everywhere nondegenerate-Hessian strengthening. For curves a local double fiber has algebra k[t]/(t²); characteristic two permits wild quadratic ramification. A unique singular point is rational over the discriminant parameter's residue field, which need not be the original ground field.

## 4. Grassmannians and the chart origin

**C-SA23-forms-032–033,081; F11.** For E=O^r and a chosen decomposition O^r=O^n⊕O^(r−n), the chart of quotients invertible on the first summand is represented by matrices [I_n A]. Its kernel is generated by the columns [-A;I_(r−n)]. This proves the chart dimension n(r−n), smoothness, the point-lifting statement and the polarity formula. F11 checks the matrix composition exactly. The determinant quotient is Λ^nG; it is the line entering the Plücker map.

A locally split exact sequence 0→E′→E→E″→0 supplies a retraction torsor, not a canonically pointed vector bundle. If E″ is finite locally free the acting Hom sheaf is the usual vector bundle. Without local splitting the retraction functor can instead be empty: 0→2Z→Z→Z/2→0 has no retraction near the prime2, even after faithfully flat base change. Identifying a quotient with E′ turns it into a retraction E→E′. The difference of two retractions factors uniquely through E″. Choosing a splitting supplies an origin, and changing the splitting translates it. The manuscript had just chosen basis decompositions, so its chart is valid relative to those choices; the broader “such an exact sequence” wording is the specific ambiguity recorded.

For a countably infinite-dimensional vector space E, P(E)=Proj k[x_1,x_2,…] is not quasi-compact. Its standard open cover D_+(x_i) has no finite subcover: for any finite list, choose a coordinate point supported on an omitted variable. Thus arbitrary quasi-coherent Grassmannian representability does not imply quasi-compactness or finite presentation. These are additional finiteness conclusions. Empty rank-n quotient functors with n>rank E are also excluded from the connected-fiber statement.

## 5. Category cohomology and the two independent Whitehead failures

**C-SA23-forms-034–038,080; F21,F22,F24.** A local system on a small category sends every arrow to an isomorphism. Requiring this only of isomorphisms imposes no condition at all. The cochain resolution for category cohomology takes products over chains of arrows; an infinite discrete category already forces products in degree zero. Nonlocal coefficient functors still have derived limits, although those are not local-system cohomology. The two manuscript margins correcting these points are retained.

A category's nerve recovers its objects, arrows and composition through its strict Segal maps. A category and its opposite have homeomorphic realizations by reversing both chains and barycentric coordinates. The category of simplices supplies a weak-equivalence replacement, not ordinary categorification of an arbitrary simplicial set. Thomason1980 pp305–306 explicitly states the localization equivalence; it is separate from his stronger model-structure result.

With components and fundamental groups already identified, all local integral coefficient tests compare universal-cover homology. The simply connected homological Whitehead theorem then gives weak equivalence of CW realizations. Dropping the fundamental-group information fails. Hatcher's finite acyclic two-complex has one vertex, two edges and two relations a^5b^−3 and b^3(ab)^−2. Its boundary matrix has determinant −1, so reduced integral homology vanishes. Its fundamental group maps onto A₅ by elements a,b of orders5,3 with ab of order2. F21 verifies the boundary and this nontrivial quotient. The collapse to a point therefore passes every coefficient system pulled back from that point but is not a weak equivalence. It does not refute a criterion which also compares the full categories of local systems.

The fixed-coefficient converse in C080 fails for a different reason. Let X=M(Z/p,n), n≥2. It is a finite simply connected CW complex, with reduced integral homology Z/p in degree n. Over Q, its reduced cellular differential is multiplication by p and is invertible (F24). Every Q-local system is constant. For the map c:X→point, exactness of the constant-sheaf functor and derived adjunction give

    RHom_X(c*V,c*W) ≃ RHom_Q(V,RΓ(X,c*W)).

The finite cellular calculation gives RΓ(X,c*W)≃W for any Q-vector space W, and vector spaces are projective. Thus constant sheaves have no positive Ext groups against each other, and the constant functor is fully faithful on bounded complexes. A bounded complex with locally constant cohomology is built by finitely many truncation triangles from constant sheaves; induction proves that it lies in the image. Therefore D^b_lc(X;Q)≃D^b(Q), although X is not weakly contractible. One may use a finite polyhedral model and its face category to express the same example with a category nerve. This refutes the expressly stated “any fixed nonzero ring” converse. Quantifying over integral or all coefficients is a stronger test.

For C085, let D be the ordered set of all integers. Filtered colimit over D^op is left adjoint to the constant-presheaf functor, preserves finite limits and all colimits, and therefore defines a geometric point of Psh(D). For every integer m the colimit of the representable y(m) is a singleton: sufficiently small indices lie below m. Evaluation at any chosen n instead sends y(n−1) to the empty set. Thus this point does not come from any functor from the terminal category to D. This proves failure of fullness, precisely the doubtful assertion on the pages, without claiming that all natural-transformation faithfulness is lost. SGA4IV Exercise7.6 supplies the checked essential/representable-point framework.

Finally D^b is not generally closed under derived tensor product. For R=k[ε]/(ε²), the periodic free resolution of k has all differentials multiplication by ε. Its kernel equals its image, but after tensoring with k every differential becomes zero. Hence Tor_i^R(k,k)=k for all i≥0. The manuscript's replacement by D^−, or a restriction to finite Tor-amplitude objects, is necessary.

## 6. Multiple categories, Picard objects and loop-space coherence

**C-SA23-forms-039–044; F22.** A double category is encoded by a bisimplicial set satisfying the strict Segal isomorphisms separately in each direction. Recover horizontal and vertical arrows in degrees(1,0),(0,1), squares in(1,1), and composition from the inverse Segal isomorphisms. Simplicial identities give units and associativity; compatibility of the two simplicial directions gives interchange. Iteration gives n-fold categories. A globular interpretation additionally requires specified object directions to be discrete.

An internal category in abelian groups is described by C_0 and C_1=ker(source), with differential target restricted to C_1. Every arrow decomposes uniquely as its source identity plus an element of C_1, and composition is addition of the latter terms. This proves the two-term complex correspondence. Iterated internal categories first produce multicomplex data; the globularity restrictions remove the extra directions. Derived Hom requires resolutions: Ext¹_Z(Z/2,Z/2)=Z/2 is not seen by ordinary Hom between complexes concentrated in degree zero.

A symmetric Picard groupoid need not be strictly commutative. In superlines over a field of characteristic different from two, the self-braiding of an odd line is multiplication by −1. That invariant cannot become the identity under a symmetric monoidal equivalence. Hopkins–Singer Appendix B.2 is the checked primary treatment separating the strictly commutative complex model from the general Picard object.

For the negative loop-space question, the exact external result used is Stasheff1963 Theorem17, pp282–283: choose its p=5,n=3 example, which has an A4 structure but no A5 structure. It is connected and homotopy associative, while a loop-space homotopy type would admit all higher structures. Connected homotopy-associative H-spaces have homotopy inverses: the shear (x,y)↦(x,xy) is a weak equivalence by its triangular action on homotopy groups, and an inverse evaluated on (x,e) supplies division. Thus the counterexample also addresses ordinary homotopy-category group objects. The exact example predates the1968 notes. May's positive recognition theorem requires the coherent A-infinity structure.

Quillen's Q-construction and Segal's Γ-space construction are later concrete developments of the K-theory programme, using different input structures. Schlichting's checked counterexample concerns a K-theory invariant required to agree with the relevant models and satisfy localization; it is not a theorem that no conceivable invariant of triangulated categories exists. The enhancement and exact-structure qualifications remain essential.

## 7. Unoriented bordism, splitting and correspondences

**C-SA23-forms-045–054; F19,F20.** The geometric relations give pullback, external product and proper Gysin operations with the dimension shift. Transverse representatives and bordisms supply their functorial relations. The universal-property assertion depends on the full stated package of operations and relations; omitting those axioms would not define the target category uniquely.

Thom's coefficient computation is Λ=MO_*=F₂[x_i : i>0, i≠2^r−1], with deg x_i=i. The reversed margin is contradicted already by MO_2 containing [RP²]. Additive splitting as an Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectrum is not by itself a statement about multiplication. Szymik Theorem1.3 gives the precise Boardman homotopy-ring splitting; Proposition5.1 and Theorem5.6 distinguish the incompatible E-infinity strengthening. The original Boardman DOI was inaccessible and its proof is not claimed read here.

An all-Gysin-compatible unital section of ordinary cohomology into MO cannot exist. Let p:RP²→point. In ordinary cohomology, p_*(1) lies in H^−2(point;F₂)=0. In MO it is [RP²]≠0: the Stiefel–Whitney number ⟨w_1²,[RP²]⟩=1 detects it. F19 computes w(TRP²)=(1+a)^3 mod(a³,2). A section commuting with p_* would identify zero with this nonzero class.

The same calculation refutes the literal assertion that the dual of every embedded submanifold is annihilated by positive Steenrod squares. The embedded RP¹⊂RP² has mod2 Poincaré dual a, but Sq¹(a)=a²≠0. A framing or a specific normal-bundle restriction would change the question, but none is silently inserted into S(X).

For closed smooth manifolds, MO Poincaré duality and the finite-free MO Künneth theorem identify MO^*(X×Y), with its dimension shift, with Hom_Λ(MO^*(X),MO^*(Y)). Composition of correspondences becomes contraction against the integration pairing. Atiyah1961 Theorem3.6 and its following remark give the checked geometric duality foundation; the MO adaptation and the finite-free algebra step are stated here explicitly. Splitting idempotents and adjoining shifts then gives finite graded projective modules because the point supplies Λ. This does not prove the separate proposed realization of every Steenrod-group representation.

Coherence of Λ follows directly from finite data. Every finite matrix over Λ uses only finitely many variables, hence descends to a polynomial ring in finitely many variables, which is noetherian. The extension to Λ is flat. Kernels of such matrices therefore come from finitely generated kernels over that finite-variable ring. This is the finite-presentation criterion for coherence; it does not imply Λ is noetherian.

Thus finitely presented Λ-modules form an abelian category, and closure of finite free modules under kernels and cokernels inside Λ-modules produces that category. This verifies the concrete reading of the expected abelian envelope in C051/MP68. An abstract universal abelian-envelope assertion needs a specified category of target functors and is not inferred from coherence alone. The source’s deleted noetherian question and its coherence answer remain distinct from that deferred universal claim.

For C054/MP69, the checked modern orientation framework is Ando–Blumberg–Gepner–Hopkins–Rezk, Introduction1.2–1.3 and Theorems4.2/4.6 with Corollary4.7. A spectrum map to bgl1(R) supplies the coherent commutative Thom-algebra construction in their stated setting, and the orientation mapping space is identified with the relevant space of lifts. Associative input yields an R-module Thom spectrum and does not by itself supply the commutative algebra conclusion. These exact hypotheses explain why a bare parenthetical request that H be commutative, associative and unital is insufficiently specified. They give a sufficient modern model, not a proof that its axioms are minimal for the1968 bordism programme.

Milnor's p=2 dual algebra has polynomial generators of degrees2^i−1. Composing strict additive series gives the displayed coproduct: the coefficient of x^(2^n) in f∘g is Σ a_i b_(n−i)^(2^i). F20 checks the first three terms. Swapping the action convention swaps the order and tensor factors. The infinite polynomial coordinate ring represents a graded strict-automorphism functor, not a finite-type algebraic group. The source's stronger categorical realization programme remains distinct from this Hopf-algebra identity.

## 8. Quillen's positivity paper

**C-SA23-forms-055–057; F12–F17.** The attached paper is a published primary text, read through all six pages. Its theorem concerns a Hermitian form on Sym^m(T)⊗V which is positive on every ζ^m⊗v with both factors nonzero. It is not restricted to a scalar polynomial. Gaussian integration gives the monomial norm α!, and the weighted sum of derivative norms gives the multi-index Vandermonde coefficient binom(k,q). F12 checks the exact combinatorics across finite degrees; the general identity is the coefficient of t^q in Π(1+t)^(α_i)=(1+t)^k. F13 checks the matrix coefficient δ!ε!/γ! appearing on both sides of the Gaussian prolongation identity.

The normal-ordering formulas follow from [D,x]=1 and induction; F14 checks both signs on a spanning family of monomials. The multiplication-weighted norm ratio is binom(k+n+q−1,q), obtained by the generating series for weak compositions. Thus a constant independent of n is impossible if n varies, but n is fixed in the paper. The estimate only needs a constant uniform in k for fixed n,q. A lower-order error term written with binom(N+i,i) instead of binom(N+m,i) is absorbed by increasing its uniform constant; it does not change the positive leading term of degree m. F15 and F17 record these facts.

The concrete scalar form x²−xy+y² becomes coefficientwise positive after multiplication by (x+y)^3; the coefficients are1,2,1,1,2,1. F16 checks the threshold in the displayed finite examples. This illustrates the theorem, not a general algorithmic bound. Corollary2 is a differential sum-of-squares statement, not a difference-of-squares statement. Corollary3 uses the homogeneous ideal argument and the usual extra-variable reduction to yield the complex Nullstellensatz. The vector-valued theorem, its operator estimate and these two different corollaries are kept separate.

## 9. The conditional étale route to Adams

**C-SA23-forms-058–063; F18.** The published1968 sketch explicitly leaves its bundle-fibration construction conjectural. Its Frobenius identity in K-theory follows from the splitting principle and F*L=L^p; a rank-n affine Frobenius has degree p^n. The Grassmannian convention using N-planes in an(n+N)-space has a quotient bundle of rank n, so those labels do not alone constitute a transcription error.

The obstructing sphere data must be completed away from residue characteristics. Friedlander1973 Theorem5.3, pp37–38, was checked with rank at least two, connected noetherian pointed base, profinite fundamental group and the allowed prime set. Its additional pro-L hypothesis for a long exact homotopy sequence is not omitted. Proposition6.6 and Theorem6.8 give the later complex Adams application. They do not automatically settle every stronger representability conjecture about the pro-sphere functor. Quillen1971 expressly gives a different proof avoiding the earlier étale construction.

For the final K_0 identity, use the two exact sequences as written in the published paper. From 0→E_3→O^n→L^k⊗E_2∨→0, dualize and tensor by L^k to obtain [E_2]=n[L^k]−[L^k⊗E_3∨]. From 0→E_4→O^m→L^k→0, obtain [L^k]=m−[E_4]. Therefore

    [E_1]−[E_2]=[E_1⊕(L^k⊗E_3∨)⊕E_4^⊕n]−nm.

F18 verifies the formal identity. The noetherian ample-line hypothesis supplies a large k for the required global generation. This is why a dual or tensor-product transcription error in the final line matters.

## 10. Formal categories, Lie algebroids and integrability

**C-SA23-forms-064–074; F01–F05.** Modulo I², the difference of source and target maps satisfies the Leibniz rule: (f+df)(g+dg)−fg=f dg+g df modulo products of first-order terms. F04 checks this. Iterated commutators with multiplication by functions lower the I-adic order of a continuous distribution, giving the differential-operator filtration. A full continuous-dual algebra statement still requires the topology, balanced tensor products and finite-projectivity assumptions recorded in the claim.

For a one-object complete augmented formal monoid, the usual filtered convolution inverse is constructed recursively from the unit term. F05 checks the inverse of x+y+xy to order eight. This finite example does not prove the general multi-object formal-groupoid assertion: source and target balancing and convergence must be specified. No exact flat-conormal theorem is inferred from the example.

The exterior differential gap is decisive even over Q with a finite free module. Set d on scalars to zero, de_1=e_1∧e_2, de_2=e_2∧e_3 and de_3=0, and extend by graded Leibniz. Then d_1d_0=0, but d²e_1=−e_1∧e_2∧e_3. F01 calculates this exactly. Thus checking square-zero only on degree zero does not make the entire exterior derivation a differential.

For a finite locally free module, duality identifies the degree-zero rule with the anchor and the degree-one rule with

    (dω)(X,Y)=a(X)ω(Y)−a(Y)ω(X)−ω([X,Y]).

Expanding d(fω) gives the scalar bracket rule. Expanding d²f gives compatibility of anchor and bracket. Once that holds, d²ω paired with X,Y,Z is the negative of the Jacobiator, up to the fixed contraction convention. F02 shows the nonzero Jacobiator dual to F01. Therefore the manuscript's “Pourquoi zéro?” is explained by a genuinely absent degree-one square-zero condition; changing a sign alone cannot solve it. Conversely the full Lie–Rinehart axioms give the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential. Finite local freeness is what identifies Λ(T∨) with Hom(ΛT,O).

A connection extends to forms with the graded sign (−1)^degω. Its square is O-linear curvature. In degree zero, expansion gives [∇_X,∇_Y]−∇_[X,Y]; reversing the overall sign changes no vanishing condition. The first-order term of a stratification is a connection, and the second-order cocycle forces this curvature to vanish. Stacks60.15.1, tag07J6, supplies the complete checked construction. On A²_Q, the rank-one connection d+x dy has curvature dx∧dy≠0 (F03), so the final all-connections statement fails even with smoothness, characteristic zero and finite free differentials. “Integrable” is the necessary qualifier, subject to the ink check.

The finite-projective integration comparison has exact later sources: Huebschmann Theorem2.1 restates the projective PBW theorem; Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum9.2.2 gives a classical formally smooth groupoid equivalence with locally projective countably generated tangent modules. The affine Hopf-algebroid integration paper by Ardizzoni–El Kaoutit–Saracco constructs adjunctions in a different category. These results do not establish the manuscript's precise flat-conormal-only, completed, underived equivalence. Its exact1968 attribution, its comparison complex and the proposed crystalline-topos reduction remain bounded literature-identification questions, not assertions of present-day open conjectures. A PD variant additionally requires comultiplication to respect the intended divided-power structures; a bare final heading does not specify that theorem.
