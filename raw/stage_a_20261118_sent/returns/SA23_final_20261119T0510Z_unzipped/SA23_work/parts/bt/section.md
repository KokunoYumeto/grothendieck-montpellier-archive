### A precise published counterpart for the de Rham construction

The strongest identification is Grothendieck's *Crystals and the De Rham Cohomology of Schemes*, §3.4, pp.326–329. It contains the same square-zero isomorphism torsor, its extension of the constant integers, the two-term complex in degrees−1,0, the resulting derived morphism and transitivity. The lectures were delivered in November–December1966 and published in1968. Because these archival leaves are undated in the supplied evidence, this is an exact match in his published work, without an unsupported claim about the leaves' chronological priority. See claims022–031 and `bt_GrothCrystals1968`.

The published formulation also clarifies three mathematical problems in the transcription: the abstract relation is additive in the Hom complex; the resolution has degrees−1,0; and the coefficient unit maps **to** derived Hom, rather than being universally isomorphic to it. The local resolution is a direct sum of the unit and a contractible complex, so its tensor quasi-isomorphism needs no invented flatness hypothesis. The relative de Rham complex belongs to the appropriate base-linear category, since its differential is generally not O_X-linear. These are verified mathematical reconstructions, not changes to the archival reading.

Grothendieck's §§1.2–1.3 and6.2–6.5 also provide the hypercohomology/Stein distinction and the characteristic-zero formal-Poincaré framework. EGA IV₄, §16.8.1–16.8.4, pp.39–41, supplies the precise principal-parts representation of differential operators. Neither source uniquely identifies the sheet's quoted finite-order “formally exact” hypothesis. That residual problem is classF where its quantifiers cannot be recovered, rather than a claim that an undeveloped theorem has been discovered.

### Corrections that affect the mathematical conclusions

The notes' principal counterexamples survive direct checking. Horizontal endpoints do not force a path integral to vanish: on a product affine line, ω=dx, D=identity and constant sections0,1 give μ=1 although all cycle periods vanish and the cohomology class is zero. Two intersecting sections a(s)=0 and b(s)=s cannot both be horizontal for one connection at their intersection. The deleted relative-closedness argument also fails: t dx pulls back along x=u to t du, whose absolute differential is dt∧du. These are reader-diagnosed issues; only the last argument is visibly withdrawn by the author. Claims009–014 retain that distinction.

In Serre's printed proof, the torsion ramification lemma is **completed** after its temporary admission. Its valuation threshold is strict, and the formal multiplicative group at p=2 gives cancellation at equality. The coprime-weight determinant, positive Laurent-polynomial support argument, and exceptional root-system dimensions have been checked, with executable outputs. These verify the printed proof's computations without treating finite experiments as universal proofs.

The handwritten marginal `M=I_alg` requires the connected component: a ramified quadratic character over Q₃ is Hodge–Tate of weight zero, so its Hodge cocharacter is trivial, while its inertia envelope is μ₂. Serre's later exposition states the neutral-component theorem. The course summary separately assumes an algebraically closed residue field; its full-image and weight-zero conclusions must retain that standing hypothesis. See claims041–042 and056–057, and `bt_Serre1979`, Theorem2/Corollary3, pp.159–160.

### What became of the p-adic questions

The ramification/Lie-filtration question is answered by Sen's1972 bounded-shift theorem, checked in the exact formulation of Fontaine–Ouyang A.85. The equal-characteristic full-faithfulness hope is answered in the normal Noetherian integral setting by de Jong's1998 theorem. The1999 erratum corrects a semistable-reduction lemma and definition, explicitly leaving the other results intact; its later digitization date is not its original publication date. The Serre–Tate passage concerns unfinished details of an alternative proof method, while the theorem itself was already known. Katz's Theorem1.2.1 and Theorem2.1 give the precise later deformation equivalence and ordinary canonical-lift parameters.

The handwritten tensor-extension question needs a sharper category distinction. Its literal generator is **all** two-weight Hodge–Tate objects, whereas the actual BT image is crystalline. A Tate curve with v(q)>0 lies in the former and is not crystalline. Liu's all-prime BT correspondence therefore settles the standard crystalline version, not automatically the literal larger-category question. Fontaine–Ouyang's admissibility tensor theorem gives the correct restricted period-functor framework. This is not a proof that every differently specified abstract extension functor is impossible.

For the final nonabelian question, the neutral fibre of restriction is normalized cocycles modulo A^π; full injectivity requires the corresponding statement after twisting. An exact A₅ computation demonstrates the difference. The stabilizer of a full splitting is A^Γ, explaining the local replacement of “torsor” by “homogeneous space.” Bare rank-one models retain nontrivial Frobenius multipliers and fail uniqueness. Those models are explicitly reconstructed tests: the source does not sufficiently fix Γ, its subgroupπ, the coefficient object or admissibility to identify one final universal theorem.
