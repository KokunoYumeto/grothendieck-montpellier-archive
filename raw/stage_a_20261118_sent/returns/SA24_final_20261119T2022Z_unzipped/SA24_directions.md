## 1. The acyclic counit: what additional hypothesis closes the handwritten proof?

**Sources:** items SA24-15, SA24-27, SA24-30, SA24-33, SA24-34, SA24-35; claims CL-SA24-030, CL-SA24-041, CL-SA24-042, CL-SA24-045, CL-SA24-048; problems MP-SA24-07 and MP-SA24-11.

**Known.** The bicomplex lemma, degree-zero calculation and formal projective-object reduction are valid in the recorded scopes. Additivity of a functor on chain complexes alone does not show that it preserves chain homotopies. Establishing the needed homotopy compatibility for FG would settle the test marked “A prouver”. Hubert’s all-complex criterion and strong-Whitehead test-category theorem give precise comparison points, with different hypotheses.

**Would establish.** A minimal, explicitly typed sufficient hypothesis for the remaining acyclic-complex counit test, or an example separating the handwritten assumptions from that test. The deliverable should identify which condition is mathematically new rather than restating the desired conclusion.

**First concrete step.** Put scans 48–49, 53–56 and Hubert2025 Proposition 4.2.5/Corollary 4.2.6 into a single dependency table. For each implication specify the category, exactness, boundedness and preservation property. Only then test the smallest missing implication. A complete comparison table is a successful run even if the general theorem remains undecided.

## 2. Universal tensor acyclicity of the interval

**Sources:** items SA24-37, SA24-38, SA24-40, SA24-75, SA24-77, SA24-79; claims CL-SA24-051, CL-SA24-052, CL-SA24-097, CL-SA24-098, CL-SA24-099; problems MP-SA24-12, MP-SA24-13 and MP-SA24-15.

**Known.** Global contractibility of A/I is insufficient even for a separated multiplicative interval; the finite tree example checks the written weaker hypotheses. Adding a higher-homology summand refutes the literal transfer assertion. Tensorwise two-out-of-three repairs transfer when the comparison itself is universally weak. The scan-86 Ext criterion is conditional on a specified exact sequence.

**Would establish.** A precise relative/universal definition that makes the augmentation-kernel tensor argument work, together with a clean separation between interval existence, endpoint compatibility, and the Ext obstruction to the proposed lift.

**First concrete step.** Transcribe the candidate predicates as maps in named categories and compare them on the existing finite examples. Identify the exact short exact sequence needed on scan 86 before doing any Ext calculation. A scan-reading decision on “sur e” belongs to the later image-based run; this task should keep both readings explicit.

## 3. The cofinality implication chart

**Sources:** items SA24-53, SA24-55, SA24-61, SA24-65, SA24-66, SA24-67; claims CL-SA24-070, CL-SA24-071, CL-SA24-076, CL-SA24-082, CL-SA24-083, CL-SA24-084, CL-SA24-085, CL-SA24-086; problems MP-SA24-24, MP-SA24-25 and MP-SA24-19.

**Known.** Representable testing gives the derived comparison under the stated acyclicity hypotheses. The BZ→e example separates an ordinary isomorphic counit from a homology equivalence. Preservation and reflection of weak maps do not themselves yield an equivalence of localizations.

**Would establish.** A corrected, variance-sensitive implication diagram for the exact page, with every extra assumption attached to its arrow and every failed unrestricted implication supplied with a checked example.

**First concrete step.** Give names to the ordinary and derived versions of each functor on scan 75, then compare the resulting statements with Hubert2026 Proposition 4.6 and the earlier category-of-elements formula. ## 4. The binomial thread from the handwritten calculation to integral cochains

**Sources:** items SA24-64 and SA24-87; claims CL-SA24-080 and CL-SA24-081; problem MP-SA24-18.

**Known.** The degree-two polynomial identity is classical and is not valid as an integral-coefficient polynomial law without the needed division by 2. The exact K(Z,n) binomial/cochain comparison, for n≥1, has been established by Horel and in derived form by Kubrak–Shuklin–Zakharov. Broader reconstruction requires their connected nilpotent finite-type hypotheses.

**Would establish.** A precise explanatory bridge from the local formula and PS VI §119 to the modern canonical map, with ordinary polynomial laws, integer-valued numerical functions and derived binomial constructions distinguished.

**First concrete step.** Write the n=1 canonical map and its normalization conventions on one page, then align the theorem numbers and hypotheses across the public PS text, Ekedahl, Horel and KSZ. This is a high-confidence commentary task; it should not advertise the already established comparison as an unsolved research problem.

## 5. The sheaf Picard σ-realization assertion

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claims CL-SA24-102, CL-SA24-111 and CL-SA24-112; problem MP-SA24-30.

**Known.** Ordinary abelian groups realize all σ by a basis construction. Breen’s exact sequence identifies the sheaf obstruction in Ext³. Neither the presence of that target nor the punctual collapse settles the universal boundary. The dossier’s recorded search did not verify the manuscript’s universal Ext-vanishing claim or a counterexample to it.

**Would establish.** An exact identification of the asserted universal classifying topos and its Ext calculation, or a precise published result settling the same boundary. This is an identification task before it is a claim of a new open problem.

**First concrete step.** Write the universal object, topology, coefficient sheaves and pinning conventions explicitly. Compare the resulting connecting map with Breen1994 (7.9.2), then follow its construction backward. Do not use Ext³≠0 as a substitute for evaluating the obstruction.

## 6. Current homotopy-hypothesis theorem: conventions and proof scope

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claims CL-SA24-004, CL-SA24-007, CL-SA24-011, CL-SA24-012, CL-SA24-014; problems MP-SA24-02 and MP-SA24-05.

Henry–Lanari establishes the specified n=3 result. OpenAI2026HH claims the all-dimensional Ara–Henry result; its linked formalization has a narrower elementary-expansion scope.

**Would establish.** A defensible statement of exactly which manuscript formulation is covered, which later claims are asserted by the preprint, and which proof steps would need independent verification. The task should separate source matching, mathematical proof review and formal-build verification.

**First concrete step.** Build a definition-by-definition comparison of the globular extensions, admissible pairs, weak equivalences and truncation functors in the manuscript and the cited theorem. Select one bounded lemma only after that comparison; do not promise to audit the full proof in a few hours.

## 7. Faithfulness, irredundancy and relative rigidity

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claims CL-SA24-008, CL-SA24-010, CL-SA24-016; problem MP-SA24-03.

**Known.** Bourke proves cellular connecting-map faithfulness. The manuscript withdraws a sign-group automorphism guess and doubts faithfulness of a chosen topological realization. None of these gives a definition-independent measure of operation order.

**Would establish.** Three separately stated questions with explicit domains and morphism conventions, plus a low-dimensional example showing whether any implication between them is valid.

**First concrete step.** Choose the precise free stage described on scans 107–109 and define what it means to fix the primitive subcategory. Compute its first nontrivial endomorphisms without changing the meaning of realization faithfulness. If the page does not determine the presentation, report that boundary rather than inventing a canonical one.

## 8. Mapping categorical groups: Z¹, H¹ and the extra modification level

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claims CL-SA24-104, CL-SA24-106, CL-SA24-113, CL-SA24-114; problems MP-SA24-28 and MP-SA24-31.

**Known.** Monoidal functor constraints, natural transformations and possible modifications occupy different cochain degrees. The ordinary self-equivalence groupoid has Z¹ at the identity, while quotienting by 0-cochains belongs to an additional higher structure. The source itself changes a torsor to a pseudo-torsor in another passage.

**Would establish.** A single conventionally consistent dictionary between pinned skeletal categories, mapping groupoids, gerbes on BG and the cochain complex used for the obstruction/pseudo-torsor statements.

**First concrete step.** Write the complete object/arrow/2-arrow equations for the split case with a nontrivial action, using the C₂/C₃ example as a discrimination test. Then state exactly where an H⁰ level appears. This would make several folder-commentary passages reusable without changing the manuscript’s claims.

## 9. Higher Picard strictness and complexes

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claims CL-SA24-115, CL-SA24-118, CL-SA24-121, CL-SA24-122, CL-SA24-123; problems MP-SA24-32, MP-SA24-33 and MP-SA24-36.

**Known.** Deligne’s two-term complex theorem, Tatar’s Picard-2-stack theorem and the stable 2-type theorem use different strictness and enhancement conventions. A strict Gray/permutative structure can retain nontrivial stable information that strict self-commutativity would remove.

**Would establish.** A dimensional comparison table giving the algebraic model, morphisms, local/global setting and lost or retained invariants for n=1 and n=2. General all-n claims should remain separately marked.

**First concrete step.** Align the exact definitions in SGA4XVIII §1.4, Tatar2011 Theorem 6.4 and GJO2019 Theorem 6.1. Track one nonzero self-symmetry example through each applicable model. A complete definition table is the bounded outcome; no all-dimensional extension is assumed.

## 10. Picard envelopes, η and the stabilization correction

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claims CL-SA24-126, CL-SA24-131, CL-SA24-132, CL-SA24-133, CL-SA24-136, CL-SA24-137; problems MP-SA24-37, MP-SA24-40 and MP-SA24-41.

**Known.** Object inversion and groupoidification have different universal properties. The projective-module core gives K₀/K₁ data with a transposition/self-symmetry invariant that can be nontrivial, for example over Z. Ordinary suspension stabilization for S¹ satisfies the cyclic-three-permutation criterion, contradicting the source’s proposed example but preserving its conditional argument.

**Would establish.** A companion account in which the category being localized and the exact morphism class are always visible, explaining both the low K-theory interpretation and the corrected stabilization example.

**First concrete step.** Put the two universal properties and the functor G in one typed diagram, then specialize separately to the projective-module core and to pointed homotopy types under smash. Compare with Muro–Tonks and Voevodsky/Robalo. Do not identify the all-space ordinary stabilization with the entire spectrum category.

## 11. Which ring-category cohomology classifies the page’s structure?

**Sources:** item IDs: none, Codex-only; claim CL-SA24-134; problem MP-SA24-39.

**Known.** Third Mac Lane cohomology classifies the stated ordinary categorical-ring convention. Braided Ann-categories use a related abelian-cohomological formulation. Symmetric multiplication adds a constraint on β, and strictness choices can alter which cocycles are allowed.

**Would establish.** An exact classification statement for the manuscript’s two symmetric operations and distributivity constraints, with hypotheses matched to one of the opened primary formulations.

**First concrete step.** List the actual coherence diagrams demanded by the page and translate them into the normalization used by Jibladze–Pirashvili and Quang–Hanh. Test the symmetric β condition on the smallest nontrivial ring/module pair. The task ends with a matched theorem or a precisely identified mismatch, not a broad analogy.
