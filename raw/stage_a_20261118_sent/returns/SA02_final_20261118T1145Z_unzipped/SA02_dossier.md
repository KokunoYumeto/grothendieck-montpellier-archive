## What the three folders contain and how the work develops

### Cote 84 — quadratic forms and quadratic-algebra structures

The first interval (scans 97–145) moves from extension duality and quadratic forms into products of quadratic algebras, relative trace and norm constructions, intermediate algebras, invariant rings and fixed-ring criteria. Its later pages turn to χ-split extensions, compatibility diagrams, tensor operations, splitting conventions and comparison maps. The notes repeatedly distinguish abstract algebraic constructions from their realization inside tensor products, and distinguish a statement under faithful/flat/injective hypotheses from one intended over arbitrary base maps.

The continuation (scans 146–193) develops the χ-extension comparison problem and then returns to quadratic-algebra products. The note package reports a local product formula and discriminant calculation on scans 172 and 192. The exact formula has a close published counterpart in John Voight, “Discriminants and the Monoid of Quadratic Rings,” *Pacific Journal of Mathematics* 283 (2016), Construction 3.14, formula (3.15), pp. 503–504; the discriminant multiplicativity is proved in Lemma 3.23, p. 505. The article gives, for free quadratic algebras `S=R⊕Rx`, `T=R⊕Ry` with `x²=tx−n`, `y²=sy−m`, the product `S*T=R⊕Rw` with `w²=(st)w−(mt²+ns²−4nm)`. The manuscript-note claim of a generator-shift match is a lead supported by the note package; exact equivalence for each manuscript occurrence still needs a source-by-source comparison. The published construction itself also cautions that the algebra generated by the natural tensor element need not be free of rank two, so abstract product and tensor realization must not be conflated.

The notes identify a finite `Z/4` counterexample separating an earlier multiplicativity axiom from a later five-axiom recap; a kernel formula for the tensor realization in a monic local model; a rank-one local χ-groupoid calculation; and a sign obstruction for a fixed endpoint convention. These are findings of the supplied note runs, not fresh proofs performed here. The note authors explicitly distinguish their calculations from Grothendieck's manuscript statements.

### Cote 90 — `KG (X)`

The supplied leaves concern saturation of modules, finite saturated submodules, dual-algebra and projective-cover constructions, graded-section functors, and changes in finiteness/countability hypotheses. The notes flag several arguments as deferred or requiring rewriting and distinguish strict projective-system assertions from their weaker versions. The strongest note-level lead is the countability boundary in the module criterion (N90-01); its exact hypotheses and proof remain to be independently checked against the complete scan set.

### Cote 91 — Greenberg–Néron and non-projective Hom functors

The supplied leaves concern relative-product criteria, centralizers, condition R, finite subschemes, separatedness, flattening, relative flat loci, quotient constructions, unramified Hom functors and coherent-module constructions. Several statements are explicitly revised, cancelled, or left unfinished. Murre's 1965 Bourbaki exposé (published 1966) is a verified historical comparator for non-projective representability constructions, but it is not by itself proof that any specific statement in these leaves is identical to Murre's result. The notes' comparison must be made at the level of the exact functor, hypotheses and conclusion.

## Principal claims and open questions

The main claim families represented in the notes are:
1. extension-duality and quadratic-form transport, including sign and discriminant identities;
2. relative norm/trace identities over possibly nonfaithful or nonflat base maps;
3. product constructions for quadratic algebras, including the difference between an abstract product and its tensor realization;
4. invariant-ring, fixed-ring and étale-locus assertions with precise generation and injectivity hypotheses;
5. χ-split extension products and their compatibility/coherence conditions;
6. module saturation and projective-system criteria in cote 90;
7. non-projective Hom, centralizer, flattening and unramified-functor constructions in cote 91.

The corresponding open-step catalogue is in `SA02_problems.json`. Its formulations are intentionally conservative: they are leads drawn from the notes, not claims that the entire literature has been searched. The strongest open directions include the exact hypothesis boundary for the saturation criterion; the full relative-product and centralizer conditions in cote 91; the missing relative trace/norm construction; and the coherence of χ-split extension comparisons.

## What the earlier notes establish, and what they do not

The three supplied note packages are already substantial research documents. They provide detailed page-by-page observations, explicit formal calculations and counterexamples, and internal distinctions between author's text, transcriber doubts, and earlier-run ideas. In particular, the 84/146–193 notes report a polynomial-ring check of the local quadratic-algebra formula, a tensor-realization kernel, a local χ-groupoid model, and a sign obstruction under specified endpoint conventions. The 84/097–145 notes report the `Z/4` axiom-list counterexample, invariant-ring and fixed-ring qualifications, and a number of type/hypothesis corrections. The 90/91 notes explicitly label some external-register evidence as first-pass, truncated or unchecked.

This run has **not** re-proved all of those calculations, audited them against manuscript images, or independently checked every cited literature claim. It therefore does not promote those reported findings to fresh results. The entry catalogue preserves all 389 source entries and maps each to one provisional item. The verdict `kept` means “retained as its own traceable record”; it does not mean “mathematically validated” or “deduplication conclusively completed.”
