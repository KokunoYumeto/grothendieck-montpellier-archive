# SA29 part 2 — ranked directions for subsequent runs

These are task definitions, not additional results. The source checks, counterexamples and calculations needed for this consolidation are already in the claims and proof registers. None of the directions below is developed into a new research project here. Each distinguishes an author question from an editorial reconstruction, states what is known, and identifies a bounded first step.

Two rankings serve different purposes. **Interest rank** estimates the mathematical value of a successful clarification. **Short-run rank** estimates how much reliable progress a run of a few hours can make, assuming the specified inputs are available. Rank1 is highest in each column. The ranking does not imply that the broad profinite or reconstruction questions can be solved in a few hours.

## 1. The pure-peripheral versus symmetric question

**Sources:** SA29-611, SA29-629, SA29-643; C-SA29-455, C-SA29-465, C-SA29-486, C-SA29-502, C-SA29-507; MP-SA29-152, MP-SA29-160, MP-SA29-167.

**What it would establish.** An exact modern formulation of the proposed equality between the pure common-multiplier peripheral group and the specified symmetry centralizer. This would determine whether the manuscript question coincides with a known GT variant or contains an extra condition. The distinction between the rank-two free profinite group and higher-point moduli groups must remain explicit.

**Known.** The symmetric/quasi-special descriptions in Harbater–Schneps retain automorphy and symmetry assumptions. Later higher-point results do not automatically identify the full pure group here. Lochak2025 §4 equation(4.1) still includes invertibility. Furusho’s completed setting is different. The finite permutation projection proves the relevant containment of the centralizer in the pure subgroup, but does not reverse it.

**First concrete step.** Write the peripheral permutation, multiplier, Aut→Out quotient and finite symmetry action on one chosen presentation of F-hat2. Compare definitions line by line with the opened primary sources and record every additional generation, determinant or normalization condition. A short run should return a precise theorem-boundary report, without promising the equality.

## 2. The genus-one central lifts

**Sources:** SA29-104, SA29-405, SA29-426–430; C-SA29-56, C-SA29-208, C-SA29-306–311; MP-SA29-75, MP-SA29-101, MP-SA29-104.

**What it would establish.** A verified translation between the manuscript’s chosen lifted generators and a particular modern elliptic extension, with the remaining epsilon compatibility expressed as a word in a defined group.

**Known.** The braid centre and the primitive boundary-kernel generator are distinct powers. The exact Aut(F2) check fixes the inverse sixth power. The determinant semidirect-product coordinates and scalar exponents have been checked, including the conditional specialization of nu2 and nu3. Enriquez2014 Proposition2.20 and Theorem8.4 supply a modern target but do not identify the manuscript’s normalization by themselves.

**First concrete step.** Freeze one boundary orientation, basepoint, composition law and central generator. Give images of every rho, sigma, ell and zeta in that model and check the defining relations before comparing scalar formulas. The deliverable should isolate the residual noncentral identity; a linear quotient is not a sufficient certificate.

## 3. The privileged-path groupoid

**Sources:** SA29-444, SA29-446, SA29-449; C-SA29-333–334, C-SA29-339–340; MP-SA29-105–107, MP-SA29-109.

**What it would establish.** A well-defined groupoid of the author’s privileged configurations with specified objects, oriented elementary paths and the precise relations still missing from148/18. It would also establish whether the comparison on148/19 uses the same subdivision or a genuinely further refinement.

**Known.** The local cell and path counts are checked. The 17/14 count is not reconciled without a common definition. The dimension-two slots on25 are unfinished. Bakalov–Kirillov’s marking-complex theorem concerns an explicit move system; the Esquisse supplies the general dimensional programme. The 2026 Basualdo Bonatto–Robertson comparator has the precise subgroup and genus-truncation limits recorded in C-SA29-333. None replaces a generator dictionary for these drawings.

**First concrete step.** Obtain the relevant scans and the continuation beyond148/28, then build an object/endpoint table for the four path types. Compare each move with one move in a specified established marking complex. A short run can produce the translation obligations and identify which proposed relations are even well-typed, without attempting a full completeness theorem.

## 4. Reconstruction at infinity

**Sources:** SA29-20, SA29-22–23, SA29-418; C-SA29-12–15, C-SA29-315, C-SA29-317–319; MP-SA29-11–12.

**What it would establish.** A precise version of the proposal to recover the boundary fundamental group and its arithmetic action from local groups and combinatorial normal-crossing data. The object called a punctured formal neighbourhood must be defined before asking for an equivalence of covering categories.

**Known.** The stable-graph genus and dimension identities are established and checked, but an incidence graph alone does not supply inertia embeddings, local parameters or Galois actions. The map from flags to components even has a smooth-unmarked exception. The author’s proposed Lefschetz reduction needs the exact dimension, smoothness and boundary hypotheses.

**First concrete step.** Choose one two-component normal-crossing example over a specified field and formulate its local covering categories and gluing maps. Compare only those objects with the relevant EGA/SGA and later logarithmic or formal-neighbourhood constructions. Before a cross-folder claim is made, obtain the actual endpoints in146,147 and149. The useful short-run outcome is a definition and hypothesis document, not a general reconstruction theorem.

## 5. D-structures and arithmetic reconstruction

**Sources:** SA29-69–71, SA29-79, SA29-81, SA29-84, SA29-86, SA29-93; C-SA29-39–50; MP-SA29-24–25.

**What it would establish.** The precise extra datum a discretification is meant to retain, and the exact functor whose faithfulness or essential image the author hopes to recover.

**Known.** Torsor twisting, transport of structure and rational-point sections have standard meanings under explicit hypotheses. A bare torsor does not classify curves fully faithfully. The finite-index transitivity assertion on144/27 remains live; the following left/right correction does not retract it. Embedding orbits depend on the restriction to the ground field.

**First concrete step.** Write separate source and target categories for the unpointed, pointed and discretified versions, including every quotient by an inner or discrete group. Test the proposed full-faithfulness statement on one fixed hyperbolic curve and on two nonisomorphic curves of the same topological type. The first run should determine exactly what information is missing, before invoking an anabelian theorem with stronger hypotheses.

## 6. The integral framing extension

**Sources:** SA29-638–639, SA29-642; C-SA29-489, C-SA29-494–496, C-SA29-501, C-SA29-505; MP-SA29-163, MP-SA29-166.

**What it would establish.** A geometric definition of the integral extension S-Gamma by Z3 and its pullback, together with the relation to the later profinite tuple model.

**Known.** The tuple group’s kernels over Aut and Out differ by the inner automorphism factor. The positive cusp transposition has a concrete local obstruction to an order-two normalized lift. Signed or interior fixed-point sections do not remove that obstruction. Once an actual extension is defined, its pullback is formal; the undefined extension class is the substantive part.

**First concrete step.** Choose one framing datum—boundary parametrization, tangent direction or local path class—and compute the finite geometric group’s action on the three integer framing coordinates. Compare the resulting extension class with the boundary-fixed pair-of-pants model. A short run can establish agreement of particular models or identify the missing choice, without asserting that every similarly named special group is the same.

## 7. The category of cartes

**Sources:** SA29-137–138, SA29-145, SA29-147, SA29-148, SA29-149, SA29-153, SA29-158–159; C-SA29-71–72, C-SA29-75–77, C-SA29-79–80, C-SA29-151; MP-SA29-33.

**What it would establish.** A single precise category in which the finite-set/flag construction is actually an equivalence, including circle components, folded edges, disconnected objects and the intended isotopy morphisms.

**Known.** The action-side quotient and full-subcategory statements are formal. The saved four-flag example shows that the early circle exclusion is not enforced by the written freeness conditions. Full and positive flags give different literal counts around a folded edge. The weighted and ordinary groups have explicitly different relations.

**First concrete step.** State the geometric objects and morphisms before constructing either functor. Decide, with source evidence, whether marked vertices repair circle components or whether those components should be allowed. Verify both composites on the exceptional circle and a folded-edge example. A few hours can produce a defensible companion theorem with its hypotheses, without claiming the author had already written that completed theorem.

## 8. A coherent marked-presentation commentary

**Sources:** SA29-104, SA29-111, SA29-133, SA29-617–624, SA29-636–637, SA29-641; C-SA29-56–63, C-SA29-70, C-SA29-471–482, C-SA29-492–493, C-SA29-499–504; MP-SA29-29, MP-SA29-74–75, MP-SA29-153–159, MP-SA29-162, MP-SA29-165.

**What it would establish.** An edition-ready explanation of the positive, signed and full mixed groups that preserves the chronological changes and makes every loop word interpretable.

**Known.** The main exact substitutions, finite-quotient counterexamples and central normal forms already exist in this dossier. They distinguish relations holding in a model from a complete presentation of that model. The pure-power exercise is actually on the page and has a proof; it is not an earlier-run invention.

**First concrete step.** Make two dictionaries for the early and restarted loop conventions, with images on the free generators and peripheral product fixed in each. Classify each equation as a live identity, cancelled attempt, mathematical reconstruction or ink alarm. For a truncated presentation, prove a homomorphism in both directions or exhibit a quotient showing insufficiency. This has high immediate editorial yield.

## 9. The lower four-point diagram

**Sources:** SA29-411, SA29-447, SA29-456; C-SA29-312, C-SA29-335–337; MP-SA29-108.

**What it would establish.** The actual subgroup and quotient intended in the lower row of148/20, and its relation to the pure/full four-point mapping-class groups.

**Known.** The S4→S3 pairing action and sign compatibility are proved. The upper diagram has a standard interpretation with a Klein-four kernel. A finite congruence certificate supports one candidate complement; it does not prove the reading of the pale slash, its uniqueness, or every arrow of the lower diagram.

**First concrete step.** Inspect the scan and retain the authoritative T symbols. Record the proposed subgroup’s generators, index, torsion and image modulo4. Compare these independently with the displayed quotients. A short run should either identify the diagram exactly or reduce the ambiguity to a small, explicitly different set of candidates.

## 10. The colour-transport obstruction

**Sources:** SA29-159; C-SA29-162–164; MP-SA29-54, with the residual-C3 context retained in that problem.

**What it would establish.** A precise theorem explaining when the author’s S3 colouring cover reduces to an unramified C3 torsor and when a compatible global three-colouring exists.

**Known.** Local odd valence produces transposition monodromy. Orientation and a chosen face bicolouring supply the sign trivialization used in the reduction. A trivial principal C3 torsor on a connected object has three compatible sections. The modern projectivity/holonomy comparison is recorded, but the exact manuscript normalization should still be stated.

**First concrete step.** Fix the category of cellulations and specify what counts as a colouring under automorphisms. Work out one sphere, one torus and one nonorientable counterexample to the omitted orientation condition. Then compare the resulting theorem with the exact projectivity statement cited in the claims map. The goal is a clear local theorem and examples, not a broad new graph-colouring conjecture.

## 11. The H4-to-E8 trace construction

**Sources:** SA29-43; C-SA29-27; MP-SA29-17.

**What it would establish.** Which rank-four lattice over the quadratic field, bilinear form, scaling and trace operation realize the integral rank-eight lattice intended by the note.

**Known.** The numerical dimension count alone does not determine an integral lattice or its discriminant. Later icosian and root-system constructions are relevant only after their exact lattices and trace conventions are matched. No arbitrary quadratic form was inserted into the present computation ledger to manufacture an E8 match.

**First concrete step.** Extract the strongest data actually supplied on144/9 and compare it with one opened primary construction. State the missing basis or normalization explicitly. A few-hour run can produce a candidate dictionary with determinant and integrality obligations, or conclude precisely that the manuscript does not select a unique construction.
