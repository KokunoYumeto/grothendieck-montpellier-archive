# SA25 — Ranked follow-up tasks

These are proposed tasks, **not results developed in this dossier**. Their starting points are the manuscript, the checked literature and the explicit repairs already recorded. “One run” means a few hours with the necessary source files available. Interest and feasibility are ranked separately; rank 1 is highest. Feasibility measures the chance of completing the stated bounded deliverable, not solving an unrestricted classification problem.

## 1. Specify the extra seeds for prescribed weak equivalences

**Origin.** Items SA25-10, SA25-11, SA25-47, SA25-53 and SA25-62; claims CL-SA25-021, CL-SA25-029–033 and CL-SA25-116; problems MP-SA25-06 and MP-SA25-10.

**What it would establish.** For one explicitly fixed topos/test category and one prescribed accessible localizer, identify a small additional family whose cellular closure, together with the interval operation, gives the intended trivial cofibrations. The objective is an exact statement about that family, not another general model-existence theorem.

**Known starting point.** The unrestricted interval-only recipe fails in Set even when all pointed intervals and both factorizations are allowed. The exact Set repair is CL-SA25-021-R1. Cisinski2002 Theorem 3.9 and Cisinski2006 Theorem 1.4.3 establish accessible existence; Remark1.3.46 and Proposition 1.3.61 distinguish the anodyne class from all trivial cofibrations and the role of completeness.

**First concrete step.** Fix a single example beyond the already checked Set case. Write the source's generator family and a published complete anodyne family side by side, with their null-domain maps and endpoint conventions explicit. Test whether each published seed belongs to the source-generated closure.

**One-run stopping point.** A proved inclusion, one missing seed with a witness, or an exact reduction to a named completeness condition. Do not claim minimality from a finite search or identify all localizers from one example.

## 2. State the coefficient descent condition for the cover replacement

**Origin.** Items SA25-8, SA25-16, SA25-22 and SA25-35; claims CL-SA25-206–209 and CL-SA25-217–218; problems MP-SA25-64 and MP-SA25-68.

**What it would establish.** Specify exactly which coefficients on the Grothendieck replacement descend to the original category, so that the cohomology comparison and the coherent square computation can be used without an implicit change of quantifier.

**Known starting point.** The explicit adjunction proves the absolute nerve equivalence. It also proves the comparison for coefficients transported as `p*η`. An arbitrary coefficient on the replacement fails even in the one-point example. The collapse lemma succeeds under its isomorphism-leg hypothesis although the unconditional strict-fibre justification fails. See RF-SA25-209, RF-SA25-218, Cisinski2008 Lemma 2.3 and Groth2013's pointwise Kan formulas.

**First concrete step.** Draw the four relevant indexing functors in the manuscript's presheaf variance and express the essential image of restriction/pullback by the actual unit or counit. Work first with the finite fork, retaining coherent diagrams throughout.

**One-run stopping point.** An exact lemma for that shape, with a necessary or sufficient descent condition and explicit comparison maps. General descent for arbitrary covers should remain a separate task. The uncertain bar over `X` must still be decided from the images, independently of the lemma.

## 3. Delimit stable versions of the square and cube axioms

**Origin.** Items SA25-3, SA25-4, SA25-5, SA25-17, SA25-19, SA25-21, SA25-28 and SA25-29; claims CL-SA25-210, CL-SA25-212–216 and CL-SA25-222–227; problems MP-SA25-63, MP-SA25-65, MP-SA25-67 and MP-SA25-70–73.

**What it would establish.** A precise sufficient framework in which a category-valued total construction gives coherent bicartesian coefficient squares and those coefficients detect the chosen weak equivalences. This would explain when the reverse edge and third cube implications are justified.

**Known starting point.** The unrestricted `W∞` versions fail. The coherent stable square and cube arguments are valid with their stated hypotheses. Groth2013 Definition 4.1 and the shift results, and Groth–Ponto–Shulman2014 Theorem 6.1, supply the relevant established statements. The later *Dérivateurs* XII propositions must retain their strong-axiom assumptions. RF-SA25-224 and RF-SA25-225 state sufficient repairs.

**First concrete step.** Select one stable coefficient theory and one class of total-category squares. Write the three logically separate obligations: the construction is coherent, each square is bicartesian, and the invariant detects exactly the intended `W` on the arrows used.

**One-run stopping point.** Prove or disprove those obligations in that example. A table distinguishing sufficient conditions from necessary ones is a useful result. “Homology localizers only” is not a consequence of the double-cone counterexample and must not be used as a classification premise.

## 4. Separate cellular size bounds from directed-diagram closure

**Origin.** Items SA25-12, SA25-13, SA25-34, SA25-37, SA25-63 and SA25-65; claims CL-SA25-112–115, CL-SA25-118 and CL-SA25-201–204; problems MP-SA25-07, MP-SA25-36 and MP-SA25-61.

**What it would establish.** A line-by-line sufficient-hypothesis version of the source's factorization construction, identifying which size bounds are needed at each stage and which closure assumptions are dispensable or invalid.

**Known starting point.** Relative smallness of generating domains is enough; target accessibility is extra strength. Identities/zero-length composites must be included. Continuous ordinal lifting closure holds. Arbitrary directed closure fails, even for the checked binary-join index poset. Hovey1999 Theorem 2.1.14 and Cisinski2006 Lemmas1.2.21–23 give set-generated versions; Chorny2006 Theorem 1.1 gives a conditional class-sized extension with uniform smallness and a solution-map condition.

**First concrete step.** Annotate the all-squares stage, successor stage, limit stage and lifting conclusion with the exact cardinal/colimit condition each uses. Keep the source's changing symbols `π`, `σ`, `Φ` and `Φ₀` distinct until the relevant images have been checked.

**One-run stopping point.** A complete proof specification for a fixed generator family and an explicit list of assumptions not supplied by the source. Do not spend the run trying to repair arbitrary directed closure without restricting the diagrams or maps.

## 5. Audit saturation from bare arrow data

**Origin.** Items SA25-30, SA25-36, SA25-57 and SA25-59; claims CL-SA25-024, CL-SA25-104, CL-SA25-106–109; problems MP-SA25-08, MP-SA25-31, MP-SA25-32 and MP-SA25-37.

**What it would establish.** Determine which actual categorical constructions the desired saturation/retract argument needs, beyond formal arrow classes and factorization. This separates the known model-category theorem from the manuscript's weaker question.

**Known starting point.** The generic cyclic implication is false for the checked parity example. Inverse images of isomorphisms satisfy it, as RF-SA25-106 proves. Quillen1967 I§5 gives model-category saturation with its finite-limit/colimit setup. Joyal–Tierney2008 AppendixA PropositionA.3.1 and Riehl2014 Lemma 11.3.3 use a specific pushout in their retract arguments. The present search did not settle every no-M0 formulation.

**First concrete step.** Express that pushout and the replacement constructions as an explicit minimal existence hypothesis, then attempt the proof with only that hypothesis. Distinguish retract closure of `W`, two-out-of-three, the split condition, cyclic saturation and strong saturation in every statement.

**One-run stopping point.** A proved conditional lemma with a smaller construction package, or a sharply specified candidate counterexample problem. A failure to find a counterexample does not establish a theorem, and a bounded search does not establish current openness.

## 6. Compare model properness with intrinsic cohomological classes

**Origin.** Items SA25-24, SA25-26, SA25-36 and SA25-55; claims CL-SA25-004, CL-SA25-010, CL-SA25-110–111 and CL-SA25-218; problems MP-SA25-03, MP-SA25-34 and MP-SA25-68.

**What it would establish.** For one fixed category of models, compare the right lifting class chosen by a model structure with the intrinsic proper/smooth classes, retaining the exact notion of coefficient base change.

**Known starting point.** Recognition of a Quillen triple does not prove properness. Cisinski2002 Theorem 4.8 gives a qualified accessible-topos criterion. Maltsiniotis2005 Corollary 4.16 identifies a categorical fibration-to-smoothness implication. These are distinct results, with different variances and quantified maps.

**First concrete step.** Choose one accessible presheaf-localizer example and restate the properness criterion with all generator, base and fibrant-object quantifiers. Then compare those hypotheses with the manuscript's proposed right class, rather than identifying classes by shared terminology.

**One-run stopping point.** One inclusion, a counterexample to the other inclusion, or a precise extra hypothesis yielding equality in that example. No intrinsic/model identification should be imported from unavailable project registers.

## 7. Resolve the definition-sensitive lifting and path leaves on images

**Origin.** Items SA25-23, SA25-24, SA25-25, SA25-39 and SA25-79; claims CL-SA25-001–010; problems MP-SA25-01–03.

**What it would establish.** Recover the actual finite-category tests, cone directions, immersion qualifiers and path-morphism convention sufficiently to decide whether a precise modern theorem matches the source.

**Known starting point.** The retained text does not determine these data. Thomason1980, Cisinski2006 §5.3 and Maltsiniotis2005 supply precise comparison theorems. *Dérivateurs* V §1 and Maltsiniotis2020 §3.2.40 use different path conventions; Shoikhet2016 Theorem 2.8 concerns another explicit replacement. None defines the cancelled `Ch(C)` merely by sharing its name.

**First concrete step.** With the scans available, examine only the high-impact symbols and deletion states in the first two mathematical leaves, using `SA25_readings.tsv` as the agenda. Transcribe the exact test arrows and allowed path morphisms before choosing a comparison theorem.

**One-run stopping point.** An image-supported decision for each targeted locus, plus a theorem match or a documented failure of identification. If a definition is absent from the leaf, mark it absent and direct the search to adjacent material; do not reconstruct ink from the theorem one hopes to obtain. This task requires the images that were intentionally unavailable here.

## 8. Delimit the dualizing-functor hope

**Origin.** Items SA25-27 and SA25-28; claims CL-SA25-213–216; problems MP-SA25-65, MP-SA25-66 and MP-SA25-67.

**What it would establish.** State a meaningful version of the proposed internal dualizing functor, separating ordinary opposition of derivators from a functor relating homology and cohomology within one coefficient theory.

**Known starting point.** Opposition exchanges left/right Kan extensions formally once variance and coefficient quantifiers match. It does not construct an internal duality. The checked *Pursuing Stacks* §§136–137 distinguishes ordinary homological operations from an exceptional duality hope. Stable derivator square exactness supplies no six-functor or correspondence enhancement by itself.

**First concrete step.** Specify a domain of coefficients, the variance, the expected source and target of the functor, and the proposed comparison with both Kan extensions. Begin with a single finite diagram shape and explicitly stated finiteness/duality hypotheses.

**One-run stopping point.** A precise formulation and a verified elementary model, or a clear obstruction showing why the preliminary formulation is too broad. A general duality or correspondence theory is beyond this bounded task.
