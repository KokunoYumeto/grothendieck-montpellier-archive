## The substantive findings

The weight calculations are productive when their support convention and coefficient hypothesis are retained. Deligne’s Hodge and Weil theories establish the numerical realization statements, while the manuscript’s geometric-generation level remains stronger than Hodge type. A smooth-projective direct summand can be passed through the derived splitting argument; an arbitrary subquotient cannot be treated as such without the explicitly questioned devissage/semisimplicity step. The later Cor5.2 is conditional. Cor5.3 has a decisive sans/sous Tate variant and must not be attributed as unconditional until its scan is read.

The Legendre elliptic family overF₅ supplies one fully checked family answering several questions. Its smooth-projective weight-one coefficient has weight0 boundary invariants injecting into compactly supportedH¹, and weight4 local inertia cohomology that is a quotient of ordinary globalH¹. Thus the proposed lower bound≥j and sharp upper bounds2n+j,2i+j,2min(i,n)+j fail even with strictly special coefficients. The mathematical reason is nonzero infinite unipotent boundary monodromy; this does not refute the constant-coefficient local level estimate.

For positive-dimensional boundary points of separated complex varieties with the source SNC resolution setup, a common Hodge-coordinate box proves the local bound min(i,2n−1−i), with weight≤2min(i,n). The proof and a finite enumeration are saved. The necessary compatibility with mixed Hodge structures is checked explicitly: Kebekus–Schnell Proposition9.2 gives the SNC graded-weight pieces, and Saito1989 Theorems1.3–1.4 allow derived restriction to the proper fibre. The resulting finite filtered complex suffices without degeneration. This realization argument does not establish every geometric interpretation of niveau. The stronger claim identifying an entire top E2 term with the surviving cohomology fails in a double blowup of a punctured regular surface: a nonzero E2^{0,2} is killed by d2. Codex reads intervient avec where Pro reads s’identifie avec, so only the strong variant is refuted. Dimension-zero/nonboundary endpoints need separate treatment.

Late dimension type is not the earlier Tate-stableD_i filtration. The early text explicitly puts all Tate objects inD0; late125 uses untwisted smooth-projective sources of bounded dimension. Consequently the SNC local classQ_l(−r) excludes typer−1 in the late convention, and the strict minus-one bound fails there. This is a change of definition, not a blanket contradiction in every use of dimension type.

The integral smooth-motive pages preserve several real gaps. An almost-all-prime integral condition needs a common reference lattice system; without it Hom can contain1/l for every prime and fail finite generation. Generic restriction on a normal base is fully faithful for unramified systems, but ambient extension closure fails for a ramified finite unipotent torsion extension. Tensor/internalHom does not imply rigidity in the presence of torsion. The weight-one functor’s full faithfulness is distinct from its essential image: Vasiu–Zink’s high-ramification regular-local dimension-two example satisfies all-prime nonramification and still does not extend as an abelian scheme. The source already retracts certainty and suspects that setting. Low-ramification criteria and NOS over traits remain positive cases, while the general arbitrary-motive one-/two-prime conjecture was not found settled in the recorded searches.

The envelope construction itself is sound in its additive setting. The manuscript intertwiner quotient equals the usual corner Hom; projectors split without semisimplicity; the universal property is an equivalence of functor categories. Finite-dimensional semisimple End algebras yield generalized inverses, kernels, cokernels and finite-length semisimple abelianness without circular assumptions. Tensor constraints and rationalSchur projectors extend. Jannsen’s numerical semisimplicity theorem is a geometric application with explicit scope, not a proof of all standard conjectures. No motivic Galois exact sequence or period conjecture is written on these retained leaves; the later Nori/André, Nori/Ayoub, Kahn descent and André period comparisons are labelled as external comparisons. Oda’s general proper-smooth injection additionally assumes a rational point, which the earlier note omitted.
