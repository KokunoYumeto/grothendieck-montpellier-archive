# Independent checks and scope of counterexamples

These are current-run reconstructions, not text inserted into Grothendieck's manuscript. The runnable exact checks are in `programs/check_calculations.py`; `outputs/calculations.json` records eighteen families. Finite examples support the general arguments below and are not represented as proofs of statements in all dimensions. Reference keys resolve in `references.bib` and `reference_ledger.tsv`.

## 1. The two-lines normalization calculation

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from l. Let T be two projective lines meeting transversally at one point, and choose two distinct smooth points a,b, one on each line. Put Y=T−{a,b}. Its normalization is two copies of A¹. The exact sequence for the proper compactification T gives

`H⁰(T,Q_l) → H⁰({a,b},Q_l) → H_c¹(Y,Q_l) → H¹(T,Q_l)`.

The first map is the diagonal Q_l→Q_l² and H¹(T)=0 (the dual graph is a tree and each component is P¹). Thus H_c¹(Y)=Q_l. For the normalization, H⁰ of the two-component compactification maps isomorphically to H⁰ of its two missing points, so H_c¹ is zero. The pointwise normalization map is proper, but pullback is therefore not injective. In the source's larger diagram X=P²−{a,b} also has H_c¹(X)=Q_l by the same diagonal map; U=P²−T is connected with a connected projective complement T, giving H_c¹(U)=0. The program verifies all four ranks. This answers an actual question on scan101 without disputing Deligne's proper top-weight quotient theorem (Hodge III8.2.5).

## 2. Arithmetic purity, geometric forgetting, and special weight zero

On SpecF_q choose the continuous representation of Gal(Fbar_q/F_q)=Zhat whose l-primary coordinate a acts by I+aN, where N²=0, N≠0. This is continuous into GL₂(Z_l). Frobenius is unipotent with both eigenvalues1, so it is pointwise pure of weight0. The unique invariant line has no invariant complement, hence the arithmetic representation is not semisimple. After passing to the geometric point the Galois group disappears; the underlying vector space is semisimple. This exactly explains why Weil II3.4.1(iii) says geometric semisimplicity. The example is not asserted to be a smooth proper motivic cohomology representation; it therefore does not disprove the separate arithmetic semisimplicity conjecture for such cohomology.

For the manuscript's special class at weight0, a smooth projective family has finitely many connected components in each fibre, varying through a finite étale component scheme. R⁰ is the corresponding finite permutation representation, so every subquotient has finite monodromy. Conversely, a finite-monodromy Q_l-representation factors through a finite group G and is a direct summand of a finite sum of its regular representations, by averaging in characteristic0. The finite étale G-cover provides the required relative-dimension-zero generator. Thus the exact corrected equivalence is special weight0 iff finite monodromy. Infinite unipotent Frobenius is lisse but not special weight0. If the writer's historic locally-constant convention already meant finite monodromy, there is no author error; the scan/context must settle that convention. A deleted Codex warning about rational versus Q_l representations does not obstruct the regular-representation argument.

Bare geometric stalks also cannot distinguish Q_l from Q_l(−1) over F_q. Any criterion for geometric origin/type must retain arithmetic descent or a relative family, rather than infer it from the underlying vector space alone.

## 4. All hypotheses of the local and global Legendre counterexamples

Take k=F₅, l≠5, U=P¹_k−{0,1,∞}, and the smooth projective elliptic family g:E→U with affine equation y²=x(x−1)(x−t), completed by its section at infinity. Its discriminant is16t²(t−1)², nonzero on U, and c₄=16(t²−t+1). Thus g is smooth projective of relative dimension1 and F=R¹g_*Q_l is the entire required strictly special coefficient, not merely an arbitrary lisse sheaf. The modular j=256(t²−t+1)³/[t²(t−1)²] is nonconstant. Weil II Lemma3.5.5 gives geometric monodromy SL₂, hence H⁰(Ubar,F)=H⁰(Ubar,F∨)=0.

At t=0,1 the discriminant vanishes and c₄ is a unit. The special fibres are nodal and their tangent cones split over F₅: at0, y²=−x² and−1=4 is a square; at1 the cone is y²=(x−1)². These are split multiplicative degenerations with nonzero tame unipotent monodromy. In cohomological geometric-Frobenius convention the local representation has an invariant Q_l line and quotient Q_l(−1). One can take N=[[0,1],[0,0]], Frob=diag(1,q), giving Frob N Frob⁻¹=q⁻¹N. Tame cohomology is H¹(I,V)=V_I(−1)=Q_l(−2), weight4. The invariant line has weight0. Ito et al., On log motives, Remark3.2.11 supplies the matching log-Tate extension; Weil II1.8.4 supplies the monodromy weight framework. A finite-monodromy assumption would change the example: unipotent here is infinite in characteristic-zero l-adic coefficients.

Let a:U→P¹. From0→a_!F→a_*F→B→0 and H⁰(Ubar,F)=0, the two rational invariant boundary lines inject Q_l² into H_c¹(Ubar,F). Hence the proposed lower bound w≥j=1 fails. On the other hand Poincaré duality and H⁰(Ubar,F∨)=0 give H_c²(Ubar,F)=0. The same boundary exact sequence gives H²(P¹bar,a_*F)=0 because B has finite support. The Leray edge exact sequence therefore makes H¹(Ubar,F) surject onto H⁰(P¹bar,R¹a_*F). The two rational boundary summands at0 and1 give a quotient Q_l(−2)², weight4. With i=n=j=1, both proposed global upper bounds2n+j and2i+j, and the local upper bound2min(i,n)+j, equal3. All fail with the precise smooth-projective coefficient hypothesis satisfied. The constant-coefficient local level theorem is unaffected.

## 5. The positive-dimensional local Hodge box

Work with separated complex algebraic varieties. Assume the source's regular immersion and a proper SNC resolution isomorphic over the source. Let n≥1 be the dimension of the closure of its image, and take a boundary point. It is enough to treat a dense open immersion into that closure; a subsequent closed immersion is exact. In the proper fibre F of the resolution the q=0 term has support dimension≤n−1. For q>0 the SNC formula (Riou Props2.2–2.3 for the étale counterpart) expresses R^q of the open inclusion as sums on q-fold boundary intersections, each twisted by−q, with fibre support dimension≤n−q.

The MHS compatibility needs an explicit argument, not merely the existence of a topological Leray sequence. Put M=j'_*Q_U^H[n] on the smooth resolution. Kebekus–Schnell Proposition9.2, author version11July2020 p29, gives gr^W_{n+q}M=⊕_{|I|=q}Q_{D_I}^H(-q)[n-q] for q≥1, with q=0 term Q^H[n]. Their proof is local for any SNC divisor, despite the surrounding resolution setting. Apply the derived functor RΓ(F,i_F^*(-)) to this finite filtration, using Saito1989 Theorems1.3–1.4. Proper base change identifies the resulting total complex, shifted by−n, with i_y^*Rj_*Q_U^H. Its graded complexes, shifted by−n, are ⊕RΓ(F∩D_I,Q^H)(-q)[-q]; their degree i groups are H^{i-q}(F∩D_I)(-q). These are therefore terms of a spectral sequence in mixed Hodge structures. No degeneration assertion and no exactness of ordinary stalk cohomology in the perverse t-structure is required. This verifies the compatibility that Hua2026, p36 and Q2 Problem3, explicitly left unchecked; it is a current reconstruction from primary ingredients, not a theorem attributed to that exposition.

Set total degree i and proper cohomology degree m=i−q. For a proper variety of dimension d, the Hodge coordinates of H^m lie between max(0,m−d) and min(m,d), and their sum is≤m. For q=0,d≤n−1 this gives the interval [max(0,i−n+1),min(i,n−1)]. For q≥1,d≤n−q, twisting by−q raises each coordinate by q and puts it inside [max(0,i−n+1),min(i,n)]. The sum is≤i+q and each coordinate≤min(i,n), so weight≤2min(i,n). The common interval has width min(i,n)−max(0,i−n+1), equal to i for i<n and2n−1−i for i≥n. This proves the displayed Hodge-level bound min(i,2n−1−i). There are no possible terms in degree2n. Proper-fibre mixed Hodge structures and their functorial spectral sequence preserve this box through subquotients/extensions. The program enumerates every permitted pair for n=1,...,8 as an independent finite check; the inequalities above are the general proof.

This argument proves a Hodge realization assertion under an SNC model. It is not a proof that every such Hodge piece is an algebraically generated motive at the manuscript geometric level. Nor does Durfee–Saito Theorem4.1, which is an intersection-cohomology link-weight theorem with its own n,d and support hypotheses, automatically identify those categories. For j:G_m→A¹, R¹j_* at0 is Q_l(−1) although the nonempty point fibres have dimension0; this refutes substituting actual fibre dimension for the source's local image-dimension invariant.

## 6. A top E2 contribution need not survive

Let S be the strict henselization of a smooth surface at a geometric point, l invertible, and U=S−{s}. This is an equicharacteristic regular local surface; it can be spread over a finite field with rational blowup centres so weights remain meaningful. Blow up s and then a smooth point of the first exceptional curve. The resulting regular surface is proper over S and isomorphic over U. Its boundary D=D₁∪D₂ is SNC, two P¹'s meeting at one rational point, with self-intersections−2,−1 and mutual intersection1.

For the local Leray sequence of the open inclusion into this resolution followed by the proper map to S, the q=0 row has Q_l in degree0 and Q_l(−1)² in degree2. The q=1 row has Q_l(−1)² in degree0 and Q_l(−2)² in degree2. The q=2 row has Q_l(−2) in degree0. The d₂ from(0,1) to(2,0) is the intersection matrix [[−2,1],[1,−1]], determinant1, hence an isomorphism. The d₂ from(0,2) to(2,1) is the nonzero alternating incidence/Gysin map, rank1. Independently, absolute purity at the codimension-two closed point yields H⁰(U)=Q_l, H³(U)=Q_l(−2), and H¹(U)=H²(U)=0. Consequently E₂^{0,2}=Q_l(−2) is killed, and cannot be identified with a nonzero top-weight piece of H²(U).

The program checks the matrix and ranks exactly. This refutes Pro's strong s'identifie avec reading if it means the whole E₂ term. Codex's intervient avec can mean contribution through a subquotient and is compatible with the example. It is not permissible to decide which verb is written by mathematical preference.

## 7. Untwisted dimension type versus Tate-stable type

For j:G_m^r→A^r over a finite field, the SNC exterior formula gives (R^rj_*Q_l)_0=Q_l(−r), weight2r. Each smooth projective source of dimension≤r−1 has cohomological weights in[0,2r−2]. Finite sums, subquotients, extensions and stratification preserve that weight interval. Therefore Q_l(−r) cannot belong to the late scan125 untwisted type≤r−1. This refutes the literal strict boundd+d′−1 in the constant coefficient test with target dimensionr and coefficient type0, and shows why tensoring byQ_l(−1) may increase this type.

In the notation actually written in Proposition4, d=r is the dimension of the excellent target A^r and d′=0 is the coefficient type; the sum gives r−1. The earlier scans35–36 instead explicitly Tate-stabilize their D_i filtration and put all Tate objects inD₀. That is a different convention. The argument above is not a contradiction to the early definition, nor a counterexample to a Hodge-level assertion, where Tate twists preserve level. Bondarko's modern dimension/slice filtration is a third setting that needs an actual comparison theorem before use.

## 8. Curve spectral sequences

For a smooth affine curve with no proper components and a lisse sheaf, H_c⁰=0; compact-support cohomology only has rows1,2. A two-row Leray sequence provides a short exact sequence of the relevant surviving terms, with the usual connecting differential incorporated. For a proper smooth curve family, rows0,1,2 all occur, so the abutment has three filtration layers, not two.

The cohomological differential has degree(r,1−r). On row1, for r≥3 the outgoing target has negative row and the incoming source has rowr≥3, outside the three rows. Thus E₃^{p,1}=E∞^{p,1}. The outer rows can still have a d₃ from row2 to row0. The final source reduction remains explicitly to verify/develop; these index facts do not supply its missing geometric-type argument.

## 9. Formal closure properties

Finite filtrations can be pulled back along exact inverse-image functors, refined by intersections, and concatenated through extensions. Subquotients inherit finite filtrations with subquotient graded terms. These operations prove closure for a class defined as the Serre envelope of its geometric generators. They do not prove that two unrelated geometric presentations admit a common filtration whose pieces satisfy two stronger generator conditions simultaneously.

Smooth projective generating families stay so after base change; Künneth supplies tensor products and Poincaré duality supplies the stated dual/Tate shifts. Where the definition insists on a relative dimension matching cohomological degree, products with projective spaces provide padding and the appropriate Tate summands. These are conditional on the usual smooth-projective cohomological formalism, not an assertion that every numerical pure sheaf has a geometric presentation. Weil II's canonical numerical weight filtration answers numerical intersections but cannot by itself prove the manuscript geometric-level intersection problem.

## 10. Integral smooth motives: precise conditional algebra

Suppose first that a rational abelian motive category with finite-dimensional Hom and exact realizations has been supplied. Suppose also that every object has one reference integral realization system, and admissible lattices agree with it away from finitely many primes. A rational morphism then has denominators at only finitely many primes and can be multiplied by one integer to become integral. Kernels and cokernels formed in rational motives and the l-adic modules remain compatible. Finite torsion systems are exactly the kernel of rationalization; clearing one denominator gives the fully faithful Serre quotient functor, while the reference lattice supplies essential surjectivity. These are conditional consequences, not a construction of the rational category from standard conjectures.

Without the reference axiom, take rational objectQ, source latticesZ_l, and target latticesl⁻¹Z_l at every prime. For each primep, multiplication by1/p takes every source lattice to the target lattice: at l=p it uses the allowed denominator, and at other primes it is integral. Thus Hom contains1/p for everyp. Any finitely generated subgroup ofQ has one common denominatorD; choosingp∤D proves non-finite-generation. This is a counterexample only to the weakened definition.

For connected normal noetherianS, a connected finite étale cover is integral (SGA1I10.1), so its generic fibre is connected. Hence G_K→π₁(S) is surjective. This proves full faithfulness for unramified representation systems. It does not make them extension-closed inside all generic integral representations: takeS=SpecC[[t]], K=C((t)), and the ramified cover t↦t^{1/l}. Its cyclic-l quotient acts onF_l² by I+N. Since(I+N)^l=I modl andN≠0, it is a nontrivial ramified extension of two trivialF_l representations. Rational part0 and all other prime parts0 make this a generic integral torsion system satisfying the finite-support conditions. Both end objects extend overS; the middle does not, becauseS is strictly henselian. This refutes épaisse only if it means ambient extension closure; it leaves full faithfulness, subquotient closure and internal abelianness intact.

Tensor product and internal Hom do not imply rigidity. ForT=Z/l, Hom_Z(T,Z)=0. IfT had a dual in the usual finite-generated integral-module setting, the evaluation/coevaluation triangle would make the identity ofT factor through zero. The source only says tensor withHom, so the nonrigidity warning is ours.

For0→A⁰→A→F→0, divisibility ofA⁰ over a geometric point allows any l-primary component class to be lifted to an l-primary torsion point after correcting a multiple insideA⁰. Thus0→A⁰(l)→A(l)→F(l)→0 is exact. SinceQ_l/Z_l is injective, applyingHom(−,Q_l/Z_l) gives the finite component term in the kernel of the dual sequence. Faltings1986§2Lemma1 extends a generic homomorphism between already-existing semiabelian schemes over a normal base; it is not an essential-image theorem asserting those schemes exist.

## 11. All-prime weight-one nonextension

Letk be a perfect field of characteristicp and R=W(k)[[x,y]]/(p−(xy)^{p−1}), withS=SpecR andU=S−{m}. The complete local ring is mixed-characteristic, regular of dimension2: its maximal ideal is generated byx,y and the defining relation eliminatesp to first order. It is a domain, excellent and normal; K has characteristic0. Its ramification order is2(p−1), outside the sufficient low-ramification conditionp∉m^p. Vasiu–ZinkTheorem28 applies because the reduction of the defining function is divisible byx^{p−1}y^{p−1} withx,y a regular sequence. It supplies an abelian scheme overU that does not extend toS; Lemma27 explains how a finite-flat obstruction produces it. Corollary4 gives the contrasting positive conditionp∉m^p.

For everyl≠p, l is invertible onS. The l^m-torsion of the abelian scheme overU is finite étale. Purity for the regular codimension-two puncture (SGA1X3.3, also Stacks0BMA clause1) extends these covers and their group operations uniquely toS; hence its generic l-adic representation is unramified overS. Forl=p, the definition only asks for smoothness onS[1/p], and that open is contained inU, where the abelian scheme already exists. All primes therefore meet the source nonramification requirement. Its generic h¹ has weight-one effectivity by construction. If it extended as an abelian scheme overS, its restriction toU would be isomorphic to the original one by full faithfulness of restriction on normal bases, contradicting nonextension. Thus even all-prime conditions do not suffice for essential image in this higher-dimensional mixed-characteristic setting. This is not a counterexample to the one-prime equivalence within the abelian/trait NOS theorem.

The Vasiu–Zink paper attributes earlier examples to Raynaud and Gabber and cites deJong–Oort1997§6 and a1992 Gabber letter. Those backward references were located, not promoted to independently read proofs. We therefore do not date first discovery to2009/2010.

## 12. The envelope construction, without circular abelianness

For projectors p onX andq onY, letI={a:ap=qa}. The mapI→qHom(X,Y)p, a↦qap, is surjective since a corner element already intertwines. Its kernel consists exactly of thosea withap=qa=0. This proves the manuscript quotient and the usual corner space coincide. Objects(X,p), identityp and corner composition formKar(C); the inclusionX↦(X,1) is fully faithful. A projectore on(X,p) satisfiespe=ep=e and splits as(X,e) plus(X,p−e). None of this needs semisimplicity.

Given additiveF:C→D withD idempotent-complete, choose an image of eachF(p), and define the extended map by restrictingF(a) between those images. Different choices are uniquely naturally isomorphic. A natural transformation onC restricts to the images and extends uniquely; restriction is therefore fully faithful and essentially surjective on functor categories. This proves an equivalence, not a canonical equality of functor-object sets. A biadditive functor extends by applying it to both projectors. Associativity, symmetry and unit maps restrict because they commute with the projectors, and their coherence follows by restriction of the original diagrams.

Now assume everyEnd(X) is finite-dimensional semisimple overF. Hom(X,Y) is a corner ofEnd(X⊕Y), hence finite-dimensional. Artin–Wedderburn expresses this latter algebra as a finite product of matrix algebras over division rings. In each block a rectangularu has a direct-sum normal form with an invertible rank block and zero elsewhere. Invert that block and use zero on its complement to obtainv withuvu=u andvuv=v. Corneringv gives a morphismY→X. Thenvu anduv are projectors. Their images are isomorphic viau,v; the complementary images are a kernel and cokernel by direct verification of their universal properties. Thus every map has theA⊕B→B⊕C form and coimage→image is an isomorphism. The category is abelian and every short exact sequence splits. Finite-dimensionality bounds the number of nonzero orthogonal idempotents, giving finite length and both chain conditions. This is the precise theorem stated in JannsenLemma2; the program verifies all identities on a non-square2×3 example but does not substitute that example for the general proof.

With the written orderingB⊕C, the reverse map usespr₁, notpr₂. This is a typing alarm on scan148. Finally, in aQ-linear symmetric monoidal completion, e₊=(1/r!)Σσ ande₋=(1/r!)Σsign(σ)σ are idempotent. Splitting them defines symmetric/exterior powers; rational Schur projectors work similarly. The program checksr=3 on(Q²)^⊗3, obtaining symmetric rank4 and alternating rank0. The characteristic-zero/invertible-denominator hypothesis is essential.

## 13. External-comparison checks

OnH*(P¹)=Q⊕Q(−1), the Lefschetz raising operator sends the unit to the top class, while a tensor automorphism preserves the unit. Its Cartan operator has eigenvalues−1,1; the cohomological weight operator has0,2 and differs by the identity on this representation. Equality up to this scalar on one object is not equality of tensor cocharacters. The saved commutator and unit calculations rule out the prior blanket identification.

On a principally polarized Jacobian, multiplication-by2 pulls back the alternating polarization form to4 times itself. The saved matrix check establishes the factor4. A Tate-module homomorphism is therefore not automatically a curve morphism preserving a principal polarization.

Deligne–Milne2.23 has a connected affine-group hypothesis and identifies semisimplicity with pro-reductivity, not centre-freeness. Their4.27 concerns symmetric polarizations of algebraic Tannakian categories overR and a compact-real-group fibre functor. StixTheorem26 concerns sections for smooth projective real curves of genus≥1 and identifies their conjugacy classes with connected components of the real locus. These are different objects and no equivalence between them is inferred. The source's tensor completion alone does not supply rigidity or a neutral fibre functor.
