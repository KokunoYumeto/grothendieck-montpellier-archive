## Cote56: quadrics and group schemes

The integral middle lattice of an even-dimensional split quadric has ruling basisα,β. The hyperplane powerα+β and primitive differenceα−β span an index2 sublattice. Consequently the primitive difference becomes twice a generator in the quotient by the hyperplane class. Rationalization hides that issue. Its square also has the parity sign(−1)^ν; the quadric surface supplies a complete counterexample to a uniform positive sign.

Both punctured-cone parity tables have a degree problem in the integral interpretation. ForQ^{2ν−1}, torsion belongs in degree2ν, not2ν−2. ForQ^{2ν}, the lower middle free group belongs in degree2ν, not2ν−1. Integral Gysin matrices through dimension8 were recomputed, and the general ruling-basis argument proves the pattern. Finite2-primary coefficients require an additional adjacent kernel group, so the uncertain coefficient symbol cannot be glossed over.

The affine quadric is explicitly identified with the tangent bundle of a sphere. This gives ordinary and compact-support cohomology and isolates dimension0 as two points. Complex orientation gives the signed middle comparison(−1)^{D/2}2 in even dimension; Deligne’s exact table agrees. A source map written with freely chosen generators may absorb a sign, so the positive2 is recorded as a convention alarm rather than a decided ink error.

The nontrivial-inertia assertion needs a stronger smoothing hypothesis than the listed flat/projective/fibre conditions. The family Σ_{i=0}²x_i²=t²x3² overC[[t]] satisfies those written conditions but has a constant smooth generic fibre after aK-coordinate change and hence trivial inertia. Its total space is singular. The checked Deligne theorem explicitly assumes a regular total space. The source’s final purity phrase is too incomplete to identify a unique modern theorem.

The SGA3 draft statements were compared with selected exact theorems and proofs of ExposéXII. Rank semicontinuity, local maximal tori, representability, conjugacy and the reductive centre are substantially developed in the later publication. The introductory promise to remove affine hypotheses is qualified: the checked nonaffine theorem retains local maximal-torus existence, and the text explicitly warns that constant reductive rank alone is insufficient there.

One broad Weyl-group statement fails. InSL4(C), letR have cocharacter(3,1,−1,−3) and letT be the connected kernel in the diagonal torus of character(1,2,−7,4). ThenR⊂T, both centralizers are the diagonal torus, W(R)=C2 andW(T)=1. All24 permutations and the lattice hypotheses were checked exactly. The standard proof works whenT is maximal. The broad wording survives in the checked2008 retypesetting; original1970 ink and a published erratum were not independently located. The mathematical counterexample does not depend on that documentary issue.
