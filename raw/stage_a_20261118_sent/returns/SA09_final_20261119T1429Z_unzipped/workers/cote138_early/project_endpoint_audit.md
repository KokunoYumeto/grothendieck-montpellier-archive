## The main correction to the earlier availability limit

The most important correspondence is now directly checkable. `E_cm_character_agent.tex` was read in full (327 lines). Its E.65 statement defines the CM parity through **two signed frame coordinates**, extends it by Galois translation, and expressly leaves the image on full GT unassigned (lines 72–79). Its proof's final reduction (lines 301–315) does not claim that the finite character has been computed on full GT. The invariant line F3(1,−1) is indeed the same finite linear-algebra object appearing in cote138 scan34, but equality with the manuscript's sign·χ3 character would still require the frame comparison maps. Matching a line is insufficient.

E.58's statement, the beginning of its lifting argument, and referee A's verdict/scope were read. The claimed element belongs to the **gentle** group GT0, with specified pure frames. E.3's complete statement in `E_agent.tex:168–202` still calls the full-GT image unresolved. Referee B's scope section makes the same distinction. The notes' separate mention of EXIT_TOWER E.49 remains a report of a different unrefereed claim: that target is absent from this background, and the present audit does not resolve the discrepancy by choosing a preferred status.

The referee qualifications are confirmed exactly as qualifications. Referee B says E.62's numerical counts were outside the scope of its check; only the action convention used by E.65 was inspected. For E.64 the report retains three unchecked dependencies: quartic frame existence, a profinite sewing reference, and the NTY Galois formula. The full E.64 statement was read, and no value of its comparison character on all of GT is asserted there. These statements are documentary findings about the return and report, not the present run's verdict on the correctness of the external theorem.

The complete LM6 A.22 statement (`LM6_POINTS_AS_SECTIONS_paper.tex:1255–1332`) confirms an unresolved residual procyclic-normalizer problem. Its subgroup lies in a common kernel invisible to the ordinary abelian or primewise tests. The statement itself warns that an open projection to a Galois group supplies no section, and that a proper Tate-module detector does not automatically supply a map of punctured curves. This precisely supports the caution in early item SA09-154 and middle item SA09-253.
