## The mathematical sequence of the folder

### 1. From simplicial modules to elementary chain complexes

The opening treats projective, acyclic and split-homology simplicial modules by their associated nonnegative chain complexes. In the notation used in the audit, D_i(P) is the identity disk P→P in degrees i,i−1 and S_j(P) is the sphere P in degree j. Nonnegativity matters: an acyclic complex of projective modules splits successively from degree zero. With projective homology, the same process separates disks from spheres. The resulting coefficient modules have intrinsic isomorphism classes; the splitting maps are choices. These are standard consequences of the Dold–Puppe framework, not a new integral reconstruction theorem (CL001–005; Dold & Puppe,1961, §§1–4).

The first significant textual fault is the surviving version of Lemma2 on scan4. A deleted acyclicity condition appears to have been replaced by projectivity while the range admits q=q′. The constant projective object Z, with q=q′=1 and M=Z, gives the identity of Ψ in the allegedly zero Hom group. Strict degree inequality gives the support argument; equality needs an additional homology-vanishing condition. This is a fully checked counterexample to the literal transcription, with an undecided ink question about the intended repair (CL003; T1:83–111).

The weight-n PD de Rham comparison first appears immediately after that lemma, in a faded diagram relating divided powers, exterior powers and a terminal kernel (T1:113–130). Its later occurrence on scan73 is expressly truncated. These two places form one open construction problem, MP-SA09-01. The existence of a precise weightwise comparison must not be replaced by an unbounded statement or a claim that all supplementary operations have been identified.

### 2. Rank polynomials, strict finiteness and the limits of numerical classification

For split projective homology, the simplicial rank has the form

\[
\rho(n)=\sum_{i\geq1}\mu_i\binom{n+1}{i}+\sum_{j\geq0}\nu_j\binom nj.
\]

Nonnegative coefficients relate polynomial growth to bounded normalization. The coefficient of n^N is (μ_N+ν_N)/N!, and evaluation at−1 recovers the Euler characteristic of the sphere coefficients. This checks the main rank argument while locating two local alarms: the missing j=0 in one decomposition, and the missing factorial in the marginal leading-coefficient formula (CL006,010; T1:178–221,352–366,436–460). The phrase describing the binomial basis must mean integer-valued polynomials; it cannot mean Z[t].

Tensor closure survives the author's withdrawal of nonlinear closure. The strict free case can be reduced to integral free disk/sphere models, exactly as the source suggests on scan10. Their integral tensor has free homology by Künneth, bounded normalization by the product rank polynomial, and therefore a free disk/sphere decomposition. Base change proves strict closure over any commutative base. Projective coefficients then give the general special case. By contrast, Γ²K(Z,1) normalizes to Z→Z with differential−2 in degrees2,1, so its H₁ is Z/2. This independently verifies the author's “faux” against nonlinear preservation of projective homology (CL007–008).

Rank data do not classify arbitrary finite projective modules. The invertible nonprincipal ideal I=(2,1+√−5) over R=Z[√−5] gives a concrete rank-one counterexample to the general special-object classification. The proof audit checks invertibility and nonprincipality, rather than citing an unexplained class-group fact. Classification must retain actual projective coefficient modules or impose freeness (CL009).

The finiteness terminology on scans14–15 overlaps with SGA6, but several surviving statements conflate properties of a chosen representative with properties in the derived category. An infinite-rank contractible disk is perfect as a derived object and has non-finite terms. Even a contractible complex with finite-free terms in every normalized degree can have simplicial rank2^n. Conversely, homology Z/p_i in degree i for distinct primes p_i over the regular noetherian base Z gives individually polynomial residue-field rank functions with no uniform degree bound, while the complex is not perfect. No bounded normalized source can map degreewise surjectively onto the unbounded contractible example. These counterexamples separate the sound strict criteria from the false arbitrary-representative formulations (CL011–014; Illusie,1971, ExposéI §§2,4; StacksPerfect; StacksPseudo).

### 3. PID dévissage and the category of bricks

The next sequence adds torsion disks D_i(a), with a nonzero nonunit in a PID. Smith decomposition gives elementary divisors and sphere factors. The Hom calculations are ordinary chain-map calculations: for equal-degree disks the upper and lower scalars satisfy m q=n p; adjacent-degree disk maps form a rank-one module; the remaining nonoverlapping cases vanish. Composition is componentwise scalar multiplication and matrix multiplication after direct sums. These checks require neither a derived localization nor a guessed Ext convention (CL015–019; Rasmussen/Marchand,2019, §3.3).

The source contains substantial index sensitivity. Deleting μ_i from the rank of the normalized term makes a single identity disk a counterexample. A saturation summand is labelled N_i[−i] although the diagram and quotient place N_{i−1} in degree i−1. The pushout diagram has an equality-looking middle arrow between an identity disk and a nonunit disk. Extension-class labels and a displayed ratio must agree with qm=np. These are separate reading alarms, not global renamings of the notation.

The number of torsion factors cannot replace the factors themselves: D₁(2) and D₁(3) have the same rank polynomial and numerical factor counts, but different homology. The omitted composition tables are routine in the concrete category; completing an abstract axiom system, extending to Dedekind/general bases, and transporting Γ remain distinct tasks. MP-SA09-04–06 preserve that division.

### 4. Density, coefficient functors and the universal cochain complex

Scans29–36 move from the brick list to reconstruction of categories and functors. Mere existence of colimit presentations by a full subcategory does not imply density of the set-valued restricted nerve. In finite-dimensional Q-vector spaces, A={Q} generates by finite coproducts, but its restricted nerve remembers only scalar actions. A homogeneous nonadditive map Q²→Q then defines a natural transformation not arising from a linear map. The additive-envelope statement is sound; the broad set-valued assertion needs canonical density rather than arbitrary generation (CL020–022; Isbell,1960).

The covariant coefficient functor M↦Hom(X,M) and its opposite dependence on X must be tracked separately. The source's finite tensor/Hom weights do not license arbitrary presheaf coends or unrestricted derived enrichment. Several limit arrows, diagram-type primes and opposite signs require image review. In particular, calling the finite-free complex category abelian is contradicted by2:Z[0]→Z[0], a noninvertible bimorphism. A later passage expressly warns about this phenomenon.

The programme becomes explicit at scans38–39: recover tensor and divided-power operations on the coefficient functor, compare the resulting cochains with de Rham data, and ask how much homotopy type they recover. This is the central question of the folder, not an incidental consequence already established by additive Yoneda. The words about reconstructing de Rham complexes are stronger than the proof supplied; the question about homotopy type is left open (CL023–024; MP-SA09-08–09).

The universal additive category then uses generators C^n and square-zero differential arrows. Its finite additive envelope is universal for nonnegative cochain objects in an additive k-linear target. Representing degree-zero cochain maps requires a kernel of du−ud between appropriate finite sums; evaluation is checked on generators. A same-index perfect-pairing formula fails at X=C^{n+1}; shifting the target of the second Hom to C^{n+1} gives a mathematical repair but no ink decision (CL025–029).

### 5. Relative exactness, evaluation and multiplication

The second package enlarges the model category to bounded nonnegative complexes of finite-free modules with degreewise exact sequences. These sequences are degreewise split, but that fact alone does not establish every proposed relative axiom system. Evaluation of a left-exact functor on the universal disk cochain complex is fully faithful; if the additive target has kernels, every cochain object extends by the finite kernel construction. The audit checks this restricted theorem using two successive cone sequences. It does not extend the conclusion automatically to an arbitrary prescribed class Σ in the target (CL030–032).

An exact contemporary public treatment by Hua was found in aNovember2026 preprint. Its detailed pages164–165 treat the restricted additive theorem and exclude wider relative variants; pages166–168 treat a homogeneous polynomial core and expressly exclude the full tensor/multigrading equivalence. This source is distinct from the absent internal modernization file cited by the older notes. The worker did not run the reported Lean sources. Both claims remain classC: a targeted proof in this run and a contemporary report are not evidence of established historical development by others.

The intrinsic description of P and its split-homology subcategory Q is inconsistent as transcribed. A sum of disks and spheres has free homology; D₁(2) has free terms, cycles and boundaries but torsion homology. The deleted H on scan60, retained H in the image description on61, and explicit Q on63 cannot all describe the same category. The exactness-testing paragraph on58 is too revised and uncertain to identify a unique axiom theorem (CL033).

Disk Hom arguments verify the relative projective/injective generators and the degree-zero sphere exception. Higher Ext statements need more. In particular, Q is not extension-closed in P: the degreewise split sequence0→S₀(Z)→D₁(2)→S₁(Z)→0 has endpoints in Q and middle object outside it. This does not by itself resolve every possible exact structure on Q; it blocks an automatic appeal to an inherited Quillen exact-category theorem. The comparison and derived-evaluation claims retain their exactness and resolution obligations (CL035–036; Bühler,2009, Definition2.1 and Theorem12.4).

Multiplication is defined degreewise in the simplicial realization and transported to complexes as *. It is not simply identified with ordinary total tensor. The functor's unit is an ordinary morphism, so “strong unit” is an unwarranted modernization. The contractible generator category K has no tensor unit; a larger category and a map into degree-zero cycles repair that issue. In the extension square on scan71, monicity proves uniqueness of a putative product, not its existence. A kernel-preservation condition is a sufficient repair, but it must be checked for the actual tensor (CL037–040).

The cup product is explicitly asserted up to homotopy, with signs. The interval Alexander–Whitney calculation distinguishes the ordinary cochain product from strictly commutative degreewise function rings. Berger and Fresse's established coherent cochain structure is an appropriate comparison target, not a proof that the page's displayed binary data already encode it (CL041; Berger & Fresse,2004). At scan73, the retraction of “non tronqué” makes the weight bound material. Good truncation preserves H^n; brutal truncation need not. The following characteristic-zero sentence does not itself supply rational coefficients or invert factorials (CL004,042).

### 6. Polynomial categories and divided powers

The closing sequence defines a category whose Hom groups are additive in one composition variable, with chosen multidegrees and representability by tensor products and Γ. Scalar weights alone fail in characteristic p, as the author observes: λ and λ^p act alike on p-torsion, although the corresponding polynomial laws differ over F_p[T]. The source therefore adds degree decompositions and considers all coefficient algebras. These are mathematical revisions, not a theorem that degrees were recovered uniquely (CL043–045).

“Strict” representability here means stability of a universal property with additional parameters; it does not mean identity associators. Under coherent exponential isomorphisms, the addition and zero maps give a graded commutative algebra on Γ*. A Hopf-object extension belongs to the earlier run and must stay labelled as such. Homogeneous substitution follows from α_{p,q}:Γ^{pq}→Γ^pΓ^q, naturality, typed coassociativity and degree-one units. One superscript in the square is ill-typed. Constants require an additional degree-zero axiom, which the author explicitly remembers after the initial list (CL046–048).

The announced equivalence with tensor-PD categories remains broader than this homogeneous construction. Mixed-degree composition, all parameter decompositions, constants, products and the relevant coherence must be audited together. Roby's module laws, SGA4's representability statements and Friedlander–Suslin's strict polynomial functors are precise established models, but none of the opened statements has the same arbitrary-category quantifiers as the whole manuscript equivalence (CL049; Roby,1963; SGA4XVII5.5.2; Friedlander & Suslin,1997, Definition2.1).

The last questions distinguish universal substitution α from actual divided-power evaluation Γ^pΓ^q(M)→Γ^{pq}(M). In rank one the composite multiplies by (pq)!/[p!(q!)^p], already3 for p=q=2; the maps are not inverse. The PD structure must be on a suitable positive-degree ideal, since unit-ideal PD over Z would force2γ₂(1)=1. Graded examples require a specified symmetry convention. Polynomial-law enrichment of the Hom modules is another condition, beyond set-level functoriality (CL050–051; StacksPD; Richter,2009).

## What the literature does and does not settle

The later public Pursuing Stacks text supplies genuine continuations: §119 records a concrete failure of a schematic/discrete comparison and poses a binomial alternative; §120 separates several operation theories; §121 distinguishes conservativity from full faithfulness; §128 discusses cosimplicial commutative algebras and restricts geometric intuition; §134 insists on normalization. These are verified textual correspondences, not evidence that every1970s question was resolved later in the author's published work.

Modern reconstruction results have different inputs and outputs. Sullivan's theorem is rational. Integral E-infinity, cosimplicial binomial, and affinization constructions come with their own finiteness, nilpotence or connectivity assumptions and mapping behavior. Their exact opened statements and locators are in the bibliography and claim map. None supplies the missing comparison functor from the complete strict structure actually specified in137. Recognition of objects, detection of equivalences, computation of a completion, and recovery of all mapping spaces remain separate questions (CL024; Mandell,2006; Toën,2020; Horel,2024).
