## 1. Projectivity, splitting and rank

Write D_i(P) for P --id--> P in homological degrees i,i−1 (i≥1), and S_j(P) for P in degree j. In nonnegative chain complexes of modules, D_i(P) is projective when P is projective, because its Hom functor is Hom(P,−_i). S_0(P) is projective for the same reason. An acyclic complex with projective terms splits from degree zero upwards: surjectivity onto the projective degree-zero term splits; its kernel is projective; repeat. A complex with projective terms and projective homology splits similarly into disks and spheres. This validates scan2 under its nonnegative convention. Splittings are choices; the boundary and homology modules are intrinsic up to isomorphism.

Lemma2 on scan4 fails after replacing acyclic/flasque by merely projective. Take the nonzero base ring k=Z, L=S_0(Z), q=q′=1, M=Z. L is projective in the nonnegative chain category. Its simplicial realization is constant Z, so L⊗Λ¹Ψ=Ψ; the target is also Ψ, and its identity is nonzero. This also avoids reliance on the inserted q=0 endpoint. If q′<q the support argument works; if q′=q it additionally needs H_q of the source to vanish. Acyclic L with projective terms supplies this via Eilenberg–Zilber.

Dold–Kan gives rank L_n=Σ_j binom(n,j)rank N_j. Hence D_i(P) contributes rank(P)binom(n+1,i), while S_j(P) contributes rank(P)binom(n,j). Coefficients are nonnegative for a nonzero connected base with finite-projective terms, so polynomial growth of bounded degree bounds normalized support. The leading coefficient in degree N is (μ_N+ν_N)/N!, not μ_N+ν_N. Evaluation at n=−1 gives Σ_j(−1)^jν_j because the positive-index disk polynomials vanish there. Constant objects require the j=0 sphere omitted in one version of scan7.

Tensor closure also holds in the strict free version. Each strictly special object is a base change of a finite sum of integral standard disks and spheres, as scan10 explicitly observes. For a tensor of two integral generators, Eilenberg–Zilber and Künneth give free homology, while the degreewise rank is the product of their rank polynomials. Nonnegative Dold–Kan ranks therefore force bounded normalization. Over Z all boundary modules are free, so the tensor is a finite sum of free disks and spheres. Base change to k proves strict closure. For projective coefficient modules, tensor the same integral decomposition with their projective tensor products. This argument avoids the invalid inference that equality of ranks implies freeness.

Ranks do not classify projective modules over a general connected base. Let R=Z[√−5], and I=(2,1+√−5). This is a nonprincipal invertible rank-one ideal. It is invertible since I times its conjugate ideal is (2) (use 4, 2(1±√−5), 6 to obtain 2). If I were principal, its additive index 2 would be the absolute norm a²+5b² of a generator; a²+5b²=2 has no integer solution. Thus S_0(R) and S_0(I) have equal rank and polynomial growth but are not isomorphic. This proof checks connectedness (R is a domain), finite generation and projectivity (invertible ideal). Classification becomes valid with actual projective coefficient modules, or with free coefficients and their ranks.

## 2. Strict and derived finiteness are different

An infinite-rank free identity disk is contractible, hence quasi-isomorphic to zero and perfect/pseudocoherent in the derived sense; its terms are not finitely generated. Thus ordinary pseudocoherence cannot be tested on terms of an arbitrary representative. Even with each normalized term finite free, the direct sum of D_{2r+1}(Z), r≥0, has normalized rank one in every nonnegative degree, is contractible, and has simplicial rank 2^n. It is derived-perfect but fails polynomial growth. No bounded normalized complex can map degreewise surjectively to it: normalization is exact and its arbitrarily high nonzero terms cannot be hit. Therefore the added epimorphic bounded-model claim also fails for arbitrary representatives.

For the opposite implication of the fiberwise claim, choose distinct primes p_i (i≥1) and N_i=Z/p_i, with zero differential. Each N_i and every simplicial degree of DK(N) is finitely generated over the regular noetherian ring Z. At a residue field F_p, precisely one normalized degree survives if p=p_i, and none survives otherwise; its ordinary fiber rank is binom(n,i), a polynomial. There is no uniform bound on the degrees i, and N has nonzero homology in arbitrarily large degrees, so it is not perfect. Derived fibers merely add a neighboring Tor term and still have individual polynomial rank. The source lacks a uniform bound and control of representatives. Over F[ε]/(ε²), the constant module F provides the separate nonregular obstruction: multiplication by ε gives an infinite periodic free resolution, so Tor_i(F,F)=F for all i, although the ordinary fiber rank is constant.

The strict criterion is unaffected: finite-projective normalized terms are equivalent to finite-projective simplicial terms by the finite direct-sum and direct-summand decompositions; strict perfection additionally requires bounded normalization. The filtration by normalized degree is legitimate. Derived perfection means existence of a bounded finite-projective model, not that every representative has those properties.

## 3. The nonlinear counterexample and PID bricks

For K(Z,1), the n-simplices are Z^n with the usual bar faces deleting an end coordinate or adding adjacent coordinates. In Γ², the degenerate submodule in degree2 is spanned by γ₂(a),γ₂(b). The remaining generator ab has alternating face sum −2γ₂(e), since γ₁(e)²=2γ₂(e). Above degree2 every weight-two monomial misses a bar coordinate and is degenerate. Hence normalized Γ²K(Z,1) is Z --(−2)--> Z in degrees2,1; its H₁=Z/2 is not projective. This verifies the author's withdrawal of nonlinear preservation of split/projective homology over Z.

Over a PID, a degreewise finite-free nonnegative chain complex decomposes by successive Smith forms into D_i(1), two-term D_i(a) with a nonzero nonunit, and spheres S_j(1); associated invariant factors, not just their number, are needed. A D_i(a) contributes H_{i−1}=R/(a). For maps D_i(n)→D_i(m), write p for the lower scalar and q for the upper scalar. The chain equation is m q=n p. Over Z a primitive solution is (p,q)=(m/g,n/g), g=gcd(m,n). Adjacent maps D_i(n)→D_{i+1}(m) form Z; other degrees vanish. Sphere and disk maps are obtained by the same chain equation. Composition is composition of the two scalar components and ordinary matrix multiplication after finite sums. This makes the obstruction qm−pn precise and shows why inverse ratio readings matter.

One disk D_1(1) falsifies the normalized-rank formula that omits μ_i: in degree1 its actual rank is1, while that formula gives0. D_1(2) and D_1(3) have identical term ranks, Hilbert polynomial, free-homology rank and number of torsion factors; their homology groups Z/2 and Z/3 differ. All complexes here are bounded finite free over the PID Z, so no hypothesis is evaded. Disks are nonzero in the ordinary additive category although acyclic identity disks become zero after homotopy localization.

## 4. Density, additive completion and the universal complex

The claim that arbitrary generation by colimits from a full subcategory implies density is false for the set-valued restricted nerve. In finite-dimensional Q-vector spaces take A={Q}. Every object is a finite coproduct of A. The restricted nerve remembers the scalar action on underlying vectors. The map h:Q²→Q with h(x,y)=x for y=0 and h(x,y)=0 otherwise commutes with every scalar, including0, so defines a natural transformation on A; it is not additive since h(1,0)+h(0,1)=1 but h(1,1)=0. Therefore the nerve is not full. The additive-enriched nerve and finite additive envelope do give the intended matrix argument. The corrected statement requires canonical A/X density, not mere existence of some colimit presentation.

For the walking cochain k-linear category with objects C^n, arrows d_n:C^n→C^{n+1} and d_{n+1}d_n=0, its finite additive envelope is universal for nonnegative cochain complexes in additive k-linear targets. A functor is determined by the images of generators and arrows; extension on finite sums is matrix substitution. All finite projectives need not be free: over k=k₁×k₂, the idempotent (1,0) on a free disk has image a projective nonfree disk. The finite-free subcategory is therefore not generally Karoubian. This is a counterexample to an unconditional version of the cancelled claim, not a correction to its surviving hypothesis.

The same-index pairing Hom(C^n,X)×Hom(X,C^n)→End(C^n) is not perfect: for X=C^{n+1}, the first factor is k and the second0. Replacing the target in the second factor by C^{n+1} gives composition into Hom(C^n,C^{n+1})=k d_n and a perfect pairing on finite sums of disks. This is a candidate index repair only. For any finite-free complex L and arbitrary complex λ, the transpose map Hom(λ,L)→Hom(L∨,λ∨) is an isomorphism: degreewise Hom(λ,k^r)=(λ∨)^r and Hom((k^r)∨,λ∨)=(λ∨)^r; the chain equations transpose. No reflexivity of λ is used.

## 5. Additive evaluation and relative exactness

Take P to be bounded nonnegative complexes of finite-free k-modules, Σ the degreewise exact sequences (automatically degreewise split), and B an additive k-linear category. Put C^n=D_{n+1}(k). For a cochain object E of B, define σ(E)(L) as the representing object, when it exists, of T↦Hom_{cochains}(L∨,Hom_B(T,E)). It is the kernel of a map between finite sums of E^i. If B has kernels it exists, sends Σ to kernel diagrams, and σ(E)(C^n)=E^n by the disk chain equation. Conversely, every Σ-left-exact F is reconstructed by a finite disk presentation: embed L into a contractible finite-free cone I(L), and embed its quotient into another such cone. F(L) is the kernel of the resulting map between F-values on sums of disks. This proves full faithfulness even if arbitrary kernels in B do not exist, since the relevant kernels already exist as F(L). The equivalence for B with kernels follows. These are targeted ordinary categorical proof checks; no universal claim about an arbitrary relative class Σ in B follows from this argument.

For Q, the bounded nonnegative finite-free complexes with free cycles, boundaries and homology, disks are relatively injective against the retained degreewise split sequences; projective generators also include S_0. However, Q is not extension-closed in P: 0→S_0(Z)→[Z --2--> Z]→S_1(Z)→0 is degreewise split, with both ends in Q and middle outside Q. Thus the available generator calculations do not by themselves make Q a Quillen exact category under this class. The exact structure and its closure axioms must first be specified. The same example distinguishes scan60/61 descriptions of P and Q. Kernel existence does not imply an abelian category: 2:Z[0]→Z[0] is mono and epi among finite-free complexes, since their degree-zero targets have no2-torsion; it is not invertible. General exact-category resolution comparison requires the composition and pushout/pullback axioms, not only the source's first two axioms. Higher Ext comparisons and derived evaluation still need an ambient abelian/functor category and enough resolutions.

## 6. Multiplication, truncation and polynomial structure

The monomorphism in the scan71 square proves uniqueness only. A valid sufficient reconstruction assumes that X*Y is the admissible kernel of I₀*J₀→(I₁*J₀)⊕(I₀*J₁), and that F preserves this kernel. Naturality then kills the composite from F(X)⊗F(Y) under both components, giving the required unique factorization. Checking this additional kernel property for the actual transported tensor is a separate task; it is not silently assumed. Associativity and choice independence follow only after the natural extension exists. The argument here checks the conditional repair, not the manuscript's unconditional extension theorem.

On interval cochains, Alexander–Whitney multiplication gives f∪e=0 and e∪f=1 for f(0)=0,f(1)=1,e([01])=1. This explains why transferred tensor and ordinary total tensor differ and why a cup product up to homotopy is not a strict commutative integral DGA. Good upper truncation has Z^n at the top; brutal truncation of the identity disk in degrees n,n+1 creates spurious H^n. Thus “cohomology through n” supports good truncation, but the ink remains undecided. Characteristic0 alone does not give 1/n!, as Z demonstrates.

Over F_p, λ^p=λ for every scalar, so scalar weights cannot recover degrees uniquely. Polynomial laws are instead natural over every coefficient algebra: T^p≠T in F_p[T]. In an additive category with coherent multidegree representations, Γ(X⊕Y)≅Γ(X)⊗Γ(Y); the fold map yields the commutative graded multiplication and zero yields its unit. A diagonal and negation also give a Hopf structure if the necessary strong symmetric monoidal coherences are present, but that extension belongs to the earlier note. Strict representation means preservation of the universal property with extra parameters, not identity associators.

For homogeneous maps f:Γ^pX→Y and g:Γ^qY→Z, define composition g Γ^q(f) α_{q,p}. Naturality and coassociativity of α, with Γ¹≅id and its unit identities, give associativity and degree-one identities by direct substitution. This proves only the homogeneous core. Mixed-degree sums, constant maps and the full tensor/exponential equivalence require additional axioms. In rank one α_{p,q} sends γ_{pq}(x) to γ_p(γ_q(x)); evaluation of the latter in the PD algebra is ((pq)!/(p!(q!)^p))γ_{pq}(x). At p=q=2 the factor is3, so these maps are not inverse. PD on the unit ideal of Z would require2γ₂(1)=1, impossible.
