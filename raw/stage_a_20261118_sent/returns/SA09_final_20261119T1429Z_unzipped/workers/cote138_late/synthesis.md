# Cote 138, scans 90–133: consolidation of the late package

## Scope and evidence

This package contains an interconnected investigation of peripheral fundamental groups, arithmetic comparison torsors, cartographic moduli, explicit three-point covers, small spherical maps, and operations on maps. Its final section asks when the conformal realization associated with a map is unique. These are the subjects actually developed on these leaves; the wider dossier title should not turn them into an unwritten discussion of the de Rham complex.

All 127 entries of `MPL_138_090-133_notes.md` were read in full, together with the relevant passages and the entire 1,683-line `MPL_138_090-133_transcription.tex`. The doubts file was also read. The transcription contains 43 scan markers: 90, 91, and 93–133. Scan 92 is absent. No source image, second-pass correction, Codex transcription, or original research certificate was supplied for this package. The present computation programs are fresh checks; the reading alarms leave the diplomatic text unchanged.

The 127 entries consolidate into 66 local items. There are 82 claims, including assertions and computations found by reading beyond the notes’ headings, and 24 problem records, MP-SA09-70–93. A problem record can describe a question already answered, a cancelled trial, an explicit drafting instruction, or an unproved construction. It is not a count of 24 currently open research problems. The items keep three layers distinct: the author's words, observations credited to the earlier transcription run, and reconstructions or new checks made here.

The arithmetic examples and later programme are particularly important for dating. The reused recommendation draft on scan 106 contains 11 January 1977; the university notices on scans 110 and 133 contain December 1977 dates. These dates belong to the reused matter and do not date every mathematical layer. The source does not contain a mathematical colophon authenticating a single composition date. In particular, neither a later theorem nor an archive catalogue date proves a priority claim about the entire packet. Exact loci are recorded in SA09-296.

## 1. Peripheral groups: the distinction between geometry and arithmetic remains essential

Scans 90–91 continue the definition on scan 89 in the preceding package. The hypothesis ν≥2 is explicit there, as are the individually indexed peripheral classes. This changes the assessment of the earlier note about an allegedly missing lower rank bound: the inner map is injective in the inherited range because a free group of rank at least two has trivial centre. The ν=1 failure is a useful boundary example, but is not a missing assumption in the complete passage. Both sides of this package boundary have been read (`MPL_138_046-089_transcription.tex`, lines 1664–1724, especially 1673; `MPL_138_090-133_transcription.tex`, lines 4–24; SA09-254–255; CL-SA09-138L-001).

The discrete picture is classical. For a sphere with ν+1 individually named punctures, the orientation-preserving pure mapping-class group is trivial when ν=2. In larger rank it is obtained from the pure sphere braid group by dividing out the central full twist of order two. Parametrized boundary circles would produce a different group. The uncertain quotient by Z/2 on scan 90 therefore matters mathematically and remains an ink question (Lochak, undated author manuscript, p.31; McReynolds, 2012, §1; CL002).

The identification of a profinite completion with its closure inside the automorphism group of a profinite free group is a separate theorem. It does not follow merely from an injective discrete action. The exact genus-zero congruence result is available in McReynolds (2012, Theorem 1.1, p.926), with the compatible closure sequence in Kent (2014, §4, Theorem 7). The earlier priority to Diaz–Donagi–Harbater, Asada and Thurston is reported from McReynolds’s primary account; the original 1989 and 2001 papers were not successfully opened. This is an established result, class B, with that bibliographic limit stated explicitly (SA09-257; CL003).

The two normality questions on line 44 must remain separate. The full extended subgroup is not normal in the full pure peripheral profinite outer group, and the equality of the oriented subgroup with the common-character kernel fails for every ν≥2. These are deductions of this consolidation, using a known finite arithmetic orbit. They are not theorems attributed to these handwritten pages or to a general identification with the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group (SA09-258–260; CL004–006; MP70–72).

Here is the obstruction. In rank two, the extended geometric subgroup is generated by complex conjugation, an involution. On the three sextic trees discussed below it acts as a transposition, whereas a suitable Galois commutator acts as a three-cycle. A subgroup generated by one transposition is not normal in S3: conjugating by a three-cycle gives a different transposition. The commutator has cyclotomic character one and is nontrivial on this orbit. In higher rank, choose all marked punctures rational and real, retaining 0, 1 and infinity. Killing the extra peripheral groups gives a quotient to rank two preserved by the specified peripheral automorphisms. The pure oriented geometric subgroup maps to the identity, and the extended subgroup maps to the same reflection subgroup. Arithmetic actions commute with this quotient. The rank-two obstruction therefore persists in every rank.

This argument is proved in `proofs.md`, §2, with its exact hypotheses and the finite certificate. It uses the sextic orbit already found in the 1992 catalogue and explicitly computed by Bétréma and Zvonkin (1993, §4.3, pp.607–608), also discussed by Jones (2013, Example 7.7). It does **not** refute normality of the oriented geometric subgroup inside the common-character kernel. In rank two that subgroup is trivial and normal. For ν≥3, the searches recorded here did not locate a result with the exact ambient group of the manuscript. Collas’s current survey (2026, §§2.2, 3 and 4.2, Definition 4.3 and Theorem 4.5) imposes extra tower and Belyi compatibility; dropping those hypotheses would change the theorem. The bounded negative search is not a claim that the question is universally open.

## 2. Torsors and inertia: several apparent identities depend on the acting group

Scans 93–96 transport fundamental groups, torsors, centralizers and inertia by a comparison torsor. The word “commutant” is supported by the explicit centralizer definition on scan 94. It does not mean that the group in question is commutative. A correctly typed contracted product transports the stated structures, but the inverse operation needs an opposite torsor and explicit left/right action conventions. Recognizing the standard construction does not certify the damaged inverse formula (`MPL_138_090-133_transcription.tex`, lines 61–130; SA09-262–265; CL008–010).

The comparison object cannot literally be the set of field isomorphisms from the algebraic closure of Q onto C. It can be the embeddings into C, or the isomorphisms onto the algebraic closure of Q inside C. The literal target is uncertain. Algebraicity and cardinality justify a reading alarm, not replacement of the written symbol. Similarly, an arithmetic action on the geometric fundamental group is canonical as an outer action; a chosen actual action requires a basepoint or a tangential point and path data. A product-one peripheral tuple requires compatible simultaneous choices, rather than arbitrary independent representatives (SGA 1, Exposé XII, Theorem 5.1; Exposé XIII, Corollary 2.12; Oesterlé, 2003, §§1–2).

At each rational puncture, characteristic-zero tame inertia is Zhat(1), and the arithmetic exponent is the same cyclotomic character. This is not a different arithmetic character at every puncture. In the later displayed cancellation, however, the independence is from the torsor element for a fixed puncture. These two kinds of independence should not be conflated. The image calculation is verified directly in `proofs.md`, §3 (SA09-266 and SA09-270; CL011–012).

The primitive-root torsor only sees the quotient by the cyclotomic kernel. It is not the original full nonabelian arithmetic torsor. The change from Γ to π and the reuse of C in the last paragraph are therefore substantive: some action or quotient data are missing under a literal reading. The finite S3 orbit supplies a concrete witness that the arithmetic group contains more than the cyclotomic quotient. The final orbit-set construction also forgets ramification multiplicities unless the orbit sizes are retained (SA09-269 and SA09-274; CL013–014).

## 3. Precartes and pinning: the draft's moduli argument needs an actual repair

The section on totally pinned maps begins with a finite weighted-set category. Its displayed fibre-cardinality law is not closed under composition. The counterexample uses weight sets (1), (2,2), and (2,4,4), with the first map having one fibre of size two and the second map having fibres of sizes one and two. Both satisfy the written law, whereas their composite would require every source weight to equal three. The auxiliary cell sets can be singletons with weight one. All displayed hypotheses are satisfied. The common-total-degree condition occurs only later and cannot be retroactively imposed on the earlier definition. This counterexample was already identified in the earlier notes and is independently verified here (`MPL_138_090-133_transcription.tex`, lines 225–267; SA09-275; CL015–016; `finite_maps.py`).

Cell labels also do not necessarily rigidify a map. The transitive permutation map

\[
r=(1234),\qquad \sigma=(13)(24)
\]

has one vertex, two edges and one face, hence genus one. Its oriented automorphism group is C4, and the square of r fixes every vertex, edge and face while acting nontrivially on the darts. The residual cell-pinning kernel is C2. The author's “vérifier” beside the rigidity claim therefore identifies a genuine dependency, not a routine omission. Fixing one dart would kill every automorphism of a connected transitive map, but that is a different marking (SA09-277; CL017; MP74; Guillot, author PDF, §§1.6–1.8).

Consequently, if γ relabels cells and K_C is the kernel of the cell action, the number of pinned isomorphism classes over a fixed map C is

\[
\frac{|\gamma|\,|K_C|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|},
\]

and the stabilizer for relabeling is Aut(C)/K_C. Neither an unweighted multiplication by |γ| nor identification of this stabilizer with the entire automorphism group is correct in general. The genus-one example has one pinned class, while the cubic star has two (SA09-279 and SA09-282; CL019 and CL022).

The representability claim fails more strongly than a counting formula. Over Q, consider the two smooth genus-one curves

\[
E_d:\ d y^2=x^3-x,\qquad f_d=x^2,\qquad d=1,2.
\]

Their branch fibres over 0, 1 and infinity have individual rational labels. They become isomorphic as labelled covers over Q(sqrt(2)), but are not isomorphic over Q with those labels. Thus the raw presheaf of rational isomorphism classes is not even an étale sheaf and cannot be represented by a scheme. The explicit proof checks the cover degree, local indices, labels and twist obstruction; it does not merely invoke the existence of automorphisms (`proofs.md`, §5; SA09-281; CL020).

The valid finite geometric-class construction survives. Finite continuous Galois sets correspond to finite étale schemes over Q (Stacks Project, Lemma 58.2.2, Tag 03QR). For a degree δ permutation cover, its monodromy group has order dividing δ!, so primes p>δ are prime to that order. The tame specialization framework for the smooth marked projective line applies to the finite geometric cover data (SGA 1, Exposé XIII, Corollary 2.9, construction 2.10 and Lemma 2.11). This supports the class scheme over Z[1/δ!], not the refuted raw moduli presheaf. Fulton's 1969 paper is retained only as a bibliographic lead from the notes: its publisher record was opened, but no theorem there was used for this exact functor (SA09-283–287; CL021–027).

The text genuinely reaches an arithmetic action on finite cartographic class sets and its commutation with relabeling. It says that the unpinned action *may* be nontrivial. It does not assert universal nontriviality, the converse that every algebraic curve admits a three-point map, global faithfulness, or regular-dessin faithfulness. These absence findings were checked against the whole late transcription, not inferred from missing headings. The later results belong to the chronology of Belyi’s theorem and the Esquisse, with their own dates (SA09-288 and SA09-295; Belyi, 1980, Theorem 4 and corollary; Grothendieck, 1984/1997, §3).

## 4. The calculations: the sextic is an exact known example, with a real degeneration

All seven rational maps on scans 104–105 were recalculated, including degrees, critical points and the fibres over 0, 1 and infinity. The allowance of unramified white points is essential: this part uses maps with folded edges, not only strictly clean dessins. The imaginary-part equation for 4z³/[27(z−1)] and the two pinned classes of z³ were also checked. Complex conjugation swaps those two labels through the cyclotomic character modulo three (SA09-304–306; CL028–036).

The substantial example is

\[
H=z^3(z-1)^2(z-c),\qquad
g=z^2-\frac{5c+4}{6}z+\frac c2,
\qquad H'=6z^2(z-1)g.
\]

Reducing H modulo g gives H≡Pz+Q, with

\[
P=-\frac{(25c^2-32c+16)(25c^3-12c^2-24c-16)}{7776},
\qquad
Q=\frac{c(5c-8)(25c^3-6c^2+8)}{2592}.
\]

This answers the explicit final question: zeros of P can indeed annihilate the quadratic discriminant factor. That factor is not extraneous algebra to be silently discarded. At c=16/25±12i/25 the two critical points coincide, and the normalized cover has a point of ramification index three over 1. Its white passport becomes (3,1,1,1), rather than the intended (2,2,1,1). The printed 24/25 alternative fails substitution, but the scan must decide why it appears (SA09-309 and SA09-311; CL038–045; MP77).

For the intended passport, the parameter algebra is obtained by imposing P=0 and inverting the discriminant and Q. It is the cubic algebra defined by

\[
R(c)=25c^3-12c^2-24c-16.
\]

A quotient by the product PQ would retain unwanted points: c=8/5 is an explicit witness with Q=0 but P≠0 and nonzero discriminant. The certificate includes Bézout and resultant calculations excluding every required collision, and the factorization H−Q=g²B on R=0. A mod-seven irreducibility test and the cubic discriminant show arithmetic group S3. The explicit transformation

\[
c=(4+18t+6t^2)/25,\qquad t^3=2
\]

identifies the parameter field with Q(cuberoot(2)). The three oriented classes comprise one reflexible class and a mirror pair, while their geometric monodromy is S6. These different groups must not be confused with the isolated Z/6Z annotation (SA09-307, SA09-309, SA09-315, SA09-323; CL037–048).

Backward literature tracing materially improves the earlier notes. Jones (2013, Example 7.7) is a precise match, but not the earliest located treatment. The author-hosted catalogue reports the relevant three conjugates in 1992; Bétréma and Zvonkin (1993, §4.3, pp.607–608) gives the identical sextic, cubic, field and orbit. Lando and Zvonkin (2004, §2.2.2.3, pp.92–94; hardcover released in 2003) explicitly explains the collision factor. The present work is a fresh exact certification and a manuscript comparison, not a novelty claim for this example.

## 5. The small spherical catalogue: finite verification with conditional picture identification

The catalogue on scans 114–120 was checked against an exhaustive enumeration of connected genus-zero permutation maps of dartdegree at most six, allowing involution fixed points for folded edges. The oriented counts in degrees one through six are 1, 3, 3, 9, 14 and 47; after identifying mirrors they are 1, 3, 3, 9, 13 and 42. These counts are this run's audit, not numbers attributed to the manuscript. The 29 explicit passport/group annotations tested on scans 115–116 have 28 matches and one conditional mismatch. The degree-five chiral pair and the theta-map group were separately checked (SA09-324–333; CL049–053; `finite_maps.py` and `catalogue_checks.py`).

Two reading alarms deserve priority. If the displayed (2,2)|(2,2)|(2,2) map is the usual planar digon and D4 means the conventional dihedral group of order eight, its full group is instead C2³. If the diagram on scan 119 is the nested planar three-loop bouquet, its full group is V4, whereas the retained annotation says C2. Both conclusions are conditional on the represented combinatorial map and on notation. They are not permission to correct ink or infer which line in a schematic drawing was intended (CL077–078).

Adrianov and collaborators provide a useful exact comparison: their 2007 catalogue, §§5.4 and 6.1–6.2, gives the oriented groups V4 for the digon and C2 for the nested bouquet. Their journal version appeared in 2009. Their convention is ordinary clean maps with at most four edges; it is not the folded-edge degree-at-most-six class or the full orientation-sensitive groups used in this audit. In particular, their total of 77 spherical maps is not the same enumeration merely because this run's oriented counts also sum to 77.

## 6. Operations and conformal uniqueness: retain the missing definitions

The heading on scan 121 calls the operation axioms provisional. The finite-quotient condition on a contravariant functor is a hypothesis, not a theorem about every such functor. For powersets it is immediate that subsets of a quotient correspond to invariant subsets upstairs. The proposed global-section formula needs actual transition arrows: cofinality of normal finite covers alone does not decide whether the displayed direct-limit sign has the right variance. The denominator in the universal powerset quotient is unfinished. These gaps remain explicit (SA09-334–341; CL054–058; MP79–81).

For finite embedded graphs, inverse image under a finite branched cover is again a graph after subdivision at branch values. The union with the original dessin requires a separate finite-intersection condition. Two finite embedded arcs can have infinitely many intersections. The repaired cellularity criterion is also facewise: a finite cellular graph A on the sphere has cellular inverse image under every connected finite cover branched in {0,1,infinity} exactly when each complementary face contains at most one of these three points. A double cover branched at two points in one face gives the annulus obstruction. Requiring A itself to contain at most one branch point is a different and false criterion. The proof is a labelled reconstruction, and the damaged negation remains in the scan queue (SA09-342; CL059–061; MP82; `proofs.md`, §8).

Postcomposition provides a precise arithmetic subclass of the proposed operations. Ellenberg’s manuscript circulated in 2000 and was published in 2002; Wood's circulated in 2003 and was published in 2006. Rational definition over Q and preservation of the branch set are essential to Galois equivariance (Ellenberg, §3; Wood, Proposition 2). Neither result classifies all natural operations, proves all six holomorphic conditions on scan 132 equivalent, nor guarantees preservation of full graph automorphism groups (SA09-375; CL075 and CL079).

Strict cleanness requires exact local indices. The square (2f−1)² preserves the Belyi property but does not universally make every index over 1 equal to two: f=z³ gives an immediate counterexample. The standard cleaning 4f(1−f) has a different white fibre. For universal strict-clean preservation, the outer map must satisfy the full local criterion in CL076: 0 and infinity map into {0,infinity}; 1 maps into {0,1,infinity}; if 1 maps to 1 the outer map is unramified there; every other preimage of 1 has index exactly two. An inequality at most two permits extra unramified white points and belongs to the folded-edge convention. The exceptional leaf at the old white point is explained by multiplication of local indices, not by an unwritten global equivalence (SA09-346, SA09-352 and SA09-373; MP83 and MP93).

The finite isotopy-kernel argument is sound under its actual hypotheses. A periodic disc homeomorphism fixing its boundary pointwise is the identity; Constantin and Kolev (1994, Proposition 3.2) give the opened proof. Applied face by face, this shows that the kernel of map-preserving isotopy is torsion-free, so a finite such kernel is trivial. It does not establish contractibility of an unspecified space of invariant rigidifications. Neither these late pages nor the supplied nearby middle pages define that space and its topology. The latter assertion therefore remains class F, not a theorem borrowed from a superficially similar contractible space (SA09-354–356; CL063–064; MP84–86).

Finally, equality of a Belyi function on a fixed surface is stronger than uniqueness of its isomorphism class. For every n≥1,

\[
F_n(z)=\frac{4z^n}{(1+z^n)^2}
\]

is strictly clean and realizes the n-orange of radial arcs from zero to infinity. Every positive dilation preserves the unparametrized map and is isotopic to the identity through such preservers. The functions F_n(az) vary with a>0. Thus the claimed absence of such automorphisms for oranges of order at least two, and the finite exception list following it, are false. The earlier notes credit Hua with the dilation observation; this run independently certifies a strict-clean model for all n. Fully colored-dessin realization theorems retain white vertices as extra data and do not contradict the example (Guillot, Theorem 2.8 and Example 2.9; Morvan, 2017, Definition 8 and Proposition 8).

A finite face with three distinct fixed boundary prime ends does give a rigidity criterion, by disc uniformization and the identity theorem. The infinite extension requires more hypotheses than the draft states. Cañas et al. (2022, §§1, 2.1–2.3 and Theorem 2) treats locally finite branched coverings, finite vertex degrees, finite-sided disc faces and tame countable actions. It is relevant later work, not a solution of an undefined unrestricted infinite version. The Esquisse's return to infinite cartography is correspondingly classified as a later formulation, not proof of this exact rigidity assertion (SA09-359–373; CL067–076; MP87–93).
