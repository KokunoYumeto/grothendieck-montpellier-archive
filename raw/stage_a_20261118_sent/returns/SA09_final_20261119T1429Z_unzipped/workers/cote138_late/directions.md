# Ranked research directions from the late cote-138 package

These are specifications for separate future tasks. None is developed in this list. The consolidation has already checked the finite counterexamples and calculations cited below; a later task should reuse those certificates rather than treat them as new discoveries. Ranking balances mathematical interest, relevance to the manuscript, and what a research run of a few hours could actually finish.

## 1. Repair cell pinning and its moduli functor

**Origins:** SA09-275, SA09-277, SA09-279, SA09-281, SA09-282, SA09-283, SA09-287 and SA09-289; CL-SA09-138L-015–026; MP-SA09-73–75. The source loci are `MPL_138_090-133_transcription.tex`, scans 98–103, lines 225–331, especially the verification margin on scan 100 and the omitted-definition instruction at line 281.

**What it would establish:** a version of the manuscript's finite moduli construction whose objects, morphisms, automorphisms, label actions and descent are all consistent. The task should distinguish a stack of covers, a rigidified moduli problem, and the finite étale scheme of geometric isomorphism classes; these are different answers to different questions.

**What is known:** the literal precarte law fails composition; cell pinning retains a C2 kernel on a genus-one bouquet; the explicit quadratic twist gives nonseparation of the raw presheaf. A single fixed dart rigidifies a connected transitive permutation map. The finite continuous Galois-set correspondence is established (Guillot, author PDF, Theorem 1.23 and Proposition 1.24; Stacks Project, Lemma 58.2.2). The tame specialization framework has exact prime-to-characteristic hypotheses (SGA 1, Exposé XIII, 2.9–2.11).

**First concrete step:** write a comparison table for the author's cell labels, one fixed dart, a full flag labeling and a specified branch-fibre point, using the existing genus-one twist as a mandatory test. Select one marking and formulate its functor before attempting a theorem. A few-hour run could complete the finite geometric category and explicitly state what is represented over Q; integral extension should remain conditional until its descent assumptions have been checked.

## 2. Specify the category of spherical operations

**Origins:** SA09-334–341 and SA09-375; CL-SA09-138L-054–058 and 079; MP-SA09-79–81. Source: scans 121–123, lines 1123–1201, with the direct-limit sign at 1173 and unfinished quotient at 1179.

**What it would establish:** one precise finite category in which the quotient by map-preserving isotopy is a functor, finite-quotient descent has a stated meaning, and the compatible-section construction has defined arrows. Only after that could a classification of subset operations be asked.

**What is known:** powerset descent under a finite quotient is elementary, and normal cores give a cofinal system of finite Galois covers (SGA 1, Exposé V; Stacks Project, §58.7). Rational Belyi-extending postcomposition is a precise arithmetic subclass (Ellenberg, 2000/2002, §3; Wood, 2003/2006, Proposition 2). It does not classify all natural operations or supply a missing universal denominator.

**First concrete step:** draw the indexing category with the direction of every covering map and induced pullback, then calculate its compatible families for a powerset functor on a very small normal-cover system. Read the scan's arrow before deciding whether an apparent direct limit is a transcription issue or a convention. One run should aim for a complete restricted construction, not an unrestricted reconstruction of all intended axioms.

## 3. Determine oriented normality in the exact peripheral kernel

**Origins:** SA09-258–260; CL-SA09-138L-004–006; MP-SA09-70–72. Source: scan 90, lines 44–48, with definitions in `MPL_138_046-089_transcription.tex`, scan 89, lines 1664–1724.

**What it would establish:** the answer, in a specified rank, to normality of the completed pure orientation-preserving geometric subgroup inside the kernel of the common peripheral character. This is the question left over after the full extended normality and equality obstructions checked in this dossier.

**What is known:** the full extended subgroup is nonnormal in every rank under the stated pure convention, and equality with the common-character kernel fails in every rank; the known sextic S3 orbit gives the obstruction. The oriented subgroup is trivial and therefore normal in rank two. The genus-zero congruence closure is known (McReynolds, 2012, Theorem 1.1), but current tower/Belyi-compatible descriptions impose additional hypotheses (Collas, 2026, Definition 4.3 and Theorem 4.5). None of the located results was identified with the exact unrestricted higher-rank normality claim.

**First concrete step:** freeze the ambient automorphism definition and write the rank-three puncture-forgetting maps, preserving named peripheral classes and the common character. Separate potential inner, geometric and arithmetic conjugators in an explicit finite quotient. A run can produce a meaningful obstruction or a rigorous reduction; it should not label a failed finite search as evidence of normality or global openness.

## 4. Classify finite holomorphic exceptions and minimal markings

**Origins:** SA09-359, SA09-361, SA09-363, SA09-366 and SA09-370; CL-SA09-138L-067–073; MP-SA09-87–89 and 91. Source: scans 129–131, lines 1521–1606.

**What it would establish:** a corrected classification, under explicit finite cellular hypotheses, of unparametrized embedded maps on a compact Riemann surface with a nontrivial holomorphic subgroup lying in the map-isotopy identity component. A second output would state which marked face centres or edge midpoints eliminate each residual subgroup.

**What is known:** every meridian orange has positive-dilation freedom, so the three-example exception list is incomplete. The strict-clean models F_n(z)=4z^n/(1+z^n)^2 are already certified. A face with three fixed distinct prime ends supplies a sufficient rigidity test. Fully colored-dessin uniqueness retains extra white-vertex data (Guillot, Theorem 2.8 and Example 2.9; Morvan, 2017, Definition 8 and Proposition 8) and cannot be quoted for the unmarked problem.

**First concrete step:** enumerate the possible one-parameter Möbius stabilizers consistent with the actual marked vertex and face data, keeping edge interiors unmarked. State the equivalence relation on embeddings before classifying its cases. A few-hour run can handle a bounded spherical case; higher-genus or infinite generalizations should be separate.

## 5. Define invariant rigidifications before proving contractibility

**Origins:** SA09-354 and SA09-356; CL-SA09-138L-063–064; MP-SA09-84–86. Source: scan 126, lines 1276–1330. The nearby middle-package reading did not provide the missing space or topology.

**What it would establish:** a precise interpretation of “invariant rigidification” and its topology, together with the exact hypotheses under which existence, realization and contractibility can be compared. The aim is to recover a well-posed assertion, not to prove a convenient substitute.

**What is known:** the finite-order disc argument proves that a finite map-isotopy kernel is trivial (Constantin & Kolev, 1994, Proposition 3.2; dossier proof §10). It does not imply contractibility of an unspecified object. Canonical conformal realization for colored dessins is also a different theorem. The exact project targets cited in the notes were not supplied, and the public partial Longue Marche edition has blank sections where some desired comparisons would lie.

**First concrete step:** obtain the scans and the exact later source passages, then list candidate data actually mentioned—cell parameterizations, marked centres, local charts, edge metrics, or another object—without choosing one from expectation. If the text still fails to define it, write clearly separated possible modern models and test only their typing and group actions. One run could identify the correct question; a proof of contractibility should follow only after that.

## 6. Separate strict-clean and folded-edge composition laws

**Origins:** SA09-346, SA09-351, SA09-352 and SA09-373; CL-SA09-138L-062, 065–066, 076 and 082; MP-SA09-83 and 93. Source: scans 125, 127–128 and 132, especially lines 1265–1267, 1445–1446, 1501–1518 and 1638–1647.

**What it would establish:** a precisely defined class of outer maps preserving all strict-clean covers, alongside the larger class preserving folded-edge maps, with their composition law and normalization conventions.

**What is known:** the local-degree criterion for strict cleanness is proved in this dossier. The square (2f−1)^2 is Belyi-preserving but not universally cleaning; 4f(1−f) has the appropriate strict-clean fibre. Wood (2006, Proposition 2) establishes branch preservation and Galois equivariance under rationality conditions, not the manuscript's inequality as a strict-clean criterion.

**First concrete step:** tabulate all outer maps of a chosen small degree satisfying the corrected local conditions, retaining the possible images of 1 and the exceptional unramified case. Verify the same candidates under the folded-edge convention. This should use the existing proof as input; it should not repeat the completed counterexample as the main result.

## 7. Compare the six holomorphic operation requirements

**Origins:** SA09-371, SA09-373 and SA09-375; CL-SA09-138L-075–076 and 079; MP-SA09-92–93. Source: scan 132, lines 1614–1647.

**What it would establish:** the correct implication diagram among preservation of compatibility on a fixed complex surface, isomorphism of canonical realizations, simultaneous compatibility of a representative with its union, and the three added automorphism-group requirements. The text lists these conditions but does not assert that they are all equivalent.

**What is known:** rational postcomposition keeps the source Riemann surface (Ellenberg, §3; Wood, Proposition 2). It need not preserve the entire graph automorphism group, and representative/union conditions depend on more data than an isomorphism class. The orange models show why “same surface,” “same map” and “same function” cannot be interchanged.

**First concrete step:** transcribe the six quantifiers into a single fixed finite category from direction 2, specifying whether each statement is about every representative, one representative, or a canonical one. Test the implications on the identity operation, the cleaning operation and one nontrivial rational Belyi-extending operation. The deliverable should be a complete finite implication diagram with bounded counterexamples, not a guessed universal equivalence.

## 8. Recover the exact graph/cellularity statement from the scans

**Origins:** SA09-342 and SA09-346; CL-SA09-138L-059–062; MP-SA09-82–83. Source: scans 123–125, lines 1196–1267.

**What it would establish:** a source-faithful corrected statement of graph pullback, union graphness and universal cellularity, distinguishing a graph from its complementary faces and retaining the allowed branch indices.

**What is known:** the finite facewise criterion and the double-cover annulus obstruction are proved in this dossier. The union of two finite embedded graphs need not be finite without an intersection hypothesis. The earlier context's condition on A itself is false; the proof does not decide whether the manuscript actually contains that reading.

**First concrete step:** inspect the negation, “au plus,” the apparent S¹/A_X alternatives, and the damaged union line in the original scan. Compare the resulting sentence with the already certified finite theorem. A short run with scans can complete the editorial issue; without scans, further algebra cannot settle the ink.

## 9. Turn the sextic method into a certified passport calculation

**Origins:** SA09-299, SA09-307, SA09-309, SA09-311, SA09-315 and SA09-319; CL-SA09-138L-037–047 and 079; MP-SA09-76–77. Source: scans 103 and 106–112, lines 331 and 433–787.

**What it would establish:** a reproducible calculation for one additional finite passport using derivative remainders, saturation by nondegeneracy factors, exact field identification and independent permutation enumeration. A general complete algorithm is a larger task.

**What is known:** the present sextic is already understood, including its collision branch and exact cubic algebra. Its published treatment goes back to Bétréma–Zvonkin (1993, §4.3) and the 1992 catalogue, with the detailed degeneration in Lando–Zvonkin (2004, §2.2.2.3). The logarithmic-derivative sketch suggests a method, but does not supply an elimination theorem or a complete numerator in the source.

**First concrete step:** select one next passport with a manageable number of oriented permutation classes, write every forbidden collision as an explicit factor, and define the saturated ideal before solving it. The finite permutation census should be an independent target count. Avoid selecting a large passport simply because software can begin the elimination.

## 10. Reconcile the spherical catalogue with its actual diagrams

**Origins:** SA09-307, SA09-324, SA09-327, SA09-328, SA09-330, SA09-332 and SA09-333; CL-SA09-138L-049–053, 077–078 and 080; MP-SA09-78 as a cancelled computation, not a revived conjecture. Source: scans 107 and 114–120, lines 487–494 and 794–1122.

**What it would establish:** a faithful diagram-to-permutation catalogue with explicit conventions for ordinary edges, folded edges, orientation reversal, duality and dihedral notation. It would resolve the conditional D4 and nested-bouquet alarms and determine which repeated drawings are intentional.

**What is known:** the exhaustive degree-at-most-six computation and 29 passport/group checks are already saved. Adrianov et al. (2007, §§5.4 and 6.1–6.2; journal 2009) provides exact oriented comparison examples. Its edge bound and symmetry convention differ from this source.

**First concrete step:** from scans, label the darts of each doubtful drawing, record the rotation and edge involution, and only then compare the resulting group with the handwritten label. Keep the signed full group separate from its oriented subgroup. A few hours can resolve the priority alarms; it cannot authenticate an absent scan through an abstract passport alone.

## 11. State a defensible infinite-cartography rigidity problem

**Origins:** SA09-363 and SA09-369; CL-SA09-138L-071 and 074; MP-SA09-88 and 90. Source: scans 130–131, lines 1574–1580 and 1597–1600.

**What it would establish:** an exact infinite version of the face-boundary rigidity question, specifying local finiteness, face degree, properness, accumulation at ends, prime-end compactification and the allowed isotopies.

**What is known:** the finite proof uses a disc face with well-defined distinct boundary prime ends and analytic continuation. The Esquisse (1984/1997, §4) returns to infinite cartography without solving this exact unrestricted question. Cañas et al. (2022, §§1, 2.1–2.3 and Theorem 2) treats locally finite branched covers and finite-sided disc faces under tame group actions; it does not remove those assumptions.

**First concrete step:** make a hypothesis-by-hypothesis comparison between the manuscript's finite proof and the modern infinite dessin definition. Identify the first step that fails for an infinite-sided or accumulating face. One run should aim at a well-posed restricted statement or a concrete obstruction, not an unrestricted infinite theorem.

## 12. Measure what arithmetic operations distinguish in a finite passport

**Origins:** SA09-299, SA09-309 and SA09-375; CL-SA09-138L-046, 057, 075 and 079; MP-SA09-76, 79 and 92. Source: scan 103, line 331; scans 121–132, lines 1123–1647. The proposal itself belongs to the earlier run's cross-comparison, not a completed manuscript result.

**What it would establish:** for a specified finite passport, the partition induced by one or more rational Belyi-extending operations, compared with known arithmetic orbits and familiar cartographic invariants. It would say precisely what extra information the operations detect.

**What is known:** rational postcomposition is Galois-equivariant, so its invariants are constant on arithmetic orbits (Wood, 2006, Proposition 2). Wood's Proposition 4 separates a particular degree-ten pair; it is not a universal separation theorem. The sextic S3 orbit provides a useful control case because no Galois-invariant operation can separate its three conjugates as different arithmetic orbits.

**First concrete step:** choose a passport with at least two known arithmetic orbits and a manageable permutation census, state the chosen extending maps over Q, and compare their induced invariants. Keep geometric monodromy, automorphism group and arithmetic orbit partition in separate columns. A few-hour run can produce one fully verified test, which is stronger evidence than an unsupported claim of universal distinguishability.
