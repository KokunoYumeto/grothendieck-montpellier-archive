## 1. Peripheral groups, low rank and congruence

Claims CL-SA09-138L-001–007 use the pure convention inherited from scan89: each puncture is individually named, and ν≥2. On a sphere with ν+1 punctures, the punctured fundamental group is free of rankν, with peripheral generators λ0,…,λν and product one. A nonabelian free group has trivial centre: a central word commuting with a basis generator is a power of that generator, and commuting with a second basis generator forces the power to be zero. Hence its inner-action map is injective. Reflection in a circle through the punctures reverses every positively oriented peripheral loop, up to conjugacy, and supplies the common sign−1. The oriented subgroup has sign+1. These statements concern punctures; parametrized boundary circles would change the group.

The pure mapping-class group of the three-punctured sphere is trivial. The extended group is C2, generated by reflection. For at least four punctures, the pure sphere braid group maps onto the pure mapping-class group with central full-twist kernel C2. Lochak’s opened author manuscript, p31, states this quotient. These observations explain the uncertain `/Z2` on scan90 without deciding its ink.

An embedding of a discrete group into an automorphism group does not automatically make its profinite completion embed in the profinite automorphism group. That additional assertion is the genus-zero congruence subgroup property. McReynolds (2012, Theorem1.1, p926) gives a full primary reproof for pure sphere mapping-class groups; Kent’s author manuscript (2014, §4, Theorem7) supplies the compatible point-pushing/closure sequence. We use these theorems rather than present a finite computation as proof of a profinite injection. McReynolds’s account reports Diaz–Donagi–Harbater1989, Asada2001 and Thurston’s2002 notes; the original1989/2001 texts were not opened, so independent priority verification stops at that report.

The weighted presentation on91 is the standard quotient by the peripheral relations λi^εi=1 for finite positive εi. If εi=∞, the corresponding relation must be omitted. An infinite exponent is not itself a meaningful group relation. This convention is our explicit interpretation, not a supplied definition.

## 2. A finite obstruction settles full normality and equality in every rank

This proves CL004 and CL006 and deliberately does not settle CL005. Put Γν equal to the profinite outer automorphisms preserving each peripheral conjugacy class up to a common unit power, and write Γν° for the kernel of that unit character. Let T̂ν be the closure of the extended pure discrete mapping-class group, and T̂ν° its oriented part. The assumptions are exactly the puncture-labelled ones inherited from89–90.

First take ν=2. By §1, T̂2=C2=〈τ〉 and T̂2°=1, where τ is complex conjugation. The three sextic trees in §6 form an S3 Galois orbit, with τ acting as a transposition: it fixes the real member and swaps the mirror pair. The exact example was already in the1992 catalogue and Bétréma and Zvonkin (1993, §4.3, pp607–608); Jones (2013, Example7.7, p12) also explicitly contrasts conjugation with the Galois3-cycle.

Choose g in the commutator subgroup of G_Q whose image on this orbit is a3-cycle. Such a g exists because the surjection G_Q→S3 sends its commutator subgroup onto [S3,S3]=A3. Since the cyclotomic character has abelian target, χ(g)=1. Conjugating the transposition ρ(τ) by ρ(g) gives a different transposition. If 〈τ〉 were normal in Γ2, then gτg⁻¹ would be τ (the only nonidentity element of this order-two subgroup), contradicting the finite action. Thus T̂2 is not normal. Also g has nontrivial image in Γ2° while T̂2°=1, so equality fails.

For general ν≥2 choose ν+1 distinct rational real punctures containing0,1,∞. Killing the normal closure of the extra peripheral groups gives F̂ν→F̂2. Every automorphism under consideration preserves that closed normal subgroup: each extra peripheral generator is sent to a conjugate of a unit power of itself, and the inverse automorphism has the same property. Therefore there is an induced map Γν→Γ2. Forgetting punctures sends the discrete pure oriented mapping-class group to the trivial three-puncture group; its closure still maps trivially. The full closure maps to〈τ〉, with reflection mapping to reflection.

The arithmetic G_Q-actions for rationally marked curves commute with this quotient, by the functorial étale fundamental-group construction (SGA1, ExposéXIII, especially2.10–2.12; Belyi,1980, §4). If T̂ν were normal, its image〈τ〉 would be invariant under the image of every arithmetic element. The same S3 conjugation contradiction applies. Hence

\[
\widehat T_\nu\not\triangleleft\Gamma_\nu
\qquad\text{for every }\nu\ge2.
\]

The same arithmetic commutator g belongs to Γν° and has nontrivial image in Γ2°, while every element of T̂ν° maps to1. Consequently

\[
\widehat T_\nu^{\circ}\ne\Gamma_\nu^{\circ}
\qquad\text{for every }\nu\ge2.
\]

This is an explicit deduction made in this consolidation, using a known finite orbit. It is not attributed as a theorem to the archive or to the cited papers. It does not show that T̂ν° fails to be normal in Γν°. In rank2 that subgroup is trivial and is normal. For higher ranks the bounded literature search did not identify a matching normality result. Collas’s2026 survey imposes tower and Belyi compatibility in its reconstruction statements; those hypotheses cannot be discarded.

## 3. Torsors, inertia and the common character

For a right Γ-torsor C and a left Γ-object Y, define C×^ΓY by (cγ,y)∼(c,γy). This construction transports groups, actions and compatible morphisms. Its inverse requires the opposite torsor with explicitly reversed actions. Thus the forward twisting diagram on93 has a precise standard interpretation, while the crowded inverse formula is not verified merely by recognizing the word torsor.

The literal Isom(Qbar,C) on94 is empty as a set of field isomorphisms: Qbar is algebraic overQ and countable, whereasC is not. Embeddings Qbar→C, or isomorphisms onto the algebraic closure ofQ insideC, form the intended kind of G_Q-torsor. The target is uncertain in the transcription; mathematics raises the alarm and does not decide which expression was written.

For a rational puncture in characteristic0, a local coordinate t and the covers u^n=t identify tame inertia with lim μn=Zhat(1). Arithmetic σ acts on roots of unity by a common χ(σ)∈Zhat*. Changing puncture changes the embedded peripheral subgroup and path comparison, not the arithmetic exponent. Actual actions on a based fundamental group require a basepoint or tangential/path choices; the outer action is canonical. A topological choice of peripheral loops has product one, but arbitrary independently chosen conjugate representatives need not retain that exact product relation. The text itself warns that the chosen comparison is not Γ-compatible.

On96 the displayed identities give, by direct substitution,

\[
\alpha'_i(\varphi\gamma)
=\beta(\varphi)\gamma_\pi\gamma_\pi^{-1}\alpha_i(\varphi)\gamma_{\pi_i}
=\alpha'_i(\varphi)\gamma_{\pi_i}.
\]

Since γ preserves Ii, the image α′i(φ)(Ii) is independent of φ. This does not make it independent of i. The contracted product must also use a group actually acting on C; the printed π-subscript is not justified by the earlier declaration that C is a Γ-torsor.

The primitive-root torsor sees Γ/kerχ, not the whole nonabelian arithmetic group. In particular the full torsor C cannot be replaced by lim μn* without quotienting by the cyclotomic kernel. The S3 action in §6 supplies a concrete nonabelian quotient, so the distinction is substantive.

Finally an inertia orbit of sizee in the geometric fibre corresponds to a ramification point of indexe. The orbit set alone discards the sizes; it must not be called a complete ramification invariant. This is the covering interpretation used in SGA1 XIII2.12 and Oesterlé (2003, §§1–2).

## 4. Precartes, cell pinning and the correct counts

### 4.1 The literal morphism rule is not closed under composition

Take only the S-set nontrivial, with A and F singleton of weight1 and identity maps. Let the target S0 have one point of weight1. Let S1 have two points, each of weight2, both mapping to S0. Let S2 have three points of weights2,4,4; send the first to the first point of S1 and the last two to the second. For S1→S0 the fibre size is2 and every source weight is2·1. For S2→S1 the fibre sizes are1 and2 and the weights are1·2 and2·2. Both maps satisfy the printed rule p′(s′)=#fibre·p(s). Their composite has fibre size3, so the rule would require every S2 weight to equal3, which none does. All sets are finite, all weights positive, all maps surjective, and the A/F conditions hold identically. No common-total-degree condition was imposed in the precarte definition at98. Hence it is a counterexample to the literal category assertion, not to a stronger corrected definition.

The actual cubic-star covering z³→z also violates this literal facet rule: over the sole black vertex the source weight3 would have to equal1·1; over the three white points the source weights1 would have to equal3·1. This shows why the claimed obvious functor cannot repair the bad rule by itself.

### 4.2 Cell pinning leaves an automorphism

Use r=(1234), σ=(13)(24), and face permutation r⁻¹σ=r on four darts. The generated group is C4, transitive. There is one black vertex, two edges, one face, so V−E+F=1−2+1=0 and the map lies on a torus. The centralizer of r and σ is〈r〉≅C4. Its element r² fixes the unique vertex, each of the two edge cycles separately, and the unique face, but permutes darts. Thus the cell-pinning kernel K_C is C2. The precise cycles and all centralizers are in `finite_maps_summary.json`.

For a fixed unpinned map C, let γ be the product of symmetric groups relabelling equal-degree cells. Its set of actual labelings is a γ-torsor. Quotienting by Aut(C), whose kernel on cell labels is K_C, gives

\[
\#\{\text{pinned isomorphism classes over }C\}
=\frac{|\gamma|\,|K_C|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|}.
\]

The relabelling stabilizer of a pinned class is Aut(C)/K_C. For the torus example, |γ|=2, |Aut|=4, |K|=2: there is one pinned class and relabelling stabilizer C2. For the cubic star, |γ|=3!=6, Aut=C3 and K=1, so there are two pinned classes, agreeing with scan104. Neither uncorrected multiplication by |γ| nor identifying the stabilizer with the full automorphism group is valid in general.

A flag-pinned connected map has no nontrivial automorphism: a commuting permutation fixing one dart fixes its entire transitive orbit. This is a valid repair, but the source pinned cells, not a flag.

### 4.3 Degree and type

Summing local indices over any fibre of a finite cover gives its degree. Thus the three weighted cell sums agree. In the ordinary-map convention complete edges contribute2 darts and folded edges contribute1. Connectivity of a graph with V vertices needs at leastV−1 complete edges, so δ≥2(V−1). These elementary bounds explain the impossible catalogue rows. They do not identify the isolated6N notation.

A connected finite cover determines its monodromy representation up to simultaneous conjugacy; the core-free model is E=G/H with intersection of all conjugates ofH trivial. Conversely this faithful transitive action recovers G as a permutation group. These data are equivalent to the marked cover, not merely the underlying curve. Therefore the negative phrase on102 cannot literally say that the dessin type is not determined by X→P¹ with its three marked branch values. Guillot’s author PDF Proposition1.24 explicitly states the core-free finite-group form.

## 5. Why the raw pinned moduli functor fails, and what remains valid

Consider the smooth genus-one curves overQ

\[
E_d:\quad d y^2=x^3-x,\qquad d=1,2,
\qquad f_d=x^2:E_d\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1.
\]

The cubic x³−x has distinct roots0,±1. The x-map has degree2, branched at0,±1,∞; composing with x² gives degree4 and passport(4)|(2,2)|(4). At0 the two local indices2 multiply to4; at±1 the x²-map is unramified and the index remains2; at∞ it is4. The Riemann–Hurwitz sum3+1+1+3=8 gives2g−2=−8+8=0. Thus these are connected smooth tame covers in characteristic0, with precisely the required branch set and white index2. All their black, white and face points are individually rational and can carry the same cell labels.

Over Q(√2), the map(x,y)↦(x,y/√2) identifies E1 with E2 and commutes with f. It preserves all labels. Suppose there were a label-preservingQ-isomorphism commuting with f. Then x′²=x² in the function field, so x′=x or−x. The individually labelled points over1 distinguish x=1 fromx=−1, forcing x′=x. An isomorphism of the two quadratic extensions overQ(x) would therefore require1/2 to be a square inQ(x). It is not: a rational function whose square is constant is constant, and√2 is not rational. Thus the two objects are nonisomorphic overQ but become isomorphic after a finite étale extension. The presheaf of their isomorphism classes is not even separated for the étale topology. A representable functor is a sheaf, so the raw functor cannot be represented as asserted on100.

This does not refute the finite scheme of geometric classes. Any finite continuous G_Q-set gives a finite étale Q-scheme (Stacks Project, Lemma 58.2.2). For fixed degree δ, there are finitely many transitive permutation triples. Their geometric isomorphism classes therefore form such a set. A Galois closure has group contained in Sδ, so its order divides δ!. At a prime p>δ this order is prime to p. Tame specialization for a smooth proper marked curve with disjoint sections, as in SGA 1, Exposé XIII, Corollary 2.9, construction 2.10 and Lemma 2.11 (recomposed edition pp.288–290; original pp.389–392), transports the corresponding prime-to-p cover data and preserves its inertia; the finite class action is unramified there. Corollary 2.12 is used separately for the peripheral presentation, not as the sole specialization justification. This gives the extension of the finite geometric-class scheme over Z[1/δ!]. It does not restore fine representability of rational objects, nor prove all family statements made in the draft.

Fixed finite monodromy type is locally constant in this tame finite-class setting. A clopen subset of the resulting finite étale class scheme follows. For arbitrary bases and a raw moduli functor, the same conclusion needs the corrected stack/descent formulation; the page does not supply it.

## 8. Finite operations, descent and cellularity

For the inverse-image powerset functor, subsets ofX/H correspond exactly toH-invariant subsets ofX. This proves the stated quotient-descent hypothesis in that example, not for an arbitrary functorF. If finite-map morphisms are genuine branched-cover morphisms and an isotopy of the target stays in its map automorphisms, lift the isotopy over the complement of branch points and extend via the local branched-disc model. The lifted endpoint gives the orbit-set pullback independent of representative. The notationAut(X)° must refer to this map-preserving group; arbitrary surface automorphisms cannot be substituted. Independence of isotopy choices uses the absence of finite deck transformations in the isotopy kernel from§10. A full general functor theorem requires these conventions to be stated.

Global sections mean compatible families. Normal cores make normal finite covers cofinal among finite covers. Neither fact determines the displayed `varinjlim` without its transition arrows. The correct indexing convention may reverse an order; the manuscript does not give it. Similarly the unfinished powerset quotient does not prove that every natural operation is obtained from a reference subset.

Conjugation-stable subsets of the sphere correspond to subsets of its closed-hemisphere quotient. A boundary-preserving hemisphere homeomorphism can be doubled to one commuting with reflection. This explains the unoriented construction, subject to its boundary-marking and identity-component conventions; it does not imply arithmetic equivariance.

For a finite embedded graphA and a finite branched coverf:X→S², subdivideA at the finitely many branch values it contains. Away from themf is a covering; near them it is topologicallyz↦z^e. Thusf⁻¹(A) is a finite embedded graph. The converse universal assertion follows by takingf=id. However,A∪[0,1] need not be a finite graph merely because each constituent is: an arc given near0 byy=x²sin(1/x), with its endpoint at0, meets a horizontal arc infinitely often. The union then has infinitely many local incidence changes. The extra finite-union condition in the damaged source is mathematically necessary.

Here is the precise finite cellularity repair. AssumeA is a nonempty finite cellular graph onS² andB={0,1,∞}. Thenf⁻¹(A) is cellular for every connected finite cover branched only inB if and only if each faceU ofA contains at most one point ofB.

For sufficiency, an unbranched finite connected cover of a disc is a disc. A finite connected cover of a punctured disc is the cyclic cover of some degreee; filling the single branch point gives a disc. Hence every component off⁻¹(U) is a disc. The inverse graph is also connected: attaching discs to its connected components cannot join two components, whileX is connected. Thus it is a cellular map.

For necessity, if one face contains two distinct points b1,b2 of B, choose the connected double cover of S² branched exactly at b1,b2. It exists explicitly after a Möbius change of coordinates from z↦z². Take a compact closed subdisc D inside the open face U containing both points. Its inverse image is connected, has Euler characteristic 2χ(D)−2=0, and has two boundary components: the total boundary monodromy is the product of two transpositions, hence the identity. It is a compact annulus. Exhausting U by such discs shows that the inverse image of the open face is an open annulus with two ends, rather than an open disc. An open face itself has no literal boundary components. If the universal condition is initially stated without assuming A cellular, the identity cover first forces that condition; on S² a nonempty finite cellular graph is connected. The local formula does not require A itself to contain at most one branch value; it concerns each complementary face.

A finite graph with chosen vertices, edge midpoints and face centres gives a topological cover by subdividing into flags and gluing copies of the reference triangles. Arbitrarily chosen geometric marks do not automatically give a holomorphic map in a fixed complex coordinate. Pullback has the exact set identityf⁻¹(φ⁻¹[0,1])=(φ∘f)⁻¹[0,1]. Arithmetic equivariance additionally requiresφ defined overQ. Ellenberg (2000/2002, §3) and Wood (2006, Proposition2) develop that rational subclass. No complete universal subset-operation classification is asserted here.

## 9. Composition indices, the endpoint exception and strict cleaning

For nonconstant holomorphic maps the local expansions give

\[
e_{\varphi\circ f}(x)=e_\varphi(f(x))\,e_f(x).
\]

For every strict-clean Belyi mapf (all local indices above1 equal2), postcomposition byφ again has that property if the following conditions hold:

1. φ is Belyi andφ({0,1,∞})⊂{0,1,∞}.
2. φ(0),φ(∞)∈{0,∞}.
3. Ifφ(1)=1, theneφ(1)=1.
4. Every othert∈φ⁻¹(1) haseφ(t)=2.

At points with t outside {0,1,∞}, f is unramified; at old 1 its index is 2. These observations prove sufficiency. For necessity, every φ-critical point is hit by f, so a critical value outside the target branch set is forbidden. Also φ(1) must belong to {0,1,∞}: every point above old 1 has inner index 2, so otherwise the composition would have a branch value outside that set. For 0 and infinity use the strictly clean family f_n(z)=4z^n(1−z^n), n≥3. Its local index at 0 is n, its local index at infinity is 2n, and f_n−1=−(2z^n−1)^2 gives index exactly 2 at each point above 1. The unbounded indices force φ(0),φ(∞) into the branch set and prevent either from being 1. No dual variants are needed. At old 1 the fixed inner index 2 then gives condition 3; all other preimages of 1 have inner index 1, giving condition 4. Thus the criterion is exact for universal strict-clean preservation. The manuscript’s weaker ≤2 may encode folded edges, and cannot silently be read as strict equality.

If the new reference graph ends at old1, the local mapz↦z² pulls its ray back to two rays joined into an ordinary edge interior. This explains why old1 need not become a new vertex. The stronger containmentφ⁻¹{0,∞}⊃{0,1,∞} excludes this situation and is not necessary:φ=id preserves strict cleanness.

The quadraticq(t)=(2t−1)² is Belyi and maps0,1 to1. Henceq∘f is always Belyi, but not always clean: forf=z³,

## 10. Finite-order rigidity within a map-isotopy class

LetC be a finite connected cellular map andh a finite-order orientation-preserving map automorphism isotopic to identity through map automorphisms. During such an isotopy the induced permutation of finite vertices, edges, faces and incidence flags is constant. Thus it is the identity permutation.

On each edge interval the induced map preserves endpoints and is increasing. A nonidentity increasing interval homeomorphism cannot have finite order: ifh(x)>x, its finite iterates strictly increase until they would return tox; the caseh(x)<x is analogous. Thus every edge is fixed pointwise. For each face, use its abstract closed-disc compactification, retaining repeated boundary incidences as separate intervals. The induced periodic disc homeomorphism fixes the boundary pointwise. Constantin and Kolev (1994, Proposition3.2) prove it is identity. Henceh is identity on every face and onX.

Every element of a finite subgroup has finite order, so a finite isotopy kernel is trivial. Conversely a trivial kernel is finite. This proves a⇔b under the explicit finite-map hypotheses. It does not prove that an arbitrary infinite isotopy kernel vanishes: positive dilations of an orange map give an infinite cyclic subgroup, all map-isotopic to identity. It also does not define or prove contractibility of an invariant rigidification space.

## 11. Holomorphic uniqueness, orange maps and extra markings

A cover isomorphism overP¹ is holomorphic off the finite ramification set by local inverse branches, and extends holomorphically across it by the local degree model. If two such isomorphisms induce the same combinatorial map, their quotient is a finite deck transformation in the map-isotopy kernel and is identity by§10. This proves uniqueness of the isomorphism in that prescribed class. It does not say the isomorphism itself is identity as a self-map of the fixed surface.

For every integern≥1 set

\[
F_n(z)=\frac{4z^n}{(1+z^n)^2}.
\]

Its zeros are0,∞ of indexn, its fibre over1 isz^n=1 with index2 at every point, and its poles arez^n=−1 with index2. These lists account for the degree2n and all ramification. To determine its graph, write t=z^n. Forw∈(0,1), solving4t/(1+t)²=w gives two positive real reciprocal roots. Atw=0 the roots are0,∞. ThusF_n⁻¹([0,1]) is exactly the union ofn radial arcs from0 to∞ withz^n≥0, and its black vertices are{0,∞}. Its complementary components are open sectors, hence discs. This is precisely the finite n-orange map, including all strict white-index hypotheses in129.

For everya>0, h_a(z)=az preserves every edge and face, fixes0,∞, and is map-isotopic to identity througha^t z. It is nonidentity fora≠1. The functionsF_n∘h_a have the same graph and black vertices but differ: their leading coefficient near0 is4a^n instead of4. Hence the claim that orangesn≥2 have no such automorphisms and the resulting finite exception list are false. Earlier-run notes explicitly credit Hua for the dilation observation; this run independently certifies the strict functions. It does not claim a new discovery.

On the real equator, Möbius transformations preserving each half-plane formPSL2(R). Fixing∞ givespositive real affine mapsaz+b, a>0,b∈R. Fixing0 as well givespositive dilations. A two-endpoint interval has the same one-dimensional group after conjugacy. Therefore the complex-affine and complex-multiplicative labels on130 are too large under the stated real/equatorial condition.

For a finite holomorphic cellular map, a face is a disc with a locally connected finite-graph boundary. Its prime-end compactification separates repeated boundary incidences. A map-isotopic holomorphic self-map fixes those incidences individually. If one face has at least three distinct boundary prime ends, its disc automorphism fixes three boundary points and is identity. The analytic identity theorem then gives identity on connectedX. This proves the author’s finite sufficient criterion; it does not prove the proposed list of all exceptions. Infinite maps can fail the finite-boundary hypotheses and must be specified separately.

Marking a nonreal point kills the real affine freedom:az+b=z witha>0,b∈R andImz≠0 impliesa=1,b=0. Marking a nonzero finite point kills positive dilations. Marking only one real point in the affine case leavesa free withb=(1−a)z. These calculations verify the author’s face-centre and midpoint statements forI,II,III, but not an unrestricted global uniqueness theorem.

Modern conventions can hide the issue. Morvan (2017, Definition6 and Definition8, §§9,11) retains the white vertices and chooses a canonical conformal realization; Proposition8 uses this stronger datum. Positive dilations of the unmarked orange graph move its white midpoints, so the propositions concern different objects. Cañas etal. (2022, §§1,2.1–2.3, Theorem2) explicitly assume locally finite coverings, finite vertex degrees, finite-sided disc faces and tame actions. Their result does not answer the archive’s unqualified infinite-map question. Neither source defines the space of invariant rigidifications in126.

## 12. What the final operation conditions do and do not assert

The three unprimed conditions on132 distinguish: compatibility on a prescribed complex surface; isomorphism of canonical complex realizations; and simultaneous compatibility for a chosen representative, the old map and their union. Their primed versions add equality of holomorphic symmetry groups. The page says to consider these conditions, not that all are equivalent. Rational Belyi-extending postcomposition gives an important source-preserving construction (Ellenberg,2000/2002; Wood,2003/2006), but its Galois naturality alone neither identifies the union geometry nor preserves every unparametrized graph symmetry.
