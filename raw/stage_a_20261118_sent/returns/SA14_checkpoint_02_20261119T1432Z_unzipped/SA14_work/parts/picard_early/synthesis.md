## Connections and the uncomputed mixed class

The first mathematical sequence defines connections by splitting principal parts, or equivalently by a Leibniz operator. Local connection forms change by dlog of a unit. This produces the obstruction of a line bundle in H¹(Ω¹), and, when the obstruction vanishes, the torsor of possible connections modulo the action of automorphisms. The continuation at scan89 explicitly starts with a relative Picard section represented by an actual line bundle; it is not a proof that every section has such a representative. C-SA14-001–004 and computations§1 preserve this point.

The abelian argument then uses the dual torsor, the vanishing of homomorphisms from an abelian scheme to a vector group, and a normalized Poincaré bundle. Restriction to the two zero sections kills the pure Künneth terms. It leaves a mixed term, whose identity normalization and sign are expressly uncomputed on scan94. C-SA14-008–009 retain that boundary; the symbolic recheck deliberately leaves the mixed component undetermined.

Mazur–Messing’s later universal-extension theorem gives a precise comparison in the abelian setting, with rigidifications and integrable connections compatible with the group law. The manuscript initially considers **all** relative connections. This distinction prevents a modern theorem’s name from silently filling the gap or changing the object. The inference at scan83 from degree-zero cohomological flatness of Ω¹ to a locally free R¹f_*Ω¹ remains a hypothesis/terminology alarm. The cancelled scan88 torsor interpretation and the cancelled absolute-connection paragraph at94 are separate claims, C-SA14-142 and C-SA14-010, rather than one generic “connection problem.” [PE_MazurMessing1974, I.2.6.7 and§3; SA14-12–15; MP-SA14-001–005,054.]

Oda’s later comparison has also been separated from this sequence and from Oort’s role in the formal Picard proof. The opened Oda paper distinguishes its abelian de Rham/Dieudonné identification from a more general smooth-proper injection. Neither statement is a universal splitting theorem. [SA14-16; PE_Oda1969, Corollaries5.11–5.12.]

## Hyperplanes: three different assertions

The scan97 table is a collection of differently qualified rows concerning Pic, Pic⁰, Pic^τ and discrete quotients. It must not be converted into one unconditional Lefschetz theorem. The conditional integral Picard rows and the stabilized thickening statement have precise published counterparts in SGA2. The final request to remove negative-twist vanishing assumptions remains a distinct question. Modern flat-cohomology comparison results retain their own syntomic, strong-ampleness or Hodge hypotheses. [C-SA14-011–018; PE_SGA2, XII.3.6 and4.8–4.10; PE_CotnerZavyalov2024, §3.]

One row is false as transcribed: bijectivity of Pic⁰ for a general smooth hyperplane already in dimension2. The counterexample must specify the embedding. Take X=P² over C embedded in P⁹ by O(3). A general hyperplane then cuts a smooth plane cubic. Pic⁰(P²) is zero, whereas the cubic has a one-dimensional Pic⁰. The Fermat cubic proves that the smooth locus in the hyperplane system is nonempty; the exact genus and ambient-dimension computations are saved. Using a cubic in P² with its ordinary O(1) embedding would fail the hyperplane hypothesis. A possible intended threshold3 is an ink question and has not replaced the source reading. [SA14-18; C-SA14-015; computations§3; readings.tsv.]

The generic-hyperplane assertion at scans99–100 is different. Its source cohomology is defined over the original field k, while the target lies over a geometric generic hyperplane. Injectivity on this original-field source cannot automatically be promoted to injectivity after extending the source. The elementary map k²→k(t), (a,b)↦a+tb, makes the logical distinction explicit; it is not advertised as a geometric counterexample. The group class, topology, differential restriction lemma and characteristic-zero specialization step still need precise formulations. [SA14-19–22; C-SA14-019–025; MP-SA14-008–009; computations§§4–5.]

The cancelled unrestricted Picard injection has a separate Segre-surface counterexample: restriction from P¹×P¹ to a smooth(1,1) hyperplane sends(a,b) to a+b and kills(1,−1). This supports the cancellation but does not refute the surviving assertion with multiplicative factors excluded. The αp and μp formulas likewise require separate exact sequences: F and1−F cannot be interchanged, nor can additive functions and multiplicative units. These are recorded as mathematical alarms without choosing the doubtful ink. [C-SA14-023–025.]

## Divisors, descent and projectivity

The normal proper Picard-component argument and the Laurent-invariance argument have different exact references. FGA VI Theorem2.1 proves the properness statement. Weibel Proposition7.4 proves normal Laurent Picard invariance; evaluation at t=1 independently gives the easy injective half. The A¹ argument in FGA is related but is not the Laurent statement itself. [SA14-23–24; C-SA14-026–027; PE_FGAVI1962; PE_Weibel1991.]

The Seshadri-style construction is one of the places where exact hypotheses matter most. The primary EGA IV₄ text was read directly at §§21.14.3–21.14.4. Its smoothness/normality assumptions and its separate product case do not establish every broader preliminary claim on the manuscript pages. Universal openness of a divisor’s support, quasi-finiteness of a parameter map, flatness of an induced relation, and existence of a quotient are different steps. The file records which local calculations work and which implications remain unsupported. The published Seshadri article’s metadata says1963, after receipt in1962; an earlier bibliographic year is not silently used as its publication date. [SA14-25–29; C-SA14-028–041; PE_EGAIV4; PE_Seshadri1963.]

Each proof keeps the assumptions needed by its mechanism. The target P¹ in the map[x:y] from punctured A² shows why an affine-target extension argument cannot be generalized by merely replacing its target. This is not the separate relative quasi-affine/non-affine example asked for in the Murre letter, which the root part indexes independently. [computations§§7–9; SA14-31.]

## Analytic and formal Picard comparison

The letter dated6July1962 and the later French proof both assert a necessary-and-sufficient finite-length condition. The source is the Picard group of an analytic germ, defined through neighborhoods, not the Picard group of the closed fibre alone. There are three distinct assertions: injectivity of the germ-to-formal map, equality of the images seen at each finite level, and existence of one germ realizing an entire compatible formal class. The last assertion is where the finite-length criterion enters. [SA14-46,48–51; C-SA14-070–082.]

The proof’s coherent input is supplied by Grauert; its integral input requires finite generation and control of the relevant stabilized images. Łojasiewicz’s triangulation theorem has Euclidean/semianalytic scope, while Goresky’s later theorem handles abstract stratified objects. Compatible analytic stratification is explicitly carried as background rather than hidden inside the word “compact.” The exponential-sequence comparison and the analytic finite-module completion criterion are independently explained in computations§§12–14. [PE_Grauert1960 and1963; PE_Lojasiewicz1964; PE_Goresky1978.]

A significant intermediate inference is too strong: formal-functions comparison for a coherent complex does not imply surjectivity onto its cohomology at every finite order. The exact two-term complex with multiplication by t has nonzero finite-order kernels whose transition maps are zero. This refutes that inference. It is explicitly **not** presented as a proper analytic family contradicting the final Picard theorem. The manuscript’s repaired language of universal finite-level images is the relevant distinction. [SA14-49; C-SA14-081; computations§13.]

The projectivity argument needs a compatible ample class. ML can stabilize the image at the closed fibre and support selection of such a class; it does not say that the entire Picard groups eventually become constant. For a proper algebraic model over a complete base, coherent formal existence supplies the line bundle. An abstract proper formal scheme needs the corresponding algebraization argument and cannot be declared covered merely by citing existence for an already algebraic model. This remaining quantifier is explicit in C-SA14-085 and MP-SA14-050. [SA14-60–64; PE_Formal1959; PE_Stacks088E.]

The letter calls a combined proper relative GAGA exposition unwritten. A precise modern comparison over complex affinoid germs is available in Abramovich–Temkin §6.3.4 and AppendixC; AppendixB includes radius-zero germs, connecting it to analytic local rings. This clarifies the modern bridge without inventing the first publication date of the exact1962 combined claim. Hall’s paper was useful because it explicitly directed attention to the local-GAGA limitation and this reference. [C-SA14-087,089; PE_AbramovichTemkin2019; PE_Hall2023.]

The algebraic formal Picard ML proof separates algebraic-group images, integral component groups and actual rational points. Finite generation or bounded torsion alone does not imply ML. Closed subgroup images in a fixed noetherian algebraic group stabilize by ascending chains of defining ideals; a dimension count alone would not prove that. Kresch–Mathur supplies a precise modern characteristic-zero theorem for actual Picard groups, and a proper smooth complete-DVR characteristic-p criterion involving Néron–Severi rank. The latter does not cover every formal object in the manuscript. [SA14-53–59; C-SA14-100–116; PE_KreschMathur2025; computations§§17–18.]

## Punctured spectra and the henselian boundary

The resolution argument controls a punctured Picard group through the regular model and the exceptional-divisor lattice. Its local coherent-finiteness and topological inputs have been separated from the quotient calculation. Mumford’s opened paper supports the exceptional-divisor comparison; it does not identify the unspecified nonalgebraizable formal example mentioned in the letter. [SA14-65–70; C-SA14-090–099; PE_Mumford1961; computations§16.]

Elkik gives a strong precise answer to the punctured henselian question: for a noetherian henselian pair(A,J), compare the complement of V(J) before and after J-adic completion. This uses the **same ideal** in the open and the completion. The letter’s broader wording about arbitrary opens with a fixed maximal-ideal completion is not a direct substitution in that theorem. Descent after an étale local neighborhood with the same residue field is also not direct descent to the original nonhenselian ring. Those three formulations have separate problem records. [SA14-67–69; C-SA14-096–097; MP-SA14-038–043; PE_Elkik1973, III.1 Theorem3 andpp573–574.]
