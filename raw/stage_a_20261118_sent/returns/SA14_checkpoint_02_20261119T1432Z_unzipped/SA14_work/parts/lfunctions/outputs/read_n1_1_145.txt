0071 ### F01 — Characteristic polynomials and exterior traces
0072 
0073 **Witness:** 60/003, opening display; **kind:** unproved claim; **quote:** “on pose”. **Context query:** `characteristic polynomial|car. pol.|endomorphisme`; **comparison:** CT 16/048 uses rational characteristic coefficients to recover an inverse, whereas this page supplies the underlying linear determinant identity. **RS:** bears on WG2 §2.4 #3 and WG2 §6. #9 only at the linear-algebraic level.
0074 
0075 The equality \(\det(1-ft)=\sum_i(-1)^i\operatorname{Tr}(\Lambda^if)t^i\) requires no semisimplicity: after extension to an algebraic closure, triangularize f and compare elementary symmetric functions of its diagonal entries. Zero eigenvalues explain why the roots are inverses only of nonzero eigenvalues, a restriction the manuscript states explicitly. This identity does not supply the rationality of characteristic coefficients for algebraic correspondences over a rational coefficient field in CT 16/048.

0077 ### F02 — Symmetric powers and exact-sequence multiplicativity
0078 
0079 **Witness:** 60/003, middle displays; **kind:** unproved claim; **quote:** “suite exacte … produit des polynômes”. **Context query:** `groupe de Grothendieck|suite exacte|rang virtuel`; **comparison:** CT 16/035 defines a split Grothendieck group in a reused Karoubi typescript, while CT 47/008 uses a virtual rank in a different derived setting. **RS:** MPL_15_195-228+18_002-017 R01 is a relevant first-pass warning about treating reused text as motivic evidence; no exact RS theorem settles this item.
0080 
0081 For an f-stable short exact sequence, an adapted basis makes f block triangular, so characteristic polynomials multiply and their reciprocals do as well. The symmetric-power trace identity follows by expanding the product of geometric series, including in positive characteristic; it does not require division by factorials. The word “additif” therefore concerns addition in a Grothendieck group mapping to ordinary multiplication of series, not addition of rational functions.

0083 ### F03 — The written product is the rational Witt construction
0084 
0085 **Witness:** 60/003 and 005; **kind:** unpursued implication; **quote:** “(1−at)*(1−bt)=1−abt”. **Context query:** `Grothendieck|produit tensoriel|lambda|sigma`; **comparison:** CT 16/035 gives a separate K-group construction and CT 47/009 uses Chern operations, but neither supplied passage identifies this rational-function multiplication. **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #3, with no construction of motives inferred.
0086 
0087 The elementary-factor rule identifies the polynomial/quotient part with the rational Witt ring, with ordinary series multiplication serving as addition and tensor product supplying the second multiplication. Blumberg et al. (2013, introduction and §6; published 2016) use precisely this rule and describe the rational subring; that is a later mathematical comparison, not a dating or priority claim about the present undated pages. The finite-polynomial restriction on scan 007 is consequently structural, not merely a convergence warning.

0089 ### F04 — Reciprocal L-functions change the additive sign convention
0090 
0091 **Witness:** 60/005, first and last displays; **kind:** reading question; **quote:** “L … = 1/(L*L)”. **Context query:** `sigma|lambda|produit|K^`; **comparison:** CT 47/009 has formal Chern powers but no matching tensor-L convention. **RS:** no exact matching entry; related only to WG2 §2.4 #3.

0093 Replacing P by \(L=P^{-1}\) is additive negation in the Witt convention, which explains why tensor and exterior/symmetric formulas acquire inversions. The next multi-factor expression is written with a product sign, so it should be checked as a Witt product before being used as ordinary multiplication: for two one-dimensional endomorphisms a,b, \(L_{a\otimes b}=1/(1-abt)\), whereas the ordinary reciprocal of \(L_aL_b\) is \((1-at)(1-bt)\). This is a notes-only interpretation question; no star has been inserted into the frozen display.

0113 ### F08 — Purity alone does not force determinant sign ±1
0114 
0115 **Witness:** 60/009, determinant paragraph; **kind:** unproved claim; **quote:** “s'il s'agit d'un motif”. **Context query:** `ration|Weil|characteristic polynomial`; **comparison:** CT 16/048 explicitly adds rational characteristic coefficients and CT 16/050 proposes deriving Weil from its conjectural inputs. **RS:** WG2 §2.3 #7; MPL_14_002-044 M29 and MPL_14_087-132 M09 remain first-pass distinctions, not proofs of realization claims.
0116 
0117 Absolute values alone give an absolute value for the determinant, not a real sign: a rank-one finite-order character with Frobenius a primitive cube root of unity is pure of weight zero but has determinant different from ±1 over a coefficient field containing that root. Rationality or a suitable selfduality/polarization is therefore a substantive extra input in the motivic clause. The opening replacement of “motif” by “Q_l-faisceau” does not erase the later explicit restriction back to a motive.

0119 ### F09 — Why the odd-weight dimension must be even under rationality
0120 
0121 **Witness:** 60/009 and 023; **kind:** unproved claim; **quote:** “tjrs entier”. **Context query:** `ration|poids|Weil`; **comparison:** adds a numerical consequence to CT 16/048–050, where rationality and the Weil programme are separate inputs. **RS:** WG2 §2.4 #3 and WG2 §2.3 #7.
0122 
0123 If the determinant is rational and equals \(\pm p^{n\rho/2}\) with p prime, then odd nρ would make its square-root factor irrational, proving nρ even. Without that rationality input, a one-dimensional \(\mathbf Q_\ell\)-representation with Frobenius \(\sqrt p\), when \(\sqrt p\in\mathbf Q_\ell\), is a pure odd-weight counterexample to the unrestricted statement. This argument uses p prime as written and should not be transferred unchanged to a square prime-power base field.
