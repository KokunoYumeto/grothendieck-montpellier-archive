## 1. Determinants, virtual objects and signs

**C-SA14-401–405.** Over an algebraic closure triangularize an endomorphism with diagonal entries α_i. Then det(1−tF)=∏(1−α_i t), and the coefficient of t^j is (−1)^j times the trace on Λ^j. The inverse product gives symmetric-power traces. A stable subspace gives a block triangular matrix, proving exact-sequence multiplicativity without semisimplicity. Tensor products have eigenvalues α_iβ_j; this proves the rational Witt product rule. The virtual identity λ_s(−V)=σ_{−s}(V) follows by multiplying ∏(1+α_i s) and its reciprocal. The cancelled formula on scan 5 is therefore not a second surviving convention. For an invertible F of rank r,

P_{F^{-1}}(t)=(-t)^r det(F)^{-1}P_F(t^{-1}).

Taking alternating products proves the corresponding identity for bounded complexes. All identities are rational-function identities; substituting t^{-1} in an arbitrary infinite formal series is not justified. CALC-01–02 checks nonscalar examples and the virtual exponents.

**C-SA14-406–408, 411–416.** Purity of weight w says that each complex conjugate of each eigenvalue has absolute value p^{w/2}. It does not say the determinant is rational or real. A rank-one representation of Gal(F̄_p/F_p) with Frobenius a root of unity ζ_3 is continuous, pure of weight zero, and has nonreal determinant. A rank-one representation with Frobenius √2 in Q_7 is continuous: √2 is a 7-adic unit and the homomorphism from Z sending 1 to √2 extends to the profinite completion into the compact closure of its powers. It is pure of weight one over F_2, but its determinant is not rational. CALC-03 finds the required 7-adic roots by Hensel lifting. These are countermodels to an *arbitrary pure ℓ-adic representation* reading, not counterexamples to a rational polarized motive.

If the determinant is rational, its absolute value p^{rw/2} forces rw even by the p-adic valuation. Over F_q with q=p^a, it only forces arw even. Selfduality is an extra perfect pairing E⊗E→Q_ℓ(−w), giving E^∨≅E(w); purity alone does not construct it. On a smooth proper n-fold, pairing degrees i and 2n−i gives Σ(−1)^i i b_i=nχ. Together with pure weights w+i this gives determinant magnitude p^{(n+w)χ/2}. The middle pairing carries the sign information. An alternating similitude of multiplier q on dimension 2m has determinant q^m: apply the similitude to the nonzero top wedge of the alternating form. A symmetric form does not give that sign; F=[[0,p],[1,0]] preserves diag(1,p) up to p and has determinant −p. The calculation keeps this distinction explicit.

The multiplicities of ±1 used for the sign are those of normalized Frobenius. The test X=P² has L=1/((1−t)(1−pt)(1−p²t)), a pole at the central fixed point and negative normalized functional-equation sign. Thus negative sign constrains the *order* of a zero or pole; it does not universally force a zero. Half-power normalizations require a square-root coordinate.

## 5. Haar construction and elementary duality on reused printed sheets

**C-SA14-452–460.** These are Bourbaki printed fragments, not claims in the handwriting. A positive compactly supported function g dominates a positive amount on a small neighborhood; finitely many translated neighborhoods cover supp(f), proving the covering set nonempty. For f=0 use a nonzero positive point measure as witness if the definition excludes the zero measure. A convolution by positive μ and nonnegative f vanishes identically precisely when μ=0 *or* f=0, not only when both vanish.

The covering ratio (f:g) is homogeneous, monotone and subadditive in f; composing finite coverings gives (f:h)≤(f:g)(g:h). Normalization by a fixed f₀ bounds the resulting ratios on every fixed f. For f=f₁+f₂, uniform continuity of f_i/f on a compact neighborhood of its positive support, with a small positive regularization where needed, lets a sufficiently small translate distribute its coefficient between f₁ and f₂ with error at most ε times the covering total. Thus I_g(f₁)+I_g(f₂)≤I_g(f)+O(ε), while the reverse inequality is subadditivity. One common ultrafilter refining the shrinking-support filter gives simultaneous limits for every f, a positive additive invariant functional, and hence a Haar integral. Separate choices for each f would not prove additivity. The printed uniqueness step refers elsewhere; existence and uniqueness are classical and were checked against Alfsen's primary article, without attributing the printed draft to the handwriting.

For a finite-length module over a PID A with fraction field K, Hom_A(−,K/A) is exact because K/A is divisible and hence injective. On A/(a), the dual is a^{-1}A/A≅A/(a); direct sums give biduality, equal lengths and annihilators. Dualizing a short exact sequence gives the orthogonal-complement formulas. The extension property is precisely exactness of Hom(−,J); Baer's criterion reduces to divisibility because every ideal is principal. This is an A-module duality. Cartier, Dieudonné and Verdier dualities need their separate structures and are not identified by the check accent.

## 7. Fitting ideals, signed divisors and exterior powers

**C-SA14-479–491, 542–547.** For a finite torsion module over a Noetherian normal domain, the height-one length divisor is additive because localization is exact and length is additive. At a local ring, a presentation vector whose entries generate a principal ideal can be divided by a generator; if the resulting entries generate the unit ideal, a change of basis makes it a basis vector and splits the corresponding cyclic summand. Iterating gives the standard invariant-factor argument when the cut antecedents provide the necessary local and principal conditions. If E has r minimal generators at a local ring, choose a surjection A^r→E. The top exterior power is cyclic; its annihilator is the ideal generated by all entries of relation vectors, which is Fitt_{r−1}(E). Killing this ideal kills all relations among the r chosen generators, so E/aE is free over A/a. Globally only primes where the top exterior power survives need be considered; the assumption Λ^{r+1}E=0 and minimality of r gives the rank statement there. The Jacobson radical conclusion uses constant local generator rank. Interpreting the word “radical” as the nilradical is false over a DVR with E its residue field.

Fitting ideals are minors of a presentation. Changing generators adds an identity block; changing relations adds redundant columns, so the appropriate ideals are invariant. Base change carries each minor to its image. A block diagonal presentation yields the convolution equality for a direct sum. For a quotient, add relation columns: this makes Fitting ideals larger. Hence Fitt_k(M)⊂Fitt_k(M/N), and the reverse printed inclusion is refuted by A→A/(X).
