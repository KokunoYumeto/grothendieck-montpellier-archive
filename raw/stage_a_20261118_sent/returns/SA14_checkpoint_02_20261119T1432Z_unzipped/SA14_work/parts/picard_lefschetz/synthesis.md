# Late Picard questions and the formal Lefschetz programme

## Scope and reading conventions

This part consolidates the **61 indexed entries of MPL_57_271–315 and the 85 indexed entries of MPL_58_020–067**. Every entry was compared with its actual transcription passage. Both complete transcriptions, both complete doubt registers and both complete note files were read, including material that no note turned into a claim. The surviving source text contains43 supplied scan pages in the first package and44 in the second. The numerical scan ranges contain gaps; they are not counts of consecutively available images.

The result is132 items,132 claims and64 merged questions or unfinished steps. There are81 mathematical reading alarms,44 links and12 future research directions. Of the links,31 concern supplied folders or checked published endpoints;13 are explicitly quarantined contextual leads to other projects or unavailable archive packets. The latter are preserved because the earlier notes made them, not presented as verified relations between two inspected documents.

All line numbers name one-based physical lines of the supplied `.tex` file. They are not reconstructed manuscript line numbers. `57:285`, for example, means the supplied `\scanpage{285}` value in cote57. The distinction matters where a physical face carries a reused printed text and handwritten additions. There are no manuscript scan images or second-pass replacements in these two supplied packages. No ink reading has been decided or modified. Images of **published** EGA, FGA and SGA6 pages were inspected to resolve published theorem statements; that does not constitute inspection of the manuscript scans.

The registers distinguish four kinds of evidence: the supplied diplomatic text, earlier-run interpretations, exact published results, and the present run's mathematical reconstructions. `outputs/proof_checks.md` contains32 scoped reasoning checks. ## 1. What the end of cote57 is doing

The packet begins with a layer of classical divisor exposition on57:271,273. Its prime divisors, orders, principal divisors and linear equivalence are reused typescript. They belong to the mathematical inventory, but their physical reuse does not make them new Picard conjectures. The same distinction is needed later for field theory, derived limits, scalar algebra and representation theory. The present claims map includes those assertions while keeping their provenance separate from the handwritten research.

The handwritten opening question on57:272 compares Picard data over a henselian local ring and its completion. The word “good” is not unexplained: the page gives a regular-completion-morphism condition, although the direction of the arrow is uncertain, and offers an algebraic henselization example. Three different questions follow. Finite-order approximation of a completed line bundle is C-SA14-202; a stronger proper-morphism assertion involving support of R¹f_*O is C-SA14-203; comparison of the Picard groups of punctured spectra is C-SA14-204. Treating these as one completion theorem would obscure both the text and its later history.

Artin1969 Theorems1.8/1.10 supply finite-order approximation in their stated settings; the modern henselian G-ring version is Stacks07QX. Applying that theorem to line-bundle data requires the finite-presentation moduli hypothesis recorded in the claim. It does not say that arbitrary algebraic and completed Picard groups are equal. By contrast, Elkik1973 III.1 Theorem3, Corollary1 and the following Picard corollary on p.573 give the exact punctured comparison for a noetherian henselian pair. This resolves the isolated-singularity question in a stronger setting. The ambiguous X/Y target in the R¹-support sentence cannot be recovered by choosing whichever of these later theorems is convenient. It remains an identification question, MP-SA14-202. [Artin1969Approximation; Elkik1973Henselian; Stacks07QX]

The same opening passage also asks whether projectivity after completion descends. C-SA14-332 and P32 preserve this claim separately. If an ample completed line bundle can be approximated on the closed fibre, the resulting algebraic bundle is ample over an open neighborhood of that fibre. For a proper family over a local base, that neighborhood is the entire base. Properness with a relatively ample invertible sheaf gives projectivity. The checked argument uses the actual ample-locus and projectivity lemmas, Stacks0D2S and0B44; it is not a claim about projectivity descending under every faithfully flat map. [StacksDivisors]

The next line of work,57:275–281, studies Pic/Pic^τ, numerical classes, torsors and representability. The quotient is not Pic/Pic⁰: torsion in the Néron–Severi group has to be removed. The FGA236 addendum, statements(vi) and(viii), supplies the precise finite-generation and numerical-equivalence identification. It does not settle every scheme quotient proposed in the manuscript. A quotient sheaf, a quotient algebraic space and a quotient scheme are different outputs. The closed-kernel diagonal argument gives a clean separation criterion once the quotient exists; it does not construct it. The sentence placing every coset inside the kernel has a literal ambient-space error, recorded without changing the transcription. [Grothendieck1962FGA236Complement; Kleiman2005Picard, Lemma4.9 and Remark4.18]

On57:283–288 the author passes to product Picard functors and quotient constructions. Rigidifying a line bundle along two sections separates the two factor Picard groups from a pointed-morphism or correspondence term. P22 checks this at the level of functors, not merely geometric points. One transient formula replaces full Pic(A) by the dual abelian scheme A′. Taking the acting group to be trivial and the other factor to be a point exposes the missing NS factor. But the page subsequently restores the distinction through an NS sequence and a Pic^τ formula, and the later full-Pic expression again retains Pic(A). C-SA14-212 therefore records **his own later correction**, not an unqualified error attributed to the entire argument.

The inversion/translation example on57:285 is also more precise than an informal label such as “pathological Picard scheme.” Its indicated torsion part is Y′×A′[2]. In characteristic2 the finite factor contributes tangent dimension but no positive dimension. Since multiplication by2 on an abelian scheme is finite locally free, this control is flat even when it is nonsmooth. It does not provide the separate nonflat family attributed to Mumford in FGA236§2.9. Nor does the basic inversion representation by itself produce every desired difference between h¹(O) and h⁰(Ω¹). The four examples announced on57:288 are retained as four requested effects whose full constructions are not present. [Grothendieck1962FGA236, §2.9; Oda1969Dieudonne, Corollaries5.11–5.12]

## 2. Specialization, p-primary information and deformation

The finite-subgroup construction on57:289–290 is an attempt to force a homomorphism of abelian varieties to acquire p-divisibility on specialization. Its intended effect is meaningful: geometric NS specialization is injective and has torsion-free cokernel away from the residue characteristic, while p-primary nonsaturation can occur. Maulik–Poonen2012 Proposition3.6 and Example3.12 were opened and give those exact statements, including a genuine example. Their CM construction is different from the incomplete family on these pages. The first arXiv posting in2009 is recorded separately from publication in2012. [MaulikPoonen2012NS]

P10 checks the manuscript's mechanism conditionally. Descent of the proposed homomorphism requires the appropriate image containment; divisibility by p after quotienting requires a further containment. The literal equality pF=G cannot hold for nonzero G when F⊂X[p], since pF=0. A generic inclusion that already guarantees divisibility also defeats the intended generic obstruction. These are mathematical alarms for the subgroup symbols, not permission to reverse the arrows in the source. C-SA14-216 is partly identified because the conditional mechanism is sound but the actual family has not been supplied. C-SA14-217 separately records the established phenomenon.

The abstract p-primary group notes on57:291–292 are not statements about finite flat group schemes. The manuscript uses a contravariant character module Hom(M,Q_p/Z_p) and a covariant inverse-limit Tate module. For finite-cotype divisible groups they are dual finite free Z_p-modules. Passing to the isogeny category requires tensoring with Q_p; killing finite groups does not leave the integral lattice category unchanged. The split-surjection criterion is checked by Smith normal form, including the small but essential exclusion of the zero testing group. These elementary assertions are kept apart from Oda's Dieudonné comparison, which uses the full p-torsion group scheme. [C-SA14-218–221; P8–P10; Oda1969Dieudonne]

The deformation passages on57:294,297 then return to geometric Picard objects. P23 derives the square-zero obstruction from the unit sequence: H² controls existence of a lift, H¹ parametrizes lifts when they exist, and H⁰ controls automorphisms. The cup-product expression with the deformation class is explained under its stated smoothness framework. This separates an obstruction calculation from the further question of constructing a relative reduced, abelian or p-divisible Picard part. The doubtful superscript written Pic^{00|∞} does not identify which of those objects is intended. Oda's absolute perfect-field results do not silently supply the missing relative functor. [C-SA14-222–223; Grothendieck1962FGA236, §2.6; Oda1969Dieudonne, Proposition4.9 and Corollaries5.11–5.12]

The question lists on57:308–315 revisit representability, separatedness, openness, flatness, dimensions, multiplication and boundedness. Their short margins are part of the source: an initial flatness hope later receives a negative answer attributed to Mumford, whereas finite type of Pic^τ, bounded NS and multiplication are marked positively. Several exact later endpoints were located. The FGA236 addendum announces nine finiteness results. The actual SGA6 scan—not a frontmatter-only PDF—was then read for XII1.1–1.7 and XIII3.6,4.7–4.8,5.1–5.4. XII distinguishes generic quasi-affineness of relative pullback from affine pullback over a field; XIII proves the multiplication, τ and NS results with their precise assumptions. [Grothendieck1962FGA236Complement; RaynaudKleiman1971SGA6XII; Kleiman1971SGA6XIII]

Two publication checks prevent serious overstatements. First, FGA236 Proposition2.10(i) on p.235 concerns **Pic^σ**, whose definition on p.223 uses torsion orders prime to the residue characteristic. It does not prove the full wild Pic^τ implication written on57:311. C-SA14-258 is therefore a partial match with an explicitly bounded remainder. Second, FGA236§2.8 proves some dimension and tame-rank assertions while expressly presenting other α/λ inequalities as conjectures. SGA3II X Remark8.7 gives the smooth-group semicontinuity case and calls a merely flat extension probable. Neither historical passage proves a blanket nonsmooth theorem, and neither historical conjecture label proves current openness. C-SA14-263 and MP-SA14-218 say exactly what was checked. [Grothendieck1962FGA236; DemazureGrothendieck1970SGA3II]

Other distinctions are equally concrete. Kleiman2005 Theorem4.8 gives separated representability for flat projective geometrically integral fibres, a positive case weaker than normality; it does not by itself settle every merely proper geometrically-locally-integral variant. A finite torsion scheme need not have finitely many sections over a nonreduced base: P26 gives infinitely many p-torsion dual-number sections over an infinite characteristic-p field. Finally, the tame Tate rank2α+μ, total reduced-group dimensionα+μ+λ, tangent dimensionh¹(O) and NS rank are different invariants. The register never uses one as a substitute for another. [C-SA14-247–263; Kleiman2005Picard]

## 3. Gluing and the ancillary printed layers

The gluing passages on57:298–306 are independent constructions, not merely motivation for a later fundamental-group project. P12 checks the Laurent-polynomial calculation for punctured axes glued by inversion: restrictions of global functions to the identified curve are forced to be constant. This obstructs quasi-affineness. It does not establish existence of the involution quotient; that remains the orbit/affine-neighborhood question explicitly marked for verification. Ferrand2003 gives exact pushout and quotient criteria, so the existence issue is not hidden behind the functions calculation. [C-SA14-228–229; Ferrand2003Pinching, Theorem5.4 and §7.1]

The projective-plane gluing argument uses restrictions of line bundles to a common elliptic curve. Choosing independent restrictions makes the kernel of the two Picard restriction maps trivial. P13 checks the degree and non-torsion conditions needed for that conclusion. The unit sequence for a pushout is handled separately in P14: compatibility of units controls the Picard kernel, while component scalings explain the torus factor in nodal cases. Ferrand's flat-module patching theorem is a precise modern framework; no unsupported closed-cover Čech theorem is used. This point is relevant to the parent's earlier cubic-gluing calculation, but the two examples are not merged merely because both concern reducible curves. [C-SA14-234,236,237; Ferrand2003Pinching, §2.2 and §6.3]

The reused algebra is not discarded. The detached derived-limit sheet is compared with Laudal1965§1.1 and Corollary1.3.2; the original Roos1961/1962 papers remain unverified backward leads. The two spectral-sequence constructions have a conditional proof check, but an undefined L_k prevents identification of the printed vanishing bound. The field-theory task on57:299 is particularly easy to misidentify: its “Zariski lemma” is about coefficients of a rational function algebraic over K(X), not the familiar finite-type-field theorem. P25 proves the actual relative-constants statement using normalized numerator and denominator and finite automorphism orbits. Searches returning the other lemma are explicitly recorded as nonmatches. [C-SA14-225–227,230–235; Laudal1965Limits; StacksAlgebra]

The later reused representation-theory page,58:63, invokes Brauer and Witt. Brauer1951§2.6 supplies the exact hyperelementary convention, and its theorem on p.250 detects prime factors of Schur indices. Fontaine1971 supplies the local-field decomposition statements and an explicit R_p definition. “Decomposed” here means a product of matrix algebras over commutative field extensions, not necessarily split over the original ground field. The missing manuscript definition is not filled from Fontaine, and the physical source leaf is not certified to be Serre1960 merely because its subject fits. [C-SA14-321–322; Brauer1951GroupRings; Fontaine1971Decomposition]

## 4. The beginning of cote58: cutting and connectedness

The second package begins with scalar-extension and associated-prime material, then continues the connectedness programme from the preceding packet. The main objects are local depth, connectedness in a specified dimension, hyperplane cuts and specialization. These should be read in that order, not retrofitted into the later Picard applications. The associated-prime base-change identity was compared with the exact flatness assumptions in Stacks0312; the scalar and determinant fragments retain their ancillary provenance. [C-SA14-270–278; Stacks0312]

A literal specialization assertion on58:23 is false if connectedness of the generic fibre means ordinary connectedness. Let A=Z_(5), B=A[T]/(T²+1) and X=P¹_B over A. The base is regular and geometrically unibranch, X is proper and integral over A, and its generic fibre is the connected P¹ over Q(i). Its special fibre is two disjoint projective lines because T²+1 splits into distinct factors modulo5. Replacing P¹ by P^{h+1} gives the same obstruction for every h≥0. P1 checks all these hypotheses; it is not merely a numerical toy. [C-SA14-273; outputs/calculations.json, split_special_fibre]

The complete-local cutting theorem in SGA2 XIII2.1 is a distinct statement. Its converse, stated immediately after the theorem, also matches the parent's fragmentary converse in K58-02:13–17. The proof uses finite avoidance sets and an S₂ reduction in precisely the relevant place. That endpoint was read and reported to the parent for C-SA14-652. The nearby ordinary-fibre wording in XIII2.2 requires a geometric convention or another qualification; proximity to a printed theorem does not invalidate the explicit counterexample or authorize an ink correction. [Grothendieck1968SGA2, XIII2.1 and following converse, original pp.177–178]

The local fundamental-group question was also traced forward far enough to avoid a common false settlement. Suzuki2023 Theorem1.2 proves finite generation of the **maximal pro-nilpotent quotient** under its complete-local dimension and puncture-connectivity hypotheses. The full fundamental group appears as Conjecture1.1 in that paper. Searches included recent formulations, arXiv, Numdam, SGA and the named discovery platforms; they did not establish a later complete theorem. This supports a precisely limited statement about the located literature, not a certified assertion that the full conjecture remains open in November2026. [Suzuki2023FundamentalGroups]

## 5. Formal Lefschetz, low degrees and category-level claims

On58:27–44 the author organizes formal comparison into weak conditions, comparison for locally free sheaves, effectivity and consequences for finite covers. The transitions are substantive. Isomorphism in one degree can imply injectivity in the next through a short exact sequence, provided the needed generated objects and bounds exist. Fully faithful transport of finite locally free algebras carries multiplication and the unit as morphisms, not merely the underlying module. Effectivity is then needed to algebraize the object; étaleness and shrinking control the passage to covers. The formal neighborhood group, the inverse limit over neighborhoods and the global fundamental group are not automatically identical. [C-SA14-279–299; P20; Grothendieck1968SGA2, IX–XI]

The line-bundle category exposes another low-degree distinction. H¹ and H² control classes and obstructions, but not the automorphisms that are H⁰ of the unit sheaf. For a DVR, the automorphisms1 and1+t have the same reduction. The relevant higher cohomology vanishes, yet the restriction functor on line bundles is not faithful. C-SA14-292 records the literal categorical failure and separates it from valid Picard-class statements. This is also why an isomorphism of Picard groups should not be advertised as an equivalence of the whole category without a unit comparison. [P2; C-SA14-290–292]

The permanence questions are retained at their actual strength. Proper support, relative flatness, projectivity and finite cohomological amplitude do different work. P21 identifies a conditional route using a bounded perfect pushforward, but it does not prove every broad permanence assertion on the pages. The later literature search found precise comparison/existence theorems, not one universal theorem permitting all those assumptions to be dropped. The problem index therefore states the missing conditions rather than assigning an automatic affirmative answer. [MP-SA14-242–247; Raynaud1974Lefschetz; Grothendieck1968SGA2]

On58:46–52, completion is considered on coherent sheaves modulo supports missing the divisor. Exactness and the Serre kernel are one question; fullness and the essential image are others. The clean Hom comparison uses depth of the target and the correct regular-ambient local setting. P5 proves the elementary Hom-depth inequality, and P6 applies the opened Raynaud theorem with its exact closure conditions. [C-SA14-300–307; Raynaud1974Lefschetz, I§0 and I1.1–1.5]

Fullness of the unrestricted quotient category fails. For A=k[[x,y,z]], t=x and the sheaf supported on the punctured V(y), the algebraic endomorphism germs are k[[x,z]]_(x), while formal endomorphisms are k((z))[[x]]. The series Σz^(−n²)xⁿ lies in the latter but not the former: inversion of one algebraic denominator permits only linear growth of pole orders. P7 proves that bound. The example has depth1 at a closed off-divisor point, so it does not refute the depth-controlled positive theorem. Undefined a,b,c′,T₁ and the doubtful “dominated” wording remain essential-image identification problems rather than variables filled by the desired conclusion.

## 6. Graded cohomology, the reused existence theorem and cones

The negative-twist calculations on58:56–62 use duality to turn cohomology into Ext-support and depth conditions. The method is genuine, but several endpoints are fragile. Exactness makes surjectivity of the preceding restriction map equivalent to **injectivity** of the indicated local-cohomology map α, not surjectivity. A skyscraper away from the formal divisor has nonzero H⁰ in every negative twist while its positive graded sections form a finitely generated module. If a condition controls only a boundary that is empty, it cannot force that skyscraper's global sections to equal its zero formal restriction. These three controls isolate a reversed word, a degree-zero conflation and an incorrectly broad scope. [C-SA14-309–317; P3, P4, P30]

The inverse-limit step is also recorded rather than assumed: formal cohomology can carry a lim¹ obstruction. SGA2 IX2.1 supplies uniform Mittag–Leffler control under its hypotheses. Positive graded finiteness, negative-twist vanishing, coherence of higher direct images and the separate degree-zero coherent-hull condition are therefore four related but distinct assertions. The reused formal-existence sheets on58:57,61 match SGA2 XII Theorem3.1 and Corollary3.2, including projectivity, flatness, fibrewise regularity and depth≥2. Their printed theorem and the handwritten grading additions remain separate strata. [C-SA14-308,312,316,317; Grothendieck1968SGA2]

The closing cone calculation is unfinished but recoverable under stated models. The affine cone SpecR, a punctured cone, the total space of a line bundle and a projective cone are different schemes. EGAII8.7.5 requires an eventual generation condition and identifies the vertex preimage;8.8.3–8.8.4 gives the contraction;8.8.6 supplies the direct-image equality under its hypotheses. Those original pages were visually checked. P18 then derives the conditional Picard calculation from the projective-bundle decomposition Pic(P(O⊕L))=Pic(X)⊕Z, restriction to the exceptional section and the descended hyperplane class. The source's own cancellation of a formula missing Z is preserved. [C-SA14-323,329,330; Grothendieck1961EGAII; Kleiman2005Picard, Answer4.15]

For the affine or line-bundle models, Traverso's seminormal homotopy theorem supplies a useful sufficient setting. A grading contraction then forces Pic of a positively graded seminormal affine cone with degree-zero ring k to vanish. Pullback to a line-bundle total space is always split injective by the zero section; seminormality gives the checked surjectivity route. None of this decides the missing primes and tildes in the source or proves the assertion for every singular cone. [C-SA14-324,331; P17; Traverso1970Seminormality, Theorem3.6]

The neighboring parafactorial and curve calculations have their own corrections. Categorical parafactoriality requires the depth≥2/unit comparison in addition to a vanishing punctured Picard group; a DVR is the boundary counterexample. Normalization gives p_a=Σg_i+Σδ_s−number of components+1, with δ a normalization length. For nodal curves this reduces to Σg_i plus the first Betti number of the dual graph, and the affine Picard part is the corresponding torus. Counting branches alone would miss nonnodal defects, while xy=0 in three variables is a surface. These are finite curve calculations, not evidence for an unavailable arithmetic tower. [C-SA14-325–328; P14–P16; Grothendieck1968SGA2, XI3.4–3.5; Ferrand2003Pinching]

## 7. What changed in the earlier notes, and what remains limited

The earlier notes were generally careful about the pages' local questions. Most entry verdicts are therefore kept or merged. The corrections concern scope rather than wholesale rejection: a completion question is split into three maps; a flat nonsmooth example is separated from a nonflat one; a transient product formula is credited with its later correction; the exact relative-constants lemma replaces a tempting but wrong name match; and a broad formal-category assertion is restricted by a concrete counterexample. Contextual Fourier, standard-conjecture, derivator and arithmetic links remain the earlier runs' ideas unless both supplied endpoints support the exact relation.

The limitations are explicit and local. Some published exact results were found; some claims are conditionally reconstructed; some literal statements fail; and some symbols or missing definitions prevent identification. **No class D assertion is made**, and no failed search is turned into a novelty claim. A class C record states the developed part and the remaining mismatch; it does not imply that the remainder is a certified open problem in2026. A class F record identifies why the source cannot determine a theorem, including reading-dependent literal failures. Original Roos and possible Serre source leaves remain unverified bibliography entries. No source suggests a historical reason for every abandoned question, so `why_open` records that absence rather than inventing one.
