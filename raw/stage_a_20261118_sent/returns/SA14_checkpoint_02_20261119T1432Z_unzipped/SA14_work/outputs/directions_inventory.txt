ROOT CLAIMS
C-SA14-601 | Let A be a complete noetherian local ring and f:X→Spec A a proper resolution, isomorphic over U=Spec A minus its closed point, with X regular and U schematically dense. Cartier divisors supported on X minus U give D→Pic(X)→Pic(U)→0; proper formal existence identifies Pic(X) with the compatible system of Pic(X_n). Only codimension-one exceptional components generate D; the blanket identification with every component of X_0 needs this qualification.
C-SA14-602 | For a non-separably-closed residue field, the exceptional divisor lattice is an étale Galois module obtained from the geometric exceptional components. Taking rational points of D→P→Q produces an H¹(k,im D) obstruction; replacing Pic(X_n) by its sheafified Picard functor adds a Brauer obstruction. Thus Q(k)=Pic(U) is not automatic. The text corrects the constant lattice to a twisted one.
C-SA14-603 | If a resolution admits an invertible ideal J defining the exceptional fibre set-theoretically and with ample conormal L=J/J² on Z, then the transition Pic(Z_{n+1})→Pic(Z_n) is eventually an isomorphism: H¹(Z,L^{n+1}) and H²(Z,L^{n+1}) vanish for large n. Hence its formal Picard pro-object stabilizes. The existence of such a J/model is a separate question in the source.
C-SA14-604 | The map from exceptional divisors to NS of the special fibre is asserted to be injective, so that the divisor lattice embeds discretely and Pic(X)/D exists with the same connected component and Lie algebra. For normal surface singularities, negative definiteness of the exceptional intersection matrix proves the required injectivity. Higher dimensions require a negativity argument with precise projectivity hypotheses.
C-SA14-605 | Under the stabilized-resolution construction, Lie(Q)=H¹(X,O_X) injects into H¹(U,O_U)=H²_m(A). Vanishing of this local cohomology, for example depth A≥3, makes Q discrete. An intrinsic functor on all k-algebras, independence of resolution, and behavior under non-geometrically-regular scalar extension are separately left open; a chosen coefficient field is part of the construction.
C-SA14-606 | For A an artinian local ring with perfect residue field k and X proper over A, seek a locally algebraic group over k representing the appropriate Picard functor formed through Witt-vector/Greenberg base change, including mixed characteristic; actual Pic(X) must be compared with its k-points through the descent obstruction.
C-SA14-607 | For line bundles on the products to which the cube theorem applies, the n-variable pullback is expressed by the base term to exponent (n−1)(n−2)/2, singleton terms to exponent2−n, and unordered pair terms to exponent1. The same cube identity yields [n]*L = L^{n(n+1)/2}⊗([-1]*L)^{n(n−1)/2}. Pair indices must be i<j; writing i≠j counts twice.
C-SA14-608 | For proper flat families with universally O_S as global functions and chosen basepoints, normalized line bundles on X×_S Y define correspondences; the product Picard functor splits into the factors and a normalized correspondence functor. Its infinitesimal rigidity uses H¹(O_{X×Y})=H¹(O_X)⊕H¹(O_Y), with the axes removing those two summands. Representability and base-change hypotheses must be kept.
C-SA14-609 | A smooth proper pointed family over a connected locally noetherian base, with an abelian fibre, is expected to carry a unique abelian-scheme law. The pages prove uniqueness via rigidity, sketch the infinitesimal extension of multiplication, and explicitly leave the DVR closedness step to be developed. The opened published theorem is stated for a projective family; its proper analogue needs the corresponding algebraic-space extension theorem.
C-SA14-610 | The proposed characterization of torsion-dense smooth commutative groups by absence of additive factors is withdrawn: a nontrivial extension of an abelian variety by G_a in characteristic zero can still have Zariski-dense prime-to-characteristic torsion. For a nonzero vector-extension class by a one-dimensional vector group, the extension is anti-affine.
C-SA14-611 | For a commutative flat group scheme of finite presentation, multiplication by n invertible on the base is étale; without flatness its n-torsion is unramified under the stated finite-type conditions. The transcription’s possible “non comm.” cannot be retained: the nth-power map of a noncommutative group need not be unramified.
C-SA14-612 | Finite étale closed subgroup schemes in a separated unramified group over a connected locally noetherian base that agree on one fibre agree everywhere; closed embeddings or the historical separated scheme convention are essential. For smooth commutative separated groups with finite étale prime-power torsion that is schematically dense, equality of rigid subgroups and of homomorphisms is detected on one fibre. Effective descent then holds for a faithfully flat finite-type cover with geometrically connected fibres, subject to the displayed rigidity hypotheses.
C-SA14-613 | The claim that a homomorphism from a rigid group has universally open kernel is withdrawn after a degeneration of a graph of a finite constant group. A fibrewise dimension argument does not prove flatness or openness of a kernel. Likewise topological surjectivity of a morphism of group schemes is weaker than faithful flatness.
C-SA14-614 | “Very rigid” groups are studied through rigidity of their closed subgroups, dense prime-to-characteristic torsion and descent over primary extensions. Any formulation quantifying over all closed subgroup schemes must restrict the subgroup to be smooth if that is part of the rigidity definition: μ_p⊂G_m is a closed nonsmooth subgroup in characteristic p. The abelian and multiplicative-type smooth cases support the intended arguments.
C-SA14-615 | For proper flat finitely presented X/S, cohomologically flat in degree0 with geometric H⁰=k, a family B⊂Pic^τ_{X/S} of maximal abelian subvarieties yields an Albanese torsor under B∨, universal for maps to abelian torsors and compatible with base change. The field case uses the maximal abelian subgroup, not the Chevalley abelian quotient of Pic⁰. Existence in arbitrary families is separately questioned.
C-SA14-616 | Smooth fibres alone do not make a relative Picard scheme smooth over a nonreduced base. In characteristic zero, for smooth projective X/S the identity component Pic⁰ is smooth even for nonreduced S; the full Picard scheme can still have jumping components that are not flat. Vanishing H²(O_{X_s}) gives a separate sufficient criterion for smoothness near s.
C-SA14-617 | For a smooth projective surface and ample H, Riemann–Roch proves h⁰(D)>0 if χ(O(D))>0 and H·(D−K)>0, since H⁰(K−D)=0. The nonstrict inequality transcribed on the page is insufficient. Applying the strict version to nH+Δ for n≫0 yields boundedness arguments for numerically trivial Δ.
C-SA14-618 | For a proper scheme over a field, numerical triviality of a line bundle equals τ-triviality; the geometric Néron–Severi group is finitely generated. In a proper finite-type family over a noetherian quasi-compact base, geometric NS ranks and torsion orders are uniformly bounded. Proper-surjective pullback, ample divisor restriction with fibre components dimension≥3, and [n] for n≠0 have the finiteness properties specified in the FGA addendum.
C-SA14-619 | For a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field and ℓ different from the characteristic, b₁=b₃=2 dim(Pic⁰_red), b₀=b₄=1, and b₂=c₂−2+4 dim(Pic⁰_red). The transcription omits the factor2 in the odd Betti numbers and consequently in the Euler-characteristic formula. The NS rank is bounded by b₂.
C-SA14-620 | A proper surjective morphism X′→X over a field induces an affine morphism Pic_X→Pic_{X′}; over an integral noetherian base it is generically representable by quasi-affine morphisms of finite type. The direction is contravariant. Affineness over a field and generic relative quasi-affineness are distinct assertions; finite type alone is weaker than either.
C-SA14-621 | For proper X→S with S locally noetherian and coherent F, the functor of base changes on which F becomes flat is represented by an unramified S-scheme locally of finite type. This is universal flattening, not merely a stratification whose members separately make F flat. The requested extension beyond the projective case is real and necessary.
C-SA14-622 | The associated-torsor sketch compares Hom sets for quotient G-sets and associated bundles but does not specify enough hypotheses for a universal full-faithfulness theorem. For a connected Galois finite étale torsor and constant finite group G, the usual equivalence with finite G-sets gives Hom_G(G/H,G/K)=(G/K)^H, equivalently cosets satisfying H⊂gKg^{-1}.
C-SA14-623 | An effective relative Cartier divisor is an invertible sheaf with a section that is a nonzerodivisor on each fibre, modulo multiplication by units. For proper flat X/S with universally f_*O_X=O_S and a cohomologically controlled sheaf L, the fibre of Div→Pic is the appropriate open part of the projectivized section module; for geometrically integral fibres every nonzero section is regular. Projective-bundle, local-freeness and base-change assertions require their separate hypotheses.
C-SA14-624 | For the normal affine cone over a smooth projective variety X with an ample embedding L and the usual section-ring hypotheses, the local Picard group at the vertex is Pic(X)/Z[L]. The blowup/line-bundle model and excision of the zero section give the quotient. Normality, generation and the distinction between the whole punctured cone and the punctured local spectrum are essential.
C-SA14-625 | For a cone over a smooth projective curve C with polarization L, formal neighbourhoods of the exceptional curve have successive Picard kernels H¹(C,L^n), n≥1, because H²(C,L^n)=0. Over characteristic zero these give the unipotent correction to completion. For a smooth plane curve of degree d, the sum of these dimensions is binomial(d−1,3) for d≥4. The source’s d≥6 inequality is a sufficient example of failure of invariance, not the first possible degree.
C-SA14-626 | For a noetherian henselian pair(A,J), restriction from U=Spec A minus V(J) to the corresponding puncture of the J-adic completion induces a bijection on Picard groups. This is an isomorphism on isomorphism classes, not automatically an equivalence of categories with all Hom groups preserved.
C-SA14-627 | For a projective commutative group scheme with equal-dimensional fibres, the pages study the reduced identity abelian varieties and the finite quotient length σ. They propose flatness/constant-σ criteria for these fibrewise abelian varieties to form a subgroup scheme, and a tentative torsion-rank test with n divisible by σ². The sufficiency argument uses a reduced base and a Hilbert/valuative construction; those restrictions cannot be suppressed.
C-SA14-628 | For a group scheme locally of finite type over a field, G⁰ is an open normal subgroup of finite type and is geometrically irreducible; its component quotient is étale. Open subgroups are closed. Smoothness over a field is equivalent to geometric reducedness in this group setting; ordinary reducedness over an imperfect field is not sufficient.
C-SA14-629 | For a commutative locally algebraic group G over a field, its torsion-component subgroup is the inverse image of the torsion subgroup of π₀(G). Its union over exponent n is filtered by divisibility. If that locus is quasi-compact, only finitely many components occur and their torsion exponent is bounded. Tests involving a subgroup H require H to be of finite type; H=G otherwise makes such a test vacuous.

It does not drop separation or openness.
