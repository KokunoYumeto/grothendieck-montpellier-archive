## P01. Envelope variance and the finite-flat converse

The displayed map Hom(T̄,E)→Hom(T,E) has the variance of a reflection into the test category. It cannot be identified with the right adjoint used from scan 63 onward. If it is a bijection after every base change for all finite discrete étale targets, then over an algebraically closed point it detects the connected components of a finite fibre: a finite scheme over an algebraically closed field is a finite union of local Artin schemes, and maps to finite discrete sets are precisely maps from this finite component set. In this finite setting a bijection for all such targets forces a bijection of geometric points. Without the finite hypothesis it only detects connected-component information, not individual points.

For the converse, in the standard finite étale-envelope situation, q:T→T̄ is finite faithfully flat: flatness is local over an étale target, and finiteness follows by factoring through the closed graph when T̄ is separated over S. Given an S-map a:T→E with E/S unramified, the two pullbacks to R=T×T̄T agree. Indeed ΔT/R is surjective on underlying points because q is universally injective; the equalizer of the two maps to E is open because ΔE/S is an open immersion; that open contains the image of ΔT and so is all R. The fpqc sheaf property then descends a uniquely. The argument remains valid after base change. This proves the familiar finite étale case and the same conclusion whenever q is fpqc. The isolated word “sch. étale” and inherited target class must be read with the preceding package; this check does not silently make an arbitrary nonseparated étale target finite. Stacks tags 02GE and 023Q provide the two descent ingredients; tag 02KB explains why finite flat is not to be replaced by finite locally free without a finite-presentation assumption.

## P03. Affine representing construction and its finiteness bound

Write S=Spec A and let Λ be an affine ring scheme representing the underlying-set functor B↦W(B). A homomorphism C=W(A)[t1,…,tr]/I→W(B) is an r-tuple in W(B) annihilating every relation in I. Each relation is a morphism Λ^r→Λ because the ring operations and constants are represented. Its zero locus is closed; imposing all relations means taking the sum of their defining ideals. This construction works for an arbitrarily generated ideal I. Finite type is not finite presentation, so replacing I by a finite list without an extra hypothesis would be a gap. This is the earlier run's proof completion, independently re-proved here.

If C is not finitely generated, express it as the filtered union of its finitely generated W(A)-subalgebras Ci. If Ei represents Hom(Ci,W(–)), their transition maps have the reverse affine-scheme direction, and Spec(colim Ei) represents Hom(C,W(–)). If the coordinate algebra of Λ is generated by v elements and C by r, the quotient of the coordinate algebra of Λ^r has at most vr generators. This is an algebra-generator count, not a claim about the number of equations. Adding relations proves preservation of closed immersions of affine targets. Finite-type preservation needs Λ of finite type; the marginal generalization on scan 79 cannot remove that hypothesis by itself.

## P06. Constructibility and affine stratification

On a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme, quasi-compact opens are retrocompact. Finite Boolean combinations of such opens give the constructible subsets. A locally closed constructible set can locally be written D(f)∩V(g1,…,gr), with finite equations for its support. Conversely a quasi-compact locally closed subset with these local finite-equation descriptions is covered by finitely many constructible pieces and is constructible. The quotient by the radical of (g1,…,gr) need not be finitely presented. It is the existence of a suitable scheme structure with that support that the argument uses.

For irreducible closed T and a nonempty relatively open constructible Z⊂T, write Z as a finite union of locally closed constructible pieces. One contains the generic point of T, hence a relatively open neighbourhood of it. After choosing a quasi-compact ambient open and the finite Boolean expression, this yields a quasi-compact open U meeting T with U∖T quasi-compact. Conversely such U gives Z=U∩T=U∖(U∖T), a constructible nonempty relatively open subset. This verification is in the spectral/qcqs setting, where the quasi-compact/retrocompact requirements hold; it does not resolve the doubtful broader topological margin.

The constructible topology of a qcqs scheme is compact, with constructible subsets clopen. Therefore every cover by constructible sets has a finite subcover. In particular any partition into nonempty constructible sets is finite. This resolves the omitted “finite” in scan 87 mathematically without changing the transcription. Finite Boolean refinement followed by affine refinement gives a finite partition into affine locally closed constructible strata. EGA IV1 Corollary 1.9.9 and Proposition 1.9.15 give the exact published compactness statements.

## P07. Atomic points, constructible maps and finite limits

Maps from disjoint unions of field spectra are exactly the corresponding maps of field-valued point functors, so atomic schemes embed fully faithfully. For two maps with reduced source having the same field-valued maps, the point maps coincide and the difference of any two corresponding sections vanishes in every residue field; on an affine reduced source this difference is zero. Localizing proves faithfulness. Reduction itself is not generally faithfully remembered: Spec(k[ε]/ε²) and Spec k have the same field-valued functor.

For a constructible map, use a finite source partition on which it is a morphism. Inverse images of target constructible sets are constructible by EGA IV1 Proposition 1.8.2, and transitivity of constructibility along the strata puts them back in the whole source. This proves closure under composition by pulling back the second partition and refining the first. A constructible inverse can be made into a genuine inverse on pieces by the same finite refinement. Set-theoretic factorization through a reduced closed stratum is valid here because the source pieces are reduced; the reduced convention from scan 87 is essential.

Every object therefore has an affine model: take the finite disjoint union of its affine reduced strata. To model a map, first stratify its target affinely, pull those pieces back, and refine the source affinely. These are affine models in the constructible category, not claims that the original scheme or morphism is affine.

For a finite diagram, finitely many partitions admit a common finite refinement. On each reduced source piece, a compatible cone of scheme maps factors uniquely through the ordinary scheme limit. Equality of atomic maps is equality of scheme maps on a reduced source by faithfulness. This verifies the finite-limit universal property. Limits are interpreted in the indicated categories: the product of atomic schemes is obtained by atomizing the scheme product, and the product in the category of schemes all of whose local rings are fields is its corresponding reflection. It need not be their ordinary scheme product. Liu–Sebag Remark 4 gives an explicit warning about that last distinction. No infinite common-refinement conclusion follows.

Finite piecewise finite-type maps are stable under composition: pull back the finitely many intermediate strata, take reduced locally closed refinements, and use composition and base change of finite-type maps. A closed immersion is finite type even when its defining ideal is not finitely generated; this is one reason not to substitute finite presentation here. Constructible subsets themselves give intrinsic objects by the finite union of their reduced strata; common refinement proves independence, the coproduct decomposition X=E⊔(X∖E), and the factorization criterion stated on scan 95.

## P08. Two noetherian/finite-type counterexamples, fully checked

These are ideas of the earlier notes, not constructions written in the manuscript.

Let C=k[x,y], B=k+xC and J=xC. These are actual subrings/ideals; B is a domain, hence reduced. J²=x²C and J/J² has k-basis {xy^m:m≥0}. Since B/J=k, a finitely generated B-ideal J would make J/J² finite dimensional over k, which it is not. Thus B is not noetherian. On the other hand B[x⁻¹]=k[x,x⁻¹,y], and √(xB)=J, so the reduced closed complement of D(x) is Spec k. Both strata are constructible: D(x) is a principal open and its complement is principal closed. The finite disjoint union Spec(k[x,x⁻¹,y]×k) is noetherian and piecewise isomorphic to Spec B. It follows that even reducedness does not rescue literal scheme-noetherianness. Underlying topological noetherianness is preserved: a finite union of noetherian subspaces is noetherian.

For finite type take A=k+x²C⊂B=k+xC. Both are affine reduced domains. Localizing at x² identifies both with C[x⁻¹]. Their reduced closed complements are Spec k, since √(x²A)=x²C and √(x²B)=xC. Thus Spec B→Spec A is a genuine scheme morphism and is a constructible isomorphism. Set I=x²C⊂A; IB=x²C and B/IB=k⊕xk[y], with square-zero ideal xk[y]. If B were a finite-type A-algebra, this quotient would be a finite-type k-algebra. A square-zero k-algebra generated by finitely many elements has finite-dimensional nilpotent ideal, whereas xk[y] is infinite dimensional. Contradiction.

The simpler square-zero example B=k⊕M for infinite-dimensional M already refutes finite type and scheme-noetherianness after forgetting nilpotents. The reduced example above shows the failure persists under the source's reduced-piece convention. ## P09. The inverse-limit question and the exact common-partition criterion

This counterexample is from the earlier run, independently verified here. Take T=A¹ over Q, distinct rational points am=m, Xn=A^n over Q, and affine transition maps deleting the last coordinates. Define fn:T→Xn by making coordinate m the indicator of {am}. Each fn is a morphism on the finite reduced partition consisting of {a1},…,{an} and their complement, and the maps are compatible. All schemes are qcqs and all transitions are affine, as required by scan 97.

Suppose the family came from a constructible map T→X=Spec Q[t1,t2,…] with a single finite partition into locally closed reduced strata. The stratum containing the generic point is a nonempty open subset of A¹ and contains all but finitely many of the am. On this integral stratum each coordinate is a regular function which is zero at the generic point, so is identically zero. Choosing am in that stratum contradicts coordinate m taking value one. Thus the canonical cone cons(X) is not the indicated inverse limit.

The exact positive replacement is proved as follows. A constructible map to X supplies one partition on which all its projected maps are morphisms. Conversely, if a compatible family admits one common finite reduced constructible partition, the ordinary scheme-limit universal property on each piece produces its map into X, and these piece maps give a constructible map. The image of the comparison is exactly the common-partition families. This does not prove that the category lacks every possible limit object. It also does not contradict finite-presentation descent for a single fixed target, or the adjoint absolutely-flat construction of Olivier. The finite shadow in the code checks stagewise compatibility only; the generic-point argument proves the infinite obstruction.

## P11. Polarization and characteristic-p commutators

Define f_k, as an explicitly external reconstruction confirmed by Bourbaki Chapter I §1 Exercise 19 p.105, to be the sum of all ordered products of k inputs over permutations. With i copies of x and k−i copies of y, each word with those multiplicities appears i!(k−i)! times, giving the first formula without dividing by those factorials. The torn upper summation bound is still an ink question.

Lx and Rx commute, so ad x=Lx−Rx gives the binomial identity for every n. Modulo p the intermediate binomial coefficients of degree p vanish, giving (ad x)^p=ad(x^p); binom(p−1,i)≡(−1)^i gives the degree p−1 formula with all coefficients +1. Substitute x=ΣTj xj and compare the coefficient of T1⋯T(p−1) to obtain the multilinear identity. Substituting x=T1 x+T2 y and applying to y, compare T1^iT2^(p−1−i): on the right each word with i x's and p−i y's is obtained once for each of its p−i choices of distinguished y. This gives the final g-identity. These are all-prime proofs. ## P14. Distinct simple modules after base extension

For finite-dimensional nonisomorphic simple A-modules M1,M2, set C to be the image of A in End(M1⊕M2). C is finite dimensional, the direct sum is faithful semisimple, and its radical is zero. The two modules belong to distinct simple factors of the semisimple algebra C. A central idempotent of C, lifted to an element of A, acts as 1 on M1 and 0 on M2. After any field extension it still does. It is therefore impossible for a nonzero module to embed in both extended modules. This proof does not need the field extension to be separable.

## P15. Tensor actions and Jordan parts

The tensor action is a sum of operators in individual slots and commutes with permutations, so preserves the symmetric and antisymmetric submodules. For multiplication μ:M⊗M→M, the induced action is (x·μ)(a,b)=xμ(a,b)−μ(xa,b)−μ(a,xb); invariance is exactly the derivation identity.

Over an algebraic closure, a semisimple endomorphism is diagonal and its action on any mixed tensor power is diagonal with eigenvalues sums of the original eigenvalues and negatives on dual factors. The tensor action of a nilpotent is a sum of commuting nilpotents, hence nilpotent in every characteristic: if the individual exponents killing the factors are Ni, total degree greater than Σ(Ni−1) kills every monomial. The two tensor actions commute and give the Jordan decomposition, which descends over a perfect field. A vector killed by the original operator is killed by both Jordan parts: its invariant one-dimensional zero-action subspace is preserved by the polynomial Jordan projectors, whose constant terms on the zero generalized eigenspace are zero. Apply this to the invariant multiplication tensor to conclude that both parts of a derivation are derivations. This checks all clauses across scans92→90, matching Bourbaki §3 Exercises1–3 pp.113–114.

## P16. The incomplete norm report and the topological-group report

For normed spaces over a nondiscretely valued field K, bounded subsets of each Ei are absorbed by scalar multiples of its unit ball. A continuous multilinear map is bounded on a product of unit balls by rescaling a product neighbourhood on which its norm is bounded. The supremum on the source's product of one strict and the remaining weak unit balls is finite, homogeneous in the map and subadditive. Nontrivial scalars permit every nonzero input to be rescaled into that product, so the supremum separates maps. It induces uniform convergence on bounded sets: one direction uses that product itself is bounded, the other absorbs any bounded set into a scalar multiple in each coordinate. This proves a precise modern version. The missing opening hypotheses prevent certification of the archive's exact full statement. For a trivially valued field, strict unit balls can make the displayed expression zero on a nonzero map. There is no uniquely determined norm from the topology alone; the formula, if valid, determines the particular norm intended.

For a continuous transitive action, the orbit map G→E is continuous and constant exactly on left H-cosets. The quotient topology gives the continuous bijection G/H→E. It need not be a homeomorphism without openness of the orbit map. Bourbaki Topologie générale III §2 no5, p.III.12, states this same distinction and adopts “stabilisateur”; that is a verified publication outcome for the report's wording question.

Let G be Hausdorff, and let the directed closed normal subgroups Hα eventually lie in every identity neighbourhood. Their intersection is {1}, so G→lim G/Hα is injective. Every basic open set of the limit restricts finitely many coordinates; directedness replaces them by one larger index, and a representative of its coset in G meets it. Thus the image is dense.
