# SA02 — Ranked research directions

**Sixteen follow-up tasks; assessment date: 19 November 2026.** These directions come from the consolidated source and claim registers. They are proposals for subsequent work, not additional results developed in this consolidation. Origin identifiers are the active canonical identifiers after the mergers in SA02_aliases.tsv. For Codex-only material, the absence of an indexed Stage 1 item is stated explicitly.

The order expresses mathematical or editorial interest. **Tractability is a separate judgment:** “high” means that a useful, bounded deliverable is realistic in one research run of a few hours, not that a broad open problem will be solved. This is not an execution order. In particular, the image audit in task 16 can supply the endpoint conventions needed by task 1, and task 3 should fix the axiom list before task 4 is attempted.

The independent proofs already completed here remain completed. In particular, root P10 establishes the early χ-extension product's coherence and invertibility account, and P11 proves the corrected coinvariant criterion, including the rank-zero exception. None of the tasks below reopens those proofs merely because later fragments are less complete. A class-C literature finding can mean that an exact published formulation was not identified, even when the mathematical statement is proved in this dossier.

## 1. Strict χ-comparison: endpoint conventions and obstruction

**Origins.** Items SA02-45, SA02-46, SA02-49, SA02-61, SA02-62, SA02-214, SA02-232, SA02-234, SA02-238. Claims CL-SA02-032, CL-SA02-033, CL-SA02-034, CL-SA02-042, CL-SA02-043, CL-SA02-207, CL-SA02-209, CL-SA02-211, CL-SA02-212, CL-SA02-213, CL-SA02-214. Problems MP-SA02-14, MP-SA02-100, MP-SA02-103, MP-SA02-105. Main source: cote 84, scans 126–142 and 154–170.

**What it would establish.** An exact comparison statement between the displayed pushout and pullback extensions, with the permitted maps on both endpoints specified. It should distinguish existence, the torsor of possible comparisons, a preferred comparison, and naturality.

**Already established and limits.** The identity-endpoint interpretation has a complete χ=0 elliptic-curve counterexample: the two classes are e and −e. The local polynomial comparison works when γχ=2; the explicitly chosen χ=2, γ=1 specialization is valid. Neither fact invalidates the earlier pushout product. The short-five-lemma argument proves that a compatible middle map is an isomorphism; it does not construct it.

**First concrete step.** Read the endpoint labels on scans 154, 166, 168 and 170 from the images, and put them beside the explicit identities at 142 in one convention table. With that table fixed, write the comparison as an equality, or sign-twisted equality, of extension classes. The one-run deliverable is a theorem with exact hypotheses or a precisely delimited obstruction statement, with the Hom-valued ambiguity retained. Without images, state conditional branches rather than selecting the ink.

## 2. The nonflat relative norm: intrinsic statement and the remaining extension problem

**Origins.** Items SA02-8, SA02-13, SA02-14, SA02-38, SA02-39. Claims CL-SA02-004, CL-SA02-005, CL-SA02-007, CL-SA02-008, CL-SA02-009, CL-SA02-010, CL-SA02-029, CL-SA02-051. Problems MP-SA02-03, MP-SA02-05, MP-SA02-11. Main source: cote 84, scans 101–107 and 121–122.

**What it would establish.** A basis-free, functorial theorem for the verified element-valued norm of the tensor algebra over the contracted quadratic product, together with a precise statement of what is additionally required for the proposed triple inverse-image construction.

**Already established and limits.** Existence, uniqueness by pure-tensor values and polarization, multiplicativity, quadratic homogeneity, and the special tower identities are checked here for genuine quadratic inputs. The coefficient proof does not assume intermediate flatness, and its characterization supplies basis independence. EGA II §6.5 has a reduced generically free alternative as well as its finite locally free case; its base-change restrictions matter. Ferrand and Biesel have different norm objects and finite locally free base hypotheses. None automatically settles every nilpotent triple inverse image. [EGAII; Ferrand1998; Biesel2024; Roby1963.]

**First concrete step.** Express the verified norm using the pushout trace and its universal characterization, then compare it with the ordinary determinant norm on the locus where the intermediate algebra is finite locally free. State exactly which map and axioms would be needed to transport that theorem to scan 122. A polished intrinsic theorem and an explicit residual extension problem are realistic; the binary polynomial identities need not be rediscovered.

## 3. Weak and strong quasi-quadratic structures

**Origins.** Items SA02-15, SA02-17, SA02-18, SA02-19, SA02-20, SA02-21, SA02-22, SA02-24. Claims CL-SA02-011, CL-SA02-012, CL-SA02-013, CL-SA02-014, CL-SA02-015, CL-SA02-016, CL-SA02-017, CL-SA02-019, CL-SA02-052. Problems MP-SA02-06, MP-SA02-07, MP-SA02-08. Main source: cote 84, scans 107–114.

**What it would establish.** Two clearly defined categories, one for the five-axiom recap and one for the earlier universally multiplicative norm, together with a dependency diagram for the constructions that follow.

**Already established and limits.** The Z/4 counterexample separates the definitions even at the polynomial-law level. Faithful scalars restore multiplicativity from the retained identities. The strong version has a precise degree-two Cayley–Hamilton comparison; the weaker one does not gain multiplicativity by being renamed. The text does not establish whether the omission was intentional. [Roby1963, Proposition II.1; Chenevier2014, §§1.5 and 1.19.]

**First concrete step.** Write every axiom as an identity of laws with its source and target. Draw only implication arrows for which a proof is available, and place the existing counterexample at the failed reverse implication. Then assign each relative trace, relative norm and biquadratic construction to the weakest sufficient set of axioms. The one-run result should be a usable definition and implication memorandum, without altering the diplomatic recap or attempting an unrestricted classification.

## 4. The scalar image of the contracted triple product

**Origins.** Items SA02-22, SA02-24, SA02-27, SA02-28, SA02-29, SA02-30, SA02-32, SA02-33, SA02-35. Claims CL-SA02-018, CL-SA02-019, CL-SA02-020, CL-SA02-021, CL-SA02-022, CL-SA02-023, CL-SA02-024, CL-SA02-025, CL-SA02-026, CL-SA02-027. Problems MP-SA02-08, MP-SA02-09, MP-SA02-10, MP-SA02-11. Main source: cote 84, scans 111–122.

**What it would establish.** A theorem or counterexample deciding whether the compatible three-subalgebra system forces the contracted triple-product map into A to have scalar image, under one explicit weak or strong axiom list.

**Already established and limits.** The common-trace argument gives scalar invariants when 2 is invertible. The all-three-étale proposition has its stated faithful-scalar, generation and invariant hypotheses. The mixed-locus theorem concerns the triple product's identification with tensor invariants; it must not be substituted for the general scalar-image assertion. The characteristic-2 example generated by e, f and e+f has excess invariants but still scalar product image, so it is not a counterexample to the actual question. [SGA1; Stacks04HN.]

**First concrete step.** Use the definitions fixed in task 3 to write a universal presentation containing the three scalar maps, involutions, traces and norms. Identify the image of the contracted triple generator in that presentation and the relations available to force it to be scalar. Keep injectivity of the base scalar map explicit. A rigorously typed universal presentation and a first restricted theorem or fully compliant counterexample are realistic; a finite search without that presentation would not settle the problem.

## 5. A global χ=2 product-duality theorem

**Origins.** Items SA02-6, SA02-222, SA02-223, SA02-257, SA02-258, SA02-259, SA02-262, SA02-263, SA02-264, SA02-268, SA02-269, SA02-270. Claims CL-SA02-004, CL-SA02-007, CL-SA02-210, CL-SA02-224, CL-SA02-225, CL-SA02-226, CL-SA02-227, CL-SA02-234. Problems MP-SA02-104, MP-SA02-109, MP-SA02-110, MP-SA02-111. Main source: cote 84, scans 160 and 174–190.

**What it would establish.** The exact global natural duality isomorphism supported by the quadratic χ=2 pairing, with determinant lines, finite projectivity and the permitted morphisms stated explicitly.

**Already established and limits.** The full four-block pairing annihilates both relation submodules and has identity matrix in the abstract dual product bases. It is perfect without inverting 2. The ordinary tensor pairing restricts to twice this pairing, so its characteristic-2 degeneration does not contradict abstract perfectness. Formal tensor-Hom duality alone does not supply the specific product duality. An arbitrary-χ statement remains a separate question. [EGAI1960, chapitre 0, 5.4.2; Loos1997.]

**First concrete step.** Define the two pushouts and the four pairing blocks intrinsically from their boundary maps. Check the change-of-frame and naturality diagrams using the morphisms already permitted by the construction. The intended one-run deliverable is a global theorem and its functorial scope, starting from the completed descent and perfectness certificates rather than repeating the local matrix proof.

## 7. The precise Dedekind stable-envelope and finite-cover theorem

Problems MP-SA02-200, MP-SA02-201, MP-SA02-202, MP-SA02-203, MP-SA02-204. Source: cote 90, even scans 2–24.

**What it would establish.** A corrected theorem for finite saturation, equivariant stable envelopes and finite equivariant covers, with the different finiteness and projectivity assumptions reconciled across the drafts.

**Already established and limits.** The squarefree-denominator example disproves the literal exclusion equivalence. The surviving finite-saturation implications and finite coefficient-coalgebra method are distinct positive results. A finite-type module cannot silently be replaced by an arbitrary quasi-coherent module in a finite-cover conclusion. The ample graded adjunction is already checked; it does not supply a stable hull without the coalgebra assumptions. [DuongHai2018; SGA3VIB1970; SGA3VIB2024; Raynaud1972; EGAII, §4.6.3.]

**First concrete step.** Make a hypothesis matrix for scans 14, 18, 20, 22 and 24, with separate columns for coalgebra freeness/projectivity, finite saturation, quasi-compactness, finite-type input and available ordinary resolutions. Match the surviving rows to the exact SGA 3 statements. A corrected theorem and an explicit list of failed weakenings are realistic outcomes.

## 8. Finite partitions, limits and the category Cons

**Origins.** SA02-549 is an indexed comparison lead. The underlying continuation is Codex-only: no indexed item exists for those additional source passages. Problems MP-SA02-229, MP-SA02-230, MP-SA02-231, MP-SA02-232, MP-SA02-233, MP-SA02-239. Source: cote 91, scans 87–122 and 136.

**What it would establish.** The exact limit compatibility of a specified finite-stratification category, with ordinary noetherianity separated from having a noetherian constructible model.

**Already established and limits.** Infinitely many point-indicator coordinates show why individually finite constructible partitions need not admit one common finite refinement. A nondiscrete rank-one valuation example separates ordinary noetherianity from constructible isomorphism. The absolutely flat envelope retains residue fields as well as points; it is not a bare-set replacement. Finite limits and an arbitrary inverse-limit interchange are different assertions. [Olivier1968; Stacks01CF; Stacks0901; Stacks01ZC.]

**First concrete step.** Choose a small skeleton, specify the allowed finite reduced strata and transition maps, and write the comparison map before claiming it is bijective. Prove the comparison on a finite-presentation/qcqs subcategory, then test exactly where the existing indicator example leaves that subcategory. A restricted theorem with a sharp failure example is a useful one-run deliverable.

## 9. Tensor-image kernels and excess invariants under base change

**Origins.** Items SA02-7, SA02-10, SA02-30, SA02-33, SA02-242, SA02-243, SA02-245, SA02-421. Claims CL-SA02-005, CL-SA02-006, CL-SA02-024, CL-SA02-025, CL-SA02-217, CL-SA02-748, CL-SA02-749, CL-SA02-753. Problems MP-SA02-04, MP-SA02-107, MP-SA02-316. Main source: cote 84, scans 84–92, 102–104, 117–122, 172 and 192. The early Codex passages have no indexed item.

**What it would establish.** A global defect description separating ordinary injectivity, R-linearly split injectivity, universal injectivity, equality with invariants, and flatness of the tensor-cover map.

**Already established and limits.** The local kernel is Ann(2,b₁,b₂)·U, and the unit-ideal condition gives an R-linear splitting. The good-locus invariant statements, the corrected all-ranks coinvariant criterion, and the simultaneous-nonétale flatness obstruction are already proved. In particular, root P11's rank-zero repair is a starting theorem, not a remaining conjecture. A field-only slogan does not describe arbitrary 2-torsion rings. [Loos1997, Proposition 2.9; Voight2016.]

**First concrete step.** Express the coefficient ideal and its annihilator intrinsically using the quotient lines and trace maps. Form a defect sequence whose kernel and cokernel measure the tensor realization and excess invariants, and state the base changes for which each term behaves well. The task is to globalize and relate established local facts, not repeat the finite counterexamples.

## 10. Exact categorical comparisons with Loos and Pirashvili

**Origins.** Items SA02-45, SA02-50, SA02-67, SA02-403, SA02-407, SA02-408, SA02-409. Claims CL-SA02-005, CL-SA02-032, CL-SA02-053, CL-SA02-200, CL-SA02-201, CL-SA02-202, CL-SA02-214, CL-SA02-700, CL-SA02-701, CL-SA02-733, CL-SA02-740, CL-SA02-741, CL-SA02-744, CL-SA02-745. Problems MP-SA02-14, MP-SA02-15, MP-SA02-101, MP-SA02-300, MP-SA02-301, MP-SA02-308, MP-SA02-315. The early graded and χ-product passages are Codex-only; no indexed item exists for those passages.

**What it would establish.** Two explicitly scoped comparison functors or obstructions: one for the graded two-sort construction versus Loos, and one for a specified χ-extension subcategory versus Pirashvili. They are separate comparisons, not a claim that all these categories coincide.

**Already established and limits.** The even quadratic product is an exact match. The positive-four odd–odd law does not match Loos's negative-four discriminant and Koszul symmetry by changing every odd sign. Pirashvili's pq+2=0 matches p=−χ, q=γ, but its unit condition, Hopf-torsor data and arrows are additional. The early product's coherence and invertibility have already been proved in root P10. [Loos1997, §§2.8–2.11; Voight2016, Construction 3.14; Pirashvili2019, §§3–4.]

**First concrete step.** Make an object/arrow/unit/symmetry table and define the proposed map on one local chart, including generator and endpoint conventions. Check one nontrivial change-of-frame square and the odd–odd symmetry before claiming a global equivalence. A precise subcategory or a concrete obstruction is realistic; a new proof of the already established early coherence is not the objective.

## 11. The parity-root functor on the correct test category

**Origins.** No indexed item exists: this is an early cote-84 Codex-only direction. Claims CL-SA02-727, CL-SA02-728, CL-SA02-729, CL-SA02-730, CL-SA02-731, CL-SA02-732. Problem MP-SA02-307. Main source: cote 84, scans 49–54.

**What it would establish.** A precise comparison between the finite quadratic cover, its congruent-root description on 2-regular test rings, and the naïve congruence-root functor on arbitrary base changes.

**Already established and limits.** The discriminant/parity classification under regularity of 2 is established. The division formulas are valid on their parity lattice. Root P07 proves nonrepresentability of the naïve congruence subgroup at a local point where 2 is nonzero and noninvertible, and provides a parity-compatible flat root cover for the analogous obstruction. Reproving that obstruction is unnecessary. [Dallaporta2025, Theorem 2.25; the exact examples and source comparisons recorded with CL-SA02-727–729.]

**First concrete step.** Define all three functors on an explicit test category and write the natural transformations among them. Evaluate them on the quadratic cover itself and on square-zero thickenings that introduce 2-torsion. Identify exactly which equality fails after unrestricted base change and whether a restricted site or additional lift data gives the intended description. The result should be a corrected functorial statement, not another unsupported representability claim.

## 12. Condition R, purity and control of components

**Origins.** Items SA02-525, SA02-526, SA02-527, SA02-528, SA02-529, SA02-530, SA02-531, SA02-545. Claims CL-SA02-536, CL-SA02-537, CL-SA02-538, CL-SA02-539, CL-SA02-540, CL-SA02-541, CL-SA02-542. Problems MP-SA02-209, MP-SA02-210. Main source: cote 91, scans 8–11, with the cote-90 component example as a comparison.

**What it would establish.** A quantified equivalence or nonequivalence between the exact live condition R and a published purity or component-control condition under specified finiteness assumptions.

**Already established and limits.** R tests open subgroups after the stated local base changes. The extra closedness condition was deleted. Its sufficient examples use finite schemes mapping to the group, not necessarily finite subgroups. The disappearing-component example is a concrete test case. Published essential-freeness and density hypotheses give relevant centralizer/transporter theorems, but have not been shown to match every quantifier of R. [SGA3VIII2009, §6; SGA3XI2008, §6; Raynaud1972.]

**First concrete step.** Write R with all base-change quantifiers and compare open, open-and-closed, noetherian-local and DVR tests in separate rows. Run the existing disappearing-component example through each row before attempting an implication. A proof of one precise equivalence, or a counterexample separating two plausible versions, would be a substantial one-run result.

## 14. Laurent stages, nilpoint substitutions and jets

**Origins.** No indexed item exists for these Codex-only handwritten constructions. Claims CL-SA02-598, CL-SA02-599, CL-SA02-601, CL-SA02-602, CL-SA02-603. Problems MP-SA02-237, MP-SA02-238. Source: cote 91, scans 134–142; the reused formal-group exercises are distinct witnesses.

**What it would establish.** The exact functorial relation among the bounded Laurent-unit stages, formal coordinate changes on nilpotent points, and automorphisms of a specified truncated algebra.

**Already established and limits.** The affine Laurent stages bound both a series and its inverse and include arbitrarily deep nilpotent negative coefficients. Zero-constant formal substitutions with invertible linear part behave as checked. A nilpotent constant term can be legitimate on a suitable formal test functor while failing to preserve a fixed truncation ideal. Over a general base, a determinant must be a unit, not merely nonzero. [ContouCarrere1994, Lemma 1.3; KapranovVasserot2004.]

**First concrete step.** Specify every source ring, topology, ideal and test algebra, and examine the translation t↦t+ε on k[ε,t]/(ε²,t²). Then derive the source's quadratic composition coefficients within the chosen convention and state the allowed transition maps between stages. An elementary theorem with explicit boundary examples is realistic; identifying all three functors by notation is not.

**Already established and limits.** The affine coefficient construction and appropriate gluing arguments are positive results. For each surviving positive entry, give the exact source theorem or a proof from the universal property; mark the extra assumptions required to pass from Weil restriction to the stated Greenberg adjoint. This is a synthesis of precise results, not a proposal to re-prove the known counterexamples.
