# Codex cote 43: completed close reading, first mathematical audit

All nine supplied Codex transcriptions were read in scan order, including diagrams, cancellations, qualifications and reused sheets. The eight supplied short notes files were read as leads only; they receive no indexed-entry verdict. K43-02 and its scans 17–31 are not supplied. K43-04's note describes an unfinished earlier transcription audit; this run uses its supplied transcription without claiming an ink check. K43-07 has no short notes file but its entire transcription was read.

## Scans 1–16 (K43-01)

The first sheets develop absolute divisors, ray class groups and idèles, then state Artin–Takagi reciprocity, existence, norm limitation and local–global compatibility. These are explicitly attributed background, not novel conjectures. The later statement on scan 10 targets the abelianization, correcting the unrestricted target G on scan 5. Potential readings: units outside the modulus versus all local multiplicative groups (4:78); ramified versus nonramified S (5:102); the incorrect intermediate quotient in the E0 line (7:244); the sign in the local-to-ideal representative (10:346–355); and the missing profinite completion in A(Kp) ≅ Kp* (13:425). On 15–16 J_C has a degree augmentation to Z, so it must not silently mean only Pic^0. Picard points versus actual line bundles still requires the base-field hypothesis. The local-symbol and Lang pairing diagrams are not full proofs.

## Scans 32–48 (K43-03)

32–35 propose Ext/cohomology comparisons through a generalized Picard object and a degree-two Yoneda class; splitting with a section is conditional on those comparisons. The category, coefficients and relative conventions are essential. 37–38 give Witt-series formulas: multiplication of series is Witt addition, Frobenius acts on coefficients in characteristic p, V substitutes t^p, and FV=VF=p. The plus-sign Teichmüller convention is to be kept consistent. 40–42 explicitly question admissibility and passage to the limit; Lang's theorem does not imply H^i(F_q,Z)=0 for every i>0 (H^2 ≅ Q/Z in continuous discrete cohomology). 43's H^1(k,G) ≅ G(k) requires a finite étale coefficient and is not canonical without additional Frobenius/module identifications. 45–48 develop restricted products along flags and iterated localization/completion. In the smooth two-dimensional model, the order of operations gives k((t))[[s]] and then k((t))((s)); ordinary localization and completion are not interchangeable.

## Scans 49–63 (K43-04)

49–53 relate flags to Hom between indecomposable injectives. 50's tensor description needs finitely presented M. A decisive gap on 51 is Hom(U_i,colim V_j)=colim Hom(U_i,V_j): U_i is finite over A_p, not necessarily finitely presented over A. For R=k[[t]], p=(0), q=(t), U=K=k((t)), every Hom_R(K,t^{-n}R/R) vanishes, whereas the quotient K→K/R is nonzero. This is a mathematical counterexample, not an ink correction. 54–58 define simplicial coefficients and then quotient interior cofaces; the degeneracy caveat is explicit and the square-zero differential needs its graded signs. 60–63 construct a graded path algebra by concatenation of saturated chains and impose Θ²=0. Associativity is actually checked by the threefold product on 63; the topological restricted-sum and convergence hypotheses are not fully settled. The displayed expansion of (u1−u2)² is incomplete as printed.

## Scans 64–78 (K43-05)

64–69 continue the flag/path algebra, its several topologies and its comparison with End of a residue complex. 65 revises 'local' to 'semilocal': multiple branches cannot be suppressed. 67's plus graded-commutator display conflicts with its immediately following degree-zero minus sign; the correct endomorphism-complex differential is d(u)=Θu−(−1)^|u|uΘ. 71–76 explicitly start with Zariski cohomology and locally trivial extensions, yielding concentration for split commutative algebraic groups over an algebraically closed base and regular local rings. The formal local-Jacobian representing pro-object is stronger than the scalar local-cohomology calculation. 77–78 change to 'Weil cohomology'; no blanket identification with arbitrary fppf/étale cohomology is justified. The assertion that a connected linear part disappears in every positive degree needs restrictive hypotheses; for example Brauer groups obstruct an unrestricted fppf reading.

## Scans 79–93 and 143–144 (K43-06)

79 leaves radicial purity as a question; 80 proposes the generalized-Jacobian complex, with H_1=G_m for proper curves and zero for nonproper curves; 81–82 are an unfinished relative/unipotent construction. 84 and 86 question the relation among fppf/fpqc, quasi-algebraic and perfect objects: killing radicial morphisms is a deliberate change of category, not harmless for α_p. 89–90 formulate local duality with shifts and supports; 92–93 define a tentative Weil site and ask for R^i j_*. The bibliography on 85/87 is reused printed material, not a manuscript date. 143–145 are English statements matching Tate's finite-module duality programme and should be identified as copied or parallel statements without assigning handwriting authorship. They include local Euler characteristics, unramified annihilators, finite-flat p-primary examples, and Poitou–Tate exactness.

## Scans 145–160 (K43-07)

145 continues the nine-term sequence with Euler characteristic and finite-group reduction of the localization kernel. 146 is a separate tentative abelian-variety extension involving finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group, not a theorem proving finiteness. 148–150 develop integral and rational l-adic representations; the arrow V_l→M_l on 149 is reversed relative to its tensor formula and the correct diagram on 150. 151–156 distinguish monodromy on a finite model from the possibly smaller monodromy after an infinite extension. Their 'Serre algebra' is model-dependent in this precise spread-out sense, not simply Lie(image π1 of the limit). 158 asks about Lie algebras and subgroup germs. The finite-dimensional analytic and closed-subgroup hypotheses are indispensable: a general compact torsion group need not be finite.

## Scans 161–177 (K43-08)

161–162 prove a coherent Ext-with-support/direct-limit comparison using injectivity of torsion in an injective module over a noetherian ring. 162–165 are internal Hom and geometric Ext tables: their nonreduced test rings matter. Hom(G_a,G_m)(k)=0 does not make the internal Hom zero; Cartier's dual V=D(G_a) is a formal object. Finite coefficients, α_p and μ_p can be checked by Hopf algebras/restricted Lie algebras; undeciphered entries and question marks remain undecided. 167–169 give a real proof of the primitive-torsor/extension correspondence under rigidity hypotheses, including associativity and unit, with inverse referred to Serre p.183. 169–170 descend étale extensions across universal homeomorphisms and identify Ext^1(G_a,Z/p) with a perfection, referring to Grothendieck's Chevalley exposé. 172–177 classify affine/formal group pieces by F and V; finite-type/perfect-base assumptions cannot be dropped in the ind/pro generalization.

## Scans 178–193 (K43-09)

178–180 continue structure diagrams, including imperfect-base qualifications. 181–184 try to glue algebraic and formal Dieudonné categories; the completion and exact category are not fixed. 185–192 ask whether affine group schemes are pro finite-type groups and whether coalgebras are unions of finite subcoalgebras. The field case is affirmed on 192 and is distinguished from the still-questioned ring case. 188–189 describe Cartier duality through continuous dual Hopf algebras, conditional over a general ring. 190 correctly warns that ordinary inverse limits forget pro-information. The proposed 'artinian objects' sufficient condition on 191 is false without a condition on the ambient category: Pro(FinSets)→Sets forgets continuity. 193's extension-closed ind-affine category is presumed abelian, not proved.

## Scans 195–208 (K43-10)

195's Ind of a small length category is standard. The converse criterion on 196 based solely on exhaustion by finite socle stages is too strong as stated: for R=k⊕V, V²=0 and dim_k V infinite, every R-module has Loewy length ≤2, but R is not a union of finite-length submodules. Local noetherianity repairs this implication. 197–202 are formal/Dieudonné module sketches with genuine convention gaps; 199's F^nU=p^nU=0 needs local, not uniform, nilpotence on the union. 200's F,V normal-form multiplications are explicitly testable. 204–205 construct the Tate pairing through the Picard–Brauer obstruction, then ask about complete cohomology for finite covers; finiteness alone does not supply a Galois Tate-cohomology formalism. 206 computes the tame symbol, reciprocity and idelic pairing; the subgroup U must be strict units for the stated orthogonality. 207–208 generalize through the Albanese kernel and a Poincaré divisor. The last independence check is unfinished, and choices of divisor and trivializations cannot be suppressed.

These are reading findings, not a substitute for the claim-by-claim literature records and certificates that follow.
