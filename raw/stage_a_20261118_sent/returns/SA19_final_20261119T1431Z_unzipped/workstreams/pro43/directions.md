## 1. Define the Weil/Néron realization dictionary

**Origin:** SA19-24, 26, 27; CL-SA19-001, 007–012; MP-SA19-01–03. The preceding Codex faces 43/92–93 are a verified cross-link, not a substitute definition.

**Would establish:** exactly which manuscript objects and coverings map to a modern rational/ind-rational site, what its valuation-ring realization does, and which left exactness, continuity and acyclicity assertions follow. The result should separate a map of sites, a premorphism and a derived comparison.

**Known:** Suzuki's Ext comparison and realization theorems are precise for their coefficient classes; the infinite-Witt filtered-continuity assertion in the manuscript is explicitly withdrawn. No equivalence between every handwritten quasi-scheme object and Suzuki's category is presently established in this audit.

**First concrete step:** transcribe the objects, arrows and covering conditions on 43/92–98 from the scans into a two-column dictionary, then verify only the finite-flat coefficient case against Suzuki's hypotheses. Record any remaining unproved functor property as a separate lemma. Do not begin by assuming an exact inverse-image functor.

## 2. Transport the characteristic-p curve table through Oda

**Origin:** SA19-9, 10, 60–67; CL-SA19-066–073; MP-SA19-26. Companion targets 54/3,5,7 and the exact Oda locators are already checked.

**Would establish:** the specific correspondence between the manuscript's Frobenius kernel/cokernel pairing, the Cartier pairing on finite Jacobian torsion, and the de Rham–Dieudonné realization. This includes the distinction between a full group scheme and its restriction to perfect test schemes.

**Known:** Oda's anti-equivalence, dual operator formulas, Frobenius twist and Corollary 5.11 are exact established results. The full-relative \(\mu_p\) row is not verified by merely invoking a perfected cohomology theory. The literal full-\(J[p]\) fundamental-group reading is false.

**First concrete step:** choose a pointed smooth proper curve over a perfect field, write the Abel–Jacobi pullback and Oda comparison maps with domains, and compute the transpose of one Frobenius map. Use an ordinary and a supersingular elliptic curve to check the realization conventions, without treating those examples as proof of the general identity.

## 3. Normalize the local-to-curve trace and support triangle

**Origin:** SA19-33, 40, 43–48; CL-SA19-022, 059, 075–083; MP-SA19-11, 23, 28–32.

**Would establish:** one consistent derived convention for the local geometric shift, the ordinary Tate shift after residue-field descent, the curve trace, and the connecting map in localization.

**Known:** Suzuki proves finite-flat local/curve duality and compatibility of the relevant localization triangles. SGA2 supplies the standard support sequence. The boundary term is generally a quotient of field cohomology, and the manuscript's unshifted box fails in ordinary degree zero.

**First concrete step:** draw the finite-flat localization triangle for a smooth proper curve minus one point, label every degree under \(H^i(K[1])=H^{i+1}(K)\), and compare its arrows with the scanned corrections on 43/135–140. Read Artin–Milne's printed shift using its own convention before translating it. This task should yield a commentary diagram, not an emendation made without ink.

## 4. Complete the low-degree abelian Ext ledger

**Origin:** SA19-16, 18; CL-SA19-042–049, 052; MP-SA19-15, 17–18; verified link to root's 43/162–165 table.

**Would establish:** exactly the truncation of internal \(R\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mu_n)\) sufficient for each global degree used in the manuscript, and the hypothesis needed for passage to \(\mu_\infty\).

**Known:** Barsotti–Weil and Ribeiro–Rosengarten's arbitrary-base Ext² theorem give internal degrees 0, 1 and 2 and global degrees 1 and 2. Higher Ext can be nonzero. A finite-level long exact sequence does not prove an all-degree derived identity or filtered continuity.

**First concrete step:** list the \(E_2\)-terms contributing to total degrees 1–4 and the possible differentials, then locate an exact continuity theorem applicable to the actual base and sheaf arguments. Keep geometric Yoneda Ext and internal sheaf Ext in separate columns. This is a bounded verification task; it is not a request to classify all higher abelian-scheme extensions.

## 5. Match local Rosenlicht reciprocity to the derived duality map

**Origin:** SA19-1, 19, 32; CL-SA19-015, 017–018, 034–035; MP-SA19-07, 10.

**Would establish:** which precise map from the local proalgebraic class group to a character group the manuscript denotes, and how it becomes Serre reciprocity or Suzuki's derived multiplicative duality.

**Known:** Serre's 1961 theorem identifies the fundamental group of proalgebraic units with the abelian Galois group for algebraically closed residue field; modern derived versions exist. The arbitrary-group, all-positive-degree displayed comparison is broader than the directly verified theorem.

**First concrete step:** use a complete discretely valued field with algebraically closed residue field, identify the author's \(J_{K/k}\) with a specified unit/class-group object if possible, and compare the finite cyclic character map. Only after this should residue Galois descent and its shift be introduced.

## 6. Determine the ultimate fate of EGA IV3 12.2.3(vi)

**Origin:** SA19-75–76; CL-SA19-091–094; MP-SA19-33–34.

**Would establish:** whether the exact universally-open/full-support/S₁ multiplicity extension and its associated-prime formulation were subsequently proved, refuted, or reformulated.

**Known:** EGA proves the flat case and explicitly leaves the stronger question unresolved in 1966. This consolidation located that exact primary passage. The \(R\oplus k\) model refutes a different literal inequality-implies-flat sentence; the regular-element depth argument answers only the stronger reading in which \(t\) avoids all associated primes.

**First concrete step:** follow citations to EGA IV3 12.2.3(vi) and search modern work on universal openness, fibre multiplicities and associated primes, keeping a literal hypothesis table. An absence of citations is not a proof of current openness. Before any new proof attempt, recover the scan's “minimal” versus “associated” reading.

## 7. Reconstruct support duality and formal completion at one local model

**Origin:** SA19-36–38; CL-SA19-053–056; MP-SA19-19–20; root duplicate CL-SA19-647 merges into CL-SA19-054.

**Would establish:** the exact degrees, dualizing object and derived/inverse-limit operations needed to turn the unfinished 43/123–125 diagram into a valid special case.

**Known:** SGA2 V and VI give regular-local duality and the coherent thickening-colimit theorem; IX gives formal functions under finiteness and Mittag–Leffler conditions. The DVR model is fully checked. Hom from a nonfinite first argument already fails the naive colimit interchange.

**First concrete step:** reconstruct the diagram for a regular complete local ring and a finite module, putting derived completion wherever the formal theorem requires it. Compare with the independently supplied Codex finiteness hypotheses on 43/161–162. Stop before generalizing to an unidentified \(F\), \(G\), or support notation.

## 8. Recover the intended finite-Witt quotient and its dual

**Origin:** SA19-87; CL-SA19-016; MP-SA19-06.

**Would establish:** a well-typed finite-group statement behind the queried quotient on 43/102, including whether its operator is Frobenius, a Frobenius power, or an Artin–Schreier–Witt difference.

**Known:** finite Cartier–Dieudonné theory and local Artin–Schreier–Witt reciprocity provide several different possible duals. They do not identify the author's expression by similarity alone.

**First concrete step:** inspect the scan, fix the operator and whether the object is a group scheme, a geometric realization or a group of field points, then calculate its Dieudonné module. Until those choices are fixed, a claim of self-duality is not a definite theorem to develop.

## 9. Trace Atiyah's intrinsic-join/diagonal comparison

**Known:** the source is Atiyah's *Thom Complexes*, p. 309. Adams–Walker and Sigrist–Suter settle relevant numerical existence criteria, but these results do not assert equality of those named maps. This audit located no exact later resolution of the map comparison.
