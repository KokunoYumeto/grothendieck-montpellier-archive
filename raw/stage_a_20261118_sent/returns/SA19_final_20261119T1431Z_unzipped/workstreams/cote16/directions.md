## 1. Local Picard discriminant pairing and later duality

The target is a proposition with every hypothesis and every comparison map specified: the finite discriminant group of the exceptional intersection lattice should carry the same pairing as the component quotient of the local cohomological pairing. This is substantially more precise than observing that both groups are finite and self-dual.

The source anchor is cote 16/19, with the cohomological background on 21 and 23. Grothendieck's published *Brauer III*, §8.4, pp. 148–151, supplies the omitted complete normal two-dimensional local hypotheses and the chosen coefficient field. Remark 8.5 explicitly says that the compatibility verification was not performed. The elementary lattice proof and the blowup matrix congruence already checked in this run establish only their stated algebraic parts. Mumford's complex topological calculation is also a distinct ingredient.

Suzuki (2024), Proposition 11.10, pp. 500–501, gives an exact later comparison in mixed characteristic for p-primary component pairings. Its proof reduces cup products and traces to a tame symbol and then to an exceptional intersection number. The first concrete step is a side-by-side definition table: base ring, residue field, coefficient prime, Picard object, component group, trace normalization, discriminant sign and resolution invariance. Only after that table should one ask whether its method directly proves the old equal-characteristic formulation or whether a separate prime-to-characteristic argument is needed. A successful short run could locate the precise later completion or state exactly which transfer is missing, without claiming that the old gap is still a modern open problem.

## 2. Intrinsic projector induction

The target is an edition-ready proof of the theorem whose draft calculations appear on 5–12 and 51–55. The theorem is established in the published Lefschetz formalism; the work here is to explain the written proof accurately and distinguish a valid repair from the ink.

Three independent faults need separate treatment: the terminal index n−1 versus n, the unsupported identity involving the global identity operator instead of π_i, and the one-sided truncation on scan 53. The H*(P²) certificate already refutes the last literal assertion while satisfying the stated inverse hypotheses. A source-degree restriction and a global identity are not interchangeable conventions.

Kleiman (1968), Proposition 2.9 and Lemma 3.15, provide the relevant formal theorem and two-sided block method. The first step is to place every operator in a table with domain, codomain, degree and the block on which its inverse identity is imposed, then compare the formulas to the scans. The resulting companion can retain the original equations, mark their actual status, and put the supported reconstruction next to them. No new claim of geometric inverse-cycle existence is needed.

## 3. The integral Lefschetz model

The target is to determine the relationship between the literal order generated by exterior multiplication and ordinary contraction and the normalized integral matrix product proposed by the earlier notes. They are not identified by rationalizing alone. The factorial obstruction makes the literal integral Lefschetz isomorphism fail, while the coefficients j(n−i−j+1) distinguish ordinary contraction from unit-step lowering even over the rationals.

The normalized replacement has already been proved to be a product of integral matrix algebras. That result verifies the earlier run's construction; it does not complete the manuscript's unfinished definition. A future task should begin with the scan's exact definition of the dual bivector, the unfinished reference 6.(5|15), the integral inscription and the proposed extension to an arbitrary base ring. It should then state one precise candidate order or lattice and compare it with the actual generated order. A few hours could settle small ranks and identify the appropriate formulation. It would not establish a universal identification without a general argument and a fixed interpretation of the source.

## 4. Four-form orbit convention and the smallest dimension

The target is the exact meaning of u on scan 29 and the author's explicit “4 (or 6?)” threshold. Under the earlier run's normalization U=(Λ²φ♯)Q♭, the six-dimensional example and the unbounded family have already been checked, including the full four-form matrix, nondegeneracy, norm-one condition and cyclic-rank certificates. Repeating those computations is unnecessary.

The first step is an ink and tensor-convention audit: identify the embedding Λ²V→V⊗V, the meaning of the tensor square, the contraction convention and the relation of ξ to φ. The norm-zero counterexample in this run shows that nondegeneracy of φ does not automatically normalize ξ. Once the convention is fixed, dimension four is a small explicit problem. The potential result would be a sharp threshold for that algebraic model, not a realization as the cohomology algebra of a smooth projective variety. Positivity and geometric realization would be separate, substantially stronger directions.

## 5. Coates-letter concordance

The target is a reliable dictionary between draft A, B, C and D, their decorated variants, and the later standard-conjecture notation. The current reading calls inverse-Lefschetz algebraicity C, whereas the later conventional label is B. The letter's assertions about cycle-Lefschetz A in dimensions at most four do not say that every fourfold satisfies the full inverse-correspondence conjecture.

A first concrete step is a table of definitions rather than letters, with the exact page and whether the definition is actually supplied. Add separate columns for polarization dependence, cohomology dependence, product quantifiers, coefficient fields, and positivity assumptions. Kleiman's Proposition 2.9, Corollary 2.10, Theorems 2.13, 3.12, 3.14 and 3.16 give precise comparison points. Katz and Messing's Theorem 2 supplies the finite-field characteristic-polynomial result; Jannsen's numerical semisimplicity theorem has different quantifiers and a different quotient.

Chronology also requires care. The two letters currently bear 4 January 1967 and 6 January 1966. The second date is confirmed by the supplied second pass and is not amended here. Katz and Messing's original paper documents Deligne's hard Lefschetz result by the 1973 lecture/submission period, whereas the full *Weil II* treatment is from 1980. The short-run deliverable would be a checked concordance and chronology with these distinct evidence levels.

## 6. Bourbaki chapter assumptions

The target is the missing context around the editorial instructions on scans 52, 54 and 56. Two errors are already decisive under the literal detached statements: F₂³ is finite étale with principal maximal ideals but not monogenic, and A×A/(t) over A=k[t] has finite étale fibres but is not flat. These examples identify what any successful reconstruction must address.

The first step is to find the referenced proposition, chapter and “Tribu64” version, then determine the field-size, flatness, finite-presentation and complete-intersection assumptions in force. The confirmed word *homogènes* must remain on the textual side of the table until its intended context is recovered. The modern Stacks statements provide exact valid comparison theorems; they do not choose the missing ink or prove that the old draft contained their hypotheses. A bounded task can produce a glossary of differents and a corrected conditional formulation. It should not conflate local standard étaleness with global monogenicity.

## 7. Reused typescript provenance

The target is bibliographic identification, with no mathematical result inferred from the physical folder. The Matlis and sheaf-injective sequence now has exact mathematical counterparts in Hartshorne's *Residues and Duality*, II §7, including Proposition 7.6 and Proposition 7.17. The preliminary numbers 3.1–3.4 and detached page numbers −7/−8 still require a draft-stage match. The way-out-functor sheet should be treated separately until its wording and numbering are matched.

The vanishing and Amitsur fragment on scans 28 and 30 is a different problem. Its citations to Shatz, Tate and Rosenberg–Zelinsky are leads, and its missing topology and hypotheses prevent a unique theorem attribution. The first concrete step is exact-phrase comparison against the relevant preliminary seminar papers and bibliographies, followed by a bibliographic sheet listing matches and mismatches. Neither fragment should be assigned to Grothendieck merely because it is in his archive.

## 8. Hodge's surface projectors

The target is to read Hodge's 1938 article and identify exactly which correspondence projectors it constructs. The title, opening page and DOI have been identified; the original full text was not obtained in this run. Grothendieck's letter reports Tate as intermediary and adds a historical judgment about neglect. Those are two different claims.

The first step is an authorized accessible copy of the original article, followed by a theorem-level comparison with the current π_i reading and Kleiman's surface discussion. Reception history would require a separately defined citation corpus and contemporaneous evidence. A single letter and a modern bibliographic record cannot establish thirty years of neglect.

## 9. The abandoned smooth-hypersurface reduction

The source explicitly abandons a chain of smooth birational modifications and smooth specialization because of Serre's fundamental-group objection. A short task should formalize the chain and the dimensions/characteristics in which its fundamental-group constraints hold, using the exact SGA specialization and birational-invariance results.

The next sentence proposes working with singular hypersurfaces and reformulating the conjectures. It does not choose a cohomology theory or a class of allowable correspondences. The useful immediate deliverable is therefore a controlled account of the failed reduction and a list of missing definitions. Constructing a singular analogue using intersection cohomology, resolutions or a motivic category would be new research and should begin only as a separately specified task.
