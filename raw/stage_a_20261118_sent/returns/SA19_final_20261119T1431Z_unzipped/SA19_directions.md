## Task specifications

### RD-SA19-01 — The equivalence kernel

**Would establish:** which quotient the programme's actual family construction realizes, and which additional assertion would be required for a universal regular representative. **Known:** Saito's Picard/incidence definition, all-codimension Betti bound and representative theorem are exact; Kleiman explicitly credits the 1964 bounded-family observation. The integral `N(ξ)` is still unspecified. **First step:** for one smooth projective variety, place annihilation by Picard correspondences, annihilation by all regular homomorphisms and the algebraic Abel–Jacobi kernel in one table, then trace the same cycle family through each. Retain isogeny qualifications (Saito, 1979, Definition 3.1, Proposition 3.4, Theorem 3.6; Kleiman, 1971, pp. 446–447).

### RD-SA19-02 — The realization dictionary

**Would establish:** a specified map of sites or derived comparison, with exact continuity and acyclicity gates. **Known:** Suzuki's theorems apply to declared rational/ind-rational/pro-étale settings; the manuscript's infinite-Witt continuity assertion is withdrawn. **First step:** inspect scans 92–98 and list objects, arrows and coverings before comparing a single finite-flat coefficient class. Decide whether the proposed functor is a morphism of sites, a premorphism or a separate derived construction. Do not assume an equivalence from matching names (Suzuki2013, Theorem 2.1.5; Suzuki2018, §2.7).

### RD-SA19-03 — Finite-group and Hodge conventions

**Would establish:** the exact square linking the curve table, Cartier pairing, Frobenius/Verschiebung and the restricted Hodge extension. **Known:** Oda's Corollary 5.11 gives the filtered de Rham realization over a perfect field, with the June 1967 dissertation distinguished from publication. **First step:** write every domain, Frobenius twist, dual variety and variance convention, then compute one transposed F map for a pointed smooth proper curve. Ordinary and supersingular elliptic cases should diagnose conventions, without replacing the general proof (Oda, 1969, Proposition 3.13, Theorem 3.19, Corollary 5.11).

### RD-SA19-04 — Local Picard pairings

**Would establish:** equality of the discriminant and cohomological pairings, not merely isomorphism of their finite groups. **Known:** *Brauer III*, Remark 8.5 admits a missing check; Suzuki's Proposition 11.10 supplies an exact mixed-characteristic p-primary comparison. **First step:** compare base ring, residue field, coefficient prime, Picard object, trace, sign and resolution invariance in the two constructions. Only then assess whether the method transports to the earlier equal-characteristic formulation (Grothendieck, 1968b, §8.4–8.5; Suzuki, 2024, pp. 500–501).

### RD-SA19-05 — Integral descent and positivity

**Would establish:** the correct lattice or isogeny-category statement preceding a positivity question. **Known:** the full-kernel integral formulation fails in the audited elliptic example; the factor-two construction yields a Néron–Severi class, not ampleness. **First step:** classify the descent denominator for a symmetric integral matrix on a product of elliptic curves and compare it with the reduced connected radical and the complementary dual isogeny. Keep primitive-sign positivity as a subsequent issue (Saito, 1979, Proposition 4.4; Peters, 2026). The withdrawn Caibar–Clemens preprint supplies no settlement.

### RD-SA19-06 — The Fourier comparison map

**Would establish:** an edition-ready proof of the particular map constructed on cote 47, including determinant and shift. **Known:** the relative inversion theorem is established; the source's compatibility at the zero fibre remains explicitly deferred. **First step:** place the top-cohomology adjunction map and the Koszul self-intersection map in the same diagram, normalize degree one from the Poincaré class and check exterior powers. Use a perfect-cone argument only after this map comparison (Laumon, 1996, Lemma 1.2.5).

### RD-SA19-07 — Trace and localization shifts

**Would establish:** one compatible set of degree conventions for geometrized local duality, ordinary Tate duality after residue descent and the curve trace. **Known:** finite-flat theorems and localization compatibilities exist, but the source has a conflicting unshifted box. **First step:** work on a smooth proper curve minus one point, label the localization triangle under a fixed cohomological shift convention, and match each actual arrow on scans 135–140. Translate Artin–Milne's convention before combining it with Suzuki's (Artin & Milne, 1976, Corollary 4.9; Suzuki2018, §3).

### RD-SA19-08 — Low-degree Ext and the mixed cells

**Would establish:** the exact truncation and continuity statement needed by a particular displayed cohomological degree, or one currently unresolved abelian-target table comparison. **Known:** geometric Yoneda Ext, internal sheaf Ext and global Ext differ; higher internal Ext need not vanish. The unipotent geometric cells are already checked and should not be recomputed. **First step:** list the local-to-global spectral-sequence terms in total degrees one through four, or select exactly the αp/A or μp/A cell and derive its long exact sequence with every connecting map present. Use the actual finite-level hypotheses of Ribeiro–Rosengarten (2026), not a degree-free duality slogan.

### RD-SA19-09 — The path-algebra topology

**Would establish:** a bounded comparison between the manuscript's chain algebra and a named adelic operator algebra. **Known:** the unrestricted Hom–colimit step fails; an action on the residue complex is weaker than all-endomorphism identification. **First step:** in a regular complete two-dimensional local ring, fix saturated flags, restricted products, topology and the corrected graded differential, then compare the multiplication with Yekutieli's action and the relevant higher-adèle operator theorem. Avoid general infinite sums until the convergence condition is stated (Yekutieli, 2003; Braunling et al., 2016).

### RD-SA19-10 — Local Jacobians and reciprocity

**Would establish:** a precise representability or Ext-comparison statement for one dimension and one coefficient category. **Known:** formal-curve universal properties and proalgebraic reciprocity exist; neither automatically proves the higher-dimensional all-group sketch. **First step:** identify the manuscript's local class object with a specified unit/class-group object over a complete valuation field with algebraically closed residue field, then compare the finite cyclic character map. Keep the fundamental two-extension and section splitting as additional named maps (Serre, 1961; Contou-Carrère, 2013; Suzuki2013, §2.6).

### RD-SA19-11 — A pencil height calculation

**Would establish:** one complete sign and normalization test for the proposed height/intersection formula. **Known:** the divisor case uses vertical corrections on a fibred regular model; the higher-cycle number-field result has different hypotheses. **First step:** choose a surface pencil, blow up its base points, compute the fibre-component correction matrix and compare corrected negative intersection with geometric Jacobian height (Ulmer, 2014, §4.3 and errata). Any comparison with Künnemann (2001) begins only after this exact geometric model is fixed.

### RD-SA19-12 — Intrinsic projector companion

**Would establish:** a transparent proof matching the existing formal theorem while preserving false source displays as source displays. **Known:** two-sided block truncation repairs the audited induction; the endpoint and global-support mistakes are distinct. **First step:** tabulate every operator's source degree, target degree and restricted inverse identity, then compare the scanned formulas with Kleiman's Lemma 3.15. Do not convert this formal repair into a proof of geometric inverse-cycle existence (Kleiman, 1968).

### RD-SA19-13 — The integral Lefschetz order

**Would establish:** the relation between the literal exterior/contraction order and the earlier run's normalized integral matrix-algebra model. **Known:** rationalization removes the factorial obstruction but not the lowering normalization mismatch. **First step:** inspect the definition of the dual bivector, the integral inscription and reference 6.(5|15), then choose one precise candidate lattice/order and compare small ranks. A universal identification requires its own general argument.

### RD-SA19-14 — The four-form threshold

**Would establish:** the sharp smallest-dimension statement under the author's actual tensor convention. **Known:** the audited six-dimensional and growing-family constructions use a specified normalization; nondegeneracy does not imply the required norm-one condition. **First step:** identify the embedding of Λ²V into the tensor square and the contraction defining the displayed operator, then settle dimension four in that convention. Geometric realization and positivity are separate, stronger tasks.

### RD-SA19-15 — Geometric Tate embedding

**Would establish:** a meaningful strengthening beyond an abstract free-module embedding. **Known:** projectivity already splits the abstract surjection; published correspondence realizations preserve additional structure and impose prime/codimension restrictions. **First step:** search the neighboring letter for naturality, equivariance, saturation or correspondence language, then compare precisely that formulation with Achter et al. (2023), Proposition 8.2. Retain the quotient modulo torsion and excluded primes.

### RD-SA19-16 — Relative Fourier integrality

**Would establish:** a family theorem with index, determinant and Todd factors visible, and a defined integral target. **Known:** the field coefficient error is repaired; the weaker integral multiple survives. Existing integral or localized-coefficient theorems have extra hypotheses. **First step:** identify the filtration called “Gr,” then derive the family statement in one declared category and compare its coefficient ring with Moonen (2026), Beckmann–de Gaay Fortman (2023) and Hasan et al. (2025).

### RD-SA19-17 — The multiplicity question

**Would establish:** a defensible modern fate for the exact universally-open/full-support/S₁ formulation. **Known:** EGA explicitly leaves it unresolved in 1966; the audit's nonflat counterexample addresses a different inequality-to-flatness sentence. **First step:** follow citations to EGA IV₃ 12.2.3(vi), maintaining a literal hypothesis table and checking minimal versus associated primes on the scan. A sparse citation trail does not establish present openness.

### RD-SA19-18 — Supported Ext and completion

**Would establish:** the exact degree, dualizing object and derived completion needed by the unfinished diagram. **Known:** SGA2 gives regular-local duality and coherent thickening colimits, and the finite DVR model is fully checked. **First step:** reconstruct the diagram for a finite module over a regular complete local ring, indicating derived completion and any Mittag–Leffler hypotheses. Compare the coherent first argument with the counterexample to unrestricted Hom continuity (SGA2, Exposés V, VI and IX).

### RD-SA19-19 — Formal/algebraic gluing

**Would establish:** one precise exact category and functor for the proposed gluing along finite local-unipotent groups. **Known:** formal Cartier and Dieudonné theories exist under specified topology and flatness hypotheses; the unrestricted socle criterion is false. **First step:** fix covariance, the completed coefficient ring, local nilpotence and admissible extensions on one finite/local piece. Check compatibility with the finite overlap before proposing an equivalence (Gabriel, 1962; SGA3, VIIB; Oort & Oda, 1968).

### RD-SA19-20 — Coalgebras over rings

**Would establish:** an exact bounded generalization or counterexample to the manuscript's arbitrary-ring approximation. **Known:** the field theorem is established; cofree existence and projective-coring local finiteness are not the asserted finite-free union statement. **First step:** formulate separately finite subcoalgebras, finite projective subcoalgebras and finite-type group quotients, then test one non-semihereditary ring satisfying the source's actual module assumptions. Follow Barr (1974) and Kihara (2016) backwards before attempting a general proof.

### RD-SA19-21 — The last pairing

**Would establish:** independence of choices for a precisely normalized form of the final pairing. **Known:** Poincaré biextensions and tame-symbol reciprocity provide mechanisms; changing a divisor adds a nontrivial raw evaluation factor. **First step:** use a curve or elliptic example and list divisor, section, trivialization and norm choices, then write the cancellation diagram. Keep the strict-unit subgroup and finite-Galois hypothesis separate from arbitrary finite-cover Tate-cohomology hopes (Deligne, 1991; Tate, 1963).

### RD-SA19-22 — Retained model versus monodromy

**Would establish:** what invariant the source's “Serre algebra” actually retains and when it agrees with the limiting representation's Lie algebra. **Known:** finite-type extension results do not apply to arbitrary infinite extensions. **First step:** choose a geometric ℓ-adic representation with a specified finite model and an infinite base extension, compute both images/Lie algebras, and formulate the model-independence condition actually used. The subgroup-germ assertion should be restricted to finite-dimensional analytic closed subgroups (Serre, 1968, Chapter I).

### RD-SA19-23 — Coates-letter concordance

**Would establish:** a definition-based dictionary among draft A/B/C/D, their variants and published standard-conjecture notation, with a separate chronology column. **Known:** the two actual dates differ; hard Lefschetz circulation in 1973 and publication in 1980 are distinct; numerical semisimplicity is not the full homological-independence assertion. **First step:** compare definitions, product quantifiers, coefficient fields and positivity hypotheses before matching letter names. Two bounded subprojects are Hodge's exact surface-projector theorem/reception and the failed smooth-hypersurface reduction; a singular reformulation would be a new research task (Kleiman, 1968; Katz & Messing, 1974; Jannsen, 1992).

### RD-SA19-24 — Missing editorial hypotheses

**Would establish:** which ambient hypotheses restore the Bourbaki statements and what the cycle-equivalence annotation actually denotes. **Known:** the finite-field monogenic and nonflat fibre counterexamples are decisive for the detached unrestricted readings; numerical=torsion-algebraic is a divisor theorem. **First step:** retrieve the referenced chapter/version and inspect the confirmed word *homogènes*, flatness and complete-intersection context. Independently inspect τ and its qualifiers before using FGA's Matsusaka theorem. Similar missing-context problems are grouped here for editorial efficiency, not asserted to have one mathematical cause.

### RD-SA19-25 — Reused units and original-map provenance

**Would establish:** document order, draft identity and exact later fate of named maps. **Known:** there are strong theorem matches with Atiyah, EGA, Matlis and Hartshorne, but some authorship and draft stages remain unidentified. **First step:** require two independent anchors from page numbering, continuation text and proposition numbering before assigning a typescript. Treat Atiyah's intrinsic-join/diagonal comparison separately from later James-number existence results. The EGA IV₄ galley comparison remains a primary-text-access lead, not a completed publication match.

### RD-SA19-26 — The finite-Witt question

**Would establish:** a well-typed quotient and duality statement. **Known:** Frobenius, a Frobenius power and Artin–Schreier–Witt difference lead to different groups. **First step:** inspect the operator, fix whether the object is a finite group scheme, a perfected realization or field points, and calculate its Dieudonné module. A presumed self-duality before those decisions is not a definite theorem.

No theorem or review status is inherited merely from the earlier note's report.
