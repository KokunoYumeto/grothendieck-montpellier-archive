For real irrational k, the literal lattice generated by e₁, ie₁, e₂, e₁+ik e₂ equals

\[
(\mathbf Z+i\mathbf Z)e_1\;\oplus\;(\mathbf Z+ik\mathbf Z)e_2,
\]

by subtracting its first generator from its fourth. Since k is nonzero, both factors are lattices in complex lines. The quotient is a product of elliptic curves and is therefore projective. The determinant and change of basis are reproduced in `scripts/computations.py`, named check `root_torus_check`.

For the replacement lattice generated by e₁, ie₁, e₂, ke₁+ie₂, the four real generators have determinant1. Thus it defines a complex torus with the elliptic subtorus Ce₁/(Z+iZ)e₁. A complementary complex line would be a graph z₁=αz₂. Its lattice vectors have the form (u+kd,z), where u=a+ib∈Z[i] and z=c+id∈Z[i]. If this graph had a rank-two lattice, choose two such vectors with z,z′ real-linearly independent. The graph equations give

\[
\alpha z=u+kd,\qquad \alpha z'=u'+kd',\qquad
k(dz'-d'z)=u'z-uz'.
\]

The coefficient dz′−d′z=dc′−d′c is a nonzero integer, while the right side belongs to Q(i). This forces k∈Q(i)∩R=Q, a contradiction. Consequently the elliptic subtorus has no elliptic complementary subtorus. Poincaré complete reducibility would give such a complement if the torus were an abelian variety, so the replacement is nonprojective. The last implication uses the standard complete-reducibility theorem as background; the elementary lattice argument itself was independently proved here.

## The parent’s proposed ringed-space counterexample

This also uses a parent-supplied formulation, not an independently inspected cote55 text. Let X be RP² with the constant sheaf of rings C. The space is compact, hence quasi-compact, and its stalks are noetherian fields. On a sufficiently small connected open set, a finite presentation over the constant sheaf is a matrix of constant complex numbers; its kernel and cokernel are constant finite-dimensional vector spaces. Thus the coherent modules are precisely finite-dimensional complex local systems, and the ring sheaf is coherent.

Such local systems are representations of π₁(X)=Z/2. Over C, every representation splits into trivial and sign summands. Pull back to the universal cover S². Its first cohomology vanishes, and positive group cohomology of the finite group Z/2 with complex coefficients vanishes by averaging. The degree-one part of the covering spectral sequence therefore gives H¹(X,L)=0 for every finite local system L. Equivalently, one can compute the cellular complex with the two possible monodromies.

The sign local system is nonzero, but its global sections are the invariant vectors of the sign representation, hence zero. The global-section functor is therefore not faithful and cannot be an equivalence. This refutes the parent’s stated criterion if its only space hypotheses are quasi-compactness, coherence of the ring sheaf, noetherian stalks, and vanishing H¹ on coherent modules. It would not automatically refute a version that assumes a locally schematic, spectral, or topologically noetherian space; the parent must compare those hypotheses with the original lemma.

This is a manual independent proof. No computer computation or external-source verification is claimed for this supplementary topology argument.
