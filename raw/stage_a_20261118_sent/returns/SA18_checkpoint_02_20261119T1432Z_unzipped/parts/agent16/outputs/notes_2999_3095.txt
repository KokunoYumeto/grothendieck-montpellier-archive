3000: 
3001: **Kind:** unpursued implication.
3002: 
3003: **Location:** cote 16, scan 54, final paragraph. **Manuscript:** “il y a une équivalence de catégories”.
3004: 
3005: Finite étale covers are indeed invariant under nilpotent thickenings, and the modern statement allows arbitrary thickenings of schemes (The Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tag 0BQB, Lemma 58.8.3). The statement concerns the category of covers, and is compatible with the underlying scheme retaining its nilpotent structure.

3015: ### R19 — Own idea: why both arguments cannot simply be bounded below
3016: 
3017: **Kind:** own idea.
3018: 
3019: **Location:** cote 16, scan 41, alternative (b) in Corollary 2.2. **Manuscript:** “the hypothesis on M• is needed”.
3020: 
3021: Over a field \(k\), take complexes \(L=M\) with \(L^i=M^i=k\) for every \(i\ge0\), zero in negative degrees, and zero differentials. Each has finite-dimensional cohomology in every degree and is bounded below, but
3022: \[
3023:  H^0R\operatorname{Hom}_k(L,M)
3024:  =\prod_{i\ge0}k
3025: \]
3026: is not finite-dimensional. Moreover \(\operatorname{Ext}^i_k(k,M)=k\) for all \(i\ge0\), so the extra uniform vanishing condition in (b) fails exactly where required. This gives a small explicit obstruction to deleting that condition, independently of the continuation missing from the supplied page.
3027: 
3028: **Editorial confidence:** high. **Reason:** over a field the Hom complex computes derived Hom here, and its zero differential leaves the indicated infinite product unchanged.

3046: ### R22 — Own idea: test the finite-fibre criterion without hidden flatness
3047: 
3048: **Kind:** own idea.
3049: 
3050: **Location:** cote 16, scan 54, the proposed equivalence (a)–(c). **Manuscript:** “B est finie sur A et B/m.B est étale”.
3051: 
3052: If this is read without further ambient hypotheses, let \(A=k[t]\) and \(B=A\times A/(t)\), with the diagonal \(A\)-algebra structure; then \(B\) is finite and its fibre at every maximal ideal is either \(k(\mathfrak m)\) or \(k(\mathfrak m)\times k(\mathfrak m)\), hence is finite étale. But \(B\simeq A\oplus A/(t)\) as an \(A\)-module is not flat, since multiplication by \(t\) kills a nonzero element, so \(B/A\) is not étale. Accordingly, the proposed equivalence needs the hypotheses of the referenced text to supply flatness or a condition that forces it; the counterexample does not decide whether those hypotheses were already in force. Using a product with the \(A\)-factor ensures that every fibre is nonempty, so this objection is not an artefact of allowing the zero algebra as an empty cover.
3053: 
3054: **Editorial confidence:** high. **Reason:** the fibres and failure of flatness can both be computed directly; applicability to the historical statement remains conditional on its missing antecedent.
