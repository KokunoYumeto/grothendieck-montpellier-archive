2864:

2877: ### R5 — Own idea: the elementary blow-up step in resolution independence
2878: 
2879: **Kind:** own idea.
2880: 
2881: **Location:** cote 16, scan 19, the sentence after (8.16). **Manuscript:** “indépendante de la résolution X′ choisie”.
2882: 
2883: For a blow-up of a regular surface at a rational point on the exceptional configuration (as in the algebraically closed residue-field setting), the new intersection matrix has the form
2884: \[
2885:  Q'=\begin{pmatrix}Q-vv^T&v\\v^T&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
2886:  S=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\v^T&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
2887:  S^TQ'S=Q\oplus(-1),
2888: \]
2889: where the integral vector \(v\) records the multiplicities at the center. Since \(S\) is unimodular and the added rank-one summand has trivial discriminant group, this step preserves the discriminant pairing in R4. The calculation isolates a concrete part of the independence claim, while reduction of arbitrary resolutions to such steps requires the relevant surface hypotheses and a common-resolution argument.
2890: 
2891: **Editorial confidence:** high. **Reason:** the displayed congruence is an exact multiplication; its geometric applicability is conditional on the stated blow-up setting.

2893: ### R6 — Own idea: a finite-field obstruction to the polynomial argument
2894: 
2895: **Kind:** own idea.
2896: 
2897: **Location:** cote 16, scan 54, the proposed proof of the lemma on pages 10–11 of the reused text. **Manuscript:** “P≠0”; “On a donc x∈R tel que P(x)≠0”.
2898: 
2899: Over \(k=\mathbb F_2\), the finite étale algebra \(R=k^3\) has no single algebra generator: every element has only two possible coordinate values, and its polynomial functions cannot separate all three factors. Nevertheless, after extending scalars to an algebraic closure there are elements with three distinct coordinates, so the Vandermonde discriminant polynomial is not the zero polynomial. Thus passage to the algebraic closure proves polynomial nonvanishing without proving existence of a \(k\)-rational point of its nonvanishing locus. This is a conditional objection to the argument as preserved on this sheet, because an infinitude hypothesis may have been supplied in the antecedent text that is not present here.

2930: 
2931: **Kind:** own idea.
2932: 
2933: **Location:** cote 16, scan 37, proof of Lemma 3.3. **Manuscript:** “the same thing as to give a homomorphism from the stalk”.
2934: 
2935: Let \(i_x:(\{x\},\mathcal O_{X,x})\to(X,\mathcal O_X)\) be the canonical morphism of ringed spaces and let \(I\) be an injective \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\)-module; the adjunction gives
2936: \[
2937:  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(F,i_{x*}I)
2938:  =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}(F_x,I).
2939: \]
2940: Since taking a stalk is exact, the right side is exact contravariantly in \(F\), proving that \(i_{x*}I\) is injective among all \(\mathcal O_X\)-modules. For the particular injective hull \(I=E(k(\mathfrak p))\) used on the page, the distinct geometric step is the identification \(\widetilde I\simeq i_{x*}I\): the displayed filtration reduces it to modules annihilated by powers of \(\mathfrak p\), for which localization is zero when the inverted element belongs to \(\mathfrak p\) and is an isomorphism when it does not. This separates the general adjunction argument from the noetherian algebra needed to establish that identification.

2942: **Editorial confidence:** high. **Reason:** the argument uses exactness of stalks and an explicit localization test; it does not infer that an arbitrary quasi-coherent sheaf is injective.

3046: ### R22 — Own idea: test the finite-fibre criterion without hidden flatness
3047: 
3048: **Kind:** own idea.
3049: 
3050: **Location:** cote 16, scan 54, the proposed equivalence (a)–(c). **Manuscript:** “B est finie sur A et B/m.B est étale”.

3175: ### S13 — Positivity kills the radical once the rational algebra is finite
3176: 
3177: **Kind:** own idea. **Status:** editorial verification. **Location:** cote 16, scan 49, final request for semisimplicity from \(C(X)+I(X\times X)\). **Confidence:** high under the stated algebraic hypotheses, by the following elementary argument.
3178: 
3179: Let a finite-dimensional rational algebra \(R\) have a faithful finite-dimensional realization, an involution \(*\), a rational-valued trace, and \(\operatorname{Tr}(x^*x)>0\) for every nonzero \(x\in R\); its Jacobson radical is involution-stable and nilpotent, so \(x^*x\) has trace zero for \(x\) in the radical, forcing that radical to vanish. Finite dimension over \(\mathbb Q\) should not be inferred merely from inclusion in matrices over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\); however, a rational positive trace pairing bounds the size of any rationally independent family by the ambient matrix dimension, since its Gram determinant is nonzero. This supplies the elementary algebra after the geometric work has produced the appropriate adjoint and rational positive pairing. Kleiman (1968, §§3.11–3.14) establishes the conditional positivity, semisimplicity, and splitting route.

3181: ### S14 — An injective map needs an algebraic splitting
3182: 
3183: **Kind:** own idea. **Status:** editorial clarification. **Location:** cote 16, scan 49, paragraph beginning “The same result should hold in fact if \(u\) is a monomorphism”. **Confidence:** high for the conditional algebra argument; the geometric splitting hypotheses remain essential.
3184: 
3185: The previous isomorphism argument cannot simply be applied to an injection between spaces of different dimensions, because no inverse exists on the whole target. If the correspondence algebra of the direct sum of source and target, with the required Tate twist, is semisimple, its matrix-block structure gives a generalized inverse \(g\) with \(fgf=f\), and injectivity of the realized \(f\) then yields \(gf=1\) on the source. The corner containing \(g\) is the required algebraic left inverse; surjections are treated with the opposite equation. This pinpoints why information about the ambient correspondence algebra and its products is needed, whereas semisimplicity of an isolated endomorphism algebra would not by itself control all maps between two varieties.
