00474 #### MPL98.B01 — Fixed quotient and Hilbert polynomial boundedness
00475 
00476 
00477 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 027–028.  
00478 **Kind:** unproved claim / own illustrative counterexample.  
00479 **Brief source quotation:** “quotients d’un faisceau donné”; “les P_F … en nb finis”.  
00480 **Context:** Hua98, “Les quotients d’un faisceau fixe,” pp. 26–27 (scans 027–028); RS, Orientation, bounded-family question.  
00481 **Register:** RS–Ø; S19 primary-literature comparison.
00482 
00483 
00484 For a projective scheme over a noetherian base, quotients of a fixed coherent sheaf with Hilbert polynomial in a fixed finite set are bounded, as realized by the corresponding finite union of Quot schemes. This agrees with Hua’s stated target theorem and with Grothendieck (1961b, Theorem 2.1), whose 1962 erratum explicitly restores sufficiency to the printed statement; the full Quot construction is not re-proved here. A fixed Hilbert polynomial without a fixed-quotient bound is insufficient: \(\mathcal O(n)\oplus\mathcal O(-n)\) on \(\mathbf P^1\) has polynomial \(2m+2\) and unbounded \(h^0\). The counterexample distinguishes the role of the two conditions rather than disputing the source’s combined criterion (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tags 0DPB and 0DPC).
00485 
00486 
00487 **Confidence and reason:** High for the elementary constant-polynomial counterexample; the positive boundedness theorem is a checked literature result, not independently reconstructed in full.

00490 #### MPL98.B02 — Fixed quotient and cycle degree do not bound even pure sheaves
00491 
00492 
00493 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 027–028.  
00494 **Kind:** own idea / conditional counterexample.  
00495 **Brief source quotation:** “Chow raffiné”; “cycles associés … de degré borné”.  
00496 **Context:** Hua98, p. 26 (scan 027), pale-pencil variant in the fixed-quotient footnote; p. 27 (scan 028), induction.  
00497 **Register:** RS–Ø; refutes a possible bounded-cycle formulation, not the fixed-Hilbert-polynomial theorem.
00498 
00499 
00500 On \(\mathbf P^1_k\), the maps \(\mathcal O^2\to\mathcal O(n)\) given by \(X^n,Y^n\) are surjective for every \(n\ge1\). Each quotient is pure of dimension one with the same associated cycle \([\mathbf P^1]\), yet its Hilbert polynomial is \(m+n+1\), so this family is unbounded. This defeats the literal fixed-quotient/bounded-cycle-degree formulation even with purity, strengthening the simpler lower-dimensional-torsion example recorded in BR01. It contradicts that mathematical interpretation of the tentative variant described by Hua, while the faint wording and any intended extra hypothesis remain separate reading questions.
00501 
00502 
00503 **Confidence and reason:** High for the explicit pure-sheaf counterexample; conditional about the scope intended by the difficult pencil text.

00506 #### MPL98.B03 — Induction requires bounded kernels
00507 
00508 
00509 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 028.  
00510 **Kind:** unproved claim / comparison with context.  
00511 **Brief source quotation:** “les noyaux de tous les F′_i sont limités”.  
00512 **Context:** Hua98, pp. 26–27 (scans 027–028), footnote and added kernel argument.  
00513 **Register:** RS–Ø.
00514 
00515 
00516 The induction needs a uniform quotient sheaf for the lower-dimensional \(F''_i\), obtained from kernels of maps from the fixed sheaf to the already bounded \(F'_i\). Parameterizing those maps and stratifying so that kernels commute with fibres supplies the missing justification in the projective setting. The frozen scan 028 already names the kernels, whereas Hua’s footnote treats the passage through them as its own reconstruction; its proof detail can be useful without replacing that source reading. Neither the mere inclusion \(F''_i\subset F_i\) nor boundedness of the \(F_i\) alone makes arbitrary subsheaves bounded.

00519 #### MPL98.B04 — Properness for variable morphisms and extensions
00520 
00521 
00522 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 028,034.  
00523 **Kind:** own idea / boundary counterexample.  
00524 **Brief source quotation:** “noyaux … conoyaux des hom de F en G”; “extensions de F par G”.  
00525 **Context:** Hua98, operation examples p. 33 (scan 034), compared with the projective argument pp. 26–27 (scans 027–028).  
00526 **Register:** RS–Ø; properness qualification checked.
00527 
00528 
00529 On \(\mathbf A^1_k\), the singleton input families \(\{\mathcal O\}\) are bounded, but the maps \(\mathcal O\xrightarrow{x^n}\mathcal O\) have cokernels \(k[x]/(x^n)\) of unbounded length. Thus boundedness of the internal Hom sheaf does not by itself bound the family of all global maps or their cokernels. The projective setting of the earlier induction provides the needed properness, and Grothendieck’s permanence statement for kernels, images, cokernels, and extensions explicitly assumes it (1961b, Proposition 1.2). This adds a boundary to an unrestricted reading of Hua’s operation list; BR11 gives the finite-presentation argument for unboundedness on the affine line.
00530 
00531 
00532 **Confidence and reason:** High: explicit cokernels and a uniform presentation-degree bound rule out a finite type parameter family.

00535 #### MPL98.B05 — Field amalgamation over the middle field
00536 
00537 
00538 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 029.  
00539 **Kind:** unproved claim / targeted proof check.  
00540 **Brief source quotation:** “On vérifie facilement que c’est là une relation d’équivalence”.  
00541 **Context:** Hua98, “Faisceaux sur des extensions du corps de base,” p. 28 (scan 029), transitivity footnote.  
00542 **Register:** RS–Ø.
00543 
00544 
00545 Transitivity must amalgamate the two witnessing overfields over the specified middle field, with its embeddings, rather than merely over the original ground field. If the witnesses are \(L,L'\) over \(K_2\), the nonzero tensor product \(L\otimes_{K_2}L'\) has a residue field into which both fields embed, and the two sheaf isomorphisms then compose. This verifies the source’s ease assertion and independently rechecks the repair credited to Hua’s project.

00548 #### MPL98.B06 — Finite-type descent of sheaves and isomorphisms
00549 
00550 
00551 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 029–030.  
00552 **Kind:** unproved claim / targeted proof check.  
00553 **Brief source quotation:** “Si X est de type fini sur k”; “K′ … de type fini”.  
00554 **Context:** Hua98, pp. 28–29 (scans 029–030), finite-presentation descent.  
00555 **Register:** RS–Ø; no underlying result file is assumed.
00556 
00557 
00558 Finite presentation allows a coherent sheaf on a finite type \(X_K\) to descend to a finitely generated coefficient field, and finitely many more coefficients descend an isomorphism, its inverse, and the two identities. The inserted finite type hypothesis on \(X\) matters: it gives the quasi-compact, quasi-separated setting in which this finite-data descent is valid. This agrees with Hua’s reconstruction and supplies the precise role of the condition already present in the source (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tag 01ZR).

00561 #### MPL98.B07 — Arbitrary compositum counterexample
00562 
00563 
00564 **File / scan:** 98.pdf, 030,033.  
00565 **Kind:** own idea / answer to a written question.  
00566 **Brief source quotation:** “peut-on prendre … n’importe quelle extension composée|commune ?”.  
00567 **Context:** Hua98, p. 29 (scan 030), reads “universelle”; its all-composita discussion p. 32 (scan 033) is the related later passage.  
00568 **Register:** RS–Ø; conditional answer to the frozen question.
00569 
00570 
00571 Let \(X=\mathbf A^1_k\), take two abstract copies \(K_i=k(t)\), and put \(F_i=K_i[x]/(x-t)\). Aligned embeddings into \(k(t)\) identify the sheaves, while independent embeddings into \(k(t_1,t_2)\) give distinct supports \(x=t_1\) and \(x=t_2\), which remain distinct after every further field extension. Thus an arbitrary prescribed compositum cannot replace a suitable witnessing compositum. This answers the composed/common-extension reading negatively and explains the source’s later instruction to use all relative positions; Hua’s different word remains reading question R04.
00572 
00573 
00574 **Confidence and reason:** High for the annihilator/support argument; conditional on the doubtful word in the question.
