## 1. The crystalline programme and its supplements

The strongest publication identification is the sequence from Codex scan38 through Pro scan48. It closely matches ChapterV of *Groupes de Barsotti-Tate et cristaux de Dieudonné*, printedpp107–120. The programme introduces the contravariant crystal, Problems1–3, the Hodge filtration, the qualified BT deformation theorem, the arbitrary-base abelian deformation theorem, universal vector extensions and the degree-one BT comparison. The published text preserves the words of uncertainty and the heuristic abelian-to-BT construction. The course is from1970 and the publication from1974; these facts do not date the archive leaves. [GrothMontreal1974, ChapterV; GrothPublishedWorks, CSG91.]

The published comparison also helps discriminate readings. The typed FV identity subscripts on Codex scan38 are reversed relative to its arrows, while Montréalp108 has correctly typed subscripts. The opposite-category exponent missing in the later Codex display is present in that publication. On Pro scan47, the doubtful extra linear-dual exponent in the covariant comparison is absent in Montréalp118. These are reasons to inspect the scans, not permission to edit them.

Several distinct theories must remain distinct. A perfect-base Dieudonné equivalence is not full faithfulness of ordinary crystals over every singular base. Lau’s exact perfect-ring categories distinguish finite torsion modules from truncated-BT modules and include conditions beyond the bare equation (FV=VF=p). De Jong’s primary survey expressly records a counterexample to unrestricted ordinary-crystal full faithfulness; the audit uses that published negative result without claiming to reproduce its pair of BT groups. Excellent lci and quasi-syntomic results provide precise positive replacements with their own hypotheses and structures. [Lau2013, Definition6.1, Theorem6.4, Corollary6.5; deJongICM1998, §2(x); deJongMessing1999; AnschutzLeBras2023, Theorem1.4.4.]

The deformation theorem’s qualifications are substantive. The source inserts nilpotent divided powers, an infinitesimal group or an infinitesimal dual. The ICM text explains why an unqualified statement fails. The abelian deformation theorem in the Montréal programme, by contrast, has an arbitrary base and locally nilpotent divided powers; it should not be restricted silently to the p-nilpotent case merely because Serre–Tate is the most familiar route. The characteristic-zero universal-extension rigidity assertion is now separately registered as C-SA04-49, so that its strong nil-thickening claim is not lost inside the general construction. [GrothICM1970, §4; GrothMontreal1974, V§4 pp116–118; FonsecaMatthes2022, Theorem2.1 and §§3.2–3.3.]

The isogeny warning has a direct mathematical content. On a disjoint union of characteristic-p points, componentwise quasi-endomorphisms with denominators (p^n) do not belong to the global rationalization of the product of integral endomorphism rings. Thus global Hom localization fails morphism descent. The published nilpotent-invariance theorem requires a single p-power killing the thickening ideal. This matters when the manuscript cancels quasi-compactness and has an ambiguous p/J glyph. [C-SA04-18,19; proof_checks.md §1; GrothICM1970, §5 p434.]

The period questions likewise separate rational and integral data, valuation-field and relative-base statements, and Zariski and analytic topologies. The two-dimensional rank example in the earlier notes is valid: total Hodge and Newton numbers agree, but a stable line violates the weak-admissibility inequality. Colmez–Fontaine settles the exact rational field criterion; Hartl’s analytic spaces do not by themselves prove the literal Zariski-open statement. The relative prismatic generic-fibre comparison is a precise later construction in a specified category, not a theorem about every filtered crystal on every noetherian p-adic base. [C-SA04-21–25; ColmezFontaine2000, §4.1; Hartl2008; AnschutzLeBras2023, Proposition5.4.2.]

The Tate supplement has a clear outcome. Tate’s published theorem assumes characteristic-zero function field. De Jong proves the characteristic-p DVR result and records the reduction to normal noetherian integral bases. The1999 erratum corrects an auxiliary kernel/cokernel assertion but does not retract Theorem1.1 or Corollary1.2. The ordinary-crystal question, the nondegenerate-F theorem and the BT theorem remain separate claims. [C-SA04-27–33; Tate1967, Theorem4; deJong1998, Theorem1.1 and Corollary1.2; deJongErratum1999.]

The original SGA 7 I scan gives a particularly precise publication connection. Exposé IX5.8–5.10 retains the characteristic-zero assumption when the prime equals the residue characteristic; Remark5.11 expressly makes removal of that restriction depend on unrestricted Tate full faithfulness. The later de Jong theorem supplies that named input. Corollary5.9 and Remark5.9.1 also give the exact Néronian-target extension statement needed by the cote100 audit, with Raynaud attribution and an explicitly unpublished Néron-model input. The proof and representability qualification were read in the original printed pages382–386; `SGA7I_primary_memo.md` records their scope. This does not certify the unpublished input independently or collate the original introduction. [SGA7I, IX5.8–5.11; deJong1998; deJongErratum1999.]

## 3. Euler characteristic in the source’s own terms

The Euler dossier is a separate sequence, interleaved with administrative research-policy sheets. The later theme list juxtaposes orientation, coverings, complex functions and arithmetic; it supplies no construction identifying these Euler axioms with Gauss–Manin or Cartier theory. The budget arithmetic is checked as metadata and supplies no date for the mathematical leaves.

The first significant correction is the author’s replacement of sheaf cohomology by singular cohomology. This cannot be ignored when applying closed/open exactness or closed-cover Mayer–Vietoris on unrestricted spaces. The algebraic Euler cancellations are correct once the appropriate exact sequences exist. The later compact conical or finite-cell setting gives a plausible comparison framework, but that framework must be stated. [C-SA04-50–56; Verdier1973, §1; Hatcher2002, Theorems2.10,2.20,2.44; Morin1962, §2.]

The provisional fibration argument has a real gap: finite constant-coefficient cohomology does not guarantee the local-system Euler lemma. The earlier notes’ locally finite telescope of projective spaces provides a full counterexample. Its rational Euler number is1, its contractible double cover also has Euler number1, and the two-point fibre has Euler number2. The sign local system has zero rational cohomology. The example verifies the broad failure, while the author’s later relative-polyhedral qualification avoids it. It does not refute the final question within the conical-at-infinity class. [C-SA04-57–60; proof_checks.md §3.]

The intended compact recursive-cone interpretation offers a modern completion. Finite-dimensional, locally contractible compact metric spaces are ANRs; compact ANRs have finite polyhedral homotopy type. A truncated cone gives a compact core and the explicit radial homotopy retracts the punctured compactification onto it. Exact-sequence subtraction then yields the end-defect formula and, conditionally on the precise class, axiomatic uniqueness. Every added compactness, metrizability and recursive-dimension assumption is identified as a reconstruction. [C-SA04-62,65–70; GuilbaultMoran2019, Proposition2.9; West1977, Corollary5.3.]

Homotopy finiteness is weaker than triangulation. The Pro witness deletes a compact dimension-three clause that Codex fails to mark as deleted. Moise’s theorem for manifolds does not settle all singular recursive-link assertions. In dimension four, the compact E8 topological manifold gives a negative answer even inside the locally Euclidean subclass. This answers a doubt on the page, not an asserted theorem. [C-SA04-63,64; Manolescu2015, §2.2.]

## 4. The Postnikov scratch leaf

The zero after (k_1\in H^3(\pi_1,\pi_2)) is grammatically ambiguous. The ambient group can be nonzero even for an infinite loop space with zero first class. A single loop space can have a nonzero first class, as verified by the reduced-quadratic-module example and its complete finite cochain calculation. Realization of that algebraic model is an external theorem, carefully separated from the calculation. Two compatible loopings force the first class to vanish, but a splitting cannot in general be chosen naturally in the original delooped space. [C-SA04-80–84; Arlettaz2000, §7; BauesConduche1995, Theorem1.9 and §§4–5.]

The first-double-loop paper’s publisher record dates receipt to9June1987, revision to15April1988 and publication toJanuary1990. These are evidence about circulation of that result, not about the archive leaf. The proof was read in Arlettaz’s primary2000 survey; the original1990 full article was not read.

## 5. Analytic and integral projective cocycles

Deligne’s I§5 is an exact classical counterpart to the analytic construction: matching two-jets, projective coordinates, the quadratic-differential torsor, the cocycle and the second-order ODE. The source normalization is minus twice the standard Schwarzian; Deligne’s is one sixth. These statements are local or sheaf-theoretic and do not imply a preferred global solution. [C-SA04-120; Deligne1970, I§5; Dumas2009, §§3.1–3.2.]

The integral Hasse formula is independently verified over the universal localized polynomial ring. Its composition identity therefore survives every base change. The square test is (1/(4t^2)) wherever (2t) is invertible; the retained (1/(2t)) is mathematically inconsistent. The cube test vanishes in characteristic two, so it cannot be used there as a nonvanishing certificate. Valid nonzero tests in characteristics two and three use (t+t^2) and (t+t^3). [C-SA04-122; saved symbolic computation.]

None of this proves classification of every allowable rational analytic operator or every base-natural integral Hasse operator. The opened smooth-group relative-cocycle theorem is a close analogue with a different domain and regularity condition. The analytic redundant-axiom claim and integral universal uniqueness retain a precise reference/proof gap. The companion audit treats coherent-target and fixed-characteristic variants separately. [C-SA04-121,123; BouarroudjOvsienko1998, §1.2; Ovsienko1993.]
