## 1. Integral universal Schwarzian problem

**What it would establish.** Define the class of base-natural finite-order operators precisely and determine whether its projectively invariant additive cocycles form a free rank-one module over the integers generated by the Hasse expression (Q=-D^{(3)}f/D^{(1)}f+(D^{(2)}f/D^{(1)}f)^2). Any claimed universal recipient must specify local generation or surjectivity, and must distinguish arbitrary modules from coherent targets. The companion audit’s C-SA04-201–203 addresses nearby formulations with different quantifiers.

**What is known.** C-SA04-122 verifies an integral composition identity before reduction modulo primes, including nonzero characteristic-two and characteristic-three examples. Deligne’s characteristic-zero normalization and the smooth relative group-cohomology theorem provide comparisons, not the all-base universal classification. [Deligne1970, I§5; BouarroudjOvsienko1998, §1.2.]

**First concrete step.** Write a finite-jet group-scheme problem over (mathbb Z), with its target and base-change functor explicit; calculate the module of cocycles through order three and examine torsion at two and three. Stop before extrapolating from those calculations to all orders. This would make the handwritten “up to sign” statement a theorem with definite quantifiers or expose a precise obstruction.

## 2. Euler uniqueness on the conical category

**What it would establish.** Turn the conditional completion in this audit into a companion theorem for one exact definition of (C_n) and (E_infty). The proof should separately establish compactness, metrizability, finite dimension, local contractibility, closure under truncated-cone boundary charts, and the chosen support-cohomology comparison.

**What is known.** The compact ANR route gives finite polyhedral homotopy models, and the radial core homotopy plus closed/open subtraction determines the two Euler functions. It gives homotopy finiteness without requiring triangulation of the original space. [GuilbaultMoran2019, Proposition2.9; West1977, Corollary5.3; Morin1962, §2 Lemma1.]

**First concrete step.** Collate the (C/E) subscripts and the word “discrete” on scan62, then write the recursive definition without silently inserting a missing compactness clause. Check that every axiom is stated on objects and morphisms belonging to that category. A short proof is feasible after those definitions are fixed; an unrestricted theorem for arbitrary locally compact spaces is not the task.

## 3. Analytic classification and local-surjectivity redundancy

**What it would establish.** Prove or delimit the claim that every admissible rational differential cocycle is a scalar multiple of the displayed Schwarzian and that rigidity implies local surjectivity. The local complex analytic coordinate groupoid is the relevant domain, rather than an unstated substitution of the real diffeomorphism group of a circle.

**What is known.** The actual Schwarzian satisfies the conditions, gives the quadratic-differential torsor, and is locally solved by a second-order ODE. The known relative smooth cocycle classification has different hypotheses. [Deligne1970, I§5.8–5.12; Dumas2009, §§3.1–3.2; BouarroudjOvsienko1998, §1.2.]

**First concrete step.** Prove the pole-support localization lemma and a realization lemma for arbitrary finite jets, tracking analytic coefficient functions. Then formulate the resulting jet-group relative cohomology problem. A scalar shown constant after classification does not itself prove classification.

## 4. Generic extension without Frobenius

**What it would establish.** Decide the exact local plain-crystal extension question that remains after the established nondegenerate-F theorem is removed from the comparison. The target should be a DVR example with a specified crystalline site, integral or isogeny coefficients, and quasi-nilpotent connection category.

**What is known.** De Jong’s Theorem1.1 treats nondegenerate F-crystals over a characteristic-p DVR admitting a p-basis; his Corollary1.2 and the stated global reduction settle the BT question. Neither establishes the plain-crystal question merely by forgetting (F). [deJong1998; deJongErratum1999; BerthelotMessing1979, §1.]

**First concrete step.** Fix (k[[t]]), inspect rank-one and rank-two horizontal morphisms over the puncture, and identify exactly where the Frobenius equation supplies the bound needed to remove poles. A counterexample must satisfy the full crystalline convergence conditions; an arbitrary differential module is insufficient.

## 5. Zariski versus analytic period loci

**What it would establish.** Identify the literal Zariski-open claim in the fixed-isocrystal question and compare it with weakly admissible and admissible analytic loci, keeping the coefficient field and point type fixed.

**What is known.** Rank counts do not characterize admissibility. The field theorem and the analytic period-domain theorem have distinct quantifiers; Hartl’s example distinguishes general analytic points even when classical point descriptions coincide. [ColmezFontaine2000, §4.1 Theorem4.3; Hartl2008, Theorems1.2,3.3,3.5 and Example3.6.]

**First concrete step.** Work out one fixed two-step isocrystal and its Grassmannian conditions, then record which locus each topology sees. This is an exact formulation task, not a proposal to prove a general relative p-adic comparison theorem in one run.

## 6. The crystalline programme as a source dossier

**What it would establish.** A page-by-page concordance between c93 scans38–48 and Montréal ChapterV pp107–120, with a separate comparison to ICM1970 §§3–5 and the later Messing/Mazur–Messing construction history. The printed programme preserves several questions and deferred arguments; its publication is not evidence that those questions were proved there.

**What is known.** The exact Montréal parallel is now collated, including the qualified BT deformation theorem, the arbitrary-base abelian theorem, universal-extension rigidity in characteristic zero, and the no-extra-dual version of the covariant comparison. The period and Tate supplements are not thereby identified as pages of the same publication. [GrothMontreal1974; GrothICM1970; deJong1995, introduction.]

**First concrete step.** Produce a diplomatic concordance with the scans, then locate the precise Pisa VII10.8.2 cross-reference and the earlier Tate letter. This would resolve real source questions without inventing manuscript dates.

## 7. Singular links in low dimensions

**What it would establish.** Determine which global triangulation assertions follow from the recursive compact-link definition in dimensions two and three, before coning them to obtain higher-dimensional local charts.

**What is known.** Finite graphs give the first local examples. Moise’s theorem treats three-manifolds; it does not automatically treat all singular recursive links. Dimension-four global triangulation fails even for compact topological manifolds. [Manolescu2015, §2.2.]

**First concrete step.** Resolve the deleted clause on scan62 and isolate the exact class of two-dimensional links. Search and test that theorem, rather than the broader slogan “locally conical implies triangulable.”

## 8. The Postnikov scratch leaf

**What it would establish.** An annotated diagram distinguishing vanishing of the ambient (H^3), vanishing of a particular class for one loop, vanishing after two loopings, and natural versus objectwise splitting.

**What is known.** The finite cochain checks and reduced-quadratic example are complete at the algebraic level; their realization is supplied by Baues–Conduché. Arlettaz proves the double-loop statement; natural splitting fails. [Arlettaz2000, §7; BauesConduche1995, Theorem1.9 and §§4–5.]

**First concrete step.** Inspect the punctuation after the (H^3) expression, then redraw the diagram with each extra delooping hypothesis labelled. This is a high-feasibility explanatory task, not a newly open Postnikov theorem.

## 9. Anonymous analysis sheets

**What it would establish.** Identify the French exercise source and compare the printed weights, support symbols and perturbation target with an authoritative edition. It could determine which defects belong to transcription and which already appear in the reused typescript.

**What is known.** The Cohen factorization result is established and the pseudo-resolvent statements trace back through exact modern citations to Hille–Phillips §5.8. The weighted Liouville and boundary exercises remain unattributed. Their presence does not authenticate Grothendieck’s authorship. [deJeuJiang2017; KurbatovKurbatovaOreshina2016, Theorem16, Corollary17, Propositions18,23.]

**First concrete step.** Collate the exact-wording lead now found in Bourbaki’s 2019 chapter I §2 exercises28–30 with an authoritative copy, then compare the unchanged 2007 reprint of the 1967 edition. Publisher metadata and the official contents were opened; the exercise pages themselves remain an unverified textual lead from an indexed reproduction. This could resolve the source attribution and recover a published counterpart to the truncated ending without inserting it into the manuscript. No manuscript chronology should be derived from the theorem’s publication date alone. [Bourbaki1967; Bourbaki2019.]
