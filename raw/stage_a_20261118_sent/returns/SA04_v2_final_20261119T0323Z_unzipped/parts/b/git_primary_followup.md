## Haboush and geometric reductivity

William J. Haboush, *Reductive groups are geometrically reductive*, Annals of Mathematics (2) **102** (1975), no. 1, 67–83, DOI [10.2307/1970974](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970974). The [publisher record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1975/102-1/p05) verifies title, author, volume and pages; July 1975 is the issue date. The attempted original JSTOR PDF redirected to a contentless landing page. Consequently the original theorem number and proof were **not verified in the 1975 paper**. No earlier circulation date was established.

A directly opened primary alternative proof is Pramathanath Sastry and C. S. Seshadri, *Geometric reductivity — A quotient space approach*, Journal of the Ramanujan Mathematical Society **26** (2011), no. 4, 415–477, [author-hosted PDF](https://www.cmi.ac.in/~pramath/mypapers/4-seshadri.pdf). Introduction, printed p. 415, states: for a reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field, every finite-dimensional rational representation and nonzero invariant vector admit a positive-degree homogeneous invariant polynomial nonzero on that vector. Printed pp. 415–416 explain the good-quotient and finite-generation consequences, crediting Nagata for finite generation. They distinguish their alternative quotient-space proof from Haboush's proof and explicitly date the latter to 1975. Received 13 December 2010 is printed on p. 415; this is not a verified first-circulation date.

Mumford's [author-hosted 1976 scan](https://www.dam.brown.edu/people/mumford/alg_geom/papers/1976a--Hilbert14thHarvardScan.pdf), *Hilbert's Fourteenth Problem — The Finite Generation of Subrings Such as Rings of Invariants*, was opened as images: PDF pp. 1–2, printed pp. 132–133. The introduction assumes an algebraically closed field. Printed p. 133 states reductive iff geometrically reductive, attributes the reductive-to-geometric direction to Haboush, and displays a commutative diagram of finite-generation and reductivity implications. This is a checked corroborating text, not a substitute claim that Haboush's original proof was inspected. The scan's precise publication container was not independently established.

### Exact p-power degree required by manuscript scan 36

The source asks for an invariant complement to V₀·Sym^(p^ν−1)V in Sym^(p^ν)V when V₀ has codimension one. The arbitrary positive degree in the opened Sastry–Seshadri statement suffices to deduce this exact p-power degree; no stronger unchecked version of Haboush is needed.

For a semisimple group, the one-dimensional quotient V/V₀ is trivial. Let v be the corresponding nonzero invariant vector in V*. Geometric reductivity supplies an invariant homogeneous polynomial F on V* with degree d>0 and F(v)≠0. Write d=p^r m with p not dividing m, and put q=p^r. In F(x+t v), let H(x) be the coefficient of t^(d−q). Since v and F are invariant, H is invariant and homogeneous of degree q. Evaluation at v gives H(v)=binom(d,q)F(v)=mF(v)≠0 in characteristic p; the binomial equality follows by expanding (1+t)^d=(1+t^q)^m. Consequently the invariant line spanned by H maps isomorphically to Sym^q(V/V₀) and is a complement to V₀·Sym^(q−1)V. If a strictly positive exponent ν is desired when r=0, replace H by H^p. This is an independent proof written in this run. The saved supplement program checks 156 finite coefficient examples only as a check on the formula; the general argument is the one just given.

## Kempf: optimality, rationality, and dates

George R. Kempf, *Instability in invariant theory*, Annals of Mathematics (2) **108** (1978), no. 2, 299–316, DOI [10.2307/1971168](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971168). The [publisher record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1978/108-2/p04) was opened.

The directly checked theorem text is the [2018 transcription of the original preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02890), not the expanded journal version. Theorem 6, pp. 7–8, gives a nonempty optimal indivisible cocharacter class, its common parabolic, and conjugacy under that parabolic for a nonzero unstable vector. Section 4, pp. 8–10, assumes a **perfect field**, a representation defined over it, a nonzero rational unstable vector, and a Galois-invariant length. Theorem 7, p. 9, proves Galois stability, rationality of the parabolic, and existence of a rational optimal cocharacter.

Ian Morrison's transcriber note, p. 1, records receiving the first preprint from Mumford in **fall 1976**; the journal publication is September 1978. The arXiv date, 8 July 2018, dates the transcription. Introduction p. 2 explicitly distinguishes this result from the general Tits Centre Conjecture. Do not identify the two.

## Imperfect fields: checked limitation and later theorem

Michael Bate, Benjamin Martin, Gerhard Röhrle and Rudolf Tange, *Closed orbits and uniform S-instability in geometric invariant theory*, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society **365** (2013), no. 7, 3643–3673, [author-hosted publisher offprint](https://rtange.github.io/research/git.pdf). The cover states electronic publication 27 December 2012; [arXiv history](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.4853) verifies first submission 30 April 2009.

Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.9, p. 3658, give rationality and compatibility under separable algebraic extension with the stated rationality and norm hypotheses. Remarks 4.10(ii)–(iii), p. 3659, distinguish geometric instability from rational instability over imperfect fields and identify the perfect-field result with **Kempf, published Theorem 4.2**. This published locator was checked through their explicit cross-reference, not by opening Kempf's journal theorem.

Remark 5.10, pp. 3662–3663, supplies a counterexample: separably closed nonperfect k of characteristic two, GL2 acting on itself by conjugation, and a in k^(1/2) outside k. The matrix [[0,1],[a²,0]] has a nonclosed geometric orbit whose boundary is aI; its orbit on k-points is Zariski closed and cocharacter closed. Thus the perfect-field hypothesis cannot simply be dropped. Theorem 5.31, p. 3670, is a fixed-subcomplex special case of the Centre Conjecture, not a general convex-set theorem.

## Centre Conjecture: exact comparison and independent counterexample

Carlos Ramos-Cuevas, *The Center Conjecture for thick spherical buildings*, Geometriae Dedicata **166** (2013), no. 1, 349–407, DOI [10.1007/s10711-012-9799-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10711-012-9799-9). [Opened PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.2761); [metadata/history](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.2761). First arXiv submission: 15 September 2009; opened revision: 6 May 2013, internally dated 17 October 2012.

Conjecture 1.1, p. 1, concerns a **convex subcomplex** K of a spherical building: K is a subbuilding, or the subgroup of ambient building automorphisms preserving K has a fixed point. Theorem 1.2 and Corollaries 1.3–1.4, p. 2, establish it for buildings without H4 factors and, in particular, all thick spherical buildings. Corollary 1.5, p. 3, gives an intrinsic polyhedral-automorphism version. These statements do not establish an unrestricted assertion for arbitrary convex subsets and arbitrary isometries.

### Literal source statement is false under the standard spherical-building meaning

Pro source: SA04_texts/pro/MPL_8_079-081+9+4+100_transcription.tex, scan 34, line **2144**; parallel Codex K100-03, line **162**. The source claims a fixed point for any finite isometry group of a convex set in the flag complex. No subbuilding alternative or size restriction appears in that sentence.

Take the rank-one spherical building of SL2(C). Its points are the lines in C², and distinct points are at distance π. The whole building is a convex subcomplex: the requirement to contain geodesics between points at distance less than π is automatic. Consider

A = diag(i, −i),  B = [[0,1],[−1,0]].

Both have determinant one. They satisfy A² = B² = −I and BA = −AB and generate Q8. Their induced projective action is finite and isometric. A fixes precisely the two coordinate lines, and B exchanges these lines. There is therefore no common fixed point. This is a complete elementary counterexample to the literal unrestricted sentence, with the convention on spherical convexity stated explicitly.

The modern theorem is consistent with the example because the convex set is itself a subbuilding. A historical intended restriction to a smaller instability region may repair the application, but it cannot silently be inserted into the transcription.
