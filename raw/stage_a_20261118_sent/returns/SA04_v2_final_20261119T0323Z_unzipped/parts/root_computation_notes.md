# Exact computations and proof checks: root stream

The accompanying program is `programs/root_computations.py`; its exact output is `outputs/root_computations.json`. These checks were made on 19 November 2026. A first run reached serialization after all assertions passed but failed because SymPy integers were not JSON serializable; the matrix entries were converted to Python integers and the complete run then succeeded. That software failure did not change any mathematical result. SymPy polynomial expansion is an exact algebraic certificate; a finite list of finite-field examples is not a proof of a universal identity.

## Weierstrass formulas, cote 93 scans 4–25

The program works over the rational function field in the independent coefficients and the coordinate-change parameters, with `u` invertible. Substitution of

\[
x=u^2x'+r,\qquad y=u^3y'+su^2x'+t
\]

in `F=y²+a₁xy+a₃y−x³−a₂x²−a₄x−a₆` gives exactly `u⁶F'`. All five transformed coefficients, the transformations of b₂,b₄,b₆,b₈,d₆,d₈,c₄,c₆,Δ, and the relations among them have zero polynomial residual. Because the cleared identities have integer coefficients, this verifies them over every commutative base ring with invertible u, including characteristics 2 and 3. It does not verify representability assertions by polynomial substitution.

The shorthand on scan 8, line 208 of K93-10, requires `−F_x(r,t)−sF_y(r,t)` for the fourth coefficient. The printed/transcribed `−F(r,t)` cannot work: its degree and constant term are wrong. The invariant differential follows by differentiating the equation: `F_x dx+F_y dy=0`. Its two displayed expressions agree wherever their denominators define the smooth coordinate chart. The definition at K93-10 lines15–27 explicitly includes elliptic, nodal and cuspidal fibres. Consequently Proposition2.5's full affine(g2,g3)-plane is correct for that convention; the smooth subfunctor is its Δ-invertible open. The initial suspicion of a missing Δ inverse was withdrawn after reading this definition. It is not a remaining scan alarm.

In characteristic 2 the smooth cubic `y²+y=x³` has coordinate automorphisms satisfying u³=1, r=s², s⁴=s and t²+t=r³. Over F₄ this yields 24 distinct automorphisms. The program exhaustively checks closure and orders. The u=1 kernel has order 8, one involution and six elements of order 4, hence is Q₈; the three maps `(u,0,0,0)` split the quotient C₃. This verifies Q₈⋊C₃, with the normal factor specified. In characteristic 3, for `y²=x³−x`, the equations are s=t=0, u⁴=1, r³=r, giving 12 maps over F₉. These are all pointed automorphisms because the Weierstrass change-of-coordinates theorem describes any such map. On a disconnected base, the assertion that automorphisms are “only ±1” must be understood as a group sheaf: independent choices can be made on distinct components.

## Cartier, cote 93 scans 169–185

For a p-basis x₁,…,x_d over Λ, the de Rham complex is a tensor product of d one-variable complexes. In one variable `d(x^i)=i x^(i−1)dx`; the coefficients i=1,…,p−1 are units. The cohomology has basis 1 in degree 0 and x^(p−1)dx in degree 1. Tensoring these split free complexes proves the exterior-algebra description, including the displayed monomial representatives. A choice of representatives depends on the chosen p-basis.

There is no additive natural map to closed forms sending dx to x^(p−1)dx. Over F₂[x,y], the proposed rule applied to d(x+y)=dx+dy has defect `y dx+x dy=d(xy)`, which is nonzero in Ω¹ and is exact. Passing to de Rham cohomology removes the defect. This checks every hypothesis of the counterexample: the base is F₂, the algebra is a smooth polynomial algebra, and {x,y} is a p-basis. Cartier's original 1957 note, §3 Theorem 1, uses the cohomology target. No ink reading has been changed.

For a smooth curve and relative Frobenius F, put Q=F_*O_X/O_(X'). The form `φ(f,g)=C(f dg)` is well defined on Q: changing either argument by a pth-power coefficient contributes either zero or an exact differential. It is alternating for odd p because `f df=(1/2)d(f²)`, and symmetric for p=2. In a p-basis x, its matrix relative to x,…,x^(p−1) and d\tilde x has `(i,j)` entry j when i+j=p, and zero otherwise. Thus the index p−1 on scan 183 is inconsistent with both the definition and the drawn matrix.

For n=p−1, the determinant is
`(-1)^[n(n−1)/2] (p−1)!`, hence, for odd p, `(-1)^[(p−1)/2+1]` in F_p. It is always nonzero. The output includes the full exact matrices and determinants for p=2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19. In particular p=5 gives −1=4, not +1. The proof of perfectness survives, whereas the asserted value +1 and the literal square-root normalization need correction. For odd p, the alternating form defines the Pfaffian map `det Q→ω_(X')^((p−1)/2)` canonically. Its normalization relative to the p-basis must be stated; the claim that the displayed basis prescription squares to the particular determinant isomorphism is false as written at p=5. The existence of the determinant-line isomorphism is a different, valid assertion. For p=2, Q has rank 1 and its self-duality gives Q²≅ω_(X'). Raynaud 1982 §4.1 explicitly contains this pairing and the characteristic-2 square root.

The Jacobson formula `(D+c)^p=D^p+c^p+D^(p−1)c` has the following universal proof. Let R be a commutative ring of characteristicp, D a derivation, M_a multiplication bya, and A=D+M_c. Since [A,M_a]=M_Da, the identity ad(A)^p=ad(A^p) implies that A^p−D^p commutes with every multiplication. It is therefore multiplication by A^p(1). The integral Bell-polynomial expansion for this element has terms indexed by Σj k_j=p, coefficient p!/(∏k_j!(j!)^k_j), and monomial ∏(D^(j−1)c)^k_j. The terms k_1=p and k_p=1 have coefficient1. In every other term all denominator factors are prime top and the numerator is divisible byp, so that term vanishes in R. The two surviving terms are c^p and D^(p−1)c. This proves the formula for every such R,D,c. The program additionally checks finite endomorphism certificates on F_p[x]/(x^p) for p≤7; these are supplementary. The classical match is Seshadri's Chevalley seminar exposition6 (1958–59), pp5–6. Cartier in top degree is a duality trace on differential forms, not the ordinary field trace on functions of a purely inseparable extension.

The universal assertion on scan 184 about pointless smooth proper rational curves over every imperfect field of characteristic 2 is false. Take k to be a separable closure of F₂(t). It is imperfect: a root of X²−t is purely inseparable and cannot lie in a separable algebraic extension of F₂(t). Every nonempty smooth finite-type k-scheme has a point with finite separable residue field, hence a k-point because k is separably closed. In particular every smooth conic over k has a k-point. This refutes the universal field assertion, not the existence of some imperfect fields with anisotropic conics. The scanned ink may of course support a different quantifier.

## A checked counterexample to scan 204

Let C be the smooth projective model over F₂ of

\[
z^2+(x^2+x+1)z=x^5+x^3+x^2+1.
\]

Put h=x²+x+1 and set `z=hy`. The function-field equation becomes `y²+y=x+1/h`, because `f=xh²+h` in characteristic 2 (also checked symbolically in the program). Its poles over an algebraic closure are the two distinct roots of h and infinity, all of order 1. The extension is separable of degree 2 (the derivative in y is 1), and is geometrically connected: a function b²+b has even order at any pole, so cannot equal a function with a simple pole. Riemann–Hurwitz for this Artin–Schreier cover gives `2g−2=−4+3·2=2`, hence g=2. At a root of h in the affine equation, the only possible y-derivative zero is accompanied by z=0 and f'(x)=1 mod h, so the affine curve is smooth. Infinity contributes one rational point, the unique ramified point above the pole at infinity. Thus the enumeration is on the asserted smooth projective genus-two curve, not on a singular affine replacement.

The program exhaustively enumerates F₂,F₄,F₈,F₁₆ points using explicit irreducible-polynomial arithmetic and obtains `N₁=3,N₂=7,N₃=9,N₄=31`. The genus-two zeta functional equation gives
`P(T)=1+(N₁−3)T+((N₂−5)+(N₁−3)²)T²/2+2(N₁−3)T³+4T⁴=1+T²+4T⁴`.
The last two point counts independently agree with the resulting recurrence. Therefore `#J(F₂)=P(1)=6`. Its rational 2-torsion has order 2: Cauchy's theorem gives an element of order 2, while the group order excludes two independent such elements. Consequently `dim_F₂ J(F₂)[2]=1`.

But `P(T) mod2=1+T²=(1+T)²`, so the multiplicity of the root 1 is 2. This is an exact counterexample to the identification in the transcription. The correct invariant for rational torsion is the dimension of the fixed subspace of the étale p-torsion representation. Algebraic multiplicity can replace that dimension only under an appropriate semisimplicity condition at eigenvalue 1. No such condition is present on scan 204. This does not refute the relation between the degree of the unit-root factor and the geometric p-rank.

## Additional targeted proof checks

For a smooth projective connected complex curve X of genus g with reduced divisor D of n marked points, Serre duality gives `h¹(K²(D))=h⁰(K⁻¹(−D))`. Thus the source's simple-pole projective-connection difference space has dimension `3g−3+n` for g≥2; n for g=1,n>0; 1 for g=1,n=0; and max(n−3,0) for g=0. Identification with a cotangent space requires the stable pointed moduli problem or its stack, and does not turn simple poles into the more general double-pole regular-singular convention.

A Čech description of a torsor under a bundle E with a fixed integrable connection uses translation cocycles e_ij and local connection one-forms a_i with `a_j−a_i=∇e_ij`, `∇a_i=0`. Changing trivializations adds a total coboundary. This proves the H¹ de Rham classification in the smooth characteristic-zero setting, and automorphisms are H⁰ de Rham. It is a proof of this precise torsor statement, not a proof of all the manuscript's general-site variants.

The Baire step in cote100 scans37/39 is valid after the stated reductions are made explicit. For a complete local noetherian domain of positive dimension, Cohen's structure theorem gives a finite integral map from a power-series ring `V[[T₁,…,T_r]]`, with V a complete DVR; in equal characteristic choose one parameter for V. For each proper closed subset Z_i, choose a nonzero element a_i in its ideal contracted to this power-series ring; such an element exists by finiteness and the domain hypothesis. Homomorphisms to V with T_i in its maximal ideal form a complete metric space. The zero-evaluation set of a nonzero power series is closed with empty interior, by the one-variable power-series identity theorem applied successively to the variables. Baire gives a section on which every a_i evaluates nonzero. Lying over its prime ideal produces a closed one-dimensional integral subscheme of the original spectrum; finite integral dimension preservation gives dimension one. Its generic point is outside all Z_i, though its closed point may lie in them. Thus this curve is contained in none of the Z_i. Reduction to minimal components handles a general ring; a zero-dimensional component is immediate. This supplies the manuscript's missing reductions and final Baire invocation. It is a targeted proof completion of a recorded lemma, not development of one of the proposed future directions.

## The reused GIT example, cote100 scans14 and16

This is Mumford's Example0.4, not a Grothendieck conjecture. Work over C and use the discriminant convention for which XY has discriminant1. The variety of binary quadratics of discriminant1 is smooth (the gradient of b²−4ac cannot vanish on that level). Sending Q to Q² identifies Q and −Q; this involution acts freely. Its quotient is the smooth orbit of X²Y², with stabilizer the normalizer N(T) of the diagonal torus in SL2. The indicated X is the product of this orbit with the punctured two-dimensional space of linear forms, hence smooth and quasi-affine.

A diagonal stabilizer element diag(t,t⁻¹) fixes a nonzero linear form only if t=1. An antidiagonal element of SL2 preserving X²Y² has square −I and eigenvalues ±i, so cannot fix a nonzero vector. Thus every stabilizer is trivial (also as a group scheme in characteristic zero). In the normal form F₄=X²Y², the torus preserves αβ and the other normalizer component changes its sign. Its invariant ring is C[(αβ)²]. For a nonzero value, the two possible values of αβ are exchanged by N(T); at value0 the two punctured coordinate axes are exchanged. Every fibre is one orbit. The quotient map is open: on the chart α≠0, αβ is a coordinate and its square map to A¹ is finite flat and open; the β≠0 chart gives the same conclusion. Invariants on a quotient-open set give the corresponding regular functions, by localization of C[α,β]^N. These facts verify the claimed geometric quotient φ(F₁,F₄)=F₄(−β,α).

The program `root_git_computation.py` verifies the matrix substitution in the source, using the inverse-substitution convention for the left action. The displayed one-parameter set Z is closed in G×X: its equations include g₁₁=g₂₂=0, g₁₂g₂₁=−1, and λ=g₂₁. Its image under Ψ has the additional closure point ((Y,X²Y²),(Y,X²Y²)) when λ→0, but no point of Z has λ=0. Therefore Ψ is not a closed map and the action is not proper. This is compatible with modern freeness, which asks for trivial scheme-theoretic stabilizers/monomorphism of the action map. ## Nilpotent algebraization and principal-parts checks

For a square-zero closed immersion S₀⊂S of complete noetherian bases and a proper smooth algebraic X₀/S₀, lifts are obstructed in H²(X₀,T⊗I), when that obstruction vanishes their isomorphism classes form a torsor under H¹, and their automorphisms are H⁰. A proper algebraization of a smooth formal scheme is itself smooth: the closed nonsmooth locus has proper closed image in the local base, and if nonempty that image would meet the closed point, contrary to formal smoothness. Proper coherent formal comparison identifies the obstruction, torsor and automorphism groups with the formal ones. A given formal lift therefore has an algebraic lift and a compatible formal isomorphism. Iterating the finite nilpotence filtration proves the source's smooth nilpotent-invariance lemma. This uses properness and smoothness; it is not a proof for arbitrary nonflat formal schemes. EGAIII1 §5.4.6 originally leaves the general reduced-algebraization question open, while §5.4.5 supplies the ample-line-bundle criterion.

For the first-principal-parts sequence, a splitting is described locally by s(e)=1⊗e plus a first-order term. O-linearity of s, together with (1⊗a−a⊗1)e=da⊗e in the diagonal ideal modulo its square, is exactly the Leibniz identity ∇(ae)=a∇e+da⊗e, up to the stated sign of the first-order term. Conversely that identity makes the displayed s linear. Extending ∇ by the graded Leibniz rule gives ∇²(ae)=a∇²(e), because the da terms cancel. Flatness means that this O-linear curvature is zero. Over a smooth Q-scheme the formal Taylor series Σ∇^I/I! supplies the compatible stratification if and only if the covariant derivatives commute. This calculation explains why division by factorials and smooth-coordinate hypotheses cannot be omitted.

## The literal building fixed-point assertion and its refinement

The preface sentence at cote100 scan34 (Pro line2144; CodexK100-03 line162) lacks the subbuilding alternative. Take the rank-one spherical building of SL2(C), whose vertices are P¹(C) with angular distanceπ between any distinct vertices. The whole building is a convex subcomplex: the condition on segments of length less thanπ is vacuous. The matrices A=diag(i,−i) and B=[[0,1],[-1,0]] generate Q8. They act by building automorphisms and isometries. The only A-invariant lines are the two coordinate lines, and B interchanges them. Hence the finite group has no common fixed point. This verifies every hypothesis of the literal sentence and refutes its unrestricted conclusion. It does not refute Tits's centre conjecture with its subbuilding alternative. Ramos-Cuevas's Conjecture1.1 and Theorem1.2/Corollaries1.3–1.4 are the exact modern comparison; the exceptional noncrystallographic H4 qualification is retained. `b_tits_check.py` independently verifies the displayed matrix facts.

## Geometric reductivity and the p-power formulation

The source's positive-characteristic formulation uses a p-power symmetric degree. Here is the refinement from an invariant of arbitrary positive degree. Let v be a G-fixed vector and F a homogeneous G-invariant polynomial with F(v)≠0, degree d=p^r m with p∤m. Putq=p^r. The coefficient of t^(d−q) in F(x+tv) is a G-invariant homogeneous polynomial H(x) of degreeq. Evaluating atx=v gives H(v)=binom(d,q)F(v). The coefficient congruence binom(p^r m,p^r)=m modp shows that this is nonzero. Thus geometric reductivity gives an invariant in a p-power degree, exactly as required for the invariant complement to the codimension-one symmetric-power kernel in Mumford's formulation. This removes a spurious mismatch between that formulation and Haboush's theorem. The proof is algebraic and general; the checked primary Sastry–Seshadri introduction supplies the modern invariant-polynomial statement.

## No additive subgroup in a Néronian generic fibre (C-SA04-676)

The final handwritten application, cote100 scan53, uses the existence of a Néron model itself to exclude a subgroup isomorphic to the additive group. This is stronger than merely assuming the generic fibre semiabelian. Both witnesses agree; the initial bracketed semiabelian assumption on scan48 is marked unnecessary. The exact published assertion is Bosch–Lütkebohmert–Raynaud, *Néron Models* (1990), §10.1 Proposition8, pp293–294; its statement and proof were opened.

Here is an independent proof for the trait actually used. Let R be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer π and fraction field K, and let G/R be a smooth separated Néron lft-model. Suppose i:Ga_K→G_K is a closed subgroup embedding. The differential v=di(1) is nonzero because a closed immersion is injective on tangent spaces. The Lie module L=Lie(G/R) is finite free over R, even when the whole model is only locally of finite type. For every n≥0, the map i composed with multiplication by π^(−n) on Ga_K extends, by the Néron mapping property, to a morphism Ga_R→G. Its value at zero is the identity: equality on the generic point extends by separatedness. Its differential therefore sends1 into L and equals π^(−n)v after tensoring with K. Thus v belongs to π^nL for every n, contradicting the separatedness of the finite free R-lattice L. No assumption that K is perfect is used for this assertion. The schematic-image and quotient-representability steps elsewhere in C-SA04-675 are separate claims and are not proved by this argument.
