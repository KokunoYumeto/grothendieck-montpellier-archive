## 1. Cycle classes, residues, trace, and correspondences

For an oriented transverse complex disc, put z=ε exp(it), 0≤t≤2π. Then dz/z=i dt and the contour integral is 2πi. For a regular codimension-p embedding with local parameters z₁,…,z_p, orient the product of p circles in that order. The integral of (dz₁/z₁)∧…∧(dz_p/z_p) is (2πi)^p. Consequently, with the manuscript's residue convention, the algebraic/de Rham fundamental class corresponds to (2πi)^p times the integral Betti class. This is the precise local calculation behind 18/019–025 and the preceding package's 18/015–017. It checks the regular-embedding case; it does not identify every unspecified arrow in the support diagram at L58.

Replacing i by −i multiplies the p-fold factor by (−1)^p. Thus the minus sign at 18/019 L31 is correct for the immediately preceding divisor example, p=1. If that sentence is meant for arbitrary p, the point class in P² disproves a uniform minus sign. The manuscript's scope remains a scan question.

Let X be smooth proper of dimension d, and Z a correspondence of codimension r on X×Y. Acting on degree q, pullback preserves degree, cup product with [Z] adds 2r, and pushforward along X removes 2d. The target degree is q+2r−2d. Converting the cycle class from de Rham to Betti contributes (2πi)^−r, while converting the trace contributes (2πi)^d in the manuscript convention. The combined exponent is d−r. For r=d, both the degree shift and the exponent vanish. C01 performs this symbolic bookkeeping. This is the justification for compatibility of degree-zero correspondences, not a license to ignore the uncertain n/m indices at L149–156.

There is a genuine convention boundary. G66 Thm1/1′ supplies algebraic de Rham comparison. D71 §§2.1.13–14 and 2.2.4–6 specifies Tate, cycle, and trace conventions. In ST03 Thm4.1, the Lipman trace compared through the usual Hodge spectral-sequence map includes (−1)^{d(d−1)/2} as well as (2πi)^−d. C01 prints this extra parity for small d. We therefore do not identify the manuscript's trace with every published trace without an explicit dictionary.

The integral Betti lattice in complex cohomology means the image of integral cohomology modulo torsion. A map from a torsion-containing group to a complex vector space cannot inject its torsion. Nor does the P¹ normalization settle whether L237 says “primitifs” or “possibles”: primitiveness would impose an additional normalization on a lattice generator. In a fixed algebraic basis, automorphic images of (2πi)^{-1} remain transcendental over Q, since automorphisms preserve algebraicity. They cannot exhaust all nonzero scalars if that were the proposed target set.

## 2. Real structures, tensor forms, and counterexamples

For a variety over R, comparison identifies two compatible anti-linear involutions on the complex realization: coefficient/Betti conjugation and the real de Rham conjugation. Their product is the complex-linear geometric real involution f. These particular involutions commute; arbitrary anti-linear involutions need not commute. On cohomology the geometric map acts on the integral singular cochain complex, so it preserves the integral lattice and its torsion-free image.

For a tensor-compatible rational involution, write V_Q=V_Q⁺⊕V_Q⁻. Inside V_C, both W_Q=V_Q⁺⊕iV_Q⁻ and W_R=V_R⁺⊕iV_R⁻ are forms. Their tensor products are compatible with the ambient tensor product: the product of two negative eigenspaces has positive parity, and i²=−1 still belongs to its rational/real positive line. C02 checks the four parity pairs. The additive map a:V_R→W_R given by 1 on V⁺ and i on V⁻ is not generally a tensor map: on two negative vectors a(v)⊗a(w)=−a(v⊗w).

The page at L359 tentatively expects that the two functors are not tensor-isomorphic. A sign Artin object proves that expectation on a category containing that object. Let A be the image of (1−c)/2 in h⁰(Spec C/R). The projector is algebraic, rational, idempotent, and orthogonal to (1+c)/2; C03 verifies the two-by-two matrices. Choose a rational basis e of A with the algebraic multiplication A⊗A→1 sending e⊗e to 1. A real map to W(A)=iR e must send e to ir e. Tensor compatibility would require (ir)²=1, or r²=−1, impossible over R. On a category generated only by a Tate object with its integer grading, the maps e_n↦i^n e_n instead give a tensor isomorphism. The Artin counterexample must not be extended indiscriminately to every tensor subcategory.

The live tentative equivalence f=1 iff the motive is generated by even Tate powers is false in the natural category of algebraic motives over R. Let T=Q(−1), and let A be the sign Artin object above. Then f_A=f_T=−1 and f_{A⊗T}=+1, but A⊗T has weight 2. Objects generated by Q(−2) have weights divisible by 4, so A⊗T cannot belong to that category. It is geometrically realized as an algebraic direct summand of h²(P¹_C regarded as an R-variety). Thus this is a counterexample within algebraic motives, not merely an invented representation. C03 checks the projector, signs, and weights. It does not depend on the deleted opening at L372.

The abstract group G_m does not recover this lost information. Each of T, T², and A⊗T can generate a rank-one tensor category with abstract Tannaka group G_m, while the distinguished character, weight, and value of real Frobenius differ. One must retain the representation and Tate data. The incomplete marginal “Q-tordu” remark is not a complete replacement criterion.

For the distinguished comparison point α, the displayed equation bar(α)=fα gives α real iff f=1, because a torsor action is free. It does not say that P(R) is empty whenever that representative f is nonidentity. Changing a comparison changes its cocycle by a coboundary. A Tate comparison can be imaginary even though its underlying split G_m-torsor has real points.

The Hodge decomposition is H^{p,q}=F^p∩bar(F^q) inside H^{p+q} for a pure smooth proper complex variety with its fixed topology. It is not inside H^q unless p=0. Geometric real Frobenius exchanges H^{p,q} and H^{q,p}; it therefore conjugates the Hodge torus action j(z) to j(bar z), and acts by inversion on the norm-one torus. Involution means order dividing two; it may be identity. The point concerning a topology-independent algebraic splitting remains separate.

For a weight-n polarization Q, real Frobenius changes the Tate-valued pairing by (−1)^n. If C is the Weil operator, then fCf^{-1}=C^{-1} and C^{-1}=(−1)^n C on weight n. The two signs cancel in the associated positive real form. C04 checks the parity. No extra positivity conjecture follows.

The line H*(X(R))=V^f cannot be read as a general fixed-locus theorem. Take P¹/R: the invariant rational cohomology of P¹(C) under its geometric conjugation has dimension 1 (degree zero), while H*(P¹(R),Q)=H*(S¹,Q) has dimension 2. For the quotient space by a finite group, rational cohomology does identify with invariants under the usual finite equivariant triangulation/transfer argument: average cochains over the stabilizers, whose orders are invertible in Q. Thus the later explicit quotient X(C)/π at L512–526 offers a plausible different interpretation. It does not silently change the earlier notation.

Finally, in the equality of fields at L433, multiplying a matrix by an invertible matrix over R does not change the R-field generated by its entries: each matrix is recovered from the other by an R-linear combination. Hence R(γ)=R(βγ), provided β and β^{-1} really are over R. Applying the same argument to the other displayed factors proves the intended equality, conditional on the preceding real-point choices.

## 3. Fullness and geometric components

In neutral Tannaka theory, tensor invariants determine the affine group acting on a fiber functor. To conclude that an algebraic-motive category has the full Hodge tensor category as its image, one needs the relevant Hodge tensors to arise from algebraic correspondences. Over R one must additionally retain real Frobenius and descend the correspondences. With rational coefficients, an invariant complex correspondence can be descended by averaging it with its conjugate and using finite descent; forgetting the real action would lose this condition.

This implication is conditional for algebraic cycles. DM12 Prop6.25 supplies the abelian absolute-Hodge version; Arapura13 Thm6.4.1 identifies a pure Nori category with André motives under its stated setting. Those are different, established categories. They are not proofs of the general Hodge conjecture.

For components, an arithmetic exact sequence with quotient Gal(C/R) yields at most two components only if its geometric kernel is connected and equals G⁰. If that kernel is disconnected, the quotient by it does not compute π₀(G). The corrected DM12 Props6.22–6.23 explicitly require the absolute-Hodge hypothesis for geometric connectedness; footnote21 records its omission from the 1982 text. Kahn25 Thm5.10 gives an exact-sequence criterion, not automatic connectedness. This is why the manuscript's generation of G by G⁰ and f remains conditional in general.

The separate assertion “semisimplicity implies trivial centre” fails already for Rep_Q(G_m): it is semisimple and its group is its own centre. C18 records this boundary. The centre-free geometric fundamental-group facts used in the other-project bridge have additional hyperbolicity and group hypotheses (Mochizuki04 Lem1.3.1); they cannot be transferred to motivic groups by analogy.

## 4. Real torsors and effective descent

For a complex affine group G with real structure, a representative f satisfying f bar(f)=1 defines a real form of a G-torsor. Replacing a point conjugates this cocycle by the appropriate coboundary, and the automorphism group is the corresponding inner twist. Neutrality asks whether the class is trivial, not whether one chosen representative literally equals 1.

DM12 Thm3.2 represents tensor-isomorphism functors by torsors over a field. StacksDescent, Lem35.37.1, proves effectivity for affine fpqc descent data: equalize the descended algebra inside the faithfully flat extension, then descend multiplication and unit. These statements rigorously support the intended descent construction once there is algebraic descent data. A cocycle for an arbitrary abstract automorphism group without continuity or a genuine scheme descent datum is not automatically an affine torsor over the proposed base. This distinction is retained at the manuscript's “à vérifier / OK”.

## 5. The intersection K_r of all real forms

Let k≅C as an abstract field, and intersect σ^{-1}(R) over all field isomorphisms σ:k→C. Every algebraic element whose conjugates over Q are all real lies in this intersection. If an algebraic element has a nonreal conjugate, the embedding sending it there extends first to an algebraic closure of Q and then, after identifying transcendence bases, to an isomorphism k→C. It is therefore excluded.

If x∈k is transcendental over Q, extend {x} to a transcendence basis. Choose a nonreal transcendental y∈C, extend {y} to a basis of the same cardinality, map the bases bijectively, and extend to algebraic closures. The resulting isomorphism sends x outside R. Consequently K_r is precisely the subfield of algebraic totally real elements inside k. In particular it is algebraic over Q. This checks L559–561 with the full quantifier over all isomorphisms; a fixed topology would give a much larger field.

## 7. Elliptic products and quotient dimensions

For two non-CM elliptic curves over C that are not isogenous, MZ99 Cor3.9 gives the product Hodge-group statement and the corresponding divisor generation. Their derived Hodge group is SL₂×SL₂. Including the common Tate character produces the group {(g₁,g₂)∈GL₂²: det(g₁)=det(g₂)}, of dimension 8−1=7. A Borel in each GL₂ has dimension 3, so the common-determinant Borel has dimension 6−1=5. C05 checks these arithmetic dimensions. An isogenous pair or a CM factor changes the calculation; the manuscript's uncertain abbreviation at L618 cannot be decided from the answer 7.

For a free diagonal action on an n-fold product of G-torsors, fix the first coordinate locally in the faithfully flat topology. The quotient then has n−1 relative coordinates in G, and dimension (n−1)dimG. The action convention determines whether these are g₁^{-1}g_j or g_jg₁^{-1}; the dimension is unchanged. This is a quotient dimension bound, not a proof that any specified comparison tuple is generic. In particular it does not identify the undefined field K whose bound appears at L628.

## 8. Adelic genericity, power characters, and strong approximation

There are three distinct objects in these pages: the full product ∏_l Q_l, the restricted finite adele ring A_f=∏'_l(Q_l,Z_l), and its compact open subring Z-hat=∏_l Z_l. The tuple (1/l)_l belongs to the first and not the second. In the full product topology, ∏Z_l is not open. Indeed every basic open set leaves almost all coordinates unrestricted; a compact open set in the additive full product would project onto the noncompact field Q_l at one of those coordinates, impossible. The unrestricted notation on18/047 must not be replaced silently by the restricted product used on18/050–052.

The strengthened-Tate premise on18/049 is ambiguous because G is merely marked “géom.” and the category's Tate convention is not defined there. Here is the exact conditional counterexample. Let the category be the tensor subcategory generated by M=Q(−2), do not automatically adjoin Q(1), use its faithful rank-one integral realization, and let G be its Mumford–Tate/Tannaka group G_m. The Galois representation is χ². Modulo each odd prime l, its image lies in the subgroup of squares of F_l^×, of index 2. For r distinct odd primes, projection to their product therefore gives index at least 2^r. Hence the image has infinite index in ∏Z_l^×. C06 computes sample projections, while the cyclicity of each finite-field unit group proves the unrestricted bound. Adjoining a Tate half changes the representation and invalidates this example as a test of that convention. The exact ink remains an open ambiguous premise; only the explicitly stated reconstruction is refuted. CM20 explains the relevant Hodge-maximality/isogeny obstruction.

The generic-coset lemma itself has a clean proof. Let G/Q be geometrically connected smooth affine, and let a coset of an open subgroup of a compact open restricted-adelic group be given. Choose a compact product box ∏B_l inside the coset, each B_l containing a nonempty l-adic analytic open set. There are countably many proper Q-closed subvarieties Z of G. For each l and Z, the set of points in the box whose l-coordinate lies in Z(Q_l) is closed and nowhere dense: a nonzero regular function on a geometrically integral smooth group cannot vanish on an analytic open set. The product box is compact Hausdorff and hence Baire. Avoiding the countably many such sets gives a point Q-generic at every l. Apply the same argument to G^{n−1} for several relative comparisons. Finite index by itself must not replace openness without a closedness hypothesis; a closed finite-index subgroup of a compact group is open.

The manuscript separately assumes strong approximation before drawing its commensurability conclusion. Under that assumption, a nonempty open coset in G(A_f) meets G(Q), so the conditional argument is valid. It is motive-dependent and does not assert density for all groups. For G_m, Q^×∩Z-hat^×={±1}; the nonempty open set of units congruent to 1 mod3 and 2 mod5 meets neither rational possibility. C07 checks the residues. This is a boundary example, not a refutation of the author's explicitly conditional sentence.

The proposed total-image openness in a target also containing G(C) is a separate, incompletely specified statement. Zariski density, analytic density, and finite-adelic openness are different properties. The full Aut(k/k0), for k≅C and k0 finitely generated, is not the profinite absolute Galois group of k0. A compactness argument is valid only after an arithmetic representation has actually factored through an appropriate profinite quotient.

## 9. Integral comparisons, ambiguity, and the deleted place map

An integral tensor comparison must first exist. Rational Tannaka reconstruction does not supply its integral points, and an ordinary Q-linear category does not automatically carry a functor to Z-lattices compatible with all Q-scalars. One must specify integral morphisms/lattices or a suitable integral enhancement. RT26 supplies a modern integral category, but not the particular simultaneous point asserted here.

Assuming such a comparison g exists, changing an integral Betti identification by h∈G(Z) changes g to gh, as on L880. With a faithful torsion-free integral realization, diagonal G(Z) injects into each characteristic-zero local realization. Thus fixing all finite components leaves no diagonal ambiguity at infinity; fixing the infinite component likewise fixes a diagonal element and hence the finite components. This is uniqueness conditional on existence.

The actual deleted assertion at L891 concerns Γ=Aut(k/k0) acting on complete complex places, not an arbitrary motivic group quotient. Normalize a complete complex place by an isomorphism v:k→C and let c_v=v^{-1}cv. An automorphism preserving the absolute value |v(·)| becomes an isometry of C; it is continuous, fixes Q, hence fixes R by density, and sends i to ±i. Its stabilizer in Aut(k) is therefore {1,c_v}. Intersecting with Γ gives Γ∩{1,c_v}. Every fiber of the orbit map Γ→Γv is a coset of this stabilizer and has size 2 exactly if c_v fixes k0 pointwise, otherwise size 1.

For normalized complete places inducing the same absolute value on k0, transitivity can also be checked: their restrictions differ by an isometric field map between subfields of C; extend to their closures. These closures contain R and are R or C. Such an extension is identity or complex conjugation, so after conjugating one place the restrictions agree. Composing the two isomorphisms k→C gives an element of Γ. This proves the stated orbit description under the complete-place convention. It does not restore the deleted universal fiber size without the real-base hypothesis. No singleton example involving a trivial motivic G is used: that would test the wrong map.

## 10. Right cocycles, inversion, gauge, and the total homomorphism

The manuscript's law is φ(τσ)=τ(φ(σ))φ(τ). Inverting gives ψ(τσ)=ψ(τ)τ(ψ(σ)), for ψ=φ^{-1}, the usual left-cocycle convention in that order. The right coboundary φ(σ)=(σg)^{-1}g satisfies the law because

τ((σg)^{-1}g)(τg)^{-1}g=(τσg)^{-1}g.

The originally written g^{-1}σg is its inverse, and cannot be inserted into the same right-cocycle formula without changing conventions. C08 verifies the cancellation in a free noncommutative word algebra; it does not assume commutativity.

Changing the reference place by g gives φ'_σ=σ(g)φ_σg^{-1}. Indeed τ(φ'_σ)φ'_τ=τσ(g)τ(φ_σ)φ_τg^{-1}=φ'_{τσ}. The missing final inverse in the deleted L1074 is corrected in the live L1078. The finite total component simplifies by the free word σ_l g_l σ_l^{-1} σ_l g_l^{-1}=σ_l. Both certificates are in C08.

The raw cocycle is not generally a homomorphism to G. For example, with k=Q(t), additive automorphisms σ(t)=t+1 and τ(t)=t+2, and g=t in G_m(k), C18 computes the nonzero difference between the right coboundary at τσ and the ordinary product of values. The correct ordinary homomorphism for a left cocycle is σ↦(ψ(σ),σ) in G⋊Γ, with (a,σ)(b,τ)=(aσ(b),στ). This supplies the target missing from the phrase “homomorphisme total”, without specifying an unjustified topology.

## 11. Genericity, actual images, and the minimal-field caveat

Let P/k0 be geometrically integral of finite type, k0 finitely generated over Q, k≅C, and α∈P(k). If α is not k0-generic, its orbit under Aut(k/k0) is contained in its proper k0-algebraic locus. Conversely suppose α is generic. Given any proper k-closed subvariety of P_k, its defining coefficients generate a finite-type field extension F/k0 inside k. There is enough transcendence degree in k to embed k0(P) into k with a generic image independent of F. An isomorphism between the two finitely generated embedded copies extends to their algebraic closures and then, by extending transcendence bases of equal remaining cardinality, to an automorphism of k/k0. Thus the orbit escapes that subvariety. Its k-Zariski closure is P_k. Translating by α identifies this with density of the actual cocycle image in G_k.

The word “actual” matters. In G_a, a two-point image {0,1} is not dense, but its generated subgroup Z is Zariski dense in characteristic zero. A multiplicative example also fits a quadratic coboundary: for g=1+√2, its nontrivial conjugate gives (σg)^{-1}g=−3−2√2. The image {1,−3−2√2} is finite, while its generated subgroup is infinite and hence Zariski dense in G_m. C15 checks the exact value and polynomial x²+6x+1, and its real absolute value exceeds1. Neither example proves any period conjecture; it separates two logically different closures. BC16 Cor2.11/Conj2.12 makes the corresponding distinction for period torsors.

At L981 the “π transcendental over k0” qualification is unfinished. Here is the precise curve-level test. Put A=−3j/(j−1728), B=−2j/(j−1728) and E_j:y²=x³+Ax+B. C09 simplifies its j-invariant to j and its discriminant to 2985984j²/(j−1728)³. At j=π the discriminant is nonzero. The curve is defined over Q(π), of transcendence degree1, and any embedded field of definition of this curve contains its j-invariant π; hence1 is minimal at the curve level. This disproves an unrestricted inference from minimal curve-field transcendence degree to π being transcendental over that field. The page, however, concerns a motive. Minimal descent of h¹ up to isogeny is a different assertion, and no isogeny/descent argument proving that stronger counterexample has been supplied. The exact unfinished motivic statement therefore remains undetermined.

BT22, Conj1.2 and Thm4.25, further clarifies the base-field issue: the arithmetic generalized period conjecture counts base-field transcendence, while the established theorem concerns complex function fields and the relative motivic group. The latter does not settle arithmetic period genericity.

The kernel-density consequence at18/061 L1035 is separate from the paragraph's tentative topological openness. If the torsor is geometrically integral and the comparison point is k0-generic, that point stays generic after the algebraic extension k0bar/k0: a polynomial relation with algebraic coefficients would, by taking a finite extension and conjugates, force a relation over k0. The same automorphism-orbit proof therefore applies to Aut(k/k0bar). When the finite realization factors through Gal(k0bar/k0), this subgroup lies in its kernel. Its dense archimedean cocycle image implies the larger kernel also has dense image. This proves CL-SA07-481 conditionally; it establishes no analytic or adelic openness and does not turn Aut(k/k0) into a profinite group.

## 12. Reused root-system sheets

For A_l, let V⊂R^{l+1} be the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane. The root lattice is Q=Z^{l+1}∩V. The weight lattice is P={x∈V:x_i−x_j∈Z}, which equals the projection of Z^{l+1} to V. If v'=e₁−(Σe_i)/(l+1), every projected integral vector differs from an integral multiple of v' by Q. Also (l+1)v'∈Q, and mv'∈Q forces l+1 to divide m from its last coordinate. Thus P/Q is cyclic of order l+1. C10 checks exact examples, and the preceding argument proves all ranks. The Weyl group permutes coordinates; symmetric polynomials restricted to Σx_i=0 give basic degrees2,…,l+1. This matches BourbakiLie4to6 ChVI §4 no5(VIII–X), pp218–219.

For the Borel positive-subset argument, let P be closed under root addition and disjoint from −P. If α,β∈P have negative inner product, either they are independent and the root-string property gives α+β∈R, hence in P, or they are collinear. Opposite collinear roots are excluded: in a nonreduced system a factor2 possibility plus closure would put a root and its negative in P. Now a shortest nonempty zero sum of roots from P cannot exist. In a purported sum y₁+…+y_q=0, one has Σ_{j=2}^q(y₁,y_j)=−||y₁||²<0, so some j≥2 has negative pairing. Replace y₁,y_j by their sum and shorten the relation. The literal L220 omits the middle terms; the sum must run over every root, and the later replacement excludes both indices1 andj.

No positive real relation can exist either: in the rational root-coordinate system, a positive real solution of the rational linear equations can be approximated inside its kernel by a positive rational one, then cleared to integers. Thus0 is outside the finite convex hull of P. Its closest point separates all elements strictly from0; perturb this functional off the remaining root hyperplanes to obtain a chamber containing P among its positive roots. This is an independent proof of the theorem. The supplied/published four-step proof uses an equivalent separation and rank-induction finish; Bourbaki's complete text confirms the missing indices. We do not change the ink.

For a finite Coxeter graph, its form is q(x)=Σx_i²−2Σ_{edges}cos(π/m_ij)x_ix_j. Positive definiteness implies the graph is a forest: on a cycle, putting1 at cycle vertices and0 elsewhere gives q≤0. In a connected component it is a tree. For an order3 edge i–j, restrict to x_i=x_j=x_p. Its new diagonal coefficient is2−2cos(π/3)=1; other coefficients give precisely the contracted graph because no external vertex is adjacent to both endpoints. Restriction preserves positive definiteness. C19 verifies the coefficient identity.

The local star Schur complement gives Σ_j cos²(π/m_ij)<1 at every vertex. It implies degree≤3, degree3 edges all have order3, and no vertex meets two edges of order≥4. Contracting order3 edges and inducting yields: either one branch vertex and all labels3, or a chain with at most one label≥4. For the branch case contract an edge to a nonterminal neighbor; the branch remains, and a second branch at the merged neighbor would create degree4. The induction removes every other branch/high label. For a chain with a high edge, contract a neighboring order3 edge until induction applies. This is the graph-shape argument corresponding to the otherwise doubted draft lemmas, and the exact published Lem7 is on English p203. Its validity in finite positive-definite context does not identify every missing draft Lem3/4 formulation.

For crystallographic roots the product of Cartan integers is4cos²(π/m), an integer smaller than4. It is therefore0,1,2,or3, giving m=2,3,4,or6. The exact value at m=5 is irrational; for m≥7 the value lies strictly between3 and4. C10 prints the values. This excludes H₃,H₄ and the other stated dihedral cases from crystallographic root systems.

Coxeter word conjugacy uses two elementary moves: commuting unlinked generators, and cyclic rotation uv→vu, which is conjugation by u. In the displayed bAa→baA→aAb→Aba, the first and third moves require a to commute with the block A; the middle move is cyclic rotation. In a forest one may choose a leaf, move it through all nonneighbor generators, and by these rotations place it relative to its unique neighbor as needed; remove the leaf and apply induction. The published finite-forest proof is Bourbaki ChV §6 no1 Prop1/Lemma1, pp121–122. The sheet's absent exponent assertion(ii) is not reconstructed merely from this theorem.

For G₂ take α₁=(2,0), α₂=(−3,√3). The squared lengths are4 and12, with α₁ short. The six positive roots listed in CL-SA07-465 have squared lengths4,12,4,4,12,12; the highest root3α₁+2α₂=(0,2√3) is long. C10 computes their coordinates and Cartan pairings exactly. “Highest root” conventions must retain which root is short. The missing factorial/gamma/table edits, the missing Weyl-denominator context, and the unknown exponent proposition remain untestable from their absent antecedents.

## 13. Reused Haar-measure sheet

Normalize additive Haar measure by μ(Z_p)=1. For n≥0, Z_p is the disjoint union of p^n translates of p^nZ_p, so invariance gives μ(p^nZ_p)=p^−n. For n<0 the containing compact open group is a union of p^−n translates of Z_p. These compact-open cosets form a basis and determine the Radon measure. C15 prints sample masses; the partition argument proves the assertion for every n.

Let μ be a nonzero positive Radon measure on an open subset V⊂G, with the local translation invariance stated on the page. Transport μ to every translate sV. On sV∩tV the two measures agree by applying the local invariance to s^{-1}t and sets remaining in V. ## 14. Reused Dedekind, adele, and length sheets

In a DVR with uniformizer π, a nonzero fractional ideal is π^aA. Multiplication adds exponents; the fractional ideal quotient subtracts; sum takes the smaller exponent; intersection takes the larger. Since equality of fractional ideals is checked at all maximal localizations, these formulas globalize to a Dedekind domain. C11 gives a finite explicit exponent table. For an extension with pB=∏p_i^{e_i}, localization at p_i turns every other p_j into the unit ideal. A uniformizer a at p therefore has valuation e_i at p_i. This is precisely the ramification calculation of18/048.

Every nonzero ideal is supported at finitely many primes; its local DVR exponent at each prime determines it. The product of those prime powers has the same localizations, hence equals the ideal. Uniqueness follows from the exponents. Clearing denominators gives the free abelian group of fractional ideals. MilneANT20 Thm3.7/3.20 and StacksDedekind supply the checked standard statements.

For approximation with arbitrary fraction-field targets x_i at distinct primes p_i, choose a nonzero d∈A such that all dx_i are integral. By CRT choose y∈A congruent to dx_i modulo p_i^{N_i+v_i(d)} (increase a nonpositive exponent if necessary), and congruent to0 modulo q^{v_q(d)} at every additional prime q dividing d. Then x=y/d approximates x_i to valuation at least N_i and is integral at all primes outside the designated set. This gives the reduction missing from the manuscript's integral-target proof. C11 checks A=Z, targets1/2 at2 and1/3 at3: y=83 mod216 and x=83/6, whose differences are40/3 and27/2, of valuations3 at2 and3 at3 respectively.

CRT also proves A dense in ∏ A-hat_p for the nonzero-ideal topology. The product is complete. Every restricted adele has a common global denominator: choose d with sufficiently large valuations at its finitely many nonintegral coordinates. Thus the restricted product is the union of d^{-1}∏A-hat_p. A Cauchy net is eventually in a coset of the open complete product, and hence converges; the restricted product is complete and K is dense by the approximation argument. This is a topological completion of K for neighborhoods given by the nonzero ideals of A. It is not the unrestricted product of local fraction fields.

For SL_n with n≥2, Gaussian elimination over any field expresses a determinant-one matrix as a product of elementary matrices. The finite-support subgroup of the restricted product of local SL_n is dense: any basic neighborhood imposes conditions only at finitely many places. Its elements are products of elementary matrices with adele coefficients, by decomposing at those finitely many places and padding with zeros. Approximate those finitely many adele coefficients by elements of K; polynomial continuity of the elementary-matrix product proves SL_n(K) dense. For n=1 it is trivial. This independently verifies the manuscript proof, while the exact BourbakiAC65 VII §2 no4 Prop4 locator is currently a discovery lead rather than a collated printed theorem.

If A is a noetherian domain whose nonzero primes are maximal, a finitely generated torsion module M has a common nonzero annihilator. A finite prime filtration then has factors A/p with p nonzero, hence maximal and of length1. Thus M has finite length. No normality is needed. For a directed union T=∪T_i, every finite chain is detected in a sufficiently large stage when witnessed by finitely many elements; alternatively, if all lengths are bounded, choose a stage of maximum finite length. Every common larger stage has the same length and equals it by additivity, so it contains all T_i and equals T. Therefore length(T)=sup length(T_i), including infinity. StacksLength/StacksSupport supplies the basic filtration and additivity results.

In a semilocal Dedekind domain there are finitely many maximal primes. Given an ideal I=∏p^{a_p}, choose by CRT an element having valuation exactly a_p at each prime (choose a target in p^{a_p}\p^{a_p+1} and approximate one order further). It then generates I. This checks the continuation18/058→056 and MilneANT20 Cor3.14. The missing list(a)–(g) at18/058 cannot be reconstructed from the many possible equivalent-condition lists in modern books.

## 16. Formal distributions and variance

Let A be the completed symmetric coordinate algebra at a formal origin, with maximal ideal m. The continuous finite-order dual is the union of (A/m^{n+1})^*. Multiplication of A dualizes to a coalgebra map. In (A/m²)^*, the functional on the constant summand is the order-zero part; its complement annihilating1 is (m/m²)^*, the tangent space. This proves the reconstructed tangent-distribution formula at18/018. Cartier56 and Dieudonne56 are checked historical expositions, but the specific reused draft's provenance remains unidentified.

A formal map f:V→W induces a continuous algebra map f^*:O(W)→O(V), and composition reverses: (g∘f)^*=f^*∘g^*. A chart transition has to include the appropriate inverse chart and follow that source/target order. C14 lists the exact map types exposing the defective displayed composite at18/022. Taking the linear term gives (f^*)_1^1:W^*→V^*, not an arbitrary endomorphism of one dual space. These are type checks, not scan decisions.

For a finite free target M, a pointed formal map is a tuple of formal power series without constant terms, one per basis vector of M. Assigning each monomial distribution its corresponding coefficient in M gives a linear map U(V)→M vanishing on U₀; conversely those coefficient values reconstruct the series, degree by degree in the completion. This proves the asserted coefficient bijection in the stated finite-free setting. The absent draft's more general separated-module convention is not reconstructed.

## 17. Links to the Lefschetz-string package

These checks concern earlier-run bridges to cote16, whose detailed manuscript claims are owned by p16. On a string e₀,…,e_m, let E e_j=e_{j+1}, normalized Λe_j=e_{j−1} when defined, and F e_j=j(m−j+1)e_{j−1}. Then h e_j=(m−2j)e_j gives [F,E]=h, [h,E]=−2E, [h,F]=2F. The Casimir h²+2h+4EF acts by m(m+2). C12 verifies the symbolic coefficient identity for arbitrary m,j and illustrates it with exact matrices. Reversing H=−h gives [E,F]=H and Casimir H²+2H+4FE. The normalized Λ and the coefficient-weighted F are different operators.

For the algebra generated by normalized E,Λ, put B=1−EΛ (bottom projector) and T=1−ΛE (top projector). On a direct sum of finite strings, B_m=BΛ^mTE^mB projects to the bottoms of exactly the strings of length m. Then E^bB_mΛ^a maps the a-th vector to the b-th in those strings and annihilates the other positions. These are matrix units for0≤a,b≤m, acting identically on multiplicity spaces. C16 verifies the assertions for lengths0 through4; the position argument proves all lengths. Additional grading projectors distinguish strings with different cohomological starting degrees. This does not validate an upper bound2n−i when the actual string length is n−i.

The weight cocharacter, relative Lefschetz cocharacter, and adjoint conjugating cocharacter differ. If degree is i and center is d, the first two act by t^i and t^{i−d}, so w(t)=t^d λ(t). A cocharacter with weights j conjugates E by t and F by t^{-1}; the relative cohomological cocharacter conjugates E by t². C17 checks the matrices. On H¹ of an elliptic curve the Lefschetz sl₂ action is trivial although the Hodge/motivic group need not be.

Finally, Lefschetz raising on h*(P¹) sends the unit H⁰ to H² and is nonzero there. A tensor derivation must annihilate the tensor unit, since the unit relation identifies its action with twice itself. Therefore this raising operator is not a Lie element of the ordinary tensor automorphism group of the motive. C13 checks the degree behavior. This blocks a proposed blanket identification of Lefschetz SL₂ with the motivic Tannaka group without additional structure.
