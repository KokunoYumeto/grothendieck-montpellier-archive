## 1. PD ideals of the p-adic coefficient ring

Write a_p(n)=v_p(p^n/n!)=n-v_p(n!). Legendre's formula gives a_p(n)=((p-2)n+s_p(n))/(p-1), where s_p is the digit sum. The ideal generated by p^n/n!, n>=i, is p^{b_p(i)} Z_p, with b_p(i)=min_{n>=i} a_p(n). For p=2 and i>0 the minimum is one: digit sums are at least one, and powers of two can be chosen above i. Consequently every positive filtration ideal is 2Z_2; its separated completion is Z_2/2Z_2=F_2, not Z_2 with its usual topology.

For odd p, a_p(n)>((p-2)/(p-1))n for n>0. Since b_p(i)<=a_p(i), any minimizer satisfies n<=floor(((p-1)a_p(i)-1)/(p-2)). This gives an exact finite computation, not merely an asymptotic bound. The positive filtration is cofinal with the p-adic filtration. Nonetheless b_p(p-1)=b_p(p)=p-1, so Gr^{p-1} is zero, whereas the degree p-1 part of Gamma_{F_p}(T) is nonzero. Cofinality does not imply the graded-free hypothesis used on scan235.

The local change is a_p(i+1)-a_p(i)=1-v_p(i+1). Thus the weak inequality a_p(i)<=a_p(i+1) holds iff v_p(i+1)<=1; the strict inequality holds iff v_p(i+1)=0. The transcription at scan241 line229 says weak inequality iff s=0 and is mathematically inconsistent at p=3,i=2 (both values two). This is an additional reading alarm, not an emendation. The later assertion b_p(i)=b_p(i+1) when p divides i+1 is correct: a_p(i+1)<=a_p(i).

## 2. Ordinary integral divided powers and the coefficient ideal

If an ordinary strictly graded commutative integral cochain model carried divided powers on every positive even class, with the boundary compatibility needed for descent, then a degree-two cohomology class x would satisfy x^2=2 gamma_2(x). For CP^2, H^*(CP^2;Z)=Z[x]/(x^3), |x|=2, and x^2 is primitive, a contradiction. This refutes that strengthened reading only. It does not refute weighted comparison in a range, operations confined to a coefficient ideal, or divided powers that do not descend to ordinary integral cohomology. The notes' counterexample is sound with these hypotheses stated.

The cancelled extension gamma_n(x+y)=gamma_n(x)+gamma_{n-1}(x)y produces a yy' cross term in the additivity axiom. Its vanishing is an extra condition, acknowledged in the cancelled calculation. The factorial coefficient for iterated divided powers is (ab)!/(b!(a!)^b); the product coefficient is Pascal's identity. The line on scan249 with x^{(p+q+1)} cannot have the required degree; x^{(p+q-1)} is the suggested reading. None of this reinstates the cancelled construction.

## 3. Four adjoints at a finite cutoff

Let S be a commutative nonnegatively graded k-algebra, S_0=k. Let C be all Z-graded S-modules and C_N those with components below N zero. For N'>=N, inclusion i:C_{N'}->C_N has a left adjoint q, quotient by the S-submodule generated by components of degrees below N', and a right adjoint tau, ordinary high-degree truncation. The latter is exact. Its right adjoint rho has component

rho(Q)_a=Hom^a_S(S_{>=N'-a},Q) for a>=N, and zero otherwise.

The module structure is induced by multiplication in S. Restricting to degrees >=N' identifies tau rho Q with Q by evaluation at 1 in the appropriately shifted free module. This proves q ⊣ i ⊣ tau ⊣ rho, including the fully faithful section functor. The functor q need not be left exact: for S=k[t], the injection tS->S is sent by q:C_0->C_1 to tS->0. This is the earlier run's example, rechecked here.

Because i is exact, its right adjoint tau preserves injectives. For a projective M in C_N, the adjunction gives Hom(M,R^r rho(Q))=Ext^r(tau M,Q). In degree a, M is a shift of S and the displayed graded Ext formula follows. These are finite-cutoff assertions; no infinite quotient is needed.

## 4. Binomial rigidity and the nonnegative unit

Take S=Gamma_k(T), with T^{(m)}T^{(n)}=binom(m+n,m)T^{(m+n)}. A degree-a map from a tail into iM=M tensor_k S is a sequence h_n satisfying

binom(j,n) h_j = binom(j+a,n+a) h_n, j>=n in the tail.

For a>=0 the proposed solution is h_n=binom(n+a,a)x. The identity is an integer factorial identity, checked symbolically and by exact arithmetic. Uniqueness and existence can be checked after localizing the underlying abelian groups at every prime ell. Choose j+a=ell^r-1. The coefficients binom(j+a,n+a) and binom(j+a,a) are units in Z_(ell), by the digit criterion for binomial coefficients. The relation recovers x from h_j and forces the stated formula for each fixed n. Compatible choices agree by going to a larger j. Local uniqueness and existence glue because the cokernel and kernel of the integer presentation vanish after every prime localization. Equivalently, the coefficient presentation represents k itself. No division by a nonunit is made. This establishes the degree-zero and weighted positive-degree rigidity in arbitrary k-modules.

It follows that iM -> rho_{0,N} tau_{N,0} iM is an isomorphism in the nonnegative category. In all Z-graded modules this is false: the negative components below give new rational-Hom terms. This ambient-category restriction is essential.

## 5. Exact left adjoint for the one-generator PD case

For a nonnegatively graded S-module P, define

L(P)=(direct sum_{n>=0} P_n)/([T^{(m)}x]_{n+m}-binom(n+m,n)[x]_n).

Maps L(P)->M are exactly compatible maps P->M tensor S, so L ⊣ i. To prove exactness, localize at a prime ell and take j_r=ell^r-1. For every n<=j_r, binom(j_r,n) is a unit. The relation expresses [x]_n as binom(j_r,n)^{-1}[T^{(j_r-n)}x]_{j_r}. Thus

L(P)_(ell)=colim_r P_{j_r,(ell)},

with transition x -> binom(j_{r+1},j_r)^{-1} T^{(j_{r+1}-j_r)}x. The multiplication identity verifies compatibility and all presentation relations. This is a filtered colimit of exact evaluation functors; hence L is exact after every prime localization, and therefore exact. Consequently i preserves injectives. Applying i to an injective resolution of M, then tau (which preserves injectives), and then rho, proves R^r rho tau i(M)=0 for r>0 and the underived unit for r=0. This repairs the special nonnegative PD case by an additional fact; it is not a proof of the manuscript's unrestricted categorical inference.

## 6. Counterexample to the unrestricted derived inference

Let B=k[epsilon]/epsilon^2. Consider the abelian category of triples (V,W,f), where V is a k-vector space, W a B-module, and f:V->W is B-linear when V has trivial epsilon action. The functor tau(V,W,f)=W is exact. Its fully faithful right adjoint is phi(W)=(Hom_B(k,W),W,eval). The exact fully faithful functor i(V)=(V,V,id) has exact right adjoint kappa(V,W,f)=V. Thus phi tau i=i.

Nevertheless R^r phi tau i(k)=(Ext_B^r(k,k),0)=(k,0) for every r>0. The free resolution ...--epsilon-->B--epsilon-->B->k is exact because kernel and image of multiplication by epsilon both equal k epsilon. Applying Hom_B(-,k) gives zero differentials. All claimed formal features survive, but higher vanishing fails. The missing hypothesis is acyclicity for the derived composite, as in Stacks 015M. Likewise, an exact complex k->B->k need not remain exact under Hom_B(k,-): its last cohomology is k. Acyclic cohomology objects do not justify applying the underived functor to an arbitrary representative complex.

## 7. Three negligible classes and a legitimate replacement

Uniformly bounded-above modules form a Serre class but need not be closed under coproducts: the sum of copies of k in degrees n is unbounded. A quotient by this class is not automatically a Grothendieck localization with a section functor. The class of elements annihilated by some sufficiently high tail is more dangerous for non-Noetherian S. In Gamma_{F_p}(T), let I=S_{>0}. The sequence 0->I/I^2->S/I^2->F_p->0 has both outer modules annihilated by I. Yet 1 in S/I^2 is not annihilated by any tail, since T^{(p^r)} survives in I/I^2 for arbitrarily large r. Every product of two positive divided powers of total degree p^r has coefficient binom(p^r,i)=0 mod p. Thus this elementwise class is not extension-closed and does not define the claimed Serre quotient without change.

A precise replacement is the smallest localizing Serre subcategory T containing bounded modules in the nonnegative PD category. The exact left adjoint L kills bounded modules and hence T. If I is injective over k, iI is injective and has Hom(T,iI)=0, so is T-local. Its image under the quotient Q is injective: a monomorphism in the quotient remains monic under its left exact section R, and Hom(-,iI) extends across monomorphisms. Thus Q applied to an i-injective resolution computes R R Q(iM)=iM. This establishes a modified recovery statement; it does not identify T with either literal manuscript class.

## 8. Negative tail components are Hom(Q,M)

For d>0, the recurrence on scan299 represents an abelian group R_d generated by e_i (i>=d), with relations

binom(j,i)e_j=binom(j-d,i-d)e_i, j>=i>=d.

There is a map R_d->Q sending e_i to 1/binom(i,d). It respects the relations by the factorial identity. Fix a prime ell. Choose j_r=ell^r-1+d, for r large. For each fixed i, binom(j_r-d,i-d)=binom(ell^r-1,i-d) is a unit, so e_i becomes a multiple of e_{j_r}. The relation between two successive chosen generators is

e_{j_r}=[binom(j_{r+1},d)/binom(j_r,d)] e_{j_{r+1}}.

The ratio is ell-integral and has valuation one, because v_ell binom(ell^r-1+d,d)=r-v_ell(d) once ell^r>d. Hence the cofinal presentation is a chain of rank-one Z_(ell)-modules with injective transition maps of valuation one; its colimit is Q. To exclude hidden relations, map an arbitrary e_i to the indicated multiple in this colimit; all relations hold by the rational factorial identity, and this constructs the inverse. Therefore R_d localized at every prime is Q, with the displayed map an isomorphism. Kernel and cokernel vanish globally, so R_d=Q.

Consequently the negative component in question is Hom_Z(Q,M), not M tensor Q. It vanishes for M=Z and bounded-torsion M; it is M for a Q-vector space. Evaluation at 1 need not be injective: the map Q->Q/Z is a nonzero homomorphism killing 1. This proves the earlier run's precise computation and rejects the tempting rationalization/subgroup shortcuts.

## 9. Cotensor and intersection

For any counital coalgebra C, the usual cotensor (M tensor C) square_C N is naturally M tensor N, without flatness. The forward map is 1 tensor coaction; the inverse is the restriction of 1 tensor counit tensor 1. The equalizer relation and counit axioms make the composites identity. This checks the ordinary comodule formula. It does not explain the manuscript's NON, which may concern a different category or failed universal construction.

The infinite-intersection interchange on scan283 is not justified by flatness. Let C=Z*1 direct sum Q with Q primitive: Delta(q)=1 tensor q+q tensor1. It is a flat counital cocommutative Z-coalgebra. For P_i=2^i Z in P_0=Z, intersection_i P_i=0, whereas intersection_i(P_i tensor C)=0 direct sum Q. Thus (intersection P_i) tensor C differs from the intersection after tensoring. This refutes the general interchange; it does not prove that the manuscript's particular recursively generated sequence always fails.

The completed-base-change warning is separate. The map (product_n Z) tensor Q -> product_n Q misses (1/n!)_n, whose denominators are unbounded. A completed tensor product may behave differently; the manuscript does not specify it.

## 10. C-valued graded modules, density, and Dold–Kan

Regard a graded ring as a small preadditive category with objects the degrees and Hom(a,b)=S_{b-a}. C-valued graded modules are additive functors into an abelian C. Restriction to the full cutoff subcategory has an enriched right Kan extension if C has small products and kernels. The component is the end of the cotensors [S_{b-a},Q_b], formed as an equalizer of products. A cotensor [A,Q] exists from an abelian-group presentation of A, using products of Q and a kernel. This proves the ordinary graded version of the representability expectation. If 'diffs.' means an additional differential module category, the construction must include the differential relations; the handwriting has not settled that scope.

For a small category D, every presheaf is the colimit of its category of elements. Thus cocontinuous functors Set^{D^op}->A are equivalent to functors D->A for cocomplete A. The universal simplex gives the manuscript's case D=Delta. Its additive version is the free additive cocompletion Ab^{D^op}. This proves the stated universal property at the categorical level, including the fully faithful restriction, but says nothing by itself about homotopy invariance. The expression Hom_Ch(C(X),K) is a group of chain maps, equivalently Z^0 of the internal Hom complex; it is neither that entire complex nor its cohomology.

Dold–Kan uses the full simplex category with degeneracies. The additive idempotent-complete formulation follows by splitting the normalization idempotents in each degree and summing over surjections. It commutes with additive functors preserving those splittings. The ordinary tensor is not transported unchanged: for K=Z[1], N(DK(K) tensor_level DK(K)) has rank one in degree one, while K tensor K has zero degree-one term. There is still a shuffle quasi-isomorphism and a transported symmetric monoidal product; Richter Theorem7.1 states this precisely. For commuting horizontal and vertical differentials, D=d_h+(-1)^p d_v on horizontal degree p has D^2=0, whereas the unsigned sum has square 2d_h d_v. The notes correctly preserve the author's deferred sign convention.

## 11. Convolution divided powers: positive and negative cases

If A is a finite projective counital cocommutative h-coalgebra and (B,J,gamma) a divided-power h-algebra, then Hom_h(A,B)=A^vee tensor B as convolution rings, and Hom_h(A,J)=A^vee tensor J. Since A^vee tensor B is flat over B, Stacks07H1 gives a unique extension of the PD structure. This proves that precise case. Yoneda can identify natural operations with universal maps A->Gamma^n(A); it does not produce these maps, or prove the composition axiom, merely from representability.

The same existence statement extends to every **flat** h-coalgebra A. This refinement was checked during consolidation, after following Lundkvist Proposition2.2(iii) back to SGA4 XVII5.5.2.5. For a flat module the canonical map t_n:Gamma_h^n(A)->(A^{tensor n})^{Sigma_n} is an isomorphism. Its free-module proof uses orbit sums; the flat case follows from filtered colimits of finite free modules because finite invariants and Gamma commute with filtered colimits. Coassociativity and cocommutativity make the n-fold coproduct land in these invariants. Set delta_n=t_n^{-1} Delta^{(n)}, with delta_0=counit and delta_1=id. All needed relations among the delta_n can be checked after the injective maps into tensor powers: the (p,q) split relation is the same iterated coproduct, and sigma_{pq} delta_{pq}=Gamma^p(delta_q)delta_p is the same coproduct with blocks of length q. Flatness of Gamma^q(A) (again the filtered-free description) justifies the latter injection.

Let e_n:Gamma_h^n(J)->J be the h-linear evaluation supplied by the PD algebra structure on J, by the universal property of the free PD algebra. No flatness of J is asserted or used: all injectivity tests in the preceding paragraph are on A and Gamma^q(A), and e_n is then applied by functoriality. For f:A->J define gamma_n^{conv}(f)=e_n Gamma^n(f) delta_n. This is h-linear in its argument a in A, although it is a degree-n polynomial operation in f. The PD identities follow from the split and block relations: addition uses Gamma^n(f+g)'s decomposition into p+q=n; the product identity uses multiplication after the (p,q) coproduct on Gamma, which equals binom(p+q,p); iteration uses the block relation and e_p Gamma^p(e_q) sigma_{pq}=[(pq)!/(p!(q!)^p)]e_{pq}. For convolution multiplication by g:A->B, the same split coproduct identity and the scalar PD axiom give gamma_n(g*f)=g^{*n}*gamma_n(f). Unit and first operation are immediate. These verify all PD axioms, naturally in the coalgebra and PD algebra. No finite-projectivity or passage of tensor through an infinite intersection is used. This proof establishes the flat refinement; it does not claim that the precise Hom theorem was found verbatim in the sources, nor any novelty for the argument.

For unrestricted, nonflat coalgebras the assertion fails. Let A=Z*1 direct sum (Z/2)e, counit(e)=0, Delta(e)=e tensor1+1 tensor e+e tensor e. The element g=1+e is group-like; this verifies coassociativity, and the formula is compatible with 2e=0. Let B=Z direct sum (Z/2)t direct sum (Z/4)u with t^2=2u and tu=u^2=0. On J=(t,u), define gamma_2(at+bu)=a^2u and gamma_n=0 for n>=3. This is well-defined: changing a by two changes a^2u by a multiple of4u. Additivity at n=2 is exactly t^2=2u; all degree>=3 mixed terms vanish. The scalar axiom reduces to the constant scalar squared, and the iteration/product axioms for indices >=2 vanish because u^2=0 and gamma_2(u)=0. The finite audit exhausts J and scalar residues mod4 and verifies all cases through index6; the preceding nilpotence argument covers all remaining indices.

Define f:A->J by f(1)=0, f(e)=t. Then (f*f)(e)=2u nonzero. Every additive g:A->B has 2g(e)=0, so f*f cannot equal 2 gamma_2(f) for any proposed divided powers on Hom(A,J). This refutes even existence in the literal unrestricted formulation. The flat case is covered by the preceding refinement; the remaining useful question is the weakest nonflat hypothesis or additional divided-coproduct structure, not flatness itself.

For the positive-index reading p,q>=1 of its composition relation, the uniqueness subquestion on324 has a separate counterexample, newly checked in this consolidation. The manuscript does not spell out that range here; if q=0 composition is also required, the example below does not satisfy it and no unconditional refutation is claimed. Let h=A=F_2, with the unit coalgebra, and B=Gamma_h(A). On rank-one generators the natural polynomial-law composition map sigma_{pq}:Gamma^{pq}A->Gamma^p(Gamma^q A) sends T^{(pq)} to (T^{(q)})^{(p)}. For SL_2(R)->PGL_2(R), the image is the positive-determinant component PSL_2(R), because multiplying a real matrix by a scalar multiplies its determinant by a positive square. The map is not onto all PGL_2(R). ## 14. The explicit additive-versus-homotopy warnings (306, 311–312)

For a simplicial abelian group $M=\operatorname{DK}(\mathbb Z[1])$,

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{sSet}}(\Delta^0,UM)=M_0=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{sSet}}(\Delta^1,UM)=M_1=\mathbb Z.
\]

Both simplices are contractible, so this raw representable invariant does not preserve their homotopy equivalence. This verifies the warning on306. It does not disprove a derived representable functor or the proposed passage to a category of fractions.

For the warning on311–312, take $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and $S^2\vee S^4$. Their integral homology groups are free of rank one in degrees0,2,4 and zero elsewhere. Their free integral chain complexes are chain-homotopy equivalent to this same zero-differential homology complex: cycles and boundaries in free abelian groups are free; the two short exact sequences split because the relevant quotients (homology and previous boundaries) are free. But the degree-two generator has nonzero square in $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb Z)$, while every such square in the wedge is zero. Thus the additive chain-homotopy invariant does not recover the cup-product ring. This is not a claim that the original normalized simplicial chain functors admit a canonical strict isomorphism. Diagonal and higher coherent data are additional input.

## 15. The degenerate divided-power object on239

The uncertain name \(\Pi_*|K_*(k,p)\) must not be promoted to an ordinary Eilenberg–Mac Lane object. Use the standard simplex quotient \(\Psi_n=k^{n+1}/kT\), with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) represented by the nonzero vertex coordinate functions. On \(\Psi_2\to\Psi_1\), the faces have coefficient rows
\[
 d_0=(-1,1),\qquad d_1=(0,1),\qquad d_2=(1,0).
\]
On the basis \(\gamma_2(e_1),e_1e_2,\gamma_2(e_2)\), a row \((a,b)\) induces \((a^2,2ab,b^2)\). Thus the normalized degree-two kernel is generated by \(e_1e_2\), and \(d_0\) sends it to \(-2\gamma_2(e)\). The normalized degree-one group is \(k\gamma_2(e)\). There are no higher normalized groups: a degree-two monomial has support in at most two positive indices, whereas being killed by every \(d_j\), \(j\geq1\), requires support containing them all. The normalized complex is therefore
\[
 0\longrightarrow k\xrightarrow{-2}k\longrightarrow0
\]
in degrees2 and1. Its homology is \(\ker(2:k\to k)\) in degree2 and \(k/2k\) in degree1. Over \(\mathbb Z\) it is not \(K(\mathbb Z,2)\), and over \(\mathbb Q\) it is acyclic. In particular, the statement that every weight is nonflasque is not valid under this standard interpretation for arbitrary nonzero \(k\). Weight1 is still nontrivial, so this does not restore an unfiltered acyclicity theorem for the full degenerate object.
