## The order of the work

The first packet begins with the title “Complexe DR à p.d. et ombres des modules,” an annotation naming an IHES lecture in1976, and a historical outline. The sequence runs from ordinary de Rham theory through the rational Sullivan construction to an integral divided-power replacement, the recovery of modules from their shadows, and larger homotopy and topos questions. It then develops the binomial arithmetic needed for the shadows. Its interleaved typescript and administrative faces are separate donor material. The second packet develops representable cochains, Stokes integration, the coefficient-sheaf problem, the larger de Rham/Koszul object and test embeddings in a ringed topos. Its final sheet defines special structures and a localization. [Items1–7,17–18,24–36,78–101; A scans2–18,20–45; B scans48–94.]

The date annotation must remain separate from publication history. Cartan's original article says that Grothendieck lectured at IHES on12December1975; it appeared inDecember1976 and acknowledges a Miller preprint in a note added in proof. The handwritten1976 annotation is not erased to make those dates agree. The exact source of the reversed English pages49,51,53 is Sullivan's *Differential Forms and the Topology of Manifolds*, delivered at Tokyo1973 and published in1975. The author-hosted typescript pages6,5,4 match the minimal-model theorem and construction. Other later donor wording differs between supplied sheets and the hosted version, so identification of the source does not imply a word-for-word match for every later leaf. [Items1,77,109; C067–C068; Cartan1976 §7p269; Sullivan1975 author typescriptpp4–6.]

The Néron, Picard, lattice and mixed-Hodge typescripts are excerpts from SGA7I, ExposéIX, published in1972. They supply an earlier line of work, not applications of the new integral PD complex. In particular, a torus denotedT in those pages is not the marked divided-power parameterT. The administrative pages date their own meetings, thesis announcements and reused paper; they do not establish a new lecture date, a Fourier computation or a Chern-class example in the live argument. [Items71–77; claimsC069–C077.]

## The construction and the theorem that was published

The first mathematical repair is explicit. Replacing the barycentric relation ΣX_i=1 by ΣX_i=t, where t belongs to a PD idealJ ofS, makes elimination of one coordinate legitimate in the divided-power algebra. The resulting complex resolvesS objectwise, while a separate augmentation lands inS/J. The author immediately warns that objectwise exactness does not compute ordinary cohomology for every triple(S,J,t). This warning is part of the result, not a gap to fill silently. [Items9–11; C008–C009; A scans6–7, lines106–160.]

The universal case uses S=Γ_k(T). In a consistent notation,

\[
C^{p,q}=\Gamma_k^p\Phi\otimes\Lambda_k^q\Psi,\qquad
w=p+q,\qquad d:(p,q)\longmapsto(p-1,q+1).
\]

The cochain degree isq and the weight isw. Alternation is inq. In particular, T has exterior degree0 and weight1, and T²=2T^[2] overZ. It cannot be treated as an odd alternating element merely because its weight is1. The fixed-weight strand is the complex; the fixed complementary-degree column is not. [Items3,12–15; C010–C012; A scans8–10, lines165–244; B scans80–83, lines1488–1645.]

The positive complementary-degree coefficient objects admit actual simplicial contractions. This is stronger information than ordinary acyclicity and can be carried through the indicated functors. It does not show that a nonlinear divided-power functor preserves arbitrary quasi-isomorphisms. The term with complementary degree0 remains exceptional. Under the stated resolution and section-acyclicity hypotheses, a weightw strand computes H^q(X,k) for0≤q≤w and vanishes abovew. The endpoint is obtained by the truncated-resolution argument; the last term itself need not be acyclic. The later supplied scans227–233 explicitly confirm the contraction, exceptional term and good truncation. [Items13–15; C011–C012; links.tsv; middle C166,C172,C173.]

Cartan1976 §7, especiallyp271, is an exact publication of this weighted comparison and its product factors. Multiplication of representatives in weightsr ands corresponds to binom(r+s,r) times the ordinary cup product. By varying sufficiently large weights, these integer factors have gcd1 and recover the ordinary pairing. This is not a product-preserving identification of one unweighted alternating PD algebra with the integral cohomology ring. The finite first-shadow pairing can already be multiplication by2 in weight2. [Items16,18,58; C013,C015; Cartan1976p271; saved exact computations.]

The later sources have different ranges or structures. Garvín etal.1998, Theorem4.2p176, proves its integration comparison for any simplicial set in the explicit strict rangep<q; Remark3.3(i)p175 shows that its fixed-length object is not(q+1)-extendable. That result must not be used to change Cartan's separate endpoint argument. Cenkl–Porter1982, printedpp10–11, gives binomial product compatibility on cohomology, a DASH/bar-coalgebra extension of integration, and higher wedge-i products. Its integration map is not strictly multiplicative as an algebra map. Their1984 cubical construction instead usesQ_q, inverting primes at mostq, and Theorem4.1p44 gives all-degree comparison for a finite-type simplicial complex with cubical subdivision. None of these is an unrestricted fixed-coefficient strict integral cochain theorem. [C012,C026,C055–C056; bibliography and source-check events.]

## The shadow arithmetic is stronger than some earlier summaries

The finite-tail theorem works for arbitrary abelian groups and arbitrary coefficient rings. If the all-pairs relations

\[
\binom ji(x_j-x_i)=0\quad(j>i\ge N)
\]

hold, the family is constant. The proof localizes at each prime and compares indices to a sufficiently large p^r−1, where all relevant binomial coefficients are units. The sparse auxiliary lemma proves only x_N=x_(N+1); it does not force all its finitely many displayed variables to coincide. An explicitF₂ test distinguishes these statements. The weighted version yields ξ_k=binom(k,i)ξ over an arbitrary abelian group. Prime-local units and finite Bézout combinations replace any illicit division by an integer. The notationZ_p in these arguments means localization, not completion. [Items41–44,53–57; C027–C038; A scans20–34.]

These results establish full faithfulness of M↦ω_N(M) and the nonnegative section unit. They do not make high truncation fully faithful on all graded modules. Inclusion, truncation and the right section are different functors, with adjunctions i_N⊣τ_N⊣ρ_N. A module concentrated just below the cutoff disproves full faithfulness ofτ_N, and a polynomial-ring Hom calculation exposes the reversed adjunction formula. The cutoff for nonnegative homogeneous maps has a strict endpoint: adding one further coefficient equation incorrectly kills a valid map overF₂. [Items44–49,61–64; C030–C032,C040–C042.]

The early Ext corollary is now checked, with its precise target and support. It asserts higher-Ext vanishing from a tail ofS into an induced moduleM⊗_kS in the nonnegative recovery range. Nagpal–Snowden's later divided-power module theory is relevant but has different finite-presentation and completeness hypotheses; its noncomplete-base counterexample usesD/(x), not an induced module, and does not refute the corollary. In this run, an exact coefficient left adjoint supplies a proof for arbitraryk andM. Its presentation becomes, afterℓ-localization, a filtered colimit of degree evaluations atℓ^r−1. Hence the coefficient embedding preserves injectives, and finite-cutoff derived recovery follows. Root and this agent independently checked the late agent's argument. This is a proof reconstructed here, not a claim that an exact later printed theorem was found. [Item52; C035; MP18; proofs.md §§14–15; late/proofs.md §5.]

The infinite quotient remains a separate issue. The literal class of modules whose individual elements are killed by some sufficiently high tail is not extension-closed inΓ_Fp(T)-modules. IfI=S_+, thenI/I² andS/I are negligible in this sense, butS/I² is not: the class of1 is hit byT^[p^r] for arbitrarily larger. The asserted thick subcategory therefore does not define the proposed quotient unchanged. A localizing Serre closure of bounded modules, used in the later reconstruction, is a replacement, not a proof that the literal classes coincide. The remaining coherent product question survives even after linear derived recovery is repaired. [Items50–51,58; C033–C034; MP04,17; late C287.]

Negative-degree maps also require precise language. The intersectionN_∞=∩nN need not be divisible. The presentation withpx=0 andp^ny_n=x gives an element of infinite height without a coherent divisible subgroup. Conversely, the actual p-local recurrence produces compatible roots, which is stronger than infinite height. The full later negative-tail calculation identifies its representing group withQ and its value withHom_Z(Q,M), notM⊗Q. These observations should be integrated without erasing the false literal identificationN_∞=the maximal divisible subgroup. [Items65–69; C043–C047; proofs.md §7; late C239.]

## Integration, coefficients and internal constructions

The second packet's cochain representability uses the full simplex category, including degeneracies. Historical “demi-simplicial” terminology here is not modern face-onlyΔ_inj. The right adjoint to evaluation at[n] has valueM indexed byHom_Δ([n],[p]) in degreep; the opposite orientation in one margin is a reading alarm. Normalized simplex cochains are exterior powers with differential wedge by the all-ones vector. [Items37,79,81–82; C048–C049.]

The Stokes argument constructs a natural additive cochain map by induction on simplex dimension, using objectwise exactness and vanishing above that dimension. A boundary of a boundary is zero because the two orders of deleting a pair of vertices have opposite signs. Cartan1976 Theorem2p266 is the exact published comparison; Theorem3p267 treats multiplicativity on cohomology under additional flat-cycle hypotheses. The manuscript itself leaves multiplicative and higher-homotopy compatibility for investigation. [Items83–84,88–89; C050,C054–C055; MP20,22.]

The arbitrary-presheaf extension is false in the literal simplicial-slice interpretation. For the simplicial circleΔ[1]/∂Δ[1], takeF=k on vertex-degenerate simplices and0 on edge-degenerate simplices. The proposed global-section tensor complex is zero. The right adjoint to evaluation at the vertex embedsF into an injective diagramI withΓI=k andΓ(I/F)=k²; the map is diagonal, soH¹(F)=k. This is a full counterexample to the sentence marked for verification on scan64. It leaves the locally constant coefficient theorem intact. [Item87; C052–C053; B scans60,64; proofs.md §9.]

The larger complexD and the kernel of wedge multiplication bydT must retain their different products. The ambient kernel has square-zero multiplication because(dT)²=0. A nonzero product recovered through a quotient is transported structure, and the author retracts the naive compatibility. The complementary-degree-zero operations and canonical splittings remain explicit additional tasks. [Items34–36,91–94; C025–C026,C057–C060.]

The internal topos construction requires a unit embedding with flat quotient and sufficiently universal acyclicity of divided and exterior powers. Absolute acyclicity in the simplicial presheaf topos does not prove acyclicity in every slice or for every relative direct image. Nor doesk-flatness alone prove universal quasi-isomorphism after tensoring overΓ_k(T). Later scans100 and111–121 sharpen the same program and record a failed flat-plus-flasque contraction argument. The early question attributed to Deligne and the later specific negative answer for a test embedding must remain separate formulations. [Items4,31–33,95–101; C022–C024,C061–C066; MP11–13,24–28.]

## The larger homotopy questions today

The integral homotopy and minimal-model ambitions are real authorial questions. They are not settled by citing a later theorem with a different target. Mandell2006 classifies nilpotent finite-type homotopy types by integralE∞cochains; the functor is faithful and not full. Horel's binomial-ring model gives a fully faithful construction for connected nilpotent finite-type spaces, with a separate completion statement more generally. Ekedahl supplies minimal numerical spaces with numerical maps and homotopy uniqueness. None of the opened sources identifies its category with the manuscript's virtual PD category, its chosen weak equivalences, or all the proposed operations. [Items22–28,36; C018–C021,C026; MP06–10,14; Mandell2006pp213–214; Horel2024 author Theorems5.4/6.1p13; Ekedahl2002 arXivTheorem2.9.]

Two elementary obstructions delimit only strengthened readings. An unweighted alternatingF₂algebra cannot reproduce the ordinary odd square inH*(BC₂;F₂). A PD structure on the ordinary integral cohomology ideal ofCP² would require its primitive degree4 generator to be twice an integral class. The weighted theory avoids both imposed identifications. The earlier notes were right to use these as scope tests; they would overstate matters if they used them against Cartan's actual theorem. [Items8,106,111; proofs.md §11.]

The later *Pursuing Stacks* passages provide useful retrospective and conceptual links, but distinguish several approaches. Its remarks about nonlinearΓ, coefficient sheaves and a binomial/unipotent example cannot be transferred as automatic counterexamples to the exact weighted construction. The project/run names cited in the notes remain context leads in a separate table. Their target files were not attached; no mathematical implication or completed project result is certified from those names. [Items107–110; links.tsv and project_links.tsv.]

## Source-level defects and the remaining ink questions

Two donor alarms survive collation with the published original. SGA7IX12.3.10 printedp472 literally gives an incidence targetZ^(I×J), whereas the normalization calculation requiresZ^I⊕Z^J. On a nodal triangle the former map has kernel rank0 and the latter rank1. The following printed formula itself uses pairs(0,C_i), corroborating the direct sum. The graph interpretation also retains the preceding at-most-two-branches scope; arbitrary multiple branches require the bipartite incidence graph. [C075; B scans57,59; originalPDF477–478; exact graph matrices saved.]

The filtration at SGA7IX13.4.6 printedp494 and the toric concentration statement atp498 use conflicting extreme indices. Both are visible in the published source. Modern decreasing-index reconstruction reverses the two extremes, but the supplied transcription remains unchanged. The Picard-to-Néron theorem likewise requires its actual published setup: a strictly local trait, proper flat relative curve, factorial local rings,f_*O_X=O_S, andd^t=1 in12.1(b). Its printed hypothesis was visually checked; merely saying “regular and cohomologically flat” loses a condition over imperfect residue fields. [C070,C076; originalPDF471,499,503.]

Other recorded alarms concern the diagonal arrow, repeatedH⁰, positive versus zero complementary degree, binomial subscripts, a missing nonzero sign, negative-degree notation, the missing slice/X, and the degree-zero exclusion in the final special-structure definition. They are mathematical reasons to inspect the ink, never permission to change it without a scan. All precise locators and dependencies are in readings.tsv.

The early evidence therefore contains both established mathematics and genuine unfinished work. The weighted comparison, arithmetic recovery and finite derived theorem are much more precise than a general slogan about integral differential forms. The remaining tasks are the correct infinite category, coherent multiplication and operations, general coefficient/slice hypotheses, and an exact comparison with a homotopy model. Negative literature searches identify the limits of this run; they do not establish novelty.
