Checked against the supplied diplomatic text, without scans, on 19 November 2026. Model: GPT-6 Astra Pro; effort not exposed; run SA02 cotes90_91. ## V01. The module equivalence on 90/2 is false

Let A=Z, K=Q and M=Σ_p (1/p)Z⊂Q, with p ranging over all primes. This is a countably generated torsionfree, hence flat, A-module. Its elements have squarefree denominators: a finite sum has denominator dividing a product of distinct primes, and Bézout's identity gives 1/(p_1⋯p_r) in the span of 1/p_1,…,1/p_r. Consequently M_(p)=p^−1 Z_(p), a free rank-one local module. It contains no copy of Q as an A_(p)-module. It also contains no nonzero A-submodule isomorphic to Z[1/p]: any homomorphism from this rank-one subgroup of Q to Q is multiplication by its value at 1, and a nonzero rational multiple of Z[1/p] has unbounded p-denominators, whereas all elements of M have p-valuation at least −1. This verifies both of the manuscript's exclusion conditions, independently of the ambiguous local-module adjective.

The saturation of Z in M is all M because M/Z is torsion. M is not finitely generated: any finite collection of rationals involves finitely many denominator primes, and 1/q for a new prime is not in their span. Indeed M/Z≅⊕_p Z/pZ, of infinite length. Thus conditions (b),(c) do not imply (a),(d), even in the countable case. Every homomorphism M→Z is zero: its value at 1 must be divisible by each prime, hence is zero, and torsionfreeness of Z then forces it to vanish on all 1/p. This does not contradict the separate sufficient condition that linear forms separate points.

A valid refinement is: finite saturation (a) is equivalent to being the directed union of finite saturated submodules (d). If (a) holds, saturate each finite submodule; if (d) holds, each finite set lies in one stage and its saturation is a submodule of that finite stage, hence finite over the noetherian base. Separating A-linear forms imply (a): on the finite-dimensional K-span of a finite submodule N, select finitely many forms whose extensions jointly inject; the saturation of N embeds in A^r and so is finite. If M is countably generated and satisfies (a), successively saturate finite generating sets. The resulting finite projective modules M_n have torsionfree finite quotients M_(n+1)/M_n, which are projective over a Dedekind domain. The inclusions therefore split; M is a direct sum of finite projective modules. This is stronger than merely possessing separating linear forms. No converse for arbitrary uncountable modules is asserted here.

## V02. Saturating a subcoalgebra; finite linear representation

Let A be a Dedekind domain, B a flat A-coalgebra, N⊂B a finite subcoalgebra, and Ñ=(K N)∩B its saturation. Flatness of the torsionfree modules B and B/Ñ gives the tensor intersections
(Ñ⊗B)∩(B⊗Ñ)=Ñ⊗Ñ inside B⊗B.
For x∈Ñ choose nonzero a with ax∈N. Both maps from Δ(x) into (B/Ñ)⊗B and B⊗(B/Ñ) are killed by a; their targets are torsionfree, so they vanish. Hence Δ(x) belongs to the displayed intersection. The counit is inherited. Finiteness of Ñ is an additional hypothesis and cannot be deduced from the false exclusion criterion V01. If B is an algebra of finite type, first choose finitely many algebra generators; a finite stable submodule containing them yields a representation faithful after any base change: a point acting trivially on those generators acts trivially on all B, and the coaction/counit recovers the group point. This proves a closed linear realization when the generating module is finite projective; it does not make its image all of GL(N).

For B=A[t,t^−1], N=A·1⊕A·t⊕A·t^−1, the action is diagonal diag(1,z^−1,z) up to inverse convention. The coordinate function x_12 on GL_3 is nonzero and maps to zero. A faithfully flat ring map is injective. Thus the cancelled assertion on 90/14 is false. C02 verifies the matrix and determinant.

## V03. The unrestricted resolution assertion

Take S=X=Spec(k), G the trivial group. Every finite k-module has a finite free cover, so the written covering hypothesis holds. The module E=⊕_(n≥0)k is quasi-coherent and equivariant but is not a quotient of any finite-rank free module: the image of k^r has dimension at most r. This refutes the literal “every module” on 90/16, while leaving the finite-type assertion on 90/10–12 intact.

For the disappearing-component example let R=k[t]_(t), K=k(t). The open subgroup of the constant two-element group obtained by retaining Spec(R) in the identity component and Spec(K)=D(t) in the other is a group scheme: the product of two nonidentity points lies over D(t) in the identity component, and the other products and inverse are restrictions of the constant group law. It is affine, flat and finitely presented, with algebra R×K. Its nonidentity component misses the special fibre, the saturation of 0×R is 0×K and is not finite. The open subgroup consisting of the identity component agrees with G on the closed fibre but not globally. Thus this one example violates both the finite-saturation property and condition (R); it is consistent with, not a refutation of, the finite-type component criterion on 90/10.

## V04. Conditional Hom-square and obstruction relations

Let V→U be a surjective A-linear map and J→J_t an injective map. Then
Hom(U,J) → Hom(V,J) ×_(Hom(V,J_t)) Hom(U,J_t)
is bijective. An element on the right is a map a:V→J and b:U→J_t with a mapping to b after composition. On ker(V→U), the image of a becomes zero in J_t; injectivity forces it to be zero in J, so a descends uniquely to U. The converse is immediate. This proves the group identity used in the earlier notes. A torsor under either group can nevertheless be empty: the identity does not by itself establish existence of a deformation or supply the compatibilities admitted on 91/19–21.

For a presentation with compatibility i(l)=β(m), the relations imposed by a functional ρ:L⊕M′→J come from ρ(ker(i,−β)). The images of ker(i) and ker(β) considered separately need not generate these relations. Example over Q: i=β=id_Q, so both separate kernels vanish; ker(i,−β) is the diagonal and ρ(l,m)=l maps it onto Q. C03 certifies the rank calculation. This is an alarm for the quotient written on 91/24–25, not a claim that every ambiguous symbol has been deciphered.

## V05. Separatedness in the section theorem

In the assertion on 91/26 set X=S=Spec(k), Z_1=X, and let P be the affine line with doubled origin. X/S is proper, flat and finitely presented. The identity Z_1→X satisfies normal-flatness and associated-point detection requirements. The local Weil restriction along the identity is P itself, hence a scheme. The section functor is P, which is not separated over k: its diagonal is not closed. Therefore separatedness of the section scheme requires an additional separatedness hypothesis on P or another condition implying it. The 11 September 1967 letter at 91/32 explicitly assumes separatedness of Y. This check does not establish that a struck marginal clause belongs to one particular hypothesis.

## V06. Weil restriction: dimension and finite étale degree

For B=k[ε]/ε² and X=A¹_B, a B-point over a k-algebra C is a+bε with a,b∈C. Hence Res_(B/k)X=A²_k. The base rank is n=2, X/B has pure relative dimension d=1, and the restriction has dimension nd=2. This refutes D(s)=nD′(s) as transcribed on 91/52 when D denotes the original dimension and D′ that of the restriction. The following pure-dimension sentence gives the correct direction. If fibres have components of differing dimension, the set of dimensions of a product is a sum-set; no uniform scalar equality of dimension sets follows without equal-dimension hypotheses.

Over an algebraically closed field, a finite k-scheme T is a finite disjoint union of local Artin schemes. A finite étale T-scheme of constant degree λ is a disjoint union of λ copies on each connected local component. Its sections form a finite set of λ^(#|T|) elements, represented by the corresponding finite étale k-scheme. For varying bases, constant rank of the resulting finite étale object requires the relevant component-count function to be locally constant; the manuscript itself adds a local-constancy condition in its subsequent statement. This argument depends on T being finite. If the cancellation on 91/54 leaves T merely of finite type, take T an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not two, and take g=[2]:T→T. It is finite étale of degree four, while it admits no section: a section would be a nonconstant map of proper integral curves and 4·deg(section)=1. The section scheme is empty, contradicting the formula 4^1. This is a scope counterexample, not a settled reading.

## V07. Constructible maps do not commute with all inverse limits

Use the finite-stratification notion on 91/89–97. Let k be an infinite algebraically closed field, T=A¹_k, and choose distinct a_1,a_2,…∈k. For each n, let f_n:T→A^n be the tuple of characteristic functions of {a_1},…,{a_n}. Each is a constructible map: use these n reduced closed points and their open complement as strata. The projection A^(n+1)→A^n takes f_(n+1) to f_n.

Suppose the compatible family came from a constructible map T→lim A^n. A finite locally closed stratification would contain a stratum through the generic point and therefore a nonempty open subset U. Each coordinate of the map restricted to U is a regular function taking only values zero or one and generically zero; because U is integral, it must vanish identically. But U contains all but finitely many a_i, and the ith coordinate is one at a_i. Contradiction. Thus surjectivity of the comparison Hom map fails. No claim is made that the correct absolute-flat or pro-constructible envelope has the same obstruction: those enlarge the source or morphism category and are separately constructed on 91/115–120.

## V08. Submodule reciprocity on the reused typed leaf

Take the two-dimensional solvable Lie algebra over Q with basis h,e and [h,e]=e. Let N=Q², with h acting by H=diag(1,0) and e by E=[[0,1],[0,0]]. These satisfy the Lie relation. Let M_1 be the one-dimensional character h↦1,e↦0 and M_2 the trivial character. Then M_1 is the stable line spanned by the first basis vector in M_2⊗N=N.

On M_1⊗N*, h acts by I−H^t=diag(0,1), and e acts by −E^t. Their common kernel is zero (C05 gives exact matrices and rank). Therefore the trivial M_2 does not embed in M_1⊗N*. The submodule/submodule equivalence transcribed at 91/90 exercise 4(b) is false for general finite-dimensional representations. The canonical adjunction instead gives a nonzero map M_1⊗N*→M_2, which is surjective when M_2 is simple; semisimplicity would permit converting a quotient into a submodule. The ink may say “quotient,” or preceding hypotheses may matter. The typed leaf is attributed as reused Bourbaki material, not established as an original Grothendieck claim.

## V09. Laurent units and infinitesimal poles

Let R contain ε≠0 with ε²=0. For every m>0, f=1+εt^−m is a unit of R((t)), with inverse 1−εt^−m. The exact product is 1−ε²t^−2m. Thus nilpotent negative coefficients occur with arbitrarily large pole order, while over a reduced ring the negative part of a unit is controlled locally by a valuation and the invertible leading coefficient. This verifies that the reduced components Z×L^+G_m do not exhaust the full functor on nonreduced rings. It also checks why the bounded-pole stages can have the same reduced underlying components yet acquire larger nilpotent thickenings. The lower cutoff on 91/134 must be −n for this particular Γ_n definition; the source's i<n is retained in the reading ledger pending a scan check.

## V10. Nilpotent polynomial substitutions and truncated jets

If an ideal m⊂A has m^(N+1)=0, a formal power series evaluated on m^n equals its degree-N truncation. A formal substitution with zero constant term and invertible linear matrix has a unique formal inverse, constructed successively by homogeneous degree; on m^n both maps become finite polynomials. Allowing a nilpotent constant term can still define maps of nilpotent points after choosing the appropriate class of test rings, but it is not automatically an automorphism of a fixed truncated algebra. In A=Q[ε]/ε², the substitution t↦t+ε does not preserve the relation t²=0 in A[t]/t²: its square is 2εt≠0 (C07). The determinant of a linear matrix over a general ring must be a unit, not merely nonzero, for invertibility. C08 recomputes every coefficient of the quadratic composition written on 91/142.

## V11. A noncommutative formal group with abelian Lie algebra

Over characteristic p>0 define F((x_1,x_2),(y_1,y_2))=(x_1+y_1+x_1y_1, x_2+y_2(1+x_1^p)). The first coordinate is the multiplicative formal group in the coordinate 1+x_1. The action factor 1+x_1^p=(1+x_1)^p is multiplicative because Frobenius is a ring homomorphism in characteristic p. Therefore this is an associative semidirect-product formal group, with identity (0,0) and inverse obtained using units 1+x_1 and 1+x_1^p. Its antisymmetric second-coordinate term is x_1^p y_2−y_1^p x_2, nonzero of degree p+1. There is no antisymmetric quadratic term, so its tangent Lie algebra is abelian although the formal group is not commutative. C09 gives exact polynomial certificates for p=2,3,5; the Frobenius identity is the general proof.

## V12. Classical calculations on the reused typed leaves

The general identity (ad x)^n(y)=Σ_i(−1)^i binom(n,i)x^(n−i)yx^i follows by applying the commuting operators L_x and R_x and expanding (L_x−R_x)^n. For prime p in characteristic p the intermediate coefficients vanish, giving (ad x)^p=ad(x^p). The polarization indices on 91/85 must be audited separately; no coefficient is filled in from this identity alone. C11 verifies the free-word formula through degree seven as an implementation check.

For the general linear group, identify the tangent matrix E_ij with the left-invariant field X_ij=Σ_k(δ_ki+U_ki)∂/∂U_kj. Applying the derivation rule to coordinate functions proves [X_ij,X_kl]=δ_jk X_il−δ_li X_kj. The restricted p-operation is matrix pth power: E_ij^[p]=0 for i≠j and E_ii^[p]=E_ii. C10 checks all sixteen GL_2 brackets. The field formula and its index order are thus independently checked; a field-characteristic or bilinear-form assumption is not dropped in the Cayley-transform passage.

For exterior algebras, the universal property gives Λ(M⊕N)≅Λ(M)⊗^grΛ(N), where the graded tensor product uses the Koszul sign. Alternation is preserved by the map m+n↦m⊗1+1⊗n and the inverse is induced by the two inclusions. Taking degree r yields ⊕_(i+j=r)Λ^iM⊗Λ^jN. The splitting and ideal-kernel statements are consequences of this explicit map; they do not depend on a dimension count or field hypothesis. C12 checks the free finite-rank dimension identity only as a consistency test.

## V13. Pullback and intersections: do not change the definition

At 91/39 the author explicitly defines the infimum of quasi-coherent submodules affinely from the module intersection; that operation must be distinguished from the intersection in all sheaves. For a DVR R with parameter t, the sheaves (t^n) on Spec(R) have literal sheaf intersection whose generic stalk is K and whose closed stalk is zero, hence not quasi-coherent, while the quasi-coherent infimum associated to ∩_n t^nR is zero. This is not a counterexample to the author's defined operation. It explains why a proof cannot commute arbitrary localization with infinite intersections without an extra argument. Similarly, ∩M_n=0 does not by itself imply the m-adic convergence invoked by a Chevalley argument over an arbitrary noncomplete noetherian local ring; completeness or a proven substitute must be supplied. The exact proper-flat intersection statement is left as a proof-audit problem, with later purity/corepresentability results distinguished from a checked proof of this particular argument.

## V14. Other structural checks

Finite-stage comodules: if B=colim B_i with the stipulated saturated finite projective stages, the image of a coaction on finitely many generators is contained in one B_i⊗M; the tensor injections and coassociativity give a B_i-comodule. For finite projective B_i, evaluation against B_i^∨ converts the coaction bijectively into a module action, and the counit corresponds to the identity. A finite module over B_i^∨ is a quotient of a finite free B_i^∨-module, which is finite projective over the base. These arguments need the stated stage hypotheses; flatness alone is insufficient.

The stable envelope for a projective coalgebra is the image of the contraction map C^∨⊗F→E. A projective dual basis proves both stability and minimality, and a finite generating set for F produces finitely many coefficients. Over merely flat coalgebras the same argument is unavailable. The revised SGA3 text gives explicit counterexamples and carefully distinguishes its later extensions from the original free-coalgebra proposition.

The open locus where a map V→U of finitely presented modules is surjective is the complement of Supp(coker), equivalently a determinantal open in a finite presentation. This independently verifies the open-locus step at 91/17, conditional on the required finite modules and their compatibility with base change. It does not establish the whole relative Hom theorem.

For finite G-sets, a transitive G-set H\G admits an equivariant map to K\G precisely when a conjugate of H is contained in K, and the maps correspond to the resulting K-cosets; this verifies the elementary orbit/transport interpretation in the relevant manuscript passage. General reconstruction of a profinite group from its finite-set category requires the fibre functor and cannot be inferred from finite-limit preservation alone without the extra hypotheses under discussion.

## V15. A left-exact endofunctor need not be an internal-Hom functor

Take a nontrivial finite group G and let F:G-FinSet→G-FinSet forget the action and then give the underlying set the trivial action. Both steps preserve finite limits, so F is left exact. Suppose F were naturally isomorphic to the usual internal-Hom functor E↦E^A for a finite G-set A. On a two-element trivial G-set, cardinalities give 2=2^|A|, hence |A|=1. Such A has trivial action and E^A≅E with its original action, while F sends the regular G-set to a trivial G-set of the same cardinality. These are not isomorphic G-sets. Thus the blanket sentence on 91/58 is false under this standard internal-Hom interpretation. If the underlined Hom denotes a different construction or additional compatibility is intended, that notation and those hypotheses must first be recovered from the scans. The correct general pro-representability theorem for a set-valued left-exact functor is a different statement.

## V16. The locally-finite-type connected-component question has a direct negative answer

The target P/X in the theorem preceding 91/27 is only locally of finite presentation. Take X=Spec(k), and let P be an infinite nodal chain of projective lines over k. One explicit construction uses affine charts U_i=Spec(k[x_i,y_i]/(x_i y_i)), i∈Z, and glues D(x_i)⊂U_i to D(y_(i+1))⊂U_(i+1) by y_(i+1)=x_i^−1. Distinct nonadjacent charts are disjoint. Each U_i is of finite presentation, and the adjacent overlap G_m is affine; the map from the tensor product of adjacent coordinate rings to k[t,t^−1] is surjective (x_i↦t,y_(i+1)↦t^−1), so the diagonal is closed and P is separated. The irreducible components are copies of P¹ meeting consecutive components at a node. P is connected, and the cover by the U_i has no finite subcover, so P is not quasi-compact and not of finite type over k.

For X=Z_1=Spec(k), the source is proper, flat and finitely presented, normal-flatness along Z_1 is automatic, associated points are detected, and the local section functor is P itself. Thus its sole connected component is not of finite type, even after adding separatedness to the target. This settles the question literally written under the locally-finite-type hypothesis. It does not settle the stronger and more interesting version with P of finite type over X; the latter is recorded separately as a boundedness question. A Hilbert-scheme example about a proper nonprojective threefold does not by itself settle a section/Hom question and is not used as if it did.
