## Substantive corrections established during reading

1. The equivalence in 90/2, Lemma 1, is false even for a countably generated flat module over the nonfield Dedekind domain Z. The subgroup M=sum_p Z/p of Q, where Z/p here means (1/p)Z and p runs through primes, has bounded p-height one at every prime, but the saturation of Z inside M is all of M and is not finitely generated. Thus absence of a divisible local submodule does not imply finite saturation globally. The notation M=sum_p (1/p)Z will be used throughout the actual proof to avoid confusing it with residue groups.
2. The proof of the relative unramified Hom theorem does continue beyond the Pro package: Codex 91/19–25 supplies two further obstruction constructions. The first explicitly admits compatibility/naturality equalities; the second rewrites the obstruction as a module quotient. Accordingly the earlier note's claim that the supplied text stops at 91/18 must be corrected, without promoting the continuation to a complete proof. The letter to Anantharaman is dated 11 September 1967 at 91/31 and includes separatedness on 91/32.
3. The inverse-limit question for constructible maps at 91/97 has a negative answer for arbitrary affine-transition targets. On A1 over an infinite algebraically closed field, the indicators of distinct closed points give compatible constructible maps to A^n; they require unboundedly many strata and do not define a constructible map to the infinite product in the finite-stratification sense of these pages.
4. The dimension relation at 91/52 reverses the factor n; the following proof and the pure-dimension special case determine the mathematically consistent direction. The degree formula at 91/54 depends essentially on the cancelled finiteness hypothesis for T/S. An elliptic-curve multiplication-by-two cover gives no section although its degree is four.
5. The theorem at 91/26 cannot conclude separatedness of the section scheme without an appropriate separatedness assumption on its target: X=S=Spec(k), with P the doubled affine line, is a direct scope test. This is a statement-level alarm, not an adjudication of the ink.
