N2039: ### 10.2. Detailed sign and splitting calculation supporting E170.4–E170.5
N2040: 
N2041: This is a research reconstruction under explicit conventions, not missing text supplied to the edition. Let the base be a scheme or ringed space with commutative structure sheaf, let both extensions be locally split, and interpret the live splitting equations on scan 170 as
N2042: 
N2043: \[
N2044: 0\longrightarrow L_i\xrightarrow{\alpha_i}E_i\xrightarrow{\psi_i}M_i\longrightarrow0,
N2045: \quad \alpha_i\pi_i+\varpi_i\psi_i=\chi\mathrm{id}_{E_i},\quad
N2046: \pi_i\alpha_i=\chi\mathrm{id}_{L_i},\quad\psi_i\varpi_i=\chi\mathrm{id}_{M_i}.
N2047: \]
N2048: 
N2049: Choose local splittings \(s_i:M_i\to E_i\), write \(E_i=L_i\oplus M_i\), and put \(\tau_i=\pi_i s_i\). Then
N2050: 
N2051: \[
N2052: \pi_i(l,m)=\chi l+\tau_i m,\qquad
N2053: \varpi_i(m)=(-\tau_i m,\chi m).
N2054: \]
N2055: 
N2056: Use the ordered four tensor summands \((L_1L_2,L_1M_2,M_1L_2,M_1M_2)\), with juxtaposition denoting tensor product. The pushout map from the first three summands to \(L_1L_2\) is \((a,b,c)\mapsto\chi a+(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau_2)b+(\tau_1\otimes\mathrm{id})c\), so a local splitting of the pushout product is the image of \(s_1\otimes s_2\). The pullback product has a local splitting whose image in \(E_1E_2\) is
N2057: 
N2058: \[
N2059: w=\chi(s_1\otimes s_2)-(\tau_1\otimes s_2)-(s_1\otimes\tau_2),
N2060: \]
N2061: 
N2062: where the last two terms use the inclusions of the respective \(L_i\). This element has mixed and quotient coordinates \((-\tau_1,-\tau_2,\chi)\), exactly the pair prescribed by \((\varpi_1\otimes\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id}\otimes\varpi_2)\).
N2063: 
N2064: For new splittings \(s'_i=s_i+u_i\), one has \(\tau'_i=\tau_i+\chi u_i\). The pushout splitting changes by
N2065: 
N2066: \[
N2067: t=u_1\otimes\tau_2+\tau_1\otimes u_2+\chi u_1\otimes u_2,
N2068: \]
N2069: 
N2070: whereas expansion of the displayed \(w'\) shows that the pullback splitting changes by \(-t\). Thus, with the same identified end objects, the two resulting extension cocycles are opposite. A local comparison fixing the ends and sending the chosen pushout splitting to the pullback splitting plus \(k\) glues precisely when \(k'-k=2t\); for \(k=\delta\tau_1\otimes\tau_2\), this is the residual \((\delta\chi-2)t\) checked in the script.
N2071: 
N2072: For the concrete case \(\chi=0\), choose \(L_i=M_i=\mathcal O_X\), \(\tau_i=1\), and any locally free extension \(E_i\) of \(\mathcal O_X\) by itself. The globally defined maps \(\pi_i=\psi_i\) and \(\varpi_i=-\alpha_i\) satisfy the displayed splitting identities, and the inclusions are universally injective because the quotient is locally free. If the first extension has a nonzero class \(e\in H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\) and the second is split, the computed classes are \(e\) and \(-e\). On a smooth elliptic curve over a characteristic-zero field the vector space \(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\) is one-dimensional (The Stacks Project Authors, n.d., Tags 0B3A, 072J and 0BY6), so such an \(e\) exists and is not killed by two; hence the two extensions are not isomorphic with both end identifications fixed. Sending the subobject by \(-\mathrm{id}\), however, removes the obstruction. This exact dependence on endpoints is why the example cannot be reported as a refutation of Hua’s whole ellipsized entry or of an unspecified sign-twisted comparison.

N2074: ### 10.3. Detailed local χ-groupoid calculation supporting R11–R13
N2075: 
N2076: **Status:** an editorial calculation made after the transcription freeze. It neither changes doubtful readings nor supplies the missing global hypotheses. Sources motivating it are cote 84, scans 146, 164, 168, and 170; it bears on Hua, findings register, 84-chi-trivialised-extension-product, as quoted in the supplied RESULTS_STATE register.
N2077: 
N2078: Let R be any commutative ring, χ∈R, and fix the endpoints L=M=R. A split extension E=Rℓ⊕Rx with α(1)=ℓ and ψ(x)=1 has a χ-trivialisation of the displayed complementary form
N2079: 
N2080: \[
N2081: \pi=(\chi,\tau),\qquad \varpi=\binom{-\tau}{\chi}.
N2082: \]
N2083: 
N2084: Thus απ+ϖψ=χ id, πα=χ, ψϖ=χ and πϖ=0. In a new splitting x′=x+aℓ, the coordinate changes by τ′=τ+χa; the script checks conjugation of both endomorphisms απ and ϖψ. This convention is a choice for the calculation, not a silent resolution of every manuscript sign.
N2085: 
N2086: Define a groupoid Gχ(R) whose objects are τ∈R and whose arrows a:τ→τ+χa compose by addition. As an actual map between the two represented extensions, this arrow has matrix F=[[1,−a],[0,1]]: it satisfies π_target F=π_source and Fϖ_source=ϖ_target; the change-of-basis matrix instead has +a. Arrows remain distinct when χa=0, so this is a groupoid with automorphisms Ann_R(χ), not merely the set R/χR. Put
N2087: 
N2088: \[
N2089: \tau\star\sigma=\tau\sigma,
N2090: \qquad
N2091: (\tau,a)\star(\sigma,b)
N2092: =(\tau\sigma,\,a\sigma+\tau b+\chi ab).
N2093: \]
N2094: 
N2095: The target equation is
N2096: 
N2097: \[
N2098: (\tau+\chi a)(\sigma+\chi b)
N2099: =\tau\sigma+\chi(a\sigma+\tau b+\chi ab).
N2100: \]
N2101: 
N2102: The script checks compatibility with composition, strict associativity, symmetry, identity arrows and the unit object 1 as polynomial identities over Z[χ,τ,σ,…]. For example, both triple products have arrow coordinate
N2103: 
N2104: \[
N2105: a\sigma\rho+\tau b\rho+\tau\sigma c
N2106: +\chi(ab\rho+a\sigma c+\tau bc)+\chi^2abc.
N2107: \]
N2108: 
N2109: An object τ is invertible up to isomorphism precisely when there exist σ,a with τσ+χa=1: this is exactly the ideal condition (τ,χ)=R, or the statement that τ mod χ is a unit in R/χR. Necessity follows because an isomorphism of τσ with 1 is such an arrow, and sufficiency gives the inverse object σ and the required arrow directly; commutativity supplies the inverse on the other side. Multiplication by τ on Ann_R(χ) is then invertible as well: if τσ+χa=1, multiplication by σ is its inverse on that annihilator.
N2110: 
N2111: This calculation gives a concrete local mechanism behind the context's coefficient-product and invertibility clauses. To pass to varying line bundles and a scheme one must construct the relevant stack/descent data, identify exactly the category of allowed morphisms, and verify that the manuscript's global pushout product induces this local law. It says nothing by itself about whether the separately drawn pullback product agrees through a map inducing the identity on both endpoints; the χ=0 sign test in the early-page analysis concerns that distinct question.
