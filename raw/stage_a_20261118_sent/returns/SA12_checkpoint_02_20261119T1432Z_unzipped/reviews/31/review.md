# Cote 31: consolidation review

## Flat and Galois cohomology: the coefficient category matters

The opening pages define two dimensions using finite faithfully flat coverings, one ranging over all relevant sheaves and another over finite commutative group schemes. The inclusion supplies an inequality, but the text does not prove equality of the two definitions or identify its site with a modern big fppf site. This remains an identification problem, CL-SA12-401/MP-SA12-101. Milne's discussion of Shatz's results explains why the all-sheaf quantifier matters; the original Shatz article was located and its metadata opened, but its full text was unavailable. That limitation is explicit rather than hidden behind the later citation. The torus criterion is correspondingly a qualified match, not a fully identified theorem with silently modernized topology. [MilneADT III introduction; Shatz1966; SGA3IX.]

The later five-criterion argument in scans47–51 is an unfinished proof programme. Several steps are classical or published subsequently: finite-coefficient dévissage, filtered continuous cohomology, the perfect-field structure of finite and smooth commutative groups, Kummer theory, Artin–Schreier theory and Tsen's theorem. They require distinct hypotheses. MilneAG2017 proposition11.3 gives the finite connected–étale splitting over a perfect field; theorem14.54 requires smoothness and connectedness for the exponent-p vector-group assertion. Nonlinear additive-polynomial automorphisms show why a classification of groups does not imply that all automorphisms are linear. [CL-SA12-461–472; MilneAG2017 11.3,14.54,16.13–16.20.]

The degree-one torsion claim is a substantive internal correction. A quadratic character over C(t) is nonzero in H¹ of its roots of unity but becomes a coboundary in the multiplicative group of a separable closure. The correct comparison is an isomorphism from degree2 onward, with an explicit degree1 kernel. The manuscript later narrows the range to degrees greater than1. The saved proof checks both the failure and the correction. A separate determinant computation isolates the unique C₂ exception in the elementary-abelian regular-representation argument; adding an inverse-determinant character repairs the embedding but changes its dimension. [CL-SA12-464,468,524; outputs/calculations.json.]

## Multiplicative-type groups: close draft-to-print correspondences

The rigidity, central-homomorphism, character-scheme, Hom/Isom and isotriviality pages have close SGA3 matches. Exact assumptions about finite presentation, flatness, connectedness, unibranchness and clopen pieces remain in the records. The cancelled subgroup formulation has a published analogue in SGA3IX5.1bis, so cancellation alone does not establish its invalidity. The countable finite-clopen converse requires the qualification recorded in the edited SGA3X discussion; the source's broad wording is not treated as settled simply because the neighboring theorem is standard. [CL-SA12-408–417; SGA3IX2–5; SGA3X5.1–5.16, especially5.11bis and editorial footnote44.]

The density page is another useful historical distinction. Its second alternative includes retrocompactness. The printed SGA3IX4.6 states the more general result, assumes retrocompactness for a simplified proof, and points to EGA for removing it. The draft must therefore retain its own hypothesis. Likewise, SGA3IX4.10 supplies precise conditions for the density of a cofinal m-power torsion family; it does not license every selection of torsion orders. [CL-SA12-503,505; MP-SA12-146.]

## Local cohomology, formal geometry and comparison boundaries

The old cofinite-module example uses a Hom-finiteness convention. Its explicit shift construction has finite annihilator but an infinitely generated quotient; under the later Ext-based definition it is not cofinite. The computation separates the historical terminology from a modern statement that would otherwise appear contradictory. This is not an ink correction. [CL-SA12-418–421; SGA2; TakahashiWakasugi definition2.2 and example2.4.]

The formal-geometric pages separate full faithfulness, algebraization and algebraization by a locally free sheaf. SGA2 XII gives strong exact comparisons, but the boundary degrees and the extra local-freeness conclusion must be matched separately. The nonproper Picard expectation remains broader than the sources located. Murre1964 gives a seven-axiom representability criterion, while its stated Picard application is proper. It is therefore a useful diagnostic source, not a solution to the unqualified nonproper programme. The exact EGAIII2 erratum restores the extra finite-generation condition the correction slip requests. [CL-SA12-435–442; MP-SA12-109–111; SGA2 XII5.10–5.11; Murre1964 theorem1.2.1; EGAIII2 erratum24.]

The Čech comparison pages require careful handling of boundary degrees and incoming differentials. The arithmetic of the spectral sequence was redone. Vanishing on every open, acyclicity for one particular cover, and a truncated comparison are different hypotheses. The records preserve the unfinished boundary questions rather than completing them by an unmarked appeal to a stronger modern theorem. [CL-SA12-431–434,477,478,483; SGA4V4.3/5.9; Stacks03AZ/03F9.]

## Finite-group actions: two explicit failures with valid replacements

The orbit-space argument distinguishes the universal covering construction from an incorrect assertion about disjoint closed simplices. Integer translation on a simplicial line has no fixed vertex, yet adjacent translates of a closed edge meet. The covering proof can instead use uniqueness of lifts or suitable open stars. The homology transfer was rederived with stabilizer multiplicities; the chain identity uses the full group sum. [CL-SA12-443–447; Armstrong1965 theorem3; Serre1953Classes.]

The relative trace criterion is valid, but the extra stable-corestriction bijection is false even with a faithful ring action. In the saved example G=C₄ acts on B=Z⁶ by (12)(3456), H is its order2 subgroup, and M=Z² uses the first two factors. A relative trace-one element exists. Tate H⁰(H,M) is F₂², its stable subgroup is the diagonal, and corestriction vanishes there, whereas Tate H⁰(G,M) is F₂. Full corestriction is still onto. The distinction between relative projectivity and the extra asserted bijection is essential. [CL-SA12-448,452,453; Broue2007 theorem6.8 and relative example; AKO IV4.2; saved computation.]

The residual H¹ comparison also fails under the written semilocal hypotheses. The local Artin ring F₂[ε]/(ε³) has the faithful order2 action ε↦ε+ε². Its units form C₄, the action is inversion, and H¹ is C₂; the residue field has trivial unit group. There is only one maximal ideal, so the transitivity and inertia assumptions are checked. The same example refutes the unrestricted GLₙ proposal at n=1. CL-SA12-523 is explicitly this review's sufficient tame Artin reconstruction, obtained by filtering principal units and averaging. It is not supplied as Grothendieck's missing text. [CL-SA12-456,457,523.]

A separate unit-norm assertion needs the finite-residue local-field scope. C((u))/C((t)), with u²=t, is ramified although every base unit is a norm, because it has a formal square root already in C[[t]]. The source's wrong target group is a reading alarm; the missing scope is a mathematical qualification. [CL-SA12-460; MilneCFT I and III; saved formal-root calculation.]

## Étale cohomological dimension: retain the hypotheses of each theorem

The Pro and Codex pages trace field extension bounds, strict-local fraction fields, support-dimension arguments, simultaneous induction and local affine Lefschetz. Several conspicuous formula errors are isolated: a strict bound fails already for Spec F₃; transcendence additivity needs a prime different from the characteristic; and a spectral-sequence total degree must use p+q rather than p−q. A finite-transcendence-degree real-embedding extension also fails for a non-Archimedean real closure of Q(t). [CL-SA12-423,424,426,483.]

SGA4 supplies many of the exact later results. Gabber's later work establishes the excellent strict-local affine theorem and also supplies counterexamples that show why excellence cannot disappear. The source's rejected pointwise affine bound is tested with an example satisfying invertibility: combine ILO XVIII-B3.2.5, where cd of a principal affine open is2d−1, with XVIII-B4.1.2 and SGA4X2.4, which bound all the proposed pointwise terms by d. This is a deduction from an opened published existence theorem, not an independently constructed explicit ring. [CL-SA12-485,489–496; ILO2014; ILOXVIIIA.]

The mixed-characteristic leaves cannot simply be declared solved by a prime-to-characteristic theorem. Gabber–Orgogozo theorem6.1 uses Kato's p-dimension and concerns affine opens inside Spec(A[1/p]). Both distinctions remain explicit. The raw δ, N and alternative φ notation remains unidentified where the source does not define it. [CL-SA12-479,484,501,502; GabberOrgogozo2008 §2.1 and theorem6.1.]

## Closed restriction, purity and base change

The Codex closed-restriction examples are developed as clearly marked reconstructions. An injective all-functions sheaf on an irreducible ambient plane has inverse image on a closed curve made of ambient germs. Two different constants on punctured branches need not extend. Two irreducible curves meeting at two points give a nonzero H¹ obstruction; an embedding in an ambient threefold transfers it to an irreducible surface. The proof avoids the additional compressed assertion of flasqueness on an irreducible curve, which remains a separate unresolved step. [CL-SA12-474–476,525,526; MP-SA12-127,128; outputs/calculations.json.]

SGA4 and SGA1 closely match the tame curve, local extension, purity, smooth local1-asphericity and ordinary smooth-base-change arguments. Modern work also provides an exact controlled nonabelian theorem: Haine–Holzschuh–Wolf prove prosmooth base change for specified Σ-torsion étale sheaves of spaces on qcqs schemes. This supports a meaningful modern fate for the H² proposal while leaving its unspecified coefficient interpretation explicit. The authors' backward reference to Giraud VII2.1.2 was verified as a citation, but the original book was not opened. [CL-SA12-509–515; HaineHolzschuhWolf2024 theorem0.1, definition1.9, corollary2.30.]

The wild-ramification programme has a precise curve-family continuation: Achinger2017 corollary2.3 assumes constant Swan conductor at a section and obtains local constancy and base change for the relevant higher direct images. Its Artin–Schreier example demonstrates base-change failure without the condition; it should not be misreported as a proof that a particular direct-image sheaf is non-locally-constant. The source's broader normal-crossings and higher-dimensional programme is not settled by that curve theorem. [CL-SA12-516–518; MP-SA12-143–145.]
