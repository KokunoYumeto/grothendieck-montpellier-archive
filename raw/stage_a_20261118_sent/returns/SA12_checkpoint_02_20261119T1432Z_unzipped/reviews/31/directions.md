## 1. A sufficient residual H¹ theorem

Origins: SA12-468; CL-SA12-456,457,523; MP-SA12-114,115. The written wild semilocal assertion fails for the saved faithful action on F₂[ε]/(ε³). The reconstructed tame Artin-local statement uses averaging on successive principal-unit quotients. A new task should determine how far this extends to complete noetherian local rings and GLₙ, explicitly controlling inverse limits and nonabelian lifting. First step: write the Artin case as a short theorem, including the group action and residue assumptions, and distinguish passage through finite quotients from passage to a completion. The counterexample is an obstruction to the unrestricted statement; it does not decide every normal or complete tame case. Sources: MilneCFT and the independent calculation record.

## 2. A dimension table with all hypotheses visible

Origins: CL-SA12-485,489–497,500–502; MP-SA12-107,131–138. SGA4's finite-type bounds, Gabber's excellent strict-local affine Lefschetz theorem, the general strict-local upper bounds and nonexcellent counterexamples answer different questions. Gabber–Orgogozo's mixed-characteristic theorem uses Kato's p-dimension and affine opens contained in the generic fiber. First step: produce one table whose columns are excellence, henselianity, strict locality, invertibility of the prime, coefficient class, and open-subscheme scope. Then trace one exact nonexcellent example from ILO XVIII-B3.2.5. This would make the unresolved raw affine-versus-fraction-field formulations reviewable without silently replacing them by a nearby published theorem. Sources: SGA4III X2.4 and XIV4.5; ILO2014 XV1.1.1 and XVIII-B3.2.5/4.1.2; GabberOrgogozo2008 theorem6.1.

## 3. The finite-flat topology behind cd′ₚ

Origins: SA12-401,404; CL-SA12-401,404; MP-SA12-101,102. The page separates all p-primary sheaves from finite commutative p-group schemes on a site with finite faithfully flat coverings. Shatz's all-flat-sheaf pathology, as reported by Milne, prevents a casual identification. First step: define the exact site, prove the representable-coefficient inequality, and search the original Shatz paper for the comparison hypotheses rather than importing its conclusion from a modern big-site convention. A useful outcome is a precise equivalence or a precisely delimited failure, not a new name for an undefined dimension. Sources: MilneADT III introduction; Shatz1966, whose metadata alone was verified here; SGA3IX.

## 4. Ambient germs and restriction of injectives

Origins: CL-SA12-474–476,525,526; MP-SA12-127,128. The saved proof gives a closed restriction with nonzero H¹ and transfers it to an irreducible surface. It avoids using the compressed assertion that the restriction to an irreducible curve is flasque. First step: write a fully categorical proof of the closed-cover exact sequence and ambient-germ obstruction; separately test that remaining flasqueness assertion under the precise noetherian irreducible-curve hypotheses. This would convert an instructive fragment into a reliable companion exposition. Sources: SGA4V and the calculation/proof record; the exact historical two-curve example was not located in the literature search. The MathOverflow module-pullback discussion is not evidence for this sheaf inverse-image claim.

## 5. Stable elements versus relative trace

Origins: SA12-465; CL-SA12-448,452–454; MP-SA12-112,113. The C₄ example satisfies faithful ring action and relative trace one, yet corestriction vanishes on the nonzero stable diagonal. Higman's criterion still supplies the valid relative-projectivity statement. First step: compare the manuscript maps with the precise restriction image and transfer theorems, and formulate the exact double-coset stability condition. A correction must specify which map is an isomorphism and which is merely onto. Sources: Broue2007 theorem6.8 and relative group-algebra example; AKO IV4.2; MilneCFT II1.29–1.30.

## 6. A controlled nonabelian H² interpretation

Origins: CL-SA12-514; MP-SA12-142. Haine–Holzschuh–Wolf's prosmooth base-change theorem establishes a modern result for Σ-torsion étale sheaves of spaces on qcqs schemes, with invertible primes and a specific truncated filtered-colimit definition. It does not endorse an untyped H² expression. First step: translate one finite prime-to-residue-characteristic gerbe into that framework and compare its cohomology set or space with the source notation. The original Giraud VII2.1.2 is a backward reference still requiring direct opening. Sources: HaineHolzschuhWolf2024 theorem0.1, definition1.9, corollary2.30; Giraud1971 remains explicitly unverified as an original text.

## 7. Constant Swan conductor as a missing hypothesis

Origins: CL-SA12-516–518; MP-SA12-143–145. Achinger's curve-family result adds constant Swan conductor at the section to obtain local constancy and base change. The source's broader normal-crossings and wild-ramification programme remains wider. First step: align the manuscript's relative curve reduction with Achinger2017 corollary2.3, recording properness, smoothness, coefficient and conductor assumptions. Use example1.2 only for its demonstrated base-change failure. This should identify a defensible small theorem and leave the higher-dimensional extension as a separate task. Sources: SGA4III XVI and XVIII; Achinger2017 theorem2.2 and corollary2.3.

## 8. The five vanishing criteria and their actual dependencies

Origins: SA12-475,480,482,487; CL-SA12-461–472; MP-SA12-119–126. The pages contain an argument in progress, not a finished equivalence theorem. Perfect-field splitting, the nonlinear automorphisms of vector groups, filtered unions, the degree-one failure for torsion coefficients, and the C₂ determinant exception must all remain visible. First step: state every criterion with coefficients and characteristic fixed, then prove or disprove each arrow separately. The current records already isolate several valid classical steps and two explicit repairs. Sources: MilneAG2017 11.3,14.54,16.13–16.20; MilneCFT; Stacks03R8/03RC/03RD/0A3J/0A3K.

## 9. Formal comparison and Picard representability

Origins: SA12-446; CL-SA12-435–438,441; MP-SA12-109–111. The pages distinguish formal full faithfulness, algebraization and the extra demand that the algebraized sheaf be locally free. The nonproper Picard expectation must not inherit the hypotheses of a proper representability theorem by implication. First step: choose a single nonproper pair meeting the source's depth and boundary conditions, compare it to SGA2 XII5.10–5.11, and test Murre's seven axioms explicitly. Sources: SGA2; Murre1964 theorem1.2.1 and the explicitly proper Picard application in partII.

## 10. Finite-range Čech comparison

Origins: SA12-442; CL-SA12-431–434,477,478; MP-SA12-108. First step: write the truncated Čech-to-derived spectral sequence with incoming differentials and show exactly which degrees must vanish for injectivity, surjectivity or an isomorphism at the boundary. Preserve the logical difference between vanishing on every open and vanishing for one cover. This task is limited and likely to yield a clean correction sheet. Sources: SGA4V4.3/5.9 and Stacks03AZ/03F9.

## 11. Identify the source's undefined invariants before proving statements about them

Origins: SA12-487; CL-SA12-470,479,484; MP-SA12-125,129,130. These are class F entries because the symbols or coefficient conventions are not sufficiently defined in the supplied text. First step: inspect adjacent material and exact historical definitions; only then compare p-bases, differential dimensions, support functions or Tate groups. Gabber–Orgogozo's p-rank calculations do not themselves identify δ or N. Any proposed reading must remain a reconstruction until scans are checked. Sources: StacksDifferentials; GabberOrgogozo2008 §2.1; SGA4III X4.1.

## 12. Multiplicative-type draft-to-print comparison

Origins: SA12-415; CL-SA12-410,413,416,503,505; MP-SA12-103,146. SGA3 supplies unusually close matches, including later editorial qualifications. First step: make a compact parallel edition of the countable finite-clopen criterion, the cancelled subgroup statement, the fiberwise-density hypothesis and the cofinal m-power torsion family. Record that the source includes retrocompactness in its second density alternative, whereas the printed general theorem removes it using EGA. This is a historical and mathematical clarification task, not an instruction to change the transcription. Sources: SGA3IX4.6/4.10/5.1bis; SGA3X5.11bis and footnote44.
