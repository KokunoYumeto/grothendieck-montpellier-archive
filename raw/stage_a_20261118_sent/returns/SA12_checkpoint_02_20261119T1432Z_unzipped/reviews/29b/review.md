It also reads the C29-05, C29-06 and C29-07 continuations and reused sheets. ## 1. The author's sequence of work

The formal tame sequence leads from a normal-bundle self-intersection class to inertia, a second-homotopy boundary and Kummer extension classes (96–105). The interleaved diagonalizable-group and EGA typescripts are separate documents. The Bertini and tame-Lefschetz sheets (113–116) then address connected covers and vanishing-cycle monodromy. The inertia sequel (117–123) first treats equivariant maps and multiplicity matrices and then explicitly revises the conjugacy formulation in its *Remords*.

The finite-infinitesimal programme begins with a cover sheet and the letter whose incipit matches the published letter to Serre of 18 August 1959. It asks for a fundamental group that retains infinitesimal torsors, a homotopy sequence, Künneth, the abelian-variety formula and a Witt-module description. The later restart (137–143) is mathematically significant: an arbitrary variable-base finite-flat category need not be closed under the equalizers the proof uses, whereas the source eventually imposes closure and also considers a fixed-ground-field category.

The subsequent pages move from descent paths and relative Künneth to the finite S_n-frame construction, Galois-category axioms, infinite discrete groups and the component Čech nerve. They then compare prorepresentability with ordinary continuous maps from an inverse limit. The last finite-type proof on 187 has a correct intended conclusion but a normalization arrow that cannot work as drawn. Reused English linear-algebra, Nullstellensatz and uniform-space sheets are kept separate; their presence is not evidence of authorship or a date for the adjacent handwriting.

## 2. Corrections that materially change the mathematics

**A vanishing homotopy boundary is not a splitting theorem.** The later curve passage explicitly excludes the P1 exceptional case and calls the remaining extension trivial. The formal elliptic example is now checked algebraically, not just by analogy with a torus: for E/C, L=O_E(0), M=L^n, the map induced by M^n≅[n]^*L gives a finite tame cover of degree n³. Its deck extension has the nontrivial theta commutator. Completion along the zero sections retains the cover. The full proof and its primary Mumford inputs are in the 29a cross-check. A separate zero-degree P1 normal bundle leaves an infinite inertia Tate module.

**Minimality needs a category with the required equalizers.** The equalizer of zero and multiplication by t on alpha_p over k[t] has generic length 1 and special length p. On the reduced connected cusp y^p=x^(p+1), the torsor z^p=x is minimal among finite-flat reductions, but zero and multiplication by x give the same trivial pointed pushforward. This refutes the unrestricted variable-base implication. It does not refute a theory with constant k-group maps or with a genuine closure hypothesis. Conversely, the infinitesimal-kernel uniqueness question has a positive answer without reducedness; pointed rigidity is the distinct assertion for which reducedness matters.

**The finite-coefficient prorepresentability argument is valid under its precise hypotheses.** H1-vanishing makes H2 left exact on finite abelian coefficients. The small category-of-elements criterion gives prorepresentability and the Artinian/minimal-object argument gives strictness. SGA4 I8.13.3 is a different theorem with accessibility and filtered-colimit hypotheses; the directly applicable small-category result is I8.3.3. Identification with a chosen pi2 remains an additional homotopy comparison.

**The later Set criterion is false even with all three written assumptions.** Take K_n=N^n and the finite-prefix functor F(I)=colim Hom(K_n,I). It is left exact, preserves filtered intersections, and its inverse-limit projections are surjective. The function on N^N sending x to x_(x0+1) is continuous but does not factor through any fixed prefix. Uniform continuity or a finite compact inverse system repairs this test. The result about continuous homomorphisms from a progroup is different because group homomorphisms have the relevant uniformity.

Further checks distinguish the unpunctured strictly henselian local spectrum from its two-divisor complement; retain the p-component in a nodal curve's ordinary étale fundamental group; and restrict the free-locus theorem to a suitable separated/finite étale setting. A doubled-origin involution gives an explicit nonopen free locus. The monic-factor proof is completed by finite-projective CRT quotients and characteristic polynomials; its incomplete source ending is not silently rewritten.

## 3. Exact later fates

The characteristic-two vanishing-cycle question is settled. Deligne's *Weil II* 4.2.7–4.2.8 treats sufficiently general pencils with rational l-adic coefficients. Orgogozo's 2005 preprint/2006 paper §§11.2–11.4 proves conjugacy up to sign and equality of monodromy images for every Lefschetz pencil with finite coefficients prime to the characteristic. Illusie's 2006 Theorem 5.2 states this explicitly and credits Gabber's image argument to a letter of 14 March 2005. The publication metadata are not used to invent an earlier circulation date for this exact result.

Nori's 1983 theorem is the exact later abelian-variety result. Mehta–Subramanian establish proper Künneth; Zhang supplies an exact homotopy-sequence criterion with torsor base change and normality conditions. The stronger hope of uniformly well-behaved infinitesimal fundamental groups in smooth proper families is not retained: SGA1 records Artin's unpublished counterexample and Pauly gives smooth base-extension failures. These three distinct properties are not conflated.

SGA3 X6.1–6.3 is the close later home for representable discrete-group torsors, strict prorepresentability and the component-nerve presentation. Its corrected footnotes preserve the distinction between scheme torsors and arbitrary fpqc torsor sheaves. Bhatt–Scholze's Lemma 7.4.6 gives the relevant modern continuous-homomorphism comparison; the larger pro-étale covering theory is not retrospectively identified with every manuscript class C1–C5.

The diagonalizable-group sheets match SGA3 VIII3.2–3.5 and §4. The EGA smoothness theorem matches final17.5.1, but the preceding draft proposition numbered17.4.10 does **not** survive at that number: final17.4.10 is a different remark. The fibrewise unramified claim instead follows from final17.4.1(d), and the preceding section proof matches17.4.9. This exact comparison required reading the full primary EGA text.

For generic Bertini, Poonen–Slavov's Theorem1.8 supplies the general morphism theorem without properness/normality. The SGA1 invocation is narrower. The higher-codimension connectedness reconstruction uses the Grassmannian incidence family and Stein connectedness; it cannot be justified merely by repeatedly assuming special sections remain irreducible. The combined local normal/unibranch/smooth variants remain an exact-source comparison task, not a claim that Bertini as a whole is open.
