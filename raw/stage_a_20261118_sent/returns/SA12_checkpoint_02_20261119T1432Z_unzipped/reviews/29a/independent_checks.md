# Independent mathematical checks for the 29a review

These arguments are reconstructions or counterexamples supplied by the earlier Pro run and rechecked here, except where explicitly identified as this review's construction. They are not silently attributed to the manuscript. The numerical calculations in `checks.py` illustrate the arguments; their finite ranges do not replace the proofs below.

## 1. Constructible finite sheaves and finite Hom sets (scan 52)

Choose a common finite stratification of a noetherian scheme on which two constructible finite-set sheaves F and G are locally constant. Refine each stratum into its finitely many connected components. On a connected component, a map of finite locally constant sheaves is determined by its map on one geometric stalk: equivalently it is an equivariant map of finite sets for the fundamental group. The product of these stalk-map sets is finite. Restriction from Hom(F,G) to that product is injective because geometric stalks are conservative. This proves finiteness of Hom without a universal-Japanese hypothesis. It does not itself prove the finite-fibre H¹ gluing lemma; that lemma also needs control of the extensions across the strata.

## 2. Finitely many finite quotients versus finite generation (scans 54–61)

Let p_i be distinct primes and d_i an unbounded sequence of positive integers. Set P=∏_i (Z/p_i)^{d_i}. For a fixed finite group G, every continuous homomorphism P→G factors through the finitely many factors whose primes divide |G|; there are finitely many such homomorphisms. Nevertheless P is not topologically finitely generated: its quotient (Z/p_i)^{d_i} needs d_i generators. Thus finiteness of H¹(P,G) for each finite G is weaker than one uniform finite generating set.

For an algebraically closed field k of characteristic p, the Artin–Schreier sequence on A¹ gives H¹(A¹,F_p)=k[x]/{h^p−h}. The classes x^m for distinct positive m not divisible by p are F_p-linearly independent. A nonconstant h has deg(h^p−h)=p·deg h; the highest degree of a nonzero finite linear combination of these monomials is not divisible by p. This proves the prime-to-p restriction is substantive, with infinitely many F_p-torsors already in dimension one.

For A², the class xy is not the sum of classes pulled back from the factors. If xy=f(x)+g(y)+(h^p−h), take the highest total-degree mixed part of h. Its pth power creates a mixed term of total degree a multiple of p strictly above every mixed term of h; repeated comparison forces h to have no mixed terms. Then xy cannot occur. Restricting to the axes gives the same contradiction after subtracting the one-variable terms. This gives a connected Z/p-torsor on A² not induced by the product of the fundamental groups. It obstructs the unrestricted étale Künneth isomorphism, not its prime-to-p version.

## 3. Chow modification and sections (scans 66–68)

Blowing up the origin in A² replaces the codimension-two origin by an exceptional P¹ of codimension one. In the chart y=xt, the exceptional inverse image is x=0. Consequently a claim that the full inverse image of a codimension-two subset remains codimension two under an arbitrary proper birational replacement is false. This refutes that particular reduction, not the finite-generation theorem it was intended to prove.

Two projective planes in P⁴ meeting in a single point form a connected reduced projective surface. A hyperplane missing the intersection point cuts them into two disjoint lines. Thus replacing irreducibility by connectedness in a generic hyperplane-section irreducibility/connectedness claim fails. On scan 67 the author himself answers this proposed replacement negatively.

Irreducibility over k alone does not imply geometric irreducibility of a generic section over an algebraic closure of the parameter field. View P²_C as an R-scheme and use the finite morphism to P²_R obtained by forgetting C. It is integral over R, but after extending to C it is a disjoint union of two planes, so a generic hyperplane section is a disjoint union of two lines. The later manuscript scan 113, checked by the parent reviewer, explicitly imposes geometric irreducibility and repairs this precise defect. This is not a claim that scan 67's ink must contain the missing word.

If X is normal and connected over an algebraically closed field, each connected finite étale cover is normal and integral. A sufficiently generic complete-intersection section remains geometrically integral on every such cover. The connected-cover criterion then gives surjectivity on π₁. For a fixed projective normal model with a codimension-two forbidden set, a generic curve can avoid the forbidden and singular loci. A smooth projective genus-g curve has a π₁ generated by at most 2g elements, so its surjective image has the same upper bound. The genus belongs to the chosen model and polarization, not to the function field alone.

## 4. Birational fundamental groups and a persistent obstruction (scans 69–70)

Fix a projective normal model Z of K/k, with k algebraically closed, and let V=Z_reg. A rational map V⇢X to a proper normal model of K is defined at every codimension-one point, hence on V minus a closed set of codimension at least two. Purity for regular V identifies the π₁ of this domain with π₁(V). The birational map from this domain to normal X induces a surjection on π₁: a connected finite étale cover of X remains connected after pullback to the common dense function-field open. The resulting compatible surjections from the single compact group π₁(V) surject onto the inverse limit over the models; finite intersection of the inverse images of a compatible finite set of coordinates is nonempty, and compactness supplies a common point. This repairs the use of unrelated genus bounds on different models. If a proper regular model exists, purity gives essential constancy on the cofinal family of models dominating it. This argument does not prove essential constancy without that hypothesis.

For the stronger proposed statement that the regular locus of *any* proper normal model already has the birational group, take k=C and S=P¹×P¹. Let the involution send (x,y) to (−x,−y). It has four fixed points, and the quotient X is a proper normal surface with four A₁ singularities. Remove these fixed points from S, obtaining V; then V→U=X_reg is a connected étale double cover. On an affine chart the invariant ring is C[a,b,c]/(ac−b²), a=x², b=xy, c=y². Its function field is C(a,t), t=y/x. The double extension is obtained by adjoining √a. The discrete valuation ord_a on C(a,t) ramifies with index 2 in that extension. This valuation has a center on every proper model of K, by properness. A finite étale cover of any such model would pull back to an étale extension of its valuation ring, contradicting that ramification. Hence this cover never extends étale to any proper model or any proper modification. Since K is rational, a proper regular model P² has trivial π₁; the birational group here is trivial while π₁(U) has a nontrivial quotient of order two. All properness, normality, connectedness and codimension-two hypotheses are met.

Uniform finite generation of an inverse system does not imply its essential constancy: the inverse system Z/ℓ^m has one generator at every level and limit Z_ℓ, yet never becomes constant. Nor does finite generation of a group and its quotient imply finite generation of the kernel. For example F_p[[T]]⋊Z_p, with 1∈Z_p acting by multiplication by 1+T, is generated by the acting element and the vector 1; the closure of their conjugates spans the power-series module. Its kernel F_p[[T]], as a profinite additive group, has quotients (F_p)^m for every m and is not finitely generated. This is only an abstract warning about the logical implication in the manuscript, not a counterexample to its geometric kernel question.

## 5. Picard schemes, tangent spaces and Frobenius (scans 71–77)

A bijection on algebraically closed points and equality of tangent-space dimensions do not force an inclusion of finite group schemes to be an isomorphism. In characteristic p, α_p→α_{p²}, given by the closed inclusion Spec k[t]/(t^p)→Spec k[t]/(t^{p²}), has a single geometric point on both sides and one-dimensional tangent spaces, but lengths p and p². (The earlier notes write α_{p^r} in this sense, not the r-fold product α_p^r.) Likewise the system α_{p^m} has dimension zero, one geometric point, trivial geometric component-group torsion and tangent dimension one, while its scheme lengths grow without bound. These examples refute the abstract inference, not the geometric Picard theorem under its full hypotheses.

The Frobenius comparison needs all iterates on the nilpotent part. The inclusion span(e₁)⊂span(e₁,e₂), with F(e₁)=0 and F(e₂)=e₁ (semilinearly extended over a perfect field), induces an isomorphism on ker F and on F-fixed vectors, but is not an isomorphism of the spaces. The kernels of all F^ν detect the missing nilpotent vector. Over an algebraically closed field the invertible semilinear part is spanned by its F-fixed vectors; together with every iterated kernel this yields the desired comparison. The tiny ν/1 on scans 72–73 is therefore decisive.

For proper connected reduced schemes over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, H⁰(O)=k and F−1 on k is surjective. The Artin–Schreier long exact sequence identifies H¹_et(X,F_p) with ker(F−1:H¹(X,O)→H¹(X,O)). This explains the fixed-vector part; it does not control the entire tangent space in the absence of the iterated-kernel argument.

Let A be a normal domain of characteristic p and q=p^ν. In its perfect closure, A^{1/q}/A is torsion-free as an A-module: if a≠0 and ab∈A for b∈A^{1/q}, then b∈Frac(A); b is integral over A, hence b∈A. On a regular finite-type scheme over a perfect field, Frobenius is finite flat, and the quotient F_*^νO/O is locally free (the inclusion is locally a direct summand as modules because a unit-coordinate is present after passing to a regular local basis). Such a sheaf extends uniquely across a codimension-two omission on a normal scheme. The manuscript's use of local freeness on Y therefore needs regularity and the finite-type/perfect-field setting; normality alone proves only the torsion-free assertion on X.

The dimension formula dim Pic⁰=(1/2)rank_Zℓ π₁^{ab,(ℓ)} is for ℓ different from the characteristic and the reduced abelian Picard variety of a proper geometrically normal connected variety. Kummer theory identifies H¹_et(X,Z_ℓ(1)) with the Tate module of the Picard scheme, while H¹_et(X,Z_ℓ)=Hom_cont(π₁(X),Z_ℓ); the dual and twist must be retained. In characteristic 3 the smooth elliptic curve y²=x³−x has four F₃-points, trace zero, and p-rank zero, so the characteristic-primary rank is zero although the Picard variety has dimension one. The program checks the point count and nonsingularity; supersingularity follows from the elliptic-curve trace criterion, whose source must be cited separately.

Geometric normality alone does not make K⊗_k kbar a field: Spec C is geometrically normal over R, but C⊗_R C≅C×C. Geometric integrality, or a separate choice of a geometric component, is needed for the field notation used on scan 76.

## 6. Diagonalizable group schemes and finite jets (reused sheets)

For an abelian group M, D_S(M)=Spec O_S[M] represents Hom(M,G_m). A map M'→M induces D(M)→D(M'). If M' injects into M, the group algebra O_S[M] is a free O_S[M']-module with basis any set of representatives of M/M'; it is therefore faithfully flat, and the morphism is affine, hence quasi-compact. Its kernel is D(M/M'). This proves the exact-duality assertion with arbitrary abelian groups and nonempty S where the converse is asserted. For a finite M of order n, O_S[M] is free of rank n. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, writing M as cyclic factors shows the geometric point count is n exactly when p∤n; μ_p has length p and a single geometric point. These are exact matches to SGA3 VIII, not mere analogies.

For a point a on a smooth manifold, finite-order distributions supported at a are the duals of the finite jet algebra O_a/m_a^{r+1}. Multiplication of jets is dual to comultiplication; the action (fμ)(g)=μ(fg) gives the module identity, and pullback of functions gives pushforward of distributions and the projection formula φ_*((f∘φ)ν)=fφ_*ν. In two independent coordinate systems, the external product of derivative functionals has total order |α|+|β|. Thus the finite-order-r image consists of combinations with total order at most r, not the full tensor product of the two order-r pieces. The order-one derivations are dual to m/m², and the coordinate change is the ordinary Jacobian chain rule. If a smooth submanifold is cut out by an ideal I, its distributions identify with those killed by I. These checks validate the formulas visible in the reused typescript; they do not identify the writer, date, or original publication. The program verifies the comultiplication and projection formula in a truncated polynomial example.

## 7. Tame coefficients, universal covers and Chern classes (scans 88–94)

The arithmetic action on prime-to-p inertia is cyclotomic. For a concrete tame example in characteristic 2, use F₄((s))/F₂((t)), t=s³. A generator τ sends s↦ζs; the residue Frobenius φ sends ζ↦ζ² and fixes s. Then φτφ⁻¹=τ², so this inertia is not central. The total Galois group is S₃. The saved finite permutation calculation verifies the conjugation relation.

For finite constant J of order prime to the relevant residue characteristics, each H¹-class is a finite J-torsor and dies after pulling back to that torsor. Taking the direct limit over finite tame covers proves H¹ of the tame universal pro-cover vanishes. Applying this directly to a profinite inverse-limit coefficient I requires a continuous-cohomology convention and control of derived inverse limits; it is not justified merely by the finite statement. The stronger margin about all higher cohomology of curves has a P¹ exception: over an algebraically closed field π₁(P¹)=1 but H²(P¹,μ_n)≅Z/n for n invertible.

Let L be the normal line bundle of X₀ in the regular formal neighbourhood and choose transition functions g_ij. On an étale cover choose h_ij with h_ij^n=g_ij. The products h_ij h_jk h_ik^{-1} lie in μ_n and represent the Kummer Chern class of L. They are exactly the obstruction to gluing the local nth roots of a normal parameter; this identifies the finite-level extension class with the Chern class, up to the explicitly chosen normal-versus-conormal and cocycle sign conventions. A sign cannot be supplied from the mathematics while claiming it was read in the manuscript.

If π₂=0, the obstruction detected by evaluation on π₂ disappears, but the extension class in H²(π₁,I) can remain nonzero. For the complement of the zero section of a degree-one line bundle on a complex elliptic curve, the topological homotopy sequence has π₂(base)=0 and central kernel Z. Its group presentation has [a,b]=c with c central; the finite Heisenberg quotient shows c remains nontrivial. Thus the extension need not split. A direct algebraic finite cover, retained after completion along the zero section, is constructed in `formal_elliptic_nonsplitting.md`; the final conclusion does not rely on an unproved analytic-to-formal comparison. Initially this tested an overstrong implication in the earlier commentary. The actual later scan 96, Codex K29-07 lines 51–64, has now also been read: it explicitly calls the non-P¹ curve extension trivial. The same example therefore refutes that later assertion under the split-extension reading, which root records as CL-SA12-203.

For X₀=P¹ and normal bundle of degree d, the induced map on the prime-to-p completion of π₂ is multiplication by d; the predicted inertia is the quotient of Zhat^(p')(1) by d. For d=0 it is infinite; for d≠0 it has order equal to the prime-to-p part of |d|. This checks the formula's zero-degree case, conditional on the identification of the tame homotopy sequence.

A finite cohomology class which is zero in a filtered colimit is zero at some finite stage by the definition of a filtered colimit. The stage may depend on the finite coefficient quotient. There is no reason one finite cover kills every coefficient level at once: on an elliptic curve, pullback of a degree-one line bundle along a finite cover of degree d has degree d, which is not divisible by every positive integer.

For a regular noetherian X₀ and the complement U of finitely many Cartier prime divisors D_i, localization of Cartier divisors gives ⊕_i Z[D_i]→Pic(X₀)→Pic(U)→0. Consequently if a line bundle becomes n-divisible on U, its class on X₀ is n times a line-bundle class plus an integral combination of the boundary classes. Intersections among the D_i do not invalidate this Picard argument. Other steps of the formal-tame proof may still require disjointness; the argument does not remove it globally.

## 8. A fully checked counterexample to unrestricted Picard n-torsion invariance

This construction independently checks the warning in Pro notes FORMAL-10 and FORMAL-17. It also refutes the unrestricted assertion presently displayed as Stacks tag 0C6S, Lemma 37.4.2, opened on 19 November 2026. It is not asserted to be a new result or an authorial error beyond the written hypotheses. The parent reviewer separately checked the mechanism and requested the explicit global-unit and direct-summand steps given here.

Let E/C be an elliptic curve and choose 0≠a∈H¹(E,O_E). Choose a finite affine cover (E_i) with a Čech cocycle a_ij representing a. Put X₀=E×G_m, with coordinate z on G_m. Glue

    X'_i = E_i × Spec C[ε]/(ε²) × G_m

on overlaps by z_j=z_i(1+εa_ij). The cocycle identity for the a_ij makes this gluing consistent because ε²=0. This is a square-zero thickening X₀⊂X' with ideal I≅O_X₀; it is flat over C[ε]/(ε²). The units exact sequence gives

    Γ(X₀,O*_{X₀}) --δ--> H¹(X₀,I) --> Pic(X') --> Pic(X₀).

Since E is proper geometrically connected, Γ(E,O_E)=C. The projection E×G_m→E is affine and its pushforward of O is ⊕_{m∈Z}O_E z^m. Cohomology on the finite affine cover commutes with this direct sum. Hence

    H¹(X₀,O_X₀) = H¹(E,O_E) ⊗_C C[z,z^{-1}].

The summand of degree zero is H¹(E,O_E), so the pullback of a remains nonzero. Also Γ(X₀,O_X₀)=C[z,z^{-1}], and its units are precisely c z^m, c∈C*, m∈Z. Constants lift globally to X'. The ratio of the chosen local lifts of z on overlaps is 1+εa_ij, so δ(cz^m)=ma, up to the one global sign convention of the boundary map. Therefore Im δ=Za exactly. Exactness now gives

    ker(Pic(X')→Pic(X₀)) ≅ H¹(X₀,O_X₀)/Za.

For every n>1, the class a/n is not in Za because a≠0 and 1/n∉Z, but n(a/n)=a belongs to Za. This is a nonzero n-torsion class in the kernel, with n invertible everywhere. Thus Pic(X')[n]→Pic(X₀)[n] is not injective, and the Picard kernel is not uniquely n-divisible. Its failure is caused by the global-unit connecting map. Finite étale μ_n-torsors remain invariant under the thickening; Kummer's units-mod-n term changes, so there is no conflict.

The same example fits the formal-geometric setting of scan 88. Glue E_i×Spf C[[t]]×G_m by z_j=z_i exp(t a_ij). The formal scheme is connected, regular, noetherian, and has the regular Cartier ideal of definition t; X₀=E×G_m and the additional boundary is empty. The transition functions exp(t a_ij/n) define a line bundle M trivial on X₀. Its nth power has transitions exp(t a_ij)=z_j/z_i and is therefore trivial; its reduction modulo t² is the nontrivial class above, so M itself is nontrivial. This refutes unique n-divisibility of the formal Picard kernel under the stated nonproper hypotheses as well.

There is nevertheless a correct replacement needed by root constructions. The groupoid of pairs (M,φ:M^n≅L), with L fixed, is a μ_n-gerbe. Nilpotent thickenings induce equivalences on étale topoi, and n is invertible, so the obstruction and torsor data are unchanged. A specified root pair on X₀ therefore lifts uniquely up to compatible isomorphism when L is fixed on the thickening. Equivalently, nth powers on principal units 1+I are bijective by the binomial-series argument for nilpotent I. This concerns a root *with its power isomorphism*; it does not assert unique n-divisibility of the entire Picard kernel. Passage to all formal levels is a separate effectiveness/inverse-limit problem; the possible lim¹ of global units must be addressed, for example by an appropriate Mittag–Leffler hypothesis.

## 9. Unramified objects versus unique lifting of morphisms (scan 87)

### Clarification of the root-pair comparison in §8

The parent reviewer independently audited the Picard counterexample and reopened Stacks0C6S on 2026-11-19. There is no contradiction with root-pair invariance: trivializing M^n by the local coordinates z_i reduces the power isomorphism on X₀ to the global unit z. Thus the special-fibre pair (O, power isomorphism z) is a nontrivial μ_n-torsor, even though its underlying line bundle is trivial. Forgetting the power isomorphism is exactly the operation that loses the changing units-mod-n term. The parent audit is a targeted proof check, not a claim of novelty or an external correction.

### Unramified-object example

Over A=C[ε]/(ε²), both Spec A→Spec A and the closed immersion Spec C→Spec A are unramified, and their reductions to Spec C are isomorphic. They are different objects over Spec A. Thus an unrestricted unique-lift assertion for unramified objects across a thickening is false. This does not contradict the formal-unramified property: it is uniqueness of a lift of a morphism to a *fixed* unramified target. Because scan 87 is cancelled and the abbreviation m.u. is unresolved, this example constrains a proposed reconstruction, rather than deciding exactly what the original draft claimed.
