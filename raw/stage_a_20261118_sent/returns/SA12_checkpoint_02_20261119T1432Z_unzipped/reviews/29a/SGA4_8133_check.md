# SGA 4 I, 8.13.3: exact prorepresentability hypotheses

Read visually on 19 November 2026 in the original scanned SGA 4 I PDF fetched by the parent reviewer. Printed pages 136–137 are PDF pages 152–153 (one-based). The theorem is on printed page 137. The nearby small-category criterion was also read on printed pages 75–76, PDF pages 91–92.

## The published statement

Corollary 8.13.3 assumes that the universe-relative category C has finite inverse limits and small filtered direct limits; that the functor denoted Ker on its arrow category is accessible; that strict epimorphisms remain strict under arbitrary base change; and that a small subcategory generates C by strict epimorphisms. Commutation of Ker with small filtered direct limits is given as a sufficient example for its accessibility. Under these five hypotheses, a covariant set-valued functor on C is prorepresentable exactly when it is left exact and accessible.

Proposition 8.13.2 gives the more general mechanism. Its ambient assumption is condition L of 9.1(b), which is satisfied when small filtered colimits exist. In part (a), accessibility of every object implies that a prorepresentable functor is accessible and left exact. In part (b), finite inverse limits and a cardinal filtration in the sense of 9.12 imply that an accessible left-exact functor is prorepresentable. The proof on pages 136–137 constructs a small cofinal subcategory of the relevant category of elements using the accessibility cardinal. The corollary invokes 9.11 and 9.13 to obtain these hypotheses from its five explicit conditions. Those two later proofs were not independently checked in this review.

## What this does and does not establish for the manuscript

For the category of all sets, accessibility remains a real hypothesis in this general criterion. Prorepresentability means a filtered colimit of covariant representable functors, equivalently representation by a pro-object. Neither 8.13.2 nor 8.13.3 identifies this functor with all continuous maps out of the ordinary inverse limit of the underlying sets. They also do not alone assert a presentation by surjective transition maps. Consequently these results do not repair the extra topological conclusion on cote 29 scan 183; that conclusion needs its separate compactness or uniform-continuity analysis, which the parent reviewer is recording.

The category of finite abelian groups does not itself have arbitrary small filtered colimits. Thus applying 8.13.3 to it verbatim would be inappropriate. The directly relevant small-category argument is the dual of Theorem 8.3.3(iii), printed page 76: for a presheaf on a category with finite colimits, left exactness together with a small cofinal family in the category of elements is equivalent to indrepresentability. Apply it to the opposite of an essentially small finite-coefficient category with finite limits. The smallness requirement is then automatic for a set-valued functor on a chosen small skeleton. This gives prorepresentability from left exactness, but the manuscript must still establish its claimed left exactness for H² and specify the coefficient category. Extension to all torsion groups instead requires the appropriate accessibility/filtered-colimit check.
