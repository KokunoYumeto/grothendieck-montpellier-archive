## 2. Finiteness, generic fibres and sections

### Finite sheaves and covering finiteness

The initial generic-fibre descent lemma is conditional on an adjective recovered at scan 48, read as a form of reduced-fibre condition. That is not a harmless uncertainty. The exact smooth-surjective normal-base version is proved in Katz–Lang, Preliminaries Lemma 2: the geometric generic fibre is connected, the base is normal connected locally noetherian, and the morphism is smooth, surjective and of finite type. Its normalization and fpqc-descent proof was read. A proper homotopy theorem does not by itself settle the manuscript’s possibly nonproper, weaker-fibre version. CL-SA12-001–002 therefore retain the exact unresolved qualifier and do not call the broader statement established merely because the familiar smooth theorem is true. [KatzLang1981; Stacks0BUM]

The constructibility statement on scan 52 has a precise stronger modern result. Déglise’s account of Gabber proves constructibility of direct image of a constructible finite-set étale sheaf under a finite-type map over a noetherian base, without the manuscript’s universal-Japanese restriction. The same source’s R¹ and punctured-local statements have different hypotheses; none is substituted for the degree-zero theorem. Finiteness of Hom between constructible finite-set sheaves is independently reproved by a finite common stratification and conservative geometric stalks. The adjacent finite-fibre assertion for restriction of H¹ across a finite partition is a further gluing claim; that full nonabelian assertion is not certified just from finite Hom. These are CL-SA12-003–005. [DegliseGabber2007, Theorems 1.1–1.4]

The finiteness reduction on scans 54–58 quantifies over normal integral subschemes, finite étale base covers, and the required subgroups of G×G. Those quantifiers are preserved in CL-SA12-006–010. Finiteness of H¹ for every fixed finite target does not imply topological finite generation of an arbitrary profinite group. The earlier-run diagnostic is valid: a product of elementary abelian groups at distinct primes with unbounded ranks has only finitely many maps to each fixed finite group but has no uniform finite generating set. The explicit proof is in `independent_checks.md` §2.

The prime-to-characteristic restriction is substantive. Artin–Schreier classes x^m with p not dividing m give an infinite independent family on the affine line. The mixed class xy on the affine plane obstructs the unrestricted affine product formula. These examples test the proposed removal of a hypothesis; they do not refute the prime-to-p theorem. Saved calculations reproduce the elementary polynomial identities, while the degree arguments establish the infinite statements. [CL-SA12-008, 011, 015; ESS2021; StacksArtinSchreier]

### Generation, presentation and the correct Bertini input

Finite generation and finite presentation must be kept separate at scan 64. The witnesses disagree on the noun. The opened modern sources establish finite presentation for smooth varieties with the specified good compactification, and for all connected proper schemes over an algebraically closed field. They do not automatically settle the exact quotient defined by divisorial wild inertia on an arbitrary singular compactification. This is why CL-SA12-017 and MP-SA12-06 preserve both the reading issue and the precise remaining comparison. [ESS2021; LSS2023]

The ordinary-irreducibility wording on scan 67 is inadequate over an arbitrary field. For example, P² over C, viewed as a scheme over R, is integral but becomes two components after algebraic closure; its generic geometric hyperplane section is reducible. The later actual scan 113 explicitly says geometrically irreducible and was read at its endpoint. This is a genuine later mathematical refinement, not a decision that an omitted word was present in scan 67’s ink.

For the corrected arbitrary-morphism generic Bertini assertion, a precise modern primary source was found: Poonen–Slavov, Theorem 1.8, with Lemma 7.1 and the proof in §7. Once the image dimension is at least two, their exceptional hyperplane loci are proper, so the generic inverse hyperplane section is geometrically irreducible. Their separate Theorem 1.5 assumes equidimensional nonempty fibres; that extra hypothesis is not silently discarded. Successive *generic* hyperplanes give the indicated codimension range. The source first circulated on arXiv on 23 January 2020; the author version opened is dated 4 August 2020. Jouanolou is an actual backward reference, but the book itself was not opened here. [CL-SA12-021; PoonenSlavov2020]

SGA 1 X 2.10–2.11 gives the proper normal hyperplane connectedness and fundamental-group surjectivity argument. For higher-codimension special sections, one should use the universal incidence family and connectedness over the normal Grassmannian, rather than blindly iterate a normality hypothesis through arbitrary special hyperplane sections. The combined unibranch/normal/smooth local theorem on the later leaf has not been matched clause by clause to a single opened printed theorem. The parent review records that limit separately.

The codimension-two argument has a specific defective step: the inverse image of a codimension-two omitted set need not remain codimension two after a Chow modification. Blowing up a surface point produces an exceptional divisor. This refutes the inference, not the intended finite-generation theorem. A fixed generic curve avoiding the bad locus gives a legitimate route in the normal projective setting, and a fixed genus bound is tied to that model and polarization. It is not a birational invariant of genus. [CL-SA12-018–025; SGA1_1971; StacksBlowup]

## 3. Birational fundamental groups and Picard schemes

### One fixed source, and a false regular-locus shortcut

The compressed inverse-limit argument can be reconstructed correctly. Fix the regular locus V of one proper normal projective model. A rational map to any other proper normal model is defined at codimension-one points. Purity on V permits omission of the remaining codimension-two set. Birationality and normality give surjectivity on the groups of connected finite covers. These maps have a single compact source, so the finite-intersection property gives a surjection to the inverse limit over all models. This is the needed compatibility argument, not an inference from unrelated genus bounds on different models. A regular proper model gives essential constancy; uniform finite generation alone does not. [CL-SA12-026–029; Stacks0BMB; Stacks0BX7; SGA1_1971]

The stronger regular-locus extension question is false in its unrestricted form. Over C, take the quotient of P¹×P¹ by (x,y)↦(−x,−y). The quotient is a proper normal rational surface with four A₁ singularities. Its regular locus has a connected étale double cover induced by deleting the four fixed points upstairs. The function field is C(a,t), and the double extension adjoins √a. The a-adic valuation is ramified and has a centre on every proper model; hence this cover cannot become étale on any proper modification. The rational function field also has the simply connected regular model P². Thus the regular-locus group of an arbitrary normal model need not already be the birational group. All the relevant geometric hypotheses are checked in `independent_checks.md` §4. [CL-SA12-030]

No exact literature settlement was located for the unrestricted model-stationarity question or for finite generation of the kernel of π₁(X−Z)→π₁(X) in the stated proper normal codimension-two setting. Those are the searched class-D records CL-SA12-029 and 031. This label records the search outcome; it does not assert novelty or present openness. An abstract finitely generated pro-p semidirect product with an infinitely generated kernel is included only to show why the surrounding group-theoretic implications are insufficient.

### Nonreduced Picard structure is the central issue

The pages on scans 71–77 concern more than the reduced classical Picard variety. Their central target is scheme-level Pic^τ invariance for a proper birational morphism with normal source and regular target, with geometric and projectivity questions in the margins. The exact all-hypothesis, nonreduced statement was not identified in an opened primary theorem. Reduced birational Picard invariance, equality of tangent dimensions, and properness of Pic^τ are weaker inputs. The earlier summaries were right to retain this proof obligation. [CL-SA12-033–046; Kleiman2005; KLOS; Groth236]

Several logical checks are decisive. A bijection on geometric points and an isomorphism on tangent spaces do not force an inclusion of finite group schemes to be an isomorphism: α_p⊂α_(p²) has one point and one-dimensional tangent space on both sides, but different lengths. The model-stabilization criterion also cannot be justified from bounds on dimensions, geometric torsion and tangent dimensions alone; α_(p^m) has unbounded length despite those bounds. These examples refute abstract inferences. They are not represented as geometric counterexamples to the full Picard theorem. [CL-SA12-037, 047]

The Frobenius comparison needs every iterated kernel, not only ker F. A two-dimensional nilpotent Jordan block and its one-dimensional kernel have identical ker F and fixed vectors, but different higher kernels. The ν/1 reading on scans 72–73 therefore changes the proof. The accompanying normal-domain integrality argument shows that A^(1/q)/A is torsion-free. Local freeness on the regular finite-type/perfect-field side uses finite flat Frobenius, and the codimension-two extension step uses the appropriate reflexive/local-free hypotheses. These ingredients were checked separately; none licenses a silent repair of the exponent. [CL-SA12-039–042; Stacks0EC0; Stacks0AY6]

The ℓ-adic dimension formula needs ℓ different from the characteristic and the appropriate reduced abelian Picard object. The smooth elliptic curve y²=x³−x over F₃ has four rational points and p-rank zero but Picard dimension one. The point count and nonsingularity were recomputed; the supersingularity interpretation uses the opened primary criterion of Sutherland. Geometric normality also does not make K⊗k kbar a field: C⊗R C is a product. Both restrictions are preserved as reading alerts. [CL-SA12-050–051; MoonenTateBT; Sutherland2012]

For the requested direct genus bound, KLOS supplies a precise route through the birational classical Picard variety and finite-kernel restriction to a suitable complete-intersection curve. Kleiman’s Remark 5.8 records a related dependence on compactness. This can bound the reduced dimension without settling all infinitesimal Picard structure or the unrestricted normal-model stabilization question. CL-SA12-046 remains the third searched class-D record. [CL-SA12-049, 052; KLOS 10.3.4–5, 10.3.9, 10.3.13; MP-SA12-16–17]

## 4. The formal tame-covering manuscript

### What Murre’s letter establishes

The missing Pro leaves 79–81 are available in Codex. They establish that Murre’s text was a preliminary working-out of Grothendieck’s notes, with substantial prerequisites, an unfinished analogue-of-Mumford chapter, and explicit doubts about realization, normality, canonicity and the reduction step. They also give a concrete dependency: ordinary Kummer lifting was used because a corresponding generalized-Kummer algebra lemma was missing; this is the stated reason for imposing pairwise disjointness of the boundary components. The absent Giraud letter is an attributed source for the proof of preliminary 9.2.3, not an inspected proof.

Several abbreviated equivalences, gluing diagrams and propositions are genuinely not identifiable from the supplied leaves. The review preserves their draft numbers and dependencies rather than inventing a modern category. The normality question may concern a formal/tame realization notion; ordinary scheme normality is étale-local and cannot simply be used to dismiss Murre’s doubt. Likewise, unramifiedness gives uniqueness of maps to a fixed target, not unique deformation of unramified objects. The dual-number example in §9 of the proof file checks this distinction. These are the principal class-F records, not assertions of new open mathematics. [CL-SA12-053–062, 095–102; GM1971; EGAI1960; StacksFormalEtale]

The Grothendieck–Murre book’s publisher contents were opened and match the formal tame group and the comparison of two H² classes at chapter level. The full theorem text was inaccessible. This is recorded explicitly in every relevant fate and link. The EGA I 10.12.3 citation was searched and its bibliographic endpoint opened, but neither the oversized web PDF nor an interrupted direct-fetch attempt yielded the exact primary proposition. No theorem-level match is certified from its number alone.

### Inertia, Chern classes and coefficient limits

In the formal setup, U is an open subset of the special divisor X₀, obtained by removing the induced remaining boundary. It is not the complement of X₀ in the underlying formal space. The tame inertia module has cyclotomic action; it is not automatically central. The explicit extension F₄((s))/F₂((t)), t=s³, has Frobenius conjugation τ↦τ² and gives the finite S₃ diagnostic. [CL-SA12-081–082; SGA7_IX; SGA1_1971]

For finite prime-to-characteristic coefficients, an H¹ class dies on its torsor and hence at a finite stage of the universal tame pro-cover. A fixed finite higher class vanishing in a filtered colimit also vanishes at some stage. Neither statement supplies a single stage for every coefficient level or automatic cohomology with a profinite inverse-limit coefficient. The π₁/π₂ disagreement in the H² term is consequently material. The finite-level Cech roots identify the extension obstruction with the normal-bundle Kummer class after a sign convention has been fixed; they do not by themselves prove the full pro-limit realization theorem. [CL-SA12-083–089; Milne2008; Stacks03PK]

The boundary correction on scan 94 is mathematically necessary. For a regular X₀, divisibility of a line-bundle class on U gives divisibility on X₀ modulo the classes of the omitted Cartier divisors. The author’s inserted sum records exactly that correction. Pairwise disjointness is unnecessary for this isolated Picard-localization step, but its removal from the entire generalized-Kummer realization argument remains a separate source comparison. [CL-SA12-090, 094; StacksDivisorLocalization]

### A checked failure of unrestricted Picard-kernel uniqueness

The claim of unique n-divisibility of the Picard kernel, without properness or control of global units, is false. The complete proof is saved in `independent_checks.md` §8 and was independently audited by root. Let E/C be elliptic, choose a nonzero a in H¹(E,O), and deform E×G_m by gluing local coordinates as z_j=z_i(1+εa_ij), ε²=0. The exact units sequence identifies the Picard kernel with H¹(O)/Z a. Indeed the global units of the special fibre are exactly C* z^Z, constants lift, and the connecting homomorphism sends z to a. The degree-zero direct summand of H¹(E,O)⊗C C[z,z^−1] ensures that a remains nonzero. Thus a/n modulo Z a is a nonzero n-torsion element.

The formal transition z_j=z_i exp(t a_ij) yields the same counterexample in a connected regular noetherian formal scheme with regular Cartier special fibre and empty additional boundary. This checks the actual formal setting, not merely an unrelated singular or nonnoetherian example. The opened current Stacks tag 0C6S contains the same unrestricted Pic[n] assertion; its proof suppresses the global-unit connecting quotient. That particular assertion is flagged as unreliable in this review. No novelty claim is made, and no external source was edited. [CL-SA12-091; Stacks0C6R; Stacks0C6S]

There is a correct refinement: retain a specified power isomorphism and work with the groupoid of pairs (M, M^n≅L) for fixed L and invertible n. Nil-invariance of the étale site and unique roots of principal units give the finite-thickening equivalence. In the counterexample the special-fibre power isomorphism is the unit z, so the root pair is a nontrivial μ_n torsor even though its underlying line bundle is trivial. This resolves the apparent conflict. The infinite formal limit and algebraization require separate effectiveness and unit Mittag–Leffler arguments; Lipman’s opened result is used only in its stated setting. [CL-SA12-092–093; Stacks04DY; Stacks03PK; Lipman1974 §2.4]

The later actual scan 96 also resolves two contextual uncertainties. It explicitly excludes P¹ with empty boundary from the curve π₂-vanishing example, but then asserts that the remaining inertia extension is trivial. A degree-one line bundle on a complex elliptic curve gives a nonsplit extension. The saved finite algebraic construction uses M=L^n and [n]*L≅M^n, producing a connected degree-n³ tame cover with a nonabelian theta group; completion along the zero sections retains the cover. Mumford’s nondegenerate theta pairing is the primary input. Root records the later false splitting claim as CL-SA12-203; the earlier item SA12-64 records the diagnostic and crosslink. [formal_elliptic_nonsplitting.md; Mumford1966; MoonenTheta]

## 5. Reused typescripts and exact published counterparts

The diagonalizable-group sheets have exact counterparts in SGA 3 VIII §§1–3. Group algebras represent the character functor and commute with base change. Coset representatives prove faithful flatness in the dual exact sequence. Finite scheme length and geometric point count differ in characteristic p, as μ_p illustrates. The Hom-representability statement requires its finite-generation hypotheses, and the opened reedition explicitly expands an original proof issue. The typed blanks remain blanks in the archival text. [CL-SA12-074–080; SGA3_VIII]

The Raynaud continuation and scan 85 match SGA 7 I IX 7.1.5–7.2.6, printed pages 402–407, read visually in the original PDF. The published torus-by-abelian extension belongs to the identity component of the Raynaud group; the full group can have a finite étale component group. The exact completion isomorphism, functorial adic base change, and finite-flat torsor argument are separated. Reading those propositions is not recorded as an independent proof of every cited descent or algebraization theorem. [CL-SA12-063–067; SGA7_IX]

The six distribution sheets are mathematically coherent as finite-jet duality: module and coproduct identities, projection formula, total-order external products, tangent derivations, Jacobians and ideal annihilators were checked. Their source, writer, date and regularity glyph remain unidentified. In particular, N385 is not assigned to the unrelated Bourbaki exposé 385. Schwartz’s opened 1947 article supplies historical background, not an attribution. MP-SA12-43 is explicitly an editorial provenance/reading problem, not a conjecture invented for the author. [CL-SA12-068–073; Schwartz1947]
