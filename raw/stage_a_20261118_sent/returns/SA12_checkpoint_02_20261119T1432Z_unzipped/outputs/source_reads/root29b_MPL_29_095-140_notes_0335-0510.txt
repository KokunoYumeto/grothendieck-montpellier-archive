389: #### N019 — Rigidity is used to pass to an exact functor of isomorphism classes
390: 
391: **Witness:** cote 29, scan(s) 126, typescript p.2, rigidity lemma and pointed-torsor functor diagrams. **Kind:** unproved claim.
392: 
393: **Source words:** “alors u=v”; “exactitude de F, et du lemme”.
394: 
395: The page defines Z(G) as the set of isomorphism classes of pointed F(G)-torsors and asserts compatibility with products and equalizers by exactness of F and the rigidity lemma. It supports the torsor-based route in Hua's finding, while the categorical closure and exactness assumptions must remain attached; the later unrestricted category of all admissible S-groups is not automatically interchangeable with this setting. Only this qualified rigidity ingredient is independently rechecked here.

491: #### N025 — Uniqueness of the infinitesimal kernel need not use reducedness under an explicit étale-quotient hypothesis
492: 
493: **Witness:** cote 29, scan(s) 137, definition of admissible group, uniqueness question and margin. **Kind:** own idea.
494: 
495: **Source words:** “déterminé de façon unique”; “S réduit ... nécessaire?”.
496: 
497: **Confidence:** high — Independent clopen-preimage proof establishes equality of the two kernels over arbitrary S when the quotients are finite étale and the kernels geometrically injective; reducedness is unused.
498: 
499: With two exact sequences having geometrically injective kernels Uᵢ and finite étale quotients Γᵢ over an arbitrary S, each composite U₁→G→Γ₂ lands in the identity section because its inverse image is clopen and contains every geometric point. Thus U₁⊂U₂ and conversely, so uniqueness does not require S reduced under these precise hypotheses; this answers the margin question about uniqueness and does not remove reducedness from the separate rigidity assertion.
