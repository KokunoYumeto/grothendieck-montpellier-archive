315: ## Legibility and limits
316: 
317: The typescripts are substantially easier to read than the rapid manuscript sheets, but carbon-copy gaps, typewriter overstriking, and handwritten replacements still require explicit doubt. The greatest uncertainty is in the opening and right edge of 096, the dense changes in 100–103, the short algebraic proof transitions in 108–110, the very compressed main text and upside-down margin of 131, the marginal neighborhood hypothesis and proof introduction in 135, and the overwritten functor notation and small diagonal annotation in 139. The pencil cover subtitle on 124 remains particularly faint.
318: 
319: The large displays and most clearly printed words are more secure than those passages. This is not a claim that every unmarked word is infallible: even after the second visual audit, fast cursive can support more than one reading. The small word-count illegibility markers are confined to physically unreadable cancelled words; no formula or diagram symbol has been replaced by an illegibility count. The drawings are hand-coded TikZ approximations of the visible contours, positions and labels, not metric facsimiles.
320: 
321: Pale mirrored show-through was distinguished from writing on the viewed face, especially in 108–109, 117–118, 131–132 and 139–140. The actual typescripts on reused sheets were transcribed in full, including when upside down; their provenance was not inferred from typography alone. No OCR, published edition, neighbouring transcription or contextual source was used to establish the readings. All 31 supplied pages received a sequential first reading and a sequential second audit before context access.
