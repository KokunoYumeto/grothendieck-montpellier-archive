# I–II: forms, segments, ficelles and rives

The two Pro cotes begin a succession of definitions, rather than a single finished theory. The122 indexed notes were all read beside the cited transcription passages and consolidated into97 items. There are26 manuscript scan markers for I and18 for II. The dates are5 and6June1986. The line numbers in this dossier are physical lines of the supplied combined transcription, not the earlier notes' per-page line numbers. The accompanying doubt table and uncertainty macros were read as documentary evidence; no ink reading was changed.

## I. The successive one-dimensional models

The opening dimension-zero model is a set of lieux with an equivalence relation. The recursive higher-dimensional promise is not implemented there. For dimension one the data become segments with a support and a two-element boundary. Several earlier drafts are crossed out; their extra clauses must not be added to the live axiom list. F1 supplies a unique subsegment joining an endpoint to another point of that segment. F2 separates the two pieces at an internal point and imposes a side-containment condition. Its original nonempty-interior clause later becomes an optional divisibility condition. F3 concerns branch germs, with a marginal strengthening needed for filtering common initial subsegments. F4 glues at a regular lieu; its composite contains the union and need not equal that union. F5 requires two branches at a regular lieu. F6 arrives after a failed local-to-global argument and itself appears in two nonequivalent versions. [SA21-1–10,17–36; C-SA21-1-001–032.]

These distinctions produce several definite results. F1 and the separation/containment parts of F2 imply support injectivity and uniqueness for fixed endpoint pairs inside one ambient segment. Divisibility forces infinitely many lieux and subsegments; after it is removed, a reduced network can instead be an arbitrary simple graph. The support order need not be total. A dense partial order with locally filtered strict cones supplies the intended interval model. In a single ambient segment the converse gives such an order, and choosing the other endpoint reverses it. Hua2026, §76, provides a precisely matched later treatment of that bounded result. Its public Lean definitions and relevant theorem statements were inspected, but no Lean compilation was run here. The general orientation problem is outside that result. [R-SA21-support, R-SA21-infinite, R-SA21-orders; Hua2026.]

The counterexamples must retain their scope. A dense ordinal-sum diamond satisfies the ambient axioms, including strong F6, but the union of two of its successive intervals does not inherit F4: the necessary composite includes the omitted branch. Thus arbitrary-subset inheritance is false. The rational fork Q⊕(Q⊔Q) is connected in the incidence sense and locally filtered but has two points with no common upper bound. It confirms the author's own withdrawal of the local-to-global claim and fails F6 exactly where expected. A rational chain with two incomparable maximal endpoints satisfies weak interior-F6 but not the stronger whole-support version. This last example does not refute the separate equal-endpoint branch lemma; it identifies a missing containment step in its weak-F6 proof. [C-SA21-1-018,027–029,031; R-SA21-restriction, R-SA21-fork, R-SA21-weakF6.]

Two principal unresolved native steps remain clearly identifiable. Global orientation requires coherence of the prescribed identifications of two-element endpoint and branch sets. The elementary transport-graph reformulation says that trivial holonomy gives two orientation classes, but the dossier does not claim the network axioms force that holonomy to vanish. The final equal-endpoint intersection proof also stops unfinished. The regular-end prolongation corollaries and F5/filter-basis equivalence are kept as separate obligations; neither is silently supplied by the single-segment order theorem. [MP-SA21-007,009,010.]

## II. Realizations and the exact ficelle convention

The geometric chapter starts with a quotient construction and a particular meaning of regular immersion: after collapsing a closed subset, its image has a conical neighborhood with compact base. This is distinct from an algebraic regular immersion and from a product collar along the original subset. The quotient-subspace embedding lemma is independently proved without unnecessary normality or closedness of the intermediate subspace. A finite simplicial pair has the asserted conical quotient neighborhood: after barycentric subdivision, a piecewise linear height function gives a product away from the collapsed subpolyhedron and a compact-to-Hausdorff cone homeomorphism after collapse. This proves the precise finite-polyhedral case, not an undefined theorem about every tame category. [C-SA21-2-001–005; R-SA21-quotient-embedding, R-SA21-cones.]

A ficelle has two disjoint closed nonopen end subsets, conic regularity at the contracted ends, and connectedness of the two punctured end-quotients. The literal definition allows disconnected ends and disconnected ambient spaces. Two disjoint intervals, with their respective left endpoints grouped into one end and their right endpoints into the other, satisfy every axiom. This refutes the later necessity of ambient connectedness under the unrestricted convention. Reduction to two point ends is valid; a reduced ficelle is connected. For connected nontrivial finite polyhedra reduced structures always exist, using two leaves of a spanning tree, and uniqueness of the unordered reduced end pair characterizes an arc. These results have explicit independent proofs and finite graph corroboration; they do not classify all nonreduced structures. [C-SA21-2-006–011,023–025; R-SA21-disconnected, R-SA21-polyhedra.]

The interval claim needs the same care. A={0,1}, B={1/2} defines a ficelle on [0,1] omitted by the unrestricted initial/final-interval classification. Requiring both ends to be connected restores that classification. Enlarging a regularly immersed end can destroy the other punctured quotient's connectedness; the author retracts the assertion, and the tripod example checks the failure even when the enlarged end is connected and contractible. The conjecture that all oriented structures are inclusion-equivalent is also withdrawn. [C-SA21-2-026–028; R-SA21-interval, R-SA21-tripod.]

Internal lieux cut a ficelle into pieces. The author first omits, then adds, regularity at the cut. The two-cone lemma requires a neighborhood-cover hypothesis absent from the literal statement. Both the counterexample and the repaired cover argument are given. A further current-run finding concerns contraction inside one side: on a tripod, choose the cutting point in the b-arm and collapse the entire a-arm including the center. The image of end a is now an interior point of an interval, and removing it disconnects that interval. The contracted set is closed, connected, contractible and regularly immersed; it lies in the indicated side. Thus the expanded side-contained claim is false if that side includes its end. The older condition excluding both ends avoids this example. The uncertainty in the U/X notation is preserved as a scan question. An independent reader checked the finite example. [C-SA21-2-012,015,017; R-SA21-cone-cover, R-SA21-side-contraction.]

The cutting and gluing assertions retain actual end spaces and gluing homeomorphisms. They give an object-level cutting/pushout relationship and the count n+1 pieces with n gluings. A category-level equivalence requires a stated morphism convention; endpoint-preserving homeomorphisms are the dossier's explicit reconstruction, not a quotation from the page. Disjoint loci are not automatically compatible: alternating two-point cuts of a circle exhibit the difference. The property-P proposals impose conditions on ends, cuts and links and remain category-dependent programs. [C-SA21-2-018–033; MP-SA21-013,016,022.]

## Rives, spectra and mailles

The rive algebra is the filtered colimit of clopen algebras of punctured neighborhoods. It records clopen germs, not individual connected components. In a compact conical model it is the clopen algebra of the link. The algebra can be infinite, as for a Cantor link; two connected links of different dimension can give the same two-element algebra. A global nontrivial birive along a disconnected node also need not separate at every stalk. These are bounded information-loss statements, not a refutation of reconstruction from enriched pieces and all their attaching maps. [SA21-64–70; R-SA21-rives.]

Three spaces must be distinguished: the global Stone spectrum of the rive ring, a relative Stone spectrum over the node, and the étalé space of ring elements. The page's implicit Spec notation is made precise only as a declared relative-Stone reconstruction. The map from neighborhood sections to sections of the restricted sheaf is always injective. For closed A in paracompact Hausdorff X it is an isomorphism; the exact opened source is Schapira2023, Proposition10.1.2(iii), which points back to Godement1958. Hua2026, §77, gives the injective rive-to-relative-boundary comparison under its specified topology. It expressly excludes the full cut and its regularity. [C-SA21-2-034–040; R-SA21-sheaves; Gillam2011.]

The full cut, the partial cut retaining two copies of the node, and the maille criterion consequently remain separate. Boolean algebra alone does not prove regularity transfer or the equivalence between the maille conditions and the partial cut being a ficelle. The final symmetry assertion for two node-and-birive pairs is announced without a supplied proof. The later cotes change the principal objects to figures and ateliers; that development does not retroactively supply these missing topological constructions. [C-SA21-2-042–046; MP-SA21-018–021.]

## What the earlier notes contribute

The Pro notes correctly distinguish deleted drafts, hypotheses lost in later summaries, the unfinished orientation and maille steps, and the difference between a graph, a poset and enriched gluing data. Their useful explicit examples are identified as earlier-run ideas and independently checked here. The finite-polyhedron proofs are retained at their actual scope. The new side-contraction counterexample is labelled as a finding of this consolidation. The source-line conventions for entries116 and119 are updated, and entry110's Esquisse locator is corrected from §6 to §5, printedpp29–30, for the Isom comparison.

Modern finite-space results support weak homotopy or simple-homotopy comparisons under stated hypotheses. Clader's particular inverse system additionally uses a quotient and a corrected continuity argument; it does not license a raw finite-space inverse-limit homeomorphism for arbitrary connected compact Hausdorff spaces. The exit-path theorem of Ayala–Francis–Rozenblyum retains conically smooth stratified-homotopy data. These precise comparisons constrain the earlier pro-object planning conjecture, while leaving the native definitions primary. [McCord1966; BarmakMinian2008; Clader2009,2016; AFR2019; Stacks09XV,08ZY.]
