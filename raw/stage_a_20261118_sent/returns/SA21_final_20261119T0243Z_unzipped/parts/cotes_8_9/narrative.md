## The progression of the mathematics

VIII begins by organizing a structure on elementary specimens, initially called a **magasin**, through incidence, refinement, and interior disjointness. In the first full version, the two orders satisfy incidence ≤ refinement; a refinement has a least receiving face; open refinement means that this carrier is the whole receiving stratum. Interior disjointness is symmetric and irreflexive, hereditary under open refinement, and holds for every pair of different faces of a common stratum. The primitive data are not merely a topological space or a face poset. The different relations record different information. Figures are compatible lower subsets, and refinements of figures are locally detectable and glue over compatible covers. The atlas reformulation needs an additional stability condition for infinite gluing; this condition must not be omitted merely because finite gluing has the usual sheaf shape. See C-SA21-8-001–014, especially scans 8–20.

The local objects called **lieux** enter through nonempty interior shadows. A crucial distinction is relational: axiom L2 says that disjointness of strata is detected by universal disjointness of the lieux in their interior shadows. It does not merely say that two sets of lieux have empty intersection, unless different lieux are already known to be disjoint. L2 implies L1, the nonemptiness of each interior shadow. The suggestion that L1 should imply L2 in practical examples is qualified and does not delimit a theorem. Our four-stratum model shows that no such implication holds under the abstract axioms alone; it does not disprove a properly delimited geometric version. C-SA21-8-009 and its separately labelled reconstruction C-SA21-8-009R preserve that distinction.

The set-theoretic case is presented in VIII scan 18 as a collection of elementary set figures closed under taking principal subfigures and containing the singleton figure at each point. Several equivalent-looking presentations then compete: a collection of figures, a primitive stratum set, or mixed data. The polyhedral variant changes which refinements and compatible unions are permitted, and the author explicitly leaves a systematic review of earlier propositions to be done. The proposed two-dimensional identification of oriented maquettes with combinatorial cell complexes is not precise enough to identify an external theorem: the attaching data, allowed multiplicities and category of complexes are unspecified. Its dimension-one core does match the classical oriented-edge definition of a graph, including loops. C-SA21-8-002–003 distinguishes these two levels.

The next substantial phase is an exact study of **indexed figures** and external support functions. In VIII scans 46–52, the author revisits chapter V's first proposition in an indexed setting. An isotone family of subsets, the existence of a least index containing each point, the resulting interior partition, saturation, and order detection have to be distinguished. The two infinite examples at scans 53–54 show why the conditions do not collapse in the infinite case. These are genuine counterexamples, not merely cautions: the natural-number-tail constructions have been recomputed symbolically in this run. The first separates a partition into interiors from saturation; the second shows that adding saturation still need not recover the index order. These are C-SA21-8-025–026.

The morphism discussion is especially fluid. Face-bijective strong maps give useful statements about figures; a later margin dated 11/12 July explicitly makes the face-bijection condition optional. The stronger conclusions must therefore retain that premise. General morphisms are wanted partly because a stratum might have empty image or a composite image. At VIII scan 64, however, the statement that any monotone assignment on elementary figures extends under a finiteness alternative is too broad: the assigned images need not be compatible. Our exact two-stratum model gives a counterexample while preserving every original magasin axiom. The repaired condition has to control compatible joins. This is C-SA21-8-030R, kept separate from the author's words.

The **support** construction then becomes central. For a symmetric disjointness relation, the double perpendicular operation gives closed subsets, with intersections as meets and closure of unions as joins. Under the appropriate irreflexivity or null-element convention, this is a complete ortholattice. It need not be a Boolean algebra, and its order embedding into a powerset need not preserve the ambient intersection operation. The pages themselves withdraw a proposed natural-support faithfulness statement for topological pseudosegments, cordes and ficelles in a 2 July margin at VIII scan 71; the still-visible assertion on scan 73 must be read under that correction. Full faithfulness later yields Boolean behavior only for subcollections of one admissible figure, not globally across incompatible figures. See C-SA21-8-033–039.

The separation asked for at VIII88 is now supplied by the team’s finite certificate R-SA21-9E-005, attached to the later IX124 question. On a four-vertex path orthoset with equality incidence and refinement, private points and pair points for nonorthogonal pairs give a normalized faithful external support. A private point outside a middle-vertex support is uncovered by all supports orthogonal to that vertex, so full faithfulness fails. The compatible middle pair also generates the non-Boolean ortholattice O6 in the virtual support lattice. MP-SA21-8-12 therefore has an explicit finite answer in this audit; proof_audits.md §N distinguishes the direct pointwise check made here from the IX reader’s saved computation. The theorem that assumes full faithfulness remains valid.

VIII finishes with an extended worked model based on an arbitrary poset L. It has point strata P_a and interval strata S_ab for a<b. The formulas involve the order of endpoints, a separate face relation and open refinement. Finite compatible configurations are organized by endpoint flags. The number of figures on a prescribed n-vertex flag is 2^(n−1), since each adjacent interval can be included independently. Finite prefigures, component separation, omitted endpoints, composition of segments and irredundant representatives are worked out in detail. The hypothesis H—every minimal element is least and every maximal element is greatest—is explicitly needed for several support/shadow identifications. It is not an optional convenience that can be lost when a later page refers back to an earlier result. The least closed constructible support assertion on scan 118 remains marked “À vérifier.” C-SA21-8-040–052 records the formulas and their precise dependencies.

IX initially revisits the interval construction, adding formal half intervals. The first draft excludes half intervals at certain extremal points; the bounded-segment residual construction at scans 24–26 explicitly reverses that convention. Removing endpoints retains the interval strata as formal objects even when their ordinary set interiors would be empty. Half intervals at minimal and maximal remaining points and the whole-interval symbol [J] are required. Thus the empty-set support of a formal interval is not a license to erase that stratum. The two drafts must be read together. The proper-interval literature does not settle all this additional residual and boundary data.

The compactology proposal is another addition, not part of the bare primitive object. A chosen family of compact strata is to control finiteness intersections and allowed locally finite figures. The author announces a Borel–Lebesgue type condition and richness under subdivision, but does not formulate the full proposed axiom here. These are open steps on the pages, not an established compactness theorem.

## The terminology change and the final spatial objects

At IX scans 38–41, the words **atelier** and **magasin** exchange roles. From that point, the atelier is the primitive stratum structure, while a magasin is a chosen collection of figures attached to it. A stratum is an element X of A, its multistratum is the principal incidence lower set, and a figure is an actual subset of A. This is a declared change of terminology, not a new theorem replacing all the earlier axioms. It also does not retrospectively contradict the root reader's finding that the word atelier lacks a full definition in the much earlier cotes 156-1 and 156-2. See C-SA21-9-021, J156-9-05:125–162.

The **spatios** at IX scans 50–52 is a complete ortholattice. Its nonzero elements can serve as support values, with orthogonality defined by x≤Cy. An elementary spatial figure consists of a pairwise orthogonal deployment of nonzero supports together with a face order. That face order is additional data; it is not the ambient order inherited from the support lattice. The object AtSpat(Σ) is built from these ordered figures. Spatiality means that the refinement can be recovered through maps of deployments respecting incidence and support containment. These definitions are more specific than a generic orthoset and must not be identified with locales or topoi without further premises.

The representation in IX scans 58–60 is mathematically substantial. Let A be a face-closed collection of elementary spatial figures over a complete ortholattice Σ, and suppose their greatest supports are join-dense. Then

\[
\beta(S)=\{X\in A:\alpha(X)\le S\}
\]

identifies Σ with the support ortholattice of A. The source invokes a later general functoriality argument, but that later argument fails in the proposed generality. The representation itself survives: a direct proof uses join-density to show β(S)⊥=β(CS), and every closed set is β of the join of its generators. Face closure then recovers each ordered deployment. This independent proof is in proof_audits.md §B and is explicitly labelled as ours. It validates the dense representation, not the failed general sorite.

## Corrections that materially affect the dossier

The early common-face disjointness axiom is stronger than the comparable-pair condition written in a later summary. A five-stratum finite model satisfies the weaker condition and deployment injectivity, yet has two nondisjoint faces of a common stratum. Thus injection of deployments alone does not repair the weakening. If the formulation separately requires each deployment to be an actual spatial figure, its well-typedness supplies the omitted pairwise orthogonality. The ink and the scope of that requirement need checking. C-SA21-8-018R supplies the model; the issue also affects C-SA21-9-024.

VIII scan 103 contains a concrete miscalculation for the poset a<b,c<b. Under the stated endpoint formulas, P_a is perpendicular to both P_b and S_ab. Thus its perpendicular is {P_b,S_ab}, and its double perpendicular is {P_a}. The printed singleton calculation omits S_ab. The separate computation for the entire elementary figure {P_a,P_b,S_ab} is correct: its perpendicular is empty, so its support is the whole five-stratum magasin, larger than its refinement shadow. The intended counterexample to a whole-support identity therefore survives, although the singleton computation does not. All original axioms are checked in the finite certificate. The possible margin “Cancelé/Calculé” remains undecided.

At IX scans 63–65, two different functoriality formulas must be separated. The author gives a nonsurjective injection between inequality-disjointness sets; it refutes preservation of orthocomplement. It does not refute the weaker double-polar closure formula, because those particular closure operators are identities. Our separate two-point example does: the source has no orthogonal pairs, the target has inequality orthogonality, and a bijection preserves disjointness vacuously while failing closure compatibility. The valid remaining inclusion is proved directly. The correction continues in IX09–13, so this contribution does not assert what the author finally settled after scan 65.

The reading table contains 63 alarms. Most concern missing primes, free variables, endpoint inequalities, omitted nonemptiness, and the distinction between incidence and positional order. One initially suspected terminology issue was resolved and removed from the alarm table: the unusual null-element sense of “antireflexive” on IX61 agrees with Birkhoff's 1948 convention, so it is not evidence of a mathematical or transcription error. The ambiguous reference at IX57 to VIII page 309/39 points by mathematical content to the set-figure classification at manuscript page 9; no page number is silently changed.

## What the opened literature establishes

The polarity mechanism was known before 1986. Birkhoff's *Lattice Theory*, revised edition (1948), chapter IV §5 Theorem 9 and its corollary, pp. 54–56, and §6 Theorem 10, p. 56, supply double-polar closure and Galois-connection foundations, including the relevant null-element convention. The scanned primary book was opened. This supports class E for the mechanism, not an assertion that the full atelier axioms were standard. [Birkhoff1948](https://math.chapman.edu/~jipsen/summerschool/Birkhoff%201948%20Lattice%20Theory%20Revised%20Edition.pdf)

There is one especially close later theorem match. Paseka and Vetterlein, *Categories of Orthosets and Adjointable Maps* (first arXiv circulation 8 January 2025; journal publication 27 May 2025), Lemma 2.3, gives the restriction/double-polar isomorphism under an explicit perpendicular-detection density hypothesis. Taking the subset to be the lieux and the detecting family to be an interior shadow matches the support reduction on VIII78. It is classified B as an independent established mathematical generalization, with no claim of historical influence. Proposition 2.4 gives the join-dense support reconstruction core of IX58–60. Lemmas 3.5–3.7 and Theorem 6.7 provide closure-compatible functoriality for adjointable single-valued maps; they do not assert complement preservation for every such map or treat arbitrary atelier correspondences. The publisher's 2025 date controls over the arXiv record's inconsistent journal-year field. [PasekaVetterlein2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10773-025-06031-4)

Jenča's *Orthogonality Spaces Associated with Posets* (first arXiv circulation 16 June 2022; *Order* 40, 2023, 575–588) defines exactly the proper-interval relational core in §4 and proves the finite orthogonal-interval endpoint-flag lemma 4.1. Theorem 4.12 requires a **bounded** poset; that qualification is visible in the theorem even if an abstract summary is broader. The paper omits Grothendieck's point strata, half intervals, carrier order, H-dependent formulas and residual constructions. Accordingly, only the exact interval core and flag subclaim receive partial-development credit; the extra assertions retain bounded negative-search status. [Jenca2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08113)

Serre's *Trees* (1980), I §2.1 Definition 1, p. 13, supplies the graph encoding. Björner's 1984 regular CW face-poset theorem, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 3.1, pp. 8 and 11, is a precise neighboring result, but does not identify the unspecified oriented-maquette equivalence. Ehrhard's *Finiteness Spaces* has a useful finite-intersection polarity (§1, p. 4 of the author version dated 20 October 2003; published online 15 July 2005), but does not contain the atelier compact-stratum or Borel–Lebesgue axioms. The SGA4 Exposé II equalizer is a formal gluing comparison, not an exact proof of these custom restriction maps.
