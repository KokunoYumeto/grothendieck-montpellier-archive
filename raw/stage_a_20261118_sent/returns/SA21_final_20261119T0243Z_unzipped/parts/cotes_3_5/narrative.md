## The development in his order

III begins with networks recovered from cuts. Finite compatible sets, compatible complements and component relations are the primary data. The first complement-injectivity argument already needs more than bare symmetric compatibility. Ordered tronçons then provide a worked model: dense posets with common upper and lower bounds and recursively well-behaved strict cones. The central successful argument recovers an order up to reversal from betweenness, and recovers betweenness from two-point cuts. It does not characterize every abstract family of cutting relations.

The second half of III tests ordinary topology against this structure. The transition is substantive. The topology of places of an actual ficelle is not automatically the topology of every abstract tronçon. Two explicit constructions in this run separate these claims: twin rational points violate T0 and sobriety, while a strict-coordinate order on R² gives the Euclidean topology without a total order. The author’s own attempted closure argument fails for a different reason and is abandoned. His ensuing boundary, complement and component language is expressly moderated, rather than ordinary topological frontier and connectedness. The later partial Boolean framework must be read with its compatibility relation still present. Its clopen-universal-compatibility hope is not a consequence of the abstract closure axioms alone.

IV changes the primitive objects repeatedly. It starts with topological closed strata and their open residuals, then passes to multistrata, places and shadows, and finally to figures with two orders: subfigure inclusion and subdivision. Each revision has a mathematical cause. A subfamily need not preserve the residuals, so the correct subfigure is a lower subfamily. The global point-shadow picture does not accommodate cords as the author intends them. Entrywise refinement can fail to extend to a subdivision, so subdivision becomes primitive. A prescribed boundary subdivision can require alteration of the larger augmented stratum; the literal union recipe is withdrawn in that abstract setting.

Two distinct representation gaps matter. Ordinary complete join-irreducibility does not make bounded joins become unions of strata: M3 is a fully checked counterexample. A family of subsets with least neighborhoods need not separate the underlying points. The later VI formulation explicitly replaces ordinary irreducibility with complete join-primality, resolving the first gap. This change should appear as a later strengthening, not as an unspoken assumption in IV.

Virtual support is formed from disjointness by double orthogonality. Its complete ortholattice structure is sound; its presumed Booleanity fails. The finite certificates include a connected seven-element multistratum model with eleven figures and six closed supports, and an independently constructed ten-element model with sixteen figures and twelve closed supports. The computation verifies the compatible-lower-ideal figure axioms, not merely an unrelated orthogonality graph. Identity subdivision supplies the limited subdivision requirements at this stage by a separate manual check. Later additional moderation axioms are outside the counterexample’s asserted scope.

V gives an unusually concrete test of the abstract language. An admissible family is a preorder on its support, or equivalently an Alexandrov topology. The preliminary sorite adds the membership-coverage condition missing from the earlier set-family definition. Its correction of a failed independence example is deferred to VIII, where the exact infinite constructions have been located by the VIII reader. The set/preorder model makes refinement, subdivision, lower subfigures, compatibility and infima precise. It also supplies an actual extension of a prescribed subdivision of a lower subfigure.

Two failures remain inside this concrete treatment. First, the claimed connectedness of all elementary set figures is false already on a two-point underlying set. The external-product construction used to connect them creates an empty residual at its proposed top stratum. Second, retaining an old closed stratum does not retain its residual: a two-point example refutes the common-stratum lemma and one direction of the claimed b,c,d equivalence. This is separate from the author’s own later retraction of the least-extension characterization.

The final general topology theorem survives. If T is closed in (S,τ) and σ is finer than the topology induced on T, the least finer extension has closed sets A∪B, where A is τ-closed and B is σ-closed in T. Its closure formula preserves the old closures of every subset of S minus T. The four weaker conditions tested on the manuscript do not characterize it, as the author’s three-point example shows. The final display’s intersection in place of union is a high-impact reading alarm. The theorem, its corrected characterization and the rejected intermediate criteria therefore receive different records.

## Literature fate and evidential limits

The class totals here are A0, B3, C6, D73, E17, F0. D is an explicitly bounded negative search result, including many identifiable constructions, internal self-corrections and independently checked elementary statements. It does not assert that the mathematics is currently open or that no treatment exists. Exact query groups and accessed primary references are recorded for every claim.

The exact new matches are limited to the cut-reconstruction theorem and the negative answer on Boolean virtual supports; source check HUA records provenance and verification limits. The public code was read but not compiled. Classical order, Boolean and polarity ingredients are compared with Birkhoff, Stong and Kochen–Specker. Their scope does not automatically include the additional moderated or subdivision axioms. Courcelle’s reconstruction work has an explicit finite/countable convention. The modern Waas–Woolf–Yokura quotient-stratification theorem is a precise partial comparison. Quillen’s Theorem A covers the principal-ideal homotopy criterion, while the stronger universal phrase on V47 still requires an exact definition. Borges’s topology-extension paper was checked as a historical lead and does not provide the exact general statement found here.

Esquisse supplies a conceptual predecessor in its tame stratification and dévissage discussion. Its composition in1984 and publication in1997 are kept distinct. No A-class later publication of the specific III–V assertions was identified. EGA/SGA searches produced general topology references, not exact identifications; the parent reader checks original editions. Direct zbMATH access and Alexandroff full-text access were limited, and those failures are recorded. Recent project-dependent MathOverflow answers were not treated as independent resolutions.

## Research directions for the master Stage6 list

1. **Minimal two-order axioms** — C4-038/041 and MP4-14/17. Determine which restriction, extension and finite-meet assumptions imply antisymmetry and cartesian induction. A first short task should enumerate small finite two-order models satisfying explicitly selected axioms. Interest high; a few hours can separate necessary clauses, but should not claim a complete geometric theory.
2. **Separation for abstract tronçons** — C3-015 and MP3-5/6. Identify additional conditions satisfied by ficelle places that exclude the two counterexamples while retaining intended non-total examples. First compare their specialization relations with the actual ficelle model. Interest high; the counterexamples make the next question concrete.
3. **When virtual supports become Boolean** — C4-033/034/035 and MP4-12. State a geometric separation or distributivity axiom and test its necessity against the seven-element certificate. Interest high; the false unrestricted conjecture is already resolved.
4. **Intrinsic recovery of subfigure inclusion** — C5-016 and MP5-3. Determine whether refinement and subdivision alone recover the third relation in the concrete set model. First compute the automorphisms of the finite relational structures for very small supports. Interest high; a short run could produce a decisive small counterexample or a useful invariant.
5. **Relational axioms for tronçon cuts** — C3-002/005/007/008 and MP3-4. Complete a converse axiom characterization with the exact density and cone conditions. First align strict/non-strict betweenness conventions and the role of comparability. Interest medium-high; the reconstruction direction is already settled.
6. **Historical attribution and categorical form of closed-locus replacement** — C5-019/021/023–029 and MP5-6/7/8. The elementary topology theorem is checked; the next task should locate an exact earlier source and fix the universal homotopical statement. Interest medium; a few hours can settle the definition and bounded historical search.

These directions are proposed as separate tasks. The present work supplies the consolidation and verification needed to formulate them, not a development of those further programs.
