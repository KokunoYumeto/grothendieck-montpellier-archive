## VI: from figures to shadows, restriction and subdivision

The stable starting point is an atelier of figures with two orders: face inclusion `≤` and refinement `≪`. Elementary figures, called multistrata, give each figure its deployment of strata. Compatibility means that figures fit in one common figure; disjointness adds zero face intersection. A lieu is minimal nonzero for refinement. These meanings should remain primary, rather than importing the language of the user’s other research projects.

An important change occurs in VI12. “Strictly irreducible” now means complete **join-prime**: if an elementary object lies below an existing join, it lies below one of the summands. IV54 used only the weaker equality-based complete join-irreducibility. The stronger VI condition makes the representation of a figure’s subobjects as all lower subsets of its deployment legitimate. The earlier equality-only version is not enough; the neighbouring IV analysis gives the M3 obstruction. This is a mathematical strengthening in the text, not merely terminology. The corresponding one-order representation is standard before1986; the checked primary sources are Speed1968 §4C and the final corollary on p730, with the modern duality in Massas2023 Theorem2.30. [C-SA21-6-001,044; internal IV–VI link.]

The large shadow remembers all elementary refiners; the small shadow remembers only lieux. The large multiombre is intended to realize an atelier as a family of set figures. The proof exposes the need for At7, transitivity of immediate/interior refinement, so that least receiving strata compose. The small multiombre has a local isomorphism on the subfigures of one fixed figure under the locus-existence axiom, but it can forget global structure. The affine versus PL interval example is used precisely to show that point shadows need not distinguish elementary figures. This distinction later drives the failures of global set embeddings inVII. [C-SA21-6-005–008,014,018.]

There are two important finite corrections in the subatelier and morphism discussion. A nonempty lower subfamily inherits bounded joins and elementary decomposition, but it need not inherit the compatibility axiom At3. The author adds Sousat2 on VI43. The saved three-point model proves why: all subsets of cardinality≤2 form a lower family, every pair of singleton strata can be joined, yet the union of {0,1} and {1,2} is absent. Separately, the literal VI51 pullback formula that retains every nonempty closed-stratum preimage can leave an empty open stratum. The closed-stratum figure { {a},{b},{a,b,c} } pulled back to {a,b} supplies an exact witness. Removing strata with empty open preimage is an analyst’s proposed repair; it has not been inserted into the transcription. [C-SA21-6-011,016; `finite_checks_output.json`.]

The local lattice-map question has a clean answer under precise hypotheses. A map between downset lattices preserving all joins and all meets, including empty ones, is inverse image along a unique monotone map of posets. The independent proof in `formalism_and_checks.md` uses the least member of the complete filter attached to each point. No sobriety hypothesis is necessary in this complete-homomorphism setting. This does not resolve every proposed category of atelier morphisms: a map preserving only finite meets is a different object, and global reflection of refinement remains additional data. [C-SA21-6-013,043; Massas2023 Theorem2.30.]

Restriction of a refinement to a face is then expressed through the least receiving stratum map. The resulting local lattice map preserves arbitrary unions and intersections. The gluing axiom At11 asks compatible local refinements to produce one global refinement. The manuscript initially overreaches in an implication diagram, then distinguishes finite gluing from existence of infinite compatible unions. At11′ is the binary compatibility part; At11bis is a separate directed local-existence condition. These distinctions matter as much as the statements themselves. SGA4II’s sheaf condition is a useful formal comparison, but it does not prove that an arbitrary atelier has the needed joins. [C-SA21-6-022–031; SGA4II Definition2.1.]

One initially suspicious assertion survives a careful check. VI91 says the local directed-existence axiom is vacuous when all figures are of finite type. With the strong complete join-prime definition and the stated local suprema, this is correct: finite generators of each local supremum occur at a common stage; finitely many generators of the ambient figure then give a global stationary stage. The analyst’s earlier note suggesting missing compactness is explicitly withdrawn. This must not be extended to arbitrary refinement-increasing families, for whichVII poses a separate question. [C-SA21-6-040; independent proof §3.]

Subdivision is introduced through equality of disjointness profiles. A support-witness axiom allows an external witness to be found inside the ambient figure. Under that axiom and locality, a subdivision is a face-maximal refinement, with maximality for `≤`, not for `≪`. The author corrects that order distinction on the page. Extension of a subdivision to a larger figure then becomes an optional extra axiom. Stronger proposals leave the exterior face pattern unchanged or fill a subdivided boundary with one elementary object. A triangle with one subdivided boundary edge shows why strict simplicial elementary fillers fail. Łojasiewicz’s simultaneous semianalytic triangulation and Baro’s normal triangulation prove related existence statements in specific categories, not the manuscript’s unchanged-exterior uniqueness. [C-SA21-6-032–039; Łojasiewicz1964 §3 Theorems1–2; Baro2010 Theorem4.3.]

## VII: reconstruction through loci, then a restart without divisibility

VII begins by tightening set refinement: intersections must be empty or faces of the source stratum in the strict/polyhedral version. It also attempts a combinatorial simplex criterion that is false as written. A square’s nonempty face poset has every stated property—finite, greatest element, meets when lower bounded, at least two immediate predecessors at every nonminimal face—but has9 faces, whereas the nonempty subsets of its4 minimal elements number15. The correct comparison uses Boolean principal intervals, as in Stanley1991 §1. [C-SA21-7-002–004.]

The locus axioms L1–L3 and the compatibility/locality criteria lead to a reconstruction from the elementary set M, its two orders and an orthogonality graph on L. The crucial lifting axiom ML1 is strengthened during the proof. Under its stronger form, every refinement has a least receiving face, and compatibility can be recovered by testing exclusive locus shadows. Some abbreviated lemmas omit a compatible/common-ambient hypothesis and would otherwise collapse the distinct orders. Those are reading and hypothesis alarms, not opportunities to silently repair the source. The final ML theorem is identifiable, but its whole equivalence has not been independently certified in this run. No exact published At/ML theorem was found in the bounded searches. [C-SA21-7-007–013.]

Replacing L by a dense test family Λ exposes another failure. The author tries to retain only a disjointness graph, then writes “Non” and recognizes that common-refiner lifting and shadow-intersection properties were not derived. The Prat/PratΛ formulations therefore keep the missing structure explicit. Finite figures can be forced by elementary data when principal face ideals are finite; infinite figures still require a choice of admitted family. A finite-type claim needs hereditary finite generation if every subfigure is also to remain of finite type. [C-SA21-7-014–016,012,051.]

The set-embedding discussion retracts several plausible converses. Local subfigure-lattice isomorphisms do not imply global injectivity. Adding injection can recover compatibility reflection under the specified criterion, but still does not make strict geometric refinement coincide with the weaker set refinement. These are separate failure modes and are not merged into one vague “nonfaithfulness.” [C-SA21-7-017–019.]

## Virtual supports: what survives and what fails

For A⊆M, take its orthogonal A⊥ and define virtual support closure by A⊥⊥. The closed sets form a complete ortholattice, with intersection as meet, closure of union as join and A⊥ as orthocomplement. This formalism is not automatically Boolean or orthomodular. A dense family Λ can recover the lattice only when it detects disjointness; ordinary density alone is insufficient. When L2 holds and all distinct lieux are orthogonal, the support lattice is the powerset of L. [C-SA21-7-020–023.]

The support-realization program seeks an actual set P with supports |X| respecting refinement, compatible intersections, disjointness, subdivision and lieu decomposition. Some useful implications are proved. In particular, if disjointness is exactly represented by set-disjointness and every point of P has a singleton support, then the support lattice is P(P). This is a conditional theorem, not a proof that arbitrary ateliers have such a realization. The proposed two-strand example does not refute it: the author explicitly notes that the natural endpoint representation fails the disjointness iff clause. Its rectangle closure calculation gives a distributivity obstruction, which the saved finite-grid calculation reproduces; it does not prove nonmodularity or verify the whole geometric model. [C-SA21-7-027–033.]

The strongest fully checked obstruction concerns the local Boolean algebra expected inside one compatible figure. The manuscript first proposes a formal ortholattice lemma, marks it false, computes a four-object example, then hopes L1 may rescue the intended local statement. It does not. Let orthogonality be the path A′—A—B—B′ and let figures be all cliques, with both orders inclusion. The elementary objects and lieux are the singletons. Every singleton is its own interior lieu, which is permitted by the written L1. The finite program checks At1–At7, including strong complete join-primality, L1–L3, C/D locality variants and the old AtSupp. For the compatible figure {A,B}, support of {A} is {A}, support of {B} is {B}, but their join is the whole four-point set. Intersecting that join with the orthocomplement of {B} gives {A,B′}, not {A}. Thus the relative-complement formula fails even with L1. [C-SA21-7-043–045; `formalism_and_checks.md` §4.]

The limitation is equally important: this model fails the optional absorption axiom AtSupp1. VII93 uses that axiom conditionally, and a later margin onVII87 calls it artificial. No refutation of a statement explicitly adding AtSupp1 is claimed. There is an exact later primary-source match for the four-point graph and its six-element lattice: Lindenhovius–Vetterlein2023 Example3.3. The paper’s first arXiv submission is dated19July2022, while the checked published version appeared10March2023; fetching v1 failed, so the exact example’s presence in that first version is not independently verified. No historical influence is inferred. Its Lemma3.4 states Dacey’s criterion for orthomodularity. Janowitz1963 supplies a verified pre1986 formulation of the missing orthomodular law. [ClassB for the exact later counterexample; bounded classD for the manuscript-specific L1 rescue, which is refuted by this run’s certificate.]

The end ofVII responds to a broader problem: without L1, nonempty elementary strata can have empty locus-based open support. Instead of forcing absorption and divisibility, the author makes interior disjointness primitive. The final VII111 Mag system has two nested orders, least receiving faces, a symmetric irreflexive interior-disjointness relation stable under immediate refinement, and disjointness of distinct faces of one elementary object. Compatibility is then defined by exclusive faces and total disjointness by all face pairs. Associated ateliers select compatible lower-set figures. VII114’s finite formula omits lowerness; the corrected version is explicitly an analyst repair. VIII8 recapitulates this exact final Mag system, so the canceled VII110 draft must not be treated as an alternative final definition. [C-SA21-7-047–052; checked VII–VIII link.]

## Literature fate and next tasks

The distribution is9 classE,2 classB,84 classD and1 classF. The high D count reflects the specificity of the changing atelier axioms and the standard of exact matching; it does not establish that all84 are original or still open. The CSG catalogue lists the manuscript as unpublished. Malgoire’s2002 seminar notice documents later exposition but supplies no published theorem. Cruz Morales’s2021 essay, *The notion of space in Grothendieck: from schemes to a geometry of forms*, discusses the program conceptually. The source searches included zbMATH, arXiv, MathOverflow, Numdam, EGA/SGA and bibliographies followed backwards. Some original older sources and zbMATH records were access-limited; their BibTeX entries are marked unverified. Public project-specific posts were used only as discovery leads, not as independent historical evidence.

The most useful next tasks are a proof consolidation of the final Mag system, an exact missing-hypothesis analysis of local support complements, and a typed account of morphisms and strict refinement. Each is separated from this consolidation in `directions.md`; no new general research program is developed here. The files retain what the pages say, what the opened literature establishes and what this run independently proves or refutes as distinct evidence layers.
