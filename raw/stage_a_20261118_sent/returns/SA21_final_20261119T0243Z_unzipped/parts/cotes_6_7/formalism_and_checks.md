## 1. Standing conventions used by the claim records

`F` denotes the set of figures, with face/subfigure order `≤` and refinement order `≪`, both partial orders, and `≤` implies `≪`. Zero is the empty figure. `M` denotes elementary figures/multistrata. A deployment is `~F={X∈M:X≤F}`, a lower set in the face order. `L` denotes the minimal nonzero objects for refinement (lieux). Compatibility of two figures means that they have a common upper bound for `≤`; disjointness means compatibility and zero face intersection. These are separate relations: incompatibility is not disjointness.

The operative definition of “strictement irréductible” in VI scan12, J156-6-02 lines77–88, is **complete join-primality**: for every family `(F_i)` whose face join exists, `X≤∨_iF_i` implies `X≤F_i` for some index. The empty family excludes zero. This is stronger than the equality-based complete join-irreducibility in IV scan54, J156-4-07 line100, checked by the neighbouring reader. The claims here use the stronger VI definition, including when VII112 repeats the terminology. Under At1 (nonempty figure set and all bounded face joins) and At2 (every figure is the join of its elementary subfigures), deployments identify the principal ideal below each figure with the full downset lattice of its deployment. Global joins still require existence of a figure upper bound.

At3 is the stratumwise compatibility criterion: if every `X≤F` and `Y≤G` are compatible, then `F,G` are compatible. At4 defines refinement stratumwise: `F≪G` iff each elementary `X≤F` refines some elementary `Y≤G`. At5 gives, for `X∈M` refining a figure `G`, a least `Y≤G` receiving `X`. Write `X≪°Y` when that least receiving stratum is `Y`; equivalently `X≪Y` and there is no proper face `Y'<Y` with `X≪Y'`. This is not a strict refinement relation: `X≪°X` always holds. At6 says compatible elementary objects with `X≪Y` satisfy `X≤Y`. At7 says immediate/interior refinement `≪°` is transitive. The wording “immediate” must not be confused with a cover in the refinement order.

The large shadow of `F` is `{X∈M:X≪F}`; the small shadow is its intersection with `L`. A multiombre is the figure whose closed strata are the shadows of the elementary subfigures; a family of shadows and its union are different types. L1 (VI At8; VII scan20 lines96–98) requires an interior lieu in every elementary figure: `∀X∈M ∃x∈L, x≪°X`. L2 (VI At9) tests disjointness on lieux: `F⊥G` iff all `x∈L` refining F and `y∈L` refining G satisfy `x⊥y`. L3 (VI At10) says that the interior lieux of two **distinct compatible** elementary figures are pairwise disjoint. The compatibility hypothesis is essential.

For `F'≪F` and `G≤F`, restriction `F'|G` is the figure with deployment `{X≤F':X≪G}`. AtD is disjointness locality on the common face of compatible ambient figures; AtD0 is preservation of disjointness under refinement. AtCL(spac) tests compatibility by disjointness of exclusive small shadows after removing the small shadow of the common face. The polyhedral variant additionally restricts the common face. At11 glues compatible local refinements on a cover by subfigures; At11′ is its binary compatibility part; At11bis supplies the separate infinite-existence part. “Spacious” changes usage during these chapters, so individual claims retain their dated criterion instead of identifying every use of that adjective.

For any symmetric irreflexive relation `⊥` on a set T, `A⊥={t∈T:∀a∈A,t⊥a}` and `cl(A)=A⊥⊥`. Orthoclosed subsets form a complete ortholattice, with meet intersection, join `cl(union)`, and complement `A⊥`. Neither distributivity nor orthomodularity follows. For figures, zero must be accounted for; on M the bottom is empty. The relation on T is the manuscript's disjointness, not compatibility.

## 2. Complete homomorphisms of downset lattices

For arbitrary posets P,Q, a map `h:Down(Q)→Down(P)` preserving **all** unions and **all** intersections, including empty ones, is uniquely `f⁻¹` for a monotone map `f:P→Q`. No sobriety assumption is needed. This checks C-SA21-6-013 and distinguishes complete lattice homomorphisms from frame homomorphisms, which need preserve only finite intersections.

Fix p∈P and put `U_p={D∈Down(Q):p∈h(D)}`. Preservation of top makes U_p nonempty; preservation of arbitrary meets makes `D_p=∩U_p` a member of U_p. Preservation of bottom gives D_p nonempty. Since `D_p=∪_{q∈D_p}↓q`, preservation of unions gives a q∈D_p with p∈h(↓q). Minimality of D_p then implies D_p⊆↓q, and q∈D_p gives the reverse inclusion. Thus D_p=↓q. Antisymmetry makes q unique. Define f(p)=q. For every downset D, membership p∈h(D) is now equivalent to f(p)∈D. If p≤p′, every downset h(D) containing p′ contains p; taking D=↓f(p′) proves f(p)≤f(p′). The equality h=f⁻¹ and uniqueness follow.

Literature match: Massas, *B-frame Duality*, Theorem2.30, printed p14, states the equivalent complete-homomorphism/downset duality (opposite-order convention); it attributes the older theorem to Raney and de Jongh–Troelstra. Speed1968, §4 CorollaryC and §5 final corollary pp727,730, verifies that the complete join-prime representation mechanism predates1986. The original Raney PDF and de Jongh–Troelstra DOI were blocked in this run and are marked unverified, rather than silently promoted by the later citation.

## 3. Finite type and directed local stationarity

C-SA21-6-040: suppose every figure is a finite face join of elementary figures, with the strong complete join-prime definition above. Let `(F_i′)` be increasing and directed for `≤`, with each `F_i′≪F`, and suppose every restricted family `(F_i′|X)` for `X≤F` elementary has a face supremum H_X in the atelier. Then the family is eventually constant. This is the precise hypothesis relevant to VI scan91 and At11bis on VI scan81; it does not say that arbitrary chains of refinements stabilize.

For fixed X, write H_X as a finite join of elementary Y_j. Because each Y_j is completely join-prime and H_X is the join of the increasing family, Y_j≤F_i′|X for some i. Directedness yields a common i_X, so F_i′|X=H_X for i≥i_X. Write F=X_1∨…∨X_n. For every elementary Y≤F_i′, At4 gives Y≪X for some elementary X≤F; complete join-primality and the finite decomposition of F give X≤X_k for some k. Thus Y lies in F_i′|X_k. Consequently F_i′ is the union of its restrictions to these finitely many X_k. Choose a common i dominating their i_Xk. All deployments then stabilize, so all figures stabilize.

An initial analyst note in `reading_analysis.md` suspected that compactness was missing. That suspicion is withdrawn: complete join-primality plus the stated finite generation provides exactly the needed compactness. The earlier note is retained as an audit trail, with this correction. This is an independent proof, not a computation or a proof attributed to the manuscript.

## 4. P4: the local support-complement formula still fails with L1

Let T={A′,A,B,B′}, with orthogonality the edges A′—A—B—B′. Let figures be all cliques, including empty. Put both orders equal to inclusion. Elementary objects and lieux are the singleton figures. The program `check_finite_examples.py` exhaustively verifies every finite quantifier in At1–At7 (including the stronger complete join-prime version of At2), L1–L3, CL(spac), D0, D, C(spac), and the older support-witness axiom AtSupp/At13. Its output is `finite_checks_output.json`.

Here is why the model meets those hypotheses. Any bounded family of cliques has a clique union. Complete join-primes are exactly singletons, and all figures are their joins. Pairwise stratum compatibility is precisely the clique condition, so At3 holds. Refinement and its stratumwise definition both mean inclusion. A singleton in a clique has itself as its unique receiving stratum. Compatible refinement is face inclusion. Interior refinement between elementary objects is equality, hence transitive. L1 holds because each singleton is its own interior lieu; there is no proper nonempty face in which it could land. This is allowed by the written L1 and is not a density loophole. L2 is the statement that two cliques have clique union and empty intersection iff every cross-pair is an edge. L3 and CL(spac) reduce to the same graph relation. D0 is heredity to subsets. D compares intersections inside compatible unions. The C(spac) sufficiency test detects exactly a missing cross-edge. For AtSupp on an elementary singleton, the refinement is either empty or that singleton, so a required witness can be taken to be the singleton itself.

Take the compatible figure F₀={A,B}. The closed supports of its two interior strata are S_A={A}, S_B={B}, while `S_{A∪B}=T`, because A and B have no common orthogonal vertex. Also `S_B⊥={A,B′}`. Therefore

`S_{F₀\{B}}={A} ≠ T∩S_B⊥={A,B′}`.

This refutes the relative-complement formula proposed on VII94 and the renewed hope on VII99 that L1 might suffice. It also refutes the unrestricted formal ortholattice lemma crossed out on VII97–98. **It does not refute a version with the additional optional absorption axiom AtSupp1**: that axiom fails here, since cl({A′})={A′,B}. The manuscript marks AtSupp1 as optional on VII93 and calls it artificial in the later margin on VII87. A claim adding it must be separately checked.

An exact independent literature match exists: Lindenhovius–Vetterlein, *A Characterisation of Orthomodular Spaces by Sasaki Maps*, Example3.3, uses the same four-vertex path and its six-element nonorthomodular “benzene ring” lattice. Lemma3.4 identifies Dacey's maximal-orthogonal-subset condition for orthomodularity. The 2023 published version was read; arXiv records first circulation19July2022, but fetching v1 failed, so the presence of this exact example in v1 is not independently verified. No influence or historical dependence on Grothendieck is inferred. Janowitz1963 p1241 supplies a verified pre1986 formulation of the orthomodular law. This points to a missing algebraic hypothesis, not to a resolution of every geometric realization problem in these pages.

## 5. Other finite checks

**Weak simplex criterion (C-SA21-7-004).** The nine nonempty faces of a square have a greatest face. Any two faces with a nonempty common lower bound have an infimum. Every nonminimal face has at least two immediate predecessors. There are four minimal faces, but their nonempty Boolean subset poset has15 elements. Hence the conditions written at VII14–15 do not characterize simplices. All these conditions are checked in the saved program. Stanley1991 §1, p319, gives the standard Boolean-principal-interval definition of a simplicial poset; it is a corrected comparison, not a theorem proving the manuscript's weaker criterion.

**Naive inverse images (C-SA21-6-016).** On {a,b,c}, the figure with closed strata {a},{b},{a,b,c} has nonempty open strata. Pull it back along inclusion {a,b}→{a,b,c}, retaining every nonempty closed preimage as VI51 prescribes. The resulting closed strata {a},{b},{a,b} give an empty open stratum at the top. Thus the written formula does not always give an admissible figure. Discarding strata with empty **open** preimage is an analyst's possible repair, not a changed transcription. The certificate checks admissibility before and failure after the pullback.

**Lower subfamilies (C-SA21-6-011).** In the powerset atelier on three points, retain all subsets of size≤2. This is nonempty and lower. For F={0,1}, G={1,2}, every pair of elementary strata has its union in the family, but F∪G does not. At3 fails. VI43 itself adds Sousat2 after the insufficient assertion on VI38; the certificate checks the exact obstruction.

**Equality of the two orders (C-SA21-6-015).** In the two-element stratum chain0<1 with both orders equal, every element is minimal or covers a minimal element. Yet0 is not an interior lieu of1: it lies in the proper face0 of1. More generally, x≤X and interior x≪°X force x=X, because x is itself an eligible receiving face. Hence L1 holds exactly when M=L is discrete. VII35 explicitly states this correction to VI50–51. The code checks the two-element witness.

**Two strands (C-SA21-7-033).** For the relation on a5×5 grid in which (a,b)⊥(c,d) iff both coordinates are strictly smaller or both strictly larger, cl({(1,3)}) and cl({(3,1)}) are singletons, but the closure of their union is the full3×3 rectangle and contains (2,2). This reproduces the relation calculation behind VII82–84 and gives a distributivity witness. The finite grid is explicitly **not** a verification of the whole dense geometric atelier. The manuscript itself notes on VII82 that the natural endpoint-set realization violates Supp1 (orthogonality iff disjoint represented sets). It therefore cannot refute the singleton-realization Boolean theorem, whose hypotheses include Supp1. The calculation does not establish failure of modularity; the page's phrase involving “modulaire” should not be expanded into such a claim.
