## 1. Objects and axiom versions

A **spatios** in this part is a complete ortholattice $L$: a complete lattice with an order-reversing involution $C$, satisfying $a\wedge Ca=0$ and $a\vee Ca=1$. Write $a\perp b$ for $a\leq Cb$. Booleanity and orthomodularity are additional conditions. A complete Boolean subortholattice is a subset closed under the ambient arbitrary joins, arbitrary meets and $C$, whose induced lattice is Boolean. “Commensurable” means contained in such a subset, not merely pairwise orthogonal.

For a symmetric irreflexive relation on a set $A$, put

\[
S^\perp=\{x\in A:\forall s\in S, x\perp s\},
\qquad \Sigma_A=\{S:S=S^{\perp\perp}\}.
\]

The order is inclusion, the meet is intersection, the join is double-polar closure of union, and complement is polar. These operations produce a complete ortholattice, but need not produce an orthomodular or Boolean lattice. This is the exact background of IX105–125; see the preceding IX orthoset construction, and the opened primary comparison in Lindenhovius–Vetterlein 2023, Example 3.3 and Lemma 3.4.

The atelier countermodels below use the primitive relations after the IX41 terminology change: incidence $\unlhd$, open refinement $\ll^\circ$, and internal disjointness $\delta$. They do not replace the two orders by one relation. In the models used for the faithful-support and kernel questions, both orders happen to be equality; this is a specified model, not an identification of the notions in general. Derived refinement is then equality. The receiving-stratum axiom is immediate because each principal incidence ideal is a singleton; the clauses concerning distinct common faces are vacuous; disjointness remains symmetric and irreflexive; heredity under open refinement holds. The code enumerates each of these clauses for source and target. No additional support axiom, divisibility, compactology, spatiality, face-bijection requirement or adjointability is silently imposed.

IX134–135 uses a formal zero with three deliberately different conventions. Incidence holds from zero to every object and from every object to zero. Open refinement has zero as its least element, and a nonzero object does not openly refine zero. Disjointness holds between zero and every object, including zero itself. Thus pointed incidence is not an order, whereas pointed open refinement is an order. The manuscript expressly says this. All morphism checks retain these conventions.

### 1.1 Targeted check of the compatible residual subatelier

C114 at IX131 adds compatibility to the broad residual. This qualification is used in its proof. Suppose $X,Y$ belong to the compatible residual of a prefigure $\Phi$, and $X\ll^\circ Y'\unlhd Y$. Compatibility of $Y$ with a stratum $Z\in\Phi$ passes to the face $Y'$. Distinct compatible strata belong to a common figure and are internally disjoint. It therefore suffices to exclude $Y'=Z$. If equality held, $X\ll^\circ Z$ and $X\delta Z$ would, by symmetry and open-refinement heredity, imply $X\delta X$, contrary to irreflexivity. ## 2. Arbitrary-set relation calculus

This proves C081–085 and the support-level criterion in C118. Let $R\subseteq E\times E'$, let $\Gamma=R$ be its graph, and let $f:\Gamma\to E$, $f':\Gamma\to E'$ be the projections. Define

\[
u(S)=\{y:\exists x\in S, (x,y)\in R\},\qquad
v(T)=\{x:\exists y\in T, (x,y)\in R\}.
\]

The graph requirement excludes duplicate edges. For an arbitrary span having repeated edges, its projection need not be injective even when the induced relation map has the relevant property.

Every such $u$ preserves arbitrary unions, including the empty union. Its singleton images are pairwise disjoint exactly when every $y\in E'$ is related to at most one $x\in E$, which is exactly injectivity of $f'$. This is equivalent to preservation of disjointness. It is also equivalent to preservation of binary intersections: if a point belonged to two singleton images, the image of the empty intersection would contradict that preservation. Conversely, when the input associated with a given output is unique, membership in $u(\bigcap_i S_i)$ is equivalent to membership in every $u(S_i)$, provided the family of sets is nonempty. The same unique-input test gives

\[
u(S\setminus T)=u(S)\setminus u(T).
\]

Even the restricted difference identity for $T\subseteq S$ suffices: take $S=\{x,x'\}$, $T=\{x'\}$ to exclude a common output for distinct inputs.

Top preservation $u(E)=E'$ means that every output has at least one input, or that $f'$ is surjective. Combining this with disjointness preservation gives exactly one input for every output. Define $g:E'\to E$ to be that input; then $u=g^{-1}$. This is equivalent to complement preservation, all-meet preservation, and the other conditions in C082. Empty meets are the top condition. There is no unspoken nonempty-set hypothesis; the empty cases obey the same classification.

Zero reflection by $u$ is equivalent to every input having some output, hence to surjectivity of $f$. Zero reflection by $v$ is equivalent to surjectivity of $f'$. If $u$ preserves intersections, it reflects disjointness exactly when it reflects zero, since

\[
u(S)\cap u(T)=u(S\cap T).
\]

Consequently faithful disjointness preservation is equivalent to $f'$ injective and $f$ surjective. Together with top preservation this says $u=g^{-1}$ for a surjective $g:E'\to E$.

Finally, assuming $f'$ injective, $u$ is the direct image of an injection $E\to E'$ exactly when each input has exactly one output, namely when $f$ is bijective. By the inverse-image classification applied to the transpose, this is equivalent to $v$ preserving complements. This resolves the precise support-level question at IX133; it does not validate the separate elementary-figure claim.

The program checks these equivalences for all **689** relations with $0\leq |E|,|E'|\leq3$. That finite check detects edge and empty-set mistakes; the preceding argument supplies the arbitrary-set proof. The literature comparisons are Ore 1944, §4 and Theorem 8, and Massas 2023, Theorem 2.29. They supply the older polarity framework and the exact complete-Boolean-homomorphism comparison, rather than a claim that every line here was first proved in a later paper.

## 3. General conjugate maps and orthomodularity

For this section the exact conjugacy read at IX90, Proposition 3, is

\[
u(a)\perp b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\perp v(b).
\]

### 3.1 Formal consequences and the limits of the powerset analogy

A join-preserving map that commutes with $C$ preserves zero and top, and De Morgan gives preservation of all meets. If $a\leq Cb$, monotonicity gives $u(a)\leq u(Cb)=C u(b)$, so orthogonality is preserved. This proves C087.

Setting $a=1$ in the conjugacy gives

\[
v(b)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b\leq C u(1).
\]

Thus $u(1)=1$ iff $v$ reflects zero, proving C089. This conclusion uses neither Booleanity nor orthomodularity. The later exact orthoset analogue is Paseka–Vetterlein 2025, Lemma 3.8; its additional adjointability assumption must be retained when comparing it with bare atelier maps.

If **both** $u$ and $v$ commute with $C$, substituting $Cb$ in the conjugacy gives $u(a)\leq b\iff a\leq v(b)$. Reversing the roles gives $v\dashv u$ as well as $u\dashv v$. The unit and counit inequalities of both adjunctions imply $vu=\mathrm{id}$ and $uv=\mathrm{id}$. This independently proves the affirmative marginal answer in C088; see R002.

The converse from orthogonality and top preservation to complement preservation fails in general. On the path $0-1-2-3$, the closed sets are

\[
\varnothing,\ \{1\},\ \{2\},\ \{0,2\},\ \{1,3\},\ V.
\]

They form $O_6$. Let $a=\{1\}$, $b=\{2\}$. Map the two atoms of the four-element Boolean lattice to $a,b$, and map zero and top to zero and top. The images have zero meet and top join, so the map preserves all meets and joins in this finite lattice. They are orthogonal. But $Cb=\{1,3\}$ and $Ca=\{0,2\}$, so the images of complementary atoms are not their orthocomplements. The canonical conjugate transpose is $v=C u_R C$, where $u_R$ is the right adjoint of the join map. The code constructs it and verifies the exact conjugacy. This is R001, a counterexample even with the stronger all-meet hypothesis. It does not address an unspecified additional geometric restriction.

### 3.2 Local decomposition, Boolean pairs and orthogonal families

If $b\leq c$, any $d\perp b$ with $b\vee d=c$ satisfies $d\leq c\wedge Cb$. Hence such a $d$ exists iff

\[
c=b\vee(c\wedge Cb),
\]

and then $c\wedge Cb$ is the greatest possible $d$. This proves C090.

For C091, assume both this identity and its dual comparable-pair identity

\[
Cb=Cc\vee(Cb\wedge c).
\]

The three pieces $\alpha=b$, $\beta=c\wedge Cb$, $\gamma=Cc$ are pairwise orthogonal. The two displayed identities and De Morgan give

\[
C\alpha=\beta\vee\gamma,\quad
C\beta=\alpha\vee\gamma,\quad
C\gamma=\alpha\vee\beta.
\]

Their subset-join map therefore preserves complement and has Boolean image. Omit any zero pieces. Conversely a Boolean subortholattice containing $b,c$ satisfies both equations. The generated algebra has $2^n$ elements with $1\leq n\leq3$ in a nontrivial lattice; $n=0$ must be allowed if $0=1$.

Requiring the local identity for every comparable pair is precisely orthomodularity. If every orthogonal pair generates a Boolean subortholattice, apply this to $b,Cc$ for $b\leq c$ to recover the law. Conversely orthomodularity supplies Boolean subalgebras for commuting finite families. For completeness of the infinite-family version, start with an orthogonal family in a complete orthomodular lattice and extend it to a maximal pairwise commuting family. The commuting-element facts in Janowitz 1963, Lemma 1, show that complements and arbitrary joins of members commute with every member. Maximality therefore closes the family under these operations; De Morgan gives arbitrary meets, and distributivity for commuting triples makes it a complete Boolean subortholattice. This establishes the precise infinite-family consequence without making the ambient lattice Boolean.

The latter distinction is witnessed by $MO_2$, the six-element horizontal sum of two Boolean four-element algebras. Its four atoms form two complement pairs. It is orthomodular, and every orthogonal family has at most two distinct nonzero members, so even an arbitrarily indexed orthogonal family lies in a complete Boolean block. Nevertheless $a\wedge(b\vee Cb)=a$ while $(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge Cb)=0$ for atoms from different blocks. This is R003. Harding–Navara 2011, p.559, explicitly identifies the model; the pre1986 orthomodular background is already in Janowitz.

### 3.3 The poset question and the ten-element reconstruction

The strict relation $0<1, 2<1, 2<3$ is a height-two partial order. Its comparability graph is exactly $P_4$. The $O_6$ lattice above fails orthomodularity at $\{1\}<\{1,3\}$, since $\{1,3\}\cap C\{1\}=\varnothing$. This settles the strict-comparability question at IX111 negatively. The same $P_4$ orthoset is an exact later published example in Lindenhovius–Vetterlein 2023, Example 3.3, and Jenča’s 2024 preprint, Example 2.6, published in 2026. The height-two orientation is supplied here. Jenča’s broader theorem concerns **incomparability**, and a 2026 correction exists; the inaccessible corrected theorem is not used.

With inclusion order and ordinary complement this is a ten-element ortholattice. For $b=\{i\}$, $c=\{i,i'\}$, one has $c\wedge Cb=0$, so the local law fails. ## 4. Boolean partitions and residual chains

Suppose $(e_i)_{i\in I}$ are nonzero, pairwise orthogonal, and join to one. The map $h(J)=\bigvee_{i\in J} e_i$ preserves arbitrary joins. If $k\notin J$, then $h(J)\leq Ce_k$; consequently $e_k\leq h(J)$ would force $e_k=0$. Thus $J$ can be recovered from $h(J)$, proving injectivity. If $h$ preserves complement, De Morgan gives all meets and its image is a complete Boolean subortholattice. Every complete Boolean subortholattice containing the $e_i$ contains all $h(J)$, proving minimality. Conversely the joins of a partition of one in a complete Boolean subortholattice obey complement preservation. This is C096.

For a family joining to $e\neq1$, include the extra piece $Ce$. If $e=1$, omit that piece, because it is zero. This is R006, the necessary qualification to IX116 and IX119. The full powerset of a family containing a zero piece cannot embed injectively by subset joins.

For finite nonzero orthogonal families $B\ll A$, every $b\in B$ lies below a unique $a\in A$: two distinct receiving elements are orthogonal, so a common nonzero lower element is impossible. Put

\[
a'=\bigvee\{b\in B:b\leq a\},\qquad a''=a\wedge Ca'.
\]

If $A\cup B$ lies in a complete Boolean subortholattice, each $a=a'\vee a''$, and the nonzero pieces in $B\cup\{a'':a\in A\}$ are commensurable. Conversely, commensurability of those pieces together with all these decompositions recovers every $a$ inside their Boolean algebra. Both generating families produce the same complete Boolean subortholattice. Its outside atom still has to be included when its total support is not one. This proves the precise C100 equivalence.

For a chain $A_0\ll\cdots\ll A_n$, residues at the same level are orthogonal because they lie under different members of that level. A residue at an earlier level has a receiving ancestor at level $j-1$. Relative to a later residue in $c\in A_j$, that ancestor either contributes to the predecessor join $c'$, or is below another member of $A_j$ orthogonal to $c$. In both cases the earlier and later residues are orthogonal. This proves C102 without any decomposition assumption.

If the original chain is commensurable, its residues are commensurable by closure of the Boolean algebra under joins, meets and complements. Conversely, if every parent decomposes as predecessor join plus residue, commensurability of all residues recovers $A_0$, then $A_1$, and so on by induction. Without that additional decomposition the converse fails: in $O_6$, take $A_0=\{a\}$, $A_1=\{c\}$ with $0<a<c<1$ on one branch. Then $c\wedge Ca=0$; the nonzero residue family is just $\{a\}$, while $\{a,c\}$ generates nonBoolean $O_6$. This is R007. It does **not** refute a reading in which the preceding $\gamma^2$ axiom is standing, because that axiom supplies the missing decomposition.

The strengthened relation “pointwise refinement and joint Boolean commensurability” need not be transitive. The code gives a complete eighteen-element example by doubling the complementary rank-two elements with bitmasks 5 and 10 in the Boolean lattice on four labels. Order between distinct bitmasks is strict inclusion; each doubled fiber is a two-element chain; complement reverses complementary fibers. Let $g=\{1\}$, $g'=\{2\}$, $f=\{1,2\}$, $f'=\{3\}$. The families

\[
A_0=\{g,g'\},\quad A_1=\{f\},\quad A_2=\{f,f'\}
\]

are nonzero orthogonal families. The adjacent unions each generate a Boolean algebra of eight elements, whereas the first and last together generate all eighteen elements and fail distributivity. All three pairwise subsets of $\{g,g',f'\}$ are also commensurable while the triple is not. The full order and complement table are in the certificate. This proves R008 for discrete spatial figures; it does not claim that every step can be made a singleton figure.

## 5. Faithful supports and elementary pullback

### 5.1 A faithful external support with nonBoolean virtual supports

Use the $P_4$ atelier with equality incidence and equality open refinement. Its figures are cliques. Give each vertex $x$ a private point $p_x$; give each distinct nonorthogonal pair $\{x,y\}$ one point $p_{xy}$. Put these points in $S_x$ exactly when their labels contain $x$. Every $S_x$ is nonempty. For distinct $x,y$, the sets $S_x,S_y$ meet precisely when the vertices are not orthogonal. Hence

\[
x\delta y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_x\cap S_y=\varnothing,
\]

also for $x=y$, since $S_x\neq\varnothing$. With equality incidence, closed and open supports are the same $S_x$, and the union-of-face-open-supports condition is automatic. Refinement monotonicity is automatic for equality. The sets cover all seven external points; their membership patterns, which are singletons or pairs of vertex labels, are distinct, so they separate points. This checks the exact ordinary faithfulness and normalization clauses of VIII68–71.

The middle edge $\{1,2\}$ is a valid figure. Its two virtual open supports are the closed singletons $\{1\}$, $\{2\}$; together they generate $O_6$, not a Boolean algebra. Therefore Supp1 fails, and consequently $\gamma^3$ fails. This is R005 and a negative answer to the ordinary-faithfulness question in C106.

The critical literature/source distinction is entirely internal: the corollary the author appears to cite is at VIII manuscript p.78, scan87, not either ambiguous p.71 or p.74 candidate. Its preceding proof at scan86:247 uses **full faithfulness**. Both candidate pages and the actual proof were directly read and saved in `source_checks.txt`. The example above does not satisfy or refute that stronger hypothesis.

### 5.2 The same full-figure pullback does not preserve elementary type

The formula at VIII55 sends a figure $F$ to

\[
f^*F=\{f^{-1}(S):S\in F, f^{-1}(S^\circ)\neq\varnothing\}.
\]

VIII56 explicitly retains surviving proper faces when a top open stratum disappears. IX132 explicitly writes the full map between figure sets and then claims elementary figures have elementary-or-zero image. These are the same displayed construction; no change of convention is supplied there.

Take $E=\{a,b,c\}$, $E'=\{a,b\}$, and the inclusion. The closed strata $\{a\},\{b\},E$ have respective nonempty disjoint open strata $\{a\},\{b\},\{c\}$. They form an elementary figure with greatest stratum $E$. Its pullback is the two singleton strata $\{a\},\{b\}$. It is a valid nonempty figure but has no greatest stratum. The finite certificate checks these facts. This proves R009.

A different partial map on elementary generators could kill a generator whose top open inverse image is empty while retaining the images of proper-face generators. This fits the later pointed language, but is not the full-figure pullback. The dossier records it as an alternative reconstruction and does not use a change of names to infer a contradiction.

For comparison, an injective direct set map transports all strata without collisions, so unions and nonempty intersections of supports are preserved. Whole-space complement is preserved only if that injection is also surjective. A noninjective map can identify two distinct open point strata; their direct images then overlap, so naive stratumwise image is not a general figure construction. These are direct set calculations, not a proof that no more elaborate pushforward can exist.

## 6. Pointed morphisms, kernel and maquette excision

Preservation of the three extended relations is stable under composition, and identity maps preserve them, so the provisional category in C120 is well defined. If $x\ll^\circ y$ and $f(y)=0$, then $f(x)\ll^\circ0$, which forces $f(x)=0$. Thus its kernel is lower for open refinement.

Orthoclosedness does not follow. Kill vertex0 of the discrete $P_4$ atelier and retain the induced atelier on vertices1,2,3. Source and target have equality incidence and open refinement. The only new image of an orthogonal edge is an edge meeting zero, which is orthogonal to everything under the stipulated convention. All three preservation clauses hold. The kernel is $\{0\}$, whereas its double polar in the source is $\{0,2\}$. This is R010. The manuscript anticipates a need for supplementary conditions; the example confirms that caution. Paseka–Vetterlein’s closed-kernel statement requires an actual orthogonal adjoint and does not apply merely from relation preservation.

For a maquette, let $A$ be its incidence poset, with open refinement equal to equality and disjointness equal to inequality. For **any** subset $N\subseteq A$, take the induced maquette $A\setminus N$, and send $N$ to zero. An incidence pair with both elements retained is preserved; if either is killed it is preserved because incidence with zero holds in both directions. Open refinement is equality and hence is preserved. A disjoint pair either remains distinct or acquires a zero, so it too is preserved. The kernel is exactly $N$, even when $N$ is not an incidence ideal.

Any homomorphism killing $N$ restricts to a unique homomorphism on $A\setminus N$, and extending by zero recovers the original map. This proves the universal property in the exact provisional category, establishing C123 independently. It does not prove existence of excision for an arbitrary atelier, or the proposed elementary-set-figure quotient in C124. The latter also has the unresolved difference between killing a closed support and killing a top open stratum.

## 7. Subdivision equivalences and the canceled condition

Suppose $G\ll F$ has receiving map $\varphi:G\to F$. Inside a common complete Boolean subortholattice, the open supports of distinct $X\in F$ are orthogonal. Every support of $Y\in G$ lies below its receiver. Therefore intersecting the equality of total supports with $X^\circ$ selects exactly the fiber over $X$. This proves equivalence of total support equality and the fiberwise equalities in C125 under explicit pair commensurability. Fiberwise equality also forces every fiber to be nonempty, since $X^\circ\neq0$, so $\varphi$ is surjective.

Absence of a local refinement disjoint from $G$ needs a separate local detection principle for its converse. Commensurability alone cannot supply it. Let $A=\{g,f,h\}$, incidence equality, open refinement $g<f$ plus identities, and the only disjoint pair $g\delta h$. The code checks every primitive clause, including heredity and the receiver axiom. The closed supports are

\[
\varnothing,\quad\{g\},\quad\{h\},\quad A,
\]

forming a Boolean four-element lattice. Thus all finite-chain commensurability conditions hold. For $G=\{g\}\ll F=\{f\}$, the only possible local refinements of $F$ are $g,f$, neither disjoint from $g$; yet the supports are $\{g\}\neq A$. This is R011. It is **conditional** as an objection to the source: the phrase “Atmag supp²” may denote a stronger local support axiom than the earlier $\gamma^2$. Its exact identity is not settled without the image. The model lacks spatiality/refinement reflection, and a hypothesis of that kind may be decisive.

The maximality statement at IX139 uses inclusion after an explicit correction from refinement order. Only the surjectivity argument is independently proved here; the divisibility-dependent maximality equivalence is carried with its stated hypotheses and remains distinct from the ambiguous local detection step.

Finally the entire proposed quotient-topology addition at IX139–140 is canceled. Even as an abstract finite-poset statement, its “ou encore” is too strong without further hypotheses. Map two disjoint two-element chains $0<1$ and $2<3$ onto the chain $0<1<2$ by the values $0,1,1,2$. Preimage is lower exactly for lower subsets of the target, so the target has the final Alexandrov topology. But its comparison $0<2$ has no comparable pair of lifts. The code checks all eight target subsets and all source pairs. This is R012, a diagnosis of the general equivalence only; no full atelier realization satisfying all subdivision hypotheses is claimed, and no canceled requirement is restored.
