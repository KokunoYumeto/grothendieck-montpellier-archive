## The argument in the order of the pages

### Powerset relations as a test for the general support calculus

Scans105–108 continue the correspondence discussion from IX90–104. A relation between sets acts on powersets by direct image; its converse acts in the reverse direction. The pages distinguish preservation of disjointness, nonempty intersections, differences, top and complement, and translate these into injectivity or surjectivity of the two graph projections. The mathematical content is elementary but its exact typing matters: inverse image by a total function, direct image by an injection, and a partial relation with disjoint output fibers are different cases. C081–085 and C118 record all the equivalences with empty-set conventions and the graph requirement stated explicitly.

The current run proves the arbitrary-set statements and checks all 689 relations on sets of sizes at most three. The check is exhaustive for that finite range; it does not replace the general proof. The precise support-level direct-injection criterion is that the transpose preserve complement, after disjointness preservation has been imposed on the original map. A confused marginal symbol at IX133 remains a reading alarm. The opened literature supplies the older relation/polarity setting and an exact contemporary powerset-duality formulation, without treating these elementary facts as discoveries made after1986. [C081–085,C118; Ore1944, §4 and Theorem8; Massas2023, Theorem2.29; formalism_and_checks.md, §2.]

The attempted extension to arbitrary spatios is much less formal. A complete ortholattice admits complements that need not be the unique lattice complement, so a map can preserve all joins, all meets, top and orthogonality without preserving the distinguished orthocomplement. R001 gives the four-element Boolean lattice embedded into the six-element ortholattice \(O_6\) with exactly this failure. Its conjugate transpose is constructed and checked. This answers the unrestricted version negatively while leaving the author's proposed, unspecified constructible restriction intact. Two genuine general facts survive: top preservation is equivalent to zero reflection by the conjugate map, and if both conjugates preserve orthocomplement they are inverse isomorphisms. The latter is already answered “oui” in the manuscript margin and receives a two-adjunction proof here. [C086–089; MP20–21; R001–002.]

### Orthomodularity emerges without its name

Scans109–117 study when comparable or orthogonal elements generate Boolean substructures. The central local identity

\[
c=b\vee(c\wedge Cb)\qquad(b\leq c)
\]

is the orthomodular law. The text's families of equivalent conditions therefore belong to established pre1986 ortholattice theory. They are not automatic in every spatios, and imposing them does not make the whole lattice Boolean. The two smallest useful contrasts are \(O_6\), which fails the law, and \(MO_2\), which satisfies it while remaining nondistributive. In the latter lattice every arbitrarily indexed orthogonal family lies in a Boolean block; the strengthening to infinite families still does not force global Booleanity. [C090–093,C098; MP22; Janowitz1963, pp1241–1242; HardingNavara2011, p.559.]

The explicit special question about an ordered set uses **strict comparability**, \(x<y\) or \(y<x\). A four-element height-two poset has comparability graph \(P_4\); its double-polar closed-set lattice is \(O_6\). The exact graph counterexample occurs in later published orthoset literature, justifying class B for C094. The orientation realizing it as the manuscript's relation is supplied by this run. Jenča's broader N-free theorem concerns incomparability, so it is not substituted for the manuscript question. An August2026 correction exists; only its publisher metadata and abstract were accessible, and the corrected theorem is not asserted. [C094; MP23; R004; LindenhoviusVetterlein2023, Example3.3; Jenča2026, Example2.6; JenčaCorrection2026.]

The author's own ten-element example at scan113 has a separate role. Its transcribed set family is not closed under ordinary complement, and a marked order edge is incompatible with the proposed complement labels. A clearly labeled reconstruction gives a valid ten-element ortholattice and recomputes its displayed joins, but does not decide the ink. The negative answer itself already follows from the independently verified six-element example, so it does not depend on repairing this diagram. [C095; reading alarms atscan113; finite certificate scan113_reconstruction.]

### From individual figures to commensurable diagrams

Scans115–122 introduce admissibility, then collective commensurability, and successively strengthen the support axioms. Supp1 places the open supports of one figure in a complete Boolean subortholattice. The proposed \(\gamma^2\) places the supports of a related pair of figures in one such algebra. The proposed \(\gamma^3\) does the same for a whole finite refinement chain. These are genuine additional requirements on the primitive atelier structure. The three levels must not be collapsed. [C096–104.]

The Boolean partition calculation has a small but consequential degeneracy: the complement of the total support is an additional atom only when it is nonzero. A uniform powerset formula that retains that zero piece is noninjective. The same outside atom is missing in the displayed common-refinement formula. These are explicit mathematical qualifications, not silent changes to the text. The residual-chain argument also needs its dependency stated: residues are always mutually orthogonal, but reconstructing the original chain from them requires each parent to be the join of its predecessor fiber and its residue. A reading retaining \(\gamma^2\) provides that identity; the standalone unrestricted lemma does not. [C097,C100,C102–103; R006–007.]

At scan123 the author doubts that the attempted commensurable-refinement relation is transitive and describes a possible obstruction. An eighteen-element complete ortholattice verifies the obstruction for discrete spatial figures: each adjacent pair generates a Boolean algebra of eight elements, while the first and last generate the entire nonBoolean eighteen-element lattice. The certificate also checks that the relevant three elements are pairwise commensurable but not jointly commensurable. This confirms a precise failure of the proposed general construction; it does not claim the narrower counterexample with a singleton figure at every step. [C105; MP24; R008.]

### Faithful realization does not supply the missing Boolean axioms

Scan124 asks whether a faithful external support implies \(\gamma^3\). The answer is negative for the explicit notion of faithfulness at VIII manuscriptp62. Give the four strata of the \(P_4\) atelier equality incidence and equality open refinement. Realize every stratum by a nonempty set consisting of a private point and shared points for its nonorthogonal neighbors. Then two strata are orthogonal exactly when their external supports are disjoint. These supports cover and separate the external point set, so the realization is normalized and faithful. Nevertheless the middle-edge figure has virtual supports generating \(O_6\), and already Supp1 fails. All primitive atelier and external-support clauses are checked separately in the certificate. [C106,C109; MP25; R005.]

The manuscript's cross-reference is unusually important. The two suggested pages, VIIIp71 andp74, do not contain the cited Corollaire1. The matching corollary is on VIIIp78, scan87, and its proof explicitly uses **full faithfulness** at scan86. The counterexample addresses ordinary faithfulness and does not refute the stronger theorem. The endpoint checks therefore identify both a page-number alarm and a dropped-hypothesis danger. [35-link table; VIII scans80,83,86–87 preserved in source_checks.txt.]

### Intrinsic supports, subateliers and provisional morphisms

Scans124–128 criticize the global spatios construction for combining supports with no common geometric context. The author wants the supports subordinate to a figure to fit into an intrinsic structure independent of that figure, perhaps recovered by inverting subdivisions. The questions of whether that localization is thin and whether it embeds into the virtual support order are both explicit. General fraction-category theory supplies conditions to check, not a solution for this particular class of arrows. The Stacks Project's precise multiplicative-system definition and localization construction were opened; the original Gabriel–Zisman book was not. [C107–109; MP26; Stacks04VB, Definition4.27.1 and Lemma4.27.2.]

The IXbis section resumes subateliers, strengthens the Sousat condition through receiving strata, and lists open shadows, figures themselves, strict residuals and compatible broad residuals. The broad residual needs its compatibility qualification; the displayed proof uses it. The proposed embeddings of all four associated support lattices into the ambient support lattice remain questions, since closure depends on the ambient orthogonality relation. No unproved general inclusion of orthoclosed sets is supplied. [C110–115; MP27.]

The elementary inverse-image example then exposes a genuine construction issue. Under the full-figure pullback formula of VIII55–56, an elementary three-stratum figure can lose its top open stratum while retaining two incomparable proper faces. Its pullback is nonempty and non-elementary. IX132 explicitly displays that same full-figure map; the dossier does not infer a contradiction merely from a change of names. A different partial map on elementary generators is a possible reconstruction, separately labeled. [C116; R009; source endpoints VIII55–56 and IX132.]

The provisional homomorphism definition at IX134 preserves three relations and adjoins a formal zero. Zero is incident in both directions, is only a bottom element for open refinement, and is orthogonal to everything. The earlier optional face-bijection condition is expressly omitted. These maps form a category, and their kernels are lower for open refinement. They need not be double-polar closed: deleting one endpoint of the discrete \(P_4\) atelier yields a valid map whose kernel has strictly larger orthoclosure. Adjointable-map results do give closed kernels, but adjointability is extra structure. [C119–121; MP28; R010; PasekaVetterlein2025, Lemma3.8.]

The general excision question remains open in the identified axiom class. The maquette case is resolved directly: deleting any subset and sending it to zero has the exact universal property, including preservation of incidence across the deleted subset. The set-figure case is less immediate and must distinguish disappearance of closed support from disappearance of the top open stratum. The author's own hesitation and postponement are retained. [C122–124; MP29; formalism_and_checks.md, §6.]

### The final subdivision criteria

Scans138–139 compare fiberwise coverage, total support equality, absence of a disjoint local refinement, and maximality under inclusion. Within one complete Boolean subortholattice, total equality and fiberwise equality are equivalent by intersecting with the receiver supports. The converse from absence of a local witness requires a further detection principle. A three-stratum model with a Boolean support lattice satisfies all commensurability requirements but fails that converse. This is only a conditional objection to the manuscript because its named “Atmag supp²” may contain stronger content than the earlier \(\gamma^2\); that glyph and axiom identification must be checked on the scan. [C125–127; MP31; R011.]

The source explicitly changes maximality from refinement order to inclusion. The extra quotient-topology requirement is canceled, including its continuation on scan140. A separate finite-poset example shows that quotient Alexandrov topology need not yield a single comparable pair of lifts for each target comparison; it does not claim to realize all the surrounding subdivision hypotheses. These scope distinctions prevent a canceled trial definition from becoming the dossier's final definition. [C128; R012.]
