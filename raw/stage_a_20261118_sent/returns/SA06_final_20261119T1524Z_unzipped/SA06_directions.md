## 1. Integral inverse to Albanese H¹ in a specified family

**Origins:** SA06-57, SA06-99, SA06-292; C-SA06-35, C-SA06-86, C-SA06-270; MP-SA06-149.

**Target.** Determine whether a chosen family admits an integral correspondence inverse to Albanese pullback, and identify the obstruction to a universal zero-cycle. Preserve the difference between a cycle inducing the identity and a miniversal cycle inducing a positive multiple.

**Known.** Rational inverses exist in the checked smooth-projective setting. Voisin (2025, Theorem 1.4) gives published threefold counterexamples; Alexandrou (2026, Theorem 1.3, preprint) gives surface counterexamples with the stated elliptic hypotheses. Representability of CH₀ alone does not remove the obstruction. The equivalence with the appropriate inverse H¹ component was checked in this consolidation.

**First step and bounded output.** Choose a family with a documented Jacobian splitting or universal-cycle construction. Write its correspondence, normalization at the origin and torsion-free cohomological component. Produce a precise positive-case/obstruction table, distinguishing sufficient symmetric-power conditions from necessary-and-sufficient conditions.

## 2. Integral Picard/top-odd inverse and the Poincaré one-cycle

**Origins:** SA06-99, SA06-292, SA06-294; C-SA06-271, C-SA06-274; MP-SA06-150, MP-SA06-153.

**Target.** Determine integral algebraicity of the inverse mixed Poincaré component for a specified abelian variety or family. This is the second manuscript question, with a different cycle dimension and obstruction from Direction 1.

**Known.** The integral reduction, its sign and subtraction of the factor components were established in the companion. Beckmann and de Gaay Fortman (2023, Theorem 1.1), together with Engel, de Gaay Fortman and Schreieder (2025/2026, Theorem 1.1, checked preprint version), obstruct the universal assertion on very general principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension at least four. Rational inverse correspondences remain available.

**First step and bounded output.** Fix a Jacobian, a product, or another family with explicit cycle classes. Write the integral Künneth lattice and inverse-Poincaré class, then compare known algebraic generators. A run should settle one formulation or example, retaining the exact torsion convention and every factorial denominator.

## 3. Identify the manuscript’s cycle quotient and incidence relations

**Origins:** SA06-42, SA06-47, SA06-48, SA06-66, SA06-74; C-SA06-22, C-SA06-28, C-SA06-29, C-SA06-47, C-SA06-97; MP-SA06-12, MP-SA06-18, MP-SA06-25.

**Target.** Compare the subgroup generated by correspondence images of Albanese kernels with the common kernel of regular homomorphisms and with incidence equivalence. Establishing the correct universal quotient controls subsequent representability and duality comparisons.

**Known.** Divisor and zero-cycle endpoints, functoriality and one comparison inclusion are checked. Samuel (1960, §2.5), Saito (1979, Theorem 3.6), and Hernandez–Peláez (2025, §4.1) use precise but differing conventions. Similar terminology does not identify the integral quotients.

**First step and bounded output.** For one smooth projective variety, give all three universal properties and prove each proposed arrow directly. Test codimensions one, two and top degree. Deliver a comparison diagram with exact remaining kernels, explicitly marking where a reverse implication requires rational coefficients or an isogeny. The related numerical strengthening of the square-zero product can then be tested against this diagram.

## 4. Define and test the local Chow restriction package

**Origins:** SA06-227, SA06-228, SA06-230, SA06-231, SA06-264; C-SA06-230, C-SA06-231, C-SA06-232, C-SA06-235, C-SA06-236; MP-SA06-113, MP-SA06-114, MP-SA06-115, MP-SA06-123.

**Target.** Recover the intended A,J,N groups on a punctured strictly local scheme, define the restriction/Gysin maps, and turn the displayed ranges into falsifiable statements.

**Known.** The pages retain regularity of X and Y. Bhatt and de Jong (2014, Theorem 0.1) supplies a precise local Picard theorem with dimension, depth and characteristic conditions, not the proposed higher-Chow package. The displayed exact-sequence inference gives injectivity, without the missing surjectivity premise.

**First step and bounded output.** Fix a regular or complete-intersection local model in dimension at least four. Define the groups and write localization with the correct codimension shift. Compare one restriction map with the exact Picard theorem and compute a low-degree test. The deliverable is a reliable local problem and its first boundary cases, not a guessed interpretation of every symbol.

## 5. Determine the determinant-orientation obstruction ξₚ

**Origins:** SA06-77, SA06-721, SA06-722, SA06-723; C-SA06-710, C-SA06-711; MP-SA06-303, MP-SA06-307.

**Target.** Define the determinant-orientation invariant of the actual slope-one-half rank-two crystal and quaternionic ideal data, including its square-class ambiguity and transport under invertible bimodules.

**Known.** Oda (1969, Corollary 5.11) provides the degree-one Dieudonné/de Rham comparison. It does not calculate ξₚ or supply Morita-compatible determinant normalization. The relation F²+p=0 gives an action of Z[F]; extension to the maximal quadratic order requires a further integral divisibility check.

**First step and bounded output.** Choose one explicit supersingular elliptic curve and write covariant and contravariant conventions side by side. Compute the determinant comparison under one radical-ideal transport. A run should identify the obstruction group, admissible normalization and one transport formula. Global canonicity and coherence should remain separate until those constructions have been specified and checked.

## 10. Compute a normalized Frobenius lifting obstruction

**Origins:** SA06-406, SA06-407, SA06-416, SA06-417; C-SA06-414, C-SA06-415, C-SA06-512; MP-SA06-204.

**Target.** Decide whether one correctly normalized Frobenius point lifts through a specified torus isogeny, by calculating its actual connecting class.

**Known.** The displayed normalization has a weight error. A nonisomorphic isogeny need not obstruct a particular rational point. **First step and bounded output.** Fix one finite CM quotient. Reconcile q=pʳ, the eigenvalue weight and any square-root choice, then define the point over its actual field of definition. Write the resulting norm equation or finite-group-scheme torsor and compute its class. The output is a complete selected lifting test and an account of what the lattice calculation does and does not detect.

## 18. Test corrected Hodge effectivity in one category

**Origins:** SA06-810, SA06-813, SA06-905; C-SA06-811, C-SA06-812, C-SA06-827; MP-SA06-336, MP-SA06-348.
