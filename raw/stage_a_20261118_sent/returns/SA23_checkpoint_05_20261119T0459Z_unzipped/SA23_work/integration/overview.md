# SA23 — Alexander Grothendieck's Montpellier papers

## Scope, result, and how to read the dossier

**Completed assessment as of 19 November 2026.** This dossier consolidates the 326 indexed Pro-note entries and the mathematical material in all 45 supplied transcriptions. Each indexed entry was compared with its cited passage before receiving a verdict. The additional Codex notes and API texts contribute source subjects, claims, problems and reading alarms, but no fictitious indexed-entry verdicts. The files record what the transcriptions say, what opened publications establish, and what this run reconstructs as three separate kinds of evidence.

The collection contains several lines of work, rather than a single unfinished book. Its order runs from tensor norms and Riemann–Roch through local algebra, differential and infinitesimal constructions, hyperplane sections, representability, categorical foundations, topoi, p-adic comparison, and the later work on orientations, polyhedra and graphs. The detailed sections retain the order of the arguments within each dossier. In particular, the categorical and homotopical sheets are read through their own definitions and questions; no general connection to *La Longue Marche* or *Les Dérivateurs* is assumed.

The main result is a change in the granularity of the evidence. Some doubts have exact answers in Grothendieck's published work; others belong to named later authors. Several purported open questions are completed on a subsequent leaf, and several attractive blanket statements fail in examples satisfying their actual hypotheses. A smaller set remains only partially identified because a definition, quantifier, coefficient convention or comparison map is missing. These are differentiated in the claim and problem registers. No assertion of novelty follows from an unsuccessful search.

The required registers are the authoritative detailed deliverables. `SA23_items.json` connects note entries and source subjects to passages; `SA23_claims.json` gives the statements, page status, computations and searched fate; `SA23_problems.json` separates the strongest source-supported question from a plausible modern formulation. `SA23_editorial_tasks.json` keeps translator requests and present-run repairs outside the count of the author's questions. The narrative explains the main findings and their consequences. Bracketed claim, problem and bibliography identifiers refer to those registers and `SA23_references.bib`. Exact theorem numbers, page numbers, access limits and date distinctions are recorded there and in the corresponding individual reference annotations, primary-source check records and `search_log.jsonl` files.

## Main mathematical and historical findings

### Tensor norms and Riemann–Roch

The functional-analysis sequence contains an extensive route from exterior powers and determinant inequalities to Schatten estimates and eigenvalue continuity. Exact counterparts occur in Grothendieck's 1955 and 1956 publications. The current analysis separates the older trace-class meaning of *opérateur de Fredholm* from the modern Fredholm property. It also separates uniform convexity from the weak-operator Kadec–Klee property, and a matching topology on eigenvalue lists from an unspecified ordering of them. Several proposed strengthenings fail: the unrestricted power-monotonicity conjecture has a certified positive 2-by-2 matrix counterexample, and the proposed fixed-fraction singular-value estimate fails on weighted cyclic matrices. The valid inequalities and their parameter restrictions are retained. [C-SA23-analysis_curves-031–038, -076–083; AC_GrothPTT1955, II pp.13,20–21; AC_GrothFredholm1956; AC_Dodds1995, Theorem2.7 and Corollary2.8; AC_Ricard2018, Introduction and Theorem3.2.]

The Riemann–Roch leaves distinguish a universal Chern ring with a **chosen geometric family** from later constructions imposing conditions on all vector bundles. That choice changes the universal object and its integral torsion. The projective-space example and the two different factorials in the Chern computation have been checked integrally; rational Chern characters alone would not prove the torsion conclusion. The nonzero 2-torsion example survives after discarding the cancelled effective-hypersurface setup. The supported theory has established rational descendants, including localized Chern characters and singular Riemann–Roch, while the exact integral nonunital filtration remains a separate formulation problem. The final surface formula also distinguishes the structure sheaf of a singular curve from the pushforward of its normalization. [C-SA23-root-011, -014–015, -025–038, -042–058, -071 and -071R; GrothendieckRRR1957; SGA6V; Mackall2020; FultonGillet1983.]

### Local algebra, corrections, and the unpublished EGA plans

Cotes 22 and 23 repeatedly turn on the difference between an algebraic condition and its topological or finite-presentation counterpart. Adic formal smoothness does not mean discrete formal smoothness; finite-presentation openness theorems do not settle arbitrary noetherian maps. The characteristic-two differential-kernel examples and the ordinary differential module of a power-series ring have been checked under their exact hypotheses. Some earlier allegations of EGA misprints are rejected by comparison with the original print; one omitted generator in EGA IV, 0.22.5.10(i), is a genuine literal defect with an explicitly labelled repair. [C-SA23-ega-017–026; EGAIV1; Stacks00TH; Stacks0593.]

Deligne's correspondence has unusually exact publication outcomes. The separation-free implication of EGA IV19.5.5 appears in the final theorem with an explicit credit to him. His objection that multiplicative lifting equations do not form linear varieties is matched by the express withdrawal of EGA0.19.3.12 in the 1967 errata. Elsewhere a conclusion remains true while a displayed equality or proof route fails. The dossier preserves that distinction instead of treating an error in a proof as a counterexample to its theorem. The cote-26 plan is consequently indexed as many different representability and deformation questions, with their respective flatness, properness, finite-presentation, freeness and base-change assumptions. [C-SA23-ega-049–061, -083–096; EGAIV4,19.5.5 and Liste3 IV no.8; EGAIII; SGA6VII; FGA; exact opened representability comparisons in the claim map; Artin1969 is an unverified original.]

### Hyperplane sections, linear systems, and quadratic singularities

Cotes 24 and 25 supply two substantial witnesses to the EGA V project. Their shared constructions are consolidated only after comparison of the precise formulations. The universal incidence argument, geometric irreducibility, Grassmannian charts and many parameter statements have exact established continuations. Arbitrary-morphism Bertini bounds, fixed-divisor conditions, finite multisections, and characteristic-two duality need more care. A later theorem on the same topic is not treated as a proof when it imposes equidimensional fibers or a different properness or projectivity hypothesis. [C-SA23-hyp-001–016, -024–055; corresponding forms claims; PoonenSlavov2022; Benoist2011; SGA7II.]

The quadratic-singularity pages themselves supply a correction: a smooth projective tangent quadric in characteristic two need not have nondegenerate polar Hessian in odd rank. The early and later definitions are recorded at their actual places. In the integral discriminant calculation, the odd-plus-odd direct-sum factor is four under the manuscript's stated normalization, not two. Other literal errors concern a graded versus cumulative Hilbert function, the direction of a determinantal codimension bound, and sheaf injectivity versus fiberwise injectivity of a conormal map. Each is paired with its exact repair or counterexample and its unresolved ink question. The generalized Bertini axioms and intrinsic supersingularity constructions remain bounded formulation tasks, rather than claims that their broad subject is undeveloped. [C-SA23-hyp-078–106 and their refinement records; SGA7II, XVII2.5,3.3–3.4; Conrad2014, AppendixC.]

### Categories, topoi, homotopy theory, and the p-adic comparison

The categorical sheets move through universal constructions, algebraic structures, sites, classifying topoi and local presentability. Several later corrections change a preservation condition or replace a proposed presheaf classifier by a sheaf topos. These changes are retained alongside the original assertions. Products of topoi are established, including the presheaf-factor case repeated on a cancelled sheet. A proposed test using only the two projection direct images is false, even though the product theorem is true. In descent, arbitrary immersions, projectivity, and the existence of some relatively ample line bundle must be separated from their more restricted positive theorems. [C-SA23-spaces-001–004, -049–061, -072; the cotes161-1/2 section and its claim map; spaces_SGA4I, ExposéIV; spaces_StacksImmersion; spaces_StacksProjective.]

The comparison between cotes161-2 and161-3 finds exact repeated constructions as well as important differences. Both write the conditional two-Yoneda formula for an already represented type of structures; neither occurrence supplies a universal existence theorem. Both also use the same map from the integer polynomial ring to the product of its localization and the integers. One wholly cancelled draft claims to recognize fields, while the other identifies pseudofields, or commutative von Neumann regular rings. The subject is consolidated, but those assertions and their different statuses are not merged, and the supplied passages do not establish their relative chronology. Recovery from set-valued models likewise retains the selected underlying constructions and representability conditions; recovery from the bare model category is a stronger, separate question. [C-SA23-groupes-077, -087, -096, -114; C-SA23-spaces-033–036; exact paired transcription endpoints in `integration/groupes_spaces_review.json`; Olivier1967; CaramelloJohnstone2009; DiLibertiOsmond2025.]

The Tapis retains substantive corrections by the author, including bounded-above derived tensor products and the interpretation of category cochains. Fixed rational coefficients do not detect weak contractibility: a simply connected torsion Moore space supplies the relevant counterexample. Strictly commutative Picard models lose the self-commutor, and bordism's additive, multiplicative and all-Gysin compatibility questions have different answers. The attached Quillen positivity article and Adams-conjecture sketch are treated under Quillen's authorship; the later complex étale proof is distinguished from Quillen's different 1971 method. The flat-conormal formal-category theorem attributed to Quillen has only partial exact matches in the literature opened here. [C-SA23-forms-034–074, -080–081; forms_HopkinsSinger, AppendixB.2; forms_Szymik,1.3,5.1,5.6; forms_Friedlander, Theorem5.3 and §6.]

The cote-93 de Rham comparison has a particularly close own-publication match: Grothendieck's square-zero isomorphism torsor, two-term resolution, derived morphism and transitivity in *Crystals*, §3.4. In cote161-5, the ramification filtration and equal-characteristic full-faithfulness hopes have exact later answers with hypotheses. The handwritten tensor-extension problem uses a larger Hodge–Tate category than the crystalline Barsotti–Tate image, so the modern crystalline correspondence does not automatically settle its literal formulation. The detached cocharacter leaf agrees with the conditional conjugation formula but does not resolve the Tate-twist sign, action-order conflict or undefined uniqueness data. [C-SA23-bt-022–031, -056–072; bt_GrothCrystals1968; bt_Serre1979; the checked Sen, de Jong, Katz and Liu sources in the claim map; C-SA23-groupes-016–017.]

### Games, orientations, arithmetic polyhedra, and graphs

The cote-68 game statements require independent terminal outcomes and well-founded, possibly transfinite, induction. Exponential algebra distinguishes nil, nilpotent and locally nilpotent hypotheses. Some earlier notes correctly diagnosed an error; others missed an inserted hypothesis and thereby refuted a statement the text no longer made. The determinant and orientation leaves require the graded sign and an explicit normal-order convention. None of these constructions is identified with a different project's character merely because it uses the same letter. [C-SA23-misc-001–039; AptSimon2021; Milne2017; EGAIV4,16.8,16.11–12; Freed2013.]

The icosahedral calculations, permutation images and graph automorphisms have been recomputed exactly. Their arithmetic all-base formulation remains incompletely specified, particularly at bad characteristics. For embedded graphs, the omitted genus term explains the boundary-count failure, while the source's fundamental-group stabilizer question has an exact later isotopy theorem under finite, tame, oriented surface hypotheses. The checked modern proof attributes the characterization to Ladegaillerie's 1984 article; the original article remains marked unverified because it was not opened. The author's later retrospective account is recorded as his account, not adopted as independent evidence of motives or publication causes. [C-SA23-misc-040–074; ColinMesmay2014, arXiv Theorem14/final Theorem4; Yagasaki2000, Theorem1.1 and Corollary1.1; Esquisse1997 §3; RecoltesNote94.]

## What the earlier notes got right and wrong

The earlier notes are often useful at locating a genuine doubt, a missing proof or a consequential sign. Their main recurring weakness is loss of scope: dropping an inserted nilpotence condition, replacing the chosen geometric family by all geometric elements, interpreting a cancelled draft as live, or using a nearby modern theorem with different quantifiers. Imported project commentary sometimes adds an attractive bridge without a supplied endpoint. Such links remain explicitly attributed leads in `SA23_project_leads.tsv`; they are not counted as verified results of the other projects.

This consolidation also corrects its own hypotheses where the source comparison demands it. The finite checks are accompanied by general arguments where a universal assertion is used. A numerical matrix result is not advertised as a theorem for all ranks. Conversely, a deep published theorem carried as background is not labelled an independent re-proof. `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv` records those different actions, including the refined target of a refutation where a separate refinement record is present.

## Register conventions and the present state of the topic

The six literature classes have the requested meanings: **A**, a counterpart in Grothendieck's own published work; **B**, developed by others into established mathematics; **C**, an exact partial development with the remaining scope stated; **D**, not developed after recorded searches; **E**, known before the relevant work; **F**, not sufficiently identifiable from the surviving text. A class is not a truth value. If a literal formula is false and a repaired theorem has a published counterpart, the page status remains false and the fate explains which repaired statement the publication proves. For undated sheets, classA records an own-publication counterpart without asserting an unproved temporal priority. The bibliography's `note = {verified}` certifies opening the recorded link; its annotation specifies whether metadata, a theorem statement, a proof or an entire relevant passage was checked. Unopened originals remain `unverified` even when an opened later paper cites them.

Every final claim has an explicit search-log route. The searches include the requested source families, exact-text and hypothesis searches, author bibliographies and backward references; access failures and absent exact matches are recorded. `SA23_fate_coverage.tsv` exposes the route from each canonical claim to those records. The problem index records an opened-source state of the art even for a question now solved, an authorial request deferred in the text, or a formulation too incomplete to identify. Translator requests and present-run repairs have their own separate register. When a source gives no reason why work stopped, `why_open` says so. An inference about a mathematical obstruction is not represented as a historical explanation.

Final identifiers are stable for items and claims. Cross-folder consolidations preserve every original witness in `source_records` and record their reasons in `integration/consolidation_plan.json`. The problem index is renumbered consecutively as `MP-SA23-001`, and so on; `SA23_id_aliases.json` resolves every former owner-qualified problem identifier and every merged subject or claim. Owner files retain their earlier identifiers for auditability. The final counts below, rather than intermediate local counts, describe the integrated dossier.

The claim-class and page-status counts use the canonical assessment of each final record. They do not erase the distinct drafting states retained in its source witnesses. Separate refinement records are labelled as this run's work and counted separately by origin. A source-provenance audit also distinguishes an authorial question or explicit unfinished step from a reader's request to check an asserted theorem. Answered questions and withdrawn unfinished proposals remain in the problem index; verification, translation and attribution tasks without an authorial question are kept in the editorial register.

The ranked directions are bounded specifications for later work. They state the originating item, claim and problem IDs, the exact prospective result, the established input and a first concrete step. Interest and plausible progress in one several-hour run are scored separately. Some high-value tasks are editorial concordances or hypothesis repairs; others concern a residual mathematical construction. None is developed into a separate research project in this dossier.
