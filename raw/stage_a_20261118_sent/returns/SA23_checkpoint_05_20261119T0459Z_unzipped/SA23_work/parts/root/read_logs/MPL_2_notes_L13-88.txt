53: ### A06 — Cote 2, scans 007–008, splitting and δ — own idea
54: 
55: The author calls the homological splitting “non naturel” and writes δ=1−Σμ_i1_i; replacing the chosen lifts 1_i by 1_i+e_i with e_i∈K_(0) changes δ to δ−Σμ_ie_i. Consequently the residue of δ in K_(0)/(Σ_i μ_iK_(0)) is independent of those lift choices, even though δ itself is not. For finitely many components this quotient is K_(0)/dK_(0), where d is the greatest common divisor of the μ_i, so relatively prime generic multiplicities force the residue to vanish. Confidence: high for this elementary abelian-group consequence of the displayed formula, and medium for its usefulness as a geometric invariant until functoriality of the chosen filtration and base-change maps is specified.

65: ### A21 — Cote 2, scan 003, multiplication convention — own idea
66: 
67: For X=P¹_k and a k-rational closed point p, the point-sheaf class [O_p] lies in the rank-zero part and is nonzero in the final K-group, since its Euler characteristic is 1, while ordinary tensor product gives O_p⊗O_p≃O_p. The locally free resolution 0→O(-p)→O→O_p→0 instead gives Tor₀(O_p,O_p)≃Tor₁(O_p,O_p)≃O_p and alternating class 0, showing why the asserted annihilator property needs a derived product or an equivalent convention. Confidence: high for this global test, medium for reconstructing the product on the preliminary ring E from it; compare the Tor-product construction in Borel and Serre (1958, §§4–5).
