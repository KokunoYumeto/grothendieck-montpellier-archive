367: ## Legibility and reliability
368: 
369: All 41 retained scans were read by the primary reader as whole sheets and at the supplied half-sheet resolution, then read again against the page files; additional crops and rotations were used for crowded and upside-down material, without OCR. The typed passages are generally more legible than the rapid handwritten argument, but overtyping, handwritten completions, cropped continuations, and page-wide cancellation lines remain genuine sources of uncertainty. The cancelled matter on008,014,018–019,025,036,038–039, the fine marginal additions on021–022/028–029/035/037, the filing note on002, and the lower-edge calculations on010/041–045 are the least secure portions.
370: 
371: The doubt table groups repeated occurrences of the same problem rather than assigning a row to every repeated glyph. Confidence describes the reading of the visible ink, not the truth of the mathematics. Explicit source anomalies—such as the021/022 sign discrepancy, the domain symbols on036 and043–044, an unfinished equality or bracket, and the zero in014—have been left in the transcription; their mathematical assessment belongs solely in the research notes. An `\illegible{n}` count estimates unreadable words under physical obliteration; it does not substitute for a mathematical symbol. The precise scope of very large cancellations and some insertion connectors is less certain than their individual legible contents.
