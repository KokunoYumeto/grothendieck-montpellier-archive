# Cotes 161-1 and 161-2: adjunctions, structures, and their universal categories

These two undated folders form a sustained investigation of how a structure can be recovered from the categories of its realizations. The starting points are concrete: an adjunction, a tensor product of a finite family, a category of ring objects, a small family of operations. The recurring question is what universal category represents the resulting construction, and which exactness, generation, and size hypotheses make that representation valid. The pages repeatedly distinguish, and sometimes initially conflate, a category of operations, its models, its classifying topos, and the category of constructions natural in every model.

The supplied catalogue assigns 19 leaves to cote 161-1 [173] and 111 to cote 161-2. Their eight transcription files and eight Codex companion notes were read completely: 5,006 physical transcription lines and 219 note lines. The resulting additional register has 27 subject items, 115 claims, and 61 authorial questions, with 13 editorial tasks kept separately. These are additional archival units; the Codex notes have no indexed Pro verdict rows. All quotations in the register are literal excerpts of the supplied transcription, with uncertainty and cancellation markup retained. Their English paraphrases and the present reconstruction are separate fields.

The most useful result of the consolidation is a precise map of the author's changing hypotheses. Several apparently open questions have short complete answers. Other formulations are false, often for small examples. A third group has exact modern theorems nearby, but the pages have not supplied their hypotheses. The dossier keeps that third group conditional. It makes no priority claim from the order of undated sheets, and it treats neither a canceled sentence nor a nearby modern theorem as evidence of a completed proof.

## 161-1: from adjunctions to dense subcategories

The first sheets examine an adjunction through the objects on which its unit or counit is invertible. The global criterion for a fully faithful adjoint is familiar; the restriction to a full subcategory is more delicate. The unit criterion becomes necessary when the composite right-left adjoint carries that subcategory into its essential closure. Without that condition, the requested one-object counterexample is the two-element poset $0<1$, mapped to the terminal category, with the right adjoint selecting 1 and the chosen subcategory containing only 0. The restricted functor is fully faithful, while its unit is not invertible. Claims 001–006 give the complete adjunction arguments and the descent of representability through a reflector. The global comparison is [RiehlContextOnline, Lemma 4.5.11 and Proposition 5.3.3](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf).

One parenthetical formulation needs a separate warning. Faithfulness tested only on a full subcategory detects monicity relative to that subcategory; it need not detect ambient monicity. A seven-arrow category supplies the exact counterexample, and the program verifies its compositions, terminal object, restricted faithfulness, and unequal parallel pair. The transcription may intend the relative assertion. The mathematical distinction is settled here; the ink is not adjudicated.

The finite-family sheets then construct a symmetric tensor category. If the component maps are required to be isomorphisms, the construction uses only the core of the original category: even its singleton families lose a noninvertible original arrow. The standard free symmetric monoidal construction uses all component arrows. A second issue concerns flattening a family of families. An ordinary functor on finite families does not by itself provide the associativity and unit comparisons needed for a coherent tensor product. These corrections retain the intended universal property with its actual coherence data. The exact modern comparison is Batanin–Kock–Weber, §1.1, journal p. 154, [arXiv:1510.08934](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.08934).

For an invertible object $L$, the self-commutor gives a scalar $\epsilon(L)$ with square 1. An adjacent transposition contributes that scalar, so a permutation contributes its parity power. The inverse-pairing question has a complete answer: the pairing required by the triangle identity is $\epsilon(L)$ times the reversed original pairing. An odd superline checks the sign, and a coherence argument proves it generally. The source's exponent written as “sign” remains a reading alarm: the numerical values +1 and −1 do not distinguish parity when the scalar has square 1. Claims 010–011 are linked to the actual self-commutor passage in the forms material and to the graded-orientation pages of the core package. The stronger strictification problem in the forms material is kept distinct.

The next sheets ask when extension from a small finitely complete category remains left exact. For a Grothendieck topos target, the flat-functor theorem gives the positive statement. Mere completeness and cocompleteness of the target are insufficient. The proposed target “sets opposite” gives a direct contradiction already involving the initial object and a binary product. When a dense generating subcategory produces a fully faithful nerve and the left adjoint to that nerve is left exact, the target is indeed a topos. Claims 014–015 match [SGA 4 I, Exposé IV, 1.2 and 9.1](https://library.slmath.org/nonmsri/sga/sga/pdf/sga4-1.pdf), with the source's positive conclusion at physical lines 568–571 retained alongside its question.

A single sheet turns to tensor gradings and semilinear conjugation. The identity is checked after fixing the action convention: if the action is ρ(σ) followed by the coefficient action of σ, invariance means $\rho(\sigma)\sigma(j)\rho(\sigma)^{-1}=j$. Reversing the conjugation without changing that convention is wrong; a symbolic two-by-two example distinguishes the formulas. Ziegler's Theorem 3.6 identifies tensor gradings with cocharacters [Ziegler2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.1981). The next comparison involving filtered $F$-crystals has insufficiently specified endpoints. Liu's precise crystalline weights-{0,1} theorem is relevant background, but it cannot identify an equivalence of arbitrary filtered objects [Liu2013, Theorem 2.2.1](https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2013__25_3_661_0.pdf). Claim 017 therefore remains class F after completed searches. The manuscript asserts this comparison; identifying its undefined endpoint is editorial task ED-SA23-groupes-07, not an authorial question. It stays separate from the shared Barsotti–Tate cocharacter occurrence.

The final sheets of 161-1 derive a pointwise left Kan extension and the full faithfulness of restriction along a dense subcategory. The remaining essential-image condition is a condition on the colimit relations respected by the restricted functor. Density alone does not justify the proposed finality shortcut. Claims 018–021 distinguish the valid Kan calculation, the exact comodel criterion, and the false shortcut. The comparison is [Kelly1982, Chapter 5, especially Theorem 5.56](https://tac.mta.ca/tac/reprints/articles/10/tr10.pdf). The full-faithfulness calculation continues onto physical lines 704–707; it is not cut off at the preceding page boundary.

## 161-2, first and second files: envelopes, quotients, and elementary classifiers

The first major construction seeks a universal category by adjoining prescribed limits and colimits, imposing equations, and inverting comparison maps. Its appeal is clear: a single construction could organize many varieties of “algebraic” structure. The difficulty lies in what is required at each stage. The early cardinal inequality is not enough to ensure that all data of a small diagram have appeared below a single stage. The later sufficiently large-cofinality requirement repairs that step. The explicit singular-cardinal example explains the repair. It does not construct the missing successor operation or define every preexactness condition.

The register separates four obligations: the successor construction, the encoding of faithful or fully faithful extension, the regularity bound needed at limit stages, and the universal property of the final category. Cylinder and fold tests settle the stated extension conditions. Modern bounded completion and sketchability results match restricted versions, with small diagram shapes and exact target assumptions. They do not license a claim about every unbounded doctrine. Claims 022–025 and problems 09–10 preserve both the successful encodings and the unfinished construction. Relevant exact comparisons are Kelly's bounded theorem and [Lack–Tendas2023, Theorem 4.4 and Proposition 5.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11056).

The following pages interpret structures through classifying topoi and quotients. Ordinary categorical localization, fullness, and geometric inclusion must remain distinct. Sheafification need not be full as a functor from presheaves to sheaves. The geometric statement involving all categories of morphisms, with all their 2-cells, does have an exact published counterpart: SGA 4 IV 9.1.4 and Exercise 9.1.6$b,c$ characterize geometric inclusions by representable full faithfulness; part $d$ supplies base change. The original printed pages 433–435 were rendered and read. Testing only isomorphism classes or only invertible 2-cells would weaken the statement and is not substituted for it.

The six elementary quotient theories are examples in a much larger lattice, not an exhaustive classification. The object classifier, inhabited objects, and open or closed conditions illustrate different kinds of quotient. A category of inhabited sets has no equalizer for two distinct maps from a singleton into a two-element set; it is therefore not the finite-limit model category or variety suggested by an overbroad reading. Constant and locally constant objects also fail the required filtered-colimit stability: on Cantor space a sequence of constant two-element sheaves can collapse away from one point, yielding a colimit whose stalk cardinality is not locally constant. These are full-property counterexamples; adding chosen structure and restricting morphisms changes the problem.

The group and monoid examples similarly require the correct classifier. A group classifying topos represents torsors, not arbitrary objects with a group action in the naive identification. A monoid presheaf point involves flatness, not every endomorphism object. The monadic and comonadic notes then need the actual Beck condition on the relevant split coequalizers. A finite split-fork example records why the missing condition matters. The comonadic topos assertion and the requested embedding into a topos with enough points have valid precise formulations. The latter is supplied by a sheaf topos's geometric inclusion into its presenting presheaf topos; it is different from a Boolean covering theorem. Claims 026–037 record these distinctions, with [RiehlContextOnline, §5.5](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf) and the exact SGA passages as the principal comparisons.

## Third file: ring properties, zero dimension, and characteristic

The ring sheets are an extended attempt to classify properties by their behavior under topological and algebraic operations. The resulting lattice is useful only if one keeps apart an external property of a ring, an internal geometric condition in a topos, and a category of rings equipped with chosen additional data. The differences matter throughout claims 038–058.

The noetherian “ultralocal” point test fails for the fppf topology. Schröer's exact classification says that a noetherian fppf-local ring is an algebraically closed field. Such tests cannot detect a nonzero nilpotent in the dual numbers. The reduced closed subscheme therefore passes all those tests although its sieve is not fppf covering: a faithfully flat map cannot kill that nilpotent. This gives a concrete answer to problem 19. Nonnoetherian local objects remain essential. The source comparison is [Schroer2016, Propositions 4.3–4.4 and Corollary 4.5](https://www.math.uni-duesseldorf.de/~schroeer/publications_pdf/points_fppf.pdf), published in 2017; the separate [Gabber–Kelly2015, Theorems 2.3 and 2.6, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5782) retains its own base and topology hypotheses.

Several ring predicates admit clean constructions. Reduction is compatible with the appropriate cartesian model interpretation. Irreducibility needs a careful distinction between a domain and a ring with prime nilradical. An ultraproduct of the rings (k[x,y]/(xy,x^n,y^n)) shows that the latter class is not coherently axiomatized in the unchanged ring signature: every finite stage has irreducible spectrum, while the two limiting nonnilpotent elements have product zero. This conclusion is confined to that signature; it is not promoted into a claim about every Morita-equivalent presentation. Connectedness also requires an explicit convention about the empty scheme.

The zero-dimensional calculation is stronger and fully checkable. Every element separates into a unit on one idempotent component and a nilpotent on the complementary component. The equations for that decomposition have at most one solution. The program enumerates finite rings to check the equations, and the algebraic proof supplies unrestricted uniqueness. The affine scheme represented by those equations is a disjoint union of a unit piece and a nilpotent piece. Its representable presheaf need not be the presheaf coproduct of the two pieces, because a disconnected test affine can meet both. That distinction is one source of the misleading factorization on the page.

The absolutely flat reflection gives a second exact comparison. Adjoining the unique quasi-inverse of each element produces the universal absolutely flat ring; its spectrum has the constructible topology and the same residue fields. The matched sources are [Olivier1967, Proposition 5](https://www.numdam.org/item/SAC_1967-1968__2__A6_0.pdf) and [Stevenson2014, Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.0399). The source's unrestricted factorization is corrected explicitly, rather than attributed to those theorems. The actual margin question asks whether the zero-dimensional ring topology has enough points. It does: finite presentations over the integers form a countable finite-limit site, and the countable families D_n and their pullbacks generate a countable coverage. [MakkaiReyes1977, Theorem 6.2.4](https://reyes-reyes.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/makkai-reyes-book.pdf) supplies enough points for that separable site; its exact definition and proof were opened. Claim 115 records the application. The displayed D_n family has no finite subcover, but this alone is not a theorem excluding every alternative coherent presentation. Internal local nilpotence and nilpotence of a global section are also separated: a product of truncated polynomial rings gives unbounded nilpotence orders.

The characteristic pages contain a particularly instructive correction. Inverting every positive integer defines a $\mathbf Q$-algebra; it does not merely assert characteristic zero. The ring $\mathbf Z$ distinguishes those conditions. An infinite intersection of open classifying subtopoi need not be open, and the proposed limit of affine open representables does not recover the $\mathbf Q$-algebra classifier. On finite-type $\mathbf Z$-tests, no nonzero test algebra can contain $\mathbf Q$, by the Jacobson residue-field result. Thus the presheaf-limit shortcut loses the intended models. SGA IV 9.2.6 guarantees finite intersections, not arbitrary intersections; [StacksJacobson, Lemma 10.35.19](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00GB) gives the exact algebraic test.

The later characteristic gluing has a valid more specific interpretation: local choices of prime-field algebra structures, with the zero-ring points identified. It is not a decomposition theorem for every ring. The wider proposal to glue arbitrary subtopoi through largest stable open factorization loci still needs existence and inverse-image stability of those loci. The author's “OK” for the explicit characteristic construction is therefore retained without closing the broader question.

The connected-component calculations have a similar boundary. A bound on finite partitions into nonzero orthogonal idempotents forces a finite Boolean idempotent algebra with at most that many atoms. Finiteness cannot be omitted: an atomless Boolean algebra has no atoms. Exact component rank fails filtered-colimit closure under unrestricted ring maps; repeated projection of $k^n$ onto its diagonal copy has colimit $k$. Orderings of a given decomposition form a symmetric-group torsor, and that torsor pulls back. What remains unproved is that the source's construction of the sheaf of all decompositions, through its proposed largest locus, itself commutes with every inverse image.

## Fourth file: geometric algebra properties and accessible categories

The module list provides several useful negative answers. Flatness has finite relation witnesses and a geometric formulation. Faithful flatness of modules is not preserved by arbitrary filtered colimits: the system $\mathbf Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbf Z\xrightarrow{2}\cdots$ has faithfully flat free stages and colimit $\mathbf Z[1/2]$, which kills $\mathbf Z/2$ under tensor product. The same example separates finite generation and projectivity. Ordinary injectivity is not preserved by arbitrary geometric inverse image: a constant rank-one local system on a circle embeds in a unipotent rank-two local system without an invariant retraction. The proof and its two-by-two calculation are recorded. These answers concern ordinary categorical properties, not an unspecified internal substitute.

The canceled tensor-invertible-module argument also has a precise repair. If evaluation $M\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(M,A)\to A$ is an isomorphism, a finite expression for the preimage of 1 gives a finite unit-ideal cover. On each piece a functional splits off a free summand; the remaining summand lies in the kernel of evaluation and must vanish. This proves local rank one without presupposing that Hom commutes with localization. It does not imply global cyclicity. The invertible nonprincipal ideal $(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$ gives the counterexample. The local proof was independently checked by the EGA owner; [StacksInvertible, Lemma 15.119.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B8I) supplies the standard comparison.

Several relative algebra properties have finite-equation geometric descriptions, but formal smoothness and formal étaleness cannot be grouped with smoothness and étaleness after dropping finite presentation. The register states the exact radicial, spectrum-surjectivity, flatness, and formal-unramifiedness criteria, then leaves the broader formal-property formulation conditional. Internal fraction fields require decidable zero as well as the domain condition. These are explicit hypothesis boundaries, not an assertion that every internal domain embeds in a geometric field.

The accessibility sheets introduce a second set of recurring difficulties: regularity, directedness, and changing cardinal ranks. The functor $X\mapsto X^{\mathbf N}$ preserves $\aleph_1$-filtered colimits, yet fails to preserve presentability at arbitrarily large successor ranks arising from singular cardinals of countable cofinality. A separate diagonal argument refutes the proposed small cofinal family of countable subsets. Ordinary filtered presentations cannot be substituted for $\aleph_1$-directed ones. These examples explain exactly why the source's cardinal bounds need sharpening; the modern sharply-larger relation has different quantifiers.

The broad question whether every cocomplete bounded category is locally presentable has a modern large-cardinal classification through Vopěnka's principle. It is not recorded as an unqualified still-open elementary theorem. Verification of the asserted accessible-category bicolimit theorem is a separate editorial task. The checked flexible-limit theorem proves bilimits under its actual convention; reversing arrows does not prove a bicolimit result. The tensor product of locally presentable categories is also established, whereas a formulation for arbitrary accessible categories with unspecified exactness is not supplied by it. Exact comparisons include [AdamekRosicky1996](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/118845/CommentatMathUnivCarolRetro_37-1996-2_20.pdf), [ColimitDense2019](https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc1904/abrocaporo.pdf), Lack–Tendas §5, and [CavigliaHorel2016, §2](https://geoffroy.horel.org/Rigidification.pdf). The search log records the failed attempts to obtain the Bird thesis rather than treating an indexed snippet as a checked proof.

## Fifth through seventh files: models, constructions, and relative classifiers

The later theory sheets return to pointwise models and Yoneda. A later insertion supplies the preservation hypothesis missing from an earlier target-change statement. The model comparison and its full-faithfulness test must be read with that insertion. For free completion under all limits, the size and cogeneration conditions are separate from the formal universal property. Right fractions give the finite-limit preservation result; an arbitrary product requires closure of denominators under that product and a common-roof argument. Neither calculation bounds all roof classes in a large category. The exact fraction result is [StacksLocalization, Lemmas 4.27.9 and 4.27.17](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04VB).

The special adjoint functor theorem is used with its actual subobject-intersection hypothesis. A small cogenerating family alone is not silently promoted to the complete hypothesis package. Bounded small shapes, by contrast, have the required regular-stage completion theorem. These are distinct answers to the all-limit and bounded-envelope questions.

Finite-limit theories admit a strong recovery theorem: the opposite of the theory is recovered from the finitely presentable objects of its Set-model category. The finite-limit hypothesis splits the relevant idempotents. For finite-product theories, recovery has a Cauchy or Morita ambiguity. Even finite sets give a presentation with the same models as all finite sets without being the same uncompleted theory. The manuscript itself moves the finite-presentability preservation condition from the model functor to its left adjoint; Groups→Set refutes the earlier direction. Claims 083–085 use [LackPower2009, Theorem 8](https://purehost.bath.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/336518/Power_JFP_2009_19_3-4_265.pdf) and the exact [AdamekLawvereRosicky2003](https://lawverearchives.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2003-on-the-duality-between-varieties-and-algebraic-theories.pdf) comparison.

The two-categorical Yoneda construction then defines constructions natural in all models. Currying proves its proposed universal property if the category of constructions is locally small, belongs to the chosen doctrine, and evaluation preserves that doctrine. Those are hypotheses, not consequences of the notation. The large transfinite argument does not yet prove a stopping ordinal. The pages explicitly distinguish adjoining operations, making arrows invertible, and making arrows equal. A total cofibred category gives a lax construction; the pseudocolimit requires inversion of the appropriate cocartesian arrows.

Several late corrections sharpen the distinction between detection and generation. A small faithful basis can determine transformations without generating all operations. In FinSet opposite, the two-element object detects transformations between product-preserving functors because the singleton is its retract; its product closure nevertheless has only even cardinalities. Finitely presentable objects are generally closed under finite colimits and retracts, not finite limits. A module over a square-zero ring with infinitely generated radical supplies the exact kernel counterexample. Models of a finite-product theory need not be points of the proposed presheaf classifier: abelian groups give all models, whereas the relevant flat points are torsion-free. A faithful forgetful functor on internal models likewise does not imply faithfulness of the classifier's inverse image. Each assertion has its own witness; these are not one undifferentiated “Yoneda issue.”

The intrinsic representability question at scans 63–64 is retained as an earlier occurrence, not lost behind the later constructions. A represented pseudofunctor preserves weighted bilimits. A sufficient modern comparison needs more: Di Liberti–Osmond's precise finite bi-accessibility and preservation hypotheses yield a left biadjoint, whose value at the terminal category represents the pseudofunctor. The pages have not shown those hypotheses for their arbitrary doctrine. The exact source is [DiLibertiOsmond2025, Theorem 5.2.3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10485-024-09794-9), circulated in 2022, first published online in 2024, and appearing in the 2025 volume. The print theorem number is retained despite inconsistent online section numbering.

The pseudofield sheets give an especially clear algebraic calculation. The quasi-inverse equations yield

\[
\mathbf Z[x,y]/(x^2y-x,xy^2-y)≅\mathbf Z[t,t^{-1}]\times\mathbf Z.
\]

The idempotent $xy$ splits the invertible and zero components, and the quasi-inverse is unique. The author's field-classifier presheaf claim is canceled and replaced by a sheaf construction. [CaramelloJohnstone2009, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1972) matches that corrected topology; Beke's presheaf-type results independently rule out the naive identification. A proposed extension from topoi to every balanced bicomplete category remains underdefined: balancedness does not supply stable disjoint coproducts or effective descent. The dossier does not invent a counterexample to every possible interpretation of that unfinished statement.

The last sheets compare doctrines, duality, stacks, and relative classifiers. Infinite powers show why full faithfulness on transformations among existing finite constructions is not essential surjectivity onto every construction available on complete targets. Opposite categories also reverse 2-cells; the duality cannot be read as an unchanged covariant 2-functor. The proposed intrinsic category of constructions still refers to the theory and its target realizations, so it has not yet been defined from the bare Set-model category.

The inverse image of a stack uses the opposite comma category, a bicategorical Kan extension, and then stackification. A set-colimit formula omits descent data. The fixed-object relative classifier, by contrast, has a precise answer as a 2-pullback of classifying topoi. The final stack of slice topoi is unfinished and is not identified with the valid stack of structures without additional variance and 2-morphism data. The exact comparisons are [StacksPullback, §8.12](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04WA), SGA IV 9.1.6$d$, and Vickers2017, Theorems 5.11–5.12. The requested comparison with Lazard's analyseurs is also delimited: the opened original §§4–6 involve graded operations and their completions, additional data absent from an arbitrary finite-limit sketch.
