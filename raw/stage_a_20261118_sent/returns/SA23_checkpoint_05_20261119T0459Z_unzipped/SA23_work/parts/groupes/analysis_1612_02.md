All physical lines 1–808 have been read. Scans 20–25 continue the geometric-theory argument of the preceding file; scans 27–32 return to the universal completion; scans 34–35 are diagram-heavy notes on Lawvere theories, monadicity and comonadicity. These are separate logical units.

## Scans 20–23, lines 6–148

1. The empty many-sorted theory on a small discrete set I is classified by the presheaf topos on the opposite of finite-support I-indexed finite sets. Its finite-product syntactic category has objects finite products of the sorts. The words multi(co)simplicial / semi-simplicial must not be identified with the modern simplex category without checking the historical convention. The singleton-sort version is PSh(FinSet^op), not ordinary simplicial sets.
2. The presentation by objects, arrows between finite products, equations, then geometric axioms is valid in the broad geometric-theory sense. The ordinary term category J_2 is a quotient of the free algebraic term category J_1, hence surjective on objects and arrows. In contrast a general sheafification/localization R_1→R_2 need not be full. Lines 51 and 73 assert surjectivity on Hom for the topoi themselves; this stronger reading is false. A counterexample is sheafification of presheaves on a nontrivial site: maps between sheafified objects need not lift to maps between the original presheaves. The later claim must distinguish the quotient of syntactic categories from a left-exact reflector. Need an explicit finite-site witness in the computation.
3. Line 61 gives the clean valid proof: choose a small finite-limit site C for a Grothendieck topos R, classify Lex(C,-) by PSh(C), then impose its covering families. Thus every Grothendieck topos classifies a small geometric theory. 'Algebraic' here includes cover/disjunction axioms and is not modern equational algebraicity.
4. Adjoining arrows is a 2-pullback of model categories, hence a bicategorical pushout in the opposite convention for geometric morphisms. Lines 88–106 construct it from compatible small sites and a bicategorical amalgamated sum U_0, then impose finite-limit and cover axioms. The ordinary strict pushout of categories is not automatically the required pseudo-pushout; line 94 must be interpreted in light of its displayed 2-fibre-product universal property. Its R,S must be R_0,S_0. The construction is established for Grothendieck topoi, with a set-sized diagram.
5. If T_0→S_0 is essentially surjective on objects, the induced R_0→U_0 is essentially surjective; passage to the topos localization gives a generating image from R, as claimed at 109. The cardinality/generator argument for small filtered inverse limits of topoi at 114–119 uses stable compatible small sites and their 2-colimit. It is not an assertion that arbitrary pointwise limits in CAT are topoi.
6. The loc? question in the scan 23 diagram concerns the presheaf functor induced by the quotient of term categories. If q:J_1→J_2 is an ordinary full essentially-surjective quotient, restriction q^*:PSh(J_2)→PSh(J_1) is fully faithful, so the left Kan extension is a reflective localization. It is not automatically left exact, hence not automatically a geometric localization; the source's displayed question remains meaningful.

## Scans 24–25, lines 153–288

7. The heading B_G=PSh(one-object group G) prompts the represented structure but supplies no answer. The precise geometric classification is G-torsors in E (flat functors), whereas the objects of B_G itself are G-sets. Relativization and 'topos produit' are authorial leads, not proved new theorems on this page.
8. Model categories T_R(E) of a geometric theory admit filtered colimits, computed objectwise in the ambient interpretation, because filtered colimits commute with finite limits in a Grothendieck topos. A blanket closure under other limits does not follow. The question at 168 is a converse: if all model categories admit finite limits preserved by change of topos, must R=PSh(C) with C small finitely complete? It requires a precise distinction between merely existing limits and limits created in the given underlying-object interpretation; do not claim the converse from the usual theorem for cartesian theories alone.
9. For a small finite-limit theory C, Lex(C,E) is accessible and complete/cocomplete when E is a Grothendieck topos. Limits are pointwise; general colimits are not pointwise. Thus 'stable under arbitrary colimits' at 171 needs the admissibility-versus-ambient-closure distinction. For example groups in Set have coproducts, but the underlying set of the free product is not the disjoint union.
10. A nondegenerate Grothendieck topos may have no Set-valued points. The line 174 identifies T_R(Set) with its category of flat points with the appropriate 2-cell variance; emptiness of points does not imply degeneracy. Exact example/source to be matched.
11. The object classifier has the six displayed quotients: all objects, locally inhabited objects (X→1 epi), subterminal objects, terminal objects, empty objects, and the inconsistent quotient (with its degenerate-base exception). Their inclusions and intersections follow from epi+mono=iso in a topos. The subterminal classifier is the Sierpinski topos, representing Sub_E(1), answering the question at 251. It is not open or closed as a subtopos of the object classifier. The weak complements in 248 require checking in the full subtopos lattice, not just this six-object diagram.
12. These are six examples, not an exhaustive classification unless the phrase 'on a 6' is intended exhaustively. There are further geometric quotients: impose (x=y)∨(x=z)∨(y=z), equivalently make the union of the three diagonals cover X^3. Its Set models have cardinality ≤2, different from every displayed class. More generally each finite cardinal bound gives a quotient. This is an interpretive alarm against treating the diagram as a classification; the source does not explicitly say 'only'.
13. The notes excluding constant, locally constant and constructible objects at 265–273 are fragmentary. A concrete reason geometric definability cannot be automatic is failure of filtered-colimit closure. On Cantor space with a point p, increasing clopen complements U_n of the length-n cylinder of p are locally constant subterminal sheaves, but their union is C\{p}, which is not clopen, hence is not locally constant. For constant objects, a sequence of constant two-point sheaves with transition collapsing the second point on U_n has a colimit with two stalk elements at p and one elsewhere; it is not constant or locally constant. These are present counterexamples to an overly broad definability expectation, not words supplied by the page.
14. Line 277's suggestion that the examples are product/finite-limit theories is false for the nonempty-object subtheory with its inherited all-maps interpretation: the equalizer of the two different maps 1⇉2 is empty. It also lacks an initial object in Set, whereas a single-sorted equational theory has a free algebra on the empty set. Terminal, empty, subterminal, unrestricted-object and inconsistent cases need separate formulations; no blanket finitary-algebraic identification.
15. Line 280 lists further classifier/subtopos questions: an arrow X→Y, an endomorphism X→X, a pointing 1→X, binary operations X×Y→Z and X×Y→X or X×X→X, and two binary operations. Each has a small cartesian syntactic category and hence a presheaf classifying topos. Classification of all quotients/subtoposes is a separate and generally enormous question. The endomorphism theory is the category of N-actions as Set models, but its classifying topos is not automatically B_N=PSh(one-object N) (which classifies flat N-actions); preserve the displayed B_N label as a scope/typing alarm.

## Scans 27–32, lines 294–535

16. This is a more precise second universal-completion theorem than K161-2-01. Small sigma gives required limit/colimit shapes and preservation; small rho gives equivalences of marked-functor categories. Compatibility B says the marked shapes of rho are among or implied by sigma. A small marked category (C,d) has a small universal Delta-envelope with equivalence of functor categories. Construction is still announced through K rather than fully supplied.
17. The successor obligations a–e include target-universality, tracking marked cones, preexactness, adjoining cone vertices, and 0/1/2-cell extension/identification. The definition of preexactness is literally unfinished at 535. The iteration argument at 406–508 is detailed but conditional on a K satisfying these obligations. Do not report a completed construction of K.
18. Unlike the earlier defective cardinal sentence, lines 453–462 now explicitly require the ordered index set to be 'grand devant' a bound. Read this as the actual cofinality requirement; the later proof repairs the earlier formulation if that phrase means every bounded-size subset is bounded. The proof also needs bounds on |Fl S|, |lambda|, and the R_i data. Taking one sufficiently large regular cardinal handles all.
19. Lines 473–486 explicitly say modulo isomorphism of cones, resolving the earlier literal-family ambiguity. The lifting of an exact cone at 506–508 uses: factor its underlying diagram at one stage; adjoin the chosen limit at the next; its comparison becomes invertible in the colimit; an inverse and its two identities occur at a later stage. A common stage exists by regularity. This is a sound proof of the exactness-lifting lemma under the stated successor obligations.
20. Duplicating cone vertices at 520–532 gives the free category of comparison maps. Every diagram in a category with the required limits extends to an exact diagram uniquely up to unique isomorphism, not literally uniquely as the unqualified 'unique' says. Exactness of the old cone iff the comparison is invertible is correct. This is an explicit local construction; it does not by itself construct all of K.

## Scans 34–35, lines 540–807

21. The group-cohomology/simplicial expression at 540–544 is underdetermined (coefficient, degree and arrows uncertain). It cannot be promoted to a theorem.
22. The Lawvere-theory heading uses a small category with n=∐_n1 and then product-preserving functors from its opposite. This is the usual dual convention. The proposed intrinsic characterization by a distinguished generator P includes copowers, effective equivalence relations, regular epimorphisms detected by Hom(P,-), and finite support, but leaves fibre products and a second Beck condition as question/blank. These hypotheses need completion; do not call the displayed list a proved characterization.
23. The canonical monad T=UF and comparison A→Alg(T), split coequalizer diagrams, and the corrected 'conservative' condition point to Beck's monadicity theorem. Exact theorem: U has a left adjoint, reflects isomorphisms, and creates/preserves the required U-split coequalizers. The fragment supplies the adjunction but not the missing numbered condition. The split-fork identities at 681–689 prove an absolute coequalizer directly; line 723 has a source/target alarm for id_X1 versus id_X0.
24. For a geometric morphism f:B→B', pi=f^*f_* is a left-exact accessible comonad on B. Coalgebras for a left-exact accessible comonad on a Grothendieck topos form a Grothendieck topos. The comparison B'→Coalg(B,pi) is an equivalence when f^* is conservative (dual Beck; it already preserves equalizers). Line 757 B_pi≃B has the wrong source: the comparison is with B', not B. Faithfulness of the inverse image is equivalent to conservativity for geometric morphisms; this does not apply to arbitrary functors.
25. The G-set/Set example has f^* the forgetful functor and f_*(Y)=Set(G,Y); the induced comonad has counit evaluation at the identity and comultiplication induced by multiplication G×G→G. These formulas can be checked explicitly, with the left/right action convention kept fixed.
26. The question at 778–785 asks for a topos with enough points containing a given topos by a geometric inclusion. Every Grothendieck topos Sh(C,J) embeds in PSh(C), whose evaluations form a conservative set of points. This answers the displayed direction. It does not construct a conservative morphism from a point-rich topos onto a pointless one, nor identify arbitrary topoi with coalgebras on Set^I without an actual conservative inverse image.
