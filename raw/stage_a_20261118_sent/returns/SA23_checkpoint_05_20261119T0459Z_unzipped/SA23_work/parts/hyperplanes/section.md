### Generic sections, exceptional loci and connectedness

The early construction treats the universal hyperplane as a projective bundle over the source. Its generic fiber gives the familiar dimension drop and ordinary irreducibility. Geometric irreducibility requires the stronger image-dimension and field-extension argument; smoothness requires the first-order incidence condition. The preliminary claim that a generic linear combination of generators is nowhere zero is false: on the affine line, the generators 1 and t give a generic linear polynomial with a zero over the parameter field. This does not invalidate the correct first-order incidence proof. Claims 001–013 distinguish those arguments and the precise relative regularity and constructibility statements.

The good-parameter loci are compared with EGA IV3, 9.7.7, 11.3.7–8 and 12.2.1–4. The latter statements retain their properness, flatness, finite-presentation and equidimensionality hypotheses. A reference to openness of a fiber property is not treated as permission to discard those hypotheses. The source's connectedness argument is checked separately: EGA III1, Corollary 4.3.7 and Remark 4.3.8 cover the integral proper-source specialization over the normal dual projective space; the manuscript's component-intersection graph provides the additional reducible-source argument. The finite Stein factor may be generically purely inseparable, so it is not silently called birational in positive characteristic. A later numerical connectedness theorem is also recorded with its projectivity and irreducibility hypotheses [EGAIII1; EGAIV3; MartinelliNaranjoPirola2014].

The proposed codimension-two bound for bad irreducible hyperplane sections of an arbitrary morphism is false. Blowing up a point of projective three-space gives a smooth, projective, geometrically integral source whose hyperplanes through that point have reducible total transform. Those hyperplanes form a divisor in the parameter space. Poonen–Slavov identifies this obstruction and proves the corrected constant-fiber-dimension theorem, with a separate description of divisorial exceptions. Its first preprint appeared in 2020; the journal publication is dated 2022. This is a particularly clear case where a modern paper matches the exact question, rather than merely sharing the word Bertini [C-SA23-hyp-026; PoonenSlavov2022].

The depth and Serre-condition questions need finer treatment. Regular sections lower depth by one, and Benoist provides precise integral and depth-one exceptional-locus results. Those sources do not by themselves establish every coherent-sheaf-valued necessary-and-sufficient criterion written in the draft. The unresolved part is therefore stated as a restricted comparison problem, not announced as a new open conjecture. Fixed base loci also require care: the Segre product of a projective line and a projective plane supplies a fixed divisorial component even though projection away from the base has two-dimensional image. The source's finite-set and surface variants are kept distinct from that false broad version [C-SA23-hyp-018–028; Benoist2011; Stacks089R].

### Finite multisections, Grassmannians and projections

The finite étale quasi-section assertion in 7.4 and the warning about nonprojective proper families in 7.5 must be read together. The proof uses a projective hyperplane construction. Published EGA IV4, 17.16.1–3 gives quasi-finite flat or étale quasi-sections, a weaker conclusion. It does not certify the proper-only finite assertion. The projective case is supported; the exact nonprojective example remains unidentified by the recorded searches. This is also a checked link to the corresponding cote 24 witness [C-SA23-hyp-016–017; EGAIV4].

The higher-section construction uses quotient Grassmannians and Schubert strata. Its dimension formulas have been recomputed, and the geometric rank argument supplies the general nonempty range. The transcribed range permits nonexistent strata; the projective-space example makes the error immediate. Two other qualifications are essential: an arbitrary quasi-coherent Grassmannian need not be quasi-compact, and retractions of a locally split extension form a torsor whose trivialization needs a chosen splitting. The source retains a preceding basis choice, so its ordinary chart is valid; extending “canonical” to an arbitrary exact sequence is an additional interpretation, not a forced reading. The final comparison corrects this run's earlier overstatement on that point. These refinements are mathematical reconstructions, not changes to the transcription [C-SA23-hyp-034–041].

The claim that the discriminant of a generic projection is geometrically irreducible also fails in its stated breadth. A pencil on a smooth conic has two distinct geometric branch values. The calculation preserves the useful ordinary-tangency conclusion while rejecting irreducibility of the whole bad-value scheme. Kmentt–Shute's higher-codimension irreducibility bound applies to embedded varieties; its Remark 1.5 only expects the more general arbitrary-morphism version. It is not counted as a proof of the draft's full higher-rank sheaf/depth exercise [C-SA23-hyp-040–042; KmenttShute2022].

Projection claims are checked against the same exceptional-fiber phenomenon as the Bertini counterexample. Generic fiber dimension does not control every fiber, and a Cohen–Macaulay source alone does not make the projection flat. The precise miracle-flatness comparison requires a regular base and the local dimension equality. Prescribed finite points, birational hypersurface models and étaleness at singular points are separate assertions; an assertion about generic birationality is not broadened to an étaleness claim [C-SA23-hyp-047–053; Stacks00R4].

### Elementary fibrations and the proposed axioms

The elementary-fibration construction has a substantial published afterlife, but its field hypotheses vary. Artin's original SGA4 III, Exposé XI, 3.3 is over an algebraically closed field. Schmidt–Stix, Lemma 6.3, handles a nonrational closed point over an infinite perfect field. The Panin–Stavrova–Vavilov appendix supplies the prescribed finite-set result over an infinite field. Panin's 2025 Theorem 5.3 and proof give the finite-field version for a smooth geometrically irreducible affine variety. The dossier records the exact statements and distinguishes preprint, publication and receipt dates. These field results do not automatically settle the draft's unrestricted relative proposals [C-SA23-hyp-043–046; SGA4XI; SchmidtStix2016; PSVAppendix2009; Panin2025].

Guo–Panin's relative weak elementary-fibration theorem is a useful comparison with explicit regular-local-base and smooth-projective-source assumptions. “Weak” matters: the fiber conclusion differs from the strong geometrically irreducible version. The manuscript's subsequent axiomatic local incidence argument is also distinguished from its proposed global irreducibility theorem. The local dimension mechanism is checked; the exact global implication from the listed axioms was not found in an opened primary theorem. Relevant later sources were located, and access limits are recorded, so this residual question is class C rather than a claim that the subject was never developed [C-SA23-hyp-045,054–055; GuoPanin2023; Spreafico1998; CuminoGrecoManaresi1986].

### Picard groups, relative divisors and linear systems

The projective-bundle Picard calculation requires both a pullback line bundle on the base and a locally constant fiber degree. The draft itself replaces an unjustified finite partition by a locally finite one; quasi-compactness restores finiteness. On a product of projective bundles, fiber multidegree zero does not remove the pullback base factor. The degree-n divisor parameter follows the quotient convention and therefore involves the dual of the symmetric power; that dual should not be replaced without qualification by the symmetric power of the dual in arbitrary characteristic [C-SA23-hyp-056–061; EGAIII1; Stacks0B9S].

The general parameter construction uses a cohomological corepresentative, a locally split quotient and the relative Cartier open. EGA III2, 7.7.6 and 7.7.9 supplies the cohomological ingredient with its own hypotheses, rather than the entire divisor theorem at once. In the transposed-family argument, a section on an irreducible noetherian S1 fiber must be nonnilpotent to be a nonzerodivisor. Mere nonzero-ness fails on a nonreduced fiber. Properness is needed at the subsequent closed-image step [C-SA23-hyp-062–064; EGAIII2].

A cancelled paragraph on scan112 separately promises the general relative divisor scheme for projective flat families. Its precise published counterpart is Grothendieck’s Bourbaki Exposé232, Proposition4.1, printed152: the relative Cartier-divisor functor is an open subfunctor of the Hilbert functor, under the section’s locally noetherian-base hypothesis. The original proposition and the relevant Hilbert-family openness argument were read, the printed page inspected, and the published erratum checked. This general functor is distinct from the fixed-line-bundle parameter space and the special projective-bundle formula. The talk is dated February1962, but its order relative to the undated cancelled leaf is unknown; class A records the exact own-publication match without inventing a later manuscript chronology. Extension to a finitely presented arbitrary-base formulation is separately identified as modern approximation [C-SA23-hyp-109; GrothPicard1962; GrothPicardErr1962].

The linear-system discussion contains two different failures. A rational map loses a fixed divisor, so it cannot recover an arbitrary system. Faithful flatness alone also fails to make a divisor family monomorphic: two distinct missing points of an open subset of the projective line both restrict to the empty divisor. The source's later global-functions repair is recorded with the withdrawn claim. Relative Hartogs extension is supported under explicit finite-presentation, flatness and fiber-depth hypotheses, using EGA IV2 and IV3. The unqualified extension across arbitrary omitted divisors is not asserted [C-SA23-hyp-065–071; EGAIV2; EGAIV3].

The dual-number calculation identifies the tangent space to Cartier divisors with sections of the normal bundle. Grothendieck's Bourbaki Exposé 221, 5.3–5.5, explicitly publishes the relevant Quot/Hilbert tangent formulas. That first-order result does not alone prove a stronger arbitrary-functor monomorphism criterion. Likewise, PGL torsors and Brauer–Severi forms supply the correct descent framework, while the wider imperfect-field question about determination by rational members remains limited to the formulation the source supports [C-SA23-hyp-072–077; GrothHilbert1961; ReinekeSchroer2017].

### Ordinary singularities, characteristic two and second-order schemes

The manuscript's later corrections are central. It initially uses “ordinary” for nondegeneracy of the polar quadratic form, then replaces this by smoothness of the projective tangent quadric and keeps a separate nonsupersingularity condition. In odd rank and characteristic two these differ. A smooth conic has a purely inseparable Gauss map of degree two, directly refuting the earlier unrestricted separable-duality claim after a Veronese embedding. The later tentative statement about inseparable degree exactly two is not broadened beyond its ambiguous intended scope. SGA7 II, Exposés XII and XVII, verifies the characteristic-sensitive distinction and the published pencil theorem [C-SA23-hyp-029,078–080,092; SGA7II].

The discriminant on the singular-zero scheme is the determinant of the polar map, not vanishing of the entire second principal part. Its line-bundle twist and its behavior under multiplying the equation by a unit are recalculated. The claimed equality with a ramification scheme reduces to an exterior-power/cofactor identity; an explicit example checks the sign convention. A different shortcut is false: injectivity of a conormal map as a sheaf need not survive tensoring with a residue field. The nodal equation xy gives an injective module map whose fiber at the node is zero. The corrected criterion requires fiberwise injectivity or a locally split injection under the stated lci hypotheses [C-SA23-hyp-079–083].

The Hilbert calculation confuses the dimension of a graded piece with its cumulative Hilbert–Samuel length. Both formulas are reproduced from the complete-intersection Hilbert series. Constant fiber rank on a nonreduced base also needs stronger scheme-theoretic rank conditions; the one-by-one matrix [epsilon] over the dual numbers is a counterexample to the broad formulation. The determinantal codimension inequality in another footnote points in the wrong direction. These are mathematical alarms for the next scan-reading run, with no alteration of the supplied text [C-SA23-hyp-084–095].

The genuinely unfinished second-order construction is carefully delimited. On the specified rank stratum the locally free target and cokernel give split exact sequences, so the kernel does commute with arbitrary base change. An initial concern about that step was removed after checking the two splittings. What remains unverified on the pages is the separate generator-independent quadratic obstruction and its factorization into the claimed intrinsic singularity scheme. The final smooth-pair transversality extension is only proposed. Those residual questions, together with the unreadable duplicate's limited content, retain their own problem and reading records [C-SA23-hyp-089–098].

### Corrected discriminants and orthogonal torsors

The appendix constructs the corrected or half-discriminant in odd rank. Division by two takes place in the universal integer polynomial ring, before specialization to bases with two-torsion. The determinant has content exactly two, its transformation law follows universally, and the corrected discriminant detects smooth quadrics. The manuscript's direct-sum coefficient is wrong under its unnormalized convention: two odd summands produce a factor of four, not two. Rank-one forms already verify this [C-SA23-hyp-099–102; Conrad2014].

Smooth forms are classified by orthogonal torsors through faithfully flat descent. Étale trivialization requires the correct smooth group; in odd rank and characteristic two the finite component is mu2, which is not étale. The finite-cover strengthening uses SGA3 XXIV, Corollary 4.2.3 for split reductive groups over semilocal bases. After taking the appropriate finite component torsor, one applies that result to the split special orthogonal factor and spreads the finite-presentation data to a Zariski neighborhood. This proves the claimed finite étale or finite flat alternatives without asserting such a theorem for arbitrary smooth groups [C-SA23-hyp-103–105; SGA3XXIV; Conrad2014].

Conrad and Dolgachev–Duncan explicitly credit the independent Grothendieck/Kneser discovery. Dolgachev–Duncan, Remark 2.4, identifies the unpublished Appendix 17.16 and the terminology of corrected discriminant. This establishes reception, not an independently verified priority date. Published EGA IV, 17.16 concerns quasi-sections and must not be confused with that unpublished appendix [C-SA23-hyp-106; DolgachevDuncan2018; Kneser2002].
