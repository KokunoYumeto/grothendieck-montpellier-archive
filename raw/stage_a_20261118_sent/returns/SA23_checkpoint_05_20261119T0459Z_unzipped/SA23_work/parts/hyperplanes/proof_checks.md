# Cote25: mathematical checks and the limits of the certificates

These are checks of assertions already on the supplied pages, not developments of the research directions. `claims.json` identifies the manuscript passages. `computations.py` and its JSON output redo the symbolic calculations; finite examples are explicitly distinguished from proofs for all ranks or all schemes. Manuscript readings remain unchanged.

## Generic incidence and the two elementary failures

For a finite-type scheme (X/k) mapped to \(P^r_k\), universal hyperplane incidence is a projective bundle of relative dimension (r-1) over (X). On a component whose image in projective space has positive dimension it dominates the dual projective space. Its generic fiber is therefore irreducible when the component is irreducible, and has dimension \(dim X-1\). This proves ordinary irreducibility; geometric irreducibility requires the additional image-dimension and primary-extension argument in Stacks0G4C. Contracted components disappear. Polynomial extension and localization give the corresponding associated-prime calculation under the noetherian/coherent hypotheses.

The deleted irreducibility condition in Corollary2.4 cannot be dropped (C-SA23-hyp-004). Take (X=P^2_k), a line (Z), and (Z'=Z\cup\{p\}), where (p\notin Z). The generic hyperplane misses the closed point (p), so the generic sections agree although the closed subsets differ. Both subsets are closed; (Z) is irreducible and has positive-dimensional image, exactly as required by the live wording. Requiring both (Z) and (Z') irreducible restores the symmetric implication.

The nowhere-zero auxiliary lemma fails for \(O_{A^1}\) generated by (1,t) (C-SA23-hyp-008). Over (k(S_1,S_2)), the generic combination (S_1+S_2t) vanishes at (t=-S_1/S_2). Both generators are global and they generate the bundle. The symbolic substitution returns zero. The valid Bertini argument uses simultaneous vanishing of a section and its first derivative: for a smooth immersed (d)-fold, that imposes (d+1) independent conditions at a fixed geometric point. The incidence dimension, rather than a globally nowhere-zero section, excludes generic singularity.

## Connectedness, exceptional sets, and fixed divisors

For integral proper (X) with image dimension at least2, generic geometric connectedness of the incidence fibers and EGA III1 4.3.7–4.3.8 imply that every hyperplane fiber is geometrically connected. One can check the essential algebra directly: the reduced finite Stein factor is integral and has a generically purely inseparable function-field extension over the normal dual projective space. On a normal affine open, every integral element (b) has a characteristic-exponent power (b^{p^e}) in the base ring, because that power lies in the base fraction field and is integral. Such an extension is radicial. In characteristic zero the extension is birational. Finite connected Stein fibers give connected original fibers. This argument does not pretend that a purely inseparable extension is always birational.

For the manuscript’s reducible source, use the finite graph of irreducible components, joining two when their intersection has positive-dimensional image. If this graph were disconnected, deleting the cross-part intersections, whose images are finite, would disconnect (X), contrary to the source hypothesis. Every joined intersection has proper positive-dimensional image and therefore meets every hyperplane. The connected component sections consequently have a connected union. This is the extra argument beyond the integral statement of EGA.

For C-SA23-hyp-026 take (f:\operatorname{Bl}_p P^3\to P^3). The source is smooth, projective and geometrically integral; its image has dimension3. Hyperplanes through (p) form a divisor in the dual projective space. In a blow-up chart, their pullback equation is (x(a+bu+cv)=0), displaying both the exceptional divisor and the strict transform. Thus the proposed codimension-at-least2 irreducibility bound for an arbitrary morphism fails. Poonen–Slavov1.5 imposes equal dimensions on all nonempty fibers, and1.8 accounts for divisorial exceptions. Neither theorem licenses dropping those hypotheses.

The same example checks C-SA23-hyp-048 and050. Project from a general point (C\ne p) to (P^2), resolving its axis. The exceptional (P^2) over (p) survives and lies in one fiber, whose dimension is at least2; the expected dimension is1. The resolution is still smooth, since the new center is disjoint from the first exceptional center. Hence Cohen–Macaulayness does not imply flatness here. Stacks00R4 requires the local dimension equality as well as the regular base and Cohen–Macaulay source.

The fixed-base counterexample (C-SA23-hyp-028) is the Segre embedding (P^1\times P^2\subset P^5), with base plane (u_1=0). Projection away from that plane has image (P^2), yet every member containing it is (u_1(a_0v_0+a_1v_1+a_2v_2)=0). The fixed component is a divisor. The base-locus codimension condition in Stacks37.32.3 is therefore essential even though the projection image has dimension2.

## Higher linear sections and rank calculations

For codimension-(m) linear spaces in (P^r), the parameter Grassmannian has dimension (m(r-m+1)). Those through a fixed point form a Grassmannian of dimension (m(r-m)). Incidence over an (n)-fold therefore has generic-fiber dimension (n-m). These identities are recomputed symbolically, not merely checked numerically.

For a fixed (n)-dimensional tangent subspace in an (r)-dimensional ambient tangent space, the corank-(k) condition on its map to an (m)-dimensional quotient has matrix codimension (k(n-m+k)). After accounting for the incidence fiber dimension (n-m), the codimension relative to the full parameter dimension is

\[
k(n-m+k)-(n-m)=(k-1)(n-m)+k^2.
\]

The quotient must remain surjective on the entire ambient space. Its missing (k) directions must come from the (r-n) complementary directions, so the nonempty range is (1\le k\le\min(m,r-n)), not the transcribed (k\le r-m). In particular (X=P^3), (m=1), has no singular hyperplane strata. The program checks the symbolic identity and200 admissible small integer cases; the rank argument proves the general range.

A discriminant in a pencil need not be geometrically irreducible (C-SA23-hyp-042). Over characteristic zero, the degree2 conic pencil ([s^2+t^2:st]) on (P^1) has affine function (z+z^{-1}). Its derivative vanishes at (z=\pm1), with distinct values (\pm2). Thus the bad-value scheme has two geometric points. The source is a smooth projective geometrically integral conic and already a degree2 Veronese. Distinctness holds on an open set of pencils. The calculation refutes the claimed irreducibility, while the ordinary-double-point conclusion of a Lefschetz pencil survives.

The infinite-rank Grassmannian example is (\operatorname{Proj} k[x_0,x_1,\ldots]). Its standard opens (D_+(x_i)) have no finite subcover: choose an index outside any finite list and the homogeneous prime generated by all the other variables. Thus arbitrary quasi-coherent representability does not imply quasi-compactness or finite presentation. For a **locally split** sequence (0\to E'\to E\to E''\to0) with finite locally free outer terms, quotients restricting to the identity on (E') form a torsor of retractions under (\operatorname{Hom}(E'',E')). A nonzero extension of (O) by (O) on a genus1 curve has no global retraction, whereas the associated vector bundle has its zero section. A canonical vector-bundle identification needs a chosen splitting. Scan63 lines2592–2608 explicitly retain the preceding basis choice, so its standard chart is valid; the deleted phrase “sommes directes” does not by itself prove that Grothendieck asserted a choice-free arbitrary-sequence theorem. This corrects this run's earlier overstrong page-status assignment. For arbitrary sequences, local retractions need not exist: a retraction of2Z→Z would require2 to be invertible, which cannot happen after a faithfully flat base change ofZ. The torsor refinement therefore explicitly requires local splitting.

## Divisor families and linear systems

Projective-bundle Picard groups require a locally constant degree and a pullback line bundle. Infinite disjoint base components can carry every integer degree, so a finite partition requires quasi-compactness. On a product of projective bundles, taking (S=P^1_k) and the pullback of (O_S(1)) gives fiber multidegree zero but a nontrivial total line bundle. This checks the missing base factor in C-SA23-hyp-061.

The hypersurface parameter is (P((\operatorname{Sym}^n E)^\vee)) in the quotient convention: a locally split line in (\operatorname{Sym}^n E) dualizes to a rank-one quotient of its dual. One must not identify this dual with (\operatorname{Sym}^n(E^\vee)) without characteristic-sensitive qualifications. The fiberwise regular-section criterion is the relative Cartier criterion; EGA III2 7.7.6 provides the cohomological corepresentative under its stated hypotheses, rather than proving the entire divisor-functor assertion by itself.

For transposed families, an irreducible noetherian (S_1) fiber has just its generic point associated. A local equation is a nonzerodivisor exactly when it avoids the unique minimal prime, equivalently when it is not nilpotent. On a nonreduced fiber, nonzero is not sufficient. The complement of the fixed-point locus is the open image of the complement of the divisor. The maximal fiberwise Cartier open in the product is open by finite presentation and flatness; proper projection makes the image of its closed complement closed.

Removing a fixed divisor loses information. The one-member systems defined by \(\{0\}\) and \(\{\infty\}\) on (P^1) have the same rational map to (P^0). Conversely, under the explicit depth-at-least2 hypothesis, the local-cohomology exact sequence gives (F\simeq j_*F|_U), so homomorphisms of extending bundles are uniquely recovered. In the relative finite-presentation case, EGA IV3 11.3.7–8 lifts regular sequences from fibers; the original noetherian depth statement is IV2 5.10.2–5.10.5. No extension across an arbitrary divisor is inferred.

Faithful flatness alone does not make a divisor family monomorphic. On (X=P^1\setminus\{a,b\}\to\operatorname{Spec}k), the two degree1 members (a,b) both restrict to the empty divisor. The map is faithfully flat and finitely presented, yet the parameter map is not injective on (k)-points. The repaired global-functions condition controls the unit relating two sections with equal divisors. Over a proper geometrically integral scheme with (H^0(O)=k), equality of their divisors implies scalar proportionality, and all projective (k)-points recover a (k)-linear section subspace. This does not settle the unspecified broader imperfect-field version.

First-order deformations of a Cartier ideal ((t)) are ((t+\epsilon a)), with (a\bmod t) a section of its normal bundle. Map deformations are derivations and give \(\operatorname{Hom}(f^*\Omega_P,O_X)\). Grothendieck’s1961 Exposé2215.3–5.5 explicitly gives the divisor tangent formula. A calculation over dual numbers is not, on its own, a proof of the stronger arbitrary-functor monomorphism criterion.

## Ordinary, nonsupersingular, and intrinsic Hessian claims

In characteristic2 the smooth conic (xz-y^2=0), parametrized by ([s^2:st:t^2]), has gradient ([z:0:x]), hence Gauss map ([t^2:0:s^2]). Its function-field degree is2 and purely inseparable. This verifies the author’s own withdrawal of unrestricted separable duality for degree2 Veronese embeddings. A smooth quadratic tangent cone and a nondegenerate polar form are different conditions in odd rank characteristic2. SGA7II XII1.1–1.2 retains the smooth tangent-cone definition of ordinary singularity; XVII uses the distinction in the pencil argument.

For (xy=0), the polar matrix is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), which is nondegenerate. For the quadratic term (y^2) over characteristic other than2, the matrix is \(\operatorname{diag}(0,2)\), so its determinant vanishes even though the quadratic term is nonzero. Thus vanishing of the full second principal part is not the supersingular determinant condition.

The invalid conormal shortcut has a particularly small example. In (B=k[x,y]/(xy)), the map (B\to B^2), (1\mapsto(y,x)), is injective because \(\operatorname{Ann}(x)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(y)=(y)\cap(x)=0\). Its tensor with (B/(x,y)) is the zero map. The elimination calculation in the program verifies the intersection \((x)\cap(y)=(xy)\) before passing to (B). Injectivity of a conormal map as a module map is not fiberwise injectivity.

On the singular-zero locus of a section of a line bundle (K) on a smooth relative (d)-fold, the quadratic term belongs to (\operatorname{Sym}^2\Omega\otimes K), and the polar map is (T\to\Omega\otimes K). Its determinant lies in (\omega^2\otimes K^d). The principal-parts sequence gives

\[
\det P^1(K)=\omega K^{d+1},\qquad
L=\det(P^1(K)^\vee)\det(Q)\det(M)^{-1}=\omega^{-2}K^{-(d+1)}.
\]

Then (\operatorname{Hom}(L,K^{-1})=\omega^2K^d), correcting the uncertain intermediate exponent. The program derives all three exponents in the free group of line-bundle characters. The product rule shows (M(uf)=uM(f)) on (f=df=0), hence (D(uf)=u^dD(f)); an exact polynomial example checks this independently.

On the prescribed rank stratum, the Jacobian target and cokernel (W) are locally free. The target-to-cokernel sequence splits, so its image is locally free; the source-to-image sequence then splits too. Therefore the kernel (K) commutes with arbitrary base change there. An initial concern that a free cokernel would be insufficient was removed after checking both splittings. This does not prove the separate unverified second-order factorization (K\to\operatorname{Sym}^2W). The determinant of its polar map, when that map is defined, lies in (K^{-\operatorname{rank}W}(\det W)^2).

The printed determinantal codimension inequality points the wrong way: the zero matrix over a regular affine scheme has its entire base as rank-drop locus, of codimension zero. General determinantal height bounds are upper bounds; equality requires additional genericity. A matrix \([\epsilon]\) over (k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)) also shows why constant rank on geometric fibers alone does not force the matrix to be a split constant-rank matrix on the nonreduced base.

## Elementary singularities and Hilbert functions

For a regular local ring of embedding dimension (r) and a quadratic initial form which is a nonzerodivisor, the graded tangent-cone algebra has Hilbert series

\[
\frac{1-T^2}{(1-T)^r}.
\]

Thus its degree-(n) piece has rank \(\binom{n+r-1}{r-1}-\binom{n+r-3}{r-1}\), while the cumulative rank through (n) is \(\binom{n+r}{r}-\binom{n+r-2}{r}\). The manuscript confuses these at the flagged location. The program checks the displayed counts for multiple ranks/degrees; the exact sequence of a degree2 nonzerodivisor proves the general generating-series identity.

The finite-residue-field condition in the elementary-singularity comparison cannot simply be omitted. On the product of a node with an affine line over an algebraically closed field, the generic point of the singular line has residue field (k(t)); its local ring has a two-dimensional quadratic tangent space with smooth projective quadric. After extending to a field where (t) is represented by a rational point, the corresponding rational point on the total product acquires the extra tangent direction along the line, and the quadratic initial form is independent of that direction. Its projective tangent quadric is a cone and is singular. This checks the distinction between local elementary singularity at a nonclosed point and the proposed geometric comparison.

## Corrected discriminants, orthogonal groups, finite covers

For (Q=ax^2+by^2+cz^2+dxy+exz+fyz), the program computes

\[
\tfrac12\det B_Q=4abc-af^2-be^2-cd^2+def.
\]

The division is performed in the universal integer polynomial ring. Modulo2 the odd polar matrix is alternating and has determinant zero, so every universal coefficient is even. Evaluation on the standard odd split form gives determinant \(\pm2\), proving content exactly2. The transformation law is proved over the torsion-free universal coefficient ring, divided there, and only then specialized to bases with2-torsion. One must not cancel2 inside an arbitrary base ring.

Under the unnormalized convention \(\delta_n=\det B\) for even (n) and \(\delta_n=\det B/2\) for odd (n), two odd summands contribute factor4. Indeed (ax^2) and (by^2) have corrected discriminants (a,b), while their direct sum has polar determinant (4ab). This is a counterexample over (\mathbf Z[a,b]), including its nondegenerate open. The rank1–8 standard-form computations check the signs without asserting a different sign-normalized convention.

For a field, a projective quadric singularity is a nonzero radical vector (v) with (Q(v)=0). In characteristic2 an odd smooth form can have a one-dimensional polar radical on which (Q) is nonzero; for example (x^2+yz). Its only projective polar-radical point is ([1:0:0]), where (Q=1), so the projective quadric is smooth. Even rank, or invertibility of2, gives the usual equivalence with polar nondegeneracy. The dimension of a nonempty smooth projective quadric is rank minus2.

SGA7II XII1.2 gives an étale hyperbolic normal form, with a final unit multiple (u z^2) in odd rank. Adjoining a square root through (A[t]/(t^2-u)) is finite locally free of rank2 and faithfully flat; it gives the standard form. Isometries are cut out by finitely many equations in (GL_n), and their sheaf is an orthogonal torsor. Faithfully flat descent of the standard module and its quadratic tensor gives the inverse construction.

The finite-cover improvement uses precisely SGA3 XXIV4.2.3, not an assertion about arbitrary smooth groups. Over a semilocal base, torsors under a split reductive group admit finite étale trivialization. For an even-rank orthogonal torsor, first take its finite étale component quotient and then the split special-orthogonal cover. In odd rank use (O=\mu_2\times SO'): first the finite locally free \(\mu_2\) torsor, then the finite étale cover for the split reductive factor. Finite presentation spreads the resulting cover and isometry from a local ring to a Zariski neighborhood. In characteristic2, (aT^2\simeq T^2) for a nonsquare (a\in k^*) needs a purely inseparable square root, so no separable extension suffices; the finite flat cover still does. The rank1 group is already \(\mu_2\), nonsmooth in characteristic2.

Conrad and Dolgachev–Duncan identify the half-discriminant and credit the independent Grothendieck/Kneser discovery. Their evidence does not determine which discovery came first. The reference to unpublished Appendix17.16 must not be confused with published EGA IV17.16 on quasi-sections.

## Recovered fixed-linear-space question — C-SA23-hyp-108

Scan81, physical lines3283–3292, explicitly asks whether the obstruction disappears after a Veronese of degree at least2. This is a smoothness question about hyperplanes constrained to contain a fixed positive-dimensional linear space, distinct from the earlier irreducibility question. The assessment concerns its unrestricted wording; no transverse-intersection hypothesis on that space is supplied.

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic0, let X=P² with coordinates [x:y:z], and take f to be the identity into P². Thus X is smooth, projective and geometrically integral, and f is unramified. Under v₂:P²→P⁵ use the six quadratic monomials as projective coordinates. The two independent linear coordinate equations U_(x²)=U_(y²)=0 cut out a projective3-space C. A hyperplane contains C exactly when its equation is a U_(x²)+b U_(y²)=0 for [a:b] in P¹. Its pullback is the nonzero conic a x²+b y²=0.

Every member contains p=[0:0:1], and all its partial derivatives vanish there. On z=1 its local equation is in (x,y)², so its one-dimensional local ring has embedding dimension2 and is not regular. Consequently every hyperplane in this pencil is tangent to the Veronese surface: the entire dual line C^o lies in the dual discriminant. This remains a counterexample if one requires the base intersection to be finite: C∩v₂(X) is supported at p with local ideal(x²,y²) and length4. It is nonreduced, a distinction that must be retained.

For any degree n≥2 one may instead use the two coordinates x^n,y^n of v_n; the resulting codimension2 linear space is positive-dimensional, and every pulled-back equation lies in (x,y)² at p. The all-degree conclusion follows from this ideal inclusion, not a finite enumeration. The degree2 polynomial and its gradient are rechecked exactly in `computations.py`, output key `veronese_fixed_linear_space`.

The first-jet criterion is the one used in the opened proof of Stacks33.47.3. That lemma's generating and immersion hypotheses give smooth generic members on the locus where they hold; they do not remove a forced zero first jet at a prescribed base point. The fixed-subscheme construction in33.47.1 supplies those hypotheses on the complement for high powers. The finite-field comparison is Wutz2016, Theorem1.2, with explicit embedding-dimension restrictions. This source comparison is not a claim that Wutz states the present pencil counterexample or settles a differently restricted reading of Grothendieck's question.
## Recovered general divisor-scheme promise: C-SA23-hyp-109

The cancelled source passage, scan112 lines4484–4494, defines relative effective Cartier divisors for flat locally finitely presented X/S and promises representability when X/S is projective. This is a separate claim from fixing L and from the projective-bundle degree-n formula. Grothendieck’s February1962 Bourbaki Exposé232, §4 and Proposition4.1 (printed150–152), supplies the exact projective-flat theorem over a locally noetherian base. The proof restricts the universal flat Hilbert family to the relative-Cartier locus: the failure locus is closed in the proper family, so its image is closed on the Hilbert parameter scheme and its complement is open. Hilbert representability is used as checked background; this paragraph does not claim an independent proof of the full Hilbert theorem. The arbitrary-base finitely presented version additionally uses noetherian approximation and is not silently inserted into the printed proposition. The linked erratum, printed303, was read; it corrects other propositions, not4.1. Relative chronology of this undated archival leaf and the February1962 talk is not established.
