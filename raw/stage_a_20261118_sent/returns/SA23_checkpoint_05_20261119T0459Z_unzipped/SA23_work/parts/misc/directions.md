## 1. Arithmetic icosahedra: formulate before classifying

The source asserts étale-local existence, conjugacy under an orthogonal group and finite étale $A_5$ stabilizers. Testing those assertions is an editorial task; the separate unresolved calculations retain their authorial problem records. A useful result would give an actual moduli functor, a clearly specified quadratic form and base, and a theorem separating ordinary icosahedral configurations from projective substitutes in bad characteristics. The first step is to collate the uncertain base and group notation on scan 22 against the ink, then write the functor over a base in which both 2 and 5 are invertible. The next finite calculation is to identify which defining equations cease to describe distinct vertices at 2 and 5.

Known starting points are the universal quadratic angle algebra, the exact characteristic-two inversion calculation, Ji2023 Proposition 3.1 for explicit good-prime matrices, and Esquisse §4's programme. LiedtkeSatriano2025 Theorems 4.7 and 5.2 concern different group-scheme and fppf-local questions. They should be used to design hypotheses, not to declare the archival assertion settled. One run can produce a rigorous problem statement and a matrix of valid special cases; an all-base theorem is not promised.

## 2. Differential automorphisms: a completed correction chain

The aim is a concise companion that reconstructs the author's withdrawal and later reassertion and then states the exact surviving implications. The first task is to put the difference-ideal criterion, bounded-unipotence sufficiency and the dual-number counterexample in one theorem-and-counterexample sequence. The ideal-power step can be proved by iterated commutators and polarization, with Zhao's checked2022 Corollary2.4 recorded as an exact later source. The final statement should give the filtration-raising repair of the internal-ideal corollary and distinguish it from the already-correct central base-ideal module example.

All mathematics needed for this commentary is checked in this run. The proposed further work is editorial and expository, with potential formalization only after a precise theorem list is fixed. It should not claim a new theorem or a reason why the lower manuscript corollary remained uncorrected.

## 3. Functors and nilpotent detection

The literal unrestricted question is already false. A future task would identify a natural category of module-valued functors for which the formal exponential map is faithful and the ordinary classification uses the appropriate local nilpotence. Start by stating separately the quasi-coherent module functor, the internal Hom functor and an arbitrary semilinear functor. Retain (A\mapsto A_{\mathrm{red}}) as the test that excludes any proposed condition too weak to detect nilpotent directions.

The coefficient proof over (K[t]/t^n) and (K[t]), Yuan's arbitrary-module classification, the rank-three base-change example and the tensor-degree caveat are settled starting points. A few hours should suffice to isolate and prove a useful exact subclass. A sweeping classification of all functors is not a justified first target.

## 5. Graph embeddings and mapping-class stabilizers

The mathematical stabilizer question has an exact answer for finite graphs in compact oriented surfaces, via the independently re-proved Ladegaillerie characterization in ColinMesmay2014. A future companion should translate the manuscript's (G/H), componentwise outer fundamental-group maps and regular-neighbourhood data into that theorem, while explicitly distinguishing punctures, boundary conditions and orientation preservation.

Begin with one two-loop bouquet in a torus: compute its rotation system, boundary cycles and the induced fundamental-group map, then compare its stabilizer with the orbit statement. Extend only after fixing peripheral conventions. Yagasaki2000 supplies the separate topological restriction-bundle mechanism. The goal of one run is a complete dictionary and worked example, not a rediscovery of the isotopy theorem or an unsupported extension to arbitrary infinite-type surfaces.
