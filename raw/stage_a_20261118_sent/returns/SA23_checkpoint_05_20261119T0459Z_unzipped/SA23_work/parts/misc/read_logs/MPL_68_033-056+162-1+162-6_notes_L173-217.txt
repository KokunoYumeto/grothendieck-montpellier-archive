188:

191: The written induction explains why a morphism must be bijective and why it is determined, but the question “Existence ?” separates that argument from existence of an isomorphism between two theories. 199: ### O05 — The axioms determine a theory up to isomorphism, not a fixed boundary-sign convention
200: 
201: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scans 050, 052 and 054, Ω(0), the positive half-space and the basis formula. **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Context:** H68, “Réduction au signe du déterminant”, his pp. 49, 51 / scans 050, 052, and its comparison with the formula on his p. 53 / scan 054; adjacent to **68-orientation-stokes-axioms**. **Confidence:** high for the conditional sign calculation; the manuscript's convention is inferred from its basis formula.
202: 
203: Hua's project interprets the formula as placing the inward normal last and computes a factor (−1)ⁿ between that Stokes map and the outward-normal-first convention in dimension n. Under those two conventions, the canonical isomorphism of the resulting theories on an n-dimensional space is multiplication by $(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}$, since its signs tₙ satisfy t₀=1 and tₙ₋₁=(−1)ⁿtₙ. This makes explicit why unique isomorphism of theories does not identify the two Stokes maps by the identity transformation on every orientation set. The extra comparison formula is ours, not a formula written on the sheets or asserted by the supplied Lean card.

205: ### O06 — A possible meaning of the unfinished I(ω,ω′)
206: 
207: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 054, upper flag U⊂W⊂V and “I(ω,ω′) ∈ Ω”. **Kind:** own idea / proposed completion. **Context:** H68, “Produits, et composition des applications de Stokes”, his p. 53 / scan 054, which explicitly leaves I undefined. **R:** none; beyond the statement of **68-orientation-stokes-axioms**. **Confidence:** medium — the construction matches the flag and the basis convention, but the missing target is not written.
208: 
209: A candidate is the orientation of V/U obtained from the exact sequence 0→W/U→V/U→V/W→0, ordering the quotient factors as (W/U,V/W). With ω on V/W and ω′ on W/U, that convention gives the orientation represented by ω′ followed by ω and makes Stω′Stω the corresponding composite boundary operation. Reversing the order introduces (−1)ʳˢ for r=dim(W/U) and s=dim(V/W), which fits the product-sign calculation below. This is a proposal for study, not a decipherment of the incomplete Ω-expression.

211: ### O07 — The product signs suggest a corner-cancellation test
212: 
213: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 054, Stokes/product formulas and the square and quadrant drawings. **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Context:** H68, “Produits, et composition des applications de Stokes”, his p. 53 / scan 054. **R:** none; beyond **68-orientation-stokes-axioms**. **Confidence:** high for the conditional sign identity; medium for identifying the purpose of the drawings.
214: 
215: For two independent codimension-one faces, the two orders of taking boundary orientations exchange two one-dimensional normal factors and therefore differ by −1 under the displayed Koszul rule. This is the local cancellation required for a boundary-of-boundary identity, and gives a concrete test of the two Stokes/product signs using the quadrant. H68 describes those drawings and derives the product signs, but the page does not itself write a chain complex or assert ∂²=0, so that interpretation remains an implication to investigate.
