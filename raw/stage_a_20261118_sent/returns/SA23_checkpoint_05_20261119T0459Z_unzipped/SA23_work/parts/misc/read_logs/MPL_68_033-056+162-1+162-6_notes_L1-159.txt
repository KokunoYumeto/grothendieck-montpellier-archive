34: 
35: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 034, condition 1 and the final “il f et s.” corollary; compare scan 033, lower half. **Kind:** own idea / correction to a contextual diagnosis. **Context:** H68, “Caractérisation sans parties infinies”, his p. 33 / scan 034, especially the footnote beginning “Pris à la lettre”. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — the logical distinction follows from strengthening a hypothesis, and the one-position example is explicit.

37: Condition 1 is literally “∀ x ∈ G̃, ∃ y ∈ R(x)”, without excluding C₀, whereas scan 033 excludes terminals from the same existential test. Hua's project correctly supplies that missing restriction in its modernised statement, but its suggestion that the proposition's conditional equality conclusion becomes false is too strong: the literal hypothesis is stronger, so the implication remains valid wherever its hypotheses can hold. What fails is the final corollary's necessity for the actual pair (G,P), as seen with one terminal position t won by the player assigned to t, where G={t}, P=∅ and R(t)=∅. Condition 2 is harmless at a terminal position by vacuous universal quantification; it is condition 1 that causes the defect.

113: ### E09 — A direct negative answer for the unrestricted formal-functor question
114: 
115: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scans 043–044, arbitrary module-valued functors and the NB on bijectivity. **Kind:** own idea / counterexample. **Context:** H68, “Foncteurs en modules”, his pp. 42–43 / scans 043–044, which leaves the formal question unanswered. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — both distinct endomorphisms and their identical images are explicit.
116: 
117: Take K=Q and the semilinear module-valued functor M(A)=Ared=A/N(A), with the A-module structure induced by reduction. Its natural endomorphisms 0 and id are distinct, but every nilpotent λ in every test algebra acts as zero on M, including after restriction to any base algebra, so exp(λ·0)=exp(λ·id)=id. Thus the proposed map EndK(M)→Homgr(Ḡa,AutK(M)) need not even be injective in the unrestricted class described on scan 043. This adds a formal-side counterexample to H68's ordinary-side objection and isolates the failure of nilpotent test parameters to detect the endomorphism.

121: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scans 039–042, the nilpotence mismatch and the equivalence involving exp. **Kind:** own idea / unpursued implication. **Context:** H68, “Opérations sur un module” and “Morphismes, et le passage à exp”, his pp. 38–41 / scans 039–042. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — the logarithm computation is finite on each vector.

125: ### E11 — “Loc nilpotent” must be interpreted on the sheaf, not just global functions
126: 
127: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 044, “Globalisation”. **Kind:** own idea / qualification of a contextual counterexample. **Context:** H68, “Globalisation”, his p. 43 / scan 044. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high for the localization objection; medium for the intended meaning of the manuscript abbreviation. **Supplementary primary check:** the unit-polynomial step is documented as `Polynomial.not_isUnit_of_natDegree_pos_of_isReduced` in [mathlib's polynomial-nilpotence documentation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/Nilpotent.html); this checks that lemma only, not the interpretation of the manuscript.
128: 
129: The page assumes a derivation of the sheaf OX and writes “Θ loc nilpotent”, whereas H68 chooses nilpotence uniformly on neighbourhoods and contrasts it with d/dx on Q[x]. That familiar ring derivation is not locally nilpotent on every open's sections: on Q[x,x⁻¹], its iterates on x⁻¹ never vanish, so it is not a counterexample to a sheafwise reading of the hypothesis. In fact, a locally nilpotent derivation of a reduced local Q-algebra is zero: exp(tD) sends a unit to a unit of A[t], which must be constant when A is reduced, and every element of a local ring is either a unit or differs from a unit by 1. Thus a stalkwise locally nilpotent reading can also force identity on the reduction, but gluing the exponentials and the exact scope of “loc” should be stated explicitly.

155: ### E16 — The literal universal bound on differential operators is broader and false
156: 
157: **Location:** `68.pdf`, scan 046, first corollary, sentence after $N(A)^{N+1}=0$. **Kind:** own idea / counterexample to the literal wording. **Context:** H68, “Automorphismes unipotents et dérivations nilpotentes”, his p. 45 / scan 046, where the sentence is restricted to the automorphism u. **R:** none. **Confidence:** high — a reduced polynomial ring gives an immediate test.
158: 
159: The ink reads “∀ opérateur diff de A est d'ordre ≤ N”, whereas H68 states only that the specified automorphism u then has order at most N. Taken literally for all differential operators, the claim fails for A=Q[t]: N(A)=0 allows N=0, but d/dt has order 1, since its commutator with multiplication by t is the identity. Thus the contextual restriction is mathematically essential, not merely a choice of prose; the manuscript wording remains unchanged.
