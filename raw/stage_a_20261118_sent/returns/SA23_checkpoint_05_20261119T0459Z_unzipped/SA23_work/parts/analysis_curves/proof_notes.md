## 1. Detecting families, representation, and support (001–012)

**Weak detection (001–003).** In a noetherian abelian category suppose every nonzero object receives a nonzero map from some member of a family G. For a fixed object F choose a maximal subobject F′ admitting a finite filtration with quotients that are quotients of objects of G. Such a maximal subobject exists by the ascending chain condition. If F/F′ is nonzero, a nonzero G→F/F′ has image Q, and its inverse image extends the filtration of F′ by Q, a contradiction. This proves the filtration assertion. Right exactness then detects vanishing; bounded cohomological functors follow by induction from the first possible degree. The boundedness is what starts the induction.

This is weaker than faithful Hom. For A=k[ε]/(ε²), the module k=A/(ε) maps nontrivially into every nonzero finite A-module (take an element in its socle), but no direct sum of copies of k surjects onto A. The distinction is mathematical, not merely terminological. For finite M over a noetherian ring, associated-prime localization gives Ass Hom(M,F)=Supp(M)∩Ass(F); this proves the support detection used in 002. On a noetherian scheme a nonzero quasi-coherent internal Hom contains a nonzero coherent subsheaf. With an ample invertible sheaf, a sufficiently positive twist supplies a nonzero global section, giving the negative-twist family of003. The sign follows from Hom(M(−n),F)=Γ Hom(M,F)(n).

**Abstract tensor and Hom (004–008).** Present a finite module as A^b→A^a→M→0. For a right A-object P of an abelian category define P⊗M as the cokernel P^b→P^a. Applying Hom(−,Q) gives exactly Hom_A(M,Hom(P,Q)); Yoneda proves independence of the presentation and functoriality. An additive right-exact functor is therefore determined by its value on A and its A-action. Arbitrary modules require arbitrary coproducts and their preservation. For example over a field T(V)=V** is exact, but T(k)=k and V→V** is not an isomorphism for infinite-dimensional V. Right exactness alone cannot prove an all-module tensor representation. The opposite-category construction gives the contravariant statement, with products replacing coproducts.

For finite I-torsion M, some I^n kills M. Apply the finite representation theorem over A/I^n. Compatibility identifies the representing objects H_n with the I^n-annihilators inside later H_m. Exact filtered colimits commute with the finite kernels used by a finite presentation, so H=colim H_n represents the functor when that exact-colimit assumption is available. Without it the compatible system, not its unsupported colimit, is the result. At the first possibly nonzero degree of a bounded-below contravariant cohomological functor, left exactness holds and the same argument applies. SGA2 IV1.1,1.3,1.4 matches these Ab-valued assertions exactly; the general abelian-target formulation is this reconstruction.

**Torsion injectivity (009–010).** Testing A/p in the support criterion identifies the associated primes of the representing H. For injectivity let N⊂M be finite and u:N→H with H I-torsion. A common I^n kills the image of a finite generating set of N. Artin–Rees gives m with N∩I^m M⊂I^n N. The induced map on N/(N∩I^mM) extends over M/I^mM by exactness on finite I-torsion modules. This proves extension for finite modules, hence Baer’s criterion over the noetherian ring. Applied to Γ_I(J)⊂J for injective J, it proves Γ_I(J) injective. The printed N∩I^mN cannot do this job. SGA2 IV2.1/2.2 is an exact published counterpart.

**Ind-representation (011–012).** Every quasi-coherent module on a noetherian scheme is the filtered union of its coherent submodules (Stacks30.10.4); if its support is in Y, those submodules have support in Y. Coherent modules are finitely presented for this filtered-colimit calculation. For a small abelian category C, an additive left-exact T:C^op→Ab is a filtered colimit of representables: pairs (M,x∈T(M)) form an indexing category; direct sums join two elements, and the cokernel of a difference of parallel arrows makes them equal, using left exactness of T. Thus Lex(C^op,Ab)=Ind(C), giving the stated supported quasi-coherent representation.

The separate formula H=colim T(O/I^n) is false globally and is not even an equality of objects in the same category unless an extra sheaf construction is supplied. Take X=P¹_k, I=0, H=O(−1), and T(F)=Hom(F,H). Then T(O)=0, while T(H)=k. Every term of the proposed colimit is zero although H is nonzero. Local affine evaluation or the complete ind-system works; global O-evaluation does not.

## 2. Depth and descent (013–019)

P1 means uniqueness of extension across Y; P2 means uniqueness and existence on every open. A sheaf epimorphism only asserts locally liftable sections, and must not be equated with surjectivity of every section map. In the noetherian spectral setting, sections over a localized open come from a neighborhood: use its quasi-compactness to choose a finite cover, then shrink finitely many neighborhoods so all equalities and gluing data hold. This is the finiteness needed by014. Noetherian induction on the remaining closed subset, extending or comparing first around a generic point, proves the pointwise P1/P2 criterion. The sheaf gluing axiom is used essentially. SGA2 XIV1.1/1.8 provides the published depth counterpart for étale sheaves and strict localizations, not an unqualified identification of those localizations with the draft’s Zariski ones.

For categorical P1/P2, Hom presheaves must at least be sheaves, hence a prestack hypothesis. P3 also needs effective descent. Here is a complete counterexample to the unqualified fibred-category claim017. Let X have points η,a,b with η generic and a,b closed; its nonempty proper opens are {η}, {η,a}, {η,b}. This is the spectrum of a semilocal principal ideal domain with two maximal ideals. Put U={η}. Assign the terminal discrete category to each proper open (including the empty open), and the empty category to X; all restriction functors are forced. This is a fibred category and a prestack: every relevant Hom presheaf for existing objects is terminal. At a and b, the localized category and its punctured restriction are both terminal, so local P3 holds. Globally the functor empty→terminal is not essentially surjective. The local objects on {η,a} and {η,b} have compatible restrictions but no global descent. Stacks8.4.1 names exactly the missing axiom.

With a stack, the same noetherian induction can glue the extended object to the old one; full faithfulness provides compatible gluing maps and effective descent provides an object. Restriction across smaller closed subsets follows by cancellation in the triangle of restriction functors, independently of this existence issue. Torsors form a stack, so the gap is absent in019. The nonabelian obstruction objects are pointed sets or sheaves of pointed sets in these low degrees; the draft does not turn them into abelian derived-functor groups. The printed author of SGA2 XIV is Mme M. Raynaud, with an explicit footnote attributing the underlying unpublished notes to A. Grothendieck.

## 3. Multipliers and singular values (020–030)

For a bounded right-module map T on a C*-algebra C, the double adjoint on C** satisfies T**(xy)=T**(x)y by weak-star density and separate weak-star continuity. Hence u=T**(1) gives T**(y)=uy, so T(x)=ux. If uC⊂C and u=u*, adjoints give Cu⊂C. Two-sided idealizers give the multiplier algebra. For an arbitrary ambient algebra the annihilator of C may act trivially, so injectivity of its idealizer action requires essentiality. This is the exact content of020; the French left/right translation label is not used to reverse the displayed identity.

Grothendieck’s Bourbaki113 Proposition3 gives the Pólya support-functional representation021. The bounded convergence-in-measure condition closes convex sublevels; dual separation and the bounded-set weak-star closure criterion yield σ(L∞,L1) lower semicontinuity and supremum of affine pairings. This proof concerns weak-star topology, not the Banach-space weak topology with the full dual of L∞. For022 the fragment does not define b. A measure atom on an arbitrary C*-spectrum need not have its indicator in the continuous functions. The conditional proof cannot supply those omitted hypotheses.

Minimax suprema must be over unit vectors or the unit ball. Otherwise A=I makes the displayed supremum infinite. A norm perturbation δ shifts every minimax level by at most δ. Polar decomposition identifies nonzero spectral distributions of A and A*, not necessarily their zero eigenspaces.

For sum/product inequalities025/082, intersect subspaces of codimensions m−1,n−1 (for the product use the inverse image under the first operator). The intersection has codimension **at most** m+n−2, so the corresponding singular value is bounded by the sum/product of the two restricted norms. Hilbert tensor identities026 follow first on finite linear combinations of basis tensors and then by completion. Simple strongly measurable functions are dense in the Bochner L² space in question. ## 4. Exterior powers, determinants, and convexity (031–050)

If a_n=(1/n!)Alt, then a_n(e1⊗…⊗en) is a sum of n! orthogonal tensors with coefficients ±1/n!, and has squared norm1/n!. The Gram-normalized wedge e1∧…∧en has norm1. Therefore the isometric inclusion is sqrt(n!)a_n and the normalized quotient is q_n/sqrt(n!); the draft swaps these factors. Their product cancels in the mixed operator estimate. Functoriality, adjoints and positive powers of exterior powers follow on decomposable wedges, then by density and continuous functional calculus. The operator norm of Λ^n A is the product of the first n singular values. Spectral radius≤norm and multiplicativity of Λ prove Weyl and product log-majorization. Grothendieck1956 I1–2 publishes this exterior/fundamental-form framework; Tam2010 Theorem1.1 supplies a checked proof of Horn’s finite converse.

For rank-one A_i=x_i′⊗y_i, the mixed trace form is det(x_i′(y_j))/n!. Multilinearity and SVD extend the formula and give the mixed S1 bound (1/n!)∏||A_i||1. Expanding Λ^n(A+B) cancels the binomial coefficient against the mixed factorials. Summing produces determinant subadditivity; truncating and aligning singular vectors produces the variational formulas. On complex operators a supremum of bare complex determinants is not ordered: absolute values or an explicit real part are necessary. The equality |det(I+A)|=det(I+|A|) is false for A=−1; the inequality is correct and occurs later in the archive.

The tentative optimality claim040 is not proved by this calculation. Fulton2000 Theorem15 characterizes complete complex singular-value sum data by all Horn index-set constraints. It is a different claim from whether one determinant inequality is attained. We do not declare the manuscript’s undefined optimality question solved merely because a general Horn theorem exists.

For 0<p<1, integration by parts gives

The derivative is t/(1+rt), not r/(1+rt). The boundary terms vanish at0 and infinity. Tonelli permits sums, including extended values. This yields the **separate-sums** inequality Σs_j(A+B)^p≤Σs_j(A)^p+Σs_j(B)^p. The stronger aligned inequality is false: A=diag(1,0), B=diag(0,1) gives2>2^p.

## 5. Symmetric spaces and duality (051–057)

Given a finite symmetric gauge N, define the maximal sequence norm by the supremum of norms of finite truncations, and the minimal space by completion of finite sequences. Finite truncations show N° is the polar gauge and (N°)°=N. If φ is a functional on the minimal space, its coordinate values form a maximal N° sequence because every finite test has bounded pairing; conversely that sequence defines a bounded functional. Thus (l_N)*=l^(N°). In a reflexive minimal space, its image is norm closed and weakly dense in the proposed dual-dual realization, hence equal. Both minimal=maximal conditions are needed for the two-sided reflexivity criterion. The dual of a maximal space can include additional singular functionals.

For operators, SVD transfers the finite tests and finite-dimensional compressions give the trace dual. An infinite-rank spectral projection for |A| bounded below would force the constant sequence into the relevant sequence space, explaining the compactness assertion outside the operator-norm endpoint. Maximal and minimal ideals remain different; an arbitrary symmetric space need not have minimal=maximal∩c0. Antezana–Larotonda–Varela §§2,5.1 confirms the modern framework, while these full duality arguments are reconstructed independently.

Norm order reverses inclusion: sup≤l1, but (1/n) belongs to c0 and not l1, refuting052’s first direction. The polar of l1 is sup, so dropping polar signs in054 cannot be harmless. ## 6. Entire functions and a Banach-holomorphic example (058–065)

The scalar Weierstrass/Hadamard framework is classical. Wiegerinck §5.4–5.6 was opened for exact Jensen, canonical-product and factorization statements. The product with zeros n^α, α>1, has exponent of convergence and order1/α, not1. At α=2 it is sin(πsqrt(z))/(πsqrt(z)), making the order1/2 explicit. The later archival argument already gives the reciprocal order.

For a summable nonnegative sequence a_j, P(z)=∏(1+a_j z) has minimal exponential type: split a finite head, which grows polynomially, and a tail with sum less than ε, whose log modulus is at most ε|z|. If |f(z)| is bounded by such a positive-coefficient radial majorant and its zeros have summable reciprocals, Hadamard gives f=e^(a+bz)Q with genus-zero Q. To see b=0 without assuming an invalid global lower bound for Q, note log^+|Q(re^{it})|=o(r) uniformly, while Jensen gives nonnegative circle mean of log|Q| after Q(0)=1. Consequently the integral of log^-|Q| is o(r). On a fixed arc where Re(bre^{it})≥c r, log|Q| cannot be≤−c r/2 everywhere for all large r. Thus b≠0 would contradict minimal exponential type of f. This proves060 with the corrected hypotheses; it also explains why arbitrary rotation and sector choices in the draft are unsafe.

For decreasing a_n→0 let ρ be the critical exponent of Σa_n^q. Monotonicity gives n a_n^q→0 when that series converges. Conversely a_n≤n^(−t) eventually yields convergence for qt>1. Hence the reciprocal logarithmic threshold uses **liminf** log(1/a_n)/log n. To refute the displayed limsup take N_k=2^(2^k) and a_n=1/N_k for N_(k−1)<n≤N_k. A block contributes approximately N_k^(1−q), so ρ=1. The logarithmic ratio tends to1 at block ends and2 at block beginnings. These analytic limits accompany the finite illustrative table in the code.

Jensen’s identity uses log|f| and f(0)≠0; its normalization term cannot disappear for an arbitrary small constant function. A principal complex logarithm across zeros has jumps, so taking real parts from the beginning is the valid counting-function calculation. Minimal exponential type and Cauchy give |a_n|≤M(R)/R^n. Taking R=n/ε, then Stirling and ε→0, proves (n!|a_n|)^(1/n)→0. The R^(n+1) display is false already for f=1+z,n=1,R=10.

The function in065 is S(λ)=Σ_(n≥0)n!e_n(λ) on l1. It is **entire in the modern Fréchet-holomorphic sense**. Near a fixed λ split a finite head h with m coordinates from a tail t with ||t||1≤q<1, uniformly on a small neighborhood. Since |e_k(t)|≤q^k/k!, the n-th summand is bounded by a finite sum of terms |e_j(h)| n!q^(n−j)/(n−j)!, j≤m. This is a polynomial in n times q^(n−m), locally uniformly summable. Every summand is a continuous homogeneous polynomial, proving local holomorphy everywhere. On the unit ball, ||n!e_n||=1: the upper bound is elementary, and m equal coordinates1/m approach it for fixed n. Thus the uniform Taylor radius at0 is1. On those same vectors the sum of its first N terms tends to N+1 as m→∞, proving unboundedness on the unit ball. ## 7. Mixed determinants and analytic Hölder (066–075)

The SVD expansion of each mixed operator yields a determinant of pairings. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its trace by the geometric mean of the two Gram-determinant sums, one on the domain and one on the range. This is066. The one-sided replacement067 is false. Take

\[
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\3/5&4/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The mixed determinant coefficient is2/5. The domain-side coefficient for |A1|,|A2| is8/25; the range-side coefficient is1/2. The valid geometric mean is sqrt((8/25)(1/2))=2/5, with equality. Exact rational arithmetic in the code verifies all coefficients.

For positive h,k, det(I+h+k)=det(I+h)det(I+(I+h)^(−1/2)k(I+h)^(−1/2)), and contraction/monotonicity bounds the second factor by det(I+k). Together with the valid mixed estimate and polar contraction this yields the arbitrary-operator determinant result. A unitary polar factor is unavailable in general: for A e_n=e_(n+1)/n, |A| has zero-dimensional kernel but |A*| has one-dimensional kernel. This compact example refutes070 and the unnecessary unitary step in068. It does not refute nonzero singular-value equality.

Finite-rank approximation and p-subadditivity prove completeness of S_p; its diagonal l_p copy proves nonlocal convexity for p<1 in infinite dimension. The integral in §4 gives071. In072 the analytic constant pπ/sin(πp) tends to1 after replacing A by A^n and p by p/n. The trace-algebra extension is established by Grothendieck113 and Fack–Kosaki under specified normal faithful semifinite/measurable hypotheses.

For074 apply Λ^n to A,B. Hölder at exponent2m gives ||Λ^n(AB)||_(2m)≤||Λ^n A||_(2m)||Λ^n B||_(2m); raising to2m gives e_n(L^m)≤e_n(H^m)e_n(K^m). ## 8. Convergence and fractional powers (076–081)

For selfadjoint compact operators, finite nonzero spectral clusters and their projections identify finite-dimensional blocks. Convergence on those blocks and convergence of total p-sums force the remaining p-tails to be small; this yields076. Zero is not necessarily isolated. Eigenvalues alone cannot provide an analogous nonnormal operator convergence test, because nilpotent matrices have no nonzero eigenvalues.

Dodds–Dodds–Dowling–Lennard–Sukochev, Theorem2.7/Corollary2.8 and the paragraph following it, explicitly proves the weak-operator Kadec–Klee property for every Schatten exponent0<p<∞. Therefore WOT convergence plus convergence of p-quasi-norms gives S_p convergence in078 even below1. This published theorem was matched in exact range; its full long proof was not independently re-proved here. Uniform convexity is a different question: it holds for1<p<∞ by the checked Clarkson framework; S1 has distinct orthogonal positive rank-one projections whose midpoint still has norm1, so is not strictly convex. The infinite-dimensional p<1 spaces are not locally convex.

Ricard2018 Theorem3.2 proves fractional absolute/signed-power Hölder continuity for all positive exponents with a constant. The selfadjoint block matrix [[0,A],[A*,0]] transfers the theorem to |A|. To get any power r>0 choose an integer m>r, apply the theorem to θ=r/m<1, and use telescoping of the m-th powers with Schatten Hölder. Bounded positive operator-norm continuity separately follows by uniform polynomial approximation.

For079 put δ=||H−K||. Operator monotonicity of x^r,0<r<1, gives H^r≤(K+δI)^r≤K^r+δ^rI; interchange H,K. Thus ||H^r−K^r||≤δ^r, with constant1. This only controls operator norm. The published positive-power constant-one Schatten inequality has input exponent q≥θ. With X=H^r,Y=K^r,θ=s/r,q=p/r, it gives

\[
F_p(s)\le F_p(r),\quad F_p(t)=\operatorname{Tr}|H^t-K^t|^{p/t},
\qquad 0<s<r,\quad p\ge s.
\]

In particular the whole interval0<r≤1 is covered if p≥1. Ricard’s all-p theorem with a constant cannot remove this restriction.

## 9. Individual eigenvalues and normal Fredholm minors (082–085)

A weighted cyclic N×N matrix with weights ε^(−N+1),ε,…,ε has characteristic polynomial z^N−1, hence all eigenvalue moduli1, while its singular values are the absolute weights. For any fixed fractional-index rule floor(n/c), choose n=N large enough that this index is at least2; then the proposed bound by that singular value fails asε→0. This settles the concrete universal index-shift hope, while Weyl’s geometric-mean bound remains valid.

One trace-class compact operator has unbounded same-index ratios: take the direct sum of blocks [[0,a_k],[b_k,0]], with a1=1/2, R_k=k+1, b_k=a_k/R_k², a_(k+1)=b_k/2. Its singular values in descending order are a1,b1,a2,b2,…; its eigenvalue moduli are a_k/R_k, each twice, with block order also descending. The series of singular values converges geometrically and the block norms tend to0. At index2k, |λ_(2k)|/s_(2k)=R_k→∞. Thus the asserted limsup phenomenon is proved with every compactness/summability hypothesis checked.

For normal trace-class u, simultaneous diagonalization of u and |u| gives the nth adjugate coefficient at eigenvector i as e_n of the eigenvalues excluding λ_i. Therefore |P_n(u)|≤P_n(|u|)≤e_n(|u|)I. The algebraic formula has alternating signs:

\[
P_n(u)=\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k e_{n-k}(u)u^k.
\]

Summing gives the entire Fredholm adjugate and ||R(u)||≤det(I+|u|). In infinite dimension the omitted-eigenvalue products approach the full positive product along eigenvalues tending to0, giving equality in the positive case. This lower norm statement is not valid without qualification for finite matrices. For u=diag(2,1),n=2 the first claimed decreasing positive summands are2I and3u, already in the wrong order; the correct alternating coefficient is2I−3u+u²=0. The valid exclusion formula needs none of the false descending-term assertion085.
