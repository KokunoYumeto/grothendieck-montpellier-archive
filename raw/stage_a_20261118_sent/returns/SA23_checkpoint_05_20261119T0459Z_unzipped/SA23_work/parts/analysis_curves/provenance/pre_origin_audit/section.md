# Cote 1: tensor norms, trace ideals and analytic spectral theory

Cote1 contains a sustained functional-analytic working sequence, beginning with isolated fragments on C*-algebra multipliers, rearrangements and traces, and continuing through Hilbert tensor products, Hilbert–Schmidt and trace-class operators, exterior powers, determinant inequalities, symmetric gauges, entire functions and Schatten convergence. The historical term *opérateur de Fredholm* on these leaves means the nuclear or trace-class object under discussion; reading it as the modern Fredholm property would change the subject. Cote21 is a separate line of work, treated below, on detecting families, functor representation and extension across a closed set. The ten supplied transcriptions were read in full, including deletions, uncertain symbols, marginal instructions and transitions across folders. The short Codex notes are leads rather than indexed Stage1 entries. [SA23-401–420; exact manuscript references in the claim map.]

The functional-analysis leaves are undated. Their close correspondence with publications therefore establishes mathematical connections, not an automatic chronology of composition. Three different dates belong to Grothendieck’s rearrangement exposé: March1955 on its heading,1956 in the Numdam volume record, and a June1957 terminal annotation. They should remain separate. *La théorie de Fredholm*, published in1956, says in its introduction that the text was written in1952 and accepted by Summa in1953 before being withdrawn because of delay. It calls itself a didactic exposition and credits Ruston and Schatten. These explicit statements are stronger evidence than a proposed manuscript date inferred from mathematical content. [AC_GrothRearrangements1955, §§2–6; AC_GrothFredholm1956, pp.319–320.]

## The main construction and its published fate

The working sequence uses Hilbert exterior powers to turn products of singular values into operator norms. Its Gram determinant normalization, mixed exterior products, and fundamental trace forms feed the determinant expansion and convexity inequalities. This explains why a seemingly small normalization choice at scan64 propagates through several later arguments. The supplied inclusion and quotient factors are reversed for the averaged antisymmetrizer: the isometric inclusion has multiplier sqrt(n!), and the normalized quotient has multiplier1/sqrt(n!). Their product cancels in the mixed operator estimate, so the final bound survives. Exact checks for n=2,3,4 and the rank-one determinant coefficient1/n! are saved; the general proof uses the orthogonal alternating basis. Grothendieck’s1956 paper, I§§1–2, gives the matching published exterior/fundamental-form construction. [C031–035; MP08; AC_GrothFredholm1956, pp.325–330.]

The scalar and mixed calculations lead to determinant domination and subadditivity, and then to the Schatten p-subadditivity inequality for0<p<1 through a logarithmic beta integral. They also give product log-majorization and Hölder for all positive exponents satisfying the reciprocal relation. These are not unsettled conclusions today: Grothendieck’s Bourbaki113 contains direct counterparts, and Fack–Kosaki supplies the faithful normal semifinite measurable-operator framework and further trace inequalities. Their precise hypotheses matter. An unspecified trace on an arbitrary C*-algebra is not enough to import every measurable-operator statement. Likewise, a polar partial isometry cannot always be replaced by a unitary. A compact weighted unilateral shift has unequal kernel dimensions for |A| and |A*|, although their nonzero singular-value data agree. [C020–025, C030, C038, C041, C048, C068–075; AC_GrothRearrangements1955, §§4–6; AC_FackKosaki1986, §2 and Theorems4.2,4.7,4.9.]

The symmetric-gauge pages distinguish the completion of finite sequences or finite-rank operators from the maximal space determined by bounded finite truncations. Their polar-duality argument is sound when these distinctions and the polar signs are retained. Several short summaries lose the polar or reverse an inclusion that the longer proof states correctly. The elementary test sup≤l1, with(1/n) in c0 but not l1, identifies the inclusion direction. Trace duality reverses source and target and replaces the gauge by its polar. The claimed three-gauge multiplication summary, with the same gauge accidentally imposed on both factors, fails for I2. The modern minimal/maximal and polar framework is checked against Antezana–Larotonda–Varela; the complete finite-truncation and compression arguments are separately reconstructed. [C051–057; MP14; AC_Antezana2011, §§2,5.1.]

## Questions that have definite answers

The most consequential literal failure is the proposed monotonicity of

\[
F_p(r)=\operatorname{Tr}|H^r-K^r|^{p/r}
\]

for arbitrary p>0. It is stronger than the operator-norm Hölder bound on the adjacent lines. For H=[[2,1],[1,5]], K=diag(1,4), and p=1/10, both matrices are strictly positive and

\[
F_p(1/2)=1.3201298807\ldots>1.0717734625\ldots=F_p(1).
\]

The conclusion does not rest on decimals. The program certifies rational lower bounds for the two eigenvalues of sqrt(H)−sqrt(K), proving F_p(1/2)>1.29>1.1>F_p(1). Squaring H,K and halving p gives a failure with both power parameters strictly below1. The surviving published constant-one inequality gives F_p(s)≤F_p(r) when0<s<r and p≥s; thus it covers the interval0<r≤1 if p≥1. Ricard’s theorem gives all-p Hölder continuity with a constant, and cannot be used to erase that restriction. [C077–080; MP20; computation `fractional_power_monotonicity_counterexample`; AC_Ricard2018, introduction and Theorem3.2.]

The weak-convergence question has a broader positive answer than the uniform-convexity route suggested on the leaves. Dodds and coauthors prove weak-operator Kadec–Klee for every Schatten exponent0<p<∞: weak-operator convergence together with convergence of the p-quasi-norm implies convergence in S_p. Uniform convexity is a different assertion, valid for1<p<∞. S1 has flat unit-sphere segments, and the infinite-dimensional p<1 spaces are not locally convex. These separate answers prevent the open margin from being left artificially unresolved. [C076–078; MP19; AC_Dodds1995, Theorem2.7, Corollary2.8 and following paragraph; AC_FackKosaki1986, Theorems5.2–5.3.]

The request for a pointwise eigenvalue bound by a fixed fraction of the singular-value index also has a negative answer. Weighted cyclic matrices can have all eigenvalue moduli1 while every singular value except the first is arbitrarily small. The valid Weyl bound is the geometric mean of the first n singular values. A separately constructed compact trace-class sum of2×2 blocks has |λ_(2k)|/s_(2k)=k+1, proving the asserted unbounded same-index ratios in one operator. Tam’s checked proof of the Weyl–Horn theorem explains the finite feasible data; the explicit constructions answer the actual margin. [C034, C082–083; MP21; AC_Tam2010, Theorem1.1.]

Several nearby proposed strengthenings fail in equally small examples. Orthogonal rank-one projections refute the aligned concave-power sum, while the separate-sums version remains valid. Symmetry cannot be deleted from the finite log-convex comparisons, even where equality of total products permits dropping monotonicity. A pair of rational rank-one2×2 maps refutes the one-sided mixed determinant estimate; the correct geometric-mean estimate includes both domain and range Gram factors and is attained by that example. Finally, the normal Fredholm adjugate has alternating signs. For diag(2,1), the proposed descending positive summands already occur in the wrong order, while the correct exclusion-of-one-eigenvalue formula proves the desired positivity and domination. [C042, C045, C047, C049, C066–067, C084–085; MP12–13, MP17, MP22.]

## Entire functions and spectral continuity

The entire-function pages supply an analytic route to trace and spectral statements, but several transcription alarms change their literal formulas. A product with zeros n^α has order1/α, not1; a later leaf gives the reciprocal result. The decreasing-sequence summability exponent uses a liminf of logarithmic decay rates, and an explicit monotone plateau sequence disproves the printed limsup. Cauchy’s coefficient estimate has denominator R^n, not R^(n+1). The genus-zero domination lemma survives after a careful minimal-exponential-type argument. These are repairs within classical Jensen/Hadamard theory, whose exact statements were checked in Wiegerinck’s author text; they are not newly discovered scalar results. [C058–064; MP15; AC_Wiegerinck2009, §§5.4–5.6.]

The function S(λ)=Σn!e_n(λ) on l1 illustrates a more substantive terminological issue. It is entire in the modern Fréchet-holomorphic sense, although unbounded on the unit ball and of uniform Taylor radius1 at0. A finite-head/small-tail estimate proves local uniform convergence near every point. Vectors with m coordinates equal to1/m prove the unboundedness. Thus the source’s phrase denying entière cannot be accepted as a denial of modern holomorphy; a later margin itself calls the function holomorphic. Modern entire and bounded-type theories distinguish exactly these notions. The current search did not locate an exact published occurrence of this particular example, so no novelty or priority claim is made. [C065; MP16; AC_ZagorodnyukHihliuk2021, §1.]

For eigenvalue-list continuity, the archive’s whole-list topology is not defined on the surviving leaves. A precise p-summable matching interpretation is therefore identified as our reconstruction. The argument needs finite nonzero Riesz clusters and uniform p-tails. The unweighted counting integral printed as finite actually diverges for a single nonzero eigenvalue; its weighted cutoff limit is the valid replacement. A convex function of logarithmic singular values gives a direct uniform-tail proof. Reinov’s2012 discussion points to Grothendieck’s1955 Memoirs16, II p.21 Remark4, for an own-publication assertion with the same Σ(p) notation for p≤1. The primary passage and its matching-space definition were then visually checked directly: II pp.20–21, physical221–222 in the inspected copy. Front matter identifies copyright1955 and fourth printing1966, a distinction retained in the bibliography. A stated published continuity theorem, an independently checked proof, and the manuscript’s incomplete proof are three different evidence layers. [C033, C037, C060, C064, C081; MP09–10; AC_GrothFredholm1956, pp.361–364; AC_Reinov2012, pp.5–10; AC_GrothPTT1955, II pp.13,20–21.]

# Cote 21: detecting families, torsion representation and depth

The first part of cote21 develops a weak detecting family in a noetherian abelian category, then constructs tensor and Hom objects valued in an abelian category. Its finite-filtration argument is valid, but its use of générateur is weaker than the usual faithful-Hom generator. Over k[ε]/(ε²), the residue field detects nonzero finite modules without generating A by quotients of sums. Finite module-functor representation needs only finite presentations; the all-module version needs coproduct preservation, and its contravariant version needs products. These existence and continuity assumptions are the mathematical content of several editorial requests, rather than optional explanatory details. [C001–006; MP01–02.]

The co-I-adic systems, first nonzero cohomological functor, and torsion injectivity arguments have exceptionally close published matches in SGA2 IV1.1,1.3,1.4,2.1 and2.2. The Artin–Rees intersection must involve the ambient M. The printed target of the published representation theorem is abelian groups; the draft’s arbitrary abelian-target versions are identified as extensions requiring the exact-colimit assumptions, not silently attributed to the printed proposition. [C007–010; AC_SGA2, reprint pp.34–37, original pp.44–48.]

The global continuation needs a further separation. Supported quasi-coherent sheaves form the ind-category of supported coherent sheaves on a noetherian scheme. The formula recovering the representing sheaf from the global groups T(O/I^n), however, is false. On P1 with I=0 and H=O(−1), T(F)=Hom(F,H) has T(O)=0 but T(H)=k. The sheaf is nonzero even though the proposed colimit vanishes. Local affine evaluation or the full ind-system recovers it. [C011–012; MP03; AC_StacksCoh, Lemma30.10.4, plus the independent category-of-elements proof.]

The later pages define uniqueness, extension and categorical equivalence across a closed subset, then compare them pointwise. The set-valued proof works through finite localization and noetherian induction with sheaf gluing. The categorical P3 proof adds a genuine unstated requirement: effective descent. On the three-point spectrum with one generic and two closed points, assign one object to every proper open and none to the whole space. This is a prestack whose local punctured restrictions are equivalences, but whose global restriction is not essentially surjective. The proposed gluing is exactly what fails. Stacks, including torsors, have the missing axiom. [C013–019; MP04–05; AC_StacksStack, Definition8.4.1.]

SGA2 XIV gives the published depth and torsor counterpart, with retrocompact complements and strict étale localizations. It must not be described as an exact unqualified Zariski statement. Its printed author is **Mme M. Raynaud**; the footnote says it is based on unpublished notes of A. Grothendieck. Preserving both facts is necessary to describe the connection accurately. The additional margin about surjectivity alone remains a formulation question: local lifting of a sheaf section and surjectivity on every open are different properties. [AC_RaynaudSGA2XIV, Proposition1.1, Corollaries1.3–1.4, Remark1.6(a), Théorème1.8.]
