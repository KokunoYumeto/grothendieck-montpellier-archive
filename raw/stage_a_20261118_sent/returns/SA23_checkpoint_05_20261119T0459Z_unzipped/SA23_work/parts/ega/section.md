## 1. Differential criteria and formal simplicity

The beginning of cote22 moves from differential criteria for regular local rings to completion, separability and analytic reducedness. Jouanolou’s differential module is the universal Kähler module, but his quasi-separable terminology and residual hypotheses must be read in their historical formulation. Several annotations have real mathematical content: the residue-field inclusion has a direction, a quotient differential module must actually be a module over the quotient ring, and a finite algebra need not contain its base ring injectively. The annotation “faux” near a localization argument cannot be reassigned to a different sentence merely because that produces an easier objection. Claims001–013 retain these distinctions; MP-SA23-ega-02 and MP-SA23-ega-04 are the actual marginal questions, while ED-SA23-ega-01 and ED-SA23-ega-03 preserve the separate reader checks.

The regular group-scheme application raises a scope question. Modern finite-type smooth group schemes over a perfect field have the expected regularity properties, but this does not certify every unrestricted Hopf-algebra formulation suggested by a margin. Similarly, the source’s credit to Nagata is evidence of acknowledged prior knowledge, while the exact general normalization application has not been reconstructed from an original Nagata proof. Jouanolou’s alleged gap in Lang’s C1 argument is a specific historical verification question: it concerns separability of the fraction field of a completion. The searches identify Lang’s1952 paper and a later Nagata improvement concerning normic forms, but do not identify that improvement with the particular analytic-completion gap. These are bounded limitations in the claim map, not declarations of modern open C1 problems. [Lang1952; Nagata1962; Stacks0BF6]

The subsequent formal-simplicity and Cohen-algebra draft is organized around lifting, associated graded modules, completeness and residue-field extensions. Its literal comparison theorem4.6(ii) repeats hypotheses on the source algebra where the comparison evidently needs information on the target. The example
\[
A=B=\mathbf Q[e]/(e^2),\qquad C=\mathbf Q
\]
meets the literal source conditions and gives an isomorphism modulo the nilpotent ideal, but its quotient map is not an isomorphism. This is a mathematical reading alarm; replacing every B by C would still require an ink check and a complete theorem-level hypothesis audit. The modern local Cohen-factorization theorems are more precise than the draft’s broad nonlocal questions. [C-SA23-ega-017–020; Stacks07NN; Stacks09Q9]

The most productive distinction in these pages is **adic formal smoothness versus discrete formal smoothness**. The power-series examples are not minor changes of notation. In characteristic zero, the ordinary differential module of a positive-dimensional power-series ring has a finite-dimensional residue quotient but a larger generic rank, detected by the transcendence of the formal exponential. It cannot be projective. When the coefficient field has infinitely many independent differentials, the manuscript’s unfinished finite-support argument can also be completed: a derivation into a direct sum cannot accommodate the infinitely many coefficient directions of one power series. The proof notes make both arguments explicit and distinguish the manuscript’s nonsplitting proposition from the earlier run’s stronger nonprojectivity idea. Finite truncations alone would not prove either result. [SA23-140–142; C-SA23-ega-021–023; MP-SA23-ega-06, MP-SA23-ega-09; Stacks00TH; Stacks0593]

The questions about the openness of geometric regularity and flatness require an equally careful scope distinction. The finite-presentation flat-locus theorem does not settle an arbitrary noetherian ring map. The Frobenius criterion connects a bad regular locus to a bad flat locus, and the literature search traces the example route through Brodmann–Rotthaus, Nishimura and the explicit modern MathOverflow application. Access to the original Nishimura proof was blocked and is recorded as such. No finite-presentation hypothesis is quietly removed. [C-SA23-ega-024–025; Stacks00R8; Stacks0EC0; BrodmannRotthaus1982; Nishimura2012; MO500040]

## 2. Differential kernels, adjunction and infinitesimal groups

The characteristic-two kernel examples in cote22 are stronger than the assertion that an inseparable extension kills a differential. They show that the kernel of the map on absolute differentials need not descend to the smaller field even when $k\cap K^p=k^p$. The rational-function and finite-coefficient variants have been checked with all their field hypotheses. A separate original-print comparison found a real defect in EGAIV,0.22.5.10(i): the displayed larger field does not adjoin the independent variable Z. Read literally, its stated generator is transcendental and cannot produce the advertised differential relation. Adjoining Z repairs the example; that repair is present reconstruction, not a change to the manuscript or printed source. Conversely, several map-label and Hom-variance errors alleged by the earlier run are absent from the original EGA print. The dossier therefore rejects a blanket claim that the comparison edition’s errors were EGA’s errors. [SA23-143–145; C-SA23-ega-026; ED-SA23-ega-10; EGAIV1]

The projective-bundle leaves in cote23 compute canonical bundles by Euler and conormal sequences. Their relative base-line factors matter: a degree-sum condition can make the canonical line a pullback from the base without making it globally trivial. The elliptic calculation gives equation weight6 and is independent of the discriminant mistake on the next leaf. For degree d forms in r+1 variables, the discriminant has coefficient degree $(r+1)(d-1)^r$. The literal linear exponent happens to agree for binary forms and quadrics, so a plane cubic is a better diagnostic than those easy cases. The source and coordinate-change characters have been compared directly with Busé–Jouanolou’s propositions. [C-SA23-ega-028–030; ED-SA23-ega-12; Stacks06BB; Stacks072T; BuseJouanolou2012]

The logarithm/exponential discussion contains two distinct literal failures. For commuting elements near1, the logarithm of a product is the **sum** of logarithms. In a Hopf algebra, the exponential of a nilpotent primitive is **group-like**; subtracting1 does not generally produce another primitive. The saved certificate over $\mathbf Q[t]/(t^3)$ shows the nonzero reduced coproduct directly. These corrections should not be collapsed into one vague “sign problem.” [C-SA23-ega-032–033; ED-SA23-ega-14]

The shortened Cartier checklist also omits an essential connectedness condition. The finite Hopf algebra $\mathbf R^{C_3}$ has no nonzero primitive and no nontrivial real group-like element, but is not the enveloping algebra of zero. Cartier’s actual HA3 condition uses an exhaustive connected filtration and excludes this example. His next exposé proves injectivity of the enveloping map; a general surjectivity assertion needs the full structure theorem. The precise characteristic-zero connected theorem is identified in Loday, with its2006 first circulation and2008 publication distinguished. [C-SA23-ega-034; ED-SA23-ega-13; Cartier1956; CartierExamples1956; Loday2008]

The ensuing bitorsor, principal-parts and infinitesimal-action constructions require a genuinely nilpotent augmentation. Finite generation and a locally free associated graded do not imply nilpotence: the augmentation of $k\times k$ has a nonzero idempotent kernel. The natural finite-order bundle question has an exact smooth-manifold analogue in Kolár–Michor–Slovák, but that theorem is not a proof of an arbitrary-base scheme-theoretic assertion. These categorical boundaries remain explicit. [C-SA23-ega-037–045; ED-SA23-ega-15, ED-SA23-ega-16, MP-SA23-ega-17, MP-SA23-ega-18, MP-SA23-ega-19; Stacks04EW; StacksWeilRestriction; KolarMichorSlovak1993]

## 3. Deligne’s corrections and their published fate

The Deligne letters form a sustained examination of definitions and proofs, especially outside the noetherian setting. A successive regular sequence need not be permutable in a nonnoetherian local ring. The idealization example in the proof notes checks this directly. Koszul acyclicity is permutation-invariant and is the right condition for the later geometric definition. SGA6VII explicitly replaces the earlier EGA formulation and records positive-degree acyclicity; a transcription saying that the complex is zero in degree0 is consequently a decisive alarm. [C-SA23-ega-049; MP-SA23-ega-23; SGA6VII]

Several apparent refutations become more informative when compared with the final publication. Deligne’s disjoint-union map is not an immersion, while the published EGAIV16.1.5–6 assumes an immersion. It does not refute that final statement. His separate question about kernels of finitely presented algebras does have a negative answer: an idealization followed by localization gives a finitely presented algebra with an infinitely generated kernel from the base. Likewise, arbitrary A-subquotients of a B-module need not be adically separated, even when the finite B-module version has the usual noetherian separation theorem. The letters’ changes of position and their December resolution are retained. [C-SA23-ega-050–051; MP-SA23-ega-24; EGAIV4]

The omission of separation in the implication b⇒c of EGAIV19.5.5 is an exact success story. The final theorem gives a⇒b⇒c without separation and reserves the stronger separation hypothesis for the reverse implication. The footnote on printedp207 explicitly credits Deligne and says that his proof is reproduced. This is a dated manuscript-to-publication connection, not an unresolved theorem left behind by the correspondence. [C-SA23-ega-052; ED-SA23-ega-25-context; EGAIV4]

The most decisive editorial match concerns **EGA0.19.3.12**. Deligne objects that solution sets of multiplicative lifting equations are not linear varieties. The original printed proof makes exactly that mistake. EGAIV4’s1967 errata then expressly **withdraw the proposition** for this reason. The present polynomial-system example demonstrates the failure of the compactness inference; it does not pretend to be a counterexample satisfying every hypothesis of the withdrawn proposition. The published withdrawal is the established historical fate. [C-SA23-ega-053; ED-SA23-ega-26; EGAIV1; EGAIV4, Liste3 IVno.8]

The relative pro-Tor comparison is reconstructed at the level actually required by the letter. After a faithfully flat completion and a flat noetherian cover, one works with a complex of finite modules. Artin–Rees makes pro-completion exact and identifies completed homology with pro-homology. This proves an isomorphism of pro-systems, which is stronger than equality of their inverse limits. The proof also keeps the relative depth convention: depth is measured with respect to the maximal ideal of A, not silently replaced by depth over B. [C-SA23-ega-054–060; ED-SA23-ega-27, ED-SA23-ega-28, MP-SA23-ega-29, ED-SA23-ega-30; Stacks0911; Stacks09Q9; StacksAuslanderBuchsbaum]

The remaining numbered errata have been checked against both original print and the later correction list. The noetherian hypothesis in0.16.1.9 is removed, locality is inserted in0.19.8.3–4, and integral closure is removed in0.20.4.13(iv). The sign correction has its own printed old/new-label transposition, which is recorded instead of concealed. Finally, Deligne’s complaint about EGAIII4.3.6 is valid: in dimension2, $\operatorname{Frac}(A)\otimes_A\widehat A$ need not be the fraction field of $\widehat A$. A formal prime with zero contraction gives a concrete example. The conclusion “analytically integral implies unibranch” survives, because the finite-subalgebra proof only needs the natural injection into that fraction field. The dossier separates the false displayed equality, the repaired proof and the true conclusion. [C-SA23-ega-061; ED-SA23-ega-31-context; EGAIII; EGAIV1; EGAIV4]


## A distinct deferred hard Lefschetz proof

In his letter of17 October1966, Deligne says he will not attempt the “théorème de Lefschetz vache” that year and hopes Grothendieck can find someone to prove it. The line gives no coefficients or base hypotheses. SGA7XVIII5.2.2, printedpp281–282, uses exactly that name for the cup-product isomorphisms and still conjectures the smooth case; the exposé is by N.Katz, based on Grothendieck’s notes. Deligne’s *Weil II*, Theorem4.1.1, printedpp217–218, proves the smooth projective ample-class statement over an algebraically closed field with rational l-adic coefficients, l different from the characteristic. This identifies a precise standard interpretation and its later resolution without pretending that every hypothesis is written in the1966 sentence. It is distinct from the parafactorial margins. No new computation is claimed: the source supplies no proof or calculation to redo. [SA23-1321; C-SA23-ega-098; MP-SA23-ega-60; EGA_KatzSGA7XVIII1973; EGA_DeligneWeilII1980.]

## 4. Descent, dimension, normalization and graded algebra

The reused étale material is now identified at exact published locations. SGA4VIII9.4 supplies the four stated surjective descent cases, and VIII7.9.1–3 supplies the geometric-point and slice-topos material also reused in cote26. The constructible direct-image fragment corresponds to Artin’s IX2.14(i), with finite and finite-presentation hypotheses. The cancelled derived comparison corollary corresponds to his XII6.3, whose final redaction has explicit nonnegative-cohomology and negative-vanishing assumptions. The handwritten R1 sketches remain separately unidentified. [C-SA23-ega-066–070,084–085,097; ED-SA23-ega-34, ED-SA23-ega-35, MP-SA23-ega-36, ED-SA23-ega-37; SGA4VIII; SGA4IX; SGA4XII]

The extension from finite étale covers to all étale objects is not automatic. Graphs and internal Hom give clopen tests in the finite separated setting; arbitrary étale objects can have nonclosed equalizers. The manuscript itself records a submersiveness caveat. Modern effective-descent results are used with their precise universally subtrusive or finite-presentation hypotheses. [C-SA23-ega-067–069; RydhDescent]

The dimension and normalization leaves contain small quantifier errors with large consequences. In $k[x,y,z]/(xy)$, killing x leaves a two-dimensional component; it is insufficient that merely one component avoid the hypersurface. Elements already in a prime ideal cannot form a nonempty system of parameters in the quotient by that ideal. The support of a normalization quotient can have codimension1, as the cusp shows; a codimension-two extension argument needs prior agreement in codimension1. The parafactorial claim has a precise later theorem in SGA2XI3.13 for local complete intersections of dimension at least4. The broader marginal Lefschetz arrows require their own hypotheses and are not all certified by that theorem. [C-SA23-ega-071–076; ED-SA23-ega-38, MP-SA23-ega-39, ED-SA23-ega-40; StacksDimension; Stacks033P; SGA2]

The proposed non-Cohen–Macaulay construction has an identifiable Tor mechanism, but its initial geometric choice is not justified by “normal and non-Cohen–Macaulay” alone. The bad locus must be controlled before one can cut the cone in the intended way. The graded-algebra leaves similarly require the missing augmented-composition condition. Under that condition the generation and section-independence arguments work by degree induction. Without it there are simple polynomial counterexamples. Equality of Hilbert series needs the preceding surjectivity assumption, and the first of the three proposed flatness implications is false even for $A=B=k[t]$ and $C=k$. The other two have the expected tensor-product proofs. [C-SA23-ega-077–082; ED-SA23-ega-41, MP-SA23-ega-42, ED-SA23-ega-43, MP-SA23-ega-44]

## 5. The representability program in cote26

The cote26 plan distributes future work among tangent and normal constructions, representable functors, Hilbert and Picard objects, moduli, quotient problems and abelian schemes. The last leaf is an actual problem list and has been indexed question by question. Later algebraic-space and stack theorems answer many suitably formulated parts, but not a single unrestricted “representability theorem.” Hilbert/Picard assertions retain their properness, flatness, finite-presentation and universal base-change assumptions. A group action “without fixed points” is not silently interpreted as a free action. Classification of bundles on relative projective space is not reduced to the splitting theorem on the Riemann sphere. [C-SA23-ega-083,086–096; MP-SA23-ega-45, MP-SA23-ega-46, MP-SA23-ega-47, MP-SA23-ega-48, MP-SA23-ega-49, MP-SA23-ega-50, MP-SA23-ega-51, MP-SA23-ega-52, MP-SA23-ega-53, MP-SA23-ega-54, MP-SA23-ega-55, MP-SA23-ega-56, MP-SA23-ega-57, MP-SA23-ega-58, MP-SA23-ega-59; FGA; Artin1969; Stacks0D01; Stacks0D2C; Stacks06PH]

The dual of a nonprojective abelian scheme is already annotated as answered by Raynaud. Later primary literature confirms existence without projectivity, through algebraic spaces, while the exact original proof was not rechecked. Oort’s Theorem2.2.1 gives the formally smooth unpolarized deformation base of dimensiong² and attributes the result to Grothendieck; its1970 lectures and1971 publication are distinguished. Katz’s ordinary Serre–Tate theorem gives additional formal-torus structure in a restricted case. Heinloth–Schmitt gives a smooth algebraic stack of bundles on a smooth projective curve for a reductive group over a locally noetherian base; it does not classify bundles on every X/Y for every affine group. [MP-SA23-ega-50, MP-SA23-ega-54, MP-SA23-ega-57; Raynaud1970; FaltingsChai1990; Brion2015; Oort1971; KatzSerreTate; HeinlothSchmitt2010]
