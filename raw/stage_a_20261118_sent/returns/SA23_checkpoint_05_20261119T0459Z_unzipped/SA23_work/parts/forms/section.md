# Hyperplane sections, formal categories and the Tapis de Quillen

This part covers the complete Blass translation in cote24, the handwritten formal-category notes of cote162-5, both typed Tapis witnesses, and the two attached Quillen papers. The work is organized by their mathematical subjects. The two typed copies are consolidated without suppressing differing marginal questions. The handwritten notes are dated6September1968; the typed Tapis is dated10September1968. Neither date is assigned to the attached undated preprint. The surviving Blass mathematics ends in§11 despite its16-section outline; the final Survivre sheets are separately accounted for and supply no mathematical chronology.

## Cote24: from universal hyperplanes to Lefschetz pencils

The draft begins with projective-space conventions, the universal incidence divisor, generic hyperplane sections, and the transfer of local module properties. It then develops geometric irreducibility, connectedness, parameter openness, finite-etale multisections, exceptional loci, projective duality, prescribed linear systems, pencils and Grassmannians. The essential mathematical architecture is recognizable in later published sources. Several local formulas and some stronger formulations nevertheless fail as transcribed.

The projective convention matters throughout. With projective space parametrizing one-dimensional quotients, equations of degree d belong to the dual of the symmetric power. This explains the first divisor-parameter alarm and the later normal/conormal duality alarms. The finite-free Grassmannian charts give the expected smooth scheme of dimension n(r−n), but arbitrary quasi-coherent E does not make its Grassmannian quasi-compact: an infinite-dimensional projective space has an infinite standard cover without a finite subcover. [forms_StacksProj;forms_StacksGrass;C-SA23-forms-001–002,032–033]

The generic-section arguments must retain the incidence equation and the correct dimensions. A generic linear combination of bundle generators can vanish; the example1,x on the affine line disproves the draft’s shortcut, while the incidence-dimension argument proves the intended smooth Bertini theorem. The outsourced primary-field lemma has a checked modern proof in Stacks37.32, following Jouanolou. The connectedness generalization requires image dimension or quasi-finiteness: the projection from(two planes meeting in a point)×P² gives an explicit failure of the unrestricted intrinsic-dimension formulation. [forms_StacksSmoothBertini;forms_StacksBertini;C-SA23-forms-006–010]

The most consequential characteristic issue concerns the conormal projection. Its unramified locus corresponds to ordinary **nondegenerate** quadratic singularities. In characteristic2 a quadratic form of odd rank may define a smooth projective quadric while its polar Hessian is singular. The example x²+yz makes the distinction explicit. SGA7XVII3.3 preserves the nondegeneracy condition; its general Lefschetz-pencil existence theorem2.5 guarantees ordinary quadratic singularities and does not erase the characteristic2 distinction. Cote25 contains the corresponding correction, so the two typed witnesses are linked while their wording remains distinct. The requested inseparable-dual example also has an exact published answer: SGA7XVII3.4 uses the degree-p+1 Fermat hypersurface, whose Gauss map is Frobenius. [forms_SGA7Katz;C-SA23-forms-020–022,026]

The exceptional-locus claims need more than a title-level match to Bertini. Benoist’s2011 bounds distinguish closed depth1 points from other depth obstructions. Poonen–Slavov’s morphism theorem includes equidimensionality of nonempty fibers. Its Example1.7 refutes the unrestricted8.17(a) precisely: for Bl_p(P³)→P³, every hyperplane through p pulls back with the exceptional divisor as a component, so the bad locus has codimension one. Theorem1.8 separates the divisors arising from fiber-dimension jumps. These results match substantial parts of the draft, but do not determine its damaged codepth strata or restore8.18’s singular/nonsingular adjective and omitted normalization argument. The finite-etale multisection claim also remains qualified: the hyperplane construction supports the projective case, while7.4’s proper formulation conflicts with7.5 in both cotes24 and25. The intended proper smooth nonprojective counterexample has not been identified. [forms_Benoist2011;forms_PoonenSlavov;C-SA23-forms-014,018,023–025,082–084]

The complete comparison with cote25 is clause-specific. The degree-d divisor functor in Blass agrees with the later corrected Proposition2.2 at cote25 scans106–107, while its printed dual differs from both the early and corrected cote25 displays. The Grassmann chart uses a chosen basis decomposition; the source does not force an unrestricted nonsplit exact-sequence interpretation. The chart is a retraction torsor before choosing a splitting. An arbitrary exact-sequence counterexample diagnoses an extrapolation, not the statement with its stated basis. The concordance retains these distinctions and separates true aliases from related corrections. [C-SA23-forms-002,081;cote24_25_concordance.json]

## Formal categories and de Rham algebras

The handwritten notes pass from formal composition to the conormal module, exterior differentials, Lie brackets, connections, descent and cohomology. Two small-looking omissions have decisive consequences. Vanishing of d² on functions does not force vanishing on one-forms: the explicitly computed rank3 exterior algebra has d²e1=−e1∧e2∧e3. Its dual bracket has a nonzero Jacobiator, explaining the questioned Jacobi step. The final correspondence must also use **integrable** connections. The rank1 connection d+xdy on A² has curvature dx∧dy and cannot satisfy a stratification cocycle. [C-SA23-forms-066–067,073;compute_checks.output.json]

Finite locally free Lie–Rinehart modules and square-zero exterior de Rham algebras have the standard dual correspondence. The manuscript’s attributed Quillen theorem is stronger: it uses a merely flat conormal module and claims an equivalence with adic formal categories, together with a symmetric associated graded algebra and a cohomology comparison. The checked projective PBW and formally smooth groupoid results are partial matches. Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum’s classical/derived comparison and Ardizzoni–ElKaoutit–Saracco’s affine Hopf-algebroid adjunction cannot simply replace that exact underived flat-conormal statement. Its precise formulation and first circulation remain unlocated in the bounded search. The PD variant further requires compatibility of comultiplication with divided powers. [forms_LieRinehart;forms_FormalModuli;forms_HopfAlgebroid;forms_StacksCrystals;C-SA23-forms-069–074]

## The Tapis: homotopy types and higher algebra

The nerve, category-of-simplices and local-coefficient constructions are established. The presheaf construction has a different fullness issue: on the ordered set of integers, filtered colimit defines a geometric point that is not evaluation at any object. This supplies a negative answer to the manuscript’s explicit doubt without confusing it with the fully faithful simplicial nerve. The manuscript’s own product correction in category cochains is necessary for infinite categories, and its “Si!” margin retracts the denial of a nonlocal coefficient interpretation. Its derived tensor correction from bounded to bounded-above complexes is also mathematically necessary; dual numbers provide an exact periodic Tor example. [forms_SGA4I;forms_QuillenK;forms_Thomason;C-SA23-forms-034–037,085]

Two coefficient tests must be separated. A finite acyclic noncontractible complex passes all abelian coefficients pulled back from a point, so those tests alone do not detect the fundamental group. Moreover, the converse derived-local-system criterion cannot hold over every fixed nonzero ring: a simply connected Moore space M(Z/p,n), n≥2, is rationally acyclic but not weakly contractible. Its bounded rational locally constant derived category is the one of a point. Thus the coefficient ring and the equivalence of local-system categories must be specified. [forms_Hatcher,Examples2.38–2.40;C-SA23-forms-038,080]

The strict higher-category discussions need their globularity conditions. Iterated internal categories give multicomplexes under normalization; globularity removes the extra terms to produce ordinary length-n complexes. Strictly commutative Picard models also omit a real invariant: the self-commutor. Superlines show why the deleted strict-commutative strengthening is false. Higher mapping objects require derived Hom and resolutions, not only the ordinary Hom complex. The bare homotopy-group-object question already has a negative example in Stasheff’s1963 Theorem17: taking p=5,n=3 gives an A4 space with no A5 structure and hence no loop-space homotopy type. Its proof credits Adams. The article’s1959 presentation dates do not establish circulation of this exact example. The K-theory programme was developed by Quillen and Segal, while Schlichting’s exact counterexample prevents replacing enhanced input by an arbitrary triangulated homotopy category. [forms_DoldKan;forms_HopkinsSinger,AppendixB.2;forms_Stasheff,Theorem17 pp282–283;forms_QuillenK;forms_Segal;forms_Schlichting;C-SA23-forms-039–044]

## Bordism and its three compatibility questions

Thom’s coefficient computation and additive splitting supply the main algebraic input. The degrees of the polynomial generators exclude2^i−1; one typed margin reverses this condition. The multiplicative question has a positive answer at the homotopy-ring level through Boardman’s theorem, precisely stated in Szymik’s primary paper. It has a negative answer if strengthened to an E-infinity map HF2→MO. Compatibility with every proper Gysin map also fails: pushing1 from RP² to a point gives the nonzero bordism class[RP²], whereas ordinary cohomology lands in negative degree and vanishes. These are three separate questions. [forms_Thom;forms_Boardman;forms_Szymik,1.3,5.1,5.6;C-SA23-forms-047–049]

The proposed criterion for classes of embedded submanifolds is false under its literal wording. RP¹⊂RP² is dual to a, but Sq1(a)=a²≠0. The normal bundle contributes Stiefel–Whitney classes, and a framing hypothesis cannot be silently inserted. The resulting proposed realization of all Steenrod representations therefore remains a qualified programme until S(X) and its completion are defined precisely. Milnor’s dual Steenrod algebra and the strict additive-series automorphism interpretation are checked separately. The withdrawn noetherian question is answered by coherence of the infinite polynomial coefficient ring: each finite matrix descends to a noetherian finite-variable subring, followed by flat extension. The anticipated abelian envelope still requires its intended universal property to be specified. [forms_Milnor;C-SA23-forms-051–054;MP-SA23-forms-68]

The final H-orientation question has a precise sufficient modern framework in Ando–Blumberg–Gepner–Hopkins–Rezk’s coherent Thom-algebra and orientation theorems. Their commutative structure hypotheses are substantive; the theorem does not identify the weakest ordinary H-space assumptions meant in1968. The combined preprint dates from2008, the separated version and journal article from2014. [forms_ABGHR,Theorems4.2/4.6 and Corollary4.7;MP-SA23-forms-69]

## The two attached Quillen papers

The positivity offprint is already a published Quillen paper, not an unpublished Grothendieck claim. Its Gaussian monomial norms, prolongation matrix entries, both Weyl commutation formulas, binomial derivative norms and scalar example have been recomputed. The main positive-leading-term argument survives the lower-error binomial notation once its uniform constant is adjusted. Corollary2 concerns sums of squared differential operators; it is not a difference-of-squares claim. [forms_QuillenPositive;C-SA23-forms-055–057]

The potential Adams proof is explicitly incomplete. Its published counterpart is Quillen’s1968 Topology sketch, received25November1967. Friedlander’s1973 Theorem5.3 and§6 complete the complex étale route under specific rank, noetherian, fundamental-group and completion-prime hypotheses. Quillen’s1971 proof is a different method and expressly avoids étale topology. Neither later theorem automatically settles the broader pro-sphere-fibration representability conjecture that the sketch says it does not need. Publication, received and manuscript dates are recorded separately; an unavailable first-circulation date remains unavailable. [forms_QuillenEtale;forms_Friedlander;forms_QuillenAdams;C-SA23-forms-058–063]
