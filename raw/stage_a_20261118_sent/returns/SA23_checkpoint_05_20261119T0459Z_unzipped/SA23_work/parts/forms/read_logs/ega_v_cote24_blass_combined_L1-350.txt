147: \uline{Section 1: Preliminaries and Notation.}
148: 
149: Let S be a prescheme, let $\underline{E}$ be a locally free module of
150: finite type over S let $\underline{E}^{\vee}$ be its dual. We denote by P = $\underline{P}(\underline{E})$ the
151: projective fibration defined by $\underline{E}$ and by P$^{\vee}$ the projective
152: fibration defined by E$^{\vee}$. We shall call P$^{\vee}$ the \uline{scheme of}
153: \uline{hyperplanes} of P. This terminology can be justified as follows.
154: Let $\xi$ be a section of P$^{\vee}$ over S which is therefore determined by an
155: invertible quotient module L of $\underline{E}^{\vee}$. From it we obtain an
156: invertible quotient L$_{P}$ of $(\underline{E}^{\vee})_{\underline{P}} = (\underline{E}_{P})^{\vee}$, on the other hand, we have
157: the invertible quotient module O$_{P}$(1) of E$_{P}$. Passing to duals we
158: may take $\underline{L}_{P}{-1}$ resp. $\underline{O}_{P}(-1)$ to be invertible submodules (locally
159: direct \uline{summands or factors}) of $\underline{E}_{P}$ (resp. of $(\underline{E}_{P})^{\vee}$) and the pairing
160: $EP \otimes E^{\vee}_{P} \to \underline{O}_{P}$ defines therefore a natural pairing (*) $\underline{O}_{P}(-1) \otimes \underline{L}_{P}{-1}$
161: $\to \underline{O}_{P}$ or also the transpose homomorphism (**) $\underline{O}_{P} \to \underline{O}_{P}(1) \otimes \underline{L}_{P} =$
162: $\underline{O}_{P}(1)$ i.e. a section of $\underline{L}_{P}(1)$ canonically defined by $\xi$. The
163: ``divisor'' of that section i.e. the closed subscheme H$_{\xi}$ of $\underline{P}$ defined
164: by the image ideal of (*) is called the hyperplane in $\underline{P}$ defined by
165: the element $\xi \in$ P$^{\vee}$(S). We could describe it by noting that locally
166: over $\underline{S}$, $\xi$ is given by a section $\phi$ of E such that $\phi$ (s) $\neq 0$ for all
167: s ($\phi$ is determined by $\xi$ up to multiplication by an invertible
168: section of $\underline{O}_{S}$); since E = p$^{*}$(O$_{P}$(1)) (p: P $\to$ S being the
169: projection), $\phi$ can be considered as a section of O$_{P}$(1) the divisor
170: of which is nothing else but H$_{\xi}$.
