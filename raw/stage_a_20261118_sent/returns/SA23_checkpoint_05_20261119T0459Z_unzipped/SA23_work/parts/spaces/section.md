## Products of topoi, spaces, and essential morphisms

Cote161-3 begins with bifaisceaux and the product of topoi, then asks how this construction compares with the sheaf topos of a product of topological spaces. The product-site construction is established: sheaf conditions in each variable generate the required topology. The manuscript's finite-limit calculation supplies its universal property, while arbitrary products require finite-support tuples and universe bounds. Lafforgue's Mondovì course, slides47–48, supplies an exact modern statement of the product construction and the locally compact sufficient condition. It does not certify every formula in the draft (SA23-701–703; C-SA23-spaces-001–004; MP-SA23-spaces-01–02).

The compact-neighborhood argument is mathematically sound with quasi-compact neighborhoods understood in their relative sense. The further condition on every nonempty locally closed subspace is more specialized: the written maximal-open argument has been checked, but the search did not find an independently named exact formulation. This is classC with a specific bibliographic limit, not evidence of novelty. Infinite-product passages require separate treatment. The unfinished blanket counterexample for infinitely many discrete factors is too broad: the product of countably many countable discrete locales is spatial. The cylinder-tree proof in proof_checks.md checks this application of Heckmann's countable-presentation theorem (online2014, issue2015; SA23-705–709).

The next leaves distinguish presheaf topoi, essential morphisms, and topoi of Alexandrov spaces. Original SGA4 IV Exercise7.6, printed414–416, was visually checked. It states the essential-point and presheaf-category criteria, including the Karoubi-envelope qualification. This matters because the draft sometimes proposes an equivalence with preorders without specifying essential morphisms. Nonprincipal directed subsets yield extra geometric points. Likewise, the inverse limit of finite binary discrete spaces is Cantor space, whose sheaf topos is not a presheaf topos. Thus the unrestricted limit assertion fails; the stronger adjoint-site construction is a different conditional claim (SA23-710,715–719).

The density and Kan-extension pages contain a useful local correction. Sending covering families to epimorphic families does not suffice for a functor on a site to extend cocontinuously. A two-point discrete space gives a complete counterexample. The appropriate condition is inversion of the associated-sheaf equivalences; the same leaf marks this localization argument “OK.” The explicit question is retained as answered on the same page in the problem index (SA23-711–714).

A related fragment in cote161-4 scan18 asks again about Fun(C^op,E). Its product equivalence is true, but its proposed membership test using only the two projection direct images is false. The checked counterexample uses a square of presheaves on two arrow categories, with a two-element value at one corner and singleton values elsewhere. Both projection direct images lie in the point subtopoi, while the square itself does not lie in their product. This corrects a proof route, not the underlying product theorem. The matching cote161-6 scan23 passage was read at both ends and is explicitly marked wholly cancelled (SA23-701; C-SA23-spaces-072).

## Algebraic structures, Morita theory, and foundational exercises

The Morita leaves retain the algebra action and evaluation isomorphism. With these data and positive finite rank, one reconstructs G as Hom_End(E)(E,F), and matrix units prove G⊗E≃F. Uniqueness up to unique compatible isomorphism follows. If the evaluation datum is omitted, scalar automorphisms and line-bundle twists remain. The source's local-inner wording also confuses an automorphism of F with an algebra automorphism of End(F). These are separate reading and hypothesis issues (SA23-722; C-SA23-spaces-023–024). Morita's1958 primary metadata was verified; the exact sheaf-level argument here is a present reconstruction, not a quotation from an unread original theorem.

The theory-of-structures passages are conditional. They study when an assignment T(C) is represented by a category with specified limit operations. Once representability is assumed, 2-Yoneda proves uniqueness and the displayed correspondence of transformations. That does not prove representability for every species of structure. Lawvere's finite-product theory is a precise model for part of the discussion; geometric theories and arbitrary first-order structures have different preservation requirements. Fields with their usual underlying-ring functor are not models of a finite-product algebraic theory, because products of fields cease to be fields (SA23-729–734).

A cancelled field-recognition test turns out to detect commutative von Neumann regular rings. The algebra Z[x,y]/(x²y−x,xy²−y) splits as Z[t,t⁻¹]×Z, and F₂×F₃ passes the proposed bijectivity test although it is not a field. The exact computation and general argument are retained. Lawvere's reprint pp79–80 discusses the corresponding distinction between division rings and generalized-inverse algebraic structure. Cancellation is not silently reversed into a new theorem (SA23-732).

Other material is foundational but still required checking: sheafification, sites, group actions and slices, classifying topoi, sobrification, specialization, category-of-points descriptions, and universes. Original SGA IV1.2, printed303, supplies the exact Giraud equivalence; IV4.2.1–4.2.4, printed337–339, supplies the sober-space reconstruction. Two fragments remain classF because their maps and hypotheses are insufficiently specified: the cancelled higher-torsor/hypercohomology calculation and the cancelled compact-power topos diagram. This is a limit of identification from the transcription, not a statement of mathematical impossibility.

## Descent and affine scheme theory

Cote161-4 begins with flatness, openness, closures, and a checklist of descent properties. These are the foundations of the later representability and affine-scheme lecture. Formal smoothness does descend under a faithfully flat base change without finite presentation: Stacks tag06CM, Lemma10.138.16, was checked through its proof, including descent of arbitrary projectivity of modules. Effective descent of quasi-coherent modules and affine schemes must be distinguished from mere full faithfulness of descent for scheme morphisms (SA23-746–755).

The arbitrary-immersion assertion is false for fpqc descent. The complete example X=⊔_pSpecF_p→SpecZ becomes an immersion after the faithfully flat affine cover Spec(∏_pZ_(p)), but is not an immersion before base change. Its failure of quasi-compactness is exactly relevant to Stacks35.23.23. The source first asks the question, later asserts “arbitrary,” and is kept in both places. No ink-level correction is made. The comment through which this example was found is dated28August2026; that date is not presented as the first historical occurrence (SA23-754; MP-SA23-spaces-33).

Descent of a specified relatively ample line bundle is a different statement from existence of some relatively ample line bundle. Projectivity and quasi-projectivity are not even Zariski local on the base; the Hironaka blowup construction in Stacks Examples was read. A separate assertion needs “universally closed” rather than merely “closed”: A¹ over a field is a closed quasi-projective morphism but is not proper. These distinctions are consequential and are not reduced to spelling corrections (SA23-756–761).

The remaining affine theory covers functors of equations, tensor-product fiber products, finite coproducts and idempotents, spectra and radicals, localization, the structure sheaf, and relative Spec/Proj. The exact polynomial and finite-algebra calculations were redone. Field-valued points determine radicals and lose nilpotents. For finite-type algebras over an arbitrary base, finite extensions of every prime residue field suffice for the detection statement; a Jacobson qualification would be needed only if one restricted to closed residue points. The omitted principal-open sheaf proof is completed by clearing denominators and using powers of covering functions to generate1. Physical readings such as A_f→A_g are flagged when the open inclusion requires the opposite arrow (SA23-768–779).

The affine quotient warning also has a precise modern continuation. GL_n/B has constant global functions over a field, so its affine coequalizer is a point although its sheaf quotient is a flag variety. Finite locally free equivalence relations have effective affine quotients (Stacks39.23.9). Mere finiteness does not imply effectivity, as Raicu's appendix to Kollár shows. Kollár's positive-characteristic theorem and Witaszek's mixed-characteristic extension address geometric existence under exact hypotheses; they do not justify an unrestricted reading of the draft's “OK.” The search did not locate a general characteristic-zero resolution and does not promote that non-result into a certified current open problem (SA23-776).
