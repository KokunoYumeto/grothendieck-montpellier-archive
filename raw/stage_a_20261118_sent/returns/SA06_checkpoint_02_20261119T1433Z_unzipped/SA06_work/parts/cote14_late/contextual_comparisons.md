## Correspondence rings, Lefschetz operators and motives

**SA06-252–253.** A graded correspondence algebra for one variety does not by itself specify a category of motives. One must give morphisms between different varieties, composition, projectors and Tate twists. The now-read external cote16/003–018 genuinely discusses grading projectors and the universal Lefschetz ring. In16/016 the preservation of the projectors is explicitly essential, and16/017 rejects uniqueness without the grading, already for n=1. The comparison is retained as a precise warning about hypotheses; no claim that a full motive category is constructed on cote14/087–132 is made.

**SA06-254.** Fix a Lefschetz string v_a=L^a v_0, 0≤a≤r. Unit lowering sends v_a to v_(a−1), whereas the sl₂ lowering compatible with e=L and h(v_a)=(2a−r)v_a sends v_a to a(r−a+1)v_(a−1). Both the general coefficient recurrence and its boundary conditions are checked in `computation_notes.md`; exact matrix calculations for finite strings are saved. Cote16's Λ is the first normalization. Künnemann (1993, Theorems5.1–5.2) and Beauville (2008, Theorem3.3) use the appropriately weighted rational Lefschetz/sl₂ construction. The source's proposed integral exterior model cannot suppress factorials: already ω²=2vol in rank4. The computation is an independent check, not a reading correction.

**SA06-255–257.** In the tensor category generated by h(P¹)=1⊕Q(−1), every tensor automorphism fixes the unit inclusion. The Lefschetz raising operator sends the unit vector to a nonzero degree-two vector. Hence its sl₂ action is not automatically an action by tensor automorphisms of the fibre functor. Similarly, a variety-dependent shifted grading is not the tensor weight cocharacter: it assigns a nonzero shifted weight to H⁰(P¹), while the unit has weight zero. If L:h^i(X)→h^(i+2)(X)(1) is a genuine motivic morphism and χ is the Tate character, naturality after a trivialization of the Tate line gives g_(i+2)Lg_i⁻¹=χ(g)⁻¹L. Thus L commutes with kerχ. The exact unit and twist calculations are saved. The formal framework is Deligne and Milne (1982, §§2–5, author-corrected text), not an extra theorem found in the assigned pages.

**SA06-258–259.** The late cote14 pages do not define standard-conjecture letters A–D. The external Coates letter and cote16 formulas now supply genuine historical endpoints, but their lettering must be retained locally. Grothendieck (1969, §3, p.196) proves B(X)⇒A(X) and A(X×X)⇒B(X), and consequently an equivalence when quantified over all varieties. This is not the assertion A(X)⇒B(X) for an arbitrary fixed X. Furthermore, late cote14's quotient modulo algebraic equivalence and whole Chow groups are finer than cohomological algebraic classes; the counterexamples in C-SA06-238–241 remain relevant even when cohomological hard Lefschetz is known.

**SA06-260.** Jannsen (1992, Theorem1) proves semisimplicity of numerical motives with characteristic-zero coefficients. His Corollary1 treats a homological-to-numerical nilpotent kernel under algebraic Künneth projectors; it does not show an arbitrary rational-Chow kernel vanishes. Corollary2's Tannakian conclusion requires the indicated Künneth/sign modification. The symbol N on cote14/117 is fixed by that page's own quotient convention, not by the word “numerical” in a modern theorem. No semisimplicity statement about that different quotient is inferred.

**SA06-261.** Künnemann's theorem is about relative rational Chow motives of abelian schemes over a smooth quasiprojective connected base over a field. The paper expressly notes that the Chow-motive category need not be abelian. A generic semisimplicity argument for arbitrary sl₂ representations cannot replace the motivic proof. Beauville (2008, Proposition5.5) gives the shifted exponent g−2p+s on CH_s^p; Proposition5.11 isolates the extra negative-piece vanishing needed for an unshifted injectivity claim. These restricted results do not establish the universal whole-Chow assertions on cote14/112.

**SA06-262.** A rational isomorphism need not have finite integral kernel and cokernel. The elementary map 0→Q/Z becomes an isomorphism after tensoring with Q but has infinite cokernel. Likewise an integral exterior tensor can recover determinant content without recovering the image lattice. The finite-error, torsion-free, rational and integral assertions are therefore separately registered; the code's small examples illustrate the full algebraic counterargument.

**SA06-263–265.** Deligne (1980, Theorem4.1.1, p.217) proves hard Lefschetz for smooth projective varieties over an algebraically closed field with Qℓ coefficients, ℓ different from the characteristic. Section4.1.3, p.218, gives the relevant cohomological invariant-cycle statement for a Lefschetz pencil. Neither theorem constructs a canonical integral complement in a local Chow group, proves algebraicity of every inverse correspondence, or supplies the manuscript's proposed cycle-valued vanishing cycles. External cote16/047 explicitly proposes reformulation on singular varieties; the current late cote14 local setup still assumes regular X,Y. The nodal rational cubic example belongs to the earlier run: its rational H¹ has dimension one, so an alternating self-pairing cannot be nondegenerate. That obstruction is rechecked topologically and algebraically, with no particular singular replacement attributed to the author.

## Arithmetic and anabelian comparisons

**SA06-266.** Tate's finite-field theorem and Faltings's number-field theorem are comparisons for abelian varieties and Galois-equivariant Tate-module maps. Faltings (1983, §5, Sätze3–4, p.360) proves semisimplicity of Vℓ(A) and the integral End comparison over a number field; Hom follows by applying the endomorphism result to A×B. The1984 erratum corrects the height argument in Satz2 and does not retract the Tate conclusion. These exact passages were read by the parent and are carried here with explicit shared provenance. They do not identify arbitrary mixed cohomology classes on the geometrically closed base of cote14/089.

Mochizuki (1999, TheoremA; Definition16.4 and Theorem16.5) treats dominant morphisms from smooth pro-varieties to hyperbolic pro-curves over a sub-p-adic field, with birational transition maps, by open continuous homomorphisms over the absolute Galois group modulo geometric inner automorphisms. The author states both full profinite and geometric pro-p versions. The present part opened the primary source and checked those exact hypotheses and the profinite deduction; it did not independently audit the entire proof. The theorem does not assert surjectivity of a section map or recognition of every procyclic subgroup. No arithmetic field, such fundamental group or section map is defined on the assigned late cote14 pages.

**SA06-267.** Semisimplicity of representations does not imply a trivial group centre: G_m already gives a counterexample. Separately, a centre-free profinite geometric group permits recovery of an extension from its outer action by the standard pullback construction. Mochizuki (2004, §1.3, Lemma1.3.1 and Corollary1.3.2) gives the exact hyperbolic-curve slimness setting; arithmetic slimness includes the stated assumption on G_K. Reductivity and this profinite centre condition are distinct inputs. The external cote16 degree-zero centre question is also a different ring-theoretic question.

**SA06-268.** Polarizability alone does not supply descent from C to R. Real descent requires a compatible semilinear involution or descent datum. Deligne and Milne (1982, §5, especially Theorem5.16 and Remark5.17 in the corrected text) describe polarizable Tate triples and compact kernels with additional real and parity conditions; Propositions5.18–5.19 give their Hodge-element formulation. The earlier run's compact-real-form analogy is retained only with those conditions, separately from an assertion that a given complex object descends.

**SA06-269–270.** Kummer theory gives the forward cyclotomic conjugation formula on tame inertia at a cusp in characteristic zero. It does not give a converse recognizing arbitrary procyclic subgroups as cusps. A Tate weight character of a Tannaka group is another structure. The normalizer on cote14/129 is specifically the algebraic-group normalizer of a Cartan subgroup. The separately retrieved LM6 A.22/A.50 endpoints are arithmetic profinite normalizer statements, and the register excerpts truncate their hypotheses. Their statuses are provenance only; see `contextual_endpoint_audit.md`.

## Nori, periods and the2026 comparison

**SA06-271.** Huber and Müller-Stach (2017, corrected2025 text, Theorem9.1.16) give the rational Nori motivic Galois exact sequence under their embedding-into-C hypotheses. Remark9.5.7 expressly keeps geometric connectedness open; the sequence alone does not establish it or a canonical splitting. Kahn (2025, Theorem5.10; Theorem10.2 and Remark10.3) supplies an étale-descent proof with coefficients in a Q-algebra. These exact primary locators were read by cote14_early and shared in this run. They are not claims on cote14/087–132.

**SA06-272.** Choudhury and Gallauer Alves de Souza (2017, Theorem9.1, arXiv v3 p.41) identify the Nori and Ayoub degree-zero Hopf algebras over a principal ideal domain for a field with chosen complex embedding. The comparison uses H_0 of Ayoub's derived Hopf object. It neither deletes higher derived information nor constructs a motivic t-structure. The shared primary inspection and those limitations are recorded.

**SA06-273.** Huber and Müller-Stach (2017, Theorem10.2.7) identify pure rational Nori motives with André motives; Arapura (2013, Theorem6.4.1) gives the corresponding pure comparison for smooth bases over subfields of C. This uses motivated correspondences, not a proof that every motivated correspondence is algebraic. The pure category is semisimple, while the full mixed category has extensions. The assigned equivalence chart on14/092 defines neither Nori category.

**SA06-274–275.** Huber and Müller-Stach (2017, Theorem13.1.4) identify the spectrum of the formal-period algebra with the Betti–de Rham comparison torsor. Its existence is distinct from injectivity of evaluation at the actual complex comparison point. Huber (2020, Conjecture5.14 and Proposition5.15, p.588) formulates the finite-object period conjecture with connectedness and the required transcendence-degree equality and relates it to evaluation injectivity for the generated category. The latter primary passages were opened and read in this part. The source range contains no period torsor or transcendence-degree assertion.

**SA06-276.** Grothendieck's own published de Rham paper contains a period expectation in note10, p.102 (Grothendieck,1966). The primary Numdam PDF was opened and the note read. The surrounding chronology distinguishes the October1963 letter, subsequent remarks, July1965 receipt and1966 publication. This corrects an earlier project's bibliographical limitation; it is not a period-conjecture statement newly found in the late cote14 pages.

**SA06-277.** Ayoub's *Anabelian presentation of the motivic Galois group in characteristic zero* was checked in its author version and published2026 metadata. Theorem2.2.3, Theorem4.4.2 and Corollary4.4.15 reconstruct motivic group objects from geometric-origin sheaf data arranged functorially across varieties, with coefficient-linear symmetric monoidal structure. Remark2.3.7 retains a stronger constructible-realization equivalence as expected. This is far more structured input than one ordinary profinite fundamental group of a curve. The theorem and its scope are a current literature comparison, with no historical attribution to the assigned sheets.
