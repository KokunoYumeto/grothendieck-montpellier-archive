## 1. Integral inverse correspondences in specified families

**Origin:** SA06-289–292; C-SA06-264–271; MP-SA06-147–150. Cross-package duplicate: the first Albanese question also occurs in early C-SA06-89.

**What it would establish.** Determine, for an explicitly chosen family, whether an integral cycle realizes the inverse to the Albanese H¹ map and whether an integral one-cycle realizes the Picard/top-degree inverse. These are separate predicates, with separate cycle dimensions and torsion conventions.

**Known constraints.** Rational inverses exist by isogeny/polarization arguments. Voisin (2025, Theorem1.4) and Alexandrou (2026, Theorem1.3) refute the universal integral Albanese assertion even with representable CH0. Beckmann and de Gaay Fortman (2023, Theorem1.1), combined with Engel, de Gaay Fortman and Schreieder (2025/2026, Theorem1.1), obstruct the second assertion on very general principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension at least4. The integral implication and its sign were proved in this consolidation; a new task should start from that result.

**First concrete step.** Fix a family with a known Jacobian-splitting or universal-cycle construction, and write its exact correspondence classes and obstruction group. A few hours can settle the equivalence of the chosen formulations and catalogue existing positive cases. It should not promise a classification of all varieties or infer integral algebraicity from rational algebraicity.

## 2. Corrected Lefschetz statements for graded Chow groups

**Origin:** SA06-232–239,261–263; C-SA06-237–246; MP-SA06-124–141.

**What it would establish.** Replace the false universal whole-Chow surjectivity assertions by a statement on an explicitly specified graded piece or algebraic representative, with a correct exponent and precise coefficient ring.

**Known constraints.** K3 surfaces and very general quintic threefolds refute several literal versions. Künnemann (1993, Theorem5.2) proves rational motivic Lefschetz for abelian schemes; Beauville (2008, Proposition5.5) gives the shifted exponent g−2p+s on CH_s^p. Patel and Ravindra (2014, Conjecture1.1 and Theorem1.4) distinguish the Chow weak-Lefschetz conjecture from their infinitesimal lifting theorem. Deligne's cohomological theorem supplies no general integral Chow inverse.

**First concrete step.** Choose abelian threefolds or a carefully specified class with a Chow–Künneth decomposition, and compare every map in the manuscript table to the actual grading. A single run can produce and verify the corrected map table; it should not rebrand the manuscript's failed statement as an already-written conjecture about a filtration that it does not define.

## 3. Local cycle groups and missing definitions

**Origin:** SA06-227–231,264; C-SA06-230–236; MP-SA06-113–123.

**What it would establish.** Give an unambiguous modern interpretation of A,J,N for a punctured local scheme and decide which portions of the conjectured ranges even define meaningful maps.

**Known constraints.** The page assumes X,Y regular, while the ambient strictly local Noetherian ring and the integer N need careful interpretation. Bhatt and de Jong (2014, Theorem0.1) establish precise local Picard injectivity statements with dimension, normality, excellence and depth/characteristic hypotheses. They do not prove the higher-Chow finiteness package. The abstract exact-sequence inference at108 fails without surjectivity on the discrete quotient.

**First concrete step.** Fix either an isolated normal complex surface singularity or a regular/complete-intersection local model in dimension at least4. Define each group, write localization with correct codimension, and compare the exact Picard theorem. The first run should yield a rigorous problem statement and low-dimensional tests, not guess the missing definitions from a modern theorem.

## 4. Integral orders in the operator algebra

**Origin:** SA06-253–257,262,289,292,298–300; C-SA06-265,271,278–282; MP-SA06-147,150,153–155. Checked external endpoints: cote16/014–018 and47/009.

**What it would establish.** Identify the order generated by integral raising/lowering or Fourier correspondences inside the relevant rational matrix algebra, for one explicit model and normalization.

**Known constraints.** Unit lowering differs from sl₂ lowering by a(r−a+1); exterior powers introduce factorials. A top wedge does not determine an integral image lattice. The existence of a rational sl₂ action (Beauville,2008, Theorem3.3) is not an integral algebraicity theorem or an automatic motivic Galois action. Moonen and Polishchuk's divided-power constructions apply under their stated hypotheses.

**First concrete step.** Specify the lattice and the three operators, then compute Smith normal forms and multiplication tables in the smallest unresolved rank, preserving exact integer arithmetic and every denominator. A few hours can certify those cases and identify a candidate general index formula. It should not claim that small ranks prove a universal order theorem.

## 5. Picard kernels in a pencil

**Origin:** SA06-225–226,230; C-SA06-228–229,235,254; MP-SA06-112,118–122.

**What it would establish.** Separate the generic-fibre Picard statement controlled by vertical divisors from its specialization to an individual smooth member.

**Known constraints.** The manuscript's irreducible-fibre presumption is not fully quantified. In a regular pencil, a generic-fibre-trivial line bundle is vertical, and irreducible fibres restrict the vertical divisor classes. Grothendieck–Lefschetz and modern local Picard results impose extra hypotheses. The independently checked generic blow-up argument covers a pencil with a regular codimension2 centre only.

**First concrete step.** Write the divisor exact sequence for one surface pencil with geometrically integral fibres, identify the vertical subgroup and then state the specialization map. A single run can prove the generic lemma and locate the precise step at which a special-fibre assertion needs more input.

## 6. Isogeny denominators and characteristic-p phenomena

**Origin:** SA06-289–291; C-SA06-263–269; MP-SA06-147–148.

**What it would establish.** Calculate the least positive d for which d·u⁻¹ is an actual homomorphism and translate it to a minimal correspondence denominator in a specified situation.

**Known constraints.** The correct d annihilates ker(u) as a finite group scheme; its order is only a bound. For [m], d=m although the degree is m^(2g). The ℓ-adic criterion detects the ℓ-primary kernel for ℓ different from the characteristic. It does not recover inseparable information from one prime-to-p Tate module.

**First concrete step.** Work through products of elliptic curves and a specified isogeny in characteristic p, recording the group-scheme exponent and the prime-to-p lattice indices. A few hours can produce a complete theorem with examples and delimit what a cohomological reconstruction cannot see.

## 7. Boundedness and the Chow–K comparison

**Origin:** SA06-248–249; C-SA06-251–252; MP-SA06-144.

**What it would establish.** Make the manuscript's five-condition diagram rigorous after choosing a precise meaning of K-equivalence and bounded families.

**Known constraints.** A common locally free quotient provides bounded kernels, but a second common twist can be required. The leading Chern-class formula carries (−1)^(i−1)(i−1)!, while rational Chern character removes that denominator. The source's continuation is121→123, and its actual chain is weaker than an equivalence of all five conditions.

**First concrete step.** Define one candidate equivalence relation using the topological filtration on K0, and prove exactly the adjacent arrows that use it. Check torsion and factorial loss in codimensions2 and3 before any universal claim. The result can be a definitive corrected implication diagram, not a new general boundedness theorem.

## 8. Integral local pairings and vanishing cycles

**Origin:** SA06-229–230,264–265; C-SA06-233–235; MP-SA06-116–123.

**What it would establish.** Compare the finite discriminant group of a surface-resolution lattice with a precisely defined local discrete divisor group, then state what a higher-dimensional cycle-valued generalization would require.

**Known constraints.** Mumford (1961, §1) proves negative definiteness for the exceptional intersection matrix of a normal complex surface singularity. Its finite lattice cokernel must not be identified with the full local Picard group. Deligne (1980, §4.1) gives cohomological invariant cycles, not the proposed integral local J-valued object. Ordinary smooth Poincaré duality fails for elementary singular examples.

**First concrete step.** Choose one explicit resolution graph, compute its integral pairing and discriminant group, and compare the exact Picard sequence. A few hours can complete this surface case and formulate a controlled extension question.

## 9. Transporter incidence and tangent estimates

**Origin:** SA06-213–214; C-SA06-253,256–259; MP-SA06-145–146.

**What it would establish.** Reconstruct a well-typed transporter/incidence statement with explicit quotient hypotheses and determine which tangent conclusions actually imply smoothness in a fixed geometric setting.

**Known constraints.** The selected transporter leaves are cancelled and carry two unattached question marks. The contracted product needs representability and flatness assumptions; quotient sheaves cannot be replaced by naive point quotients. A tangent dimension inequality does not alone establish smoothness. The regular-Cartan comparison belongs to SGA3 XIII, and its restored unirationality argument proves dominance by the norm, not differential surjectivity in every characteristic.

**First concrete step.** Fix a smooth algebraic group over a field, a specified closed subscheme and a flat normalizer, then compare the incidence functor with G×^N W and calculate one fibre and tangent sequence. A few hours can finish the precise statement and show exactly which extra smoothness premise is needed. The task cannot decide what the two handwritten question marks point to without the scans.

## 10. Ampleness over a nonclosed field

**Origin:** SA06-216–224; C-SA06-216–227; MP-SA06-105–111.

**What it would establish.** State exactly which fixed Euler/H0 thresholds characterize ampleness after accounting for constants and geometric integrality, and separate them from the robust growth criterion.

**Known constraints.** The Euler-growth criterion is established in Mumford–Oda VIII.1, Theorem1.11. P¹_C over R with the trivial bundle refutes the unnormalized threshold≥2. Strictly nef divisors need not be ample, and positive top degree plus positivity on curves fails in dimension3. DELV's counterexamples prohibit extending divisor arguments to arbitrary higher-codimension nef classes.

**First concrete step.** Normalize Euler characteristics by the constant field of each integral curve/subvariety and test the resulting formulation against the original induction. A few hours can establish the correct elementary version or a precise counterexample; it should not conflate this repair with an unproved higher-cycle Nakai criterion.
