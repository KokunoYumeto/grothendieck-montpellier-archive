## 1. Integral inverse cycles on scan 71

### 1.1 The Albanese inverse and a universal zero-cycle

Let X be smooth projective over C of dimension n, and put B=Alb(X), with a chosen base point for a:X→B. The map a* on H¹ with integral coefficients is an isomorphism. For an integral Γ∈CHⁿ(B×X), its transpose cohomological action Γ*:H¹(X,Z)→H¹(B,Z) is the inverse of a* exactly when the induced homomorphism uΓ:B→Alb(X)=B is the identity. Indeed H¹(uΓ)=Γ* a*, and the H¹ realization of homomorphisms of complex abelian varieties is faithful. Conversely uΓ=id immediately gives Γ*a*=id. The same conclusion follows if the proposed equality holds in Zℓ-cohomology for a single ℓ, because the Tate realization is faithful.

This formulation uses the (1,2n−1) Künneth component modulo torsion; it does not assume H*(X,Z) is torsion-free. Voisin (2025, §2, immediately before Lemma2.1) states exactly this equivalence. Her Definition1.1 defines such a Γ as a universal zero-cycle, and Theorem1.4 constructs a smooth projective complex threefold with representable CH0 but without one. Alexandrou (2026, Theorem1.3, p.2; Theorem5.1 and its application, pp.22–23) improves the example to a surface S with Alb(S)=E, for example when End(E)=Z. It still has representable CH0. Thus representability of the group and existence of a cycle that universally realizes its inverse are different requirements.

A rational section of Sym^m(X)→B over the generic point would yield an integral incidence cycle over a dense open subset of B. Take its closure in B×X. The induced Albanese homomorphism agrees with the identity on that open set (after subtracting the base-point value), hence everywhere. This proves the sufficient condition suggested on the sheet. It does not prove the section exists. Rational universal cycles do exist: choose a curve whose Jacobian surjects onto B, split that surjection in the isogeny category, clear denominators, and use the Poincaré divisor of the curve. Voisin's Lemma2.1 and its following paragraph give this construction. The HTML equation(4) reverses the typed composition; the typed domains and the subsequent construction require j_*∘s=k id_B. No manuscript reading is changed on account of that modern typographical issue.

### 1.2 The Picard inverse: integral decomposition of its cohomology class

This is an inference proved here from the opened primary results of Beckmann–de Gaay Fortman (2023) and Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder (2025/2026), not a theorem attributed verbatim to either source.

Take a complex abelian variety A of dimension g, its dual A∨, and the normalized Poincaré bundle P on A∨×A. Its divisor class defines the canonical isomorphism D:H^(2g−1)(A,Z)(g−1)→H¹(A∨,Z). An integral one-cycle Γ on A∨×A realizing D⁻¹ would have codimension 2g−1. Since abelian-variety cohomology is torsion-free and exterior on H¹, its class has only three possible Künneth components: (2g−2,2g), (2g−1,2g−1), and (2g,2g−2).

Let p and q be the two projections and let 0 denote each factor's origin. The integral cycle

Γ′=Γ−p_*Γ×{0_A}−{0_A∨}×q_*Γ

has exactly the same mixed component as Γ and has zero unmixed components. Here proper pushforward selects the relevant unmixed component by integration over the other factor. The other subtracted cycle does not alter that component: its one-dimensional part projects to a point and therefore pushes forward to zero as a one-cycle. This is an integral operation in Chow groups, requiring no Künneth projectors and no division.

The inverse mixed class is, up to a unit sign,

ρ_P=c₁(P)^(2g−1)/(2g−1)!.

The following general calculation proves both its integrality and the coefficient being ±1, rather than an unspecified nonzero integer. Put m=2g. Choose integral bases x₁,…,x_m of H¹(A∨) and y₁,…,y_m of H¹(A) dual for P, so c₁(P)=Σ x_j∧y_j. Orient both factor top wedges to integrate to1; a change to geometric trace orientations can change the final sign only. Order all x's before all y's. Since the summands x_j∧y_j commute and square to zero,

ρ_P=(−1)^((m−1)(m−2)/2) Σ_j x_hat_j∧y_hat_j,

where each hatted wedge omits the j-th basis vector and preserves the remaining order. Set s=(−1)^(g−1). Integration over A∨ sends x_j∧ρ_P to s(−1)^(j−1)y_hat_j. Integration over A sends c₁(P)∧y_hat_j to (−1)^(j−1)x_j. Thus D∘ρ_P*=s id. The unique inverse mixed class is sρ_P. The sparse-exterior implementation recomputes every column for g=1,…,5 and checks exactly this identity over Z; the displayed formula proves it for every g.

Consequently, existence of Γ would make ρ_P algebraic integrally. Beckmann–de Gaay Fortman (2023, Theorem1.1(i)⇒(iv)) then implies the integral Hodge conjecture for one-cycles on A. Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder (arXiv2507.15704v3, Theorem1.1, p.2) prove that for a very general principally polarized complex A of dimension g≥4, every algebraic codimension-c class, 2≤c≤g−1, has even coefficient relative to θ^c/c!. In particular θ^(g−1)/(g−1)! is an integral Hodge one-cycle class and is not algebraic. This is a contradiction. The second universal integral inverse assertion on scan71 is therefore false already for these abelian varieties.

The conclusion remains valid for the manuscript's ℓ-adic formulation: over C, comparison carries integral algebraic cycle classes to their ℓ-adic classes, and the free integral mixed lattice injects into its tensor product with Zℓ. Equality with the inverse at one ℓ forces the same integral mixed class. This argument says nothing against rational inverse cycles. It also does not identify the two questions on scan71 in arbitrary dimensions: each has its own obstruction.

## 2. Abelian correspondences and integral lattices

**Graph additivity (C-SA06-200).** For an elliptic curve, [2]* acts on H² by4. The sum of two identity graphs acts by2. On H¹ both act by2. Thus the additive Hom realization is its H¹ part, not the whole graph-correspondence class. The code records this elementary counterexample.

**One-cycles and numerical equivalence (C-SA06-261).** A one-cycle Z on X×Y induces Pic⁰(X)_red→Alb(Y) by restricting line bundles to the normalizations of its curve components and pushing degree-zero divisors to Y. This construction is additive. Compose Z with the two normalized Poincaré divisor correspondences to obtain a divisor correspondence on Pic⁰(X)×Pic⁰(Y). If Z is numerically trivial, that divisor is numerically trivial because numerical equivalence is preserved under correspondence composition. A numerically trivial divisor on a product has no mixed Hom component: Pic⁰ of the product is the product of the two Pic⁰ groups. Hence the induced homomorphism vanishes. This avoids an unjustified general identification of numerical and homological equivalence for higher cycles.

**Best denominator (C-SA06-265).** If u:A→B is an isogeny, d·u⁻¹ is an actual homomorphism exactly when [d]_A descends along A→A/ker(u)=B, equivalently when [d] kills the finite kernel group scheme. The least such d is its exponent. For u=[m] on a complex g-dimensional abelian variety it is m, whereas deg(u)=#ker(u)=m^(2g). The saved values m=2,3,5, g=1,2,3 are examples of this general proof. The prime support still agrees, so the ℓ-integral statement survives the incorrect best-denominator equality.

**Top wedge and lattice (C-SA06-279–281).** A nonzero decomposable ω∈ΛʳV determines W by W={v:v∧ω=0}. This is a statement over a field. In Zℓ² the lattices ℓZℓe₁+Zℓe₂ and Zℓe₁+ℓZℓe₂ are distinct but have the same determinant line ℓZℓ(e₁∧e₂). Hence top wedges do not recover an integral image. In degree i, trace-duality uses Λ^(2n−i), not the fixed Λ^(2n−1), which works only for i=1. One determinant valuation at ℓ determines only the ℓ-part of an isogeny degree.

**Kummer and finite NS terms (C-SA06-272–275).** From 0→μ_N→G_m→G_m→0 in the étale topology, with N invertible, and N-divisible global units, H¹(X,μ_N)=Pic(X)[N]. Multiplication byN on Pic⁰ is surjective, so 0→Pic⁰[N]→Pic[N]→NS[N]→0 is exact. The finite torsion subgroup of the finitely generated NS group has zero Tate module. Passing to inverse limits gives H¹(X,Zℓ(1))=TℓPic⁰. Finite-level Poincaré duality, followed by the correct inverse limit, retains a finite dual of the full ℓ-primary NS torsion in top odd cohomology; dualizing only the free Tate modules would lose it. No full profinite statement at the residue characteristic is inferred from prime-to-characteristic Kummer theory.

## 3. Ampleness, cones and intersection computations

**Euler thresholds (C-SA06-217).** X=P¹_C, regarded as an integral proper R-scheme, has H⁰(O_X)=C and H¹(O_X)=0. Thus χ_R(O_X)=h⁰_R(O_X)=2 while O_X has degree0 and is not ample. This satisfies the literal fixed threshold on the only positive-dimensional integral closed subvariety. It refutes that version over an arbitrary base field; it does not refute the growth-to-infinity criterion or its algebraically closed variant.

**Surface Riemann–Roch (C-SA06-226).** On a smooth projective surface χ(O(nD))=χ(O)+(n²D²−nD·K)/2. When D²=0 the linear coefficient is −D·K/2. A claim about its sign needs information on D·K. The script expands the formula but does not substitute an unproved positivity statement.

**Strictly nef, positive top degree, not ample (C-SA06-222).** Let S be Mumford's complex smooth projective surface with a strictly nef line bundle L satisfying L²=0, as in Hartshorne (1970, Example10.6, p.56). Take an ample globally generated line bundle A on S, form X=P_S(O⊕A) using quotient lines, and put ξ=c₁(O_X(1)), D=ξ+π*L. The bundle O⊕A is nef, hence ξ is nef. Every vertical curve has D-degree>0; every horizontal curve has π*L-degree>0 and ξ-degree≥0. Thus D is strictly nef. The relation ξ²=ξπ*A and π_*ξ=1 gives D³=A²+3A·L>0. But the section from the quotient O⊕A→O has D|_S=L, which is not ample. Therefore D is not ample. This is our construction, distinct from Hartshorne's Example10.8. The Groebner reduction checks the intersection expansion only.

**Nef powers (C-SA06-223).** In a finite-dimensional numerical divisor space over R, a nef divisor D is a limit of ample R-divisors D+εH. For every effective i-dimensional integral subvariety Z, (D+εH)^i·Z≥0, and intersection numbers are polynomial and continuous, so D^i·Z≥0. This argument applies to divisor powers. It does not prove that products of arbitrary nef higher-codimension classes are nef; the exact counterexamples are DELV2011 TheoremA.

**Cubic (C-SA06-227).** (D′+nD)³=D′³+3nD′²D+3n²D′D²+n³D³. The symbolic difference from this polynomial is exactly0. Extracting a sign of D³ from eventual nonnegativity is legitimate only after that eventual nonnegativity has been independently supplied.

**Numerical finite freeness (C-SA06-203).** Fix smooth projective X and a Weil cohomology H over a characteristic-zero coefficient field. Choose finitely many complementary algebraic cycles β₁,…,β_r whose classes span the subspace of complementary algebraic classes. Send an integral codimension-i cycle z to the integer vector (deg(z·β_j))_j. If this vector is zero, its cohomological pairing with every complementary algebraic class is zero by linearity, so z is numerically trivial. Conversely numerical triviality clearly kills the vector. The numerical quotient embeds into Z^r and is therefore free of finite rank. This is an independent proof of the conclusion of Kleiman (1968, Theorem3.5), not a finite-generation claim for algebraic-equivalence quotients.

## 4. Lefschetz ranges and the limits of the analogies

**Five-lemma gap (C-SA06-236).** Compare the exact rows 0→Q→Q→0→0 and 0→Q→Q²→Q→0, with the left vertical identity, middle map x↦(x,0), and right map0→Q. The J map is bijective and the N map injective, but the A map is not surjective. Finite errors and rationalization cannot repair this counterexample. No geometric map is being refuted solely by this abstract model.

**K3 tests (C-SA06-238,240,241).** Let X be a complex projective K3 surface and Y a smooth ample curve. If CH0(Y)_Q→CH0(X)_Q were surjective, all degree-zero cycles on X would be parameterized by a fixed Jacobian and thus be finite-dimensional in Mumford's sense. Mumford (1969) rules this out because h⁰(Ω²_X)=1. Also Pic⁰(X)=0 while CH0⁰(X) is nonzero, refuting the proposed shifted surjectivity on algebraically trivial groups. Finally Beauville–Voisin (2004, Theorem1) puts all divisor products in Zc_X. Hence L:Pic(X)_Q→CH0(X)_Q is not surjective, even though n=2,i=1,p=1 meets the manuscript's stated whole-Chow threshold.

**Modulo algebraic equivalence (C-SA06-239).** A very general smooth quintic threefold has finite-rank NS. Clemens (1983, Theorem0.2), together with the vanishing of the algebraic Abel–Jacobi image cited in that paper, supplies an infinite-dimensional rational Griffiths group in CH²/alg. Therefore the image of NS_Q under L cannot exhaust CH²/alg_Q. This counterexample addresses the quotient by algebraic equivalence, not only the rational-equivalence group.

**Localization (C-SA06-232,242).** For a smooth divisor Y in X, codimension-indexed Chow localization is CH^(i−1)(Y)→CH^i(X)→CH^i(X−Y)→0. The shift is forced by the dimension of a pushed cycle. If Y is a principal divisor, its class kills pullbacks by the self-intersection formula; it does not imply that every cycle on Y is such a pullback. Vanishing of all Gysin pushforwards needs an additional hypothesis.

**Cone and primitive exponent (C-SA06-243).** The punctured affine cone of an n-fold, with its standard line-bundle construction, has dimension n+1. Removing the zero section of the corresponding line bundle gives CH^i(U)=coker(L:CH^(i−1)(X)→CH^i(X)). If a Lefschetz decomposition is available in the chosen realization, the primitive kernel in H^(2i) is ker L^(n−2i+1), whereas the hard Lefschetz isomorphism uses L^(n−2i). The script checks these distinct exponents in sample dimensions, and the graded degree calculation proves the distinction generally. No such decomposition for the whole Chow ring is inferred.

**Cohomology is the verified comparison, not the cycle theorem.** Deligne (1980, Theorem4.1.1, printedp217) assumes smooth projective pure n-dimensional X over algebraically closed k, ℓ≠char(k), and an ample bundle. After a stated Tate trivialization, L^i:H^(n−i)(X,Qℓ)→H^(n+i)(X,Qℓ) is an isomorphism. Formula4.1.3 on p218 identifies the image in H^(n−1) of a smooth Lefschetz-pencil fibre with the monodromy invariants. Both formula pages were rendered and read. Neither result supplies the manuscript's integral Chow complement or finite Chow errors.

## 5. Algebraic families, Albanese and boundedness

**Rectangle (C-SA06-248–249).** For points on connected smooth projective varieties over an algebraically closed field, ([x]−[x₀])×([y]−[y₀]) is algebraically trivial simply by varying x in a connected family. This cannot force rational equivalence or injectivity of the Albanese map. The stronger rational rectangle statement fails on a product of elliptic curves: it would force the zero-cycle group to be its finite-dimensional Albanese quotient, contradicting Mumford1969. Grothendieck's letter to Mumford of20November1968, Letter46 p151, explicitly recognizes this failure. Under the rational rectangle hypothesis for X×X and its appropriate functorial versions, the map δ:x↦[x]−[x₀] is additive on the Albanese parameter and would give an inverse to the Albanese quotient; this is a conditional argument, not a universal proof. Kahn2021 explains a related pitfall: a family-induced map to a Chow group can be merely a set map although its composite with a regular homomorphism is a group homomorphism.

**Quotients and points (C-SA06-253).** The fppf quotient G_m/μ₂ over R is G_m under squaring. On real points its image consists only of positive real numbers. Thus a sheaf quotient cannot be replaced by the naive quotient of point sets. This elementary example is the exact obstruction needed for the scan128 marginal caution.

**Bounded K-classes (C-SA06-251).** If F,G are locally free quotients of a common Q=O(−n)^N, let R=ker(Q→G). Because G is locally free, R is locally free. In K0, ξ=[F]−[G] implies ξ+[Q]=[F⊕R]. Boundedness of the possible G and their quotients makes the kernels R bounded (Grothendieck1961, Proposition1.2(i)). It therefore supplies a second common twist and number of generators for all F⊕R. It need not be the same n,N as before. Conversely fixed common presentations with finitely many Hilbert polynomials form bounded families by that paper's Theorem2.1.

**Cycles and K0 (C-SA06-252).** For an integral codimension-i subvariety Z of smooth X, the leading Chern character is ch_i(O_Z)=[Z]. The Newton identities with lower Chern terms zero imply c_i(O_Z)=(−1)^(i−1)(i−1)![Z]. The script records the sign and factorial for i=1,…,7. This explains the factorial in scan121; a sign can be absorbed into a virtual representative only if explicitly stated. The undefined manuscript K-equivalence prevents an unconditional integral reconstruction of all five conditions. Scan123 continues scan121 and states the weaker chain (ii)⇒(i)⇔(iv)⇒(iii)⇒(v); it is not an isolated unidentified theorem.

**Generic pencil (C-SA06-254).** In the usual pencil with smooth codimension2 centre C, the blow-up formula writes every Chow class on X′ as f*α plus an exceptional class from CH(C). A class on the generic fibre extends after shrinking the base, and localization lifts it to X′. If the base is rational, surjectivity CH(C)→CH(C_{k(S)}) follows by taking a dense open in affine space, homotopy invariance and localization, then passing to the generic point. Restriction therefore proves the generation by X and C in this precise pencil situation. The more general j in the detached diagram has incompatible possible codimension readings; the run does not promote it to a general theorem. The written u_y=u means descent of a class, not literal equality on different schemes.

## 6. Foundational sheets and earlier-run constructions

**Square-zero lifts.** If I²=0 and v₁,v₂:B→E lift the same A-map to E/I, D=v₂−v₁ has values in I. Expanding v₂(bb′) gives D(bb′)=v₁(b)D(b′)+v₁(b′)D(b); the quadratic term vanishes. Conversely v₁+D is a lift for every A-derivation. The displayed domain A of v₁ on scan99 does not type-check this calculation. EGA0_IV Proposition20.1.1 and Corollary20.1.3 are the exact published torsor statement.

**Polynomial group law on A¹.** For a polynomial associative law F over a field with two-sided unit0, let m be its degree in the first variable. Associativity forces m²=m by comparing degrees in the outermost first variable; leading coefficients cannot cancel in the polynomial domain. Similarly the degree in the second variable is1. The unit identities therefore give F(x,y)=x+y+axy. The inverse is −x/(1+ax), polynomial only if a=0. The code checks associativity and this substitution; the degree argument is the general proof. No assertion over nonreduced base rings is made.

**Cancelled cohomological-dimension bound.** Under the visible literal assumptions, A=R[[t]], f=t gives dimension1 and U=Spec R((t)), whose2-cohomological dimension is infinite. A real closure gives an order2 subgroup of the absolute Galois group. In the periodic C₂-resolution over F₂ both differentials act by0, so H^q(C₂,F₂)=F₂ for everyq. The code records sample degrees0–12; periodicity proves all degrees. Adding strictly local and prime-to-characteristic hypotheses changes the question; it does not un-cancel or identify the sheet.

**Torus norm.** After a finite separable splitting extension L/k of degree d, the norm map Res_L/k(T_L)→T composed with the diagonal inclusion is [d]. On character lattices the latter is dI, of full rank. Hence it is dominant and the norm is dominant, proving unirationality from a rational restriction of scalars. In characteristic dividingd its derivative can vanish, so differential surjectivity is not the proof. This matches SGA3 XIII Lemma3.4.

**Earlier-run sl₂ proposal.** On a Lefschetz string v₀,…,v_r, put e v_j=v_(j+1), h v_j=(2j−r)v_j and f v_j=j(r−j+1)v_(j−1). Direct multiplication gives [e,f]=h, [h,e]=2e and [h,f]=−2f. Replacing every lowering coefficient by1 fails as soon as r≥2. The code checks r=1,…,8, and these formulas prove the identities generally. This operator action on the cohomology of one object is not a tensor automorphism of every object: a tensor automorphism fixes the unit, whereas e sends the degree-zero unit of the P¹ string to its hyperplane class. Tate compatibility reads (g_target⊗χ(g))L=Lg_source, so after trivialization conjugation scales L byχ(g)⁻¹. These are checks of earlier-run ideas, not recovered manuscript assertions.

**Other earlier-run checks.** The map0→Q/Z becomes an isomorphism after tensoring with Q but has infinite cokernel; rational isomorphism does not mean finite error. A nodal rational cubic has the homotopy type S²∨S¹; its one-dimensional H¹ admits no nondegenerate alternating pairing, illustrating the need to replace smooth Poincaré duality in a singular correspondence formalism. Neither example settles the manuscript's unconstructed local theory. The centre of G_m is nontrivial although its characteristic-zero representation category is semisimple; reductivity is not centre-freeness. The elementary Kummer action at a tame cusp gives conjugation by the cyclotomic character, but supplies no converse recognizing a procyclic subgroup from its normalizer.

**Cofinal Čech comparison (C-SA06-209).** Suppose u:C→C′ preserves the fibre products used by a covering family and source covering families map to a cofinal system of covers of u(U). For a target sheaf F, the Čech cochain groups of the pulled-back family with coefficients u*F are exactly the cochain groups of its image family with coefficients F, since each iterated intersection is preserved. The differentials are the same alternating restriction maps. Taking the filtered colimit over covers therefore gives identical Čech groups, because the image system is cofinal. Apply SGA4 V Proposition4.3/Corollary4.5 under their site and acyclicity hypotheses. This supplies the stated preservation result. Continuity without the two extra conditions does not justify either the cochain identification or the cofinal colimit.

**Zero-cycles modulo algebraic equivalence (C-SA06-283).** On a connected smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field, any two closed points can be connected by a chain of integral curves (for a projective embedding, use sufficiently ample connected sections, and normalize curve components as needed). On each connected curve, moving its point gives an algebraic family of degree-one zero-cycles, so the difference of the endpoints is algebraically equivalent to zero. Thus every degree-zero zero-cycle is algebraically trivial. Degree is preserved by algebraic equivalence, and an algebraically closed field supplies a degree-one point, hence CH0 modulo algebraic equivalence is Z. This says nothing about rational equivalence or the Albanese kernel.
