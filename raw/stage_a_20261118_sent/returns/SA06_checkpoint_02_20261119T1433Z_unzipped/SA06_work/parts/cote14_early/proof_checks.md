## 1. Scope and conventions

The motivic fragment begins in the middle of a list of structures. The category \(M\), the full content of structures \(1^\circ\)–\(3^\circ\), and the admissible set \(I(G)\) are not recovered by replacing them with a modern category. Claims C-SA06-1, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 20 and 53 therefore retain the missing definitions in their status. A modern comparison can show that a formal mechanism exists; it cannot identify an absent quantifier.

For the Albanese arguments, the main reconstructed setting is an algebraically closed field \(k\), smooth projective connected varieties when properness is needed, integral Chow groups, and integral correspondences. Smooth quasi-projective targets are admitted in the definition where the source text does so. The projective parameter varieties remain essential. Write
\[
N^i_{\rm Alb}(X)=
\left\langle Z_*\nu:
 T\ {\rm smooth,\ projective,\ connected},\
 Z\in CH^i(X\times T),\
 \deg\nu=0,\ {\rm alb}_T(\nu)=0
\right\rangle.
\]
Then \(A^i_{\rm Alb}(X)=CH^i(X)/N^i_{\rm Alb}(X)\), and \(P^i(X)\) is the image of \(CH^i_{\rm alg}(X)\). This is the manuscript's image-generated relation. Samuel's common kernels of regular homomorphisms and Saito's common kernels of divisor-valued correspondence actions are different definitions until comparison is proved.

The rationalized group \(CH^i(X)/{\sim_{\rm alg}}\otimes\mathbb Q\) in the 1965 letter is also different from the vector space of algebraic cohomology classes in Grothendieck's published standard conjectures. This difference is decisive in §11.

## 2. Effectivity, tori and character tests

**Claims C-SA06-2–8, 11, 14–19; bounded observations concerning 1, 9, 10, 12, 13 and 20.**

Let \(i:\mathbb G_m\to G_{\mathbb C}\) and decompose a finite-dimensional representation \(V=\bigoplus_aV_a\). A subrepresentation inherits these weights, a direct sum takes their union, and a tensor product has weights \(a+b\). Hence nonnegative-weight objects are closed under the operations asserted on scan 5. If a distinguished one-dimensional object has \(i\)-weight \(m>0\), twisting \(N\) times changes a weight \(a\) to \(a+Nm\). Since the weight set is finite, one \(N\) makes them all nonnegative. This proves C-SA06-2; computation T01 records the exact formula.

If \(i'=gig^{-1}\), then \(\rho(g)V_a\) is the \(a\)-weight space for \(i'\). This proves inner-conjugation invariance. Field automorphisms require the separate descent condition; they cannot be treated as inner conjugation.

An intersection of such subcategories retains the three closure properties, but can lose eventual effectivity. Take \(G=\mathbb G_m^2\), characters \(x,y\), Tate character \(x\), and \(i_m(t)=(t,t^m)\) for every integer \(m\). For a fixed twist \(N\), the character \(yx^N\) has \(i_m\)-weight \(m+N\); \(m=-N-1\) makes it negative. Each individual subcategory has the twist property. Their intersection does not. This is a counterexample to an unrestricted intersection assertion, not a claim that these data satisfy the absent polarization axioms. T02 checks the displayed value \(-1\).

For a split torus the Weyl group is trivial. The cone generated by \((2,0),(1,1)\) is not fixed by the outer lattice automorphism \((u,v)\mapsto(u,-v)\): expressing \((1,-1)\) in those generators requires coefficient \(-1\) on the second generator (T03). This confirms the source's warning that Weyl invariance need not mean invariance under a larger automorphism group. Positivity cones also lose integral information: \(2\mathbb N\) and \(\mathbb N\) have the same nonnegative dual half-line. That observation does not decide the manuscript's undefined saturation condition.

For a finite-rank torus lattice \(M\), continuity of Galois action and a finite basis give an open subgroup fixing the whole basis. The image is finite. The analogous assertion fails for a protorus built from infinitely many independently split quadratic tori: its total image can be infinite. For a connected reductive finite-dimensional group over an algebraically closed field, every cocharacter is conjugate into a maximal torus and conjugacy there is measured by the Weyl group. These are the precise finite-dimensional facts used in C-SA06-6.

Applying the rational character \(\epsilon\) to \(i_1\bar i_1=j\) and \(\epsilon i_1={\rm id}\) gives \(\epsilon j(t)=t^2\). It fixes the effective Tate weight \(+2\) in this convention (T04). It does not reconstruct the remaining axioms.

For a torus \(T/F\) in characteristic zero, Milne (2004, Proposition 15.6, p. 122; key `Milne2004Shimura`) identifies simple representations with Galois orbits in \(X^*(T)\). The character map therefore identifies the representation ring with the invariant group ring \(\mathbb Z[M]^\Gamma\). If the action factors through a finite group \(\pi\), this invariant ring is finitely generated over \(\mathbb Z\). Indeed, choose finitely many Laurent generators of \(\mathbb Z[M]\); each satisfies its monic orbit polynomial. Let \(B\) be the \(\mathbb Z\)-algebra generated by all coefficients of these finitely many polynomials. Then \(B\) is noetherian, \(\mathbb Z[M]\) is finite over \(B\), and the invariant subring is a \(B\)-submodule of that finite module. It is consequently finite over \(B\). This proof needs neither division by \(|\pi|\) nor characteristic-zero averaging over \(\mathbb Z\).

The compact-kernel assertion is used only under the polarized Tate-triple hypotheses of Deligne and Milne (1982, Theorem 5.16 and Remark 5.17, corrected text p. 53). For a torus, inner automorphisms are trivial, so a compact inner form of the Tate kernel is the kernel itself. The additional parity condition for neutralness remains separate. The crossed-out attempted proof and the scope of “Fausse” are not resolved.

Two elementary counterexamples separate positivity tests. The actual character \(x^{-1}\) is positive on \(\mathbb R_{>0}\), although its degree relative to the standard cocharacter is negative. The virtual character \(x^2-x+1=(x-\frac12)^2+\frac34\) is strictly positive there but has a negative multiplicity. By contrast, a finite Laurent polynomial with nonzero lowest coefficient and lowest exponent \(a<0\) is unbounded as \(t\to0^+\). Thus boundedness characterizes nonnegative exponents after restriction to a *specified* cocharacter. T05–T06 record the identities.

Changing fibre functor transports the Tannakian group by its isomorphism torsor (Deligne & Milne, 1982, Theorem 3.2). A conjugation-invariant and descent-compatible condition on cocharacters transports locally and glues. This proves the conditional form C-SA06-11. It does not prove that the missing definition of \(I(G)\) has that property, or that motivic effectivity is always given by one cocharacter.

Finally, unrestricted fixed-weight finiteness cannot count all objects: finite direct sums of one nonzero object already give infinitely many isomorphism classes. The real-torus example \( \mathbb G_m\times U(1)^2\), with the chosen cocharacter ignoring the second compact factor, likewise supplies infinitely many irreducibles of the same weight and cocharacter degree. It violates an additional weight-zero restriction suggested elsewhere in the fragment, so it is not a counterexample to the fully unspecified motivic axioms. These are scope tests, not resolutions of C-SA06-20.

## 3. Earlier-run Lefschetz and Tannakian comparisons

**Items SA06-21 and SA06-29–39. These are explicitly earlier-run ideas, not new claims attributed to these manuscript pages.**

On a length-\(r\) string with basis \(v_0,\ldots,v_r\), set
\[
e v_j=v_{j+1},\quad
f v_j=j(r-j+1)v_{j-1},\quad
h v_j=(2j-r)v_j,
\]
with vectors outside the range zero. Direct coefficient subtraction gives
\([e,f]=h\), \([h,e]=2e\), \([h,f]=-2f\), and
\[
h^2+2h+4fe=r(r+2)\,{\rm id}.
\]
Checks L01–L04 redo the symbolic coefficients and all matrices for \(0\le r\le8\). Dividing \(f v_j\) by \(j(r-j+1)\) gives unit-step lowering on non-bottom vectors; division is over \(\mathbb Q\), not an integral assertion (L03). On \(\bigoplus_{r=0}^n\operatorname{Sym}^r\mathbb Q^2\), the distinct Casimir values separate the summands by polynomial interpolation. Inside each summand, the distinct eigenvalues of \(h\) isolate the weight lines, and powers of \(e,f\), with nonzero rational coefficients, give all matrix units. With primitive multiplicities the action remains the identity on those multiplicity spaces; it is not every endomorphism of the total cohomology.

For a smooth projective \(d\)-fold, \(h\) acts on \(H^r\) by \(r-d\). Hence the degree operator is \(h+d\), and the corresponding cocharacters agree only after the scalar \(t^d\) on this chosen object (L06). There is no object-independent universal equation with a varying dimension inserted.

Tensor naturality for \(L:V\otimes\mathbb L\to V'\) gives \(gLg^{-1}=\chi_{\mathbb L}(g)L\), with the convention for the Tate character stated. On \(h(\mathbb P^1)=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbb L\), \(g={\rm diag}(1,t)\) satisfies this relation (L05). The Tannaka group of this Tate subcategory is \(\mathbb G_m\), so a nontrivial \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) acting on the two cohomological degrees cannot be inserted as a subgroup of that motivic group. This is the normalizer/centralizer distinction tested by the earlier run.

For \(g\in GL_2\), \(\theta(g)=\det(g)^{-1}g\) fixes \(SL_2\) pointwise and sends the determinant character to its inverse (T07). It is therefore relevant only if the distinguished character is allowed to change. It does not decide whether the manuscript glyph is \(SL\) or \(GL\).

The other comparisons were checked at their exact sources:

- Künnemann (1993, Theorems 5.1–5.2, p. 96) supplies rational Chow-motivic Lefschetz isomorphisms for abelian schemes under the stated base hypotheses; it is not a theorem for all varieties or an integral lowering theorem.
- Choudhury and Gallauer (2017, Theorem 9.1) identifies Nori's group with the degree-zero stable Ayoub Betti Hopf algebra over its coefficient PID and a field embedded in \(\mathbb C\); it does not construct a general motivic \(t\)-structure.
- Arapura (2013, Theorem 6.4.1) and Huber and Müller-Stach (2017, Theorems 10.2.7–10.2.8) distinguish pure motivated/Nori semisimplicity from the mixed pro-unipotent extension.
- A nontrivial mixed example is \(H^1(\mathbb G_m,\{1,2\})\): the cohomological exact sequence has subobject \(\mathbb Q(0)\) and quotient \(\mathbb Q(-1)\); after twisting, its Hodge extension is \(\log 2\) modulo \(2\pi i\mathbb Q\). Since \(\log2\) is nonzero real, it is not in that imaginary rational lattice. The period matrix is printed in Huber and Müller-Stach, §14.1, pp. 301–302.
- The Nori arithmetic exact sequence (Huber & Müller-Stach, Theorem 9.1.16; Kahn, 2025, Theorem 5.10 and §10) does not establish geometric connectedness. Remark 9.5.7 and Conjecture 13.2.1 keep that boundary explicit.
- The algebra quotient \(\mathbb Q[u]/(u^2)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Q\) is a counterexample to inferring that an arbitrary representation of the former factors through the semisimple latter. It is an algebraic warning, not a geometric counterexample to standard conjectures.

## 4. The sign computation

**C-SA06-21; items SA06-40–41.**

On \(E\times E\), let \(\Delta\) be the diagonal and \(F_1=E\times0,\ F_2=0\times E\). Each selfintersection is zero: the normal bundles of the fibres and the tangent bundle of the elliptic diagonal are trivial. Each distinct pair has intersection degree one. For \(\Theta_0=\Delta-F_1-F_2\),
\[
\deg(\Theta_0^2)=-2.
\]
The matrix with diagonal zero and off-diagonal one gives exactly this result for vector \((1,-1,-1)\) (I01–I02).

Set \(Y=E\times P\), with \(P\) a smooth projective curve, and choose a line bundle \(L\) on \(Y\). Realize \(Y\) as the zero section of a projective completion of its line bundle, with normal bundle \(L\). This gives a smooth projective threefold \(X\). On \(E\times X\), write \(z=(1\times i)_*(\Theta_0\times P)\). The selfintersection formula yields
\[
z^2=(1\times i)_*\bigl((\Theta_0\times P)^2\cdot {\rm pr}_Y^*c_1(L)\bigr).
\]
Taking degree gives \(-2d\), where \(d=\deg(L|_{\{0\}\times P})\). Taking \(L={\rm pr}_P^*\mathcal O_P(p)\) and its inverse gives respectively \(-2\) and \(+2\) (I03). The extra \(E\)-factor in the transcribed support formula is a type alarm recorded in `readings.tsv`. This is a valid sign test for the displayed unqualified pairing; no primitive Hodge-sign positivity theorem is refuted.

## 5. The image-generated Albanese relation

**C-SA06-22–29 and 31–38; Sorites crosschecks C-SA06-78–82.**

A degree-zero cycle on a connected smooth projective parameter is algebraically trivial. Correspondences preserve algebraic equivalence, so every generator of \(N_{\rm Alb}\) is algebraically trivial. Divisors of rational functions are generated by differences of fibres over \(\mathbb P^1\), whose Albanese variety is zero. Thus rational equivalence is contained in the relation on raw cycles.

For two generators \(Z_*\nu\) and \(Z'_*\nu'\), choose parameter basepoints \(t_0,t'_0\). On \(T\times T'\), the cycle
\[
\widetilde\nu=\nu\times[t'_0]+[t_0]\times\nu'
\]
has degree and Albanese image zero. Pulling the two correspondences to the product and adding them gives their sum, because the unwanted cross terms are multiplied by \(\deg\nu\) or \(\deg\nu'\). This checks the single-presentation assertion rather than merely defining a generated subgroup.

In Chow theory, multiplication by \(c\in CH^*(X)\) sends a generator to
\[
c\cdot Z_*\nu=(Z\cdot{\rm pr}_X^*c)_*\nu.
\]
It therefore preserves \(N_{\rm Alb}\). Pullback between smooth varieties is interpreted by the refined intersection operation, which is compatible with this composition; proper pushforward is direct composition of correspondences. The source's raw-cycle assertion about simultaneous good position remains a separate geometric statement. The Stacks Project, Lemma 43.24.3, provides simultaneous moving against a finite collection on a smooth projective variety. That does not silently extend every raw formula to arbitrary base schemes.

For zero-cycles on a smooth projective \(X\), the diagonal shows
\(\ker({\rm alb}_X)\subset N^{\dim X}_{\rm Alb}(X)\). Conversely a correspondence acting on zero-cycles induces a homomorphism of Albanese varieties, hence preserves their kernels. The source's later Sorites, Corollary 1.2 on scan 52, proves exactly this missing input. The quotient retains degree; only its degree-zero part is \({\rm Alb}_X(k)\).

For divisors on smooth projective \(X\), a divisor correspondence on \(X\times T\) gives a morphism \(T\to {\rm Pic}(X)\); after normalization it factors through \({\rm Alb}_T\to{\rm Pic}^0_{\rm red}(X)\). Hence it kills the parameter Albanese kernel. Thus \(N^1_{\rm Alb}(X)=0\) in \(CH^1(X)\); on raw divisors the relation is linear equivalence. This is the endpoint needed in §8.

Let \(z=Z_{t_1}-Z_{t_0}\) and \(w=W_{s_1}-W_{s_0}\) be algebraically trivial classes, using projective smooth curve or abelian parameters. The product family is evaluated on
\[
[t_1,s_1]-[t_1,s_0]-[t_0,s_1]+[t_0,s_0].
\]
Its degree and its Albanese coordinates both vanish (A01–A02), since
\({\rm Alb}(T\times S)={\rm Alb}(T)\times{\rm Alb}(S)\). Hence the product lies in \(N_{\rm Alb}\). Applying the diagonal yields \(P^*(X)^2=0\). Projective parameterization is valid over algebraically closed fields; Achter, Casalaina-Martin, and Vial (2019, Theorem 1 and Proposition 3.14) give a checked modern formulation and cite the earlier classical result.

The same parameterization by an abelian variety and surjectivity of multiplication by every positive integer on its \(k\)-points show that \(P^i(X)\) is divisible. If \(w\) is \(\tau\)-trivial, choose \(m>0\) with \(mw\) algebraically trivial and write \(z=mz_0\) for an algebraically trivial \(z\). Then
\[
zw=z_0(mw)=0.
\]
This verifies C-SA06-38 (A09). It does not replace \(\tau\)-triviality by numerical triviality; C-SA06-32 remains a separate question.

If a cycle \(D\) on \(X\times Y\) is algebraically trivial, the product of \(D\) with a degree-zero parameter cycle is zero modulo \(N_{\rm Alb}\) by the same argument, and pushing forward gives zero. Divisibility permits the \(\tau\)-trivial extension. This checks the dependence only on the \(\tau\)-class asserted on scan 31 and proved later on scan 34.

## 6. Rational equivalence, box cycles and the exact negative answer

**C-SA06-30; MP-SA06-13.**

On \(E_1\times E_2\), the box cycles
\[
[p,q]-[p,0]-[0,q]+[0,0]
\]
have degree zero and Albanese image zero. Modulo their span, every point cycle is a sum of cycles on the two axes and a degree term. On each elliptic axis, degree-zero \(CH_0\) is the elliptic curve itself. Consequently these boxes generate the Albanese kernel.

Mumford's theorem applies because \(E_1\times E_2\) is a smooth projective complex surface with a nonzero holomorphic two-form. If its Albanese kernel were zero, its zero-cycles would be parameterized by the finite-dimensional Albanese data, contrary to that theorem. Some box is therefore nonzero modulo rational equivalence. The argument does not assert that every box, or any particular pair of points in the source, is nonzero.

This is also a documented historical answer, rather than merely a modern inference: Grothendieck's letter to Mumford of 20 November 1968 explicitly draws the negative conclusion for Samuel's box-cycle question on two elliptic curves (Letter 46, p. 151, key `Grothendieck1968LetterMumford`). The private letter date, Mumford's communicated date of 28 November 1968, and the 1969 journal publication remain separate.

## 7. Algebraic families, denominators and universal cycles

**C-SA06-33, 35, 39–45, 89, 91 and 97.**

A cycle action factors through \(CH_0(Y)/\ker(\deg,{\rm alb})\) by the very definition of \(N_{\rm Alb}\). Restricting to degree zero gives \(u_Z:{\rm Alb}_Y(k)\to P^i(X)\). A miniversal cycle on \(Y\times{\rm Alb}_Y\) induces multiplication by some positive integer \(n\). Composing correspondences therefore realizes \(nu_Z\) by a family on the Albanese variety. Achter et al. (2023, Lemma 4.7) states the required multiple construction. It does not say \(n=1\).

The definition of an algebraic family has two parts: factorization through the graded Albanese group, and an integral multiple represented by an integral cycle. Inside the group of already factored maps, saturation is immediate: if a multiple \(m\phi\) is represented after another multiple \(n\), then \(mn\phi\) is represented. For arbitrary point maps the first part can fail. Let \(X=E/\mathbb C\), take a nonzero \(t\in E[2]\), and define a nonconstant set map \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)\to\{0,t\}\). Its double is zero, but it cannot factor through \({\rm Alb}_{\mathbb P^1}=0\) as a normalized family. This refutes only the unrestricted reading of C-SA06-40; the corrected assertion is C-SA06-91.

Addition and pullback of families follow by adding cycles and composing the induced Albanese maps. The constant/normalized decomposition uses a basepoint \(y_0\): subtract the value at \(y_0\), and the remainder factors through the degree-zero Albanese. Composition of algebraic maps is checked by multiplying their two denominators and composing the two integral correspondences.

Rational correspondences need not act on the integral point groups. In particular there is no group homomorphism \(h:E(\mathbb C)\to E(\mathbb C)\) with \(2h={\rm id}\): for a nonzero point \(t\) of order two, homomorphicity gives \(2h(t)=h(2t)=0\), contradicting \(2h(t)=t\). A08 checks the finite torsion obstruction. Thus dividing a cycle by two is not a canonical operation on these integral groups.

For represented abelian varieties, an isogeny has an inverse in \({\rm Hom}\otimes\mathbb Q\). This checks only the represented version of the source's inverse-map question. The general abstract \(P\)-group need not already be an abelian variety.

The first denominator question in scan 51 is related precisely to an integral universal zero-cycle: Voisin (2025, §2, pp. 890–891) identifies the relevant integral cohomological inverse component with such a cycle, using torsion-free homology conventions. Her Theorem 1.4 supplies a published threefold counterexample even with representable \(CH_0\). Alexandrou (2026, Theorem 1.3) supplies surface examples for the specified elliptic Albanese varieties, as a checked preprint result. Neither source prevents a positive multiple. Neither automatically settles the distinct top-odd Picard inverse. The apparently reversed composition in Voisin's displayed equation (2.2) is interpreted using the domains and the correctly ordered subsequent text, not repeated as a well-typed identity.

## 8. Transposition: an integral abelian-source case proved here

**C-SA06-46–47 and the limited part of C-SA06-97; MP-SA06-18.**

Assume \(k\) algebraically closed, \(X\) smooth projective of dimension \(n\), \(A\) an abelian variety, and \(Z\in CH^i(X\times A)\) integral. Normalize its family by \(Z_a-Z_0\). Suppose this family vanishes in \(P^i(X)\) for every \(a\in A(k)\).

Let \(j=n+1-i\), and take an algebraically trivial \(w\in CH^j(X)\). It can be written \(W_t-W_{t_0}\), with \(T\) smooth projective connected and \(W\in CH^j(X\times T)\). Compose the two correspondences to obtain
\[
D=({\rm pr}_{A,T})_*
  \bigl({\rm pr}_{X,A}^*Z\cdot{\rm pr}_{X,T}^*W\bigr)
 \in CH^1(A\times T),
\]
since \(i+j-n=1\) (A05). For every \(a\),
\[
D_a-D_0={}^{t}W_*(Z_a-Z_0).
\]
The expression on the right belongs to \(N^1_{\rm Alb}(T)\), because correspondences preserve the defining relation. By §5, that subgroup is zero in \(CH^1(T)\). Thus all these line-bundle fibres are isomorphic.

Normalize \(D\) by subtracting the pullback of its fibre at \(0\). The seesaw principle says the resulting line bundle is pulled back from \(A\). Equivalently, \(D\) is a sum of pullbacks from \(A\) and \(T\). Its restrictions at \(t\) and \(t_0\), viewed in \({\rm Pic}(A)\), are consequently equal. Therefore
\[
{}^{t}Z_*w=D^t-D^{t_0}=0 \quad\text{in }{\rm Pic}^0(A).
\]
This proves independence of the transpose for integral representatives with an abelian source. Applying the argument to the difference of two representatives proves the desired equality. The transpose already kills \(N_{\rm Alb}\) by correspondence functoriality, so it descends to the indicated \(P\)-quotient.

Three limitations are substantive. First, this proof does not show a pairing on arbitrary \(P\)-groups is nondegenerate. Second, a homomorphism defined only by saying its multiple has an integral representative need not allow division of the transposed point-group map. Third, the full announced bijection in C-SA06-97 is stronger than representative-independence. Saito (1979, Theorem 4.4, Corollary 4.5 and Theorem 4.6) proves related duality and rational realization for the *incidence quotient*; no equality with \(N_{\rm Alb}\) has been inserted.

## 9. Forward odd-cohomology vanishing over the complex numbers

**C-SA06-48, with C-SA06-46, 49–52 and 102; MP-SA06-19–20.**

Continue with integral \(Z\), and now assume \(k=\mathbb C\). Every generator \(W_*\nu\) of \(N_{\rm Alb}(X)\) has Abel–Jacobi image zero. Indeed the Abel–Jacobi map on top-degree cycles of its smooth projective parameter \(T\) is the Albanese map, and Abel–Jacobi commutes with algebraic correspondences. Since \({\rm alb}_T(\nu)=0\), its image under \(W\) also has zero Abel–Jacobi invariant. Thus Abel–Jacobi on algebraically trivial cycles factors through \(P^i(X)\). This comparison was already suggested by the earlier run; the following scoped implication is the present check.

If \(u_Z=0\), the normalized algebraic family map
\[
A\longrightarrow J^i_{\rm alg}(X)
\]
is zero. Its rational Hodge-structure map
\[
H^{2g-1}(A,\mathbb Q)(g)
 \longrightarrow H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q)(i),\qquad g=\dim A,
\]
is then zero. To justify the last step, the source has Hodge types \((-1,0),(0,-1)\), so its image lies in the level-one sub-Hodge structure defining the algebraic intermediate Jacobian. The Jacobian functor is faithful on such rational weight-\(-1\) structures: a zero torus homomorphism has zero derivative and zero map on its rational lattice. Equivalently, a real Hodge image lying in both \(F^0\) and its conjugate is zero in weight \(-1\).

Poincaré duality identifies the adjoint with
\[
{}^tZ_*:
H^{2(n-i)+1}(X,\mathbb Q)(n-i)
 \longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathbb Q),
\]
so this transpose vanishes. Betti–étale comparison gives the stated \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) conclusion over \(\mathbb C\).

This proves the forward implication over \(\mathbb C\) in every codimension, not the arbitrary-characteristic statement. The cohomological degree is forced by the shift \(2i-2n\); the exponent \(n-i+1\) without the extra factor two does not generally land in degree one (A03–A04). The relevant curve Künneth terms have degrees \((N,0),(N-1,1),(N-2,2)\).

The standard pointed-curve projectors are \(c_0\times C\), \(\Delta-c_0\times C-C\times c_0\), and \(C\times c_0\), with order fixed by the convention for action. Their compositions give orthogonal idempotents summing to \(\Delta\); the genus-two cohomology matrices in A06 are only an illustrative exact test. A generating ample curve \(C\subset A\) gives a surjection \(J(C)\to A\). It does not give a surjection \(C(k)\to A(k)\) when \(\dim A>1\), because the curve is a proper closed subvariety. Both transcription alarms are retained.

For finitely generated \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-modules, \(f\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell=0\) is equivalent to the image of \(f\) being torsion, hence to zero on torsion-free quotients. This follows directly from localization: \(m/1=0\) exactly when some \(\ell^r m=0\) (Stacks, §10.9). It does not identify a torsion-free lattice with its rationalization. Accordingly, the rational vanishing converse cannot discard integral information.

Achter et al. (2023, Proposition 8.2, pp. 119–120) gives a separate precise positive-characteristic comparison: for a surjective regular homomorphism and a miniversal cycle of degree \(r\), the Tate-module map is injective for \(i=1,2,\dim X\) and \(\ell\nmid pr\); endpoint algebraic representatives give isomorphisms. Its hypotheses and its quotient have not been replaced by the manuscript's abstract \(P\)-group.

## 10. Tate's trick and the elliptic test

**C-SA06-53–55.**

The manuscript's undefined \(\mathfrak g(k)\) and “pseudoalgebraic” condition prevent an exact general theorem from being assigned to C-SA06-53. The source states an invariant-generation strategy, with two alternative hypotheses; the alternatives must not be merged.

For a finite-field elliptic curve \(E\), the claim about every power \(E^r\) is the classical assertion that all rational Tate classes over the algebraic closure are generated by divisors. “Tate” here means fixed after a finite field extension. Tate's 1966 main theorem proves the divisor comparison, and Milne (2001, Appendix A, Theorem A.3) supplies the checked Lefschetz/Tate-group criterion for the elliptic-power assertion. This does not extend by wording alone to arbitrary products of unrelated abelian varieties.

The displayed root-of-unity gloss cannot hold for all elliptic curves. On
\[
E/\mathbb F_5:\ y^2=x^3+x+1
\]
the discriminant is nonzero modulo \(5\), and exact enumeration gives nine points including infinity, so the Frobenius trace is \(-3\). If both eigenvalues divided by \(\sqrt5\) were roots of unity, their sum \(-3/\sqrt5\) would be an algebraic integer. Its square is \(9/5\), a rational noninteger, impossible for an algebraic integer. Thus at least one normalized eigenvalue is not a root of unity (F01–F04). The two-eigenspace alternative is not refuted. The point count and nonsingularity, not merely a remembered “ordinary” label, verify the example.

The July–October 2026 literature search found broad new source claims about CM Hodge and finite-field Tate conjectures. The OpenAI preprint's exact theorem statements, reduction outline and consequences were opened; its central theta construction was not independently verified. It is therefore recorded as a source claim, not used to upgrade this manuscript's general assertion. The Broe preprint could not be opened at its primary endpoint and remains an unverified bibliographic lead. Neither is needed for the elliptic-power case just checked.

## 11. The 1965 draft conjectures and their failure modulo algebraic equivalence

**C-SA06-56–65 and 92–96; especially MP-SA06-22–26.**

The supplied continuation fixes the text of the letter of 27 August 1965: scan 44 ends mid-sentence and scans 45–47 carry its typed pages 2–4 and signature. The group \(C^i\) is explicitly cycles modulo algebraic equivalence with rational coefficients. The following counterexamples use precisely that relation.

Take a very general smooth quintic threefold \(X\subset\mathbb P^4_\mathbb C\). It is smooth, projective, connected and three-dimensional. Lefschetz gives \({\rm Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z H\), while Clemens (1983, Theorem 0.2 and Corollary 0.3, pp. 19–20) gives an infinite-dimensional rational Griffiths quotient in codimension two. The very-general qualifier is retained.

For draft \(A\), \(i=1,n=3\), multiplication by \(H\) has image \(\mathbb Q H^2\). A nonzero rational Griffiths class is homologically zero but nonzero modulo algebraic equivalence. It cannot equal \(\lambda H^2\): intersecting its cohomology class with \(H\) gives \(0=5\lambda\), hence \(\lambda=0\) (S01–S02). Thus \(C^1(X)\to C^2(X)\) is not surjective. Every hypothesis of the draft is met.

For \(A'\), take any smooth hyperplane surface \(Y\subset X\). Here \(i=1\) and \(2i=n-1\). Its divisor classes modulo algebraic equivalence form \(NS(Y)\), a finitely generated group (SGA 6, XIII, Theorem 5.1). Their rational image in \(C^2(X)\) is finite-dimensional. The target contains the infinite-dimensional Griffiths quotient. Hence the stated pushforward cannot be surjective.

For \(A''\), put \(U=X\setminus Y\). The complement of the hyperplane section is affine, smooth and three-dimensional. Localization for Chow groups gives
\[
CH^1(Y)\longrightarrow CH^2(X)\longrightarrow CH^2(U)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This remains right-exact after quotienting by algebraic equivalence. To check the potentially nonformal point, an algebraic-equivalence relation on \(U\) can be represented by a cycle on \(U\times T\) with a smooth connected curve parameter. Its closure in \(X\times T\) gives a Chow class whose Gysin fibres restrict to the original fibres. Their difference is algebraically trivial on \(X\). Thus every algebraically trivial class on \(U\) lifts as required; no assertion about flatness of the raw closure is needed.

The image from \(CH^1(Y)\) factors through \(NS(Y)\) modulo algebraic equivalence on \(X\), and is finite-dimensional over \(\mathbb Q\). Quotienting an infinite-dimensional \(C^2(X)\) by that image remains nonzero, indeed infinite-dimensional. Therefore \(C^2(U)\ne0\), although \(2\cdot2>3\). This also disproves the suggested extension to all smooth affine varieties. It does not contradict affine cohomological vanishing: the rational degree-four cohomology of this complex affine threefold vanishes in the appropriate comparison, so the cycle-equivalence assertion is strictly stronger.

For the draft \(B\), these quintic arguments alone are insufficient. \(B\) concerns primitive middle cycles on an even-dimensional variety. A codimension-two Griffiths class on a threefold is not such a primitive middle class. No unchecked product or suspension argument is used to declare \(B\) false. The general stronger positivity formulation remains bounded at exactly this missing hypothesis check.

Grothendieck's published paper (1969, §§3–4, pp. 196–199) uses algebraic cohomology classes for the Lefschetz and Hodge-type conjectures. Its implication from algebraic Lefschetz operators to Künneth projectors, trace/integrality results, and numerical–homological comparison is the exact later match for several consequences. The published paper does not make algebraic equivalence equal to homological equivalence. The 1965 letter itself retracts an earlier claim of equivalence between \(A\) and Künneth algebraicity; only the asserted implication is retained.

Weak Lefschetz was already available in the letter's setting (Artin, SGA 4, XIV, Corollaries 3.2–3.3). Deligne's later hard Lefschetz theorem (1980, Theorem 4.1.1) proves the cohomological theorem. Deligne's 1974 Theorem 1.6 proves the finite-field eigenvalue assertion. Neither repairs the stronger cycle quotient used in the draft.

The image-membership argument C-SA06-95 is valid under its specified categorical hypotheses. In a semisimple category, the image of \(f:M\to N\) is a direct summand with projector \(p\). An algebraic class is a morphism \(\mathbf1\to N(i)\). A faithful realization detects whether \((1-p)\) kills it. If it does, the class factors through the image summand, and the split epimorphism \(M\to{\rm im}(f)\) lifts it. This is the precise categorical reason for the statement. It is not a proof of the premise for the draft's false equivalence relation.

Likewise, Jannsen's semisimplicity theorem applies to numerical motives with characteristic-zero coefficients. Passing from that category to Galois semisimplicity requires an exact faithful realization with the appropriate fullness or motivic-group comparison and closure conditions. A semisimple algebra of correspondences does not by itself imply that the entire Galois representation is semisimple. The letter abbreviates this bridge; C-SA06-96 retains it as conditional.

## 12. Sorites: Picard, Albanese and numerical dependence

**C-SA06-77–90 and 101.**

For smooth projective connected \(X/k\), \(k\) algebraically closed and \(\ell\ne{\rm char}(k)\), the Picard–Albanese duality and Kummer construction identify rational \(H^1(X)\) with \(H^1({\rm Alb}_X)\). Poincaré duality gives the top-odd comparison with \(H^1({\rm Pic}^0_{\rm red}X)\), with twist \(n-1\). The reduced identity component is essential in positive characteristic. Integral torsion issues are kept separate from the rational isomorphisms in the summary.

For \(Z\in CH^n(X\times Y)\), the Poincaré bundle converts \(Z\) into a divisor correspondence on \(B\times Y\), \(B={\rm Alb}_X^\vee\). Its normalized fibre map induces
\({\rm Alb}_Y\to{\rm Pic}^0(B)={\rm Alb}_X\). The compatibility on degree-zero zero-cycles follows from the projection formula and simultaneous moving. The transcribed \({\rm Pic}^0(A_X)\) is ill-typed; the later line correctly gives \({\rm Pic}^0(B)\), so it is an alarm, not an edited reading.

Numerical dependence is proved as follows. If \(Z\) is numerically trivial, the resulting divisor correspondence is numerically trivial because numerical equivalence is compatible with products and proper pushforward. SGA 6, XIII, Proposition 4.6, identifies numerical and \(\tau\)-equivalence for line bundles on the relevant proper variety. A positive multiple of the divisor is therefore algebraically trivial, and induces zero normalized Albanese-to-Picard homomorphism. Since \({\rm Hom}\) of abelian varieties has no torsion, the original homomorphism is zero. To see torsion-freeness directly, a homomorphism killed by an integer has connected image contained in a finite group scheme; a reduced connected abelian image must then be zero. This proves C-SA06-78 with the exact divisor input requested by the margin.

For any nonempty open \(U\subset Y\), zero-cycles representing every degree-zero Albanese class can be moved off the complement. Over an algebraically closed field, a standard curve through the finite support and general auxiliary points reduces this to moving divisors on a smooth projective curve. Equivalently, the projective moving lemma moves a zero-cycle away from a prescribed proper closed set. This proves the uniqueness input C-SA06-80. It does not assert surjectivity from individual points of \(U\) when its dimension is smaller than the Albanese dimension.

Applying the cycle-class map to the construction commutes with pullback, pushforward and products. Hence the diagram in Theorem 1.6 on scan 56 is commutative with \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) coefficients. For abelian \(X,Y\), the Albanese maps are the identity, so an integral cycle acts on \(H^1\) as the pullback of an actual homomorphism \(Y\to X\). Conversely its graph supplies the cycle. Faithfulness on \(H^1\) gives uniqueness: a nonzero homomorphism has a positive-dimensional abelian image, whose \(\ell\)-adic Tate module is nonzero. The cycle determines that homomorphism independently of \(\ell\). This is an assertion about integral cycle actions, not a claim that every abstract \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-linear map is algebraic.

A homomorphism of abelian varieties is an isogeny exactly when its rational \(H^1\) map is an isomorphism. The kernel and cokernel dimensions are detected by Tate modules; a finite kernel and equal dimension make it an isogeny. A suitable integer multiple of its inverse is an actual homomorphism, so its graph represents the inverse in the rational correspondence category.

For the mixed ample degree-one statement, replace each ample divisor by a sufficiently high very ample multiple and choose a smooth complete-intersection curve \(C\). Restriction \(f:{\rm Pic}^0_{\rm red}(X)\to J(C)\) has finite kernel, by weak Lefschetz on \(H^1\). Pulling back the principal Jacobian polarization along \(f\) gives a polarization on \({\rm Pic}^0_{\rm red}(X)\), hence an isogeny to its dual. The corresponding cohomological map is restriction followed by Gysin, namely cup product with \([C]\), a positive integer multiple of the original product of divisor classes. Dividing that scalar in \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) proves the asserted isomorphism. This argument proves the degree-one mixed statement in arbitrary characteristic; it is not a general mixed hard-Lefschetz theorem in every degree.

The four Picard/Albanese cases reduce rationally to the canonical divisorial correspondence and the preceding isogeny inverses. Composition with fixed rational comparison correspondences also preserves numerical equivalence; the resulting homomorphism is zero whenever the original cycle is numerically zero. Rational zero implies integral zero in \({\rm Hom}\), by torsion-freeness. This supplies the qualified reconstruction C-SA06-101. Integral inverses are a separate question, as §7 explains.

A one-cycle on \(X\times Y\) acts on an algebraically trivial divisor \(D\) by a zero-cycle on \(Y\). Its Albanese value varies regularly with the line bundle and is additive. Thus it defines the unique homomorphism \({\rm Pic}^0_{\rm red}(X)\to{\rm Alb}_Y\) in Proposition 2.1. The supplied assigned text ends at the beginning of this proposition; its continuation on scans 59–61 was read only to verify the cross-package link and belongs to the later cote-14 tranche.

The arbitrary-base remark is not silently promoted to a theorem. Grothendieck's FGA exposition (1962, Theorem 3.3 and Remark 3.4) supplies a relative Albanese torsor under existence of the required abelian Picard subscheme and warns of failures over arbitrary bases. The manuscript also needs its \(CK\)-intersection operations and base-change compatibility. C-SA06-85 retains those conditions.

## 13. The reused English algebra leaves

**C-SA06-66–74 and 103–105; items SA06-86–90.**

These typed leaves are reused material. The title is a bibliographic lead toward Bernice Auslander's paper; its exact collation and authorship were not established. The modern mathematical checks below do not attribute these results to Grothendieck.

For a commutative ring \(R\), a finite projective \(R\)-module \(P\) has
\({\rm End}_R(P)=P^\vee\otimes_RP\). Localizing proves the tensor-Hom identities and compatibility with base change. For \(P\) of everywhere positive local rank, its endomorphism algebra is faithfully finite projective, central, and the map
\[
{\rm End}(P)\otimes_R{\rm End}(P)^{\rm op}
 \longrightarrow {\rm End}_R({\rm End}(P))
\]
is an isomorphism, checked locally on matrix units. This is the Azumaya criterion, rather than an assertion that every Azumaya algebra is globally an endomorphism algebra.

Faithfulness is unnecessary for separability alone. In a rank-\(r>0\) matrix algebra, set
\[
e=\sum_{i=1}^r E_{i1}\otimes E_{1i}\in A\otimes_R A^{\rm op}.
\]
Multiplication sends \(e\) to \(\sum_iE_{ii}=1\). For a matrix unit \(E_{ab}\),
\[
(E_{ab}\otimes1)e=E_{a1}\otimes E_{1b}
 =(1\otimes E_{ab}^{\rm op})e,
\]
with multiplication in the opposite factor understood. This splits the multiplication map as an \(A\)-bimodule map. Rank-zero components cause no obstruction to projectivity over the enveloping algebra. Localization and the finite-presentation criterion give separability of \({\rm End}_R(P)\); its center is \(R/{\rm ann}(P)\). Bass (1967, Chapter 3, Proposition 2.13, p. 75) states this exact result. M04 verifies the identities for ranks 1–4, while the index proof above proves every rank.

The sheaf criterion C-SA06-66 uses finite presentation to pass between stalks and local modules and to commute the relevant Hom constructions. Finite generation alone would not suffice over arbitrary rings. If a locally separable algebra is finite projective over the base and \(M\) is finite projective over it, then locally \(M\) is a direct summand of a finite power of that algebra; it is therefore finite projective over the base. This is C-SA06-104.

For a bimodule \(M\), evaluation at one identifies
\[
{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda\otimes_R\Lambda^{\rm op}}(\Lambda,M)
 \simeq \{m\in M:xm=mx\text{ for all }x\in\Lambda\}.
\]
A bimodule map satisfies \(f(x)=xf(1)=f(1)x\); conversely a centralizing element defines \(x\mapsto xm\). These maps commute with restrictions, so the same formula identifies sheaf commutants. It proves C-SA06-105 without a finite-generation assumption.

For two Azumaya tensor factors \(L,M\), their mutual commutants in \(L\otimes M\) are \(L\) and \(M\). The ring theorem is Bass, Chapter 3, Corollary 4.3. M01–M03 explicitly check the split \(2\times3\) case: the tensor basis has dimension 36 and the two commutants have dimensions 4 and 9. These computations neither prove the arbitrary-ring theorem nor assume every algebra is globally split.

Let \(\Lambda={\rm End}_R(P)\) with \(P\) finite projective faithful. The evaluation map
\[
{\rm Hom}_\Lambda(P,M)\otimes_RP\longrightarrow M
\]
is an isomorphism for left \(\Lambda\)-modules \(M\). Locally \(P=R^r\); matrix units decompose \(M\) into \(r\) equivalent summands, and evaluation identifies it with \(r\) copies of \(E_{11}M\). This constructs the inverse and the adjunction unit. Canonicality makes the maps glue in the sheaf setting. The tensor product is over the commutative base \(R\), not over \(\Lambda\). A separate scan alarm concerns the typed claim that this Hom is itself a \(\Lambda\)-module: its required base-module finiteness follows from the direct-summand argument even when that action has not been defined.

The Brauer group law, inverse algebra and zero-class criterion follow from this Morita theorem and the enveloping-algebra identity. In particular a central separable algebra has trivial Brauer class exactly when it is an endomorphism algebra of a finite projective faithful module. Over \(\mathbb R\), the Hamilton quaternions are central simple and division: the exact formula
\(q\bar q=a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2\) gives an inverse for every nonzero \(q\) (M05). They are not \(M_2(\mathbb R)\), which has zero divisors, and dimension rules out another matrix size. This verifies the need to distinguish the split class \(C'_1\) from the whole class \(C_1\). It does not decide the manuscript prime on the image.

## 14. The reused French affine-reflection leaves

**C-SA06-75–76 and 98–100; items SA06-91–93.**

An affine map \(x\mapsto Ax+u\), after origins are chosen, has homogenization
\[
H(A,u)=\begin{pmatrix}A&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The block product is \(H(A,u)H(B,v)=H(AB,Av+u)\), which proves functoriality and injectivity. R02 verifies the formula for fully symbolic two-dimensional affine maps; the block formula is dimension-independent.

If an affine map fixes a hyperplane pointwise, choose an origin in that hyperplane. Its translation part then vanishes and its homogenization is \({\rm diag}(A,1)\). The fixed-hyperplane and involution properties reduce to those of \(A\). In characteristic different from two, an orthogonal nonidentity involution has orthogonal plus and minus eigenspaces. If its plus space is a hyperplane, its minus space is a nonisotropic line, and it is the orthogonal reflection. Conversely that reflection is an isometry. This establishes the exact qualified statement without presupposing the absent earlier definition of “pseudo-reflection.”

For parallel nonisotropic hyperplanes \(H,H'=H+t\), choose the normal decomposition and an origin on \(H\). The first reflection negates the normal coordinate; the second is its translate by \(2t\). Hence \(s'\circ s(x)=x+2t\), with the stated order. R01 records this in a one-dimensional affine matrix. For a positive real form, the shortest joining vector is the orthogonal vector \(t\), so its norm is the distance. The infinite-order corollary over \(\mathbb R\) is an editorial observation, not a statement on these leaves.

A locally finite union of real affine hyperplanes is closed because near each point it is a finite union of closed sets. It is nowhere dense for the same reason: finitely many proper hyperplanes contain no open ball. A connected component of the complement lies in one open half-space for each hyperplane. Conversely the intersection of those selected half-spaces is convex. Joining any of its points to one fixed point of the component gives a segment avoiding all hyperplanes, so it lies in that component. No finite reflection-group hypothesis is required. The printed \(S'/S\) discrepancy is retained as a notation alarm.

The exact local join of the reused leaves is scan 22, typed page −14−, to scan 20, typed page −15−. No absent cote-54 page was used to assert a consecutive three-leaf sequence, and the publisher's modern Bourbaki volume metadata does not establish the exact draft-number collation.
