## P1. Characters, semisimple operators, and compactness

For a group of multiplicative type T over Q, evaluation at f in T(Q) gives a Galois-equivariant map X*(T_Qbar) to Qbar*. The cyclic subgroup generated by f is Zariski dense exactly when this map is injective: a proper closed subgroup of a diagonalizable group is detected by a nonzero character trivial on that subgroup. Conversely a discrete Galois-stable subgroup M of Qbar* defines D(M), with evaluation f. Finite generation of M is needed to obtain a finite-type group; arbitrary M gives an affine inverse limit. This qualification matters at Pro90: the printed group/category correspondence should not mix finite-type and proalgebraic objects.

Finite-dimensional representations of D(M) are finite M-graded spaces with descent. The corresponding operator is invertible and semisimple. Direct sums, tensor products, subobjects, and **duals** recover the group M. Without dual closure, for example the subcategory generated by the scalar 2 using only nonnegative tensor powers has eigenvalues 2^n, n≥0, a monoid rather than a subgroup. The page's ambient motivic rigidity can supply the missing hypothesis; the literal closure list alone cannot.

Let w:M→Z be the weight map, let epsilon be the character represented by q>0, and let w(epsilon)=alpha≠0. If the connected quotient by j(G_m) is compact over R, a positive power f^nu is u j(x), with u in a compact real torus. Consequently every weight-i eigenvalue has absolute value |x|^(i/nu), and q^nu=|x|^alpha gives q^(i/alpha). Absolute values are needed when x<0; epsilon(u)=1 is the multiplicative meaning of the source's additive zero notation.

The converse can be proved directly on characters, without reviving the deleted argument. If |sigma(m)|=q^(w(m)/alpha) for every conjugate, complex conjugation c satisfies m c(m)=q^(2w(m)/alpha) as an equality of algebraic numbers. On ker(w), c acts by inversion. After passing to the character group of the connected quotient, this says c=-1, precisely the character criterion for a compact real torus. Thus the numerical criterion has a correct converse under these explicit multiplicative-type and density hypotheses. The crossed-out rank-two example M=<2,3> does not meet them: 2^{w(3)}=3^{w(2)} forces w(2)=w(3)=0, incompatible with either modulus being greater than one.

Literature comparison: MilneCM2020, §4 (multiplicative type), and DeligneMilne1982, §2 and §4 (tensor reconstruction and positivity). The character argument above is independently supplied, not quoted from those sources.

## P2. Rational common norm and CM fields

Suppose lambda≠0 is algebraic and every conjugate has squared absolute value Q in Q_{>0}. Put a=lambda+Q/lambda. For every embedding, a maps to 2 Re(sigma(lambda)), hence is totally real. Moreover d=4Q-a² is totally nonnegative. An algebraic element with one zero conjugate is zero, so either d=0 or all its conjugates are strictly positive. Thus lambda satisfies T²-aT+Q and Q(lambda)/Q(a) is either trivial or a totally imaginary quadratic extension. Conversely, in a CM field complex conjugation commutes with all embeddings; a rational identity lambda c(lambda)=Q therefore gives the same modulus at every embedding. This proves the equivalences on Pro131–132, including the zero-discriminant exception.

A real such lambda is ±Q^(1/2), not necessarily the positive root. The cancelled p^(1/4) example has common squared modulus sqrt(p), not rational, and misses the premise. There is a separate quantifier discrepancy on Pro94: clause (i) permits weight i in Z while clause (iv) prints i in N. For q>1, lambda=q^(-1) has weight -2; it satisfies the former and cannot satisfy the latter if N denotes nonnegative integers. Either allow integral i in (iv), or impose nonnegative weight in all clauses. Neither repair is an ink decision.

The proposed polynomial x²+a x+b on Pro108 conflicts with the definition a=x+sigma(x); direct multiplication gives x²-a x+b. The later Pro131 formula supports the mathematical alarm, not a forced transcription replacement.

Literature comparison: MilneCM2020, Proposition 1.4, Corollary 1.5, Remarks 1.6–1.7. The rational-norm equivalence is proved here directly from the supplied manuscript argument.

## P3. Finite-field eigenvalue existence and its limits

Fix q=p^n>1. Let lambda be a nonzero algebraic integer whose conjugates all have modulus q^(w/2), w an integer. If w<0, its nonzero integer norm would have absolute value strictly between 0 and 1, impossible. If w=0, all powers of lambda have monic integer minimal polynomials of bounded degree and bounded coefficients, since their roots have modulus one. There are finitely many such polynomials and roots; two powers coincide, so lambda is a root of unity. If its order is r, it occurs in H^0(Spec F_{q^r}), a smooth projective zero-dimensional F_q-scheme. Geometric connectedness was not imposed and would change this boundary case.

For w>0 choose beta with beta^w=lambda. It is integral and every conjugate has modulus sqrt(q). Honda–Tate realizes beta as an H^1 Frobenius eigenvalue of an abelian variety A/F_q. In H^w(A^w), the H^1(A) tensor factor has eigenvalue beta^w=lambda. This proves the reconstructed numerical strong conjecture of Pro100; it is not a proof that every motive is generated by abelian varieties, that numerical and homological equivalence agree, or that all Tate classes are algebraic.

Primary comparison: TateHonda1968, Theorem 1(a), talk November 1968, published 1971; MilneFiniteFields1994, Proposition 2.6 and Remark 2.7. The latter's full bijectivity/generation description explicitly uses the Tate conjecture. We use Honda–Tate only for the existence step above.

## P4. Power maps, torsion, and rationalized lattices

Under the manuscript's full Weil-character-group definition, roots of unity form the torsion subgroup. For P_m:M(q)→M(q^m), x↦x^m, the kernel is mu_m; the displayed inclusions imply that the cokernel C_m is killed by m. Contravariant diagonalizable duality yields

`1 → D(C_m) → G(q^m) → G(q) → D(mu_m) → 1`.

The Galois action on mu_m is cyclotomic, so its diagonalizable dual over Q is the constant Z/m group. The kernel D(C_m) need not vanish. At the direct limit under all power maps, torsion dies and the weight-zero part is rationalized. This explains the geometric protorus calculation without identifying its character group with all numerical motives unconditionally. For a fixed CM field and a fixed weight normalization, finite integral-index defects survive before this limit.

Primary comparison: MilneFiniteFields1994, 2.25–2.29. The exact-sequence calculation is independent here.

## P5. Norm-one divisors and the Q(sqrt(-23)) obstruction

Let L/K be a quadratic extension of number fields, sigma its involution, and P a split prime over K. Finite class group gives y with div(y)=h(P-sigma P) for some h>0. Then x=y/sigma(y) has norm one and divisor 2h(P-sigma P). The factor two is correct and sufficient for the existential positive-multiple assertion. In a CM field, norm-one p-units have anti-invariant p-divisors. The divisor kernel consists of units all of whose complex absolute values are one and is therefore the finite root-of-unity group, by the bounded-polynomial argument in P3. Applying the construction to each split pair gives finite index in the anti-invariant divisor lattice; it gives no saturated-lattice assertion.

For L=Q(sqrt(-23)), put omega=(1+sqrt(-23))/2. Its minimal polynomial is T²-T+6. Modulo 2 the roots 0 and 1 are distinct, so (2)=P Pbar, where P=(2,omega+1). Alpha=1+omega has norm 8, vanishes only at P, and therefore div(alpha)=3P. A generator of P would have norm 2, whereas

`N(a+b omega)=a²+a b+6b²=(a+b/2)²+23b²/4`

cannot equal 2: b≠0 makes the last term >2 and b=0 requires a²=2. Thus P has exact ideal-class order 3. Conjugation acts by inversion because I Ibar is a rational principal ideal. A divisor (a,b) at P,Pbar is principal precisely when a-b is divisible by 3. On the anti-diagonal (t,-t), this says 3|t. Hence the norm-one lattice is 3Z(P-Pbar).

Beta=alpha/2 has norm 2 and valuations (2,-1); u=alpha/alphabar has valuations (3,-3). These form a basis of the full principal 2-divisor lattice, with matrix [[2,3],[-1,-3]] and Smith factors 1,3. At base q=8 the sum of valuations must be divisible by 3; together with a-b≡0 mod3 this is exactly 3Z², with Smith factors 3,3. These are statements about the specified finite-field lattice, not all possible character groups.

## P6. Fixed CM field versus enlargement

The deleted prescribed-field assertion is false even without integrality. If a²+b²=3 with a,b rational, clear denominators to a primitive integer triple A²+B²=3C². Reduction modulo 3 forces 3|A,B and then 3|C, a contradiction. Thus Q(i) has no element of norm 3. The integral clause fails in Q(sqrt(-23)) at norm 2 by P5, although beta=alpha/2 is a rational field element of that norm and generates L.

The live enlargement construction has different quantifiers. Given finite totally real K/Q and rational Q>1, choose an integral primitive element a of K. Choose i>0 so large that 4Q^i-sigma(a)²>0 for every embedding. A root lambda of T²-aT+Q^i generates a quadratic CM extension containing K, because a=lambda+Q^i/lambda. If beta^i=lambda then every conjugate of beta has squared modulus Q, so P2 puts Q(beta) in the maximal CM field and it contains K. For integral Q both monic equations show integrality. The explicit polynomial T^6-3T^3+8 is irreducible over Q and gives a degree-six enlargement containing Q(sqrt(-23)), with beta³=(3+sqrt(-23))/2 and norm modulus 2. The polynomial check is illustrative, not the proof of the universal construction.

## P7. Valuations, fields of definition, and isogeny lifting

For Galois CM L/Q and a prime P over p with cP≠P, the functions v_P on the rationalized full p-unit lattice distinguish primes: anti-invariant divisors distinguish different conjugate pairs and their two signs. The stabilizer of v_P is therefore the decomposition group D_P. Its field of definition is L^{D_P}. This uses the full lattice, not an arbitrary smaller motivic sublattice on which two valuations could coincide.

Let L_0=L^{D_P}. Since Gal(L_P/Q_p)=D_P, the completion of L_0 at the restricted place is Q_p; its completed integer ring is Z_p. Centrality of c and c∉D_P imply L_0 is totally imaginary. Other completions need not be Q_p. Conjugation transports the pointed fields, giving the asserted isomorphism type. A normalization v_P(p)=1 is rational-valued when p ramifies; only integral valuations define integral cocharacters without a denominator correction.

For an isogeny 1→K→G'→G→1, a rational point f_0 lifts exactly when its connecting class delta(f_0) in H^1(Q,K) vanishes. An index defect alone does not decide that class. The square map on G_m is a nonisomorphism and lifts 4 to 2, disproving the broad statement but not the manuscript's special Frobenius point. That point first needs repair: epsilon(f j(sqrt(p)))=q p, whereas a weight-zero normalization has epsilon=1 and formally uses j(q^(-1/2)). Rationality and the square-root choice remain separate descent questions.

Literature: MilneANT2020, Proposition 8.10; MilneFiniteFields1994, Proposition 2.27. Proof and normalization checks are supplied here.

## P8. Totally real closure and maximal CM quotient

Let Pi=Gal(Qbar/Q), c complex conjugation, N the closed normal subgroup generated by its conjugates, and Qtr=Qbar^N. An algebraic element is totally real iff every conjugate of c fixes it. This proves the fixed-field description and refutes the literal Pro108 phrase “contains no conjugate” for total reality: H=Pi, K=Q is an immediate test.

The abelianization H=N/[N,N] (closed commutator subgroup) is topologically generated by elements of order at most two, hence has exponent two. In the coinvariants H_{Pi/N}, all these generators coincide. This quotient has order at most two, and Qtr(i)/Qtr supplies a nontrivial quotient, hence it is Z/2. The unique nontrivial quotient with all conjugate complex conjugations equal therefore cuts out Qtr(i).

Every CM field F is of the form F_0(sqrt(-d)), with F_0 totally real and d totally positive. The algebraic number sqrt(d) is totally real, so F⊂Qtr(i). Conversely any finite subfield of Qtr(i) is totally real or quadratic totally imaginary over its intersection with Qtr; complex conjugation is central on Qtr(i). The same statement for algebraic subfields follows elementwise. Thus the quotient of Pi by the closed subgroup generated by [c,g] is the maximal quotient in which c is central. It contains Qab. The cone of totally nonnegative elements defines a **partial** order; it need not define a total order, as sqrt(2) and -sqrt(2) are neither totally positive.

Literature comparison: MilneCM2020, Remarks 1.6–1.7. The coinvariant and partial-order details are independent verifications.

## P9. Real tori, parity, and fibre functors

For a connected torus with epsilon j=2 on character/cocharacter lattices, the divisibility of epsilon divides 2. A primitive epsilon has connected kernel. If epsilon=2epsilon_0, then epsilon_0 j=1, so j splits the torus and epsilon is the **square** of the second projection. Pro119's epsilon=pr_2 is incompatible with its square formula for j.

For a compact real torus T, choose coordinates T_C=(G_m)^d with real involution z↦1/bar(z). For one factor, invariant points are S^1 and norms are z/bar(z), which cover S^1, so H^2(R,T)=0. One-cocycles are real nonzero numbers and coboundaries are positive reals, giving H^1(R,T)=Z/2. Products give (Z/2)^d, canonically T(R)[2]. For the constant mu_2 factor, H^2 is Z/2. These computations concern continuous Galois cohomology; for smooth tori over R it agrees with the relevant fppf groups.

An antilinear operator J=A conjugation satisfying J²=-I obeys |det A|²=(-1)^r. Its dimension r is even. In rank two, A=[[0,-1],[1,0]] gives J²=-I explicitly. This is the quaternionic descent obstruction behind the rank-two example. It does not follow merely from epsilon j=2: Rep_R(G_m), epsilon=t², has a neutral fibre functor but lacks a nondegenerate alternating form on its one-dimensional odd object. The page's polarized motivic premises and fixed weight-zero realization are essential.

When a gerbe is neutral, isomorphism classes of its objects form the appropriate H^1 torsor after a base object is chosen. An equivariant map between two nonempty torsors under the same group is bijective: fix one point and translate. This justifies the last step of Pro119 once the two torsor constructions are established. It does not prove transitivity under any of the three larger automorphism groups queried on Pro121. The source's mu_2 domain for i_1 is also too small for the displayed cocharacter equations unless they are explicitly restricted.

Literature: DeligneMilne1982, Theorem 3.2, 4.24, 5.13, 5.15–5.19. Computation block: real_descent.

## P10. Effectivity, finite index, and parity

The valuation identity is v(lambda)+v(bar(lambda))=v(q^w), not the product of valuations. In the Q(sqrt(-23)) example the values 2,-1 give a particularly transparent failure of multiplication. A rational valuation functional defines a cocharacter only after denominators are removed. For actual algebraic integers, effectivity in the Weil-number sense is nonnegative valuation at every finite place; if support is only above p, it is enough to test all conjugate p-valuations. A single selected place does not suffice without its conjugates.

If epsilon i=epsilon i'=1, their difference lies in ker(epsilon_*). Therefore differences cannot generate a finite-index subgroup of the whole cocharacter lattice when epsilon_* is nonzero: that kernel has codimension one. A corrected finite-index assertion can concern the weight-zero lattice. Even finite index there says nothing about reduction modulo 2: 2Z and 3Z both have finite index in Z, but their mod-2 images are respectively zero and all of Z/2. This is precisely the distinction needed for the preferred mu_2-polarization packet. We do not infer a universal orbit theorem from the finite-index computation.

Complex conjugation on a CM character group is central Galois-equivariant, acts by inversion in weight zero, and fixes the weight direction. It defines a band automorphism. Lifting a band automorphism c to a gerbe of class h requires c_*(h)=h. If c acts by -1 on its commutative band this requires 2h=0. The existence of an automorphism inducing c, and equipping it with coherent order-two structure, are distinct further questions.

## P11. A conditional order-three test for the global gerbe claim

The literal Pro125 class is only described conjecturally; its category M and precise reduction are not fully specified. The following is a **test of a modern interpretation**, not an unconditional manuscript refutation.

Take pi=1+sqrt(-7), q=8, K=Q(sqrt(-7)). Its polynomial is T²-2T+8 and both complex roots have modulus sqrt(8). The field discriminant is -7≡1 mod8, so 2 splits with both local degrees one. The 2-adic Newton polygon has points (0,3),(1,1),(2,0), slopes -2,-1; the two valuations of pi are 1 and 2, while v(q)=3. Honda–Tate gives a simple A/F_8 with division endomorphism algebra D over K, local invariants 1/3 and 2/3 at the primes over 2, and zero elsewhere. Their sum is zero in Q/Z, the denominator is three, [D:K]=9, and 2 dim(A)=3[K:Q]=6. Thus A is a threefold and [D] has exact order three.

The ratio pi/bar(pi)=(-3+sqrt(-7))/4 is neither 1 nor -1. These are the only roots of unity in K, so Q(pi^n)=K for every n>0. Under extension F_8→F_{8^n}, normalized valuations and the division degree remain unchanged. The simple isogeny class associated to pi^n still has dimension three; therefore A stays simple under every finite extension and End^0(A_Fbar)=D. This checks the geometric-endomorphism premise rather than assuming it from simplicity over F_8.

Now suppose the specified modern pure-motive category is Tannakian after the required sign convention, contains h^1(A) with rank6, and has its usual endomorphisms D. These hypotheses must be verified in any proposed interpretation of the manuscript. A natural test is the category generated by h^1(A) and Tate objects; alternatively one can work inside Milne's explicitly Tate-conjectural full description while retaining that hypothesis. Under the displayed Tannakian premises, a fibre over Qbar carries the faithful D action M_3×M_3. Rank6 forces one copy of each three-dimensional simple module. Its centralizer consists of scalars on each summand; after adjoining the rank-one Tate object, the band of the generated category is therefore a subgroup of a torus and is of multiplicative type. The orbit of the character attached to the simple object has field K. Evaluation defines a band map to Res_{K/Q}G_m. By MilneFiniteFields1994, Lemma 3.15, the induced H^2 class, under H^2(Q,Res_{K/Q}G_m)=Br(K), is [D] (possibly its opposite under the dual convention, with the same order).

Fixing the ordinary fibre functor on the Tate subcategory gives a reduced gerbe with band U=ker(epsilon), when this reduction is defined. The inclusion U→P sends its class h_red to the original gerbe class: forgetting the Tate trivialization is exactly the pushout of the banded gerbe. Composing with the character map above therefore still sends h_red to [D]. If 2h_red=0, then 2[D]=0, contradiction. Restriction to the character's field and pushout are homomorphisms; they cannot increase a class of exponent two to exponent three. Consequently the broad modern claim “every such global Tate-reduced class is killed by two” fails in this model. A category that excludes h^1(A), uses a different coefficient field, or denotes a different class by h has not been tested by this example.

The real calculation H^2(R,T×mu_2)=Z/2 in P9 cannot establish this global assertion. Conversely, on the weight-zero band, inversion cannot preserve an h with this order-three image. This supplies a concrete obstruction to a gerbe lift in the same specified model, while the uncertain “ne/me” on Pro125 remains undecided.

Primary locators: TateHonda1968, Theorem 1(a),(c),(d), pp.95–96; MilneFiniteFields1994, Theorem 2.19 and Lemma 3.15. Saved block HondaTate_order3 contains every norm, Newton slope, invariant, dimension, and non-root-of-unity test used here.

## P12. Tate reduction, finite objects, and Artin–Tate objects

For a Tannakian category containing an invertible Tate object whose tensor powers are pairwise nonisomorphic, its Tate-generated subcategory has band G_m. Restriction of fibre functors is a morphism of gerbes. The fibre over a fixed standard Tate functor has band ker(epsilon). This is a two-fibre product of gerbes, not an assertion that the original gerbe has a rational object. If distinct Tate powers were isomorphic, the G_m assertion would change.

Finite tensor monodromy is characterized by a tensor generator whose powers are controlled by a fixed finite collection of objects. Identifying all finite objects with Artin motives needs a theorem about the chosen motive category; a formal Tannakian definition alone supplies only the maximal profinite quotient. Likewise a geometric motivic kernel need not be identified with the identity component solely from the exact arithmetic sequence.

For the actual category of finite-image Q-representations of Gal(ksep/k), every object factors through a finite quotient H. Let V be an H-representation. Choose a finite H-stable spanning set S: take the orbit of any finite basis. The permutation module Q[S] surjects onto V. Its kernel has an H-equivariant complement by averaging an arbitrary linear splitting over H, dividing by |H|. S is the geometric-point set of a finite étale k-scheme. Thus every such representation is a direct summand of a finite étale permutation motive. This proves the Artin-generation statement in its precise category, including nonabsolutely irreducible representations.

If the Tate weight and Artin part are independent as assumed on Pro137, an Artin–Tate object is a finite sum of V_i(i). Tensor degrees add and duality gives (V_i(i))^vee=V_i^vee(-i). A chosen geometric point supplies the finite-étale fibre functor; this choice is not a canonical neutralization of the whole motivic gerbe.

Literature comparison: DeligneMilne1982, Proposition 2.20 and Proposition 5.8; HuberMuellerStach2017, §9.4, Theorem 9.1.16 and Remark 9.5.7. The finite orbit/splitting proof is independent; exact C3 matrices appear in block Artin.

## P13. Correspondences, Lefschetz strings, and Tate characters

These are earlier-run comparison calculations, not assertions on Pro135–137. In the stated contravariant convention, a correspondence of codimension dim(X)+r represents h(X)→h(Y)(r). Composition is p_13*(p_12*alpha · p_23*beta), with the appropriate twists; this product differs from ordinary intersection on a fixed X×X. Homological triviality implies numerical triviality because intersection degrees are computed by the cycle-class pairing, giving the quotient functor to numerical motives. It does not prove equality of equivalence relations.

For P^1, h(P^1)=1⊕L. Its ordinary degree-zero motivic endomorphism algebra is Q⊕Q. Matrix units occur instead in the total graded correspondence algebra, allowing all Tate shifts; the off-diagonal units are twisted correspondences. A Lefschetz raising map consequently has a Tate twist and is not an ordinary endomorphism commuting with the motivic G_m-action. If that G_m acts by1 on the first summand and t on L, the determinant is t. A two-dimensional standard SL_2 representation has determinant1. Thus the two actions are different structures, and the existence of the Lefschetz sl_2 does not imply a forbidden tensor-subgroup identification.

On a primitive string v_k=L^k v, k=0,...,n, set H v_k=(2k-n)v_k and Lambda v_k=k(n-k+1)v_{k-1}. Direct substitution gives [H,L]=2L, [H,Lambda]=-2Lambda and [L,Lambda]=H for every k, including endpoints. The Casimir H²+2H+4 Lambda L is n(n+2). Weight projectors are the Lagrange polynomials product_{j≠k}(H-(2j-n))/(2(k-j)). These denominators explain why rational coefficients are natural; a finite matrix check cannot establish integral divided-power assertions. On P^1×P^1, (a h_1+b h_2)²=2ab[pt], another elementary integrality warning.

## P14. Reused algebraic-geometric fragments

Over a normal noetherian domain, a finite torsion-free module embeds in its fraction-field vector space and its reflexive hull is the intersection of its localizations at height-one primes. Therefore a map that is an isomorphism at every such prime induces an isomorphism of reflexive hulls. Calling the original map an “isomorphism” makes the Pro87 conclusion tautological. For extension from an open subset with codimension-at-least-two complement, the normal/reflexive hypotheses identify the extension with the double dual of a coherent extension. The anonymous theorem's arbitrary subset Y and set P are not fully defined on the fragment, so this comparison does not recover its exact quantifiers. Checked comparison: StacksReflexive2026, Lemmas 31.13.12–14, and StacksHull2026, Lemma 15.24.19.

For a morphism of ringed spaces, adjunction is Hom_{O_Y}(f* M,N)=Hom_{O_X}(M,f_*N). The mutually inverse maps send a morphism phi to m↦phi(1⊗m), and psi to a⊗m↦a psi(m). The coefficient ring on the right is O_X; Pro103 prints the wrong ring. The pushforward map on internal Hom comes from applying a sectionwise morphism, and its tensor map sends m⊗n to their tensor product. The pullback tensor isomorphism follows from associativity of extension of scalars. Checked locator: StacksRinged2026, Lemmas 6.26.2–3.

For étale sheaf descent, form the equalizer of the two descent arrows. If the requisite direct images commute with a base change, that equalizer commutes with the base change. After pulling back along the covering, the doubled cover has its diagonal section; descent there is split and effective. Conservativity then detects the comparison isomorphism. This proves the formal mechanism, while proper/base-field base-change theorems are separate inputs. Checked source: SGA4II, VIII 9.4.1–9.4.4. The stalk criterion at VIII 3.13 uses very density after every étale base change, not merely density in X.

The projectivity fragments use the usual evaluation square: surjectivity H^0(V,L)→H^0(D,L|D) transfers generation at a point of D. From 0→L(-D)→L→L|D→0, vanishing H^1(D,L|D) gives surjectivity on H^1; if the two H^1 dimensions coincide, that map is an isomorphism and restriction on H^0 is surjective. The line bundle must have the same twist on both sides. This proves the precise step behind the m/m' alarm. Similarly the two long exact sequences on Pro115 yield inequalities (21),(22) with the explicitly asserted vanishing input; they do not independently prove that vanishing. A base-point-free linear series defines a morphism with pullback O(1)=L by its transition functions. The retained fragments do not supply the whole proof of eventual very ampleness. ## P15. Deuring and localization: what the missing boundary actually supplies

Pro61 lines 481–507 define reasonable products by P⊗_O X≅Hom_O(P^vee,X) for finite projective P, and define the additional “jolie” condition. Codex B15-01 scan 63 begins by completing that sentence. Thus the definition is present in the combined supplied material and must not remain an artificial package-boundary gap.

In an additive idempotent-complete A-linear category, write P=e O^n. Define P⊗_O X as the image of the idempotent e on X^n. Morphisms between such images are the corresponding corner matrices f M_{m,n}(O)e; this proves full faithfulness of P↦P⊗X when O=End(X), and the tensor/Hom comparison. An admissible functor is determined by its value on X and the O action, with existence of the required tensors; natural transformations are likewise determined on the generator. Yoneda gives the separately asserted fully faithful embedding into the coadmissible functor category.

If the Hom-localized fibre category is a stack on the small Zariski site of Spec(A), locally free rank-one O-modules and objects locally isomorphic to X descend by the same transition matrices. This proves the equivalence in scan81(a). Additivity gives finite ranks; idempotent completeness gives finite projectives. An R action is descent data consisting of endomorphisms satisfying the relations of R, so it descends with the object on Spec(A). This does not itself prove localization on Spec(R), exactly as the manuscript warns. The identical-object construction on the big site need not be a stack: start with finite-dimensional Q-vector spaces and base change to a Q-algebra having a nontrivial projective line module. Locally free rank-one descent can produce that line module, absent from the free-module objects.

## P16. Analytic identity, gluing, and path primitives

The recycled analytic sheets are not attributed as original motivic results. Where the context specifies K=R or C and Banach-valued analytic functions, the identity theorem follows by restricting power series to lines and applying the one-variable isolated-zero argument. If overlaps are connected, equality on a nonempty open subset gives equality on the entire overlap, so the functions glue. A chain of extensions glues on its union; Zorn then gives a maximal single-valued extension. Uniqueness holds on a fixed connected domain containing the original one, not uniqueness of all maximal domains. The two square-root branches on a disconnected overlap show why the connectedness hypothesis matters. In that example the second H should refer to the other component H'; the formulas themselves determine the sign distinction.

On a totally real affine slice of a complex domain, a holomorphic function vanishing on a relatively open slice has all its Taylor coefficients zero at a slice point; hence the stronger uniqueness assertion is valid. In one variable an accumulation point inside the domain similarly suffices.

The sheet's “contractile” means every loop is null-homotopic, i.e. simply connected in the present open-domain setting; it does not state a contraction of the identity map. A connected open subset of R^n or C^n is polygonally connected, since the set reachable by polygonal paths from a fixed point is open and relatively closed. For a closed C^1 real one-form, or a holomorphic one-form, local primitives and subdivision of a homotopy into small rectangles show homotopy invariance of integrals. On a simply connected domain define g(z) by integrating from a fixed base point; path independence and local primitives give dg=omega. Any two primitives differ by a locally constant function, hence a constant. This proves the real C^1 extension raised as a question on scan31. The interpolation denominator on scan33 must be t_{a+1}-t_a for the endpoints to be right; the printed variable denominator has a pole at the endpoint.

The abstract continuation paragraph on scan27 is cut off after its final “enfin, si”. ## P17. Analytic power series and coefficient alarms

For absolutely convergent Banach-valued power series, re-expansion at a inside radii r is valid on |u_k|<r_k-|a_k| by absolute summation. Reordering absolutely summable families proves associativity of substitution and composition. A continuous multilinear map bounded by C product||x_i|| sends absolutely summable products to absolutely summable products; this proves the multilinear and coordinatewise analytic claims.

On any strictly smaller polyradius, polynomial factors in multiindices are dominated by an exponential geometric factor. This justifies termwise derivatives and the primitive coefficient division in characteristic zero, since |1/n|≤n^beta for the induced absolute value on Q. More explicitly, a trivial restriction gives1, an archimedean restriction gives n^(−a)≤1, and a p-adic restriction gives p^(a v_p(n))≤n^a; these are the cases of Ostrowski’s theorem (MilneANT2020, Theorem7.12, pp110–111). It does **not** justify simultaneous convergence at the same boundary radius: sum(n+1)^(-2) converges but multiplying by (n+1)^2 produces the divergent constant series. The intended germ conclusion survives by shrinking radii.

The Taylor remainder on scan51 has coefficients bounded by M r^{-|n|-|m|}; extracting t² from terms |m|≥2 leaves a factor r^{-2}. The printed bound omits it. With p=1, r=1/4 and remainder w², M=r²=1/16; taking u=0,w=1/8 makes the printed geometric majorant at most 1/256 while the left side is 1/64. The corrected bound proves strict differentiability: the remainder is o(max|u_k-v_k|) uniformly for both points approaching the centre. Analytic derivative coefficients then prove C^infinity regularity. In characteristic zero, a zero of order h is characterized by the first nonzero derivative of order h. In positive characteristic that derivative criterion needs Hasse coefficients instead.

The scalar implicit-function majorant solves its displayed quadratic; direct substitution verifies the radical branch and the zero constant term. ## P18. ODEs, Frobenius, and the missing dependence hypothesis

For analytic U_{ij}(x,y), the system ∂_i y_j=U_{ij}(x,y) corresponds to commuting vector fields X_i=∂_{x_i}+sum_h U_{ih}∂_{y_h}. Computing [X_k,X_i] gives

`∂_k U_{ij}+sum_h (∂_{y_h}U_{ij}) U_{kh} = ∂_i U_{kj}+sum_h (∂_{y_h}U_{kj}) U_{ih}`.

The last manuscript factor U_{jh} has the wrong index. Analytic local ODE uniqueness gives flow composition and local inverse flow; it also supports the radial integration proof of Frobenius once this compatibility equation is correct. For the ODE coefficient recursion, expansion of f_i(x,u_1,...,u_p,z) necessarily uses products of h_j, the coefficients of the j-th component. The printed repeated h_i fails already for u_1'=u_2, u_2'=1, zero initial data, whose solution is u_1=x²/2,u_2=x. All parameter multiindices need their final component index. The formal recurrence plus analytic majorants establishes local germs over complete valued fields of characteristic zero; uniqueness is a germ assertion and cannot imply unique patching on arbitrary disconnected ultrametric neighbourhoods.

The inverse function theorem is local. For z^13 on |z-1|<1/2, the derivative never vanishes, while 1 and exp(2pi i/13) lie in the disk and have the same image: their distance is 2sin(pi/13)<2pi/13<1/2, using pi<22/7<13/4. This supplies an exact integer to the page's “n large enough”.

Scan47's request for “dependence” when a Jacobian vanishes identically leaves the quantifiers unspecified. On a constant-rank neighbourhood the analytic rank theorem gives local relations. At an arbitrary germ, the stronger claim is false. Let f(u,v,w)=(u,uv,uv e^v). Its Jacobian determinant is identically zero and its generic rank is two. Suppose a formal relation F(X,Y,Z) vanishes after substitution. Decompose F into homogeneous polynomials F_d. Then the coefficient of u^d is F_d(1,v,v e^v). A polynomial relation between v and e^v is impossible: in a nonzero exponential polynomial sum_j P_j(v)e^{jv}, divide by the largest exponential and let v→+infinity to contradict polynomial growth. Thus every F_d is zero. There is no nonzero formal, hence no analytic, relation at the origin. This is Osgood's classical example, rechecked here, not a new result and not an identification with the distinct formal-versus-analytic rank problem of later Grothendieck/Gabrielov discussions.

Primary comparisons: BourbakiVarietes1967, §§1.5.1,5.10.6,9.1.1,9.1.7–8,9.3.3; BelottoCurmiRond2021, Example 1.16, p.1339. ## P19. Lusin spaces and the dyadic index

For binary sequences indexed from zero, sum epsilon_n 2^{-n} has range [0,2]. Removing the nonzero eventually-zero sequences leaves one representation per value (retaining zero), and the removed set is countable. Its complement in Cantor space is a G_delta Polish space, zero-dimensional. The historical Bourbaki word “éparpillé” here means a Hausdorff space with a clopen basis, not a modern scattered space.

The opened published comparison, BourbakiTopology2007, IX §6 no.4, Definition 6 and Proposition 11, explicitly sets I=[0,1] and prints the same sum from n=0. Thus the scaling discrepancy is present in that printed witness as well. For the manuscript, which does not locally define I, the transcription remains frozen; candidate repairs are starting at n=1 or using 2^{-(n+1)}. This is evidence for a real formula problem, not proof that the OCR misread a symbol.

A Lusin space in this convention is a Hausdorff continuous bijective image of a Polish space. Countable sums are obtained from countable Polish sums. For products, take products of the finer Polish models. A countable inverse limit is the closed subset of the Hausdorff product cut out by equalizer equations, so its inverse image in the finer Polish product is closed and Polish. This proves the source's inverse-limit assertion. The distribution statement concerns the **strong** dual topologies on D'(Omega) and E'(Omega), not just weak duals; the checked comparison is BourbakiTVS1987, III §6 no.2, Theorem 2, corollary and Example 2, pp.III35–36. No general claim about every Fréchet strong dual is inferred from the OCR's ambiguous countability wording.
