## 1. Missing coherence in successor-only sheaf reconstruction

**Origin:** SA10-682; SA10-C353; MP-SA10-161. Also SA10-473 and MP-SA10-120 for the broader reconstruction aim.

**What it would establish.** A precisely defined enlargement of the successor diagram whose compatible sheaf data reconstruct sheaves on a specified class of stratified spaces. The statement must say which pair, nonadjacent and triple incidence objects are included, which maps are used, and what compatibility is imposed.

**What is known.** The point–line–plane counterexample checks that the written successor graph omits a real compatibility. Ordinary closed/open recollement and higher descent give established templates. Shah's finite-poset reconstruction uses a full toposic locally cocartesian fibration and its right-lax limit [Shah2026, Theorems 5.13/5.22]; the manuscript-to-gluing-data comparison remains necessary. A successful sphere gerbe calculation alone does not provide it. See also the exact SGA and modern vanishing-topos/exodromy locators in the p4 reference audit.

**First concrete step.** Write the three-stratum descent category explicitly and require its objects to pass the non-diagonal branch-specialization test. Compare the resulting compatibility morphism with the manuscript's pair/triple programme before adding more strata.

**Boundary of the run.** A full all-poset reconstruction theorem should follow only after the three-stratum statement and its coherence are fixed. The expected short-run output is the exact corrected candidate and the test, not a universal claim.

## 2. Neighborhood cofinality versus tube base change

**Origin:** SA10-600; SA10-C358; SA10-C359; SA10-C361; SA10-C367; MP-SA10-162; MP-SA10-163.

**What it would establish.** A functorial tube construction with explicitly stated behavior under closed, proper, open and locally closed maps. It must distinguish the ordinary neighborhood system from removal sets whose indexing changes when actual containments change.

**What is known.** Closed-map cofinality is proved. The containment-defined removal set and the literal tube fail the claimed base change in different finite examples. Open restriction also fails the literal neighborhood pro-homotopy comparison already on pro-components. Those counterexamples remain constraints on any proposed repair.

**First concrete step.** Make a four-column table of the category of maps, behavior of the index order, removal-set formula and neighborhood cofinality. Test an index-defined removal rule and an order-reflecting pullback condition against the existing examples; do not assume either yields the intended geometric object.

**Boundary of the run.** A useful result is one stable definition and its exact domain of functoriality. Comparison with a different toposic shape should be a separately stated theorem.

## 3. The missing finiteness in the moderate-model axioms

**Origin:** SA10-477; SA10-874; SA10-877; SA10-862; SA10-C435; SA10-C440; MP-SA10-203; MP-SA10-204.

**What it would establish.** An axiom comparison explaining exactly which additional 121-style finiteness assumptions exclude the all-compact model and support the single-map generic-triviality conclusion in a chosen category.

**What is known.** M1–M11 alone admit all compact subsets. The Cantor binary-value map refutes the generic product-fiber conclusion under those axioms alone. Fixed o-minimal real-field structures provide the positive theorem and compatible-subset version [Coste1999, Theorem 5.22]. The Boolean recursion and later triangulation clauses are distinct additional inputs.

**First concrete step.** Fix one finite list of axioms from one dated or otherwise explicitly located 121 draft, and check it against compact semialgebraic sets and the all-compact countermodel. State whether the desired implication is finite fiber types, a finite partition, or a compact relation extending a product graph.

**Boundary of the run.** Do not seek a weakest universal axiom system immediately. A precise sufficient package and a list of independent omissions would already make the companion materially better.

## 4. Relative parameters in the tame-isotopy comparison

**Origin:** SA10-17; SA10-264; SA10-486; SA10-487; SA10-491; SA10-C264; MP-SA10-103; MP-SA10-166; MP-SA10-166.

**What it would establish.** A comparison of explicitly defined simplicial spaces of admissible isomorphisms, relative to marked subobjects and prescribed parameter faces. The proposed theorem must distinguish a bijection on components from weak equivalence and from a homotopy equivalence in a chosen model.

**What is known.** Compact marked Hauptvermutung and isotopy through homeomorphisms are checked. Relative results and the standard noncompact representatives provide further input [Shiota2010, Theorem 2.1, complement and relative proof; ShiotaII, Supplement and Theorem 4; ShiotaYokoi1984, Theorem 4.1]. The *Esquisse* asks for more than an object-class comparison.

**First concrete step.** Specify the parameter object for a single simplex and its boundary, and write the required relative straightening lemma with its fiber-preserving condition. Match that exact lemma against the opened relative source statements, including their compactness and boundary hypotheses.

**Boundary of the run.** A few hours can isolate the missing relative lemma or establish that an existing one applies. It should not be promised to settle every tame theory, every map class and every noncompact system at once.

## 5. Faithful cone cutting with marked bases

**Origin:** SA10-30; SA10-31; SA10-35; SA10-37; SA10-38; SA10-C042; SA10-C044; SA10-C048; MP-SA10-03; MP-SA10-08; MP-SA10-10.

**What it would establish.** A complete finite equivalence between properly equipped ambient cone data and cut spaces with collar, attachment and marked-base data, preserving the manuscript's allowance of extra base points.

**What is known.** The ambient-frontier, forgotten-mark and nonclosed-attachment-image counterexamples identify three separate missing conditions. The dossier proves local repairs and the elementary radial formulas. It does not conflate those repairs with the full recursive groupoid equivalence.

**First concrete step.** List every datum and every morphism on both sides, including the marked base subsets and the ambient-interior condition. Check the two composites on one nonpure marked example before attempting induction.

**Boundary of the run.** The output should be a corrected finite theorem or an explicit remaining morphism/topology gap. Uniformity for infinite families and full filtration independence should remain separate questions.

## 6. A specified geometric model for deployment

**Origin:** SA10-207; SA10-220; SA10-221; SA10-224; SA10-227; SA10-228; SA10-C119; SA10-C120; SA10-C137; SA10-C138; SA10-C139; MP-SA10-11; MP-SA10-50; MP-SA10-51; MP-SA10-52; MP-SA10-53.

**What it would establish.** An explicit comparison between the manuscript's deployed flag system and one established controlled, conically smooth or fibered-corner model, with all source, target, boundary and transversality conditions checked.

**What is known.** Mather provides controlled local-triviality results under stated stratumwise hypotheses [Mather1970, Proposition 11.1]. AFT provides conically smooth cone/tube results [AFT2017, Corollary 7.3.7 and Propositions 8.2.3/8.2.5]. The November 2026 ABP version supplies smooth-atlas/resolution comparisons [ABP2025v2, Theorems 2.10, 2.13 and 5.2]. Their hypotheses are stronger and more organized than an unspecified non-wildness condition.

**First concrete step.** Choose one compact finite-depth model and translate each manuscript structural map into that model. Mark every place where simultaneous control data, a boundary fibration or a collar-extension choice is genuinely required.

**Boundary of the run.** Do not assert that transversality or the mixed fiber-boundary condition is redundant until that particular implication has been proved. A complete axiom dictionary for one model is a worthwhile initial result.

## 7. Canonical normal/tubular comparison with its boundary map

**Origin:** SA10-63; SA10-475; SA10-829; SA10-C301; SA10-C304; SA10-C305; MP-SA10-150; MP-SA10-114.

**What it would establish.** A canonical comparison of the specified normal and tubular objects whose fundamental-group diagram retains the connecting homomorphism from the base's second homotopy group and distinguishes the map into a global complement.

**What is known.** The conditional fibration exact sequence is established. The external 149 passage retracts an unrestricted meridian-injectivity expectation and then admits an additional extension assumption. The sphere separates local tubular behavior from the global complement. No canonical comparison functor has been supplied merely by this analogy.

**First concrete step.** Write the actual pointed objects and every arrow on both sides of the proposed comparison in one locally trivial example, then in an example with nonzero boundary map. Specify the orientation and basepoint choices before calling any identification canonical.

**Boundary of the run.** A commuting diagram with a precise obstruction is a realistic result. The full global geometric or étale comparison requires further input.

## 8. Exact scalar-object recognition

**Origin:** SA10-840; SA10-853; SA10-C413; SA10-C428; MP-SA10-202.

**What it would establish.** A representation theorem for the intended concrete finite-limit category and its scalar object, with the weak coordinate conditions distinguished from image closure and with nullary objects explicit.

**What is known.** The forward construction, graph composition and scalar-probe test are checked. The manuscript's abbreviated converse has not been matched to a theorem with every strong image hypothesis. General ind/pro language does not fill that omission.

**First concrete step.** Extract a formal list of the concrete-functor hypotheses and ask which admitted subsets and graph images can be reconstructed from them. Prove the weak version first or exhibit a concrete category separating it from the strong version.

**Boundary of the run.** This is a small representation problem, not a request to axiomatize all tame topology. An exact countermodel or a complete weak theorem would settle the immediate textual issue.

## 9. Actual arrows and contraction in the word category

**Origin:** SA10-802; SA10-803; SA10-804; SA10-809; SA10-C400; SA10-C401; SA10-C404; MP-SA10-200.

**What it would establish.** A faithful mathematical reconstruction of the stated word/disk/tree category and its recursive operation, after the scan-based decisions that mathematics cannot make.

**What is known.** The finite permutation dictionary, chord dual and noncrossing-tree theorem are established and checked. They do not specify the missing arrows, the current adjective, the surviving partner under contraction or the changed rho identification [Cori1975].

**First concrete step.** Resolve the listed ink/convention questions, then state each candidate contraction on a fully labeled finite set and write its induced map on words and disks. Test the two existing diagnostic matchings before asserting an equivalence.

**Boundary of the run.** The likely value is an accurate companion and recovered convention. Do not present known tree combinatorics as an undiscovered theorem merely because the manuscript formulation is unfinished.

## 10. Local privileged partitions versus global diagram triviality

**Origin:** SA10-864; SA10-879; SA10-C436; SA10-C439; MP-SA10-205; MP-SA10-208.

**What it would establish.** The exact local condition in 121, and the relation between its existence/coarseness conjectures and the global finite-diagram question on 120/25.

**What is known.** Global natural-isomorphism triviality fails even for proper compact semialgebraic finite-poset diagrams. Compatible nested-subset triviality succeeds. The local definition uses open embeddings whose images need not be full inverse images, so the global rotation test does not decide it. Shiota's restricted diagram result has its own nonrepetition and acyclicity assumptions.

**First concrete step.** Formalize one local product chart, including exactly which arrows and neighborhoods it must preserve. Decide whether a full-fiber version is actually implied under the manuscript's properness assumptions, rather than assuming that implication.

**Boundary of the run.** Keep existence of a partition, a unique coarsest partition and simultaneous compatibility as separate propositions. The immediate deliverable is an exact testable conjecture, not an all-diagram theorem.

## 11. Flag resolutions and actual descent

**Origin:** SA10-214; SA10-218; SA10-250; SA10-408; SA10-C123; SA10-C135; SA10-C136; SA10-C210; MP-SA10-56; MP-SA10-105; MP-SA10-112.

**What it would establish.** A precise comparison between a manuscript flag diagram and a cover or hypercover to which a specified cohomological or homotopy descent theorem applies.

**What is known.** The actual locally finite closed-cover spectral sequence has a proof. Open-cover and hypercover homotopy descent are established in their settings. The pro-neighborhood version still needs its site, matching maps, functoriality and convergence or realization comparison.

**First concrete step.** Choose a finite example with overlapping bands, retain the singleton-overlap terms, and construct the comparison to the full intersection diagram. Identify whether the desired result is a set/topological colimit, cohomology isomorphism or weak homotopy equivalence.

**Boundary of the run.** One exact finite comparison is enough. A general abstract realization theorem should not be inferred from the nerve identities or from the already successful sphere.

## 12. The settled left-covering question as companion work

**Origin:** SA10-436; SA10-437; SA10-C247; SA10-C248; MP-SA10-113.

**What it would establish.** A concise commentary that states the false three-test reduction, the correct all-finite comparison theorem, and the exact relation to the manuscript's question.

**What is known.** The two-element-group orbit example refutes the shortcut. The doubled-limit-cone argument proves the strong-epimorphic all-finite theorem, with exact later sources in Vitale1994 and CarboniVitale1998. The empty diagram is included.

**First concrete step.** Align the manuscript's finite-cone notation with the exact left-covering definition, then write the kernel-pair proof and the counterexample side by side with the current line locators.

**Boundary of the run.** This is a completed mathematical question awaiting good exposition. Its historical and editorial return is high; its likely research novelty is low.
