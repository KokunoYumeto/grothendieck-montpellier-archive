## The corpus and its own organizing questions

SA22 brings together three bodies of work that the catalogue places under teaching and research directions. They should not be made into a single retrospective programme. Cote 66 is a four-sheet list headed **“Travaux de thèses”**, with a boxed 1964 and later assessments written into or beside individual subjects. Cote 67 concerns **“Chirurgie des surfaces conformes”**: cutting, welding, capping, canonical metrics, marked surfaces and arithmetic enhancements of nodal curves, together with explicit calculations in projective geometry and real elliptic geometry. Cote 68 begins with **“Jeux de position”**, interrupts that investigation with rearrangement and convolution, returns to games, and then moves through exponential algebra, differential automorphisms, orientations and determinants. The three typescripts among the last sheets are retained as literary witnesses, without treating their placement as evidence of a mathematical allegory. ## Cote 66: “Travaux de thèses” and its later annotations

### Subjects 1–10: cohomology, duality and Riemann–Roch

The first ten subjects form a deliberately connected cohomological programme. Subject 1 proposes the general topology and cohomology of the fppf site, with an inserted relation to crystalline cohomology. Subject 2 asks for local and global duality and is expressly subordinated to subjects 1 and 11; subject 3, the p-primary part of an Ogg–Shafarevich formula, is subordinated to subject 2. Those dependencies are part of his proposed order of work. The sheets do not supply a coefficient category, conductor, Euler object or universal comparison map, and none has been silently inserted into his statement. [CL-SA22-037–039; cote 66 scan 2.]

The later literature realizes important precise branches. Artin–Milne's 1976 flat duality and Milne's arithmetic duality treat finite flat commutative groups with the required topologies and compact supports. Milne III.8.2 is a Pontryagin-duality statement on an open curve over a finite field; III.11.1 is a distinct derived sheaf-duality statement on the perfect site over a perfect field of characteristic p > 0. These should not be compressed into a claim that every cohomology group is finite. Non-étale finite flat p-primary groups require a flat topology, while characteristic-p étale theories also exist. The particular formula intended in subject 3 remains unidentified: the ordinary prime-to-p Euler–Swan formula does not settle an unspecified p-primary variant. [R-ArtinMilne1976, §4; R-MilneADT2006, III.8.2 and III.11.1; R-MilneSurface1976; R-SGA5, X.7.1.]

Subjects 4–6 ask for étale Riemann–Roch, Euler characteristics with an operator ring, and explicit Lefschetz formulas for discrete étale or coherent coefficients. SGA5 provides close own-work continuations, rather than merely a shared vocabulary. Its III.4.7 gives the Lefschetz–Verdier formula, and III.6.7 the coherent comparison with relative perfection, properness and Tor-independence retained. In the étale setting, the coefficient ring is a commutative noetherian torsion ring annihilated by an integer invertible in the base field, and the constructible complexes have finite Tor dimension. SGA5 X develops the operator-valued Euler formalism, but the sheet's particular abelian-variety formula is not determined. The oral 1965–1966 seminar and the 1977 volume are different dates. [CL-SA22-040–042; R-SGA5, III.1, III.4, III.6 and X; R-IllusieSGA5.]

Subject 7 asks for satisfactory global duality on schemes of finite type over the integers. Geisser's cycle complexes give global derived duality and finite perfect arithmetic pairings in a precise separated finite-type setting. The opened historical version states the number-ring result conditionally on Beilinson–Lichtenbaum; Voevodsky's later motivic comparison supplies the relevant development. This global theorem is not an unrestricted assertion of sheaf biduality at every characteristic and coefficient order. Subject 8, complexes of differential operators, integrable connections and crystals, has a particularly close own-work continuation in Grothendieck's *Crystals and the de Rham cohomology of schemes*, §§6.4–6.5, from the 1966 lectures published in 1968. Saito's 1989 differential-complex theorems and Narváez Macarro's 2004 comparison provide precise later duality statements over complex manifolds or smooth algebraic varieties, with good coherence and, where needed, holonomicity. The entire positive-characteristic or singular-base heading is not thereby settled. [CL-SA22-043–044; R-Geisser2010, §7.2, Theorem 7.8; R-Voevodsky2011; Grothendieck1968Crystals; Saito1989DifferentialComplexes, Definition 1.15 and Theorems 3.11–3.13; NarvaezMacarro2004, Theorem 3.1.1.]

The analytic-duality heading has a strong realization in Ramis–Ruget 1970, including the separated topological quotients needed for the duality statement. Their account expressly relates the construction to Grothendieck's ideas, but does not identify this particular sheet as its source. Subject 10's pseudocoherent Riemann–Roch has an inserted “Cf SGA6”. SGA6 supplies the algebraic perfect/pseudocoherent framework and a projective lci theorem with an ample target. The analytic map results of Toledo–Tong and O'Brian are further branches with their own hypotheses. The SGA6 annotation is evidence of revisiting the list; it prevents dating every visible layer simply by the boxed 1964. [CL-SA22-045–046; R-RamisRuget1970, §3 Theorem 1 and §4 Theorem 2; R-SGA6, I, III and VIII§3; R-OBrian1987; R-OTT1985.]

### Subjects 11–22: models, approximation, topoi and representability

The Néron cluster begins with “schémas morcelés”, a category not defined by these sheets or identified by the bounded independent-source search. Its Néron-model branch is recognizable; the literal whole heading is not. Subject 12, marked as begun by Raynaud, has an exact later counterpart in Raynaud 1970, Theorem 8.1.4, for the specified proper flat relative curves, with factoriality and residue-field or multiplicity hypotheses. Subject 13 is marked less pressing after Artin's approximation results. Greenberg's 1966 theorem starts from a sufficiently accurate finite-precision solution over a Henselian DVR and gives a quantitative conclusion; Artin approximation starts from an existing formal solution. The modern formal-solution theorem for noetherian Henselian G-rings does not say that Henselianity alone suffices. [CL-SA22-047–049; Neron1964, metadata only; Raynaud1970Picard, §8.1; Greenberg1966, Theorem 1; Artin 1969Approximation, Theorems 1.10 and1.12; StacksApproximation07QX.]

Subjects 14 and 15 ask about fibre functors, representable stacks and the homotopy type of topoi. Deligne's enough-points theorem applies to the coherent setting, formulated here through finite limits and finite covering refinements; it is not a theorem about every topos. Artin–Mazur's hypercovers give a precise developed homotopy construction, and their Theorem 11.1 gives profiniteness in the pointed geometrically unibranch noetherian setting. The uncertain “infinitésimaux” phrase is not repaired by importing a later homotopy theory. The references to partial work by Artin–Mazur and current reflections by Quillen are historical annotations, not complete statements of those authors' results. [CL-SA22-050–051; StacksDelignePoints00YQ, with its SGA4 VI Appendix9.0 attribution; ArtinMazur1969, §8 and Theorem 11.1.]

Subject 16 is deleted and marked with an Artin counterexample. SGA1 X, Remark 2.5, footnote 2, reports an unpublished counterexample in exactly the discussion of a “true” fundamental group incorporating infinitesimal torsors. This is a strong contextual match to the proposed local constancy, but the construction of the example was not recovered. The dossier records a documented negative outcome rather than claiming an independent proof or refuting ordinary étale specialization. Subject 17 is likewise deleted and marked resolved by Raynaud. His Theorem 8.2.1 gives a precise comparison among cohomological flatness, a multiplicity invariant, representability and separatedness for relative curves over a trait satisfying his condition(N)*. It does not establish representability over every possible base. [CL-SA22-052–053; early_SGA1, re-editionp208/originalp271; Raynaud1970Picard, Definition 6.1.4, §6.1.11 and Theorem 8.2.1.]

Subject 18 contains two questions whose scopes must remain separate. SGA3 VIA.3.2 gives a positive quotient theorem over an Artinian local ring for the stated flat locally finite-type group schemes and homomorphism with finite kernel. General scheme-effectivity of flat descent is false: the opened Stacks construction already gives failure over an étale cover, with a complex-threefold base. That counterexample is not Artinian and cannot decide an Artinian-only interpretation of the second phrase. Subject 19, ampleness for cycles of arbitrary dimension, also admits distinct developments: Ottem's ample-subscheme theory and Fulger–Lehmann's positive cones of higher-codimension classes. The former need not be invariant under rational equivalence, so the theories cannot be treated as interchangeable answers. Ottem first circulated on arXiv on 12 May 2011 and was published in 2012; Fulger–Lehmann first circulated on 21 August 2014 and appeared in Algebraic Geometry in 2017. [CL-SA22-054–055 and075; SGA3VIAQuotients, Theorem 3.2; SGA3VQuotients, §4.1; StacksDescent08KE; Ottem2011Ample, Theorem 5.4 and Example7.4; FulgerLehmann2014, Theorem 1.3.]

The next three subjects concern vanishing, comparison with a closed subscheme, and tensor reconstruction. Kempf vanishing provides the dominant-line-bundle theorem for flag varieties, not vanishing for all homogeneous spaces and all line bundles. Gabber's henselian-pair theorem gives the all-degree étale comparison sought in the affine complete/henselian direction. Equality of degree-zero cohomology for one fixed sheaf is insufficient: a complex projective line and a closed point agree in H⁰ for a constant finite sheaf and disagree in H². The source's possible all-sheaf quantifier is therefore preserved as a different reading. Subject 22 explicitly names Saavedra and gerbes; neutral Tannakian reconstruction requires an exact faithful fibre functor, while the nonneutral gerbe framework is separate. [CL-SA22-056–058; R-MilneAG2022, Theorem 22.51; R-Gabber1994; R-Stacks09Z8; R-DeligneMilne1982, Theorem 2.11 and §3.]

### “Pour mémoire”, then subjects 23–28

The “Pour mémoire” block occurs before subject 23 and relates resolution, purity and base change to biduality and finiteness. The dossier retains that placement. Hironaka's characteristic-zero theorem does not settle arbitrary positive-characteristic resolution. Absolute étale purity requires a regular immersion between regular schemes and coefficients invertible on the base. Proper base change works for torsion étale sheaves; the smooth-base-change theorem used here additionally requires invertible torsion orders. The questioned “régulier” does not determine a unique further theorem. Affine cohomological dimension is a dimension bound, not acyclicity. Grauert 1960 and EGAIII 1961 already supply the coherent proper-direct-image core before the list's 1964 date, whereas the intended extensions signalled by “genre” remain unspecified. Tate's rigid-analytic notes circulated in 1962; their later Russian and English publications should not be mistaken for the subject's first appearance. [CL-SA22-067–074; R-Hironaka1964; R-CisinskiDeglise2016, §4.6; R-MilneLEC2013, Theorems 15.1,17.10 and20.1; R-Grauert1960; R-EGAIII1961,3.2.1 as checked through R-Illusie2004Formal, §2.6; R-MilneTate2012, §3.2.]

The final six subjects move to intersections and motives. Subject 23 asks for Chow pullback with target regularity alone, after deleting an earlier regularity restriction on the schemes. Rational intersection rings for finite-dimensional noetherian regular schemes with affine diagonal, integral lci Gysin maps and functoriality for nonsingular projective varieties are verified positive cases. They do not settle an unrestricted integral pullback without fixing the category. Subject 24 contains two different comparisons: Griffiths gives the recorded negative answer concerning algebraic equivalence up to torsion on threefolds; Lieberman's Hodge-theoretic result settles numerical versus rational Betti-homological equivalence on smooth complex projective fourfolds. The latter does not settle the corresponding unrestricted positive-characteristic question. [CL-SA22-059–060 and065; R-SGA6, XIV§8; R-GilletSoule1987; R-Stacks0FEX, R-Stacks0FEZ, R-Stacks0B0H; R-VoisinHodgeSurvey, Theorem 3.1; R-Lieberman1968, bibliography checked, theorem attribution through R-BrownWalker2020, §1.1.]

Subject 25 asks about the product of two algebraically trivial cycles and about the Albanese kernel. Mumford's result, communicated inNovember 1968 and published in 1969, gives the decisive obstruction. On E×E, some products `(p−0)×(q−0)` are nonzero in integral CH₀; otherwise all degree-zero classes would be parametrized by a fixed finite-dimensional family, contradicting Mumford. This is an existential consequence, not an evaluated example at chosen points, a claim about every product, or a claim about a single self-square with rational coefficients. A complex quartic K3 surface supplies a clear Albanese-kernel counterexample. Relative Rosenlicht theory and “Jacobiennes locales” remain too broad to identify uniquely, although generalized Jacobians and higher adèles have precise established forms. The abstract-motives programme has substantial realizations in triangulated rational and étale motives with six operations. These do not automatically supply an unconditional abelian category of all mixed motives and its expected t-structure. [CL-SA22-061–064 and066; R-Mumford1969; R-VoisinHodgeSurvey, Theorem 2.1; R-MilneJV1986, §8; R-BraunlingWolfson2019, Theorem 5.1.2; R-CisinskiDeglise2019; R-CisinskiDeglise2016.]

## Cote 67: conformal surgery, metric choices and arithmetic expectations

### The first surgery sheets and the Bers letter

The early sheets begin with cutting and sewing conformal surfaces and with graphs embedded in them. The desired finite-dimensional moduli description encounters a specific obstruction: a disc component supplies no canonical centre for the proposed boundary normalization. The “pure” combinatorial replacement is not sufficiently defined to identify with a general categorical construction. Euler additivity can be checked; the proposed parameter inequality and the claim that every torus face has genus zero need stronger topological assumptions than those written. The elliptic scraps that follow are related marking dictionaries—primitive lattice vectors, oriented closed geodesics, origins and projective fixed-point choices—rather than a single completed classification theorem. The trace calculation is the classical Joukowski relation. [CL-SA22-101–112; early67 proof checksP01–P03; early_RS2006; early_GrothCartanI1961; early_ChaoLiMumford.]

The six-page letter to Bers dated 15 April 1984 organizes the next major cluster. It proposes an intrinsic capping operation, a product description of its fibres, a description by tangent radii, a spherical “orange-cell” model, and a comparison of metrics. These are five different mathematical ambitions. The opened 2024 CSG transcription verifies the letter's public provenance and text; it is not independent evidence for the truth of its expectations. The letter itself retreats from the suggestion that the desired scaling is simply the freezing of other Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates. [SA22-164; CL-SA22-113–123; early_GrothBers1984, pp1–2 and4–9.]

The clearest positive identification is Mondello's capping theorem. A geodesic boundary of length p receives a semi-infinite flat cylinder; the coordinate `exp(2πiw/p)` turns it into a punctured pointed disc and identifies arclength translation with the required toric rotation. On the stable marked stratum with positive lengths, capping together with the boundary-length vector gives the product parametrization of Theorem 5.4. This is a match of the actual map, not an inference from equal dimensions. The first arXiv circulation was 3 April 2008 and the journal publication 2011. For the earlier letter's zero-length formulation, Mondello's enlarged space retains a projective decoration over the all-zero vector; the ordinary nonnegative-length product requires collapsing that decoration on both sides. [CL-SA22-116–117 and214; early_Mondello2011, §§5.4–5.5, pp659–661; late67 proofs P2.]

Boundary lengths must remain distinct from tangent shadow radii. The early question asks whether admissible caps are determined by their radii and whether the possible radii are independent intervals with potentially finite upper endpoints. No exact settlement of those questions was identified in the bounded search. The later claim of an unrestricted positive-radius target is false: on the sphere marked at 0,1,∞, a cap at 0 omitting 1 and ∞ has normalized conformal radius at most 4 by Koebe's quarter theorem. Radius 5 is impossible. This does not refute the early finite-bound formulation or injectivity onto the feasible image. [CL-SA22-119–120 and216–217; MP-SA22-106–107; L67_Bishop2024Koebe, Theorem 10.15.]

The orange-cell model has its own obstruction. On the pair of pants with lengths(32,0,0), the checked extremal-length lower bound from hyperbolic area exceeds the upper bound supplied by an explicit annulus in the proposed cell complement. The rational comparison is certified in code. The argument permits the two collapsed caps allowed by the letter and refutes the universal fixed-shape model, not the distinct radius-injectivity question. The remaining metric-comparison question likewise is not answered merely by knowing that the capped and uncapped surfaces exist. [CL-SA22-121–123; early67 proofsP06 and computationC03; early_Mirzakhani2007, §§2–3; early_WHW2009, §2.5.]

### Canonical metrics, real structures and nodal enhancements

The middle sheets organize uniformization through concrete real structures and groups of metric symmetries. The positive-Hermitian description of area-one spherical metrics, its pullback action and its hyperbolic-three-space model supply a full check of the compact-group proposition. After compact conjugation, the invariant family is controlled by the common fixed spatial vectors. Its metric is unique exactly when there is no common fixed point on the sphere. The nonunique cases yield the pole, reflection and trivial-group families of dimensions 1, 2 and 3. The surrounding H-invariance hypothesis is indispensable to the self-flagged proposition. [CL-SA22-124–151; early67 proofsP08–P09; early_ACampoPapadopoulos2024, §§4.5–4.6; early_Borcherds261.]

Several local corrections explain how the notebook develops. The author reverses his initial centralization assignment: pole-fixing antiholomorphic maps invert rotations, while pole-exchanging ones centralize them. Abstract extension cohomology and geometric realization are different questions; the geometric noncentral extension splits even though the abstract inversion-action H² can be nonzero. A free double quotient halves area, and the final C* metric convention normalizes circumference to 1. The invariant metric on a complex double descends to geodesic boundary and generally differs from the complete hyperbolic metric of the open interior. The Klein-surface category needs folded dianalytic morphisms; “boundary preserving” alone does not repair every categorical statement. [CL-SA22-132–142 and146–151; early_AllingGreenleaf1969; early_Greenleaf1971.]

Scans 48–51 turn to stable curves with a “propagateur normal”, Galois actions, inertia-like subgroups and arithmetic presentation systems. The ordinary étale interpretation has a concrete negative test. Glue 0 to ∞ and 1 to −1 on P¹ over Q, obtaining a proper stable genus 2 nodal curve with rational branches. Effective descent of finite étale covers gives a free profinite geometric group on two generators and a trivial arithmetic outer action. A Galois-stable procyclic free factor therefore need not carry the cyclotomic character. This refutes an automatic cyclotomicity assertion for ordinary π₁; it does not refute an unspecified logarithmic enhancement [Y,N]. [CL-SA22-152–157; early67 proof P11; early_SGA1, IX.4.12.]

Hoshi–Mochizuki provide substantial qualified development of the log/combinatorial direction. The 2009 preprint published in 2011 assumes, among other conditions, cusps and cuspidal compatibility. The vertex/node-preservation theorem in *Topics II*, Theorem 1.9(ii), is already in the November 2012 RIMS1762 version; its later revision and 2022 book publication are separate dates. Source NN-type and target IPSC-type hypotheses remain essential. None of this identifies every undefined enhancement in the manuscript over every finitely generated characteristic-zero field. For the presentation sheets, the SL₂ central lift and GL₂ reflection relations receive abstract proofs as well as matrix checks. The pure four-puncture group F₂ cannot be PSL₂(Z), which has torsion. The unfinished mod 4 denominator remains unfinished. Bakalov–Kirillov's Lego move complex is a precise topological development, not a proof of every arithmetic expectation for the three presentation systems. [CL-SA22-155–166; early_HoshiMochizuki2011, TheoremA; early_HoshiMochizuki2022, Theorem 1.9(ii); early_ConradSL2; early_BakalovKirillov2000.]

### The later surgery draft: regularity, caps and smoothing tensors

The January 1984 draft returns to the analytic foundations. Reflection proves uniqueness of a boundary complex structure when the chart is a boundary-preserving topological chart holomorphic in the interior. The isolated sentence in which interior holomorphicity is struck out is false: `x+iy↦x+2iy` satisfies the remaining topological conditions and fails the Cauchy–Riemann equations. This is not a refutation of the actual qualified lemma. Likewise, cutting along a Jordan arc, prescribing a bank identification and proving regularity of the resulting seam are different operations. Bishop's theorem shows that even arclength gluing of rectifiable boundaries need not determine a unique ordinary conformal welding; finite-length removability presupposes a realized seam. [CL-SA22-202–211 and281; L67_Bishop1990, p61; L67_Younsi2015, §3.2; late67 proofsP1.]

The late capping and shadow objects coincide with earlier objects and are merged while preserving their later qualifications. The normal tensor now appears on the actual page, whereas the early note had reconstructed it from the phrase “propagateur normal”. The local node model is `xy=t`, with first-order smoothing line the tensor of the two branch tangent lines. A full analytic family additionally needs coordinate germs and a small domain. Deligne–Mumford's formal model and Hubbard–Koch's local plumbing theorem support this local statement, not the proposed unrestricted global inverse. [CL-SA22-224–230; L67_DeligneMumford1969, Theorem 1.6; L67_HubbardKoch2013, §§8–10 and Theorem 9.11.]

A present-run consequence of the checked Koebe estimate refutes that unrestricted tensor target. On a stable four-pointed nodal curve made of two rational components, normalize each node and its two external marks to 0, 1, ∞. Each branch cap has radius at most 4, so the norm of the propagation vector is at most 16. Tensor norm 17 cannot occur. The vector and its dual functional have reciprocal norms; the manuscript's own margin distinguishes them. Small plumbing is unaffected. The hesitant choice-independence argument and its subsequent withdrawal remain separate records, as does the final request to examine the tangent map for the four-holed sphere. ### Four points, involutions and real elliptic curves

The four-point and octahedral sheets have exact algebraic content. Over a quadratically closed field of characteristic different from 2, the standard Klein group generated by `z↦−z` and `z↦1/z` has normalizer S₄; the quotient `z²+z⁻²` has degree 4 and branch values∞,2,−2. The groupoid retains its generic Klein-four isotropy, so a coarse parameter bijection is insufficient. In characteristic 5 the six fixed points form all P¹(F₅), whose stabilizer has order 120, while the chosen Klein normalizer has order 24. The antipodal pairing recovers data lost by the bare six-set. Characteristic 3 and the distinction between a face and an antipodal face pair require separate care. Complete finite enumerations accompany proofs of the relevant field bounds. [CL-SA22-231–240 and257; L67_Faber2023, §4.6; L67_Hua2026v2, §42, Counterexample42.2; late67 proofs P4.]

The module theorem on the following sheets is valid for modules over arbitrary possibly noncommutative rings on which 2 is injective. An involution is encoded by its two eigenspaces and a submodule of their reductions modulo 2 meeting both coordinate axes trivially; morphisms must preserve that submodule. The inverse-image construction proves the equivalence without finite-generation or projectivity assumptions. An exact public match was found in Hua's November 2026 text: v1 §23, Theorem 23.1, and v2 §25, Theorem 25.1. The written proofs were read and an independent proof is included here. Hua reports Lean verification; no Lean files were fetched or executed in this run, and no peer-reviewed publication is asserted. [CL-SA22-241; L67_Hua2026; L67_Hua2026v2; late67 proofs P5.]

The rank-two corollaries do not inherit that full validity. A product ring permits unequal eigenspace ranks on different components. Over a PID, the count of nonsplit classes depends on unit reduction; the deleted surjectivity proviso cannot be omitted. The checked norm-Euclidean ring Z[(1+√−11)/2] has residue fieldF₄ and only the trivial unit image modulo 2, producing three nonsplit classes as well as the split class. The real elliptic calculations then distinguish pointed groups from genus-one torsors and split from nonsplit lattices. The quadratic-form ratio in the hexagonal case is 3, while√3 is the length ratio. A tentative logarithmic j-formula fails reciprocal invariance and the square value 1728. [CL-SA22-242–253; L67_Milne2006; late67 proofs P5–P6.]

The final torsor request is substantially answered on the same page. The lattice sequence gives `H¹(R,E)≅Λ⁺/(1+σ)Λ`, of order 2 in the split case and trivial in the nonsplit case. The unique nontrivial torsor has a fixed-point-free affine antiholomorphic involution and a boundaryless Klein-bottle quotient. This identifies the earlier real genus-one notation and permits a genuine cross-package merge. The cancelled “sans” before “bord” remains a high-weight reading question. [CL-SA22-125 and255; L67_GrossHarris1981, Proposition 1.3 and Corollary 1.4.]

## Cote 68: games, convolution and algebraic structures

### “Jeux de position”: winning, compatibility and termination

The game sheets distinguish positions, legal successors, the player with the initiative, and designated terminal outcomes. A multistrategy chooses a nonempty set of moves at controlled positions and wins only when every compatible maximal play reaches the required terminal target. Thus reachability under the strategy is stronger than bare graph reachability. The cancellation of an early gluing argument and the later reconstruction with compatible paths are part of the argument's development. Neutral terminals are allowed, so a position from which nobody forces a designated target need not be an infinite draw. [CL-SA22-001–018; cote 68 scans 2–14 and 24–32; root proof checks G1–G5.]

The correct modern framework is the least reachability fixed point. Its universal-predecessor clause must exclude terminals, whose outcomes are separate data. Ordinal rank proves the existence of one positional strategy winning from every point of the winning region, even on arbitrary set-sized arenas. This does not prove the existence of a greatest winning multistrategy. Allowing every move that stays in the winning region can permit an infinite compatible loop; unions of individually winning multistrategies may fail. The greatest-policy assertion survives with the actual termination condition. These distinctions are checked by explicit small arenas and independent general proofs, with the modern reachability/safety comparison matched to its own terminal conventions. [CL-SA22-006–009; R-DiekertKufleitner2022, §§2–3; root proof checks G1–G2.]

No infinite play, finite height and finite-stage construction are three different properties. A root above finite chains of every length has no infinite play but no natural-number height bound. In the corresponding universal attractor construction it appears at stageω+1, beyond the union of finite stages. Finite branching at universal positions restores the finite-stage union theorem. The later selector proof has its own reading alarms: the witness reachability must start at the chosen successor, and an elementε(x), not the whole setE(x), is propagated. A finite wrong-start counterexample diagnoses the issue without deciding the ink. [CL-SA22-002,010–015; root proof checks G3–G4 and exact computations.]

Scans 33–34 continue the alternating two-player recursion. Under no infinite play, well-founded induction supports the conditional inclusion argument. The final claimed necessity is stronger and fails at a winning terminal with no successor; the preceding proposition is not thereby refuted. The later N-player restart on scan 48 asserts scheduler uniqueness only where the move labels actually occur at nonterminal positions. This is an elementary coverage fact, proved here; its class D records bounded nonidentification of that exact formulation, not a mathematical open problem. [CL-SA22-401–404; late68 proof checks; R-DiekertKufleitner2022, Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 3.6.]

### The intervening rearrangement and convolution sheets

Scans 15–22 study nonnegative finitely supported functions and families of nested subsets. The scratch profiles are rerun in explicitly specified finite groups. A partially labelled F₂³ interpretation remains a reconstruction, while the odd cyclic E/F interval cones receive a proof for every odd order, supplemented by 270 finite products. The exact two-cone packaging was not identified in the bounded literature search; that bibliographic result does not weaken the proof or establish priority. The global system of half-centres and set families remains less determined than these examples. In particular, an indicator convolution is usually not an indicator, and the written homomorphism does not justify an added surjectivity claim. [CL-SA22-019–021; root proof checks R5; R-HLP1952; R-Pollard1974; R-Grynkiewicz2010; R-MadimanWangWoo2021.]

The core pairing and majorization arguments are classical and admit complete layer decompositions. Equality in maximal rearranged pairing is characterized by cross-nested nonempty superlevel sets. All sorted partial sums, including the first, are needed for weak submajorization. Peak convolution extremality is weaker than full majorization extremality: the four-point sets{0,1,3,4} and{0,1,2,3} have equal self-convolution peaks but different top-two sums. ## What the consolidation changes

The principal improvement is to separate statements that the earlier notes sometimes placed too close together. The capping product is established by the actual map; unrestricted radius and tensor targets are refuted; finite-bound radii and injectivity remain different questions. Ordinary nodal étale groups do not carry the proposed automatic cyclotomic information, while enhanced log/arithmetic programmes have substantial qualified later developments. Uniform positional winning does not imply a greatest permissive strategy. Peak convolution extremality does not imply every majorization inequality. A nilpotent coefficient ideal, a locally nilpotent operator and a unipotent automorphism are different hypotheses. A determinant model is not a uniqueness theorem. These distinctions are the substance of the claims map, rather than labels added to a bibliography.

The earlier runs' genuine contributions remain attributed to them: counterexample ideas, proposed reconstructions and links to external projects are not rewritten as Grothendieck's assertions. Present-run consequences, including the unrestricted normal-tensor obstruction and additional scope repairs, are marked separately. The five shared-object merges preserve all occurrences and their different local roles. The common real-torsor cohomology formula is one claim with its later split/nonsplit evaluations retained. Missing Longue Marche, Dérivateurs and named-run endpoints remain unverified leads in their own table; thematic resemblance is not a checked result.

Finally, the documentary and mathematical tasks are deliberately separable. The source-entry table records read verdicts, the claims table records mathematical scope and searched fate, the problem index preserves the author's questions even when now answered, and the readings table preserves every unresolved ink alarm. Proof appendices and exact computations support the check events at the strength actually attained. An opened bibliography record verifies a real source and its stated access scope; it does not imply that every proof in that work was read. The ranked directions are cuts for future work, with a concrete first step and no development of those research programmes inside this consolidation.
