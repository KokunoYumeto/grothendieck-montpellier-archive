# Ranked directions from late cote 64

These are tasks cut from the consolidation, not developments undertaken here. Ranking combines mathematical interest, centrality to the author’s programme and what a focused research run of a few hours could actually establish. Existing computations and closed authorial steps are inputs, not newly advertised open problems. Every direction names its source items, claims and problem records.

## 1. Identify the exact symmetric level-four normalization

**Interest: highest. Feasibility in one run: high for a local comparison and a precise remaining-obstruction statement; medium for full relative descent.**

Sources: SA16-506, SA16-513, SA16-516, SA16-538, SA16-560–561; C-SA16-504–505, C-SA16-509–515, C-SA16-535, C-SA16-537–538; MP-SA16-161, MP-SA16-164 and MP-SA16-165. The same task runs from scans 83/93 through 100 to 118–121; the later cyclic differences do not fix its scale.

The target is an explicit functor from the symmetric sum-zero halves of the three nonzero two-torsion points to the manuscript’s `Q0`-twisted Legendre pinning, over an arbitrary base with two invertible. It must retain the orientation torsor and tensor-square normalization. A successful result would identify equations 73 and 83c with a precise modern construction, or isolate a specific mismatch.

Known input: the square-root torsor equivalence, the checked finite group and characters, the ordinary Legendre atlas in Antieau–Meier §4, and Conrad’s canonical mixed-level construction in Proposition 3.2. None of these alone identifies the symmetric normalization.

**First step:** choose one ordered split chart and one primitive fourth root, write both pinning maps with the same tangent/cotangent convention, and compare their ratio as a unit. Then test that unit under the two generating branch permutations and under changing the temporary fourth root. End the run with either a descent-compatible formula or a specific failed identity. Do not begin with an arbitrary identification of two order-twelve groups.

## 2. Recover the principal-part convention for the companion edition

**Interest: high for a reliable companion; feasibility: high when the scans are available.**

Sources: SA16-521–529 and SA16-531–533; C-SA16-516–528; MP-SA16-166 and MP-SA16-167 as the neighboring completed branch-restriction and addition steps. The problem records are closed; the new task is editorial and explanatory, not a claim that Riemann–Roch or the cubic construction remains open.

The target is one consistent account of the author’s `ε_n`, the invariant derivation, the cubic coefficient and the graded/filtered distinction. The consolidation proves the all-degree coefficient-one formula cannot be multiplicative, but also shows that monicity by itself does not select the ordinary principal-part convention. A companion should not repair one formula by making another incompatible.

Known input: the two coherent normalization models, the exact positive-characteristic kernel and the corrected Rees kernel, all in `proof_notes.md`, §4. Milne’s standard construction is a comparison model, not a reading of the author’s symbols.

**First step:** inspect the actual marks at scans 102–106 and 115, especially the principal-part arrows and the nonzero tangent hypothesis, then build a small convention table with one Laurent parameter. If the ink still leaves `ε_n` unspecified, retain both possibilities in the commentary and state exactly which conclusions are invariant. No new mathematical claim should be inferred from the choice of typography.

## 3. Make the arithmetic appendix into a complete normalized-cover model

**Interest: high. Feasibility: high for the stated quadratic example; lower for a modular interpretation not written on the page.**

Sources: SA16-580–588 and SA16-556; C-SA16-554–565; MP-SA16-172. The sufficient normalization criterion has been proved in this consolidation, so that theorem is an input rather than a remaining open step.

The target is a companion-ready description of the two affine charts, their overlap, the branch locus and the fibres of the normalized quadratic cover. It should distinguish the order `S²=P`, its normalization, the étale open and ordinary-node singularities. The proposed identification of the unnamed exceptional parameter with `1728` must remain conditional until a source fixes the coordinate and value.

Known input: the signed radicand criterion, `u=(S+r)/2`, the corrected infinity chart `u²−u+mx=0`, and the precise DVR versus higher-dimensional and unramified versus ramified distinctions. The opened EGA and quadratic-algebra references justify the standard ingredients.

**First step:** with an explicitly named integer `λ=4m`, write the overlap map between the finite-`T` normalized order and the `x=1/T` chart, and check it over `Z`, including characteristic two. Only after the integral model is fixed should the run compare it with a modular family. This prevents the unproved identification `λ=1728` from becoming a hidden hypothesis.

## 4. Reconcile the two analytic drafts in a convention-preserving companion

**Interest: medium to high. Feasibility: high.**

Sources: SA16-562–572, SA16-575–577 and SA16-599; C-SA16-539–552; MP-SA16-168–171.

The target is a side-by-side reconstruction of the complex-line, oriented-lattice and framed-family descriptions, keeping the author’s withdrawals visible. The universal covering base, total elliptic family and coarse parameter must be typed separately. The outer-action paragraph should use rank-two abelianization and explicit generator conventions.

Known input: the exact orientation matrix, right/left conversion, automorphy cocycle, semidirect law and free-word modular relations, together with the opened period-family and outer-automorphism results. These settle the local mathematical checks. They do not decide the uncertain bars, inverses, Greek letters or deleted subscript on the scan.

**First step:** construct a correspondence table for each symbol and arrow in scans 131–141 and 143–152, using one declared ordered basis. Test the period map on `τ=i` and on the two modular generators, then insert the scan readings once verified. The deliverable is a consistent commentary with explicit original/corrected formulas, not a rewritten diplomatic transcription.

## 5. Test the proposed normal-complement monodromy comparison

**Interest: high if the external La Longue Marche sources can be supplied. Feasibility: medium; conditional on that source access.**

Sources: SA16-507–509, SA16-538–540 and SA16-576–577; C-SA16-507–510, C-SA16-535 and C-SA16-545–550; MP-SA16-162 and MP-SA16-165. External leads are in `our_project_links.tsv`: LM_P053 S06–S09, B5 and LM2 C.d1/C.r1–C.r3.

The target is an actual comparison of the manuscript’s pinned universal family with the reported `λ(1−λ)` twist and normal complement in `Γ(2)`. A bare isomorphism with `Dic3` is insufficient: branch ordering, tangent character, base point and loop generators must match. Zavyalov’s ordinary-Legendre subgroup is a useful nearby construction, but its defining congruence conditions must not be substituted for the reported normal complement merely because both produce level-four data.

Known input: the finite extension and characters are proved here. The original normal-complement source, its complete conventions and the qualified outer-map assertion are unavailable in the current archive. Their reported statuses are not validated.

**First step:** obtain the exact LM scans and note versions, transcribe the two monodromy representations with their generators, and compare their kernels before comparing the elliptic equations. A single run should be able to establish equality after an explicit conjugation/twist, or identify the exact obstruction and which prior report must be qualified. It should not attempt the full arithmetic-image programme.

## 6. Compare exceptional characteristic behaviour across cotes 62, 63 and 64

**Interest: high. Feasibility: medium for a precise comparison diagram, with deformation calculations separated.**

Sources: SA16-506, SA16-512–513, SA16-553 and SA16-582; C-SA16-505–506, C-SA16-512 and C-SA16-559–561; MP-SA16-161 and MP-SA16-172. The checked folder endpoints are cote62 scans 42–44 and 50, cote63 scans 96–100, and cote64 scans 84–85 and 155–157.

The target is to identify which exceptional behaviour comes from branch relabelling, object automorphisms, a nonreduced fixed scheme, a deformation divisor or the tangent cone of a quadratic order. Those are related objects, but their dimensions and bases differ. The c63 tangent retraction cannot be justified solely from the dyadic DVR calculation, and the c64 characteristic-three fixed parameter is not the characteristic-five bare-octahedron exception.

Known input: both folder endpoints are read and the relevant local formulas are now explicit. The root c63 analysis supplies its own deformation calculation; no result from it should be imported without checking the versal-model and coordinate comparison.

**First step:** name the moduli functors, complete local rings and morphisms in one diagram, with the parameter normalization fixed on every side. Then compare the induced tangent maps. This first run should aim to establish or refute an exact identification, not to infer a common theorem from the words “exceptional” or “tangent.”

## 7. Turn the S20 connection lead into a falsifiable source comparison

**Interest: medium, below the author’s explicit pinning problem. Feasibility: high for a precise test once the missing sources are supplied.**

Sources: SA16-544–550 and SA16-578–579; C-SA16-517–520, C-SA16-537, C-SA16-544 and C-SA16-551; MP-SA16-169, whose holomorphic period construction is the available authorial input. External leads: S20, MPL_93 P09/E14/C29 and the absent cote159 connection material.

The target is a source-level comparison between a named connection in the missing manuscript and the structures that cote64 actually supplies. A fibre derivation, a nonzero tangent vector, a rank-two local system, a Cartier operator on forms, a Hasse invariant and p-curvature are not interchangeable. The earlier-run Picard–Fuchs, Hasse and characteristic-three p-curvature examples have been independently rechecked, but none is attributed to the cote64 page.

Known input: the period construction and the exact bridge calculations are available; the historical identification is not. Katz’s opened results distinguish zero p-curvature descent from the nilpotence bound.

**First step:** read the absent connection passage and record its base, bundle, rank, connection, integral model and reduction operation. Compare those six data with the existing examples before computing anything further. A successful few-hour run can confirm an exact identification or rule out a proposed one; it cannot infer Grothendieck’s historical intention from vocabulary alone.
