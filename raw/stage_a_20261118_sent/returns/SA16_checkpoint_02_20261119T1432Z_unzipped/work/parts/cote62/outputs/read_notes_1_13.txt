ENTRY 1 ### A01 — The divisor convention and the Lie line

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 004; opening definition and first displays.  
**Kind:** reading question.  
**Manuscript:** “multiples” de D; “faisceau de Lie”.

The prose about “multiples” of the zero divisor sits beside a section \(1:\mathcal O_C\to\underline L\), positive pole spaces, and \(s^*\underline L\simeq\operatorname{Lie}(C/S)\), so the working convention behaves as \(\mathcal O_C(D)\), not as the ideal \(\mathcal O_C(-D)\). The normal-bundle identity explains that convention: \(s^*\mathcal O_C(D)=N_{D/C}\simeq s^*T_{C/S}\), whereas invariant differentials give its dual. This is an interpretative consistency question, not a proposed replacement of “multiples” in the frozen text.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 54, scan 3, distinguishes the tangent line from invariant differentials; N, D01–D02, already flags variance in universal extensions; cote 62 adds an explicit divisor realization, with a possible sign-convention ambiguity.  
**Results register:** WG2 §1.1 #3 (unchecked placement question): additional elliptic/Picard material.

ENTRY 3 ### A03 — Riemann–Roch needs the relative base-change step

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 005; Proposition 1 and proof.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “on est ramené au cas”; “pour n=0 elle est connue par ailleurs”.

For a degree-\(n\) line bundle on a genus-one fibre, Riemann–Roch and duality give \(h^0=n,h^1=0\) when \(n>0\), \(h^0=0,h^1=-n\) when \(n<0\), and the trivial bundle has \(h^0=h^1=1\). The passage from these fibre calculations to locally free direct images and arbitrary base change uses the relative theorem, with the degree-zero trivial-bundle case separately justified, as the manuscript itself indicates. A modern framework is the perfect complex computing proper flat cohomology and its base changes (Stacks Project, n.d., Tag 0A1G); fibre dimensions alone should not be substituted for that argument over a nonreduced base.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 47, scans 7–8, also uses cohomological base change, but for the particular Fourier comparison map; N, F7–F8, leaves its unit-fibre compatibility open; this simpler positive-degree calculation supplies no missing kernel computation.  
**Results register:** WG2 §2.4 #4: a related method, not a completed Fourier witness.

ENTRY 4 ### A04 — The omitted length-two separation argument

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 005; last paragraph.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “un raisonnement connu n'utilisant que RR donne le résultat”.

The missing very-ampleness argument is to test every length-two subscheme \(Z\) of a geometric fibre: for \(n\geq3\), the line bundle \(\mathcal O_C(nD)(-Z)\) has positive degree and hence vanishing \(H^1\), making restriction to \(Z\) surjective. This separates distinct points and tangent directions, and relative base change then supplies the relative embedding; the general degree-\(2g+1\) criterion is recorded in Stacks Project (n.d., Tag 0H2V). The uncertain inequality in the proposition remains a reading question even though this argument identifies the mathematical threshold.

**Context comparison:** No matching pole-divisor very-ampleness proof occurs in T/N/D; T, cote 44, scan 4, has hyperplane-section statements of a different kind.

ENTRY 5 ### A05 — Universal injectivity and the exceptional degree one

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 006; Proposition 2.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “universellement injective”; “n,m … ≠1”.

The universal-injectivity assertion requires the cokernels of the inclusions \(\mathcal E_i\subset\mathcal E_j\) to be locally free, obtainable successively from the divisor exact sequences and the degree-positive \(H^1\)-vanishing. Degree one is exceptional because \(\mathcal E_1=\mathcal E_0\), so its associated graded piece is zero rather than a line. The displayed exclusion is mathematically material and should be retained when comparing the multiplication maps with the later intrinsic description.

ENTRY 6 ### A06 — What does generation by E₃ mean?

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 006–007; filtered-algebra paragraph and second corollary.  
**Kind:** reading question.  
**Manuscript:** “E₃ engendre Γ_*(f,L)”.

Taken as a statement about the filtered affine algebra of \(C-D\), generators with poles of orders two and three give the intended conclusion by their leading terms and induction on pole order. Taken literally as generation of the homogeneous section ring by its degree-three piece alone, it cannot generate degrees one and two; that ring instead uses a degree-one section together with weighted degree-two and degree-three generators. The surrounding shift between the filtered colimit and the direct sum of its filtered pieces is therefore a substantive notation question, not a license to rewrite the corollary.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 44, scan 3, distinguishes a graded direct sum from a product completion; N, D07, makes category restrictions consequential; the present ambiguity is between filtered and homogeneous generation, not that completion issue.

ENTRY 8 ### A08 — The leading-pole calculation and characteristic p

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 007–008; Proposition 3 and live corollary.  
**Kind:** unpursued implication.  
**Manuscript:** “ssi p est premier à n”; “est nul”.

For a local parameter \(z\), an invariant vector field with \(D(z)\) a unit gives \(D(z^{-n})=-nD(z)z^{-n-1}\), which explains exactly the invertibility and vanishing of the induced leading-term map. The vanishing when \(p\mid n\) concerns this associated graded map, not the whole derivative of every function with pole order \(n\), whose lower-order terms can survive. It consequently does not compute a Hasse invariant, a pth-power operator on the Lie line, or the full \(F,V\)-structure.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 54, scan 7, and N, D03–D05, concern Frobenius, Verschiebung, and restricted Lie data; the present calculation adds a concrete characteristic-p example without settling those comparisons.  
**Results register:** WG2 §1.1 #3 (unchecked placement question): additional elliptic/Picard material.
