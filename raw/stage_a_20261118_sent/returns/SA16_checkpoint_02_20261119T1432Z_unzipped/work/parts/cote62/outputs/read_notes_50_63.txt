ENTRY 50 ### C01 — The trisection definition repeats the wrong apparent base

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 042; opening sentence.  
**Kind:** reading question.  
**Manuscript:** “non ramifié sur X, d'ordre 3 sur X”.

The written definition places both unramifiedness and degree three over \(X\), although the subsequent construction treats \(X'\) as a finite étale degree-three object over \(S\) embedded in the relative genus-zero curve \(X\). A closed subscheme of \(X\) can be unramified over \(X\), but that does not express the asserted three-point family over the base. The expected \(S\)-relative formulation is a mathematical reading question only, and the repeated \(X\) remains in the transcription.

ENTRY 51 ### C02 — Why a trisection gives an S₃ torsor

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 042; classification paragraph.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “classification dans un H¹(S,S₃)”.

For the intended finite étale trisection, the scheme of its orderings is an \(S_3\)-torsor; over that cover, the unique projective transformation taking the ordered three points to \(0,1,\infty\) trivializes the genus-zero curve. Conversely an \(S_3\)-torsor twists that standard marked projective line, giving inverse descent constructions. This explains the classification of pairs and their automorphisms; a torsor need not be connected even if it is called a Galois covering.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 16, scan 56, explicitly records finite étale trivialization after faithfully flat base change; the present pair-classification adds its concrete descent datum; the general torsor-plus-equivariant-map formalism is Stacks Project (n.d., Tag 04UV).

ENTRY 52 ### C03 — The associated trisection is an involutive construction

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 042; X′ to X″ paragraph.  
**Kind:** self-flagged.  
**Manuscript:** “on restitue X′ [à préciser…]”.

Because \(\sigma\) centralizes the standard \(S_3\)-action, it descends through every ordering torsor and takes the original triple to the associated harmonic triple. The identity \(\sigma^2=1\) then returns the original trisection, so the construction is intrinsic under the tame hypotheses used in the explicit formulas. This supplies the requested clarification once the pair-descent construction has been made precise.

ENTRY 53 ### C04 — The bisection is descended from the cyclotomic fixed locus

ENTRY 54 ### C05 — The invariant is complete on the free-action locus

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 044–046; last bracket of scan 044 and first sentence of scan 046.  
**Kind:** self-flagged.  
**Manuscript:** “À déduire … des formalismes catégoriques généraux”; “l'invariant suffit”.

Where \(i\) and \(i-1\) are units, the pullback of \(U\to\mathbf A^1_i\), with \(U=\mathbf P^1-\{0,1,\infty\}\), is an \(S_3\)-torsor because the action is free there. An equivariant map from the ordering torsor of a triple to that pullback is an isomorphism of torsors, which recovers the triple from \(i\). This is an object-level equivalence on the free locus, and it does not extend unchanged across the ramified fibres.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 16, scan 56, gives the relevant finite étale descent mechanism; Stacks Project (n.d., Tag 04UV) supplies the general quotient-stack formalism; no identical invariant reconstruction appears in T/N/D.  
**Results register:** Hua, findings register, 62-triples-rigidified-moduli (candidate claim, not an established novelty result).  
**Own-idea confidence and reason:** High for this completion of the bracketed step: an equivariant morphism between two torsors is locally a translation and therefore an isomorphism.

ENTRY 56 ### C07 — Finiteness of normalization and the degree-three algebra

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 046; two numbered conditions.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “une certaine extension de corps — de degré 3”.

After making the base interpretation explicit, the generic object may be a product of separable fields, so a degree-three étale algebra is more general than a degree-three field extension. Over a locally Noetherian normal integral base, its integral closure is finite by the separable trace-pairing argument, applied componentwise (Stacks Project, n.d., Tag 032L). The remaining substantive requirement is that this finite normalization be étale everywhere; finiteness does not itself provide that condition.

ENTRY 57 ### C08 — When the rational-section condition becomes automatic

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 046; condition (ii).  
**Kind:** own idea.  
**Manuscript:** “Qu'une certaine application rationnelle … est partout définie”.

Suppose \(S\) is normal integral, \(i\) is regular, and the full generic \(S_3\)-torsor extends to a finite étale torsor \(T\to S\). Since \(U\to\mathbf A^1_i\) is finite and \(T\) is normal, the generic equivariant map \(T\dashrightarrow U\) extends uniquely by integrality of its coordinate functions, and equivariance extends by uniqueness. Under these hypotheses the extra section-extension condition follows from the étale-torsor extension, although mere unramifiedness at codimension-one points would not justify this argument.

ENTRY 58 ### C09 — The branch indices give local divisibility tests

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 019, 046; normal local scratch work and ‘quels i’ question.  
**Kind:** own idea.  
**Manuscript:** “quels i conviennent ??”

Over a discrete valuation ring with six invertible and \(i\) generically different from zero and one, the tame local models give \(2\mid v(i)\) when the closed point maps to zero and \(3\mid v(i-1)\) when it maps to one. These conditions make the relevant extracted-root extension unramified: after removing the divisible uniformizer power, the remaining equation extracts a prime-to-residue-characteristic root of a unit. They explain the exponents suggested on scan 019, but are local tests and do not automatically extend to every normal higher-dimensional base.

ENTRY 59 ### C10 — Codimension-one unramifiedness is insufficient on a singular normal base

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 046; normalization criterion.  
**Kind:** own idea.  
**Manuscript:** “est non ramifié sur X”.

Take \(R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xy-z^2)\), with \(k\) algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and \(i=x\): its divisor is twice the prime \((x,z)\), so the order-two divisibility test passes in codimension one. The normalization obtained by adjoining \(\sqrt{x}\) is \(k[[u,v]]\), with \(x=u^2,y=v^2,z=uv\), the quotient cover by simultaneous sign change; it is étale away from the closed singular point but ramified there. Since \(R\) is strictly henselian, a realizing triple would have trivial ordering torsor and a local harmonic parameter with \(i\) a unit times a square, forcing \(\sqrt{x}\in R\), a contradiction. This is a counterexample to replacing the manuscript's everywhere-unramified requirement by only divisorial tests.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 16, scans 52 and 56, keeps ramification and étaleness as scheme-level conditions; no matching singular-base example was found in T/N/D.  
**Results register:** Hua, findings register, 62-triples-rigidified-moduli (candidate claim, not an established novelty result).  
**Own-idea confidence and reason:** High: the explicit invariant-ring cover is finite, free away from the origin, and has a fixed point at the origin; the contradiction uses only Henselian square roots of units.

ENTRY 60 ### C11 — Invariant zero and the harmonic stabilizer

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 048; first live assertion.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “H¹(S,Z/2)”.

On a reduced base with six invertible, a section with \(i=0\) factors through the reduced harmonic orbit \(\{-1,1/2,2\}\), whose stabilizer is a constant order-two subgroup. Reduction of the ordering torsor to that stabilizer gives the claimed \(H^1(S,\mathbf Z/2)\) description, retaining the usual groupoid automorphisms. Over a nonreduced base the factorization into that reduced orbit need not follow from \(i=0\), as C13 makes explicit.

ENTRY 61 ### C12 — Invariant one has a cyclotomic twist

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 048; second special-value assertion.  
**Kind:** own idea.  
**Manuscript:** “H¹(S,Z/3)”.

On a reduced base with six invertible, the order-three stabilizer is naturally the cyclotomic form \(\mu_3\), so the special fibre is described, after choosing its evident neutral object, by \(H^1(S,\mu_3)\), becoming the written constant-group description after splitting the cyclotomic cover. Over \(\mathbf Q\), the triple \((\mathbf P^1,\{x^3=1\},\infty)\) has \(i=1\), but its ordering torsor has nontrivial sign character from \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\), so it cannot reduce to the constant alternating subgroup \(C_3\subset S_3\). The automorphisms fixing infinity and preserving those three roots are multiplication by \(\mu_3\), which gives a direct check of the missing twist.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 16, scan 52, n°5, and N's Kummer qualification distinguish root extraction from a split constant cyclic action; this provides a specific counterexample to an unrestricted reading of scan 048.  
**Results register:** Hua, findings register, 62-triples-rigidified-moduli (candidate claim, not an established novelty result).  
**Own-idea confidence and reason:** High: the discriminant of \(x^3-1\) has square class \(-3\), and the affine automorphism group of its root set fixing infinity is exactly \(\mu_3\).

ENTRY 62 ### C13 — Infinitesimal special fibres are not just stabilizer torsors

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 048; both special-invariant classifications.  
**Kind:** own idea.  
**Manuscript:** “En tous cas, si i=0”; “Et si i=1”.

Let \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), with \(k\) algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and take the trivial ordering torsor with \(t=-1+\epsilon\): the squared factor in B16 makes \(i(t)=0\), yet no constant anharmonic transform takes this nonconstant section to \(t=-1\). All finite étale torsors under \(C_2\) over \(S\) are trivial, so the two resulting triples cannot both be classified by the stated \(H^1(S,C_2)\); similarly \(t=\zeta_6+\epsilon\) has \(i=1\) by the cubic factor but is not equivalent to the constant cyclotomic section. Thus the scheme-theoretic branch fibres contain infinitesimal directions omitted by the reduced stabilizer description. The earlier nilpotent warning on scan 019 is relevant evidence that this issue was already visible in the scratch work.

**Context comparison:** T, cote 16, scan 56, has étale descent but no infinitesimal branch example; R's Hua entry needs this qualification if its equivalence is taken to include classification by the scalar invariant at special values.  
**Results register:** Hua, findings register, 62-triples-rigidified-moduli (candidate claim, not an established novelty result).  
**Own-idea confidence and reason:** High: the double- and triple-zero formulas make the calculation exact modulo \(\epsilon^2\), and every Möbius transform has nonzero derivative at these allowed points.

ENTRY 63 ### C14 — The intermediate double quotient and its quadratic cover

**Source:** 62.pdf, cote 62, scan(s) 048; diagram and last line.  
**Kind:** unproved claim.  
**Manuscript:** “trois sections”; “it²”; “√i”.

After quotienting by the normal order-three subgroup and choosing the three indicated disjoint sections as infinity, zero, and one, the remaining degree-two map is normalized as \(u\mapsto iu^2\). Its preimage of one is \(u^2=i^{-1}\), which is isomorphic to the quadratic cover defined by \(\sqrt{i}\) because \(i\) is a unit. This explains the last phrase while keeping the intermediate coordinate and its normalization distinct from the original anharmonic parameter.
