## P1. Pole filtration and cubic model

Let f:C→S be an elliptic scheme, D its identity Cartier divisor, and T=Lie(C/S). The conormal I_D/I_D² is T^∨, hence O_C(D)|_D=T. On a geometric fibre, Riemann–Roch and Serre duality give h⁰(O(nD))=n, h¹=0 for n>0; (1,1) for n=0; and (0,−n) for n<0. The base-change proof must use more than constant fibre dimensions over a nonreduced base. For n>0, vanishing of fibre H¹ gives locally free H⁰ and arbitrary base change. For n<0, fibre H⁰ vanishes; the two-term finite-free cohomology complex has an injective split first differential locally and hence locally free H¹, compatible with base change. For n=0, the unit section maps onto the one-dimensional fibre H⁰, so the cohomology/base-change criterion gives H⁰=O universally and locally free H¹ of rank1. This uses the proper-flat relative theorem and its two-term complex, not a deduction from dimensions alone. For n≥2 the divisor sequence gives E_n/E_(n−1)=T^n; E_1=E_0=O. All nonnegative filtration inclusions have locally free cokernels, so remain injective after base change.

For degree n≥3 and a length-two subscheme Z of a geometric fibre, H¹(O(nD)(−Z))=0 since the dual line has negative degree 2−n. Thus sections separate length-two subschemes, including tangent vectors. Degree one has one section and degree two gives a degree-two map, so neither is very ample. Degree positive is exactly the fibrewise ampleness condition. The relative assertion uses the relative ampleness/embedding theorem.

Choose pole-order 2 and 3 functions y,x. Products with leading orders 2a+3b generate each filtered quotient n≥2; subtract a leading term and induct on n. This proves generation of the filtered algebra. The homogeneous section ring needs the degree-one generator as well. Seven monomials of pole order≤6 lie in a rank-six module, yielding a primitive relation x²+ay³+bxy+cy²+dx+ey+f. The |3D| plane embedding has ideal a relative effective Cartier cubic. Two local equations differ by a unit because both generate this invertible ideal; this is stronger than saying a scalar is nonzero. Homogenization must include eyz². A rank-three projective bundle has fibres P².

An invariant vector field extends to localization along D. In a parameter z, D(z^−n)=−nD(z)z^−n−1. Thus the leading map is scalar −n; divisibility by p kills the leading map, not necessarily the derivative itself. Inversion fixes the pole-two coordinate in the general Weierstrass equation. When 2 is invertible, projectors (1±[−1])/2 split E_3 and the odd part complements E_2; the leading derivative coefficient −2 gives the claimed map. In characteristic two that map need not be a complement.

The admissible triangular changes preserve the pole filtration, the flex point and tangent. Weights satisfy μ²=λ³; u=μ/λ gives u²=λ,u³=μ. This does not require roots. Completing the square requires 2 invertible, and depressing the cubic requires 3 invertible. A weight-12 discriminant can be set to one after adjoining a twelfth root of a unit: the algebra is monic finite free and faithfully flat, but is étale only when 12 is invertible. The five/six coefficient atlas does not prove minimality among all possible atlases.

## P2. Anharmonic quotient and its arithmetic

Work over Z[1/6]. Set U=P¹−{0,1,∞}, Q=t²−t+1, F=(t+1)(2t−1)(t−2), and i=−F²/(27t²(t−1)²). The program verifies invariance under all six anharmonic maps, the composition table with row applied first, and 1−i=4Q³/(27t²(t−1)²). Hence the fibres over 0,1,∞ have multiplicities 2,3,2 at the harmonic triple, cyclotomic pair and removed triple respectively. The cyclic quotient is f=F/(3√−3 t(t−1)), after adjoining √−3. Its derivative is a unit times Q²/[t²(t−1)²]. Thus the two C3-fixed points are the branch points of the degree-three map. The harmonic triples are unramified at that stage and ramify in the later degree-two quotient. This distinguishes the two maps confused in scan34.

The matrix J=[[1,−2],[2,−1]] has determinant3 and square−3I, so σ(t)=(t−2)/(2t−1) is an involution over Z[1/6]. The program checks that it centralizes the two generators 1−t,1/t, exchanges the two triples and sends i to i^−1. The normalizer can also be checked over every Z[1/6]-algebra, including nonreduced ones. Fppf locally lift a projective centralizer element to an invertible matrix M. Write P=[[-1,1],[0,1]] and R=[[0,1],[1,0]]. Projective commutation gives MPM^−1=aP and MRM^−1=bR for unitsa,b. Squaring gives a²=b²=1; the trace ofPR is1, so conjugatingPR gives ab=1. Since2 is invertible, the base splits into open-and-closed loci where a=b=1 or a=b=−1. In the first case the two linear commutator equations force M to be scalar. In the second, the two anticommutator equations force M=cJ; detM=3c² makes c a unit. Thus the centralizer sheaf is exactly the constantC2, with no infinitesimal thickening. Every automorphism of the constantS3 is locally inner and its centre is trivial. Multiplying a normalizer element by the correspondingS3 section therefore gives a centralizer element, so N_PGL2(S3)=S3×C2 as fppf group sheaves, hence as group schemes. The matrix equations are independently rerun symbolically. Beauville supplies the field antecedent; this relative argument is a reconstruction in this run, not attributed to his field statement. The stabilizer of the C3-fixed pair is the torus split after adjoining the roots ofQ, extended by inversion.

An ordered trisection of a genus-zero curve determines its unique coordinate 0,1,∞; its ordering scheme is an S3 torsor. Descent therefore identifies pairs (X,D) with S3 torsors, and triples with a disjoint marked section with torsors T equipped with an equivariant T→U. Conversely this datum descends the standard triple and section. These constructions on objects and isomorphisms are inverse. On the open i(i−1) invertible the action is free, so the quotient stack is represented by that open in A¹_i. At exceptional values one cannot replace the entire scheme-theoretic fibre by its reduced residual gerbe.

Because σ centralizesS3, it descends to an intrinsic automorphism on every unpointed trisection form. It sends D to the disjoint harmonic tripleD′. Order D and transport its ordering byσ toD′: the resulting intrinsic involution is σσ σ^−1=σ, independently of the ordering. Applying the associated-trisection construction twice therefore returnsD. The fixed locus ofσ is the S3-stable quadratic divisorQ=0; its discriminant−3 is a unit over Z[1/6], so it descends to an étale bisection. These assertions do not say thatσ maps U to itself: it exchanges two different removed triples. This distinction matters when a marked section is added.

For R=k[ε]/ε², char k≠2,3, t=−1+ε has i=0 because F² is divisible by ε². It is not in the constant harmonic orbit: the orbit values −1,2,1/2 have no ε coefficient, and reduction selects only −1; its nontrivial stabilizer changes the first-order coefficient to its negative. A nonzero tangent vector cannot become zero after a faithfully flat extension. Thus the unrestricted BC2 assertion is false. Likewise t=ρ+ε, Q(ρ)=0, has i=1 but is not the constant cyclotomic orbit; 1−i vanishes to order3. On reduced bases these equations force F=0 or Q=0, respectively.

There is a second arithmetic issue at i=1: Q has discriminant−3. Without √−3 the C3 stabilizer is twisted. The triple given by roots of x³−1 and marked infinity over Q has cyclic automorphism group μ3, not the constant C3 group scheme. The alternating function r=F/[t(t−1)] has r²=−27i, so the sign quotient of an ordering torsor has square class−3i. It differs from the fixed bisection with square class i. These are distinct constructions, though they agree after a cyclotomic base change.

The quotient of a proper elliptic curve by inversion is proper and has fibres P¹, so cannot itself be a torsor under an affine line. Removing its marked infinity makes an affine-line torsor. On this torsor an étale n-section has a barycentre whenever [n] is an isomorphism of the acting commutative group: étale-locally sum its n points relative to an origin and divide by n; changing the origin changes the sum by n times that change, so the barycentre glues. The continuation scans52,54 does exactly this for n=3. The resulting line and centred cubic have coefficient weights2,3. An elliptic lift needs a square-root line M with M²=L, giving weights4,6. Its obstruction is the Kummer class of L in H²(μ2); when lifts exist, their groupoid is a μ2 gerbe and isomorphism classes form an H¹(μ2) torsor over the fixed data. This is an explicit reconstruction, not stack terminology quoted from Grothendieck.

Any genus-zero curve with a degree-three Cartier divisor D already has the degree-one line O(D)⊗ω. Its direct image has rank2 and evaluation gives a projective-bundle identification. This kills the Brauer obstruction but provides neither a trivial rank-two bundle nor an ordering of D.

## P3. Extension across a normal base

For normal noetherian integral S, a finite separable generic algebra has finite normalization (componentwise for a product); the trace-pairing argument proves finiteness in the domain case. It must be étale at every point to provide a trisection. Once the full generic ordering torsor extends finite étale, its generic equivariant map to U extends uniquely: U→A¹_i is finite, so its coordinate functions are integral over the regular invariant; normality of the covering forces them to extend. Uniqueness also preserves equivariance. Thus the section-extension condition is redundant under these precise stronger hypotheses, not under an arbitrary cubic normalization condition.

Over a strictly henselian DVR with 6 invertible and generic i≠0,1, a lift near i=0 requires v(i) even and near i=1 requires v(i−1) divisible by3. These conditions are sufficient étale-locally because the remaining units admit the necessary prime-to-characteristic roots by Hensel lifting; the local quotient is a square or cube times a unit. Globally on a merely normal base divisorial conditions are insufficient. Let R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xy−z²), k algebraically closed characteristic0. R=k[[u,v]]^{±1}, x=u²,y=v²,z=uv; it is normal, complete and strictly henselian. Its height-one divisor (x,z) has v(x)=2. The finite normalization R⊂k[[u,v]] in Frac(R)(√x) is étale away from the closed point but not there: the involution fixes the origin, or equivalently the cotangent map is not étale. Set i=x; i−1 is a unit. A harmonic lift would imply √x after extracting a unit square root in R, impossible since √x∉R. This is the singular-base failure of purity, consistent with SGA1 X.3.1, whose target is regular.

## P4. Completion, filtered lifting and missing hypotheses

For a countable decreasing filtration, a series whose terms tend to zero is Cauchy by the ultrametric maximum inequality. Conversely choose a subsequence of a Cauchy sequence whose successive differences lie in the nth neighbourhood and sum them. This proves the series criterion; uniqueness of its limit additionally requires separatedness. To prove a quotient complete, successively lift a rapidly Cauchy subsequence while correcting the lifts by kernel elements. Closed submodules are complete. A nonclosed quotient may be complete but nonseparated: the typescript explicitly uses that convention.

For A[[X]], membership in (m,X)^n says its jth coefficient belongs to m^(n−j) for 0≤j<n. These are exactly a cofinal family of product-coefficient m-adic neighbourhoods. Coefficientwise sums prove completeness when A is complete, even if sums are nonunique. The printed p,q≤n filtration inequality is false for decreasing filtrations: A=k[[u]], E=F=A, p=q=0,n=1 gives A²⊄uA². Reversing the inequality is a valid sufficient repair.

For a filtered map E→F with E complete, F separated, and surjective associated graded, lift the initial form of the error, subtract, and iterate; the errors approach zero and the sum of corrections converges. Starting in filtration n proves strict surjectivity, assuming the usual exhaustive, bounded-below conventions. The same construction with coefficients in complete A and separated E lifts finitely many homogeneous generators of gr E to generators of E. Applying it to each submodule with the induced filtration proves the noetherian implication. Applied to E/mE it proves the complete version of Nakayama without assuming E finite. Separatedness is necessary: k((t)) over k[[t]] has E/tE=0 but E≠0.

Artin–Rees follows by considering ⊕(I^nE∩F)t^n inside the finite Rees module over the noetherian Rees ring. Finite homogeneous generators of degrees≤c imply the displayed equality for n≥c. It identifies intrinsic and induced filtrations. Inverse limits of the quotient short exact sequences are then exact since their transition maps are surjective. A finite presentation yields Ê≅Â⊗E. Exactness on finite modules yields flatness of Â by the ideal criterion; faithful flatness uses I⊂Jac(A), as reduction mod I meets every maximal ideal. The tensor-completion identity itself does not require that Jacobson hypothesis. Completion preserves each discrete quotient and associated graded. A separated complete finitely generated-adic ring with noetherian quotient is noetherian by the graded lifting argument. Hence formal power series over a noetherian ring are noetherian.

A strict exact sequence of metrizable abelian groups remains exact after separated completion. To check surjectivity, use rapidly convergent quotient approximations and strictness to lift differences tending to zero; to identify the kernel use the induced topology and closure of the first image. Mere continuity is insufficient: Q_discrete→Q_usual→0 is algebraically exact with continuous maps, but Q→R→0 after completion fails surjectivity. Strict maps are not arbitrarily composable: Z→R, n↦nα (α irrational), is a closed discrete embedding and R→R/Z an open quotient; their composite has dense, nondiscrete image and is not strict. This refutes the general shortcut, not the exact-sequence theorem. The extension target of E→H is Ĥ unless H is already complete.

For a Lie algebra g finite as a module over noetherian commutative K, the standard degree filtration on U(g) has gr U(g) a quotient of Sym_K(g), a noetherian algebra, so U(g) is left and right noetherian. PBW injectivity or freeness is not needed for this argument. Without finiteness of g, an infinite-dimensional abelian Lie algebra has U(g)=k[x1,x2,…], with a strictly ascending chain of ideals generated by its first n variables. Scan58's Nakayama scratch only produces a basis after suitable finite/flat and faithfulness hypotheses; vanishing of a coefficient in B does not imply its vanishing in A for a noninjective A→B.

## P5. Valuations

A maximal local extension of a place to an algebraically closed residue target exists by Zorn. The argument adjoining either x or x^−1 proves its domain is a valuation ring. A place is a map from that domain to the residue target, not a field homomorphism defined on every nonzero element. The exact theorem allows any homomorphism from any subring of a field to a fixed algebraically closed field k, and extends it to a k-valued place without enlarging k. Deuring1959 Lecture3, Section5, printedpp12–13 and Gardam2024 Theorem1.97, printedpp21–22 state and prove these exact quantifiers.

The manuscript uses a different but valid route. Its initial general extension theorem gives a place into some overfield L of k; that theorem is an input, not proved anew here. Choose by Zorn a maximal intermediate field F between k and L with a k-valued place fixing k. If x in L is transcendental over F, evaluation x→0 followed by the existing place defines such a place on F(x), contradicting maximality. Thus L/F is algebraic. Extending the valuation to L gives a residue extension algebraic over k, hence equal to k because k is algebraically closed; the place therefore extends to L. Composition returns a k-valued place of the original field extending the given subring map. The exact external sources were opened before identifying this as a known theorem. Whether its proof belongs at this point in the reused rédaction is the separate editorial question on scan35.

For an algebraic valued extension, a polynomial relation on x has at least two terms of minimal value; their equality shows a nonzero multiple of v(x) lies in the base value group. Hence the quotient of value groups is torsion. Reducing a suitably rescaled integral equation gives algebraicity of the residue extension. Convex subgroups correspond in an ordered-group inclusion with torsion quotient: saturation and intersection are inverse, so rank is unchanged.

For a finite extension L/K, choose elements whose values represent distinct cosets modulo Γ_K and residue representatives linearly independent over k_v. The products are K-linearly independent: group a relation by value coset, choose its least value, rescale one group and reduce; distinct value cosets prevent cancellation between groups, and residue independence prevents cancellation within one. This proves ef≤[L:K]. It also proves finite index and finite residue degree. A finite-index ordered overgroup of Z embeds into (1/e)Z and is discrete; the converse follows by restricting a discrete group with finite index. An infinite algebraic extension need not remain discrete, for example the union of k((t^(1/n!))).

Prolongation rings are the localizations of the integral closure at primes over the maximal ideal, and the integral closure is their intersection in L. Distinct prolongations are incomparable; residue separation in finitely many incomparable valuation rings gives the extension-count argument. Full simultaneous approximation requires independence, not mere distinctness in higher rank. For a concrete distinction, on k(x)((u)) compose the u-adic valuation with the x-adic and (x−1)-adic valuations of k(x): the two rank-two valuations are incomparable but share the nontrivial u-adic coarsening, so cannot approximate 0 and u^−1 simultaneously to positive u-adic accuracy. This tests the precise hypothesis rather than weakening the typed theorem.

## P6. Classical elliptic functions

Throughout this section a lattice is rank two over Z in C and has an oriented basis 2ω1,2ω2 with Im(ω2/ω1)>0. Two merely distinct periods do not suffice: exp(z) has 2πi and 4πi as periods and is not an elliptic function of a rank-two lattice. The later definition explicitly requires real independence.

The argument principle on a translated fundamental parallelogram gives equal zero/pole counts; opposite edges cancel the residue integrals. Integrating z f′/f gives equality of divisor sums modulo the lattice. A holomorphic elliptic function is constant by compactness. Thus a single simple pole is impossible; two functions with the same divisor differ by a constant, and with the same principal parts differ by a constant. Dimension growth of bounded pole spaces proves algebraic dependence. These are the exact compact-torus arguments behind the stated general properties.

Regularized lattice sums for wp and ζ and the canonical product for σ converge normally on compact sets off the lattice, because subtraction leaves summands O(|ω|^−3) and lattice sums of exponent>2 converge. Pairing ±ω gives wp even, ζ odd and σ odd. Differentiation gives ζ′=−wp and σ′/σ=ζ. Comparing Laurent coefficients in wp′²−4wp³ yields −g2 wp−g3, g2=20c1,g3=28c2. All poles cancel and the remaining elliptic function is constant, fixed by the Laurent term. Halfperiod roots are distinct for a nonsingular lattice. The program checks the coefficient recurrence and the small σ expansion; a coefficient cancellation proves each identity universally, unlike its numerical spot checks.

Every even elliptic f is rational in wp: wp gives a two-sheeted quotient by z↦−z, and even f descends to a meromorphic function on P¹. Alternatively induct on matched even principal parts. For arbitrary f, split into even and odd parts; f_odd/wp′ is even. Thus f=R1(wp)+wp′R2(wp), uniquely. If poles occur only at the lattice, both R_i are polynomials; the odd part vanishes at halfperiods before division. The cancelled scan77 argument that every torus→P¹ has even degree is false: wp′ has a triple pole and degree3.

The Hermite decomposition matches each pole's principal part by ζ for residue terms and wp derivatives for higher orders. Since wp^(r−2)(z) has leading (−1)^(r−2)(r−1)! z^−r, the coefficient is (−1)^(r−2)/(r−1)! and derivative order r−2. Residue sum zero gives periodicity of the ζ part. Subtracting all principal parts leaves a constant. These facts decide the mathematical factorial/order alternatives but do not decide ink.

Quasiperiodicity follows because ζ(z+2ω_i)−ζ(z) is constant; σ(z+2ω_i)=−exp(2η_i(z+ω_i))σ(z). Integrating ζ around a parallelogram gives η1ω2−η2ω1=πi/2 with the chosen orientation. This fixes the minus sign in σ's quasiperiod law. For a divisor with Ω=sum poles−sum zeros=2n1ω1+2n2ω2, the σ ratio changes under a period by exp(2η_i Ω). Its correcting exponential must therefore be exp(−2(n1η1+n2η2)z), using Legendre to remove the integral phase. The positive sign in scan65 is incompatible with the two preceding shifted-product formulas.

The wp/ζ/σ addition formulas follow by comparing principal parts as elliptic functions of one variable and evaluating one constant; duplication is their limit. The halfperiod translation is e_i+(e_i−e_j)(e_i−e_k)/(wp−e_i); the program verifies its differential equation and product relation. An even divisor on C has a meromorphic square root after analytic continuation because every loop encloses an even divisor multiplicity; periods may become antiperiods. Sigma ratios with fixed value/derivative fix these signs. This yields the generalized sn1,cn1,dn1 functions. Their squared relations and derivative identities follow by differentiating wp's cubic. In particular the residues of cn1 and dn1 involve reciprocal square roots, not square roots, of root differences.

The alternative Möbius expression for sn in scan66 is exactly verified, including its initial derivative. The squared-pole construction on scan67 is another normalization with different root ordering. The normalized Jacobi initial values sn(0)=0,cn(0)=dn(0)=1 fix the signs in their ODE system. All Taylor coefficients shown are rederived in the program. The period/zero/pole tables follow from the theta quotients and quasiperiods, not from a principal square root chosen globally. Shift/addition identities follow either from those quotients or by verifying the differential system and initial values. With K″=−K−iK′, composition of the two quarter shifts gives dn(u+K″)=−ik′sn(u)/cn(u).
