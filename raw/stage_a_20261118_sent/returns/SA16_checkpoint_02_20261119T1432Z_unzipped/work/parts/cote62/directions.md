## 1. Characteristics2 and3

**What it would establish.** The precise changes in the anharmonic normalizer, its fixed divisors and successive quotient maps when2 or3 is not a unit, without carrying the tame formulas across their excluded boundary.

**What is known.** P2 now proves the full normalizer S₃×C₂ over everyZ[1/6]-base, including nonreduced bases, by fppf-local matrix equations. The extra involution has matrix determinant3, and splitting its scalar commutation signs uses2 invertible. These identify the exact limits of that proof. [Beauville2010; C-SA16-19; MP-SA16-12,13]

**First concrete step.** Compute the centralizer equations and fixed-divisor fibres separately overF₂ andF₃, keeping their scheme structure. Only then formulate a relative statement over bases meeting those characteristics. This extension has not been developed here.

## 2. Exceptional fibres

**What it would establish.** A precise local presentation retaining the order-two/order-three infinitesimal thickenings and the cyclotomic band, and a clear distinction between the fixed bisection and the sign cover.

**What is known.** The dual-number counterexamples and the quadratic classes are already checked; quotient-stack and Kummer theory give the framework. Hua’s field theorem supplies no certificate for nilpotent bases. [StacksQuotient; StacksKummer; Hua2026]

**First concrete step.** Write the completed local invariant maps at a harmonic point and a cyclotomic point, retaining stabilizer actions and descent over the unsplit base. The output should make explicit which reduced-base statement is actually true and which scheme-theoretic fibre it differs from.

## 3. Singular-base extension

**What it would establish.** A usable criterion separating extension of cubic data, extension of the full ordering torsor and extension of the marked section, with regular and singular cases stated independently.

**What is known.** Full étale ordering-torsor extension makes the map extension follow by integrality. DVR parity/cube conditions are the local tame conditions. TheA₁ singularity in P3 shows why codimension-one tests are insufficient on a normal base. SGA1 purity requires regularity. [SGA1 X.3.1; C-SA16-23,24]

**First concrete step.** Formulate the lifting problem on the punctured spectrum of a normal local ring and identify the exact finite étale torsor whose extension is required. Compare the regular case with the recorded singular example before proposing a root-stack or purity formulation.

## 4. The elliptic lift gerbe

**What it would establish.** A full equivalence between centred trisection data equipped with a square-root line and elliptic curves over a base with6 invertible, with isomorphisms and twisting included.

**What is known.** P2 checks the coordinate weights and Kummer obstruction, and AOV gives abstract rigidification. The source continuation itself centres the trisection but does not state a gerbe theorem. [AOV2008 AppendixA; StacksKummer; StacksWeierstrass]

**First concrete step.** Fix a single convention for the underlying vector line and its dual coordinate, write local equations and transition functions, then prove that the two constructions preserve all arrows. This is a companion theorem, not an assertion that the manuscript already uses modern stack language.
