# SA15 — Ranked research directions

These are bounded tasks cut from the completed dossier, not claims of new
results. Each keeps the manuscript formulation, the established literature
and the proposed new work separate. “One run” means a few hours of focused
work. No direction below has been developed beyond the checks needed to
adjudicate the existing pages. The ranking balances mathematical interest
with the chance of a definite output in that time.

## 1. Specify the finite-group triangles and test the two cross-composites

**Origin:** SA15-268, SA15-270, SA15-271, SA15-275;
CL-SA15-245, CL-SA15-247, CL-SA15-248, CL-SA15-254;
MP-SA15-87, MP-SA15-88, MP-SA15-91.

**Known.** Perfect-field Dieudonné theory and several relative crystalline
classifications exist, but do not identify the draft's category of complexes
with specified triangles. Its two cross-triangle composites are not made
zero by a single triangle axiom. Relative Ext also needs a defined morphism
and cone convention.

**Goal.** Produce one convention-complete candidate for the author's
two-term object and a precise proposition saying which compatibility implies
the boxed vanishings. A negative example in that candidate category would
also be a definite result.

**First step.** Transcribe scans 4, 6, 8, 16 and 20 into a table of complexes,
degrees, scalar rings, twists and connecting maps, then test α_p, μ_p and
Z/p over a perfect field. Keep any chosen enhancement labelled as a
reconstruction.

**One-run prospect:** high for the complete formal problem and first models;
moderate for identifying the intended relative functor. **Interest:** very high.

## 2. Turn the cote 4 torsion gap into an exact geometric criterion

**Origin:** SA15-357, SA15-362, SA15-364;
CL-SA15-358, CL-SA15-369, CL-SA15-373; MP-SA15-127, MP-SA15-129.

**Known.** The defect is b+c, while dim ker φ=c−k₀. The formal equivalence
fails without control of preceding-degree stable torsion and ker v. The
surface example survives this objection; the dossier supplies no new
higher-dimensional geometric counterexample.

**Goal.** State the sharp geometric hypotheses under which equality and
bijectivity coincide, or find a genuine smooth projective example realizing
their separation with the required ample-section assumptions.

**First step.** Make the two universal-coefficient sequences and the
integral Lefschetz hypothesis explicit for one candidate dimension-three
example. Check Frobenius on every torsion factor before attempting products;
a positive-degree projective-space class loses slope zero.

**One-run prospect:** high for a sharp conditional statement, moderate for a
fully verified example. **Interest:** very high.

## 3. Identify the two integral Lubin–Tate lattices before comparing them

**Origin:** SA15-293, SA15-296, SA15-297, SA15-298;
CL-SA15-270–CL-SA15-274; MP-SA15-95, MP-SA15-96.

**Known.** The rank-one isocrystal calculation, stable-lattice construction
and normalized Lubin–Tate existence theorem are established. They do not
identify the two specific lattices on scans 48–50 or prove α=0. Rational
admissibility does not settle integral equality.

**Goal.** Give a precise, basis-independent definition of both lattices and
decide their relative position under a stated ramification hypothesis.

**First step.** In a companion run with scans, resolve the lattice labels,
the Ω/p versus Ω/π expression and the dual convention. Then compare the
unramified case and one tame ramified case against the exact modern integral
classification, retaining the e=p−1 nilpotence boundary.

**One-run prospect:** high for the convention dictionary; conditional on ink
for the full comparison. **Interest:** very high.

## 4. Extend the precise virtual-good-reduction length result

**Origin:** SA15-289; CL-SA15-266, CL-SA15-301; MP-SA15-93.

**Known.** The prime-to-p argument is elementary. The dossier proves the
exact integral statement for finite K/Q_p with p>2 from crystalline descent,
Breuil and Raynaud; its rational filtration proof works for every p. No
opened source was found that explicitly resolves the full arbitrary
henselian-trait formulation of SGA7 IX 5.13.1.

**Goal.** Remove a specified remaining restriction, beginning with integral
p=2 over finite K, or determine the precise obstruction to carrying the
argument to more general residue fields or traits.

**First step.** Locate and read the exact modern theorem realizing every
specified crystalline Tate lattice at p=2, then check its hypotheses against
the final integral recovery step. Treat the general-trait problem separately.

**One-run prospect:** high for the p=2 literature-to-proof bridge; lower for
the full trait statement. **Interest:** high.

## 5. Finish the determinant duality signs left on cote 9:29

**Origin:** SA15-347, SA15-349, SA15-350;
CL-SA15-339, CL-SA15-343–CL-SA15-346; MP-SA15-118–MP-SA15-120.

**Known.** The positive Rosati argument resolves polarization normalization
and its integral lattice. Naturality uses inverse-dual variance. The final
biduality sign and mod-p comparison are different uncompleted checks.

**Goal.** Produce a functorial determinant-line statement with its biduality,
product and reduction compatibilities under one explicit alternating-form
convention.

**First step.** Fix the determinant order and duality maps for an ordinary
elliptic curve and an ordered product of two such curves, then write the
three comparison diagrams. Do not reopen the sign question already solved
on scan 27.

**One-run prospect:** high for a complete lemma and convention table.
**Interest:** high.

## 6. Resolve the intrinsic analytic completed-diagonal comparison

**Origin:** SA15-262, SA15-263; CL-SA15-240–CL-SA15-242;
MP-SA15-84, MP-SA15-85.

**Known.** Ambient analytic de Rham, flat algebraic completed-diagonal and
algebraic infinitesimal–Betti comparisons have exact theorems. They are not
identical formulations of the arbitrary intrinsic analytic assertion.

**Goal.** Determine whether an existing theorem already supplies the missing
analytic/coefficient comparison, and otherwise formulate the smallest exact
local lemma needed.

**First step.** Follow the primary references from Hartshorne IV.2.1 and
Liu's comparison back to the local completed-diagonal construction. Write
the proposed comparison map and check completion-exactness on one singular
analytic local model. A verified existing theorem is a successful output.

**One-run prospect:** high for a decisive literature and hypothesis audit;
moderate for a new local proof. **Interest:** high.

## 7. Test derived direct images in the parallel-arrow topoi

**Origin:** SA15-24–SA15-30; CL-SA15-019–CL-SA15-024;
MP-SA15-04, MP-SA15-05.

**Known.** The comparison categories are topoi by the checked accessibility
and Giraud argument, and their geometric universal property is outgoing.
The higher-direct-image claim requires more than the mere existence of a
left adjoint.

**Goal.** Give a sufficient acyclicity condition for the displayed derived
comparison, or an explicit counterexample to its unrestricted version.

**First step.** Write the adjunctions for the N-action and Z-action models
and compare injective resolutions with the underlying abelian groups. State
exactly which square of geometric morphisms the calculation addresses.

**One-run prospect:** high for a useful theorem or countertest.
**Interest:** medium to high.

## 8. Separate full ordinary stratification from one Frobenius descent

**Origin:** SA15-339–SA15-342; CL-SA15-328–CL-SA15-333;
MP-SA15-113, MP-SA15-114; SA15-124; CL-SA15-102–CL-SA15-104.

**Known.** Full ordinary coherent stratifications are locally free on
finite-type field schemes. Unit-F modules correspond to étale local
systems. Neither statement alone proves the necessity of ordinarity in the
manuscript's full universal-deformation compatibility problem.

**Goal.** State and test the exact “forces ordinary” implication, including
the F/V compatibility and the strength of the stratification.

**First step.** Compare the universal deformation of an ordinary and a
supersingular elliptic curve with the first two Frobenius-divided levels.
Keep geometric torsion points and descent visible throughout.

**One-run prospect:** high for identifying the correct obstruction, moderate
for a general theorem. **Interest:** high.

## 9. Put the fixed-X unit-root monodromy question in a testable form

**Origin:** SA15-360, SA15-365, SA15-366;
CL-SA15-364, CL-SA15-375–CL-SA15-378, CL-SA15-384;
MP-SA15-130, MP-SA15-131, MP-SA15-132.

**Known.** Eventual positive rank and unbounded generic rank are established.
Full monodromy on curve p-rank strata does not settle fixed-X hypersurface
families. Dimension one has a permutation obstruction to the unrestricted
full-GL formulation.

**Goal.** Give an exact candidate for the geometric image after removing
constant pieces, with n≥2 and all rank, determinant and duality constraints
stated. This precedes the every-open point-count application.

**First step.** Choose one fixed smooth projective surface and one degree
range, identify the unit-F local system and its determinant, and inspect
the precise monodromy literature for that family.

**One-run prospect:** high for a sharp formulation and a useful special
case; low for a uniform theorem for every X. **Interest:** very high.

## 10. Clarify the rank-one Chern obstruction and its converse

**Origin:** SA15-3, SA15-4; CL-SA15-002, CL-SA15-003;
MP-SA15-02.

**Known.** Later integral crystalline Chern results establish precise
necessity statements. They do not identify the original obstruction group
or prove the page's ambiguous sufficiency direction.

**Goal.** Separate the ordinary-stratified, integrable-connection and PD
crystalline rank-one questions, and decide one exact converse.

**First step.** Fix the coefficient group and compare line bundles on one
smooth projective curve with their Chern class, connection and Frobenius
descent data. Check the exact Esnault–Shiho and Bhatt hypotheses before
claiming either a converse or a counterexample.

**One-run prospect:** high for a precise corrected problem; moderate for
one useful classification statement. **Interest:** medium to high.

## 11. Make the virtual inertia class independent of choices

**Origin:** SA15-335–SA15-338; CL-SA15-320–CL-SA15-325,
CL-SA15-380–CL-SA15-381; MP-SA15-110, MP-SA15-111.

**Known.** Good models are unique; their finite inertia traces have the
stated integrality and field-extension compatibility. The mod-p torsion
recipe fails even with μ_p⊂K. Independence of an arbitrary virtual
filtration is a separate assertion.

**Goal.** Specify the exact Grothendieck group and prove a dévissage theorem
for the allowable generic filtrations, or isolate the failure of exactness.

**First step.** Write the restriction, refinement and field-extension maps
for two length-two filtrations and identify the one exactness statement
needed to compare them. Use the elliptic counterexample only to exclude the
false mod-p recovery route.

**One-run prospect:** high for a rigorous reduction and model cases.
**Interest:** high.

in parallel with the mathematical tasks above.
