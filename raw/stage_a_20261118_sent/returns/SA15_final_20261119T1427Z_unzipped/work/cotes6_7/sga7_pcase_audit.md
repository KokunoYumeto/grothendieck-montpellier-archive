# Exact audit: SGA 7 I, IX, Remark 5.13.1

Run: SA15-20261119/cotes6_7. Reviewer: GPT-6 Astra Pro; reasoning effort is not exposed. Checked 19 November 2026. This supplement concerns root-package cote 8/032; it does not add a manuscript claim to the 75 claims of the cotes 6–7 package.

## Finding and documentary limit

The original question concerns an arbitrary generic-fibre Barsotti–Tate group over a henselian trait. It assumes an existing finite filtration by **strict** Barsotti–Tate subgroups whose graded groups have good reduction. If a finite extension permits such a filtration with at most n nonzero graded groups, must one already exist with at most n over the original trait? The remark asserts the prime-to-residue-characteristic case. This is the exact question in [SGA 7 IX, 5.12 and 5.13.1](https://grothendiecksga.com/read/sga7/fr/9-5.html).

**No paper explicitly resolving Remark 5.13.1 in its full stated generality was identified by the exact searches recorded below. This is a search residual, not a claim that the question remains open.** The adjacent Proposition 5.13(c) treats the Tate group of an abelian variety and length two; it cannot be substituted for arbitrary Barsotti–Tate groups and arbitrary n.

There is, however, a complete deduction in the finite-local-field case: **if K is a finite extension of Q_p and p>2, the answer to the exact integral Barsotti–Tate filtration question is yes.** The proof below is this run's reconstruction from independently opened primary theorems, not an assertion that those papers explicitly formulate Remark 5.13.1. The rational crystalline-filtration argument works for every p. The integral step here is certified only for p>2, using the particular Breuil theorem actually read.

## Primary ingredients and their scope

[Fontaine 1994, Exposé VIII](https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1994__223__321_0.pdf), 2.1.3 and 2.3.2–2.3.4, supplies the potentially semistable period functor, its exactness, and the criterion that a potentially crystalline representation is crystalline precisely when the residual inertia action on its period module is trivial. These passages are read, including their complete discretely valued characteristic-zero and perfect-residue-field hypotheses.

[Breuil 2000](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~christophe.breuil/PUBLICATIONS/p-divisibles.pdf), Theorem 5.3.2, gives a Barsotti–Tate realization up to isogeny for crystalline representations of weights {0,1}, with p≠2 and residue field contained in Fbar_p. Corollary 5.3.4 explicitly uses Raynaud to recover the specified integral Tate lattice. The downloaded author version has 54 PDF pages: Theorem 5.3.2 is printed on p.51 and its conclusion/proof continues on p.52. These are version-specific pages, not asserted journal-page numbers. The [journal record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/11849) verifies Annals 152 (2000), no.2, 489–549, DOI 10.2307/2661391. The author's bibliography says volume151; the official journal and arXiv metadata both say152, which is retained. First arXiv submission: 1 September 2000; no earlier full-circulation date is certified here.

[Raynaud 1974](https://www.numdam.org/article/BSMF_1974__102__241_0.pdf), §2.1 pp.259–260, constructs finite flat closures and their quotients. Proposition 2.3.1, pp.261–263, says that a generic-fibre p-divisible group in mixed characteristic extends if every finite level admits a finite flat model. The statement and proof were opened and read. Together these results justify the integral-lattice step below.

The weight convention follows Breuil: the Tate modules of Barsotti–Tate groups have weights {0,1}. With the opposite Hodge–Tate convention, replace this set throughout by {−1,0}.

## Present-run proof of the finite-local-field assertion

Write C_K for the category of crystalline Q_p-representations of G_K whose Hodge–Tate weights lie in {0,1}. It is stable under finite sums, subobjects and quotients. Good-reduction Barsotti–Tate groups give objects of C_K. Breuil and Raynaud give the converse for every specified G_K-stable lattice when p>2.

**Descent lemma.** Suppose W is potentially crystalline and has a G_K-stable finite filtration whose graded representations are crystalline over K. Then W is crystalline over K. Indeed, every subquotient of W is potentially crystalline. Apply the exact potentially semistable period functor to the filtration. On each graded period module inertia acts trivially because its representation is crystalline over K. Consequently every inertial operator on the whole period module is unipotent. The inertia image on this module is finite, whereas a finite-order unipotent operator over a characteristic-zero field is the identity: its minimal polynomial divides both a power of T−1 and the separable polynomial T^m−1. Hence inertia acts trivially. Potential crystallinity already makes monodromy zero. Fontaine's criterion gives crystallinity over K. This argument does **not** assume that arbitrary extensions of crystalline representations are potentially crystalline.

**Induction on n.** Let V possess some C_K-filtration, and suppose V restricted to G_L possesses a C_L-filtration of length at most n. Enlarge L to a finite Galois extension without changing that upper bound. Let U be the first nonzero member of the latter increasing filtration, and put W=Σ_g gU, the sum over representatives of G_K/G_L. This is a G_K-stable subspace. Over L it is a quotient of a finite direct sum of crystalline representations, so W is crystalline over L and has weights {0,1}. Intersecting W with the original K-filtration gives crystalline graded pieces, as subobjects of its original graded pieces. The descent lemma therefore puts W in C_K. The induced filtration on V/W still has C_K graded pieces. Over L, its first member U has disappeared because U⊂W, and all remaining graded pieces are quotients of the original ones. Thus V/W has a C_L-filtration of length at most n−1. Induction produces the required K-filtration of V of length at most n. The n=0 case is V=0.

**Integral recovery.** For the original generic Barsotti–Tate group take its free Tate lattice T⊂V. Intersect T with each rational filtration member. These intersections are saturated, so the successive quotient lattices are finite free. A crystalline quotient lattice T' has a Barsotti–Tate model as follows. Breuil first supplies some good lattice T_0 in its rational representation. After scaling T_0, arrange T_0⊂T'⊂p^(−m)T_0. For every a≥1, T'/p^aT' is a subquotient of p^(−m)T_0/p^aT_0, hence of the p^(a+m)-torsion of that Barsotti–Tate model. Raynaud's schematic closure and quotient construction supplies a finite flat model at each level. His Proposition 2.3.1 then supplies a compatible Barsotti–Tate model of T'. Under the characteristic-zero equivalence between generic Barsotti–Tate groups and free continuous Tate lattices, the saturated filtration therefore corresponds to a filtration by strict Barsotti–Tate subgroups. Its graded groups have good reduction and its length is at most n, as required.

This proves a precisely stated special case of the original question. Neither an unrestricted henselian-trait theorem nor the integral p=2 extension is marked as verified by these citations. A later classification may remove the latter limitation, but no unchecked substitute is entered as a certified result.
