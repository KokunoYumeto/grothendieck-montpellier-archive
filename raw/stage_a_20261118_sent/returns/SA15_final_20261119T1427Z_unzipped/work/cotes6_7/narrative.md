# Cotes6/038–067 and7/001–060: the development in the author’s order

The latter part of cote6 joins Tate–Hodge questions, formal groups and deformation theory to the machinery that becomes the infinitesimal site. Cote7 first builds that machinery, then pauses for a critical account of de Rham and p-adic cohomology, and finally returns to stratification and Čech calculations. The package contains255 unindexed `##` notes. Their judgments are preserved through82 consolidated items;75 manuscript claims are distinguished from five explicitly labelled refinements made in this run. The supplied transcription, including covers and reused leaves, was read throughout. No scan reading has been changed.

## Tate–Hodge questions and formal groups

The headings on6/038–040 associate the packet with Tate without proving authorship of every leaf. On041 and045 the author seeks canonical comparisons compatible with products and cycles (P67-I002; P67-C001–C002). Faltings1988, ChapterIII Theorem4.1, supplies the proper-smooth Hodge–Tate theorem. The earlier request involving a filtered de Rham splitting is stronger and is not silently identified with that graded comparison. The two representation questions on041 have different answers: an arbitrary continuous irreducible representation need not embed in a Tate twist of C_p, whereas an irreducible crystalline representation with two distinct Hodge–Tate weights gives simultaneous embeddings after twisting (P67-C003–C004; BrinonConrad2009, Theorems8.3.6 and15.1.7). Both deductions are spelled out separately from the source theorems.

Reused analytical leaves interrupt the sequence on042,044 and046. They concern joint continuity of translation, holomorphic functional calculus and Cauchy measures, not crystalline cohomology. Their reconstructed statements have independent proofs, but the missing topology definitions, K/L mismatch and exact typescript source remain reading or identification questions (P67-I006,I009–I010; P67-C006–C008).

The Barsotti question on047–048 uses “hypersingulières” to mean p-rank zero. That definition must survive modernization. In dimension two the slope restriction forces supersingularity; in higher dimension the broad isogeny assertion is false. LenstraOort1974 supplies geometrically simple varieties of the precise formal types needed to compare a simple fourfold with a threefold times a supersingular elliptic curve (P67-C010–C011). On049 the strengthening from “isogenous to a product” to “isomorphic to a product” also fails. An explicit genus-two Jacobian over Fbar_7 has nonisogenous ordinary and supersingular elliptic quotients but cannot itself be their product (P67-C013). The unsure surface/curve wording remains for the ink to decide.

The transcendental-unit construction on050–051 admits a rigorous finite-field reconstruction. A central p-adic unit yields continuous descent at every finite p-power level, and transcendental Frobenius eigenvalues prevent an abelian embedding after every finite extension. The further finitely generated extension clause needs another argument and is retained as a proof obligation, not advertised as a currently open theorem (P67-C015–C016).

On052–057 extension classes become formal torsors and then equations for ordinary polarized moduli. Katz1981, Theorem2.1, verifies the formal torus and the pushout/pullback morphism criterion. Character-lattice equations give the characteristic-p complete-intersection, Gorenstein and smooth-reduction consequences with framing or an adequate level structure. They do not settle the cancelled mixed-characteristic flatness remark or every coarse moduli quotient (P67-C017–C020).

The Witt-extension calculations on059–060 require greater restraint. The exact Demazure–Gabriel locators are recorded, but the opened perfect-field statements do not certify every arbitrary-base quantifier. The author’s immediate “Non” rejects a global Ext inference; a separate Hom paragraph is cancelled. These are different statuses (P67-C021–C024). On061–066 finite étale groups lead to unit-Frobenius modules. SGA1I§8 and BhattScholze2023 Proposition3.4 supply precise counterparts. A derived-duality formula on062 needs a grading check: the elementary Z/p test detects a missing shift under standard conventions (P67-C026,C076).

## Formalization and the first negative result

Cote7/003–015 constructs diagonal neighbourhoods as pro-objects, carefully keeping their several scalar actions. A decisive collation is internal to the package:005 ends where059 begins, and059 ends where006 resumes. The earlier notes’ apparent fragments therefore have extant continuations (P67-I031). Formalization of differential operators, the extra-degeneracy contraction and the spectral sequence must retain coherence, boundedness and inverse-limit conditions (P67-C030–C036). Fiorot2011 gives a precise bounded coherent characteristic-zero development; it does not identify every proposed Artin–Rees category or remove quasi-coherence.

On016–017 the author corrects the hope that his first, non-PD site will provide the desired characteristic-p Weil cohomology. The ordinary-abelian example loses the upper degrees. Grothendieck1968 §6.5 preserves that withdrawal; Ogus1975 Theorem4.9 and Corollary4.11 later identify the Frobenius-stable part under explicit smooth-proper embedding hypotheses. Ogus’s proof flags one unchecked compatibility, which the dossier records. Modern PD crystalline cohomology must not be substituted into the rejected statement (P67-C037). The weak-lifting site proposed on018–019 is a response to this failure, not a renaming of the same construction.

## The lecture’s tests and the return to stratification

The review on021–035 contrasts the smooth complex comparison with the deficiencies of naïve de Rham theory. Grothendieck1966 Theorems1 and1′ give the precise smooth theorem. The characteristic-p affine-line calculation is valid, but the ordinary cusp on024 is not the advertised singular counterexample: the full exterior Kähler complex, including its nonzero second term, is contracted by the Euler derivation. Its positive-degree cohomology vanishes (P67-C042,C045). ArapuraKang2011 supplies a genuinely non-quasihomogeneous replacement; Hartshorne1975 concerns ambient completed complexes.

The finiteness and independence questions must be dated precisely. SGA4XIX5.1 retains a resolution hypothesis; unconditional ordinary positive-characteristic finiteness is assigned here to Deligne’s SGA4½VII1.1. KatzMessing1974 Theorem2(2) supplies integral correspondence polynomials in the smooth projective finite-field setting (P67-C049–C050). Serre’s quaternion obstruction explains why a two-dimensional faithful Q_p-valued H¹ cannot serve as a universal Weil realization; Witt coefficients are a different proposal.

The lifting discussion on035–047 supplies several exact tests. SGA1III§6, Corollaries8 and10, is the affine/formal lift reference. Restricted completion has infinite-rank H¹; integral primitives require both coefficientwise integrality and a convergence tail. The printed positive-exponent radius bound does not express overconvergence. Integral affine Monsky–Washnitzer finite generation fails already on A¹, although rational H¹ vanishes; ErtlSprang2018 proves integral lift independence and ErtlShiho2020 proves stronger nonfiniteness (P67-C058–C066). BMS2018, Theorems1.1 and2.1, replaces the proposed universal torsion equality by inequalities and a strict example.

Finally048–060 seeks canonical comparison through stratification. Derived base change alone does not imply ordinary cohomology base change. Cartan homotopy gives Gauss–Manin, with the local construction published in Grothendieck1968 §3.3, while an explicit smooth Legendre family in characteristic3 has nonzero nilpotent p-curvature, confirming the author’s withdrawal of ordinary stratification (P67-C057,C069–C071). The singular infinitesimal/Betti conjecture on056 is distinct from naïve Kähler cohomology; Liu2025 Theorems1.1/14.1 and1.2 give its exact modern comparison. The Čech–Alexander construction on057–060 continues into061 of the next package, where mutual induction of coefficients controls the limit calculation. The sequence ends with an existing continuation, not a lost page (P67-C073–C075).
