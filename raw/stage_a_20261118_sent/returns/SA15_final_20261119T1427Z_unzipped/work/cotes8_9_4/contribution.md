# Curator contribution: cotes 8:79–81, 9 and 4

## What the pages hold

Cote 8 ends its co-Lie calculation by changing coefficients through the classifying topos of a character local system. The two cohomology sheaves are a tensor product and a Tor group; their common rank in the locally free Z/n case leaves a degree-two attaching class. The next page identifies this class with a representation-lifting obstruction and predicts universal nonvanishing from rank two onward. That prediction has small exceptions. The explicit integral S3 representation in the saved calculation gives a complete counterexample for GL₂(F₂), while the complete exception list is read through Singla’s precise backwards reference to Sah and Ginosar. The original classification proofs are not falsely marked read.

Cote 9 contains three related investigations. First comes reduced Dieudonné duality and a coefficient-functional model. The standard F/V duality is established mathematics, but the finite presentation cannot simply be certified: its truncation index is uncertain, a displayed V-action uses F, and the two transcriptions disagree over cancellation of an isomorphism. All of these remain scan questions. The coefficient identities themselves have been checked with nontrivial Witt Frobenius, and their ordinary finite-ring shadows have explicit certificates.

Next comes a virtual good-reduction character. The author invokes Tate’s extension theorem, then asserts the K₀ construction and its independence. These are kept separate: uniqueness of an integral model does not alone prove independence of a filtration of generic objects. The inertia trace is linear only after restricting to inertia, while the residual quotient also acts on coefficients. A useful direct check shows that the Dieudonné trace is Zp-valued because it commutes with semilinear Frobenius; the margin’s additional roots-of-unity condition is unnecessary for that particular conclusion.

The broad attempt to recover this character from mod-p torsion is explicitly withdrawn. Its restricted elliptic test is also false. An independent proof in this run takes K=F(5^(1/8)), with F/Q5 unramified of degree four. Both y²=x³+5⁴ and y²=x³−x have constant 5-torsion over K, and hence μ5⊂K, yet their finite potentially-good inertia traces differ. The bad curve’s 24 nonzero torsion points are obtained by two simple-root Hensel lifts, checked with exact polynomial and finite-field arithmetic. Its discriminant valuation forbids good reduction over K; a tame cubic extension gives supersingular good reduction and inertia trace −1. The other curve has good ordinary reduction and trace 2. This concerns finite Weil–Deligne or ℓ≠5 inertia, not the full raw 5-adic Tate module. It is not attributed to Tate, whose marginal annotation has no secure leader in the supplied witnesses.

The last part of cote 9 turns to ordinary deformation stratifications, Hasse invariants and a polarization-independent étale Tate determinant. A warning about descending equality through Frobenius is repaired on the following page by passing to the smooth universal deformation space. The literal use of rational p-torsion points over every perfect field is too strong; an ordinary curve over F5 with eight rational points gives a complete counterexample. Geometric points with descent, or an étale sheaf, are required.

The determinant work must be read through its conclusion. The author cancels a false assertion for all endomorphisms, restricts to positive Rosati-symmetric operators, proves the sign on scan 27, and checks the integral lattice on scan 28. The intermediate sign question is therefore not a surviving open problem. The final biduality sign and mod-p comparison are still left to check. The correction from dual to inverse-dual variance is essential. The earlier-run notes were useful in finding these steps but sometimes froze an intermediate doubt before its later resolution.

## Cote 4 and the revised torsion sequence

Cote 4 estimates unit Frobenius eigenvalues in vanishing cohomology. Its “semisimple” operation means the stable, bijective Frobenius part. It does not mean that an associated Galois representation is semisimple. The author admits crystalline universal coefficients, duality and an integral weak Lefschetz statement, then revises the correction term. The live sequence is

0 → ker v → Hⁿ_cris(X)[p]_st → (E(Y)/p)_st → coker v → 0.

The old cup-product term is canceled. Consequently φ is surjective, and the live examples concern failure of injectivity or bijectivity. Modern integral Lefschetz verifies sufficiently high ample powers; it does not, merely by being rational weak Lefschetz, justify the stronger all-degree saturated-cokernel hypothesis.

The exact later literature is particularly informative here. Stable de Rham augmentation is SGA 7 II, XXII, formula (3.3.4). The generic eventual positivity assertion has an exact match in XXI, Theorem 1.4, for projective equidimensional Cohen–Macaulay X. Koblitz’s Theorem 1 and Lemma 4 also establish unbounded generic stable rank, with leading term Ddⁿ/n!. Thus rank growth should not remain listed as an unresolved obstacle. Full GL monodromy for every fixed-X hypersurface family is a different question. Achter–Pries proves full monodromy for components of curve p-rank strata in moduli; that does not settle the manuscript’s parameter spaces.

One additional algebraic qualification is necessary. Put i=n−1, and let b be the stable dimension of the preceding crystalline torsion modulo p, c the stable dimension of Hⁿ_cris[p], and k₀=dim ker v. Universal coefficients give defect b+c in the displayed coherent-versus-free-crystalline rank inequality. The revised sequence gives dim ker φ=c−k₀. Their equivalence therefore requires extra hypotheses. The surface example avoids the preceding-degree torsion issue; no new geometric counterexample in higher dimension is asserted. The saved proof separates the exact algebraic gap from any unverified construction.
