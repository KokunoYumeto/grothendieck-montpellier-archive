## Cote 5: divided powers, sites and several inserted texts

The opening diagram connects crystals, stratifications, connections and their topoi. It asks about points and proposes a rank-one Chern obstruction. Those questions remain more specific than a general comparison slogan: the connection/ordinary-stratification equivalence requires a smooth morphism over a **Q-scheme**, not merely a ring of characteristic zero. Integral crystalline Chern vanishing now proves precise necessity statements, but does not identify the coefficient group or prove the ambiguous converse on this page (P56-I001–I004; P56-C001–C003; P56-P001–P002; BO1978 2.15, EsnaultShihoChern2019 Theorem1.1, BhattChern2023 Theorem1.1).

The first sustained calculation constructs a finite universal divided-power algebra. Its p-typical variables satisfy triangular relations with factorial coefficients of valuation one. Translation by the canonical divided powers of p gives the Θ-coordinates used in the asserted normal form. The manuscript leaves the coefficient-necessity verification to the reader and then states quotient formulas W(ν,r). This run supplies a general weighted-ideal proof for every r, including ideal stability through carries and the terminal constant term. Twenty exact multiplication/Smith computations corroborate finite cases; they are not the proof. Hua2026 §35 reports a contemporary formalization of the basic construction but explicitly excludes the r>0 extension from its verified range. No Lean certificate was executed here. The underlying algebra is now justified; exact matching of every truncation axiom in the proposed universal category remains separate (P56-I006–I010; P56-C005–C011; P56-P003).

Legendre’s factorial valuation then explains a boundary that recurs throughout the folder. For a mixed-characteristic DVR with e=vπ(p), divided powers on its maximal ideal exist exactly when e≤p−1. The structure **induced from that DVR** on a nontrivial quotient is PD-nilpotent exactly when e<p−1. At equality, the p-power divided powers of π retain valuation one. These are different assertions, and other PD structures on a quotient are not ruled out by the latter test (P56-C017–C018, C040–C041; BO1978 Proposition3.28).

The parallel-arrow construction forms objects E with a map α*E→β*E, or its invertible variant. The omitted small-generator step can be supplied using accessible categorical inserters and isomorphers, followed by the other Giraud conditions. Its geometric universal property is outgoing: a lax coequalizer, not the incoming universal property of a geometric inserter. The later claim about higher direct images remains conditional on a missing exactness or acyclicity argument; existence of a left adjoint alone is insufficient (P56-I024–I030; P56-C019–C024; P56-P004–P005; Positselski2024 Theorems1.6–1.7, StacksExactAdjoint).

The subsequent divided-power sheets develop free algebras, envelopes, quotient stability, p-typical generation and principal ideals. Modern exact counterparts are available in Roby and the checked Stacks passages. Three local corrections matter. The literal logarithm has the wrong initial sign; the proposed p-unit normalization need not be 1 modulo p; and a torsion-free principal ideal needs πⁿ/n! **inside the ideal**, not merely inside the ring. The earlier notes already isolated these difficulties. Their diagnostics are retained as earlier-run work and rechecked here, including the rank-three torsion-free counterexample x²=2y, xy=y²=0 (P56-C031, C037–C039; P56-P009–P012; StacksPDAxioms, StacksPDPolynomial).

The site pages use locally retractive truncated PD thickenings, explicitly without choosing a retraction as part of the object. Their diagonal envelopes, two-index system, Čech complex and affine inverse limits link directly to cote7/061–064. The modern PD comparison supports the method but does not erase the draft’s transition-map obligations. The finite-algebra Poincaré examples on c5/037 are a separate ordinary Kähler calculation: Euler contraction proves the positively graded cases, while a non-graded Artinian Jacobian algebra has nonconstant closed functions. That earlier-run counterexample does not refute completed-diagonal or derived comparison theorems (P56-I041–I056; P56-C042–C045, C082–C087; P56-P006–P008, P014; BO1978 7.1, Liu2025).

Cote 5 is not a continuous crystal treatise. The CM conductor leaf is exactly Serre–Tate1968 Theorem12; quasi-function/height fragments remain unidentified; Kuiper and K-theory course leaves are foreign material. The finite-target differential leaves have a secure local order, 048→046, from both source pagination and the word join, but their draft numbering is not an exact final-EGA identification. The separate étale-normality note does match EGAIV4 18.12.15 and its proof dependencies. The closing 1965 letter raises relative étale duality and then doubts a naïve p-adic comparison. Deligne’s SGA4 XVIII gives precise later duality statements, classed B under the strict authorship rule; Scholze’s period-sheaf comparison changes both coefficients and topology and does not validate the literal constant-Z_p proposal (P56-C004, C046–C063; EGA0IV, SGA4XVIII, Scholze2013).

## Cote 6: a theory revised while being built

The Pisa project begins with global nilpotent thickenings, then corrects the construction by varying the open subset and imposing stack descent. Crystals, ordinary stratifications and connections must be read with those revisions. Cartier descent describes one zero-p-curvature Frobenius descent; an ordinary stratification requires an infinite compatible system. The literal early equations FV=p and VF=1 force the object to vanish in the intended p-local setting, whereas later pages use symmetric weight relations. This raises an ink alarm rather than licensing an emendation (P56-I097–I118; P56-C067–C096; Grothendieck1968 §4.1, Katz1970 Theorem5.1, Gieseker1975 Theorem1.3).

The finite perfect-field lattice argument is sound: bounded positive F-orbits yield a finite F-stable lattice, and the corresponding F/V statement follows using the commuting relation and bounded denominators. It does not establish uniform effectiveness for arbitrary families. Proper algebraization assumes an actual proper lift. A useful strengthening of the literature verdict concerns local freeness: BO1978 Proposition2.16 and Note2.17 prove it for coherent **full ordinary stratifications on any finite-type field scheme**, without smoothness. Their Artin-neighborhood descent proof was checked. The nonfree connection and PD-lattice examples concern different categories and are not counterexamples (P56-C097–C108; P56-P022–P026).

The cohomological program asks for Gauss–Manin integrability, Leray, perfect derived objects, base change, duality and F/V operations. Katz–Oda1968 supplies exact integrability and Leray successors. PD crystalline theory supplies the later comparison package, with its base and coefficient hypotheses; derived base change does not imply unrestricted base change for every individual cohomology module. The author himself corrects the ordinary positive-characteristic comparison and retreats from automatic integral weight assertions (P56-C110–C117; P56-P028–P031; BO1978 7.1,7.8,7.13).

The abelian pages are especially revision-sensitive. Universal lift independence and a rigid group-valued crystal are followed by a “faux” margin, a modular elliptic-family obstruction and canceled ordinary-case arguments. Grothendieck1968 §3.5 publishes the obstruction. Messing establishes universal vector extensions and deformation by lifted Hodge filtrations under nilpotent PD hypotheses; this is not a restoration of arbitrary ordinary-thickening independence. The later Barsotti–Tate pages ask how much filtered data retains and how to recover the generic integral Tate module. AnschutzLeBras2023 v4 Proposition5.4.2 answers a precise successor using the stronger admissible prismatic invariant over quasisyntomic rings (P56-I141–I160; P56-C118–C136; P56-P032–P041).

Finally, ordinary elliptic extensions lead to Kummer parameters, supersingular image speculation and reduction tests. The earlier CM example over Q₃(i) refutes unrestricted open GL₂ image; the ramified quadratic twist of a Tate curve shows that nonintegral j need not give multiplicative reduction over the original field. Both examples were rechecked, with their hypotheses and refined statements recorded. The split Tate valuation question and the p-divisible good-reduction criterion have exact classical answers; the equal-characteristic formula and the adjective before “simple” remain unresolved readings (P56-C137–C146, C149, C151; P56-P042–P045; KatzSerreTate1981, Tate1995, SGA7IX 5.10–5.13).
