16: 
17: - **C7.01 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 061, affine calculation.** The insertion “et s'induisent mutuellement” is essential to the passage from affine acyclicity at finite order to the inverse-limit calculation: quasi-coherence alone does not make an arbitrary transition system surjective. For compatible pullbacks across the closed immersions of successive neighbourhoods, the corresponding quotient maps on affine sections give the stated strict system, after which the Mittag–Leffler argument applies; the analogous calculation in the supplied context is cote 5, scans 030–031, where the divided-power truncation introduces a second index. The proof should specify the indexing and demonstrate the compatibility before discarding derived inverse limits (Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tags 02MY and 07KV).
18: 
19: - **C7.02 — Own idea; Cote 7, scan 061, inverse-limit display.** For “par les arguments à la M.L.”, use the exact sequence $0\to R^1\!\varprojlim H^{n-1}(K_i)\to H^n(R\!\varprojlim K_i)\to\varprojlim H^n(K_i)\to0$ as a bookkeeping device, with the $K_i$ the finite-neighbourhood section complexes. Confidence: high for this diagnostic under a countable inverse system of complexes of modules, because it explicitly identifies the otherwise suppressed obstruction; it does not independently justify replacing the manuscript's sheaf-theoretic construction by that derived limit. This also shows exactly which step must be revisited if the quasi-coherence hypothesis is weakened on cote 7, scan 065.

22: 
23: - **C7.03 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 062, hypercohomology.** “ces faisceaux forment un faisceau cosimplicial” requires that the coface and codegeneracy maps commute with restriction to Zariski opens and with the inverse systems in scan 061. A proof can construct the normalized cosimplicial complex and then apply a Cartan–Eilenberg or derived-totalization argument, but must retain a convergence hypothesis appropriate to its nonnegative grading. Cote 5, scan 031 supplies an earlier version for the structure sheaf, whereas cote 7, scans 061–062 expressly allow the compatible coefficient system $F$.

25: - **C7.04 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scans 062–063, covering assertion.** “$\widetilde Y$ couvre le faisceau final” uses the infinitesimal lifting property of the formally smooth ambient $Y/S$: locally, a map from the reduced or smaller thickening lifts to $Y$. This is the replacement for the covering by $\widetilde X$ which scan 062 correctly says is unavailable without formal smoothness. Compare cote 5, scan 029, where the locally retracting definition of the restricted site supplies that covering; the two arguments use different hypotheses and should not be amalgamated.

29: - **C7.06 — Reading question; Cote 7, scans 063–065, terminology and context.** These leaves write “site cristallin” but calculate with ordinary infinitesimal neighbourhoods and formal completions; the corresponding construction is called the infinitesimal-site calculation in Grothendieck (1968, §5.2). Cote 5, scans 028–030 explicitly distinguish a divided-power De Rham site from its non-divided-power counterparts, so a modern identification with a particular PD crystalline site cannot be inferred from the word “cris” alone. This is a historical notation issue, and neither the frozen subscript nor the context transcription has been revised to force modern terminology.

32: 
33: - **C7.05 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 064, affine and nonaffine calculations.** “donc les $Y(i)$ affines” is justified by affineness of nilpotent thickenings of an affine scheme, rather than by assuming the entire ambient $Y$ affine. The proposed nonaffine replacement must then be glued from neighbourhoods of each open $U\subset X$, with compatibility under replacing an ambient open $V$ by a smaller one. The displayed equality is thus a formal-completion descent statement whose independence from those choices is part of the proof.

35: ### Scan 065
36: 
37: - **C7.07 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 065, §6.3.** “les complétés formels ... sont identiques” gives the central reason for nilpotent invariance: the ideals defining $X_0$ and $X$ determine cofinal systems of powers. To obtain the asserted cohomology isomorphism, one must also identify the evaluated coefficient systems and their cosimplicial maps, then descend from affine embeddings. Cote 6, scans 019–021 posed a much wider de Rham crystal programme; this particular completion argument proves only the version with the hypotheses on $F$ stated on cote 7, scan 064.
38: 
39: - **C7.08 — Conjecture; Cote 7, scan 065, final bracket.** “Il faudrait regarder si on peut se débarrasser de l'hypothèse type quasi-cohérence” asks for an extension beyond the class used in the proof. The equality of the underlying formal neighbourhood systems does not by itself imply that every sheaf has the same cohomology, since both finite-level acyclicity and the interchange of limits can fail outside the controlled coefficient category. A useful first formulation would specify whether $F$ is still cartesian, derived-complete, or acyclic on the relevant thickenings; no answer for arbitrary sheaves is asserted here.

43: - **C7.09 — Unpursued implication; Cote 7, scan 066, lift independence.** “ne dépend pas ... de la façon dont on a relevé” separates the invariant cohomology object from a chosen smooth lifting, while the final insertion asks to extend it even to thickenings that do not locally retract. This revisits cote 6, scans 020–021, but the characteristic-zero clause on the present leaf matters in light of the explicit retractions on cote 6, scans 027–030. In particular, it cannot be read as reinstating the earlier unrestricted integral crystal assertion in positive characteristic.

45: - **C7.10 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 066, “on verra”.** “can. isom” to de Rham hypercohomology is the missing comparison needed to turn lift independence into the canonical de Rham stratification. The smooth characteristic-zero comparison is established in Grothendieck (1968, Theorem 4.1 and §5.3); its applicability requires interpreting characteristic zero as a base over $\mathbb Q$, with smoothness as on the manuscript. Passing from a cohomology-group isomorphism to an absolute crystal on $Rf_*\Omega^\bullet$ additionally requires coherent functorial comparison maps in the derived category.

55: ### Scan 069
56: 
57: - **C7.13 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 069, properness and M.L.** “de dim. finie” makes the images of any inverse system of the relevant cohomology groups stabilize at each fixed level, because a decreasing chain of finite-dimensional subspaces stabilizes. The further passage through cohomology in the cosimplicial direction should be justified using the corresponding finite-dimensional cycle and boundary systems, not merely by writing a limit outside $H^p$. This identifies a second use of Mittag–Leffler beyond the affine section calculation of scan 061 (Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tag 02MY).
58: 
59: - **C7.14 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scan 069, GAGA step.** “grâce à GAGA” is a finite-neighbourhood comparison before it is a completed comparison: each coherent finite-level sheaf is compared with its analytification, and scan 069 then takes inverse limits. Serre (1956, Theorems 1–3) is the primary projective GAGA reference; the manuscript's word “propre” calls for the appropriate extension to proper complex schemes. Commutation with the infinite completion is a separate step and is not a formal consequence of analytification alone.

61: ### Scan 070
62: 
63: - **C7.15 — Conjecture; Cote 7, scan 070, analytic conjecture.** “Pour tout espace analytique ... une résolution” is a local exactness assertion for the completed diagonal cosimplicial complex, including singular analytic spaces. It corresponds closely to Grothendieck (1968, Conjecture 5.1, pp. 343–344), whereas the preceding smooth comparison does not by itself settle the singular case. The algebra examples in supplied cote 5, scan 037 are useful tests but concern particular differential complexes and do not prove this universal resolution statement.
64: 
65: - **C7.16 — Unpursued implication; Cote 7, scan 070, later formal analytic comparison.** For the assertion “une résolution”, Hartshorne (1975, IV, Theorem 2.1, p. 92) proves that the de Rham complex of a complex manifold, formally completed along a closed analytic subspace, resolves the constant sheaf on that subspace. This provides a precise later comparison theorem for an ambient formally completed de Rham complex. Applying it to the exact complex $\mathcal F^*_{\mathfrak X}$ written here still requires a comparison between that complex and the ambient one; the theorem's title alone does not discharge that step.

67: - **C7.17 — Own idea; Cote 7, scan 070, formal-fibre heuristic.** The sentence “Cela semble plausible, en regardant les ‘fibres formelles’” suggests testing whether formal-fibre exactness detects exactness of the original completed complex. Confidence: medium as a proof strategy, because although $A\to\widehat A$ is faithfully flat for a noetherian local ring and detects exactness after tensoring for arbitrary $A$-modules, identifying that tensor product with the manuscript's termwise completions requires additional hypotheses. A valid argument must justify the comparison between completion and tensor product for this particular system, or construct a homotopy with the required convergence; faithful flatness by itself does not make that identification (Stacks Project Authors, 2026, Tags 00MA and 00MC).

69: - **C7.18 — Unproved claim; Cote 7, scans 070–071, conditional theorem.** “Si la conjecture ... est vraie” makes the proper comparison theorem conditional, and “les hom de transition sont des isom” is the crucial claim in its extension to infinitesimal thickenings in scan 071. One must check that the isomorphisms commute with transition maps and remove any $R^1\!\varprojlim$ term before identifying the inverse limit with a single topological cohomology group. The cancelled assertion about all $Y(i)^{\mathrm{an}}$ should not be silently restored as an available hypothesis.

71: - **C7.19 — Unpursued implication; Cote 7, scans 070–071, later infinitesimal result.** The intended comparison with “$H^*(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb C)$” is established for the infinitesimal site of a complex scheme locally of finite type in Liu (2025, Theorems 1.1/14.1), with an explicit ambient de Rham comparison in Theorem 1.2. The checked December 2, 2025 author manuscript is a preprint and is cited as such. It concerns the infinitesimal-site side; it should not be used to claim that every coefficient or every stratifying-site assertion in cote 7, scans 071–072 has thereby been proved.

73: - **C7.24 — Own idea; Cote 7, scans 070–072, singular test programme.** Compare the completed-diagonal complex, an ambient completed de Rham complex, and ordinary Kähler de Rham forms separately for a node, a cusp, and a nilpotent thickening. Confidence: medium as a research programme, because these examples separate singularity, nonnormality and nonreduced structure, the three mechanisms potentially hidden by the phrase “tout espace analytique”. Cote 5, scan 037 supplies related local algebra tests, but any successful calculation must record which of the three complexes it actually computes.

75: - **C7.25 — Unpursued implication; Cote 7, scans 070–072, completed diagonal comparison.** For the written completed-diagonal construction and “À prouver”, Bhatt (2012, Corollary 4.30, p. 13) identifies the derived pushforward of Hartshorne's de Rham complex with the totalization of the structure-sheaf cohomology of the formal completions of the iterated fibre products along the diagonal, for a finite-type flat map of noetherian $\mathbb Q$-schemes. These are ordinary termwise adic completions with structure-sheaf coefficients; for a finite-type affine $\mathbb C$-scheme the resulting completed Amitsur complex computes its complex Betti cohomology (Example 4.23, p. 12). This supplies a precise later algebraic comparison for the flat case, without settling the fragment's arbitrary-analytic-space or unrestricted-coefficient assertions.

81: ### Scan 072
82: 
83: - **C7.21 — Own idea; Cote 7, scan 072, affine reduction.** For the proposed nilpotent invariance, test $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ and $X_0=\operatorname{Spec}k$ over a field $k$: every diagonal ideal in $X^{n+1}$ is nilpotent, so the completed-diagonal terms are the ordinary tensor powers $A^{\otimes_k(n+1)}$. The augmentation $A\to k$ gives the augmented Amitsur complex a contracting homotopy, yielding $k$ in degree zero and zero in positive degrees. Confidence: high for this explicit case, because evaluation of the first tensor factor supplies the contraction; it is only a test case, consistent with the Artinian examples listed in cote 5, scan 037.

85: - **C7.22 — Conjecture; Cote 7, scan 072, nonarchimedean remark.** “la validité de la conjecture” for the nonarchimedean analytic setting raises a topology-sensitive analogue, with parts of the rigid-analytic wording cancelled. Supplied cote 5, scans 051–052 had already proposed a naive comparison through constant $\mathbb Z_p$ coefficients and then doubted its ability to recover the correct Betti numbers. The present remark, including its caution about global implications, should therefore not be read as a claim that ordinary constant-sheaf cohomology already provides the later $p$-adic comparison theory.

87: - **C7.23 — Own idea; Cote 7, scan 072, convergence test.** To test the transfer from formal exactness to nonarchimedean functions, over $\mathbb Q_p$ consider $f(T)=\sum_{r\ge1}p^rT^{p^r-1}\in\mathbb Q_p\langle T\rangle$. The only possible primitive, up to a constant, is $\sum_{r\ge1}T^{p^r}$, whose coefficients do not tend to zero, so $f(T)dT$ has no primitive in that closed-disc Tate algebra although it does on smaller discs. Confidence: high because coefficientwise differentiation determines those coefficients; this demonstrates a convergence obstruction, not a refutation of every local assertion for the incompletely specified “flasque” spaces in the manuscript.
