611: - **T.25 — Conjecture; Cote 8, scan 075 — “Hodge (simple, généralisée)”.** The agenda does not specify whether “généralisée” is Hodge’s original filtration statement or a corrected sub-Hodge-structure formulation. Grothendieck’s published distinction is essential: ordinary rational Hodge classes and generalized coniveau assertions are separate, and the naive generalized equality needs correction (Grothendieck, 1969, §§1–2, pp. 299–301). The heading alone cannot determine which formulation the author means or date this particular leaf relative to that correction.
612: 
613: - **T.26 — Conjecture; Cote 8, scan 075 — “Tate (simple, généralisée)”.** A concrete benchmark is Tate’s divisor assertion $\mathrm{NS}_k(X)\otimes\mathbf Q_\ell\simeq H^2_{\mathrm{ét}}(X_{\bar k},\mathbf Q_\ell(1))^{G_k}$ for smooth projective geometrically irreducible varieties over a finite field and $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$; his paper proves it for products of curves and abelian varieties (Tate, 1966, §3, pp. 142–144, Theorems 3–4). The manuscript’s juxtaposition of Galois and Frobenius actions fits this restricted comparison, but does not specify a higher-codimension or generalized statement. Even in a numerical reformulation one must distinguish the dimension of fixed vectors from the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue $1$, as a nontrivial Jordan block shows.

615: - **T.27 — Own idea; Cote 8, scan 075 — testing a possible Frobenius/filtration reading.** “Frobenius dans Ch. de De Rham (à connexion)” should not automatically mean preservation of the Hodge filtration: take $D=\mathbf Q_pe_0\oplus\mathbf Q_pe_1$, $F(e_0)=e_0$, $F(e_1)=pe_1$, and $\mathrm{Fil}^1D=\mathbf Q_p(e_0+e_1)$, $\mathrm{Fil}^0D=D$, $\mathrm{Fil}^2D=0$. Its Hodge and Newton totals are both $1$, its two proper $F$-stable lines satisfy the weak-admissibility inequalities, but $F(\mathrm{Fil}^1D)\ne\mathrm{Fil}^1D$. **Confidence: high**, from these explicit calculations using the criterion cited in item T.03; this leaves existence, horizontality, and cycle-detection readings of the heading genuinely open.

639: - **E.35 — Unproved claim; 8:078, (1.1)–(1.3) —.** The boxed formula agrees with Bergh and Rydh's functorial statement for a multiplicative-type group: its co-Lie complex $Le^*L_{G/S}$ is the derived tensor of its character sheaf with $\mathcal O_S$ (2019, Proposition B.4, pp. 33–34, in the dated version cited below). 641: - **E.36 — Own idea; 8:078, (1.1)–(1.2) — isolate the infinite-resolution issue.** When $M$ is locally a finitely generated abelian group, choose locally a finite free resolution $0\to R\to\mathbb Z^r\to M\to0$, and use the resulting two-term complex to compare with the canonical but potentially infinite resolution $\mathbb Z[M]\to M$ on the sheet. The substantive check is that changing the resolution gives the same derived object and that these local identifications descend. Confidence is high as a proof strategy because the manuscript already states both the resolution and the derived tensor target; it avoids assuming that a possibly infinite-dimensional “torus” automatically satisfies every finite-presentation hypothesis of a transitivity theorem.

643: - **E.37 — Own idea; 8:078, (1.3)–(1.4) — a torsion and scalar-change diagnostic.** For the test character group $M=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, the derived formula gives $[\mathcal O_S\xrightarrow{n}\mathcal O_S]$ in degrees $-1,0$: it is acyclic when $n$ is invertible, while for $n=p$ in characteristic $p$ both cohomology modules are $\mathcal O_S$. Derived associativity also gives the factorization through any intermediate coefficient ring $\Lambda$ displayed in (1.4). Confidence is high because both statements follow by tensoring the elementary free resolution of $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$; the example is a research test, not an attribution of torsion to a word “réseau” that may have been intended to mean torsion-free, and the ending “et si” supplies no additional conclusion.
