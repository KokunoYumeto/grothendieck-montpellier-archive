## 1. Literal axioms before a weaker theorem

**Origins.** Items SA26-1, SA26-21, SA26-24, SA26-25, SA26-52. Claims C-SA26-106-01, C-SA26-106-02, C-SA26-106-03, C-SA26-106-18, C-SA26-107B-05, C-SA26-107B-06. Problems MP-SA26-01, MP-SA26-04, MP-SA26-71.

**What this would establish.** Whether H106 and the modern fundamental-localizer axioms are actually the hypotheses intended in the two manuscripts, and which disputed arrows or subscripts the source contains. It would convert a sufficient modern reconstruction into a source-accurate companion where the ink permits that conclusion.

**What is known.** Brown’s checked original uses A–E in the fibrant convention. The frozen cote 106 text reads C1–C3 and names Brown; contextual notes sometimes say Baues. The initial object is inserted. The section diagram needs opposite directions for the weak map and its section. In cote 107, Cisinski’s filtered-stability theorem applies to every fundamental localizer, but a class merely called W need not satisfy that definition. [Brown1973; RB2009; Cisinski2006]

**First concrete step.** In a scan-equipped run inspect 106/002 L4–5, 106/014 L1372–1379 and 106/016 L1573–1579. Then locate the original definitions cited by 107/019 and make an axiom-by-axiom source table. Resolve the attribution before looking for a weaker theorem. A few hours can settle or sharply delimit these readings if the defining leaves and scans are available.

## 3. Minimal denominator hypotheses and recognition of W

**Origins.** Items SA26-8, SA26-17, SA26-18, SA26-20, SA26-37. Claims C-SA26-106-08, C-SA26-106-13, C-SA26-106-18. Problems MP-SA26-03, MP-SA26-04, MP-SA26-05.

**What this would establish.** An exact distinction between the hypotheses needed to localize a pushout-stable denominator class D and an extra condition ensuring this is the localization at a larger class W. The intended result is one quantified implication or counterexample, not an unspecified W-only theory.

**What is known.** The strict D-roof component construction localizes D when D contains identities, is closed under composition and cobase change, and the relevant pushouts exist. Under H106, split factorization forces every W-map to become invertible. Couniversality alone describes a cobase-change property; it is not the split-factorization premise. Batanin–Berger’s comparison with trivial h-cofibrations has its own exact hypotheses. [C-SA26-106-08,13; BataninBerger2017, Lemma 1.10]

**First concrete step.** Choose one precise weakening, retain the explicit D-closure and pushout assumptions, and remove one recognition premise. Write the dependency table, then seek a finite relative-category countermodel to that one missing implication. A few-hour run can settle a narrow implication or produce a sharply formulated next question. A new literature search would still be needed before any novelty claim.

## 4. Higher information in the revised roof category

**Origins.** Items SA26-19, SA26-37, SA26-39. Claims C-SA26-106-07, C-SA26-106-18. Problems MP-SA26-02, MP-SA26-04.

**What this would establish.** Whether the joint-cofibration replacements comparing the revised F1 roof category with F0 give an equivalence of nerves under a chosen hypothesis package, or only the connected-component bijection proved here.

**What is known.** The original F0 mapping-space framework is established by the dual Cisinski result and Horel. The present finite-zigzag construction proves π0. It does not supply contractible comma categories or coherent higher choices. Those are additional proof obligations; their absence from this dossier is not evidence that the stronger theorem is unknown. [Cisinski2010K, Proposition 3.23; Horel2016]

**First concrete step.** Fix the exact direction and morphisms of J→F1. Form the relevant comma categories and test separately for nonemptiness, connectedness and contraction data. Follow primary cocycle-calculus references for that orientation before developing a proof. A few hours should identify a valid reduction or a concrete obstruction; a general mapping-space theorem may take substantially longer.

## 5. Relative-square diagrams and Quillen’s original pagination

**Origins.** Items SA26-31, SA26-33, SA26-52. Claims C-SA26-107B-09, C-SA26-107B-10, C-SA26-107B-11. Problem MP-SA26-72.

**What this would establish.** A source-accurate account of the surviving existential iff, the compatible homotopies, the cancelled two-endpoint attempt and the original reference to Quillen.

**What is known.** The criterion is settled under the stated cofibrancy, fibrancy and cylinder/simplicial assumptions. The covering-map example refutes automatic compatibility. The modern retypesetting’s Proposition 2.2.4 gives the relevant extension theorem but does not settle the source’s handwritten chapter/page alternatives. [Quillen1967; Hovey1999, Lemma 4.2.2]

**First concrete step.** Inspect 107/021–025 for barred φ, prime labels, ph/pk, endpoint marks and i±1. Compare an original 1967 Quillen scan and preserve the distinction between cancelled and surviving formulas. A few hours can resolve the editorial issue if the images are available; this is not an unresolved lifting theorem.

## 6. A positive theorem with an explicitly bounded target class

**Origins.** Items SA26-32, SA26-33, SA26-52. Claims C-SA26-107B-04, C-SA26-107B-08, C-SA26-107B-12, C-SA26-107B-13, C-SA26-107B-14. Problems MP-SA26-70, MP-SA26-73, MP-SA26-74.

**What this would establish.** A useful positive mapping or retraction theorem for one specified geometric class of sequences and targets, with a verifiable condition eliminating the component-compatibility or lim¹ obstruction.

**What is known.** The unrestricted assertion is false. Mapping spaces for cofibration sequences form fibration towers, giving surjectivity; the exact obstruction is additional compatibility, not stagewise existence. General lim¹ is a pointed orbit construction, and the telescope is a finite-subcomplex phantom example rather than a finite-type skeletal one. [Milnor1962; Hatcher2002; Hirschhorn2015]

**First concrete step.** Select one class actually motivated by the archive, state whether targets are fixed or universally quantified, and test stabilization of the relevant restriction groups or components. A result for a restricted target class must not be called an ordinary colimit in all of Hot. A few-hour run can evaluate one candidate vanishing hypothesis; it should not attempt a classification of all phantom-free diagrams.

## 7. Historical attribution of the stronger nonexistence argument

**Origins.** Item SA26-52. Claims C-SA26-107B-04, C-SA26-107B-12. Problem MP-SA26-70.

**What this would establish.** An exact primary citation, if there is one, for nonexistence of any ordinary colimit of degree-p circles, distinguished from failure of the telescope cocone.

**What is known.** The proof using discrete targets, all abelian K(M,1), Yoneda and the universal coefficient injection is valid and is supplied in full. The opened early sources give the cohomological defect and standard tools. No checked primary source located in this run states that exact stronger argument, so its first publication is left unassigned. [Milnor1962; Hatcher2002]

**First concrete step.** Follow published bibliographies on phantom maps and incompleteness of homotopy categories backward, looking for this exact universal-property contradiction and its unbased conventions. Record a negative result as bounded. A few-hour run may close the citation gap; it would not establish that the argument is new.
