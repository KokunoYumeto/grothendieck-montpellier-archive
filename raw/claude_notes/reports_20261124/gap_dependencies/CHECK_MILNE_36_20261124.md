# Milne's report, §3.6: does the missing sign reach Theorems 3.1 and 3.5?

(Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max). This answers double-check item 3 of `GAP_DEPENDENCIES_20261124.md` (register row 8).

## Verdict

**Unaffected.** The sign is missing only in the display of §3.6, which restates the Hodge standard conjecture. Neither
proof uses that display.
- **Theorem 3.1** (Tate) does not touch §3.6 at all.
- **Theorem 3.5** (Hodge standard conjecture) is Milne 2002's theorem. The source states and proves it with the sign
  (−1)^r. The report's sketch of the proof works with "geometric pairings" being positive, and so carries the signs
  correctly.
- **The repair is in the text only.** In §3.6, the form should read (a, b) ↦ (−1)^r a·b·ξ^{g−2r} on P^r(A).

## What I read

The numbering below follows the shared paragraph counter of §3 (`HTS.tex` l.445 onwards); the labels are the source's.

- **Theorem 3.1** (label h20, l.480-483): "The full Tate conjecture holds for all abelian varieties over finite fields."
  The proof sketch follows it:
  - §3.2 (h21) is the Lichtenbaum–Milne form;
  - §3.3 (h22) gives the Lefschetz classes;
  - §3.4 (h23, "a serendipitous equality") argues tannakian-ly. The group G of Mot(F) satisfies G ⊆ S, by the CM Hodge
    theorem, and G ⊆ L. Tate is equivalent to P = G, and P = L ∩ S.

  No positivity and no sign occurs anywhere in this chain. The report puts its summary of Milne 1999a,b at l.601,
  *before* the subsection that contains §3.6.
- **Theorem 3.5** (h24, l.606-609): "The standard conjecture of Hodge type holds for all abelian varieties." Then:
  - **§3.6** (h25, l.611-628) defines P^r(A) = Ker L^{g−2r+1} and says the conjecture is that a·b·ξ^{g−2r} is positive
    definite on P^r(A). **This is where (−1)^r is missing.**
  - §3.7 (h26) reduces to finite fields by specialization.
  - §3.8-3.9 (h27, h28) give the tannakian reformulation: a polarization "for which the geometric pairings are positive".
  - §3.10 (h29): Hodge theory gives such a polarization on CM(Q^al), and it passes through the diagram of §3.4 to Mot(F).

  The sketch never uses the displayed unsigned formula. Its positivity statements are the Hodge–Riemann relations and
  polarizations of tannakian categories, which carry the signs.
- **The source of Theorem 3.5,** Milne, *Polarizations and Grothendieck's standard conjectures*, Ann. of Math. 155
  (2002); arXiv math/0103175 (the builder's cached e-print, `ArxivMilne.tex`). It states the conjecture with the sign:
  "for r ≤ n/2, the bilinear form (x, y) ↦ (−1)^r⟨L^{n−2r}x·y⟩ : P^r(V) × P^r(V) → Q is positive-definite". It proves
  positive-definiteness of the same signed forms, for instance on DP^r(A) through the Lefschetz motives.

**Minimal check that the literal display is false, so the repair is needed.** Take A = E × E with the product
polarization ξ = e₁ + e₂, so g = 2 and r = 1. Here e₁ and e₂ are the pullbacks of a point class, with e_i² = 0 and
e₁e₂ = 1.
- x = e₁ − e₂ is primitive: x·ξ = e₁e₂ − e₂e₁ = 0.
- x² = −2 < 0. The unsigned form is negative on P¹, which is the Hodge index theorem.
- The signed form (−1)^1·x² = 2 is positive.
