### NK1. Kodaira vanishing fails for very ample line bundles on surfaces in every characteristic; the open item of our P90a note is already closed inside the pile

- **Statement.** For every prime p and every algebraically closed field k of characteristic p there is a smooth projective
  connected surface X with a very ample line bundle A such that H^1(X, A^{-1}) is nonzero. For every smooth connected
  H in |A|, the restriction Pic_X -> Pic_H is therefore not a monomorphism: its kernel has Lie algebra H^1(X, A^{-1}).
- **Where it is proved.** POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 1, `thm:all-primes` (RR/POLISH_CRISTAUX_checkpoint_33_.../workbench/papers/
  paper1/main.tex l.115), with `prop:polarization` (l.210) and `lem:roots` (Section 3). The construction: the curve
  y^p - y = x^{hp-1} (h = 8 for p = 2, h = p^2 - 2 for odd p), the Tango-Mukai extension V, the cyclic cover S of degree
  p+1 of P(V), the generated ample L = O_S(pE) (x) f^*N, then simultaneous q-th roots (q = (p+1)^2, prime to p) of a basis
  of H^0(L^q). The pullback sections of L and the root sections together embed X; H^1(S, L^{-1}) survives as the
  trivial-character summand.
- **Check status.**
  - a512cf71 (Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max), cp12 intake: targeted proof check of Thm 1.2's vanishing ranges and the
    choice of m.
  - Re-derived here at reading level, for every p:
    - nonvanishing: the i = p summand of R^1 f_* L^{-1} is (Sym^{p-2} V)^vee (x) T^{p-2}, and it contains O_C through
      V -> T. Since f_* L^{-1} = 0, Leray gives H^1(S, L^{-1}) != 0;
    - generation of L: N = O_C(nP_inf) is generated by 1 and x^{n/p}, with n = p(p-2) for odd p;
    - very ampleness after roots: the root sections alone do not separate the diagonal mu_q-orbit; the pullback sections of
      the generated L do. This is why a generated polarization is needed.
- **Why it matters.**
  - Our posted note hexagon:2611.00936 (P90a = SWEEP3_B's SA14 A3 = POLISH_ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY Paper 2 Thm 1.4) answers
    Raynaud's question in characteristic 2 only. Its unposted v2 (`reports_20261123/p90a_note/p90a_note_v2.tex`, l.467-469)
    lists "Odd characteristic" as open: "we have not checked whether a twist of Raynaud's bundle along the cusp section is
    globally generated". POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 1's `prop:polarization` proves exactly that, for every p.
  - So v2 can state every characteristic, or the two papers should be merged into one note. No cluster file or intake
    connected the two.
- **Credit and literature.**
  - Paper 1 credits Raynaud, Mukai and Zheng: the nonvanishing is Zheng's range endpoint a = p, b = 1. Lauritzen-Rao
    answer the very ample question only in dimension >= 6, and with the penultimate cohomology group (SWEEP3_B, item 2).
  - The scheme-level Picard failure for ample divisors in all dimensions follows from Cotner-Zavyalov, Example 3.3.6, with
    Totaro's observation on Mukai's higher-dimensional examples (cited in Paper 1 l.137; I did not open Cotner-Zavyalov).
  - The new parts are very ampleness on a surface, and the explicit degree thresholds.
- **Frame.** SGA 2 XII (Lefschetz for Pic; its conditions Leff and Lef need H^i(X, L^{-n}) = 0); cote 57:97-100
  ("noyau nul"); cote 4 §8.3 (the admitted integral weak Lefschetz, refuted by the same surface times P^1).

### NK2. The Jacobian-factor index of a very general principally polarized abelian variety: s_J = 2 in dimensions 4 and 5, s_J = 6 in dimension 6

- **Definitions.**
  - I_J(A) is the set of integers n such that [n]_A factors through a product of Jacobians of smooth projective curves; it
    is an ideal. s_J(A) is its positive generator (SA06 C.4; POLISH_ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY Paper 5, Thm 1.3).
  - For a principally polarized (A, theta) of dimension g, put gamma = theta^{g-1}/(g-1)!, let M(A) be the set of integers
    m such that m·gamma is the class of an integral algebraic 1-cycle, and let m(A) be its positive generator.
- **Theorem (proved here).** For every complex principally polarized abelian variety A,
  s_J(A) | m(A) | s_J(A)^2. In particular rad s_J = rad m, and s_J = m whenever m is squarefree. Consequently, for a very
  general complex principally polarized A of dimension g:
  - s_J(A) = 2 if g = 4 or 5;
  - s_J(A) = 6 if g = 6;
  - for every g >= 4, s_J(A) is even and divides (g-1)!.
- **Proof.**
  - (s_J | m.) If an integral 1-cycle has class n·theta^{g-1}/(g-1)!, then n·chi(L) is in I_J. This is SA06's Matsusaka
    step, POLISH_ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY Paper 5 `lem:scalar`. Here chi = 1, so M is contained in I_J. Since theta^{g-1} is
    algebraic, (g-1)! is in I_J.
  - (m | s_J^2.) Let [n] = r o i with i: A -> P = prod J(C_a). Then i_* gamma is an integral Hodge class on P. By
    Beckmann-de Gaay Fortman (arXiv 2202.05230, Compositio; abstract opened), products of Jacobians satisfy the integral
    Hodge conjecture for 1-cycles, so i_* gamma = [Z] for an integral 1-cycle Z. Then r_*[Z] = [n]_* gamma = n^2 gamma,
    because [n] acts on H_2 = Lambda^2 H_1 by n^2. Hence m | n^2.
  - (Very general values.) Engel-de Gaay Fortman-Schreieder, arXiv 2507.15704 v3 (27 Mar 2026; abstract opened): every
    curve class on a very general ppav of dimension >= 4 is an even multiple of gamma, so 2 | m. SA06 C.4m (Beauville's
    generalized Pryms, every ppav of dimension <= 5, all characteristics) gives 2 in M. Hence m = 2 for g = 4, 5. For
    g = 6: Engel-de Gaay Fortman-Schreieder, arXiv 2512.04902 (4 Dec 2025; abstract opened): m = 6. SA06 C.4o (ADFIO 2020
    Prym-Tyurin of exponent 6) gives 6 in M. Squarefree m gives s_J = m.
- **Status.** Proved here, every step checked by me. Inputs: SA06 C.4m and C.4o (re-proved or targeted-checked by
  a0012f1f, Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max); BdGF and EGFS at abstract level.
- **What it adds.**
  - SA06 states the sixfold minimal-class sharpness (C4_pt6_addition.tex l.136-140), but not s_J; its equivalence
    (C.4e) is the case s_J = 1 only.
  - The general relation s_J | m | s_J^2 and the three exact values are new as far as the run and its intakes record.
  - They make POLISH_ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY Paper 5 Thm 1.3 sharp in dimensions 4, 5 and 6 on very general members, and
    answer Grothendieck's integral Jacobian-factor question (cote 14:69-71) there: "no, but [2] (resp. [6]) does factor".
- **Frame.** Motives and correspondences with Z-coefficients: s_J is the exponent of the obstruction to h^1(A) being a
  direct summand of h^1 of a product of curves.

### NK3. A shift law for the inertia-character index (POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 2), and a gate that is already closed at v = 0

- **The two results it unifies (same paper, different threads).**

    nu_min(p, e, 2) = ceil((1 + log_p e)/2); that is, nu_min = r for p^{2r-3} < e < p^{2r-1}.
  - The merge run's bands (l.127, l.6786, l.6926): for p >= 5, v >= 0, p ∤ k,
    nu_min(p, (p-1)p^v k, 2) = v+1 (k = 1), v+2 (2 <= k <= p^2+p+1), v+3 (p^2+p+2 <= k <= p^3+p^2+p+1).
- **Computation (here).** At v = 0 the bands coincide with the log formula for every k in their range (p = 5, 7, 11, 13;
  0 mismatches; `geomap/checks.py`). So at v = 0 the merge run's gate "multipliers beyond p^3+p^2+p+1" is already settled
  by thread A's theorem: nu_min = 3 for p^2+p+2 <= k <= p^4+p^3+p^2+p+1, and 4 from e > p^5.
- **Conjecture (the shift law).** For p >= 5, v >= 0 and p ∤ k,
  nu_min(p, (p-1)p^v k, 2) = v + ceil((1 + log_p((p-1)k))/2), that is, nu_min(p, p^v e', 2) = v + nu_min(p, e', 2) for e'
  prime to p.
  - Evidence: it holds on every proved range: all k at v = 0; all v for k <= p^3+p^2+p+1; `thm:first-wild-general`

    Each factor p of the wild exponent costs one level.
  - First open case: v >= 1, p^3+p^2+p+2 <= k <= p^4+p^3+p^2+p+1, predicted value v+3.
  - What would close it: run the cubic-band argument (the two necessary configurations of `lem:cubic-two-patterns`) with
    the log-formula's upper comparison instead of the fixed bound p^3.
- **Status.** Conjecture; consistency computed here; no proof.
- **Why it matters.** It turns the merge run's open "higher multipliers" into one statement with a mechanism, for its

### NK4. Grothendieck's cote 133 question over orders in quadratic fields: polynomial generation holds exactly for maximal orders of class number one

- **Theorem (proved here, from two runs' checked theorems).** Let K be a quadratic number field, O an order of K, and
  Phi_O: Z[h_1, h_2, ...] -> R(O) = ⊕_n K_0(Lat_O(S_n)) the map h_n -> [trivial lattice]. Lattices are finite projective
  O-modules with S_n-action, and exact sequences are O-split, as in SA17. Then Phi_O is an isomorphism iff O = O_K and
  h_K = 1.
- **Proof.**
  - (If.) SA17 `C:regular-coherent-criterion` (C_finish_regular_coherent.tex l.194): for regular coherent k, Phi_k is an
    isomorphism iff char k = 0 and Z -> K_0(k) is an isomorphism. O_K is Dedekind, hence regular coherent, and
    K_0(O_K) = Z ⊕ Cl_K.
  - (Only if, O = O_K.) Degree 0 forces Cl_K = 0.
  - (Only if, O non-maximal, conductor f, p | f.) Then O (x) Z_(p) = Z_(p)[fw] = Z_(p)[t]/(F), with
    F = t^2 - f Tr(w) t + f^2 N(w). F ≡ t^2 mod p, so this ring is local; F is distinguished with a_0 in p^2.
    NIGHT_GROTHENDIECK_QUESTIONS `thm:singular-distinguished-monic` (papers/A133_lattices/singular_distinguished_monic.tex
    l.14; the base DVR need not be complete; checked by a0805369, Claude Opus 5.5 at effort high, as Thm 26.1) gives
    eta: K_0(Lat_{O_(p)}(S_p)) -> ⊕ Z/2, onto, killing every Young permutation lattice.
  - Localization K_0(Lat_O(S_p)) -> K_0(Lat_{O_(p)}(S_p)) is onto. Given N, put N' = N ∩ ∩_{q≠p} (L_0)_q in N[1/p],
    with L_0 = O (x) V_Z. This is locally free, hence projective, and S_p-stable, with N'_(p) = N. Young lattices go to
    Young lattices. So eta o loc is nonzero and kills the image of Phi_O in degree p.
- **Example.** Z[sqrt(-3)] has trivial Picard group, so K_0 = Z, and still fails. Grothendieck asks on cote 133, scan 29
  "whether locality, or the condition K_0(k) ≃ Z, suffices"; this example answers "no" globally, not only for local rings.
- **Status.** Proved here: my step is the localization lemma; the two theorems are the runs' and were checked by others.
- **Extension (argument; one step remains).** The same proof works for every order O having a prime p with O (x) Z_(p)
  local, singular and monogenic, for example monogenic orders Z[alpha] with alpha's polynomial ≡ h^e mod p (NGQ's
  `thm:monogenic-residue-degree` covers residue degree f > 1). For semilocal O (x) Z_(p) one must pass to the completion
  at the singular prime. The gluing then needs the generic fibre of the detecting lattice to come from a K-representation,
  which is automatic when that completion is a domain.
- **Frame.** λ-rings and K-theory (cote 133, scans 28-29; Swan-Gersten for the Dedekind case, as NGQ credits).

### NK5. Int of the two-branch domains is never flat; the one-branch/two-branch dichotomy for integer-valued polynomials

- **Theorem (proved here; extends Ma, arXiv 2604.05922 v2, Thm 1.1, which treats q = 2).** For every prime power q, let
  T = F_q[t] localized at the complement of (t) ∪ (t-1), m = t(t-1), and D_q = F_q + mT (SWEEP3_B's SA20 B.016 domain).
  Then Int(D_q) is not flat over D_q.
- **Proof.** Ma's argument, Prop. 4.1, with X^q - X in place of X^2 + X, checked here step by step:
  - D_q is local Noetherian of dimension one with maximal ideal M = mT, and T = D_q + D_q t, since t^2 - t = m.
  - The conductor is M, so M^{-1} = (M : M) = T, an overring. By Elliott's criterion (Comm. Algebra 39 (2011), Prop. 2.13,
    as quoted by Ma), flatness would force Int(D_q, T) = T·Int(D_q).
  - f = (X^q - X)/m lies in Int(D_q, T), because (a + mv)^q - (a + mv) = m(m^{q-1}v^q - v).
  - f is not in T·Int(D_q). If X^q - X = Σ a_i h_i with a_i in M and h_i in Int(D_q), evaluate at u = t(t-1)^{n+1} (n
    large) and at 0. Each h_i(u) - h_i(0) lies in D_q ∩ N_1 = M, so u^q - u lies in M^2 and hence u lies in M^2. But
    v_{(t)}(u) = 1 < 2 <= v_{(t)}(M^2).
- **Status.** Proved here (an independent re-proof of Ma's q = 2 case, plus the extension).
- **The dichotomy.**
  - **One branch.** POLISH_TOPOLOGY_PERIODS S20 (hexagon:2611.00937; generalizing SA20 B.022-B.024): Int(R) is free for
    every one-dimensional analytically irreducible Noetherian local domain. SA20 B.050/B.054: for one-dimensional
    Noetherian domains, the tensor maps Int(R)^{⊗n} -> Int(R^n) are isomorphisms, with a unique compatible plethory,
    exactly when R is analytically irreducible at every finite-residue maximal ideal.
  - **Two branches.** SA20 B.016: no compatible comultiplication tensor, for every q (Jiang-Li-Sun-Wang-Xiao-Yu, arXiv
    2605.25259 v2, Thm 3.4, is the case q = 2; it answers Cahen-Fontana-Frisch-Glaz Problem 21). Non-flat, by NK5 (Ma is
    q = 2; that is CFFG Problem 19).
  - **Conjecture.** For a one-dimensional Noetherian domain R with finite normalization: Int(R) flat ⇔ Int(R) projective
    ⇔ R is analytically irreducible at every maximal ideal with finite residue field.
  - Reduction: SA20 B.044 (Int commutes with one-dimensional finite-residue completion) reduces the conjecture to complete
    reduced local rings with at least two branches.
  - Remaining step: an ideal I with I^{-1} an overring that separates two branches, in place of M = N_0 ∩ N_1.
- **Why it matters.** It fixes the picture CFFG Problems 19 and 21 ask about, and it is the statement S20 v2 should
  carry.
- **Frame.** Binomial rings are λ-rings with trivial Adams operations; Int(Z) is the free binomial ring; flat Int(R) is a
  plethory (Elliott; Borger-Wieland).

### NK6. One congruence explains the three Q(sqrt(-23)) phenomena of cote 15 (SA06 B.3b, ARCH_ALG's Honda check, NIGHT_GROTHENDIECK_QUESTIONS A15)

- **Lemma (proved here; computed for e <= 15).** Let L = Q(sqrt(-23)), with h_L = 3 and 2 = 𝔭𝔭̄, [𝔭] of order 3. A Weil
  2^e-number π in L has (π) = 𝔭^a 𝔭̄^{e-a} with a ≡ 2e (mod 3), and every such a with 0 <= a <= e occurs.
  - Proof: (π) principal forces [𝔭]^{2a-e} = 1.
- **Consequences.**
  - **e = 1.** There are none: no weight-one Weil 2-number in L. This is SA06 B.3b's "no element of norm 2".
  - **e = 3.** Only ordinary ones (a ∈ {0, 3}), for instance α = (3 + sqrt(-23))/2. This is the Frobenius of
    E: y^2 + xy = x^3 + x^2 + (t+1) over F_8. As a weight-3 Weil 2-number, α appears on H^3(Res_{F_8/F_2} E) (B.3b).
  - **e = 9.** Slopes 1/3 and 2/3 appear, with Brauer invariants 1/3 and 2/3, hence index 3. This is ARCH_ALG's simple
    threefold A' over F_512 with Weil number 12 - 4 sqrt(-23) = 8ᾱ = α ᾱ^2.
  - **Minimum field.** A model of A' over F_{2^e} with central Frobenius in L needs a = e/3 ≡ 2e (mod 3), so 9 | e. This is
    a two-line proof of NGQ A15's minimum ("exactly when 9 divides e") in the central-Frobenius case. NGQ's paper handles
    the non-central models separately; I did not check that part.
- **Computation (here).** The Λ^3 characteristic polynomial (T^2 - 3T + 8)(T^6 - 24T^3 + 512)^3 of B.3b is confirmed, and
  T^6 - 24T^3 + 512 is that of H^1(A)(-1). Its roots β satisfy β^3 = 12 ± 4 sqrt(-23). So ARCH_ALG's weight-one Weil
  512-number is, over F_8, the Weil number of the Tate twist h^1(E)(-1).
- **Status.** Lemma and consequences proved here; computation run here. The pieces are classical Honda-Tate arithmetic.
- **Value.** It is a companion and teaching item (cote 15), not a research paper. It tells the conference that the three
  runs found one phenomenon.
- **Frame.** Motives over finite fields, weights, Weil numbers (cote 15).

### NK7. Picard kernels at three levels, with one mechanism behind the point level

- **Scheme level.** For Y connected and reduced, Lie ker(Pic_X -> Pic_Y) = H^1(X, O(-Y)).
  - Surfaces, very ample: NK1 (every p).
  - Threefolds, very ample: POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 1, `thm:intro-injectivity`, char 2 (two Tango iterations, 843 roots of
    order 445). The kernel has Lie algebra of dimension 1203 for Y in |H| and 1 for Y in |H^2|, and is a monomorphism at
    the Lie level for |H^d|, d >= 3.
  - Ample divisors, every dimension: Cotner-Zavyalov Ex. 3.3.6 with Totaro's remark on Mukai's examples (cited, not
    opened).
  - So the scheme-level reading of Grothendieck's boxed "noyau nul si n >= 4" (57:97-100) fails for ample divisors.
  - For very ample sections in dimension >= 4, one more Tango iteration should do it. The remaining step is a generated
    polarization at that stage.
- **Point level.** This is SA14 A1/A2 ("universal point injectivity"), which is open. The kernel is a finite p-group in
  dimension >= 3 (A4b).
  - The necessary conditions A2V, A2W, A2Y and A2Z all come from the dlog form of an order-p bundle trivial on Y.
  - POLISH_ALGEBRAIC_GEOMETRY Paper 2's `lem:positive-square-logarithmic` is the same lemma on surfaces (I re-derived it:
    p | m from closedness; the leading coefficient is a section of Ω^1_C(-mC)). `thm:positive-excess-picard` is its
    quantitative continuation.
  - P90a v2's open item "Picard kernels on geometric points" (Kweon Q7.7(i)) is the target of that theorem.
  - Corollary of the lemma alone: if K_X·Y < (p-1)Y^2, no order-p class is trivial on Y.
- **Frame.** FGA (Picard schemes), SGA 2 XII, cote 57.

### NK8. Hasse-Witt monodromy of curves on surfaces in characteristic 2: one template with two instances

- **The instances.**
  - SA15 `thm:plane-char2-all-groups` (sections/A_char2_uniform.tex): for plane curves of degree d >= 4, the monodromy of
    H^1_et(C, F_2) over the ordinary locus is GL_g(F_2) for even d. For odd d it is the full stabilizer of the canonical
    covector given by O_C((d-3)/2) (x) B_C^{-1}, where B_C is the sheaf of exact differentials.
  - POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 21, new at cp34 (RR/POLISH_CRISTAUX_checkpoint_34_..., NEW_RESULTS `thm:transvection-full-image`
    and `cor:full-image-reduction`): on |3dΘ| of an ordinary principally polarized surface, the relative system has rank
    9d^2 - 1. It has no invariant covector for odd d and three for even d. Its full image is equivalent to irreducibility
    of W, conditional on a natural GL_8 block.
- **The template (conjecture).** For C in |L| on a surface S, the invariant covectors are spanned by the classes
  (M|_C) (x) B_C^{-1}, for M running over the square roots of K_S(C) in Pic(S). Their number is 0 if no root exists, and
  otherwise 1 + rank of the restricted Pic(S)[2](k). The eventual image is the full pointwise stabilizer.
  - P^2: 1 for odd d, 0 for even d.
  - Ordinary abelian surface with L = 3dΘ: 1 + 2 = 3 for even d, 0 for odd d.
  - Both instances fit.
- **Group theory.** SA15's endgame (a primitive prime divisor of 2^a - 1, and H^1(GL_N(F_2), natural) = 0) is the core
  that Paper 21's full-image criterion needs. SA15's route through elements of order ℓ_a could supply Paper 21's missing
  irreducibility of W.
- **Status.** Conjecture plus a cross-run method suggestion. The odd-degree plane obstruction itself is known
  (Cais-Ellenberg-Zureick-Brown 2013, Thm 4.2; Castryck-Streng-Testa 2014; SWEEP3_B).
- **Frame.** Cote 4 §8.6 (Grothendieck's question on Hasse-Witt monodromy); unit-root F-crystals.

### NK9. Where the crystalline Dieudonné functor can fail: finite projective dimension of differentials is enough for full faithfulness on reduced bases of finite type

- **Chain (cited theorems; each step standard).** Let R be a reduced local F_p-algebra essentially of finite type with
  pd_R Ω_{R/F_p} < ∞.
  - Mok-Xu (hexagon:2611.00721; abstract opened) prove Vasconcelos' conjecture for reduced algebras in characteristic p
    exactly when [k : k^p] <= p, which holds for k = F_p. So R is a complete intersection.
  - Then R is lci, hence quasisyntomic.
  - Then the crystalline Dieudonné functor is fully faithful over R (Anschütz-Le Bras, Thm 4.74, as SA17's research notes
    locate it).
- **Consequence for SA17.** SA17's reduced counterexample (two planes meeting in a point,
  B:crystal-counterexamples, sections/B.tex l.1200) must have pd Ω = ∞, as must F_p[x,y]/(x,y)^2. So the B.3 question of
  cote 128-133 (scan 114) is reduced to rings of infinite projective dimension of Ω.
- **Status.** A chain of cited results; not checked beyond the abstracts and SA17's locators.

### NK10. Weak-formal H^1 (POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 9) and the outside integral theories

- **Paper 9** (paper9/main.tex abstract l.23):
  - integral weak-formal H^1 of a smooth proper X is finite free and embeds in H^1_cris; with a lift it is the
    constant-coefficient étale system (Ogus);
  - it vanishes on a supersingular elliptic curve;
  - rational-coefficient H^1 is rigid cohomology;
  - H^2 of A^1 contains ⊕ K/W.
- **So** integral weak-formal H^1 sees the slope-0 (unit-root) part. That is the object of SA15's unit-root counts
  (A2-UNIT, `thm:all-X-explicit-positive-rank`) and of Grothendieck's cote 4.

### 3.1 One very ample object, four readings (P90a, SA14 A3, SA15, POLISH_CRISTAUX Paper 1)

- **The object.** X is the char-2 surface (y^2 + y = x^15; 556 ninth roots; h^0(A) = 562; h^1(A^{-1}) = 1).
- **Its four readings:**
  - (a) Kodaira failure for a very ample bundle (P90a);
  - (b) Lie ker(Pic_X -> Pic_H) ≠ 0 (Kweon Q7.7(ii));
  - (c) on Z = X × P^1, every smooth Y in |A ⊠ O(m)| has more H^2_cris torsion than Z (SA15

- The **mechanism** of (c): the conjugate spectral sequence and crystalline universal coefficients turn the extra H^1(O)

  for hyperplanes.

  non-liftable surfaces in the pile, by a Hodge-class obstruction instead of a vanishing obstruction.

  characteristic", merging P90a v2, Paper 1 Thm 1.2, the Picard reading and the crystalline consequence.
