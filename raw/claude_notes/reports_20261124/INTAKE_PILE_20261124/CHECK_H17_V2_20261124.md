## The steps, as checked

**The exact form of (III′).**

  f(x, y) = g(y, x)⁻¹g(x, y) [LS2], (III′) reads f(σ₁σ₃, σ₂²) = g(x₄₅, x₅₁) f(x₁₂, x₂₃) f(x₃₄, x₄₅), "in Γ̂_{[0,5]}".
  - It uses x₄₅ = (σ₁σ₂)³ and x₅₁ = (σ₂σ₃)³.
  - NS Definition 1.1: IΓ is the set of (λ, f) ∈ GT̂ satisfying (III′) and (IV).
  - The note's statement and its g (= β_F⁻¹, unique by Lemma 7(a) and C_B̂(s) ∩ F̂ = 1) are exactly these.

  ρ₂ = −Ψ₂^{(0)}. Furusho writes "in B̂₅" where NS write Γ̂_{[0,5]}. The note follows NS, whose definition of IΓ is the
  one at stake.

**Step 2: closure of the discrete centralizer. Checked at the source.**
- Boggi–Zalesskii, arXiv:2406.08639 (Bull. LMS 57 (2025)), Theorem A, opened (`goodtorsion18b.tex`, lines 327-341).
  - Hypotheses: G of finite virtual p-cohomological type; G → R dense; H^i(R, M) → H^i(G, M) an isomorphism for discrete
    F_p[[R]]-modules M and i ≫ 0.
  - Conclusion (iii): C_R(H) is the closure of C_G(H) for every finite p-subgroup H of G.
- Here G = Γ_{0,[5]}, R = Γ̂_{0,[5]}, p = 2 and H = ⟨ε⟩. ε is not pure, so H ∩ Γ_{0,5} = 1.
  - The pure subgroup Γ_{0,5} = π₁(M_{0,5}) is torsion-free, of finite index and of finite type.
  - The groups are good, which gives the cohomology comparison.
  - So C_{Γ̂_{0,[5]}}(ε) = N̄. The note reaches this through its Lemma 9(b), whose deduction from BZ I checked on 23 Nov.
- N̄ ∩ Γ̂_{0,5} = closure of N₁, because Γ̂_{0,5} is open: an open subgroup of a closure is the closure of the
  intersection.
- L lies in that closure. τ₁τ₃ and τ₂² commute with ε, and L is pure, since the permutations of τ₁τ₃ and τ₂² commute.
  So L ∈ closure of N₁, and R ∈ closure of N₁ by Step 1.

**Step 3: injectivity. Checked.** Take γ ∈ N₁ with p(γ) = 1.
1. Its image in Γ_{0,[4]} is pure and lies in V, so it is 1.
2. So γ = Push(c), and Push is injective (Birman exact sequence; the 4-punctured sphere is hyperbolic).
3. ε fixes the pushed point ∞, so εγε⁻¹ = Push(ι_*c), and ι_*c = c.
4. Π ⋊ ⟨ι_*⟩ is the orbifold group of (P¹ ∖ {±a, ±b})/ι: two cone points of order 2 (at 0 and ∞, the base point) and
   two punctures. So it is C₂ ∗ C₂ ∗ Z.
5. In a free product, the centralizer of a nontrivial element of a finite factor lies in a conjugate of that factor
   (Kurosh). Π is torsion-free, so c = 1.

The image p(N₁) ⊇ ⟨x², y, xyx⁻¹⟩, the index-two subgroup of F. I recomputed the three images:
- p(τ₁²τ₃²) = u⁴ = x²;
- p(τ₂²) = v² = y;
- p(τ₁τ₃τ₂²τ₃⁻¹τ₁⁻¹) = u²v²u⁻² = xyx⁻¹.

So p(N₁) has finite index in B, N₁ carries the full topology, and the completed map is injective.
