## 1. Theorem E(b), the four points

**φ(F) ⊂ U.**
- U is the kernel of F → H₁(S; Z/p) ≅ (Z/p)^{m−1}. It is open and normal, and contains c^p for every c.
- Hence φ(c_j) = g_j (c_{τ(j)}^p)^ν g_j⁻¹ ∈ U. Here U is closed under the profinite power and under conjugation by
  any g_j ∈ F.
- In genus 0 the c_j generate F topologically, so φ(F) ⊂ U.
- In positive genus nothing places φ(a_i) and φ(b_i) in U. That is why the statement is for genus 0.

**Each boundary component of S_U has degree exactly p.**
- The class of c_k in H₁(S; Z/p) = (Z/p)^m/⟨(1,…,1)⟩ is nonzero when m ≥ 2. So it has order p.
- The covering is Galois with group (Z/p)^{m−1}. The components over the k-th boundary circle correspond to the cosets
  of ⟨[c_k]⟩: there are p^{m−2} of them, each of degree p. So r = m·p^{m−2} > m.
- Since U is normal, a·c_k^t·a⁻¹ ∈ U if and only if p | t. So the minimality hypothesis of Lemma 2.2 holds with e = p.
- The proof of Lemma 2.2 is right. The lift of c_k^t from the end of the lift of a first closes up after the degree of
  the component it runs along, and the closed loop is freely homotopic to that component traversed once.

**w = [b₁] + … + [b_m] ≠ 0 when r > m.**
- In Lemma 2.1 the kernel of Z^r → H₁(S′; Z) is Z·(1,…,1): H₂(S′, ∂S′) is generated by the fundamental class, whose
  boundary is the sum of the boundary classes.
- w is the image of a 0/1 vector with m ones and r − m ≥ 1 zeros, which is not constant. So w ≠ 0.
- The b_j are distinct because they lie over the distinct circles τ(j).

**ν·w = 0 forces ν = 0.**
- H₁(S_U; Z) is free, being the group of a compact surface with nonempty boundary, and U^ab = H₁(S_U; Z) ⊗ Ẑ.
- A coordinate n ≠ 0 of w gives νn = 0 in Ẑ = ∏ Z_p, and multiplication by n is injective there. Zero divisors of Ẑ
  play no role.

**The rest.**
- φ(c₁)⋯φ(c_m) = 1 gives Σ_j[φ(c_j)] = 0 in U^ab.
- [φ(c_j)] = ν[b_j], from v_j = a_j g_j⁻¹ ∈ U and ξ_j = a_j c_k^p a_j⁻¹ ∈ U₀.
- If p | μ then μ = 0. So a nonzero μ lies in no pẐ, i.e. it is a unit.

The author's `checks/check_fermat_cover.out` (m = 3, p ≤ 7) agrees with the count. For p = 3 the cover has 9 boundary
components, genus 1, and H₁ of rank 10.

## 2. Theorem A and Lemma 3.1

I recomputed Lemma 3.1 in the form of relation (II) that Boavida de Brito-Horel-Robertson use:
- The substitution (x,y) ↦ (x,z) sends z to w = xyx⁻¹ = z⁻¹yz.
- Relation (I) gives f(x,z) = C⁻¹, f(w,x) = xA⁻¹x⁻¹ and f(z,w) = z⁻¹B⁻¹z.
- Using x⁻¹z⁻¹ = y gives the note's equation (3): xA⁻¹y^{m+1}B⁻¹z^{m+1}C⁻¹x^m = 1.
- Multiplying the two solved forms gives A⁻¹y^λA = x^{−m−1}Cz^{−λ}C⁻¹x^{−m}.
- Hence φ(xy) = (x^mC)(xy)^λ(x^mC)⁻¹.
- m = (λ−1)/2 is defined because Ẑ has no 2-torsion. Nothing is inverted.

Theorem A then reads: φ(c_j) is a conjugate of c_j^λ for (c₁, c₂, c₃) = (x, y, z) with xyz = 1, so E(b) with m = 3
gives λ ∈ {0} ∪ Ẑ^×. Since λ ∈ 1 + 2Ẑ, λ ≠ 0. Then E(a) gives an automorphism.

Theorem E(a), re-read:

## 3. Proposition C, and Lochak-Schneps Question 1.1

**k = 1.** With either convention for composing permutations, [(1 2 3),(1 2 4)] = (1 2)(3 4). Conjugating (1 2 4) by this
involution gives (2 1 3) = (1 3 2) = q(x)⁻¹. Since (1 2 3) and (1 2 4) generate A₄ (no subgroup of order 6), the image
of φ_{1,f} in A₄ has order 3.

**k = 2.**
- Right-to-left: (1 2)(1 3) = (1 3 2), so [q(x),q(y)] = (1 2 3) =: t, and t(1 3)t⁻¹ = (1 2).
- Left-to-right: t = (1 3 2), and the conjugate is again (1 2).
- So the image in S₃ has order 2.

Relation (I) holds identically for both examples, since [y,x] = [x,y]⁻¹. The author's `checks/check_examples.out` gives
the same values.

**Lochak-Schneps, Question 1.1.** I read it on p. 5 of the copy in `MAP_GALOIS_ANABELIAN_sources`. It says: "Let (λ, f) ∈
Ẑ* × F̂₂′. Does x ↦ x^λ, y ↦ f⁻¹y^λf extend to an automorphism, or can it actually determine a non-invertible
endomorphism? One can ask the same question when (λ, f) satisfies (I), (II), (III)." It is followed by "Note that this
question pertains to the full profinite setting only." The introduction has "there is no effective way to ensure this".
The note's reading and its two quotations are correct. Proposition C answers the first half negatively, and Theorem A
answers the second half positively, already under (I) and (II).

## 4. Corollary D, with the sources opened

I opened the TeX of arXiv:1504.01605v2 (Horel) and arXiv:1703.05143v1 (Boavida de Brito-Horel-Robertson). The numbering
agrees with the note's bibliography.

**Horel:**
- Definition 7.1: pairs (λ, f) ∈ Ẑ × F̂₂ with (1), (2) with m = (λ−1)/2, and (3). He states that the map to End(F̂₂),
  x ↦ x^λ, y ↦ f⁻¹y^λf, "is a monoid map".
- Theorem 7.2 [Drinfeld]: the monoid "is the monoid of endomorphisms of PaB̂ inducing the identity on Ob".
- Corollary 7.3: the group is the group of units.
- Proposition 7.4: f maps to 0 in Ẑ².
- Lemma 7.5: the centralizer of x^λ, λ ≠ 0, is the closed subgroup generated by x.
- Corollary 5.11: the pure braid groups are good.
- Theorem 8.4: "There is an isomorphism of monoids" between the monoid and End_{Ho WOp pS}(Ê₂).

**Boavida de Brito-Horel-Robertson:**
- Definition 7.2: the monoid is "the monoid of endomorphisms σ of F̂₂ of the form σ(x) = x^λ, σ(y) = f⁻¹y^λf" with
  (I), (II), (III). They add that "the pair (λ, f) is uniquely determined by σ", because (III) puts f in the commutator
  subgroup, and that "the Grothendieck-Teichmüller group is the group of units".
- Proposition 7.3: the monoid is the monoid of endomorphisms of PaB̂ fixing the objects.
- Theorem 8.4: an isomorphism with π₀REnd(M̂); its proof calls it an isomorphism of monoids.
- Theorem 11.1: an isomorphism with π₀REnd(NM̂₊), induced by the action of the monoid.

The deduction is complete:
- The set P of pairs is closed, and by Theorem A it lies in Aut(F̂₂).
- The map P → Aut(F̂₂) is continuous: modulo N of index n, φ_{λ,f} depends on λ mod n! and f mod N.
- So the monoid is a compact, hence closed, submonoid of a profinite group, and by Lemma 5.1 a subgroup.
- Lemma 5.1 is right: γ^{n!} ∈ N for n ≥ [Γ:N], so γ⁻¹ is a limit of γ^{n!−1} ∈ M.
- (b) and (c) are the cited identifications, each an isomorphism of monoids.

Remark 5.2's use of Proposition 7.4 is accurate, since the closed commutator subgroup is the kernel of F̂₂ → Ẑ².
