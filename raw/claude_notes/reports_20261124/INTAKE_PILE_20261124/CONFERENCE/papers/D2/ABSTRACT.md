# Classifying spaces of finite monoids with bounded homology

**Abstract.** The classifying space BM of a monoid M is the geometric realization of the nerve of M, regarded as a
category with one object; its fundamental group is the universal group U(M), the group generated by the elements of M
with the multiplication table of M as relations. Let M be finite. We prove that if the integral homology of BM
vanishes in all large degrees, then U(M) is trivial; more precisely, if a prime p divides the order of U(M), then
H_k(BM; F_p) ≠ 0 for infinitely many k. It follows that three conditions on a finite monoid are equivalent: BM is
homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex; it is homotopy equivalent to a finite-dimensional one; its homology is
bounded. In that case BM is simply connected. Together with a theorem of Ben-Bassat, that every finite simply
connected CW complex is homotopy equivalent to BM for a finite monoid M, this determines these homotopy types: they
are the finite simply connected complexes. A conjecture of the same paper, that every connected finite CW complex
with finite fundamental group is of the form BM with M finite, is therefore false; the real projective plane is a
counterexample. The proof compares the K-theory of BM with that of the classifying space of a finite group contained
in M, through Atiyah's completion theorem. Two examples are worked out: a monoid with six elements whose classifying
space is simply connected and has homology in every degree from 2 on (it is a wedge of spheres), and a monoid with 23
elements whose classifying space is the complex projective plane.
