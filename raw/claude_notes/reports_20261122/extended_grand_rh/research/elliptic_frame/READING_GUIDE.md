# The 269a1 elliptic realization

Read elliptic_frame.tex, Theorems 1–3. The general cubic resolvent and quaternion quotient are classical. The note specializes them to the curve with coefficients \([0,0,1,-2,-1]\), computes its explicit unordered-pair field and complementary quadratic character, and derives the finite-field trace tests.

The modest test at \(p=43\) distinguishes two operations that are easy to conflate. There are no quartic roots in the prime field, all four appear over its quadratic extension, and the cubic character takes the value \(2\). Only the identity 3-torsion point is rational over that quadratic extension. All nine points are rational over the quartic extension. A single projective torsion line permits Frobenius to act on its generators by opposite signs.

The primary sources are John Cremona's Table 1 (269A1), Fukuda–Yoshikawa, Sections 3.1 and 4.1, and Bisatt, Lemmas 6.3.2–3. The all-prime Artin identity compares the permutation on unordered pairs with that on single torsion lines. The weight-two elliptic Hasse–Weil function does not enter this identity. At the primes of residue characteristic 3 and 269, use inertia invariants. The displayed finite-field tests assume \(p\nmid3\cdot269\).
