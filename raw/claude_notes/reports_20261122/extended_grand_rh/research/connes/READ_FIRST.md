# Reading order for the Connes and cubic workshop

**The two open estimates concern different objects.** The zeta spectral triple asks whether the actual lowest vector selects the desired entire function as its window grows. The cubic Hecke question asks how well one explicitly decidable set of primes follows its main term.

1. In `spectral_prolate_leakage_interpolation.tex` read AC-S47, especially (LI.3) and (LI.4). The tail interpolation estimate is unconditional. The Rayleigh bound for the actual trial uses the supplied rational RH input \(A\).
2. In `spectral_real_zero_selection.tex` read AC-S48, equations (RZ.3)–(RZ.5) and steps 1–3. Under \(A\), it would suffice that the Rayleigh quotient of the normalized even trial divided by the actual **second** eigenvalue tends to zero. This gives local uniform convergence of the scalar-normalized Fourier transforms to \(\Xi\) throughout the complex plane and convergence of the zero multisets on domains with zero-free boundary. The bound on the second eigenvalue is open. Convergence of the zero multisets alone leaves individual multiplicities undetermined. AC-S49 addresses the stronger premise on this particular trial; see the checkpoint 23–24 addendum below. The extra weighted hypotheses in S47 determine stronger convergence norms.
3. The original 2025 Connes–Consani–Moscovici source is `CCM_zeta_spectral_triples/mc2arXiv.tex`. Read Lemma 5.2, Definition 5.3, Lemma 5.4 and Theorem 5.10. Its four figure inputs are included. The real-zero compactness step uses the result of Clunie and Kuijlaars that convergence in measure of the polynomials on a positive-measure real set to a limit nonzero on a positive-measure subset forces local uniform convergence throughout the plane, under the theorem's restriction on their zeros. See the original Corollary 1.3, printed p. 110. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1006/jath.1994.1116) · [University repository scan](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/2851818/178_2351y.pdf)
4. In the existing checkpoint 18 text, `modules/trace_rational_assembly.tex` (AC-T46) and `modules/trace_unconditional_converse.tex` (AC-T50, T51) give the fixed-conductor scope of the cutoff trace. The tests on \(C_{\mathbb Q}\) see finite sets of primitive Dirichlet characters and fixed unitary norm twists. Passing to a different base field requires its own trace and positivity statement. In `modules/kummer_lambda.tex`, lines 11–20 give the two exact zeta factorizations, and lines 542–561 specify the one-sided arithmetic-window premise. In `modules/bcm_neutrality.tex`, AC-B05 gives the Erdős–Straus norm-pair identity and the three-point negative-eigenvalue example. AC-B06 gives the residual Kummer factors after cyclotomic base change to \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\).
5. The short editable note `cubic_prime_test.tex` specializes classical Artin formalism and Chebotarev to \(X^3-269\). One exact relative prime-density question suffices to test this Hecke function. Its local rank-two character is orthogonal in the prime mean square to every fixed rational congruence observable, including finite sums with fixed norm twists. A direct implementation of the finite examples and the six-column prime-power table is `check_frobenius.py`.

## 22 November 2026 · Checkpoints 23 and 24

AC-S49 strengthens the consequence of AC-S48's original ratio. Under A, if the quotient of this particular even prolate trial by the second eigenvalue tends to zero, every nontrivial zeta zero is simple. At a hypothetical multiple zero there is an unbounded subsequence on which the ratio stays bounded below. The ratio remains unproved. AC-S50's separate unshifted construction uses the weight of a fixed probe to recover each full multiplicity by eventual rounding. AC-S51 concerns the actual bottom even vector. A strict parity gap at one window makes that window's lowest eigenvalue simple. An eventual uniform relative parity gap gives the distinct-ordinate product; a separate simplicity hypothesis identifies it with normalized Xi.
