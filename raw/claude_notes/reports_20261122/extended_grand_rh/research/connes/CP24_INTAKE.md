# What Connes checkpoints 23 and 24 change

**The original prolate trial now connects directly to zeta simplicity.** Under the supplied RH input \(A\), checkpoint 23's AC-S49 states that proving \(q_L(v_\lambda)/a_2(L)\to0\) for this particular trial would imply that every nontrivial zeta zero is simple. Checkpoint 24 gives two different reconstructions of the zeros. The distinction matters for prompts 62 and 68.

| Result | Hypotheses | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| CP23, AC-S49 | \(A\) and the exact original trial ratio tends to zero | Every nontrivial zeta zero is simple. At a hypothetical multiple zero the ratio stays bounded below along an unbounded sequence. |
| CP24, AC-S50 | \(A\) | Computable finite arithmetic polynomials converge locally uniformly to \(\Xi(z)/\Xi(0)\), with each fixed zero's full multiplicity eventually recovered. The construction uses an unshifted Weil space, a fixed probe and finitely many prime powers in each matrix. No effective convergence rate is supplied. |
| CP24, AC-S51 | \(A\) and \(a_e(L)<a_o(L)\) at one window | The actual lowest eigenvalue of the window is simple and even, and its eigenvector has nonzero integral. For convergence as \(L\to\infty\), additionally require \(1-a_e(L)/a_o(L)\ge\kappa>0\) eventually. |
| CP24, AC-S51 with the uniform gap | As above | The normalized transform of the actual lowest eigenvector tends to the entire product with **one zero per distinct ordinate**. Additional simplicity identifies this product with \(\Xi/\Xi(0)\). |

**Source and check scope.** The integrated TeX modules are `spectral_prolate_ratio_multiplicity.tex`, lines 30–51, 411–460, 485–509; `spectral_arithmetic_multiplicity_reconstruction.tex`, lines 78–134, 187–203, 279–326, 390–459; and `spectral_parity_gap_edge_comparison.tex`, lines 11–25, 69–137, 170–197, 449–476. For S49 I checked the subsequence selection **given the source's oscillatory series and remainder bound**, and the min–max contradiction given its two-trial estimate. Its Bessel matching, exterior jump estimates and upstream fixed-index prolate asymptotics were not independently rederived here.

For S50 I checked how one spectral coordinate can encode a zero of multiplicity \(m\). Its norm carries the weight \(m\), so the squared projection of the fixed probe onto its unit coordinate is \(m|\widehat\phi(\gamma)|^2\). Norm convergence of the compact compressions yields convergence of the corresponding isolated spectral projections for each fixed ordinate. The positive transform allows division and eventual integer rounding. The logarithmic cap and min–max bound control the product tails. S51's fixed-window simplicity follows by taking the compact primitive of a hypothetical mean-zero even bottom vector. It is odd with the same Rayleigh quotient, contrary to the strict odd gap. These targeted checks leave the actual large-window gap open.

**An explicit improvement to the probe.** The autocorrelation of \(e^{-x}{\bf1}_{[0,1]}\) has a strictly positive Fourier transform bounded below by \((1-e^{-1})^2/(1+t^2)\). This gives an explicit conditioning bound on the multiplicity division in S50. It supplies a useful error budget for finite computations while leaving spectral separation to the underlying operator. The derivation is in `S50_PROBE.md`.

**The earlier analogies.** Suzuki's number-field Theorem 1.2 gives the distinct-ordinate product for a distinguished unshifted local differentiation operator under RH. In his separate curve paper, sampling reaches the endpoint at a finite window and the diagonal reproducing kernel there is \(1/m_\gamma\). Its central-zero case requires a different phase and removal of the zero eigenspace before inversion. Translation through \(\log q\) has a geometric Frobenius interpretation for the curve. The analogous geometric identification over the integers remains missing. CP24's new spectral modules do not identify the supported additive zero of a semiring with a zeta zero or prove an Erdős–Straus occupancy statement. [Suzuki on the number-field spectrum](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32704v1#S1) · [Suzuki on curves and multiplicities](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41115v1#S10)

**The cubic chart advances in four families.** G119–G123 construct and transport an auxiliary seed despite the exclusion of the former original endpoint. G124 has four quadratic local models. The conductors are 4 and 20 in residue \(e\equiv267\bmod268\), and 1076 and 5380 in residue \(e\equiv133\bmod268\), retaining the original modularity and coefficient hypotheses. G125/G126 give zero strict Selmer length on these four rows. The cotangent lengths may be larger. On \(G_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})}\), \(\chi_{12}=\chi_{-4}\) and \(\chi_{60}=\chi_{-20}\) as restricted characters, recovering the workshop signs without adding independent families. A second reader independently reproduced the G118 Bernoulli residue \(188\bmod269\) after division by 269, and the G126 principal-unit coefficients \(75\sqrt{15}\) and \(20\). The stored PARI certificates exclude 269 from the required class numbers; the reader did not rerun PARI. See `cubic_prime_character_strict_vanishing.tex`, `cubic_psi20_strict_vanishing.tex`, lines 228–355, 360 onward, and `cubic_quadratic_family_transfer.tex`. The complementary all-prime sum, H04.4 and cubic Kummer RH remain open. The retained ES norm-pair formula and limit of the phase-positivity argument are still AC-B05.
