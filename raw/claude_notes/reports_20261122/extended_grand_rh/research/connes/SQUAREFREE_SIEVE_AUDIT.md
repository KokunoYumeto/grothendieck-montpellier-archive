# Check of the squarefree sieve on Eisenstein ideals

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\omega)\), where \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\). Let \(\psi\) be the cubic ray character of conductor \((269)\) attached to \(K(\sqrt[3]{269})/K\). Fix \(3\mid M\), omit the rational primes dividing \(3\cdot269M\), and let \(z\ge2\).

**The input sum really is centered on each admissible norm class.** Every integral nonzero ideal of \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\) has six generators. The ray character on a generator is invariant under all six units. In the residue ring modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(269,M)\), choose a unit \(v\) with nontrivial cubic character. This is possible by lifting a residue unit at 269 and prescribing unit components elsewhere. The residue class \(u=v/\overline v\) has norm one and \(\psi(u)=\psi(v)^2\ne1\). Multiplication by \(u\) preserves the exact norm class and the excluded-prime conditions. It forces the mean of the character on every residue fibre to be zero, even if \(269\mid M\). A fundamental domain for the fixed residue lattice has bounded diameter. Translating it over the norm disc leaves \(O_M(1+\sqrt T)\) boundary tiles. Division by six gives \(O_M(\sqrt T)\) for the complete sum up to ideal norm \(T\ge1\), uniformly over invertible classes. Partial summation adds at most a logarithm for the weight \(\log n\).

**A second derivation of the local sieve.** At a split rational prime \(p>z\) write \(u,v\) for formal prime-ideal variables. Permitting only the norm exponents zero and one gives \(1+u+v\). Dividing by the local all-ideal series gives
\[
 (1+u+v)(1-u)(1-v)=1-u^2-uv-v^2+u^2v+uv^2 .
\]
Put \(u=\psi(\mathfrak p)T\), \(v=\overline{\psi(\mathfrak p)}T\). Then \(uv=T^2\), \(u+v=t(p)T\). At an inert prime the ideal has norm \(p^2\), hence no nontrivial permitted term, giving correction \(1-T^2\). Combining the two gives \(1+(\chi_{-3}(p)-t(p)^2)T^2+t(p)\chi_{-3}(p)T^3\). At \(p\le z\) the desired factor is one, so the correction is \(1-t(p)T+\chi_{-3}(p)T^2\). At omitted primes either retain the corresponding finite inverse Euler polynomial or use imprimitive local factors from the outset.

For the correcting coefficients \(h_z(d)\), absolute summation over \(d\le X\) with weight \(d^{-1/2}\) costs at most a fixed power of \(\log(2X)\) from large primes, because their first possible power is \(p^2\). The small primes contribute \(\exp(O(\sqrt z/\log(2z)))\), using Chebyshev's bound and partial summation. For \(d\) prime to \(M\), the residual ideal norm lies in the class \(d^{-1}\bmod M\). If \(d\) is noninvertible the original norm-one progression contains no such term. Thus the total log-weighted sum of the squarefree \(z\)-rough signed coefficients on this progression is \(O_{M,\epsilon}(X^{1/2+\epsilon})\) at \(z=(\log(2X))^2\).

**The prime contribution has the correct sign.** Squarefree products of inert primes contribute zero. Summing the two choices of prime ideal over each split prime gives coefficient \(\prod_{p\mid n}t(p)\). The prime contribution is precisely the rational first-prime race above \(z\); the primes below \(z\) cost \(O_M(z)\). The composite contribution \(C\) has weight \(\log n\), and \(R=-C+O_{M,\epsilon}(X^{1/2+\epsilon})\) on the stated cumulative convention. In particular a lower bound on \(R\) becomes an upper bound on \(C\). If the cutoff varies with the dyadic block, use each block's own cutoff when recombining. The \(X^{1/2+o(1)}\) loss respects estimates with arbitrary positive exponent slack and the corresponding sparse-window criterion; it is insufficient to transfer an exact \(O(\sqrt X)\) bound or an exact logarithmic mean.

A separate program summed the cubic character on Eisenstein generators to obtain the Hecke coefficients. It compared the local convolution against direct enumeration of all admissible squarefree products through 30,000 at \(z=11\), for every coefficient with norm prime to 807. The total and the prime-plus-composite decomposition matched on moduli 3, 12, 807 and 217083. For \(M=12\) the signed prime and composite sums were about \(-78.383\) and \(38.179\) respectively. These are finite checks of the identities. They provide no bound on the signed composite fluctuation at growing heights.
