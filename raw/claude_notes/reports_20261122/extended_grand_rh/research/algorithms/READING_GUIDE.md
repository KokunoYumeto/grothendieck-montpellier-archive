## Exact scopes for prompt 67

1. Tang, Hexagon 2611.00135v2, Theorem 1.1 already constructs a presentation of every promised prime-power order in uniform deterministic polynomial bit time, on its cited external results. Its Lemma 2.1 provides actual auxiliary primes satisfying the stronger \(1\bmod12r\) condition for \(n\ge2,\ p>\max(n,3),\ r\le n\). The algorithm has an empty-table branch. The public v3 carries the same theorem and analytic input as the supplied v2. Tang distinguishes a separate revised proof from the authors' official version of the Cureton–Thorner zero-free result. The individual Dirichlet-only derivation of small primes remains an analytic question. [Tang v2](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00135v2)
2. For fixed \(r\), changing which prime varies gives square-root-sized exceptional sets among field characteristics in \((X,2X]\). A common table of \(O_r(\log X)\) tests has entries \(O_r(\log X\log\log X)\) and misses \(O_r(\sqrt X\log\log X)\) characteristics. Allowing entries up to \((\log X)^{1+\epsilon}\) leaves \(O_\epsilon(\sqrt X)\) exceptions for all sufficiently large \(X\) depending on \(r,\epsilon\). Each test prime can satisfy \(1\bmod12r\). Constructing the table takes polynomial time in the input bit length. Character expansions estimate its exception count. The uniform estimate permits every prime index up to \((\log X)^B\) simultaneously with \(O_B(\sqrt X(\log X)^B\log\log X)\) exceptional characteristics. OpenAI's algebraic driver transfers this to dense prime-field factorizations in that degree range. The exception bound is absolute. It applies also after restriction to the higher-valuation branch. [Prime-field algebraic driver](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/Deterministic-Polynomial-Factorization-over-Prime-Fields-October-4-2026)
3. The existing GRH_NIGHT checkpoint 07 gives the affine permutation-character identity in CX-ALG-024 (consolidated TeX lines 14723–14826) and identifies the single Hecke function in NT-L17 (lines 2992–3019). Write \(A_D\) for ordinary Dirichlet GRH. Then \(A_D+\operatorname{RH}(\zeta_{\mathbb Q(p^{1/r})})\) controls the cyclotomic zeta function and one order-\(r\) ray-class character of \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_r)\). In the higher-valuation case the conductor is exactly \(p\mathcal O_K\). A direct specialization of Wesolowski's explicit theorem saves a factor \(r^2\) in the sufficient bound compared with the checkpoint's use of the full normal-closure discriminant. The additional Hecke hypothesis is sufficient. Necessity for the search problem is unproved. [Wesolowski, Theorem 1.1 and Section 3](https://msp.org/obs/2019/2-1/obs-v2-n1-p28-s.pdf) · [Holmes, Lemma 3.1(ii)](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.1311)
4. For fixed odd \(r\) and fixed \(p\ne r\), every rational coprime residue class compatible with \(\ell\equiv1\bmod r\), after imposing that condition, contains split and nonsplit Kummer outcomes in relative proportions \(1/r\) and \((r-1)/r\). This unconditional Chebotarev calculation concerns finite rational congruence descriptions. Other analytic methods remain possible. In the cubic example \(p=17\), the least witness is \(7\), while \(37\) and \(73\) give opposite outcomes in the class \(1\bmod36\). The least-norm reformulation gives a concrete individual question for \(p\equiv-1\bmod9\). A uniform \(O((\log p)^C)\) bound from \(A_D\) on its least detecting Eisenstein norm would settle the original auxiliary search in that subclass.
5. OpenAI's *Primitive roots for every admissible integer base*, Theorem 1.1 in sources/OpenAI_primitive_roots/sections/01-introduction.tex, claims an unconditional lower bound \(c_a x/(\log x)^2\) in \((x,2x)\) for all sufficiently large \(x\) for each fixed admissible integer \(a\). Theorem 1.2 gives a uniform strip \(\Re s>1-10^{-6}\) for finite-order Hecke characters over cyclotomic fields containing \(\mu_{12}\), including the principal character. Hooley's positive-density asymptotic uses stronger Kummer zero hypotheses. The fixed-base infinitude bound gives neither that asymptotic nor uniform least-prime control as \(a,r\) vary. This intake checked the preprint's stated theorems and their quantifiers. Its analytic and sieve machinery are used as cited. [Versioned author source](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/Primitive-roots-for-every-admissible-integer-base-October-4-2026)
