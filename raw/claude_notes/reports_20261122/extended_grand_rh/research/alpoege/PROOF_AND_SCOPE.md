## The mechanism

Alpöge’s note gives the origin of the arithmetic construction. Stepanov’s function-field argument gets high-order vanishing from a divisor. The number-field argument builds divisibility through a chain of theta lifts. Page 4, steps 8–16, describes definite unitary groups, a seesaw identifying a period, lattice indices, a divisibility–size comparison and averaging across characters. The analytic scale is explicit in `SizeM`. Writing $x$ for scale and $\theta=\epsilon/100$, the allowed lift count satisfies $x^{-\theta}\sqrt{x\delta_1}\le m\le x^\theta\sqrt{x\delta_1}$. The paper’s informal $m\approx\sqrt x$ suppresses the scale loss. [Author’s note, pp. 1 and 3–4](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Size hypothesis, lines 11 and 27–29](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/ElevenLe.lean#L24-L33)

The elementary comparison inside the construction is that a nonzero integral determinant with specified lower bounds on its prime valuations has logarithmic size at least the weighted sum of those lower bounds. The work lies in producing the determinant and estimating its height and its divisibility sharply enough. The abstract analytic endpoint `landau_window` assumes explicit pole data and a one-sided window estimate and bounds the real parts of all the poles. In the published zeta-specialized working exposition, long intervals [(Y,Y^{3/2})] at arbitrarily large (Y) suffice for the appropriate smoothed prime-power sum. The two error terms of scales (x/m) and (m) explain the square-root choice. That exposition takes its main arithmetic inequality from the Lean proof. [Determinant comparison, lines 153–160](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Formalism/ProductFormula/StatementOCCM.lean#L153-L160) · [Window theorem, line 83](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/DedekindLFunctions/LandauTheorems/landauWindow.lean#L83) · [Working exposition, Part I](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/zeta-paper.pdf)

The finite-field paper writes out Alpöge’s **separate** footnote 3 argument. The compactly supported cohomology of a hyperplane section of a product and that of the product cancel in the trace formula outside a controlled pair of terms. After one overall sign, the tensor power and a kernel enter with the same sign. Their sum is consequently an actual Frobenius representation. A path of nondegenerate bilinear couplings gives the remainder a bound by Fourier orthogonality. Taking power sums on all finite extensions detects the largest eigenvalue modulus, while increasing the tensor power removes the fixed excess in the exponent. ## A finite-field test of what the sign controls

Let $f=X^3+X+1$, $h=X^3+X^2+1$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Both cubics give 1 at each of 0 and 1, so both are irreducible. Each zero scheme is isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_8$. In degree $r$ each has three roots if $3\mid r$, and zero roots otherwise. Hence **all** individual Frobenius traces, eigenvalues, tensor-power traces and zeta functions agree. The common-root counts for the pairs $(f,f)$ and $(f,h)$ differ. The first intersection has degree three and the second is empty. Algebraically their coordinate rings as intersections in the affine line are $\mathbb F_2[X]/(f)$ and the zero ring, respectively. This remains true even though their respective pairs of abstract zero schemes are isomorphic.

| Polynomials | Individual Frobenius cycle lengths | Length of the intersection over the affine line |
|---|---|---:|
| $f,f$ | $3$ and $3$ | $3$ |
| $f,h$ | $3$ and $3$ | $0$ |

This comparison pinpoints the data a sign-based common-factor algorithm would need. It must retain the common embedding or an equivalent joint incidence operation. It gives no complexity lower bound for algorithms with both polynomials as inputs.
