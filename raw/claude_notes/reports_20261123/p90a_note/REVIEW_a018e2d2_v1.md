## Is the result useful, and why

**Yes.** In his 1978 counterexample to Kodaira vanishing, Raynaud printed a question (Remarque 3, p. 278): can Kodaira
vanishing fail for an ample line bundle that is generated by its sections, or even very ample? He asked it on his own
surface, in characteristic 2, whose line bundle is generated only outside a curve.
- Lauritzen answered the very ample question in dimension at least 6, on homogeneous spaces, and Lauritzen–Rao made
  his example elementary.
- Lauritzen–Rao say plainly (p. 2) that examples of smaller dimension are wanted. They also say that their failure
  need not survive passage to a hyperplane section, so their examples cannot be cut down to a surface.
- Surfaces are the smallest dimension in which the question has content.

The result answers both halves of Raynaud's question, in his own setting:
- on Raynaud's characteristic-2 surface itself, a globally generated ample Q with h¹(Q⁻¹)=1;
- on a cover of that surface of degree prime to 2, a very ample L with h¹(L⁻¹)=1.

No one has posted or published this, as far as the pass below shows. The note is **clear**: no neighbouring work on
Hexagon, and nothing recent elsewhere that overlaps it. Bernasconi–Valloni (May 2026) is the nearest recent work. They
prove that A^{⊗p} is globally generated and separates points on certain Raynaud surfaces with p>2. They prove neither
very ampleness nor nonvanishing for A^{⊗p}, and they do not mention Raynaud's question. The note credits them in one
sentence.

## Comments on the draft material, most important first

1. **The run's text hides the idea.** The very ample bundle comes from a general principle that the run proves only
   in its special case: if Q is globally generated and Q^m is very ample with p∤m, then taking m-th roots of a general
   basis of H⁰(Q^m) produces a very ample L whose H^i(L⁻¹) contains H^i(Q⁻¹) as a direct summand. So Raynaud's
   very-ample question reduces to his globally-generated question, in every dimension and characteristic.

   The note states this as Theorem 2 and proves it once. The surface then needs only:
   - global generation of Q (the new twist 2T in place of Raynaud's T);
   - very ampleness of Q⁹;
   - the cohomology.

   Without the separation, a reader sees 556 roots and does not see why. (Mukai, p. 2, already observes that the
   failure survives separable covers of degree prime to p, because L⁻¹ is a direct summand. The note credits that
   observation.)
2. **A strengthening, reported here and proved in the note.** The run's statement uses the ground field
   k = an algebraic closure of 𝔽₂(λ_ij), so that a "generic" basis exists. That detour is not needed. Smoothness of
   the divisors, pairwise transversality and the absence of triple points are finitely many nonempty open conditions
   on a tuple of sections, by Bertini over an algebraically closed field. Every nonempty open subset of an affine space
   over an infinite field has rational points.

   So the example exists over every algebraically closed field of characteristic 2, 𝔽̄₂ included, and hence it is
   defined over some finite field. This answers part of the run's own "further questions" (an example over an explicit
   finite field), but without an explicit field of definition. The statement changed from "there exist k, Y, L" with k
   transcendental to "for every algebraically closed k of characteristic 2". The proof of the change is in §3 of the
   note; it is recorded as a check event in RECORDS.md.
3. **The very-ampleness of Q⁹ was sketched; the note proves it fully.** The run's step "near each point choose a
   nonvanishing section of BM … ratios generate" is correct, but it is local while the claim is on charts. The note
   argues on the chart D(b): there ψ_*O_S is generated as an O(D(b))-algebra by the sections of M, and these are
   generated by the ratios σ/b. No statement changes.
4. **Credit.** Raynaud's surface, curve, ruled surface and triple cover are his. Remark 3 fixes h=8 and N=O_C(2∞), as
   the run uses them. The twist 2T, the global generation, the ninth power, and the roots of a basis are what is added.
   The note's title names Raynaud's question. Other credit:
   - the roots-of-a-basis cover is the one Bhatt uses for global generation (Prop. 7.2, p. 1771);
   - the Kummer covers of Bloch–Gieseker type are standard; I cite them from memory and say so in RECORDS.
5. **Context that the expert will want.** Kodaira vanishing for nef and big bundles holds on surfaces that are neither
   of general type nor quasi-elliptic (Mukai, Thm 3; Ekedahl, p. 99). Raynaud's characteristic-2 surface is
   quasi-elliptic (Raynaud, Remarque 1). For minimal surfaces of general type the pluricanonical bundles satisfy
   vanishing, with one characteristic-2 exception (Ekedahl, Main theorem (i)). So a very ample counterexample cannot be
   pluricanonical. One sentence each.
6. **Who is not served by the run's text.**
   - The newcomer: there is no statement of Raynaud's question in his words, and no account of why surfaces are the
     smallest case.
   - The expert: there is no comparison with Mukai and Ekedahl.
   - Raynaud's estate and Lauritzen: Lauritzen's earlier answer in higher dimension must be named in Section 1.
7. **What remains open, stated exactly:**
   - odd characteristic (for p odd it is not checked whether a globally generated twist exists on Raynaud's surfaces);
   - the minimal irregularity and degree;
   - an explicit finite field of definition and explicit equations.
