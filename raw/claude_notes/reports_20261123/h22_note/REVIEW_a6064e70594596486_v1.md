## The two questions

1. **Is this what we want to say?** The theorems: yes, and they hold (one step of one proof needs a repair, comment
   3). What we want to say *about* them is missing: that Z_0 is the initial inverse semiring, so that the note
   computes the base of Faul, Goswami, Joubert and Manuell's theory, and that for R = Z the theorems are statements
   about all commutative inverse monoids.
2. **Whom does it not serve as it stands?** The four authors, whose paper contains more of this note than the note
   says and whose notation it does not use; the newcomer, who gets three theorems before a definition of a semimodule
   or one example; the expert in semirings, who is given our words ("support diagram", G(R), τ, e) for objects that
   have standard names; and Connes, Consani and Lorscheid, who are named in one sentence that says nothing checkable.

## Comments, most important first

### 1. The credit to Faul, Goswami, Joubert and Manuell is too thin, and partly wrong by omission

The note credits Definition 3.6 and Proposition 6.1. Their paper has, on the same object:

- **The functor.** Definition 3.6 defines (−)_0 as a functor on inverse semirings, f ↦ f_0. Functoriality is theirs;
  ours is that it is full and faithful on commutative rings (Proposition 5.2 of the note).
- **Z_0 is the initial inverse semiring** (Proposition 5.1), so a commutative inverse semiring is a commutative
  Z_0-algebra, and the free one on n generators is Z_0[x_1, …, x_n] (Proposition 5.2). The note never says this. It
  is the reason the note matters: Spec Z_0 is the base over which every commutative inverse semiring lives, and
  Theorem 1 computes it.
- **The two reflections** (Proposition 4.13): the ring reflection (0_1 ~ 0) and the idempotent reflection E(R)
  (0_1 ~ 1); for R_0 they are R and {0, 0̂} ≅ B, and Remark 5.5 names the maps Z_0 → Z and Z_0 → 2. The note's maps
  p and χ are these.
- **The embedding into ring × idempotent semiring** (Theorem 7.2, for E-unitary inverse semirings). The note's
  "normal form" G(R) → R × B is this theorem for R_0, and is presented as ours.
- **The ring of scalars** G(0_1) and the groups G(z) as modules over it (Propositions 4.7, 4.8): the fibre structure
  of Theorem 2 for the semiring itself.
- **Subtractive = kernel = downward closed** (Theorem 6.5, Proposition 6.6, with Golan, Ch. 14 and 15.18).
- **An announcement** (last paragraph): a paper of Faul and Manuell, "Clifford semigroups, inverse semirings and the
  Grothendieck construction", shows that inverse semirings are lax monoidal functors from their idempotents to
  abelian groups. Theorem 2 is the statement of that kind for modules over R_0.

**A gate before posting:** find out whether that paper of Faul and Manuell has appeared, and read it. If it describes
modules as functors on the semilattice of idempotents, Theorem 2 is a case of it and must be presented so.

Also: the note writes G(R), τ and e. Their notation is R_0, 0 for the new zero and 0̂ for the old one, with
0_x = x + (−x); and G(z) is their name for the group of an idempotent z. A paper that continues theirs uses theirs.

### 2. The paper never says why anyone should care, at its true scope

"Adds a characteristic-one point to every affine scheme" is a picture. The facts are:

- Z_0-semimodules are exactly the commutative inverse monoids (from their Propositions 5.1 and 6.1 and Example 3.3; a
  five-line proof). So Theorem 2 for R = Z is Clifford's theorem, Theorem 2 in general is Clifford's theorem with
  coefficients in R, and Theorem 3 for R = Z classifies the finitely generated projective commutative inverse
  monoids. That is a statement semigroup theorists can use, and the note does not make it.
- For R = 0 the classification is the finite case of Horn and Kimura's theorem on projective semilattices; for one
  idempotent it is the definition of a projective module. Theorem 3 interpolates between the two.
- The classification is complete: J and the Q_j are determined by M. The note proves this without saying it.

### 3. One step of the proof of Theorem 3 fails as written

"If" part: "for fibres R^{n_j} the same map on G(R)^{J′}, J′ = ⊔_j {j} × [n_j] ordered through J, is a retraction
onto P(J, (R^{n_j}))". With n_j = 0 for some j (the natural choice when Q_j = 0) the set J′ has no element over j,
its down-sets are the order ideals of a smaller poset, and the image is not P(J, ·). Also "ordered through J" is a
preorder, not an order. **Repair:** choose every n_j ≥ 1 (add a free summand to Q′_j) and take the down-sets of the
preorder. The statement is unaffected, and it does include Q_j = 0: the smallest case is B itself, P(point, 0), a
retract of R_0 by 1 ↦ the top idempotent. Checked; recorded as a check event.

### 4. The opening does not follow the standard

- The abstract's first sentence is right in kind. Its last three sentences are our drafting history; the standard
  asks for one sentence on who produced and who checked.
- §1 states the theorems before "semimodule", "projective" and "support diagram" (with its morphisms) are defined,
  and without one example. The smallest ones cost five lines: (F_2)_0 with its tables; R_0 over itself; the chain
  a < b, which gives the first projective that is neither free nor an R-module.
- "Credit" mixes their work with "this project's first papers" and "later notes". Our history belongs in the note on
  method only. The opening of Section 5 has the same fault.
- "The first classification we know of" needs the search behind it stated (it was a web search on 21 Nov, not
  exhaustive, and the semigroup literature was not searched).
- "Proof overview" is a subsection; the standard asks for a section. It should also say where each hypothesis is used.

### 5. Standard names

A support diagram is a strong semilattice of R-modules (Clifford's term, as in Howie). The pair of Proposition 5.1
is "zerosumfree and entire" in Golan's vocabulary; "subtractive ideal" is their Definition 6.3. "The same adjunction
appears in Golan's book" was not checked by anyone and should go. That every ideal of R_0 is subtractive has a
consequence worth one sentence: the spectrum of Theorem 1 is the same whether one takes all prime ideals or only
the subtractive ones, which are the kernels.

### 6. Connes and Consani, and Lorscheid

"Other frameworks for adjoining semiring points to schemes; we do not compare" serves neither. What can be said
exactly: R_0 is not of characteristic one, its localization at the new point is B, and the fibre of a semimodule at
that point (its semilattice of idempotents, B ⊗ M) is a B-module, an object of Connes and Consani's homological
algebra in characteristic one. Nothing about the arithmetic site or the scaling site follows, and the last section
should ask the question in that form. For Lorscheid the sentence in comment 5 on subtractive ideals is the relevant
one (his ideals of a blueprint are, as I remember, the subtractive ones; not reopened).

### 7. Small points

- Theorem 1: for J = R the second quotient is (0)_0 = B, the support map; R = 0 is excluded two lines earlier.
- Theorem 1 can say what the topology is: the open sets are ∅ and {η} ∪ U, U open in Spec R.
- The sentence on ζ after the proof of Theorem 1 is true; it should say what is counted (the finite quotient
  semirings of Z_0 by their number of elements).
- The gluing (R × S)_0 = R_0 ×_B S_0 appears only in the note on method, as a computation; it is one line and
  belongs after Theorem 1.
- The units of R_0 are the units of R; {0, 0̂} is B with its own unit 0̂, which is not the unit of R_0.
- The abstract says "GPT-5-series Pro models … in its later notes": "its" has no antecedent.
