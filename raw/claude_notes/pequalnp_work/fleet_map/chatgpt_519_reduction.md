# Why adjacent degree-three vertices retain the full lower-bound problem

[Shared proof map](FLEET_MAP.md) · [Local mathematical evidence](<local>/Documents/Codex/2026-11-22/the-two-tracks-share-no-sound/work/fleet_proof_map/)

Let \(B(G)\) be minimum fan-in-two AND/OR/NOT circuit size for the single Boolean output \(\bigvee_{ij\in E(G)}x_ix_j\), with every gate charged. The live 519 desk question asks whether \(B(G)\ge m+\tau(G)-1\) on all subcubic simple graphs of girth at least seven that have an edge. The assignment's shard 2 singles out graphs with two adjacent degree-three vertices. [Posted statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-519.ed26ac472458.json)

**Reduction.** This shard implies the general statement, even if the two vertices must lie consecutively on two seven-cycles. The reduction preserves connectedness when the input is connected and preserves a violation gate for gate. In making the stronger seven-cycle statement, the connected-input construction below needs a vertex of degree at most two. The unrestricted formal statement allows disconnected graphs, including cubic inputs.

Let \(K\) have two vertices \(a,b\), the edge \(ab\), and two otherwise disjoint six-edge paths \(a,p_1,\ldots,p_5,b\) and \(a,q_1,\ldots,q_5,b\). It has 12 vertices and 13 edges. Its simple cycles have lengths 7, 7 and 12. The set \(U=\{a,b,p_2,p_4,q_2,q_4\}\) covers every edge. The six disjoint edges \(ap_1,p_2p_3,p_4p_5,q_1q_2,q_3q_4,q_5b\) prove \(\tau(K)=6\).

If \(G\) has a vertex \(r\) of degree at most two, adjoin a disjoint \(K\) and the edge \(rp_2\). Call the result \(G'\). The bridge creates no cycle. Both \(a,b\) still have degree three, and any minimum cover of \(G\) together with \(U\) covers the bridge. Conversely, any cover restricts to covers of \(G\) and \(K\). Thus \(m'=m+14\) and \(\tau'=\tau+6\). To extend **any** circuit for \(G\), assign the 14 extra edges to their incident vertices in \(U\), assigning the bridge to \(p_2\). Each of the six centers owns a private matched edge (equivalently, use partners from the displayed matching). Its star on \(j\) owned edges costs \(j\) gates. Fifteen wires are not required at any gate: the 14 gates build the six stars, five ORs combine them, one OR joins their value to the old output. Hence \(B(G')\le B(G)+20\). Applying the hypothesized lower bound at \(G'\) gives \(B(G)\ge m+\tau-1\). The gate extension has no dependence on the particular input assignment, nor a hypothesis on the structure or monotonicity of the old circuit.

If \(G\) is cubic it already has an adjacent pair. For the stronger seven-cycle requirement one can take a disjoint \(K\) for any \(G\). The increases in \(m,\tau\) are 13 and 6, and a circuit for \(G\) extends with 19 gates by the same construction. This last version need not preserve connectedness. The deduction concerns the difficulty of the shard. A proof or counterexample for the universal inequality is still needed.

The cover construction is equation (1.2) of Jukna with each edge assigned once. Fleet 105 proves its upper bound, and 221 supplies a positive circuit. The explicit use here turns an outside literature construction into a reduction between the shard and its exact public target.
