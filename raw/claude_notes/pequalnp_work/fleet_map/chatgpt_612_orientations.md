# A direct circuit bound for shard 2 of L-meta-612

[Shared proof map](FLEET_MAP.md) · [Local mathematical evidence](<local>/Documents/Codex/2026-11-22/the-two-tracks-share-no-sound/work/fleet_proof_map/)

The shard concerns bipartite graphs without a four-cycle, with degrees at most three on one side. The recorded theorem quantifies over **all** graphs without a four-cycle and all fan-in-at-most-two positive circuits computing the edge predicate on every input. A shard result alone cannot discharge that declaration. Write $m=|E|$, $t=\tau(G)$, and $A(C),R(C)$ for the AND and OR counts. [Recorded statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-612.b880515a708a.json)

## Exact counts beyond cycles

**Every connected, four-cycle-free bipartite unicyclic graph, with arbitrary trees attached at any cycle vertices, has minimum positive circuit size $m+t-1$.** The result also holds for a four-cycle-free bipartite pseudoforest admitting a uniquely restricted maximum matching. Both arguments cover unrestricted nesting and fan-out.

1. For every nonisolated $u$, let $H=G-N[u]$. Setting $x_u=1$ and all its neighbors to zero leaves exactly the edge function of $H$ and saves an AND. The input $u$ is essential. Any dependency path from it to the output contains a first AND, since an OR-only path would accept the singleton $u$. That first AND now has a constant-true operand, so it can be bypassed. Constant propagation introduces no ANDs. This proves $A(G)\ge1+A(H)$ for the least AND count with ORs free. It uses the ordinary circuit model directly. The fleet has a corresponding checked reduction with weaker correctness tests in its separate AND-count circuit representation. [L705 proof](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/fe7ee7a47ff41dcad342bdeca2f838c38069cf44/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_705.lean)
2. If $u$ is a leaf any minimum cover may be changed to contain its neighbor in place of $u$, giving $t(G)=1+t(H)$. Repeating this and discarding isolated vertices in a connected unicyclic graph leaves either no edge or its unique cycle. For graphs with $M$ maximal independent sets, $2^{A(C)}\ge M$. On each maximal independent set $J$, select one zero operand at every false nonconstant AND gate, making arbitrary choices at true gates. Replace each such AND by the selected operand. This OR-only function dominates the original and is still false at $J$ by induction over the wires. Its false inputs form a single down-set of the form $2^S$; hence $S=J$ by maximality. Distinct $J$ require distinct binary choice vectors. Constants and unary gates can be simplified first. The 6-, 8- and 10-cycles have respectively 5, 10 and 17 maximal independent sets, giving exactly 3, 4 and 5 ANDs. For $C_{12}$ this test sees 29 and gives five.
3. Checked L599 gives $R(C)\ge m-1$ for every four-cycle-free pseudoforest. Thus the AND bound gives the claimed total. The cover circuit attains it. In particular, attaching a fresh two-edge pendant path at **every** vertex of $C_{10}$ gives 30 edges and cover number 15, so the optimum is **44 gates**. Every cycle vertex has degree three, outside the six-consecutive-degree-two criterion of L520. The analogous $C_6,C_8$ examples have exact sizes 26 and 35. [L599 proof](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/fe7ee7a47ff41dcad342bdeca2f838c38069cf44/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_599.lean) · [Monotone upper bound](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-221.77c66c477468.json) · [L520 hypothesis](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-520.8ca33af959dd.json)

**Arbitrary cycle length.** Run the leaf-pair reduction until no leaves remain. If a cycle vertex was deleted, the remaining graph after that step is a forest, so the terminal graph has no edges. The AND recurrence yields $A\ge t$ and L599 gives the desired total.

If the whole cycle survives, consider each tree $T$ formed by the hanging branches with their common cycle root $r$. The $b$ deleted pairs in this tree form a matching disjoint from $r$. Their $b$ partners (neighbors of the chosen leaves) cover $T$ and avoid $r$: every deleted edge still present at a step meets the partner, and only isolated off-cycle vertices remain. Every cover therefore costs at least $b$ off-root vertices. The minimum costs excluding and including $r$ are respectively $b$ and $b+1$.

Start with the bare cycle, where checked L520 gives $|E(C)|+\tau(C)-1$ even with NOT gates. Reattach the rooted trees one at a time. For a base edge function $g$ of minimum unrestricted size $c$, checked L549 says the cost of attaching $T$ is at least either $|E(T)|+c+|S|$ for an excluding cover or $|E(T)|+p+|S|-2$ for an including cover. Here $p$ is the minimum size of $g\lor(x_r\land y)$ for fresh $y$. L543 gives $p\ge c+1$, so both alternatives pay $|E(T)|+b$ in addition to $c$. The hypotheses hold because $g$ contains a cycle edge, and the zero assignment and the singleton $r$ are false on its two root slices. The minimum cover also rises by precisely $b$, as the matching avoids $r$ and the $b$ partners cover all the new edges. Iteration proves the lower bound, and the monotone cover construction attains it. The active-root criterion in the companion graph analysis handles many special cases; this two-case argument removes the restriction that active roots lie in one bipartition class. [L520](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-520.8ca33af959dd.json) · [L549](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/fe7ee7a47ff41dcad342bdeca2f838c38069cf44/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_549.lean) · [L543](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/fe7ee7a47ff41dcad342bdeca2f838c38069cf44/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_543.lean)

For the matching assertion, call matched pairs $(u_i,v_i)$ with $u_i$ on the left. A uniquely restricted matching is the sole perfect matching on its endpoints. Its cross edges $u_i v_j$, $i\ne j$, form an acyclic digraph $i\to j$: a directed cycle is exactly an alternating cycle. Repeatedly choose a sink $i$. Deleting $N[u_i]$ preserves all other matched pairs, including when it deletes unmatched vertices. Thus $A(G)$ is at least the size of any such matching. A maximum matching has size $t$ in a bipartite graph. More generally deleting at most $\beta=m-n'+c$ matched pairs leaves a uniquely restricted matching: fix a spanning forest, and for each extra edge meeting two matched endpoints delete one incident matched pair. Here $n'$ counts nonisolates and $c$ nontrivial components. Combined with checked L598 this gives $|C|\ge m+t-1-2\beta$ even when the pseudoforest hypothesis fails. [L598 proof](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/fe7ee7a47ff41dcad342bdeca2f838c38069cf44/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_598.lean)

## Which orientation bound transfers

Let $d(G)$ be the minimum affine dimension of an admissible orientation family. It too satisfies $d(G)\ge1+d(G-N[u])$. Indeed extend every stable set of $H$ by $u$. These orientations all point away from $u$, and their restrictions on $H$ have dimension at least $d(H)$. A stable singleton containing a neighbor reverses one fixed dart at $u$, contributing a further dimension. The all-cycle theorem therefore gives $d(G)=t$ for **every connected bipartite unicyclic graph except those retaining a square after leaf deletion**. In the shard there is no square. The vertex-cover orientation construction gives the matching upper bound.

There is a precise bridge from circuits. Checked L201 gives each edge $uv$ an AND birth whose two operands accept opposite endpoint singletons and reject the other respective singleton. Suppose a birth can be chosen for each edge so that every chosen gate is false on every stable set. At each chosen gate and each stable $J$, pick a false operand. By monotonicity its singleton endpoint lies outside $J$. Direct each edge toward that endpoint. Its encoded bit equals the chosen gate's bit or its complement, so $d(G)\le A(C)$. In fact the singleton supports of the two operands of each such sound gate are disjoint and form a complete bipartite graph. On a four-cycle-free graph it is a star, and its center covers every assigned edge. This gives $A(C)\ge t$ under the sound-birth hypothesis for all graphs in the shard. Combining with L599 gives the full count on pseudoforests in that circuit class. [L201 birth theorem](https://github.com/brilliant-hq/pequalnp-proofs/blob/fe7ee7a47ff41dcad342bdeca2f838c38069cf44/lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_201.lean)

**A legal circuit can have zero sound births.** For $C_6$ take the monotone CNF whose five clauses are the complements of its maximal stable sets. Group the two clauses for the bipartition triples under one AND and the three clauses for opposite pairs under a binary AND tree. Each group rejects every singleton, since its stable sets together cover the six vertices. Thus the final AND is sound but cannot be a birth. Every earlier AND is unsafe: a missing maximal stable set satisfies each clause present in that proper group. Consequently no edge has a sound birth in this particular circuit. Independently, the orientations with clockwise bits $101010,010101,100100,010010,001001$ on those five sets have affine dimension four. The cover circuit uses three ANDs. Thus an arbitrary admissible family cannot be charged against a fixed circuit's AND count. A size-preserving circuit transformation or a different choice principle is required.

L696 counts low-merge **(gate, line) pairs**. L697 ensures gate uniqueness across lines only for a wire whose value ignores line inputs. The bounded degree of lines provides no such condition. L616 gives 21 gates on the cubic Heawood graph, where the cover construction costs 27; its hypotheses and conclusion are distinct from a 27-gate lower bound.
