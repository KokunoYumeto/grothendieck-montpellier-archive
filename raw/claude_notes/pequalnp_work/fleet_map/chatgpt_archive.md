## 1. Vertex-cover gaps transfer to minimum gate counts

**Original proof and checked fleet statements.** OpenAI, *The Factor-Two Hardness Threshold for Vertex Cover*, 23 September 2026, `build/sections/introduction.tex`, lines 15–30, `thm:gap`. For each fixed integer m≥4 it reduces 3-SAT in deterministic polynomial time to explicitly listed simple unweighted graphs G on n vertices. A satisfiable formula yields τ(G)<(1/2+1/m)n; an unsatisfiable formula yields τ(G)>(1−1/m)n. The proof takes the perfect-completeness Label Cover source in `sections/source.tex`, constructs the explicit graph in `sections/graph.tex:187`, selects an independent set from a satisfying labeling at `graph.tex:314`, and decodes any independent set of density at least 1/m in `sections/decoding.tex:144`. The parameter choices and final reduction are in `sections/conclusion.tex:40`. The local proof source ends in `OAI.VertexCover.explicit_cover_gap` and `OAI.VertexCover.every_fixed_factor_below_two` in `lean/OAI/Computability/VertexCover/Main.lean`. I inspected the declarations and paper arguments without rebuilding that upstream library. [Original TeX, pinned revision](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Factor-Two-Hardness-Threshold-for-Vertex-Cover-September-23-2026/build/sections/introduction.tex) · [Original proof assembly](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/OAI/Computability/VertexCover/Main.lean)

**Fleet inputs.** L-meta-105 gives the edge-predicate circuit upper bound `e+|T|−1` for any vertex cover T of a graph with at least one edge. L-meta-204 gives the corresponding lower bound on graphs whose cycles have length at least seven and whose distinct vertices of degree at least three are at distance at least seven. Take the extra isolated true-input set A to be empty. The circuit basis is AND, OR and NOT of fan-in at most two, with every noninput gate counted. The frozen rows L-meta-288 and L-meta-290 establish the forward and reverse shifts of the minimum cover under even subdivision. L-meta-301 proves the internal path lower bound with its extra unit for two uncovered endpoints. All five are marked checked in this export. The exact-subdivision identity is fleet mathematics. [L-meta-105](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-105/dbe2df52532b.lean) · [L-meta-204](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-204/1308ac8f2ec8.lean) · [Frozen meta-complexity program](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/map/program/meta_complexity.c86fc18f7221.json)

**Composition.** Given a nonempty-edge G, replace each edge by a seven-edge path with six fresh internal vertices, obtaining H. If G has e edges, H has 7e edges. Every cycle uses whole replacement paths and distinct hubs are at least seven edges apart. With original vertices S selected, the minimum internal cover cost of one path is 3 if S meets the original edge and 4 otherwise. The three disjoint internal pairs force at least three vertices. With both endpoints excluded both outermost internal vertices are forced, and direct alternation gives the fourth; the four endpoint cases are also enumerated in `archive_probes.py`. If u(S) original edges are uncovered, the optimal extension costs `3e+|S|+u(S)`. Adding one endpoint of each uncovered edge gives a cover of G of size ≤|S|+u(S), and taking a minimum cover gives equality in the minimum. Therefore

`τ(H)=3e+τ(G)`, and `optCircuit(edge_H)=7e+τ(H)−1=10e+τ(G)−1`.

The question about circuit size here takes **the graph as succinct input**. Materializing the truth table of the edge predicate would use exponentially many entries. Fleet subdivisions may allocate unused slots as isolates; A=∅ makes these slots immaterial. An edgeless G has τ(G)=0 and can be classified directly in the source reduction. The source gap with m>4 implies an NP-hard additive separation for the raw optimum with thresholds `10e−1+(1/2+1/m)n` and `10e−1+(1−1/m)n`. For instance m=8 makes an additive approximation of the optimum within n/8 sufficient to distinguish the two cases, by comparing to `10e−1+3n/4`. Subtracting the known `10e−1` gives exactly τ(G) and retains the original factor-two threshold on that residual. A factor-two inapproximability statement about the full raw circuit size would require control of e/n that this reduction does not provide. This does not bound unrestricted SAT circuit sizes or prove hardness of minimum-circuit size with an explicitly supplied truth table.

**The algorithm in the other direction.** Fixed-width #SAT suffices to compute the exact optimum and construct an optimal circuit here. In OpenAI's *The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False*, 25 October 2026, Theorem 1.1 gives deterministic time `poly(n+m) 2^{O(n log log n/√log n)}` on 3-CNF and `poly(n+m) 2^{O(n/√log n)}` at fixed clause density, with polynomial space. The actual TeX is archived under `Documents/pro_prompt_builds/PAPERS_20261122/source_cache/finite_fields_eth_gct_20261122/literature/OpenAI_ETH/`. See `main_full_version.tex:73`, and the graph-counting corollary and its proof at lines 150–176 for the independent-set CNF and functional gate encoding. [OpenAI paper record](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026)

Use n bits for the vertices of G, with one negative binary clause per edge. Add a threshold test that at least j vertex bits are true. A carry-save population count takes O(n) constant-fan-in Boolean gates: at each binary place, replace any triple of equal-weight wires by a full adder's sum wire at that place and carry wire at the next place. Each full adder decreases the total number of wires by one. The remaining two rows have O(log n) places and use O(log n) more gates for binary addition and comparison with j. Encode these gates with width-three clauses and uniquely determined gate variables using fixed-size truth-table encodings for binary gates. There are O(n) variables and O(e+n) clauses. Each independent set of size at least j extends uniquely. Test positivity by binary search for its maximum size α(G); τ(G)=n−α(G). Fixing individual bits while preserving that maximum obtains a minimum cover in polynomially many further calls. Its alternating extension gives a minimum cover on H, and assigning edges to its covering vertices gives a star circuit attaining the fleet lower bound. The displayed subexponential bounds hold in the n original vertices of G, with the faster bound if e=O(n). This reconstruction works on the reduction's explicitly presented G. It does not require recognizing an arbitrary hidden subdivision. Bhattacharjee's independent-polynomial theorem `thm:indep` at `06_applications.tex:240` in Hexagon 2610.00594v1 gives a second direct `2^{o(n)}` route by taking the degree of that polynomial. [Bhattacharjee source](https://hexagonmath.org/src/2610.00594v1)

I compared all four endpoint patterns using all 64 internal assignments. I then compared the resulting minimum with direct enumeration of the minimum covers of every labelled graph on one to five vertices (1,099 graphs). These are tests of the subdivision shift; the unrestricted circuit lower bound comes from the checked fleet theorem.

## 2. A gate lower bound for inner product with an arbitrary output rule

OpenAI, *Average Sensitivity of Polynomial Threshold Functions*, 25 September 2026. `build/sections/01-introduction.tex:20` proves `I(sign p)≤8d√n` for every real multilinear polynomial of degree d on the uniform n-cube, with zero assigned positive sign; multilinearization preserves arbitrary real polynomials' cube values. In `sections/04-edge-recovery.tex` and `sections/05-parity-coupling.tex`, the proof uses the weight |p| and parity times sign(p). The binomial marginals of the two grading indices and their one-sided displacement bound control the squared anticommutator norm. Lines 88–98 of the latter section convert that estimate to the stated sensitivity count. The original Lean solution is `OAI.LeanBlast.GotsmanLinial.gotsmanLinialStatement`, `lean/OAI/Combinatorics/GotsmanLinial/Main.lean:123`. The similarly named comparator declaration with `sorry` is the challenge, not the solution. [Original paper source](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/preprints/Average-Sensitivity-of-Polynomial-Threshold-Functions-September-25-2026/build/sections/05-parity-coupling.tex) · [Original solution](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/lean/OAI/Combinatorics/GotsmanLinial/Main.lean)

Let an **arbitrary Boolean function** of bottom gates `sgn(p_j(x,y))` compute inner product modulo two on two n-bit halves. Let dⱼ be the degree of each bottom polynomial, allowing interaction between halves. Fix y=1ⁿ. Inner product becomes parity in x. Every cube edge must change at least one bottom output. Summing probabilities in each coordinate and using the union bound gives

`n = I(parity) ≤ ∑ⱼ I(sgn(pⱼ(·,1ⁿ))) ≤ 8√n ∑ⱼ dⱼ`.

Coordinate re-encoding from bits to signs and fixing y cannot increase degree. Constants have zero influence. For a degree bound d the number of gates is at least √n/(8d), irrespective of the output function or its weights. For an implementation with uniform error ε over all (x,y), the same argument in the x-coordinates gives `∑ⱼ dⱼ ≥ (1/2−2ε)√n/8` for ε<1/4. On the random input the target flips in coordinate i with probability 1/2. At most 2ε of such comparisons can be lost by an error at either endpoint; then average the bottom-gate bound conditional on y. At exact equality the fixed y=1ⁿ argument is stronger.

**Relationship to fleet.** L-clb-026 is checked and gives a stronger Ω(n/log n) lower bound for the original integer depth-two threshold model on this same inner-product language. Its proof combines a lower bound on total top weight with a small-integer-weight normal form for a threshold on the bottom outputs. Its top gate must be a threshold. L-clb-020 and L-clb-021 use separable two-half scores `a(x)+b(y)` to obtain exponentially small correlation or a large top weight. A polynomial threshold with cross-terms need not have that form. The archive supplies a separate guarantee for polynomial bottom gates and an arbitrary top rule. This is a weaker size bound over a broader model, not a solution to the polynomial-size lower bounds in L-clb-011/013.
