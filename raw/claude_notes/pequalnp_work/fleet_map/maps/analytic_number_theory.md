## 1. The strongest candidate bridges

1. **ANT-01, Cauchy-Davenport onto L-gct-117** (zero-sum-free sets in Z_p). The node's own plain statement names the
   route; Mathlib in the fleet's pin has `ZMod.cauchy_davenport`. A Lean proof is short. 117 feeds the Fourier-mode
   level-set bounds on the L-gct-086 line.
2. **ANT-02, Mahler measure onto the DIV hypotheses L-gct-103/104.** Divisibility by (X+1)...(X+N) forces
   N! <= K (T H)^M, H the largest coefficient of the g_i. So DIV holds outright unless some coefficient has
   log H of order T^e log T / M: DIV is a statement about enormous heights, not about sparsity. The work is to read
   the height regime the fleet's permanent-to-DIV reduction produces.
3. **ANT-03, Chebotarev's theorem on roots of unity (all minors of the prime-order Fourier matrix are nonzero) onto
   L-gct-211, 116 and the eigen-decomposition of shift-invariant level spaces.** It is the p-adic content of the
   prime-order step, and the 2024-26 extensions to orders pq and square-free orders
   (arXiv:2412.08600, 2505.24326, 2505.01189v3) give the next cases beyond prime powers.
4. **ANT-04, Gauss sums and Singer difference sets onto the meta NOT ladder** (L-meta-660, 661, 714, 715, the
   heavy-input step, 885 and instances). The planes in 650 and 660 are already given by the fleet as Singer sets
   ({0,1,3,9} mod 13; {0,1,3,7,15,31,36,54,63} mod 73). The circulant spectrum (q+1 once, modulus sqrt(q) otherwise)
   gives the expander-mixing bound on lines missed by a point set, which is the T2 term of 715.
5. **ANT-05, Kummer and Legendre onto L-gct-127 and 149**, and **ANT-06, the binomial idempotent onto L-clb-033**
   (Beigel-Tarui). Both are closed by short identities with Lean routes through lemmas in the pinned Mathlib.
6. **ANT-07, A (Dirichlet GRH) onto the succinct explicitness of the meta hard families and the NEXP rows** through
   our GRH_ALGORITHMS constructions of F_{p^r}; **ANT-08, Koiran's HN in AM under GRH onto L-gct-011 (GCT V route) and
   a new HN node**, with the gate recorded in ALPOGE_GCT Q-C-KOIRAN.

## 3. Notes on the top maps

**ANT-01.** The fleet's plain statement of L-gct-117 already reads "from the Cauchy-Davenport theorem of the pinned
library applied to (g - 1)(S union {0}), which misses -S". The map's content is the check that the pinned Mathlib has
the prime-modulus form (`ZMod.cauchy_davenport {p} (hp : p.Prime) {s t : Finset (ZMod p)} (hs : s.Nonempty) ...`,
line 198) and the induction for the (g-1)-fold sumset. With 116 and 211 (Codex's proofs, gct.md 5.3), the 086 line
then has all its number-theoretic service rows closed.

**ANT-02.** The bound uses only the size of the coefficient vector of F; it does not use that the g_i are sparse or
that F is a sum of M-th powers. Its translation is therefore precise about where DIV lives: a counterexample needs a
coefficient of size at least exp(T^{27/25}(27/25 log T - 1)/3) roughly (M = 3). The sparse-Wronskian ladder of the
fleet (L-gct-161, 164, 172-174) and the Koiran-Portier-Tavenas Wronskian bound (arXiv:1205.1015, real roots of sums of
products of sparse polynomials) are height-free; the Mahler bound is height-bound and sparsity-free. The two meet only
if the reduction's heights are known.

**ANT-03.** Order of work: the paper rewrite of 211 via Chebotarev is independent of Lean; the composite-order step
needs the recent principal-minor results to be opened and matched to which minors the level-space identity uses.

**ANT-04.** This is the number-theoretic face of the finite-geometry constructions in algebraic_geometry.md AG-01: the
constructions give lower bounds on ext_q(W) (prover's choice in 715), the spectrum gives upper bounds, and 715's
min-max needs both.
