# How far the same loop data can agree in base 13

The geometry reader constructs two six-sheeted covers of a connected base consisting of two triangles meeting at a vertex. Their monodromy actions have the same rational permutation representation. The regular action of the Klein four-group together with two trivial points is equivalent over the rationals to the disjoint union of its three two-element quotient actions. Thus every closed walk permutes the six sheets with the same cycle type in both examples. The common equivalence intertwines each edge separately, including after arbitrary weights are put on the edges of the base. This is the classical elementary-abelian Brauer relation. [Bartel and Dokchitser](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.2047)

Let the cut polynomial sum over all vertex bit assignments, with a factor of x for each edge whose endpoints disagree. The reader obtained the following difference for the two lifted graphs, both with 30 vertices and 36 edges.

    ΔZ(x) = −16(1+3x²)⁶(3x+x³)²(1−x²)⁶.

With boundary bits fixed, a length-three path has weight A = 1+3x² for agreeing endpoints and B = 3x+x³ for disagreeing endpoints. Summing over the six bits on the fibre of the common vertex gives the two mismatch histograms (0:8,4:48,8:8) and (0:8,2:16,4:16,6:16,8:8). Their difference gives −16 A⁶ B² (A²−B²)², and A²−B²=(1−x²)³. This proves the identity directly. The companion program independently enumerates the 512 binary cycle-space elements of each lift. The owner's SAT field-count construction, Theorem “A graph cut polynomial in the second block,” has a first derivative P′(0) equal to a shape term plus 4Z(2) at replication one. Hence in the starting pair the derivative difference has valuation 6, with leading base-13 digit 1. [Field-count theorem, equation (cut)](field_cut_theorem.tex) · [Cover computation](check_covers.py)

For every r≥0, attach r new triangles to the common base vertex, giving each new edge identity transport. At every lift of that vertex there are now r triangles meeting the old graph only at their root. Fix either bit at the root. Of the four assignments of the other two bits, one makes zero cut edges and three make two cut edges. At x=2 their total weight is 1+3·4=13. Summing them out multiplies each cover's cut value by 13⁶ʳ. Every new closed walk still has the same cycle type in the two actions. The covers each have 30+12r vertices and 36+18r edges, so their SAT shape terms still agree. It follows that

    v₁₃(P′_X(0)−P′_Y(0)) = 6+6r,
    (P′_X(0)−P′_Y(0))/13^(6+6r) ≡ 1 (mod 13).

Thus for any desired initial precision one can construct an equal-shape pair, in size linear in that precision, in which all the loop cycle types and at least that many low base-13 digits agree, then a higher digit differs. This statement is about the normalized derivative of the field construction. That paper's second block recovers it to the chosen precision. Both original graph covers are disconnected; their common base is connected. The argument permits general graphs in the cited field-count theorem.

There is a constructive remedy in this example. Alongside the cycle data, measure how many sheets are fixed by both generators simultaneously. The values are two and zero. More generally a six-sheet Klein-four action is a union of regular, two-element quotient and trivial orbits. With the fibre size and the three nonidentity fixed-point counts in hand, the single extra simultaneous fixed count determines those orbit multiplicities. Hence it recovers the transport action, and the two-spin sum above, for this fixed base. One can now test whether similarly small joint statistics suffice on other recent arithmetic examples. This comparison makes no general algorithmic complexity or priority claim.
