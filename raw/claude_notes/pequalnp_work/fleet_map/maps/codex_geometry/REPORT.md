# What the rational monodromy of a graph cover misses

The two six-sheet actions in the classical Klein-four Brauer relation give explicit counterexamples for two different targets in the local research program. The first is a 13-adic cut digit in the actual forced-center SAT construction. The second is the exact all-gate minimum for an edge predicate. A third, positive result recovers compatible labels of permutation constraints by a modular trace form when their monodromy is a finite p-group.

## 1. Same edge-labelled spectrum, different forced-center field digit

Let G be the Klein four group with generators a,b. Take X to be its regular set together with two fixed points. Take Y to be the disjoint union of G/H over the three subgroups of order two. The identity has six fixed points, and each nonidentity has two fixed points in both actions. All word permutations have identical cycle types. The rational permutation representations are isomorphic. Both contain three trivial representations and each of the three nontrivial sign characters once. The relation between these actions is classical. The explicit strong form of rational similarity used below is verified by the determinant −1/2 intertwiner in check_minima.py. [Bartel and Dokchitser, Theorem B(2)](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/149653/1/149653.pdf)

Take a base graph made of two triangles sharing a vertex. In each triangle make the first two edges carry identity transport and the closing edge carry a or b. Lift this graph with X, then with Y. Each lift has 30 vertices, 36 edges and three components. Blockwise conjugation by the same rational intertwiner at each base vertex simultaneously conjugates the two adjacency matrices whose six base-edge weights are independent indeterminates. This holds equally for separate weights on each directed base edge. It also applies to traces of arbitrary products of these base-edge operators. The explicit T and its inverse both have common denominator 12. Thus the very same similarity holds over the 13-adic integers and modulo **every** power of 13. Weights distinguishing individual sheets are outside this assertion. The sheet sets have two and zero common fixed labels, respectively.

![The two triangle transports on the six central sheets](six_sheets.png)

For unequal adjacent spins charge x and for equal spins charge one. Summing the two internal spins along a length-three path gives equal-end weight A=1+3x² and unequal-end weight B=3x+x³. There are twelve such paths. After assigning spins at the six central sheets, let w count paths with unequal ends. In X the regular orbit contributes a square with each edge doubled, and the two fixed points are free spins. In Y the three pairs have respectively two, two and four cross-pair paths. It follows that the histograms in the variable z are

$$
H_X=8+48z^4+8z^8,\qquad H_Y=8+16z^2+16z^4+16z^6+8z^8.
$$

Substitute in A¹²H(B/A). Since A²−B²=(1−x²)³, the resulting polynomial identity is

$$
Z_X(x)-Z_Y(x)=-16(1+3x^2)^6(3x+x^3)^2(1-x^2)^6.
$$

At x=2 we get Z_X=2027766116948104 and Z_Y=2038800897382600. Their difference divided by 13⁶ is 10 modulo 13. The graphs and cut polynomials were compared two independent ways. The second method constructs the actual edges and enumerates all 512 binary cycle-code words in each graph. Character orthogonality converts them to the full spin polynomial. The methods agree at 37 distinct integers, sufficient at degree 36.

## 2. A linear difference in exact minimum gate counts for connected cospectral graphs

Add one hub to each 30-vertex lift and join it to the six central sheets. Call the two connected 31-vertex, 42-edge graphs B_X and B_Y. Take r copies of the same type and join their hubs into a path. The graph has 31r vertices, 43r−1 edges, and maximum degree at most eight. Each copy has an intertwiner T preserving the all-one column and the all-one row. Indeed split off the all-one subrepresentation and its rational sum-zero complement, or use the explicit matrices in check_minima.py. Thus the six-spoke incidence block is preserved under conjugacy. Extend by identity at every hub. Give each copy its own variables per base-edge orbit and its spoke class, and each bridge any independent weight. The resulting adjacency pencils of the X and Y graphs are simultaneously rationally similar.

Fix the set S of selected central vertices in an independent set of one gadget with its hub absent. A subdivided three-edge path contributes exactly one internal vertex unless both endpoints belong to S, in which case it contributes zero. This remains valid when the ends correspond to the same sheet. Consequently

$$
\alpha(B-\mathrm{hub})=12+\max_S\bigl(|S|-\#\{(s,c):s,cs\in S,\ c=a,b\}\bigr).
$$

For X every selected fixed sheet costs two; its net contribution is negative. On the square a maximum independent subset has size two; any selected adjacent pair has at least two repeated-path penalties. Explicitly, subsets of sizes 0,1,2,3,4 have maximum net scores 0,1,2,−1,−4. For Y, in each of the first two pairs every selected sheet forfeits one to its identity generator; the last pair gives at most one and attains it. Thus the optima with the hub absent are 14 and 13. With the hub present every central sheet is excluded, and every one of the twelve paths gives one independent internal vertex, for total 13 on each graph. All hubs can be absent simultaneously on the chain. Its independence numbers are 14r and 13r and its cover numbers are 17r and 18r.

Subdivide every edge of the chain into seven. The two new connected graphs have N=289r−6 vertices, girth at least 21, and distance at least seven between distinct vertices of degree at least three. Similarity survives with one common variable for each position along each oriented edge class. The six new layers along each lifted edge use copies of T, including the interiors of spokes. The hub and bridge layers use the identity where they have just one vertex. All internal incidence maps are identities, except the original endpoint transports and the preserved all-one blocks. This proves pencil similarity after subdivision directly.

Let C be the minimum number of binary AND, binary OR, and unary NOT gates, every gate charged, for the single Boolean function detecting an edge with both endpoints active. M-020, which combines the checked L-meta-105, 204, 288 and 290, gives C=10e−1+τ on the seven-edge subdivision of a graph with e edges. Hence

$$
C_X=447r-11,\qquad C_Y=448r-11,\qquad C_Y-C_X=(N+6)/289.
$$

Any estimate determined by this common labelled spectral data incurs absolute error at least (N+6)/578 on one member. For one pair the minima are 436 and 437 at N=283. Each independent number was also recomputed from individual edges by branching on vertex inclusion, and hub-state combinations were checked separately for r=1,2,5,10. The gate equalities follow from the checked fleet theorems, rather than an exhaustive search over circuits. The family gives an obstruction to the specified spectral representation. It makes no computational hardness claim for general input algorithms. ## 3. Exact joint section counts from a modular trace form

For a finite set of labels Ω and a connected graph of bijective label constraints, choose a spanning tree to identify all fibers. Solutions are in bijection with labels fixed by every cycle generator. On the two-triangle example the global counts are two and zero. A Boolean coding of six states uses three bits per vertex, with a range check and one permutation relation per edge. Each assignment of the diagram gives one satisfying labelling, and conversely. Every single cyclic subsystem, including one defined by an arbitrary word, agrees as a permutation action in the two examples.

There is an exact general arity obstruction. Let G=(F_p)^r, r≥2, and for every subgroup H put d=codim H and c_H=(−1)^d p^((d−1)(d−2)/2). Split Σ_H c_H[G/H] into positive and negative actual G-sets X and Y. The mark difference at K is p·1_{K=G}, since c_H[G:H]=pμ(H,G) and Σ_{K≤H≤G}μ(H,G)=1_{K=G}. The formula μ(H,G)=(−1)^d p^(d(d−1)/2) follows from the Gaussian binomial identity obtained by evaluating ∏_{i=0}^{d−1}(1−p^i t) at t=1. For any proper K all marks of the restricted virtual set vanish; the downward triangular orbit-mark matrix of K then gives identical actual restricted permutation sets. Thus *all* systems of at most r−1 arbitrary generated word constraints are indistinguishable even as whole restricted sets, while the global counts are p and zero. The difference p is minimal: for any codimension-one K, the remaining quotient of order p acts on X^K, so |X^K|≡|X^G| modulo p. This is the integral multiple p of the classical top mark idempotent. It gives no polynomial-size promise for increasing r. For (p,r)=(2,3),(2,4),(3,3) the paired fibers have 30, 270, 120 points. The independent checker compares the full orbit multiplicities of every proper subgroup. [Yoshida, §4, Theorem 4.2](https://www.math.sci.hokudai.ac.jp/hmj/page/19-3/pdf/HMJ_19_3_1990_509-574.pdf)

For the constructive direction let the monodromy P be a finite p-group, and suppose the transported permutation module M=F_p^Ω is supplied together with its dot product in the label basis, equivalently the trace of coordinatewise multiplication. Under unknown basis change the form must be carried along. Compute W=∩ker(g−I) on cycle generators. Then

$$
|\Omega^P|=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}\bigl(\langle\ ,\ \rangle\vert_W\bigr).
$$

Indeed, the characteristic vectors of the orbits form a basis of W, and the Gram diagonal in this basis consists of their sizes. A p-group orbit has size prime to p exactly when it is a singleton. The rank is an *integer* fixed-point count, even if it exceeds p. Over the integers the saturated invariant lattice and its Gram matrix give the full multiset of p-power orbit sizes by Smith normal form. This is a finite linear-algebra implementation of the fixed-basis count in the classical Brauer quotient. Computing W and the Gram rank costs O(rm³) elementary field operations with r generators and m explicit states. With explicit permutations the direct fixed-label test already costs only O(rm). The matrix version is useful when supplied a mixed-basis representation and trace pairing. The p-group and trace-form hypotheses matter: for the V4 pair the mixed-basis ranks in characteristic two are 2 and 0; in characteristic three both give 3. This recovers solution counts for bijective local constraints. It does not by itself evaluate the cut polynomial of section 1. [Giannelli, Lim, O'Donovan and Wildon, Corollary 2.4 and preceding orbit-sum argument](https://www.ma.rhul.ac.uk/~uvah099/Maths/sept16v4.pdf)

The arithmetic-interface lesson in the theta-chain checkpoint 11, finite-pair-example.tex, equations FP2–FP6, is concrete. Identically normalized rational forms can acquire the reciprocals of binomial coefficients on the full standard lattices, even at branching depth zero. In HODGE_FINISH final, small_detectors_blind/independent_detector_proof.tex, Lemma lem:degrees, the Smith invariants (1,3) of a polarization matrix determine its actual integral type. These results motivated carrying an integral lattice or its distinguished modular form through the transport comparison. The proof above uses orbit indicators and contains no Hodge conjecture or theta-lift assumption.

Even at the target prime 13 there is a repair for the six-sheet character fiber. Retain the distinguished trace form and restrict it to the simultaneous invariant subspace over F₁₃. In an orbit-sum basis its determinant is 4 on X and 8 on Y. A coordinate change multiplies the determinant by a square. Since 2 is a nonsquare modulo 13, the quadratic character of this determinant is +1 and −1, respectively. These are the only two actions with the prescribed character: if f is the number of fixed sheets and b_H the number of two-sheet orbits with kernel H, then f+2b_H=2 for each H. Thus f=0 or 2, and the total of six determines the free orbit count. The discriminant identifies the action, its cut polynomial and the minimum-size transfers on this promise. With unrestricted degree there can be several further actions with the same character and discriminant; the positive claim is specific to the six-sheet fiber.

## Connected covers without an added hub

Grothendieck's Montpellier cote 132, manuscript pages 1–3 (record scans 002–004), computes connected components of a fiber product from the double cosets of the monodromy images. This suggested testing a *transitive* point/line pair. The seven points and seven lines of the Fano plane give two transitive actions of GL(3,2). Their use for isospectrality is classical, in particular in Buser–Conway–Doyle–Semmler's seven-triangle example. Let D be their incidence matrix, D_{uv}=1 if u·v=0 over F₂. Then D intertwines the point action with the contragredient action on line normals, and DᵀD=2I+J. Hence |det D|=24. This proves simultaneous similarity over Z₁₃ for all base-edge operators on a graph lift, independent of the number of monodromy generators. [Buser, Conway, Doyle and Semmler, §4, example 7₁](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~doyle/docs/drum/drum.pdf)

The three involutions printed in the classical seven-triangle example give identical cut histograms when used directly on the triangle bouquet. The group alone does not force an Ising difference. To find a separating input I changed the generating words. Encode nonzero binary three-vectors by the integers 1 through 7, least significant bit first. Take matrices with ordered columns (1,3,6), (4,2,7), and, for the three-loop version only, (1,2,6). The first two already generate all 168 elements, as checked by exact multiplication. Attach one triangle for each generator, all at one central vertex. Both seven-sheet lifts are connected.

For two generators, with the same A and B as in §1, the difference of the 128 central-spin histograms is \(-4z^4(1-z^2)^3\). Consequently

$$
Z_{\rm points}(x)-Z_{\rm lines}(x)=-4 A^4 B^4(1-x^2)^9.
$$

For these 35-vertex, 42-edge connected covers the normalized derivative difference has valuation four and leading residue three at 13. Both covers have independence number 16. With all three generators each graph has 49 vertices and 63 edges. The cut difference at 2 has valuation seven and leading residue eight, giving derivative residue six after division by 13⁷. The respective maximum independent sets have sizes 23 and 22. Their seven-edge subdivisions therefore have **655 and 656 gates** on 427 labelled vertices, while still being connected covers of one common subdivided base. The group closure, incidence conjugacy, complete 128 central-spin histograms, and all independent-set boundary scores are in check_fano.py. The full simultaneous invariant line in either action is generated by the all-one vector of norm seven. Thus even the full-space invariant Gram determinant of §3, which separates the six-sheet pair, agrees on this connected seven-sheet pair.

### Keeping the complete standard pairing

The Hodge and S6 lattice examples suggest retaining the full marked pairing as well as the linear transport. The point/line construction over F₄ supplies a test of that stronger interface. Here there are 21 points and lines. Their 21-by-21 incidence matrix satisfies DᵀD=4I+J and D1=5·1. The single matrix

$$
O=(7D-J)/14
$$

intertwines every point permutation with the corresponding line permutation, satisfies OᵀO=I and fixes the all-one vector. All of these identities hold over Z[1/14], hence over Q, R, Z₁₃ and every Z/(13ᵏ). Thus *even the whole standard trace pairing* with the full linear transport system agrees up to a pointed orthogonal isomorphism at the field prime. Coordinatewise multiplication of sheet functions is extra structure.

To specify the examples, write F₄={0,1,2,3} with 2²=3 and 3=2+1. Matrices are given by rows. Take

### The order-48 affine subgroup

The first two displayed matrices generate a subgroup of order 20,160 and the three together generate 60,480 elements, the full PGL₃(4). This is checked by multiplication of the 21-point permutations. Their matrix determinants in F₄ are 1,1,2. In particular the order-48 subgroup H=(F₄)²⋊F₄× used in the owner's separate lattice calculation actually lies in this group. Every one of the 48 maps [x:y:z]↦[t x+a z:t y+b z:z] was checked for membership. The on-paper orbit decomposition uses the affine plane and its line at infinity. On points the affine translations are transitive on 16 affine points, and all five points at infinity are fixed individually. On lines the line at infinity is fixed, and the four affine lines in each of five directions are transitively permuted. These same partitions hold for the translation Sylow 2-subgroup. The common ordinary character on H is 21 at the identity, 5 at any of the 15 nonidentity translations, and 6 at all 32 remaining homotheties. Thus the two restricted sets have **five and one fixed labels**. The invariant trace-form ranks are five and one in characteristic two, six and six in characteristic three, and six and six in characteristic thirteen. This identifies the exact finite source of the modular distinction. The order-48 representation in the owner's 24-dimensional lattice calculation has different traces and is not identified with these permutation representations.
