## 1. AC-1 in full: inverse semirings carry the Boolean circuit and its polynomial together

**Source** [opened].
- H22 Theorem 1(b): every point of Spec R_0 lies in the closure of eta; {eta} is open; the localization of R_0 at eta is
  B. In R_0 the new zero is additive identity and multiplicatively absorbing; every element of R (the old zero
  included) becomes 1 at eta.
- CFMM Theorem 8.5 (GATE_CFMM): inverse semirings correspond to idempotent semirings Q with a lax monoidal functor
  F: Q -> Ab; elements (e, g), g in F(e); addition along joins, multiplication through the lax structure. Remark 8.7:
  FGJM's R_0 is Q = B, F(0) = 0, F(1) = R.

**Translation** [mine].
1. The one-point case. Lift a Boolean input x in {0,1}^n to x~ in Z_0^n with x~_i = the new zero if x_i = 0 and
   x~_i = a_i in Z if x_i = 1. A monotone circuit C (OR -> +, AND -> ·) evaluated on x~ returns the new zero iff
   C(x) = 0, and otherwise the value at a of the sum of those monomials of the formal polynomial P_C whose support lies
   in supp(x). Localizing at eta returns C(x).
2. The universal case. Q_n = monotone Boolean functions of n variables under (OR, AND), an idempotent semiring.
   F(f) = Z-span of monomials m with supp(m) in the up-set of f (that is, f(supp m) = 1); for f <= f' the inclusion
   F(f) -> F(f'); F(f) ⊗ F(f') -> F(f AND f') by multiplication. By CFMM 8.5 the Grothendieck construction T_n is an
   inverse semiring; the input x_i is (x_i, x_i); the circuit computes (f_C, P_C).
3. Support criterion. C computes the edge function f_G iff (a) every monomial of P_C has support containing an edge and
   (b) every edge is the support of some monomial. (Single-vertex or empty supports are independent sets, so (a)
   excludes them; then a monomial with support inside an edge has support equal to the edge.)
4. Line-degree. With the lines marked, the fleet's syntactic line-degree (AND adds, OR takes the maximum) equals the
   y-degree of P_C (no cancellation occurs over N).
5. Consequence. For every graph G: (least monotone circuit for f_G) = min over P in Sup(G) of (least monotone
   arithmetic circuit for P), where Sup(G) is the support class of 3. So 606, 608, 612 and 647 are statements about
   monotone arithmetic circuits for polynomials with prescribed minimal supports.

**Where the known arithmetic argument stops.** meta.md 5.5: for P = sum over incidences x_p y_w exactly, homogenization
plus "a product of a point form and a line form has a rectangle of monomials as support, which must be a star" gives
tau products and |I| - 1 additions. In Sup(G) the polynomial may contain x_p^a y_w^b and monomials with extra
variables; homogenization by total degree no longer isolates the bilinear part. Passing to the multilinear quotient
(x_i^2 = x_i, the image of T_n in which supports are what is remembered) restores the edge monomials but destroys
degree, which is the idempotence the fleet's line-degree ladder works around.

**Work to complete.**
1. Lean: define evaluation over T_n (or over Z_0 with formal inputs) for the fleet's circuit type; prove the lift lemma
   and the support criterion.
2. Proof: the arithmetic lower bound for all P in Sup(G), G bipartite and C4-free. A natural first step keeps the
   bidegree-(1,1) component in (x, y): every monomial of P has bidegree at least (1,1) (its support contains an edge,
   which has one point and one line), and the bidegree-(1,1) monomials are x_p y_w with {p, w} an edge. But an edge may
   be covered in P only by a monomial of higher bidegree (x_p^2 y_w, or x_p y_w times further variables of the edge),
   so the (1,1) component is sum over a subset E' of the edges. The step to do: (a) the bihomogeneous-component
   construction (a monotone circuit of size s for P gives one of size O(s) for each component of bounded bidegree; for
   (1,1) the constant matters, since 608 is exact), and (b) a charge for the edges in E \ E', which are exactly the
   edges the Boolean circuit covers only through idempotence or absorption.
3. If the constant in step 2 is not 1, the result is a 606-type statement (linear in |E|) for all bipartite C4-free
   graphs; record which.

## 3. AC-4 and AC-5 in full: one dictionary for the level-space problem

- **The objects.** k! S_k(t) = sum over ordered distinct row k-tuples and column k-tuples of prod t_{i_h j_h}.
  Moebius inversion over the partition lattices of the row slots and the column slots gives
  k! S_k = sum over (pi, sigma) of mu(pi) mu(sigma) N(pi, sigma), with N unrestricted sums. On doubly centred t every N
  with a singleton block vanishes. The single-block term is ((k-1)!)^2 p_k, so S_k = ((k-1)!/k) p_k + (terms from
  partitions into blocks of size >= 2 with at least two blocks). Codex's r-moment lemma kills those terms when the row
  and column spans have vanishing moments through r, for k <= 2r + 1 (chatgpt_gct_preparation.md).
- **What our results supply.**
  - Lemma K (122) and the Fermat lemmas (061, 067): bounds on dim W when p_2, ..., p_k vanish on W.
  - O-011 (Hessian eigenvalues) and A5 (S_4 on DC): the first two layers.
  - GCT_PLETH's moment maps (R-046, R-050): surjectivity of power-sum moment maps onto isotypic components, with
    "independent-row polarization, triangular Moebius moment substitution and finite centered evaluation"; the
    same three operations appear in the S_k expansion above.
- **The map.** The N(pi, sigma) with blocks of size >= 2 span the degree-k invariants of S_n x S_n on DC matrices that
  are not power sums; R-046/R-050 give these spaces in a power-sum moment basis. A level space kills S_2, ..., S_n; by
  the barrier theorem (gct.md 5.1(c)) a linear bound must use orders up to about n/(C+1).
- **Work.** (1) Fit S_5 and S_6 on DC matrices (exact rational fit as A5 did; n = 6, 7, 8). (2) Compute the dimension of
  degree-k S_n x S_n invariants on DC matrices for k <= 8 and express S_k in the moment basis via R-046. (3) Formulate
  "all N(pi, sigma) of order <= k vanish on L" as a conjunction of power-sum vanishing statements on auxiliary spaces
  (row spans, column spans, Gram products), to which 067 applies; Codex's circulant (row (1, i, -i, -1)) shows the
  conjunction must include the Gram trace tr((T T^T)^2).
