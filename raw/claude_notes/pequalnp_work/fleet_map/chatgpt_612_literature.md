# L-meta-612, bipartite graphs with one side of degree at most three

[Shared proof map](FLEET_MAP.md) · [Local mathematical evidence](<local>/Documents/Codex/2026-11-22/the-two-tracks-share-no-sound/work/fleet_proof_map/)

The live statement requires a single positive fan-in-two circuit to accept precisely the vertex subsets containing an edge. All AND and OR gates count, and every input assignment matters. I found no published counterexample or unrestricted proof for this shard in the sources below. The tracked paper proof here shows why the attempt to charge each local merge at a bounded number of vertices fails, even for an optimal circuit on a path and even when the side can be chosen after inspecting the circuit.

## One gate can supply every local merge at arbitrarily many vertices on both sides

For $k\ge2$, take the path $x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2,\ldots,x_{2k},y_{2k}$. Its edges are $x_i y_i$ and $y_i x_{i+1}$. Choose the cover $T=\{y_1,\ldots,y_k,x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{2k}\}$. The $2k$ disjoint edges $x_i y_i$ certify minimality. Assign each edge to its endpoint in $T$, giving the one edge with both endpoints in $T$ to $y_k$. Write $N_v$ for the neighbors of $v$ on edges it owns. Each $N_v$ contains the partner on the matching. Form

$$h_v=z_v\land\bigvee_{u\in N_v}z_u \qquad(v\in T).$$

Combine the $h_v$ whose subscripts are even in one binary OR tree, the odd ones in another, and combine the two outputs with a final OR $g$. Each edge appears once in the expansion. The star gates and their neighborhood ORs cost $4k-1$ gates in total, and aggregation costs $2k-1$. Hence the complete circuit has $6k-2=|E|+\tau-1$ gates. Fleet L-meta-104 establishes optimality on forests, including against AND/OR/NOT circuits. [L-meta-104](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-104/75617a57212d.lean) · [Cover construction L-meta-105](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-105/dbe2df52532b.lean)

Use the precise L-meta-663 definition. Fix a vertex $v$ at one and all other inputs on its side at zero. A wire is *low* when it vanishes with every opposite-side point outside $N(v)$ on and all neighbors off. Its *alive set* comprises the opposite vertices whose singleton input it accepts under this restriction. A binary OR is a low merge when it is low and its operands' alive sets are incomparable. [L-meta-663](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-663/b53ff8a50e00.lean)

For $v=x_i$ with $2\le i\le k$, the only surviving star terms are $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$. For $v=y_i$ with $k+1\le i<2k$, the only surviving terms are $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$. In both cases the terms lie in opposite parity trees. Thus $g$ merges the two distinct singleton alive sets and is low. Every aggregation OR below $g$ has at most one live operand. A neighborhood OR among the fixed-side variables becomes constant. A neighborhood OR among the opposite-side variables cannot be a low merge either. To be low with two live singleton inputs it would have to contain the same two neighbors as $v$, giving a four-cycle through its distinct center. Therefore $g$ is the **only** low merge for every one of these $k-1$ vertices on each side.

I checked the actual gate lists on all 256, 4,096 and 65,536 inputs for $k=2,3,4$, respectively, and used the complementary assignment and every opposite-side singleton to identify every low merge at the claimed vertices. The sizes were 10, 16 and 22. This family attains the target and rules out bounded single-gate multiplicity as the missing step between L-meta-696, which counts (gate, line) pairs, and L-meta-612, which counts gates. L-meta-697 already restricts a *line-independent* merge to at most one line. The final OR here is line-dependent whichever side is designated. ## Literature and checked scope

* Lenz and Wegener count AND gates with ORs free. Their single-AND-level optimization is biclique edge cover. In a bipartite graph without four-cycles every nonempty biclique is a star, so here its value is $\tau$. Their numerical separation for conjunction count alone gives no smaller total gate list for this shard. I verified the journal abstract and Jukna's account of the example. [Lenz and Wegener, 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(91)90194-7) · [Jukna, §2](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/single1.pdf)
* Jukna's Theorem 2.1 does count all monotone gates. Its graphs are saturated extensions of Sylvester graphs, obtained by making each vertex part a clique. This construction introduces triangles and, for part size at least four, four-cycles. His partial graph-representation model only forces the values on edges and nonedges as pairs. Full edge-function evaluation also requires rejection on larger independent sets. For a monotone circuit, testing every edge and every **maximal independent set** does suffice. Jukna's Problem 8.2 asks for an explicit superlinear circuit bound. The unsharded L-meta-612 would give one on finite projective-plane incidence graphs. The bounded-degree shard only asks for a linear bound. [Jukna, §§2–4 and Problem 8.2](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/single1.pdf)
* The simultaneous neighborhood-output theorem is classical. Fleet L-meta-529, contributed by johnpascoe43, proves $\sum_i(|S_i|-1)_+\le\#\mathrm{gates}$ for pairwise intersections of size at most one, with arbitrary shared positive AND/OR gates. Even the OR-count version follows from Mehlhorn's proof of Lemma 1(a), which removes the ANDs without adding ORs at $h=1$, followed by the OR-only count. Mehlhorn credits Nečiporuk, Lamagna–Savage and Tarjan for $h=k=1$. A quick alternative proof follows a wire backwards from $\bigvee S_i$, always keeping it false on the complement of $S_i$. At an AND take a predecessor false there, preserving its successful singleton inputs. At an OR take both, stopping at wires accepting at most one singleton. At least $|S_i|$ distinct terminals and hence $|S_i|-1$ distinct branching ORs are needed. Each charged OR accepts two singletons in $S_i$, precluding a charge by a second row. The path above shows exactly what changes under *different* one-hot restrictions of a single-output circuit. [Fleet L-meta-529](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-529/eb99d80eb8fe.lean) · [Mehlhorn, Theorem 1 and proof of Lemma 1, 1979](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e3379453f082008ccb0af8ad2343089fcc298c6a&repid=rep1&type=pdf) · [Pippenger, 1980, §§1 and 3](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/boolean/Pippenger.pdf)
* In export c22e5e0031b89e03, L-meta-613 proves the all-gate bound for flat circuits with centered ANDs, each implying the edge function, and no AND below an AND. L-meta-615 proves the exact bound on theta graphs. L-meta-133 gives a rectangle-partition count for the monotone communication relation, applicable to formulas; unfolding a shared circuit can enlarge it. L-meta-668 gives an actual 16-gate circuit for the six-cycle with three leaves and refutes a proposed count of ANDs plus ORs true on two singleton inputs. Here $|E|=9$ and $\tau=3$, so its circuit exceeds the 11-gate cover circuit. L-meta-582 still asks for a 26-gate Heawood circuit and L-meta-583/723 ask about a 14-gate subdivided-\(K_4\) circuit. Both examples lie in the shard. [Flat case](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-613/35da654ded42.lean) · [Theta graphs](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-615/88a84586961e.lean) · [Rectangle partition](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-133/e943f55b309d.lean) · [Finite circuit](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-668/622628f0526d.lean)
