# Local literature on graph edge functions and the six fleet statements

[Shared proof map](FLEET_MAP.md) · [Local mathematical evidence](<local>/Documents/Codex/2026-11-22/the-two-tracks-share-no-sound/work/fleet_proof_map/)

The most direct archive match is the **unique-matching argument in SAT witness return 07, Paper III**. It yields a lower bound on AND gates in the unrestricted-depth positive circuit model. If a maximum matching of size $t$ has $P$ perfect matchings on its induced endpoints, then every such circuit needs at least

$$t-\lfloor\log_2 P\rfloor\quad\text{AND gates}.$$

This gives all $t$ ANDs on a family of subcubic four-cycle-free graphs with arbitrarily many independent cycles. Separately, one private leaf per vertex on a whole side gives an exact $2^t$ maximal-independent-set certificate. For the graph of 761/762 this and the fleet's OR lower bound give **11 gates**, refuting the existence requested in 761 on paper. Both circuit arguments below are mathematical deductions. The underlying fleet checker runs attach to the original fleet theorems.

## Matching choices become AND eliminations

**Archive source.** The Corollary “A unique core matching of small directed depth” in *Paired minors with a common matching, after Eiben, Koana and Wahlström* contracts each matched row-column pair and directs each other entry from its row pair to its column pair. A directed cycle flips to a second matching, and the symmetric difference of two perfect matchings contains such a cycle. The return explicitly credits Golumbic, Hirst and Lewenstein, *Uniquely restricted matchings*, Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 of the author version. The return's own algorithm concerns at most three core labels and constructs a witness in $2^{O((d+1)t_{\rm exc})}\operatorname{poly}(h,N)$ bit operations, where $d$ is directed depth and $t_{\rm exc}$ is exceptional label mass. Its isolation proof at lines 918–929 chooses a **minimum-weight** matching in a prescribed histogram. Uniqueness after weighting does not say the unweighted graph has one perfect matching. The corollary and proof at lines 2313–2334 are identical in returns 06 and 07. Both are primary TeX, member `workbench/papers/paper_III/main.tex`. [Return 07](<<local>/Downloads/SAT_WITNESS_EXTRACTION_checkpoint_07.zip>) · [Return 06](<<local>/Downloads/SAT_WITNESS_EXTRACTION_checkpoint_06.zip>)

**Circuit deduction.** Restrict to the induced endpoints of the chosen matching by setting all other inputs to zero. As long as the remaining graph has more than one perfect matching, choose a vertex with more than one possible mate. At least one possible edge occurs in a positive fraction at most one half of the perfect matchings. Fix it and remove its two endpoints. The residual matchings correspond bijectively to the retained matchings, so at most $\lfloor\log_2P\rfloor$ pairs are removed before uniqueness. This is a counting proof of existence. On the remaining board contract the unique matched pairs as above and choose a sink pair. Its left endpoint $u$ has exactly its matched neighbor $v$ on this board. Set $u=1,v=0$; this leaves the edge predicate on the other pairs. In any positive circuit an essential path from $u$ must meet an AND, since an OR-only path to the output would accept $u$ alone. The first AND then has a true operand and can be bypassed. Repeat, using the acyclicity of what remains. This forces one AND per surviving pair. The fleet's 705 proves a related removal in its distinct, partial-function AND-count representation. Together with checked 599 this gives $|C|\ge m+t-1-\lfloor\log_2P\rfloor$ on four-cycle-free pseudoforests. [L705](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-705.072644be9f9b.json) · [L599](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-599.1fe048e7df9c.json)

**Three-label example.** With indices $0,\ldots,k-1$, put an edge $u_i v_j$ for $j\in\{i,i+1,i+3\}$ in range. The six nonzero signed differences between $0,1,3$ are distinct, so two rows share at most one column. The diagonal is the unique perfect matching of the upper triangular board. For $k\ge3$ this connected graph has $2k$ vertices, $3k-4$ edges and cycle rank $k-3$, and its exact AND minimum with free ORs is $k$. A cover circuit attains it. Reversing the columns realizes the archive's actual antidiagonal labels $k-1,k-2,k-4$ for $k\ge4$. Its contracted depth is $k-1$. Checked L598 adds $2k-2$ ORs for a total lower bound $3k-2$. The proposed 612 bound is $4k-5$, leaving $k-3$ gates. Thus the difficult part on this family is the OR contribution or a joint gate count. **Second archive proof, with the inequality direction checked.** OpenAI's *Entropy and Face Dimension of the Perfect Matching Polytope*, September 2026, §1, “Pointwise entropy bounds”, and §4, “Separable estimates of the matching count”, give $\ln P\le \max_x F(x)\le\ln P+2t-2$ for the balanced induced board, where $F(x)=-\sum_e x_e\ln x_e$. Therefore an independently certified upper bound $U\ge\max F$ can replace $\log_2 P$ above by the weaker $U/\ln2$. The same section's lower bound from edge-disjoint perfect matchings has the opposite direction for this application. For the triangular family the polytope is one point and $F=0$. ## When counting stable sets certifies gates

The canonical-index hit by Michael Han, Sycamore Herlihy, Kirsti Kuenzel, Daniel Martin and Rachel Schmidt, *The number of independent sets in bipartite graphs and benzenoids*, distinguishes arbitrary from maximal independent sets at lines 79–92. Its line-101 theorem, credited to Prodinger and Tichy, gives $i(C_n)=F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}$. Thus $C_6$ and $C_8$ have 18 and 47 independent sets, respectively. They have only 5 and 10 **maximal** ones. Counting all 47 would incorrectly force six ANDs on the four-AND circuit in 788. There is an exact conversion: add one private leaf at every vertex of an arbitrary graph $H$. Independent sets of $H$ then correspond to maximal independent sets of the enlarged graph, by choosing the private leaf exactly at each omitted old vertex. [Local manuscript, lines 79–101](<F:/user/Documents/arxiv_latex/library/other/The number of independent sets in bipartite graphs and benzenoids/main.tex:79>) · [788's specified circuit](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-788.8cb8110e0897.json)

If $H$ is bipartite and one private leaf is added to each vertex of just one side $X$, the resulting number of maximal independent sets is exactly $2^{|X|}$. Choose any $B\subseteq X$, take its members, take precisely the private leaves at $X\setminus B$, and take every old opposite vertex outside $N(B)$. Every maximal independent set has this form. A positive circuit with $a$ AND gates has at most $2^a$ maximal zero inputs: at a false maximal input retain one false operand of each false AND and any operand of each true AND. The resulting OR-only function dominates the original and still vanishes there, so its unique largest zero input is the prescribed one. Simplify constants first. This gives $a\ge|X|$, attained with one AND per center. The three centers in 761/762 yield eight maximal sets and at least three ANDs. Checked 599 contributes at least eight ORs, and the three star terms cost eleven gates altogether. The analogous count on the undecorated $C_8$ gives at least four ANDs and 599 gives seven ORs, matching the 11 gates in 788. Proving 788's exact declaration additionally requires evaluating the specified gate list. The general bipartite saturation description in fleet 733 agrees with the private-leaf bijection. ## Exact boundary of the classical circuit papers

Stasys Jukna, *Disproving the Single Level Conjecture*, §3.2, Lemma 11, identifies single-AND-level multiplicative complexity with the minimum number of bicliques and the one-level formula leaf count with minimum total biclique weight. On a four-cycle-free graph all such bicliques are stars. Thus the minimum number is exactly $t$ and the minimum weight is $m+t$: a cover assigns each edge once to a center, while any star cover pays at least one occurrence per covered edge and one per center. This gives exactly $m+t-1$ gates in a binary single-level formula. The distinction for shared OR subcircuits is explicit immediately before Remark 12; Wegener's overlap lemma is Lemma 14. For flat circuits the fleet's checked 613 pays all gates under its exact soundness, center and no-AND-ancestor hypotheses. Jukna's separation theorem 4 uses saturated extensions that contain cliques on each side; those graphs do not give shard-2 counterexamples. His earlier *On Graph Complexity*, §7.2, Theorem 7.3, supplies a leaf-size lower bound for arbitrary-depth monotone formulas. A proof for formulas, or for positive circuits, cannot settle 519, which also allows charged NOT gates.
