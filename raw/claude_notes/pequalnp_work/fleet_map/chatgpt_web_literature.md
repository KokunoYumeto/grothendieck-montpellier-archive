## An exact value in a neighboring circuit model

For every nonempty bipartite graph \(G=(U,V,E)\) with no four-cycle, let \(A_G\) be its biadjacency matrix, \(m=|E|\) and \(\tau=\tau(G)\). Then

\[
                  \mathrm{OR}_2(A_G)=m+\tau.
\]

This specializes Chistikov, Iván, Lubiw and Shallit's rectangle characterization in §2 of *Fractional coverings, greedy coverings, and rectifier networks*. Their size counts **wires**, and their depth-two convention requires every maximal path to have exactly two edges. A rectangle costs the sum of its side sizes. Every nonempty all-one rectangle here is a star because a rectangle with two vertices in both parts would give a four-cycle. A star with \(r\) edges costs \(r+1\). Any rectangle cover therefore costs at least \(m\) for the covered incidences and at least \(\tau\) for its centers. Conversely, assign each edge to one endpoint in a minimum cover. The resulting stars have total cost \(m+\tau\). This is an exact value for a multi-output OR computation of all neighborhoods. [Chistikov et al., arXiv:1509.07588v2, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07588v2)

The **bipartite portion of L-meta-612**, including the one-side-degree-at-most-three shard, can thus be restated as the sharp comparison \(\mathrm{OR}_2(A_G)\le C_{\rm mono}(f_G)+1\), where \(f_G\) is the one-output predicate that the selected vertices contain an edge. A cover yields a one-output implementation of size \(m+\tau-1\). The remaining direction must lower-bound an arbitrary shared AND/OR circuit. Reading the same circuit with different first-part inputs fixed to one vertex at a time produces separate cofactors. A simultaneous rectifier at that cost would require a construction. The full posted L-meta-612 permits triangles and has no bipartiteness assumption. [Posted statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-612.b880515a708a.json)

Chvátal and Hammer's *Aggregation of inequalities in integer programming*, Theorem 3, already gives \(t(G)=|V|-\alpha(G)=\tau(G)\) whenever \(G\) is triangle-free. Their \(t\) counts threshold subgraphs covering the edges. Every triangle-free threshold subgraph is a star together with isolated vertices. Francis, Majumder and Mathew, Observation 1, give the complement convention for their intersection threshold dimension, \(\dim_{\rm TH}(\overline G)=t(G)\), and Proposition 1 identifies the graphic Boolean function. The cover parameter is thus classical even for nonbipartite graphs in L-meta-519. The extra \(m-1\) and the effect of gate sharing require circuit arguments. [Chvátal and Hammer, Theorem 3, report p. 15](https://ftpmirror.your.org/pub/misc/bitsavers/pdf/stanford/Stanford_CS_TR_Collection_2025-12-12/OCR/CS-TR-75-518-ocr.pdf) · [Francis, Majumder and Mathew, arXiv:2202.12325v2, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12325v2)

## Exact finite search

Kulikov, Pechenev and Slezkin describe SAT-based exact synthesis by variables for the output of gate \(i\) on input \(x\), with constraints for the operation and predecessors. Their *Finding Efficient Circuits* subsection distinguishes this global encoding from local circuit replacement. For positive circuits a smaller test set suffices. Require output one on each two-vertex edge and zero on every maximal independent set. Every true assignment contains an edge and every false assignment extends to a maximal independent set. Monotonicity proves the full truth condition. [Kulikov, Pechenev and Slezkin, arXiv:2102.12579v3, *Finding Efficient Circuits*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12579v3)

## Limits of alternative lower bounds

* **Graph entropy.** Rezaei and Godsil, Corollary 4.5, show that the uniform distribution maximizes entropy in a bipartite graph without isolated vertices exactly when it has a perfect matching. The eight-vertex path and eight-cycle both have uniform graph entropy one bit. The average of the two bipartition vectors is feasible in the stable-set polytope, while AM–GM along a perfect matching gives the matching lower bound. Their edge-predicate costs are 10 and 11 respectively, by the checked forest and long-chain bounds and the cover construction. Uniform graph entropy together with vertex count cannot recover the exact gate count. [Rezaei and Godsil, Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6561v1) · [Fleet forest bound](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-104/75617a57212d.lean) · [Fleet long-chain bound](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-520.8ca33af959dd.json)
* **Graph complexity and bilinear forms.** Cavalar and Oliveira, §§2.2.2 and 4.1, Lemma 39, use a universe of ordered vertex pairs and row/column star generators. The associated Boolean function reads binary names for one vertex on each side. The fleet circuit reads one selection bit per vertex. Mirwald and Schnorr's rank characterization in §3 concerns a sum of edge products over \(\mathbb F_2\), with XOR and constants free. Selecting both edges of a length-two path already distinguishes parity from the existential edge predicate. [Cavalar and Oliveira, arXiv:2503.14117v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14117v1) · [Mirwald and Schnorr, §3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(92)90235-8)
* **Full edge functions.** Jukna, Theorem 2.1, gives graph functions with linear-size general monotone circuits and one-AND-level size \(\Omega(n^2/\log^3 n)\). The proof makes each side of a bipartite Sylvester-type graph a clique. This step in Lemma 3.8 creates triangles and four-cycles. Theorem 2.3 and its proof in §6 yield an induced-matching lower bound for the unrestricted-depth, unbounded-fan-in monotone model. This falls short of \(m+\tau-1\) in general. [Jukna, Theorems 2.1 and 2.3](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/single1.pdf)
* **Gate elimination.** Carmosino, Dang and Jackman, §5, Theorem 18 and Appendix C, fix one input of parity, leaving a smaller parity or its complement with all remaining inputs essential. They count costly binary AND/OR gates with negations free. With an edge predicate, setting a vertex to zero can isolate its neighbors, and setting it to one adds unary neighborhood terms. An analogous three-gate charge needs a separate invariant. [Carmosino, Dang and Jackman, arXiv:2602.17942v1, §5 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17942v1)

## A Hexagon source for the vertex-cover parameter

Frank Vega's *An \(83/84\) approximation for a cubic three-occurrence Horn class*, Theorem 3 and Lemma 20, gives an independent encoding of the cover number. For an arbitrary finite simple \(n\)-vertex, \(m\)-edge graph its constructed MAX-2-SAT instance has optimum \(n+3m+\alpha(G)\). Hence \(\tau(G)=2n+3m-\mathrm{OPT}(T_G)\). In the proof every selected but inconsistent clone cycle loses a transition clause. After discarding these vertices, deleting one endpoint per violated edge leaves an independent set of the asserted size. A maximum independent set with constant clones attains equality. For the 518, 761/762 and 788 graphs the optima are 45, 42 and 36, from independently enumerated independence numbers 5, 6 and 4. This checks the numerical cover side. The other side of the fleet inequality requires a universal statement about circuits. [Vega, Hexagon 2611.00827v1, native TeX](https://hexagonmath.org/api/v1/works/2611.00827v1/source?path=paper.tex)
