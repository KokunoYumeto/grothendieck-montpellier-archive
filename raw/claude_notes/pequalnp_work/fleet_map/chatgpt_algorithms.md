This costs polynomial time in the written string even if an identifier is exponentially large. Feed the formula, or its satisfiability-preserving 3-CNF, to the local planar threshold construction at threshold one. With growing precision the digit at `J=s(4M+2)+j−1` of the root count is one exactly for satisfiable formulas. The local proof builds the field, sparse coefficient list, digit index and phase calibration in polynomial time. Consequently a deterministic polynomial-bit-time evaluator for this promised family of written instances closes L-alg-001 and implies P=NP via L-lib-031. The local construction is a mathematical proof outside the fleet's Lean library. At strict majority the same construction instead encodes a PP-hard threshold. ## 2. When the exact CNF count feeds the threshold-circuit target

**Existing positive scope.** OpenAI's 25 October manuscript, Theorem 1.1, gives exact model counts for each *fixed* clause width in polynomial space and time `poly(n+m)·2^O(n log log n/√log n)`. For fixed density `m≤Dn` the exponential factor is `2^O(n/√log n)`. The local coefficient recovery theorem reconstructs the signed clauses on its normalized Hermitian coefficient list, preserving model counts and original arity up to the explicit symmetries. It therefore gives these same original-variable exponents for that normalized digit, with polynomial overhead in the written field. The planar separator bound on the full degree uses `2^O(√q)` with `q` measured *after* planar construction. See `outputs/P_VS_NP/reading_clauses_from_field_coefficients.tex`, “Algorithms for the digit,” and the planar threshold paper following its programming proof. [Theorem 1.1 in the authors’ source](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex)

The circuit-analysis branch L-clb-015 asks for SAT on all polynomial-description-size depth-two threshold circuits in `2ⁿ/g(n)+O(1)` time for an eventually superpolynomial `g`, where `n` counts their *original free inputs*. L-clb-024 asks for an additive estimate satisfying `r·|estimate−#C|≤2ⁿ`, for every fixed natural `r`. L-clb-028 asks for inverse-polynomial error on the bounded-bottom-weight and unit-top-weight promise. Chen and Williams, Theorem 8, derive a language in NEXP outside polynomial-size THR∘THR from a nontrivial SAT algorithm on that circuit class, or from a constant-error acceptance-probability estimator at the specified speed. Their Corollary 10 includes the bounded majority-circuit route. The fleet's L-clb-022 records its own precisely encoded SAT-to-NEXP implication as a conjecture. Its L-clb-013 is the lower-bound conclusion, also a conjecture. **A quantitative sufficient bridge.** Suppose the input circuit on `n` free bits can be compiled in polynomial time to a bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit with `S` gates. Give each gate one output variable, impose its full truth-table equivalence by clauses of width at most three, and pin the output. Each input has exactly one consistent internal assignment, and every accepting input is counted once. With `N=n+S` variables and `O(S+1)` clauses, the fixed-density bound gives `poly(|C|)·2^O(N/√log N)`. For instance, `S=o(n√log n)` suffices for `2^o(n)` exact counting on this subclass. Arbitrary polynomial-size depth-two threshold circuits can have polynomially many gates with long binary weight lists. A polynomial compilation bound alone does not imply the required bound on `N`. A general polynomial-time local coefficient evaluator would also settle L015 by threshold one, but the existing original-variable and planar algorithms leave this conversion open. The separate reviewed Bhattacharjee profile algorithm already covers bounded-support gates plus dense weight rows of sufficiently small joint range, as recorded in the earlier joint map. [Bhattacharjee, weighted profiles and paper source](https://hexagonmath.org/src/2610.00594v1)
