# Which edge-function lower bounds would transfer to ordinary circuits?

[Shared proof map](FLEET_MAP.md) · [Local mathematical evidence](<local>/Documents/Codex/2026-11-22/the-two-tracks-share-no-sound/work/fleet_proof_map/)

For a graph \(G\) on \(n\ge2\) vertices, let \(f_G\) accept sets containing an edge. Jukna §3 also studies monotone functions that give the right answers on all *two*-vertex inputs. The magnification argument in §1 and §3 gives a lower-bound route through this latter representation model. The domain quantified in fleet 612 comprises *every* input. Here is an exact obstruction to identifying those two tasks on the relevant graph family. [Jukna, §1 and Observations 3.2–3.5](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/single1.pdf) · [Fleet 612](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-612.b880515a708a.json)

**Uniqueness criterion.** The only monotone representation of the pair answers is \(f_G\) precisely when every vertex has a nonneighbor other than itself and the independence number is at most two. Sufficiency is Jukna's Observation 3.5. Every singleton must be false since it extends to a negative pair. Every set with at least three vertices contains a positive pair. For necessity, a universal vertex \(v\) allows the distinct monotone function \(f_G\lor x_v\); an independent triple \(T\) allows \(f_G\lor\bigwedge_{v\in T}x_v\). Both preserve the entire pair table.

**Consequence for 612.** A four-cycle-free simple graph of order at least seven always has an independent triple. Suppose otherwise. For each \(v\), its nonneighbors other than itself induce a clique. Its size is at most three since a clique of size four contains a four-cycle, so \(d(v)\ge n-4\). Any two vertices have at most one common neighbor. Double counting unordered pairs of neighbors gives \(\sum_v\binom{d(v)}2\le\binom n2\). For \(n\ge8\), \((n-4)(n-5)>n-1\), contradicting that inequality. For \(n=7\), equality requires every degree to be three, an impossibility because the degree sum would be odd. The order threshold is attained by two disjoint triangles, which also have no universal vertex.

Thus every four-cycle-free graph of order at least seven admits a monotone circuit whose pair answers all agree and whose full truth table disagrees. Even a proof of the proposed full-function gate lower bound leaves a specific missing step before Jukna's magnification could be applied: a lower bound for **all** circuits representing the pair answers, or a conversion that controls the cost of checking larger independent sets. The witness function above shows there is no unconditional identity of the two semantic requirements. It makes no claim about the optimal circuit sizes of these functions being different.
