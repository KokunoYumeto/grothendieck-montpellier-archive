## The three exact cycle targets

On the specified list of edges of $C_n$, choose an orientation $O(J)$ for every independent vertex set $J$ such that no dart points into $J$. The posted right side is the binary dimension of the direction space of the affine hull of the full $n^2$-coordinate dart indicators. Restriction to the $n$ clockwise darts is linear, so any lower bound on the dimension of those projected orientation vectors proves the bound in the posted representation. Indeed, the reverse-dart indicator is one plus the forward bit, and off-edge indicators vanish, so the affine dimensions are equal. The left side is the minimum cover size $\lceil n/2\rceil$. An alternating independent set has size $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, and no independent set on a cycle has more because its successor set is disjoint and equally large. [C5 exact statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-366.735f2406fc86.json) · [C6 exact statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-367.872bbcceba20.json) · [C7 exact statement](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/lemma/L-meta-369.5be866b60da1.json)

**C5 and C6.** The source vertices of an orientation are independent. They contain $J$, so for a maximal independent $J$ they equal $J$. Different maximal independent sets therefore require distinct orientations. In every finite graph the resulting affine hull has dimension at least $\lceil\log_2 t\rceil$ if there are $t$ maximal independent sets, since a binary $d$-flat has $2^d$ points. The five maximal pairs of $C_5$ and the two alternating triples and three opposite pairs of $C_6$ give $2^d\ge5$, hence $d\ge3$. The alternating vertices $0,2,4$ cover each cycle.

**All odd cycles, in particular C7.** Set $n=2k+1\ge3$ and index edges $e_j=(j,j+1)$ modulo $n$. For each $j$ take the independent set

$$J_j=\{j+2,j+4,\ldots,j+2k\}.$$

Only edge $e_j$ has neither end in $J_j$. Writing the clockwise orientation vector as $v_j$, its entry $j$ is some bit $t_j$. At $j+s$, $1\le s\le 2k$, the entry is one for even $s$, zero for odd $s$. The row differences in the direction space are

$$v_j+v_{j+2}=t_j e_j+e_{j+1}+(1+t_{j+2})e_{j+2},$$

where $e_i$ on the right denotes the coordinate unit vector. Column $i$ has ones in row $i-1$ and exactly one of rows $i,i-2$. This is the binary incidence matrix of a loopless multigraph on the $n$ row indices. Every row occurs because its middle entry is one. Each component therefore has at least two vertices, giving at most $k$ components. An element of the left kernel is exactly a vector constant on each component. Rank-nullity gives rank at least $n-k=k+1$. The actual affine dimension has this lower bound, proving the C7 target and all odd cycle lengths.

**All even cycles with at least six vertices.** Let $n=2k$ with $k\ge3$. Write $V\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n$ for the direction space of the projected orientations, $Q=\mathbb F_2^n/V$, and $h_i$ for the image of the $i$th coordinate vector. The two alternating independent sets force complementary orientations, so $\sum h_i=0$. Put $P_0=0$, $P_{i+1}=P_i+h_i$, with cyclic indices, and $S_i=\{P_i,P_{i+1}\}$. These sets may be singletons.

Fix any even edge index $a$ and odd edge index $b$. Write $d=(b-a)\bmod n$ as an integer from 1 to $n-1$. Take the independent vertices at positions $a+t$ for

$$t=2,4,\ldots,d-1\quad\text{or}\quad t=d+2,d+4,\ldots,n-1.$$

The only uncovered edges are $a$ and $b$, including when the indicated interval is empty. On every other edge the chosen orientation differs from the one forced by the odd vertices precisely on the open clockwise interval from $a$ to $b$. Since their difference belongs to $V$, $P_b+P_{a+1}$ is of the form $s h_a+t h_b$ with binary $s,t$. Consequently $P_{a+1}+s h_a=P_b+t h_b$, proving $S_a\cap S_b\ne\varnothing$ for every even $a$ and odd $b$.

All odd-indexed segments together cover every prefix point. Fix an even segment $S$; each of the $k$ odd segments contains at most one point outside $S$. If $k$ distinct points outside $S$ existed, each would occur in exactly one odd segment. An even segment containing two such outside points could only meet their two odd segments, contradicting $k\ge3$. Therefore every even segment other than $S$ covers at most one outside point, while $S$ covers none. The even segments also cover every prefix point, giving at most $k-1$ outside points, a contradiction. Thus all the prefix points together number at most $2+(k-1)=k+1$. They contain zero and span $Q$ because $h_i=P_i+P_{i+1}$, giving $\dim Q\le k$ and $\dim V\ge k$.

**Sharpness and the exceptional square.** For any vertex cover $S$ of any graph assign each edge an owner in $S$. Orient the edge out of its owner if that owner belongs to $J$, and into the owner otherwise. Independence ensures the edge points away from either endpoint in $J$. Each dart bit is a coordinate of the $S$-membership vector, or its complement, giving an affine family of dimension at most $|S|$. Thus the minimum affine dimension is exactly $\lceil n/2\rceil$ for every simple cycle $n\ge3$ except $n=4$. Every independent set of the square lies in one of its two bipartition classes. The two complementary orientations pointing out of those classes suffice, and both are forced, so its minimum dimension is one. The unrestricted four-cycle-free version, L-meta-390, remains open in this snapshot and does not appear in the desk pool. The duplicate rows 391–393 were retired.
