# A linear additive gap for the size of an edge-predicate circuit

[Shared proof map](FLEET_MAP.md) · [Archive crosswalk](<local>/Documents/Codex/2026-11-22/the-two-tracks-share-no-sound/outputs/SHARED_FLEET_MAP/chatgpt_archive.md)

**Cubic base graphs.** Given the edge list of a graph $H$ on $N$ labelled vertices, let $C(H)$ be the minimum number of gates in a circuit computing the single output $f_H(x)=\bigvee_{uv\in E(H)}(x_u\land x_v)$ on every Boolean input. The basis consists of binary AND, binary OR and unary NOT, all charged one gate. Approximating $C(H)$ to two-sided additive error $cN$ is NP-hard for every fixed $c<1/3872$. For the usual minimization guarantee $C(H)\le z\le r C(H)$, every fixed $r<15019/15014$ is NP-hard. The constructed graphs have degree at most three and girth at least 28.

**Source gap.** Chlebík and Chlebíková's Theorem 17 proof gives a decision promise on the *value* of a minimum cover. For each fixed sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, let $p$ be the optimal satisfied fraction of a regular instance of Max-E3-Lin-2 with $q=mk$ equations. The source distinguishes $p>1-\varepsilon$ from $p<1/2+\varepsilon$. For its fixed cubic consistency gadget $K$, put $a=M(K)/k$. Section 3 constructs $a\le202/5$ and $|V(K)|=2M(K)$. The graph $G$ has $n=q(2a+16)$ and cover number $\tau(G)=q(a+10-p)$. Thus YES gives $\tau(G)<q(a+9+\varepsilon)$ and NO gives $\tau(G)>q(a+19/2-\varepsilon)$. The proof of Theorem 20 identifies these graphs as triangle-free and cubic. Corollary 18 gives the familiar cover factor $1.0101215>100/99$. [Chlebík–Chlebíková, accepted manuscript, Theorems 2, 16, 17 and 20 and Corollary 18](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/1887750/3DM_JOURNAL_revision_old.pdf) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2005.11.029)

**Conversion.** Replace each of the $e=3n/2$ edges of $G$ by a seven-edge path with six fresh internal vertices. Then $N=10n$, distinct degree-three vertices are at distance at least seven, and every cycle has length at least $7\cdot4$. Even subdivision gives $\tau(H)=3e+\tau(G)$. The fleet's matching upper and lower bounds give $C(H)=7e+\tau(H)-1=15n+\tau(G)-1$. The exact identity uses the checked fleet results L-meta-105, 204, 288 and 290 at export `35bcffc8ae4940c0`. The cover shift is also the $k=3$ case of Chlebík and Chlebíková's subdivision theorem. [Fleet L-meta-105](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-105/dbe2df52532b.lean) · [Fleet L-meta-204](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-meta-204/1308ac8f2ec8.lean) · [Frozen fleet program](https://data.pequalnp.com/data/map/program/meta_complexity.c86fc18f7221.json) · [Chlebík–Chlebíková, Theorems 3.2 and 3.6](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/1741272/siam_final.pdf)

The upper YES threshold and lower NO threshold for $C(H)+1$ are, respectively,

$$
q(31a+249+\varepsilon),\qquad q(31a+249.5-\varepsilon).
$$

Their separation divided by $N$ is $(1/2-2\varepsilon)/(10(2a+16))$. Comparing an estimate for $C(H)+1$ with the midpoint distinguishes the cases for every fixed $c<1/3872$ by choosing a sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon$. The limiting NO/YES ratio is at least $1+(1/2)/(31(202/5)+249)=15019/15014$. Subtracting one from each positive threshold only increases the ratio. For a two-sided *multiplicative* estimate $C(H)/s\le z\le s C(H)$, the distinguishing condition is $s^2<15019/15014$. Each bound is strict at its limiting constant.

**Degree four.** The same primary proof supplies a stronger bound when maximum degree four is allowed. Here $a\le217/10$, $n=q(2a+6)$, $\tau(G)=q(a+5-p)$, $N=13n$, and $C(H)+1=20n+\tau(G)$. The two threshold densities give limiting one-sided factor $1+(1/2)/(41(217/10)+124)=10142/10137$ and additive radius $5N/12844$, again with strict inequalities. Thus $1+1/2028$ and $N/2569$ are concrete admissible hardness bounds. [Chlebík–Chlebíková, Theorem 17 proof and Corollary 18](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/1887750/3DM_JOURNAL_revision_old.pdf)

**Earlier circuit results and input model.** Jukna writes the same edge function and its cover-based single-level upper construction in §1. His Theorem 2.1 separates single-level and unrestricted-depth *monotone* circuits. Lenz and Wegener prove NP-completeness for minimizing the number of ANDs at a single AND level in monotone circuits. Arpe and Manthey's Lemma 3.1 gives an exact $|E|+\tau(G)$ for a circuit required to output *every* monomial $x_0x_ux_v$ separately, using AND gates. These primary results give precedents for the representation, cover construction and optimization approach. The present lower bound uses the fleet's all-gate theorem. In Kabanets–Cai's MCSP the input includes the complete $2^N$-bit truth table. Here the polynomial-time reduction outputs the graph's edge list, or equivalently the $O(N)$-term quadratic DNF. It gives hardness for that compact input representation.
