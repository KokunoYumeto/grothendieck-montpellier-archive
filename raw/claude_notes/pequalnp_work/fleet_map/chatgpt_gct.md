## What 086 would give

L-gct-086 asserts that one natural number C bounds every complex n×n level space L by dim L≤Cn, where per(J+t)=n! for every t∈L. Write LS(n) for the maximum dimension and κ(n) for the maximum among doubly centred level spaces. Checked L080 gives LS(n)≥2n−3 for n≥2, hence any such integer C is at least two. L092 formally gives LS(n)≤κ(n)+2n. Since κ(n)≤LS(n), a uniform linear upper bound on the doubly centred core is equivalent to the goal. Restricted classes, individual values of n, and bounds of order n² each leave this uniform problem open. ## What the Taylor-order obstruction proves

Fix n and a nonnegative real C. Suppose a fixed Ω⊆{1,…,n} has the following sufficiency property for **every** complex linear matrix space L: if Sₖ vanishes identically on L for every k∈Ω then dim L≤Cn. Put a=⌊C⌋+1 and Q=⌊n/a⌋. Then

    |Ω| ≥ ceil(Q/a),      and, when Q≥1, max Ω ≥ Q.

These are two different conclusions. The first controls the number of distinct homogeneous vanishing assumptions; the second controls the maximal degree of one such assumption. This gives a lower bound within the stated implication model. A proof using the single inhomogeneous identity per(J+t)=n! for all t∈L already imposes every order via scalar substitution. Additional structural hypotheses on L can change the implication.

**Proof of Claude's count.** For each 1≤q≤Q choose a primitive qth root ω and put aq rows into a blocks of length q. In block I the rows are ωʰcᵢ, independently varying the n component entries of each row vector cᵢ. All remaining rows are zero. The dimension is an>Cn. Moving each block cyclically one place scales the entire matrix by ω; row invariance and homogeneity give Sₖ=ωᵏSₖ. Zero remaining rows also force Sₖ=0 for k>aq. Thus Ω must meet {q,2q,…,aq} for every q≤Q. Each order k meets at most a such sets since k=jq with 1≤j≤a, giving the cardinality bound. The case q=Q gives the degree bound. Both hold without q|n. For an untruncated block family q|n, and q≥2, column centring is automatic and the doubly centred subspace has dimension (n/q)(n−1). For q=1, the full family is the entire matrix space and its doubly centred part instead has dimension (n−1)². Also S_{q+2} can survive at q=2, since 2 divides 4. For example the full family with two active pairs supported on their own disjoint pairs of columns has S₄≠0.

The hypergraph formula in Claude §1.4 follows by grouping the q assigned columns of each block and applying checked L089 for rank-one perturbations. One q-block contributes q!(1+(−1)^{q−1}∏cᵢⱼ). The first possible nonconstant order is q, with S_q=(−1)^{q−1}q!∑ᵢ e_q(cᵢ). Supporting each free row on q−1 fixed columns kills every nonconstant block term and gives a genuine level space of dimension n−n/q. Consequently the larger full family supplies counterexamples to truncated conditions; it supplies no superlinear fully level space. [089 Lean](https://data.pequalnp.com/artifacts/L-gct-089/7e034ecd113a.lean)

**Stronger count for the candidate C=2.** For q≤⌊n/3⌋ with both v₂(q) and v₃(q) even, the sets {q,2q,3q} are pairwise disjoint. The parity pair of a product jq recovers j, which then recovers q. Such q have natural density (2/3)(3/4)=1/2, by independence of the two valuations via the Chinese remainder theorem and summing the two geometric series. Hence |Ω|≥(1/6−o(1))n. At n=120, 19 disjoint triples force at least 19 orders, compared with 14 from the elementary count. More generally use q with vₚ(q) divisible by 1+⌊logₚa⌋ for every prime p≤a; residue classes of the valuations of jq then determine j∈{1,…,a}. This strengthening is a paper deduction from Claude's block family.
