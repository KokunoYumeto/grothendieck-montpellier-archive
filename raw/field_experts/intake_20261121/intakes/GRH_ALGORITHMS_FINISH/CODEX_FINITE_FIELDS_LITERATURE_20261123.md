# What Tang's factorization theorem already gives for prime-degree fields

The 23 November 2026 overview describes the cofactor construction in the 159-page finishing manuscript. Qiyue Tang had explicitly stated the same field-construction output, with a stronger uniform time bound, on 6 November. The manuscript itself attributes that result to Tang in Theorem 1.8 (p. 8) and gives the reduction in §21.3 (p. 66). Tang's title alone understates the overlap. [Tang, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 4.1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00135v2)

**The output.** Given binary \(q\) promised to be a prime power, Tang constructs a monic irreducible polynomial presenting the field of order \(q\) in deterministic time polynomial in \(\log q\). Set \(q=p^r\). Its bit length is of order \(r\log p\), so the result covers every prime degree, with no cyclotomic cofactor restriction. It is an unconditional theorem as asserted in the preprint. Tang's proof uses OpenAI's auxiliary-prime factoring algorithm and a Dedekind zeta zero-free theorem of Cureton and Thorner. Tang reports a repair to the written proof of their Lemma 8.1. These are dependencies, and this comparison does not certify them. A second deduction combines Shoup's 1990 reduction with the uniform prime-field factoring theorem of OpenAI, whose proof uses its companion Hecke zero-free theorem. [Tang, Remarks 1.2–1.3](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00135v3), [Cureton and Thorner](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00200v2), [Shoup, Theorem 3.1](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf), [OpenAI, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Deterministic-Polynomial-Factorization-over-Prime-Fields-October-4-2026)

**What both proofs do.** For odd \(r\ne p\), both start from an element of order \(r\), lift it through repeated \(r\)-th roots to maximal \(r\)-power order and use it to construct an extension. Tang factors to obtain a field component and its roots. The finishing manuscript works in the formal algebra \(\mathbb F_p[Z]/\Phi_r\) and can extract roots over all components without first being supplied a component. A field presentation also gives an equivariant maximal-order unit via Lenstra's construction, as §3 of the manuscript explains. Listing that unit as a second output does not establish priority for the field construction. [Tang, §4](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00135v3)

**The separate result.** In Theorem 1.1 (P144) let \(d=\mathrm{ord}_r(p)\). From \(N=\Phi_d(p)\) remove the full powers of \(2,r\) and of every odd prime \(\ell\ne r\) up to a chosen cutoff \(L\) satisfying \(r\nmid\ell-1\). Write \(C_L\) for the residue. The claimed bit bound is \((r+L+C_L+\log p)^c\), with absolute \(c\). Both \(L\) and \(C_L\) are charged at their numerical values. The general Theorem 5.1 (P145) also allows selected primes for which roots of unity are supplied. In its root-free step the auxiliary algebra has rank \(\ell-1\). Once the proof obtains \(y^r=z\) there, its norm \(b\) satisfies \(b^r=z^{\ell-1}\). If \(u(\ell-1)+vr=1\), then \((b^u z^v)^r=z\) in the original algebra. The difficult input to this identity is finding compatible roots across the algebra at the asserted cost. Tang's proof takes a different route and supplies no such algebraic bound independent of the recent zero-free results.

**The closest classical comparison.** Bach, von zur Gathen and Lenstra's Theorem 3 (printed p. 7) uses a presented field and primitive prime-order roots for every prime dividing the cyclotomic value. Their Theorems 1–2 give the associated factorization and root-of-unity reductions with their specified auxiliary data. Sze charges a numerical residual in a root algorithm for \(q-1\). Ivanyos, Karpinski, Rónyai and Saxena have a related congruence condition for factoring split polynomials when \(p-1\) is smooth. These sources deserve credit alongside Lenstra. I found the precise combination of the formal algebra, simultaneous extraction and the displayed input-free cyclotomic cofactor bound in the finishing manuscript. Establishing first priority for that combination would require a wider review. [Bach, von zur Gathen and Lenstra](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/2001a/art.pdf), [Sze](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.5852v1), [Ivanyos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3165)

**A check by hand and finite arithmetic.** The manuscript's example \(p=197,r=3\) has \(d=2\) and \(N=198=2\cdot3^2\cdot11\). The earlier cofactor is 11. At \(L=11\) it becomes 1 because \(3\nmid10\). The printed cubic \(X^3-3X+6\) has no root among the 197 residues, which proves its irreducibility. This illustrates exactly what changed in the cofactor; it measures no speedup over Tang.

**Suggested wording for the overview.** “Tang already obtains prime-degree field presentations in deterministic polynomial time using recent zero-free results. Here a separate algebraic construction avoids those inputs. Its cost is polynomial in the degree, the logarithm of the characteristic, a prime cutoff and the remaining cyclotomic cofactor. The construction removes eligible prime powers without requiring roots of unity as input.”
