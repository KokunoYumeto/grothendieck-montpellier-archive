## 0. The shape in one paragraph

GCT is a field in which the analytic content of RH is already free, and the open content is effectivity or
multiplicity: whether a signed quantity is a dimension.
- In the nonstandard RH (GCT VII/VIII), every realization we have of the structure constants is a sign-twisted Tate
  object, so its zeta is a product of ζ(s − j) and L(s − j, χ₋₄). Purity is then A. The remaining question is whether
  the realizations compose: the dimension ring.
- In the counting results of NEG_NIGHT the same happens on the complexity side. A places the zeros, and the hardness sits
  in a multiplicity N, or in N mod 2 read off a Hasse–Witt scalar.
- In the GL_n data of GCT (Kronecker and plethysm Hilbert series), the "zeta functions" have only root-of-unity poles,
  weight zero. What our refutations measure there is a conductor (the order of the root of unity), not a weight.
- The level-space problem of the pequalnp fleet has an Euler-product and cumulant structure, computed in §1.4.

C44_POS is the one place in our files where purity does produce positivity. The contrast with the GCT cases is
instructive (§3.2).

### 1.2 Kronecker and plethysm coefficients: GL_n data whose zeta has weight zero

**Objects.**
- k_n(s) = g((s^n), (s^n), (s^n)), the Hilbert function of C[(Cⁿ)^{⊗3}]^{SL_n³}.
- The plethysm sequences f_n of P-F31.
- Their generating series H(t) = Σ k_n(s) t^s are rational, with poles only at roots of unity.

**The functor.**
- A quasi-polynomial of period L ↔ a rational series with poles at L-th roots of unity.
- Any trace model k_n(s) = tr(A·F^s) with F of finite order ↔ a representation of Ẑ through a finite cyclic quotient: an

**What is transported.** The order of the poles, the conductor-like quantity, and the dimension any trace model needs.

  for Kronecker coefficients.
- **The mechanism of H02b.** k_n ≡ b_n mod 3, where b_n is the trace of the cyclic permutation of the three tensor

### 1.4 The level-space problem L-gct-086: an Euler product and its cumulants

**The fleet's problem.** Bound dim L for linear spaces L of n×n complex matrices with perm(J + t) = n! on L.

**What is known.**
- Writing M_k(t) for the sum of k×k subpermanents, the condition is M_k = 0 for all k ≥ 1 (their checked L-gct-084:
  perm(J + t) = Σ (n − k)!·M_k(t)).
- On doubly centred (DC) matrices: S_2 = p_2/2, S_3 = (2/3)p_3, S_4 = (3/2)p_4 − ….
  - Checked L-gct-105/106 give the first two; L-gct-134 gives the order-4 identity.
  - The pattern is ((k − 1)!/k)·p_k, from Möbius inversion on the row-slot and column-slot partition lattices
    (`pequalnp_work\fleet_map\gct.md` §5.1, `maps\algebra_combinatorics.md` AC-4).

**New in this note [here].**

1. **The permanent as a coefficient of a matrix zeta function.**

   perm(X) = [x₁⋯x_n]·det(I − diag(x)·X)^{−1} = [x₁⋯x_n]·exp(Σ_{k≥1} tr((diag(x)·X)^k)/k).

   This is MacMahon's coefficient identity, verified exactly here for n ≤ 4 on random integer matrices
   (`gct_checks\macmahon.py`, PASS). The determinant is the same coefficient of det(I + diag(x)·X). **So det_n against
   perm_n is the numerator (Λ, fermionic) against the denominator (Sym, bosonic) of one matrix zeta function**, as odd
   against even cohomology in Z(X, T) = Π det(1 − TF | H^i)^{(−1)^{i+1}}.

2. **Rook cumulants.** Put R_t(x) = Σ_k M_k(t)·x^k and c_k(t) = [x^k]·log R_t(x). Level means R_t = 1, which means all
   c_k = 0.
   - **Exponential-formula proof.**
     - k!·M_k = Σ_{π,σ} μ(π)·μ(σ)·N(π,σ), over pairs of set partitions of the k row slots and k column slots.
     - N(π,σ) factorizes over the connected components of the slot–block incidence, and the μ's are multiplicative.
     - Hence c_k = (1/k!)·Σ_{(π,σ) connected} μ(π)·μ(σ)·N(π,σ).
     - On DC matrices every term with a singleton block vanishes, because it is a row or column sum.
     - The term (1̂, 1̂) is ((k − 1)!)²/k!·p_k = ((k − 1)!/k)·p_k.
   - **Checks [here]** (`gct_checks\cumulants.py`, exact rationals, 4×4, 4×5, 5×6 DC matrices; all PASS):
     - c_2 = p_2/2 and c_3 = (2/3)p_3.
     - **24·c_4 = 36·p_4 − 18(R_4 + C_4) + 6Q.** The term 3p_2² of L-gct-134 is exactly the disconnected part M_2²/2, and the
       cumulant removes it.
     - c_k is additive on block-diagonal sums (Codex's multiplicativity of R).
     - For diagonal t: c_k = (−1)^{k−1}·p_k(d)/k, the logarithm of the Euler product Π(1 + d_i x), each cell a "prime".
     - On the barrier family F_{3,3}: M_1 = M_2 = 0 and M_3 ≠ 0. So c_k = 0 unless 3 | k, as the barrier theorem says.
   - **The barrier theorem in this language.** On the Z_q-eigenfamilies F_{q,r}, every c_k with q ∤ k vanishes. Each
     block factor Σ_a ω^{a|β|} is the orthogonality of the characters of Z/q (gct.md §5.1(c)).

**The Hodge-theoretic picture** (`pequalnp_work\fleet_map\maps\hodge_theory.md` HT-01, HT-03).
- The core K = L ∩ DC is isotropic for c_2 = p_2/2, which is positive definite on real DC matrices.
- So K ∩ K̄ = 0, and K is the H^{2,0} of a weight-2 Hodge structure: a point of the isotropic Grassmannian
  OG(k, (n − 1)²).
- The higher c_k are sections of Sym^k of the dual tautological bundle.
- The F_q families are eigenspaces of a finite-order symmetry: the Weil-type mechanism.
- **In cumulant form,** the level variety in OG is the common zero locus of the connected cumulants c_3, c_4, …,
  restricted to the core. By the computation above, c_4 restricted to the core has no p_2² term, so its form is
  (3/2)p_4 − (3/4)(R_4 + C_4) + Q/4.

### 3.2 When purity gives positivity, and when it does not

**Positive instance: C44_POS A28/A30** ([TPC], PRIMER_C44_POS §1).
- On selected closed fourfold axes, Frobenius generates a maximal torus of SO(W).
- Invariant tensors are spanned by graphs of Frobenius powers.
- Their Gram matrix is Tr_{E₀/Q}(a·ā) on the CM algebra E₀ = Q[Φ], where ā = Φ^{−1} is the adjoint. It is positive
  because all roots have absolute value 1 (Weil).
- **Purity gives Hodge positivity.**

**Negative instances: GCT.**
- At rank 4, purity holds by A for every Tate realization, but strict positivity fails (F57, INT-NSRH-4, F58, F67).
- In NEG C027, purity holds, and the multiplicity is hard.

**Dictionary between the two.**
- C44 positivity needs the relevant classes to be spanned by Frobenius-generated correspondences, so that the form is a
  CM trace form.
- In GCT the structure constants are not Frobenius-generated: they are traces of products of several operators P_i.
  Positivity is then a cone condition, not a trace-form condition.

**A point where the two meet: roots of unity.**
- C44's selector (C44-A12, A28) needs Frobenius without root-of-unity normalized eigenvalues. Potential Tate directions
  are exactly where the sign gate is open; A15 allows at most one.
- The six non-unimodal rank-3 constants live on the Gaussian line R, whose middle cohomology has normalized eigenvalues
  ±1. Its trace is 0, a "supported zero 1 + ε" (NSRH-recall §2.3, §2.6(a)).
- In both fields the failure of the naive sign sits at normalized eigenvalues that are roots of unity: in C44 as residual
  Tate classes, in GCT as the inert sheet.

### 3.4 Positivity as a signature, the real case

**Level spaces.**
- On real DC matrices c_2 = |t|²/2 > 0. So real cores vanish, and real level spaces have dim ≤ 2n − 2 (their checked
  L-gct-158).
- The whole complex excess is totally non-real (gct.md §5.1(b)).

**Weil's explicit formula.**
- RH is equivalent to positivity of Weil's quadratic functional on test functions built from log ζ, the cumulants of
  the prime distribution.
- NSRH-recall §2.7 carries the Li–Toeplitz form: exactly one positive eigenvalue per section, checked to size 9
  (`reports_20261120\split_zero\consults\check_li_toeplitz_signature.py`).

**Dictionary.**
- log R_t corresponds to log ζ.
- The connected cumulants c_k correspond to prime-power terms.
- Positivity of c_2 on the real form corresponds to Weil positivity.
- The level condition (all cumulants vanish on a subspace) is a totally isotropic condition for the whole family of
  forms. Its large complex solutions are the Hodge-structure points of HT-01.
