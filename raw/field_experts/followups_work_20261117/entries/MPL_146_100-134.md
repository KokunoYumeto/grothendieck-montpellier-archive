### R03 — A finite colimit of free actions need not stay free

**Scan:** 100, “comme … Lim (finie)”; frozen page-file line 21. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the elementary counterexample; its relevance depends on which colimit and operator structure the manuscript intends.

As a test of the proposed inference, take the free $C_2$-set $C_2$ and the two equivariant maps $\mathrm{id},\tau:C_2\rightrightarrows C_2$, where $\tau$ is translation by the nonidentity element: their ordinary coequalizer is a singleton with a fixed point. Thus finite colimits alone do not preserve free actions, and the assertion needs a property of this particular diagram or a distinction between ordinary and homotopy/2-categorical colimits. This **differs from**, rather than contradicts, the structured equivariant extensions in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scans 85–88, because those pages impose extra structure and do not assert this unrestricted colimit principle.

### R06 — Reconstructing an extension from its one-coordinate quotients

**Scan:** 103, “elle est connue quand on connaît les ext. correspondantes”; frozen page-file line 4. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high under the stated factor-preservation hypothesis, because the kernel and lifting argument is explicit.

For an extension $1\to\prod_i A_i\to E\to B\to1$ whose kernel factors are each preserved by conjugation, let $E_i=E/(\prod_{j\ne i}A_j)$; then $E\to\prod_B E_i$ is injective by intersection of kernels and surjective by adjusting a common lift with its separate kernel coordinates. This gives a concrete proof model for “elle est connue,” but a groupoid version must retain compatible objects and bands, and a permutation of factors requires descent from the cover where they are labelled. The claim **restates and makes more explicit** the promised fiber-product description in `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 81, while scans 80 and 85–86 explain the local-system qualification that prevents treating every coordinate as globally distinguished.

### R11 — A circle fibre need not inject into the fundamental group

**Scan:** 103, bundles on $\widehat M_G$; scan 104, vertical lattice extensions; frozen page-file line 2 and lines 27–30 respectively. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the obstruction; the actual base intended by the hats remains a reading question.

To test the passage from a torus bundle to an extension by its full lattice, use the segment $\pi_2(B)\to\mathbb Z^C\to\pi_1(E)\to\pi_1(B)\to1$: the lattice injects only when the connecting map vanishes. The Hopf bundle $S^1\to S^3\to S^2$ supplies a direct counterexample to an unconditional inference, since its total fundamental group is trivial (Hatcher, 2002, Theorem 4.41 and Example 4.45). This **adds a diagnostic example to**, and **agrees with**, `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 80, which invokes the $K(\pi,1)$ property, and scan 130, which explicitly conditions the extension description on vanishing $\pi_2$; it does not show that the manuscript's intended moduli bundle fails.


### R12 — “Cartesian” after taking fundamental groupoids needs a hypothesis

**Scan:** 104, “Par passage aux groupoïdes fondamentaux”; frozen page-file lines 25–31 and 51–54. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high, because the counterexample is computed directly from a homotopy pullback.

An unconditional rule that $\Pi_1$ preserves homotopy pullbacks fails for $*\to S^2\leftarrow *$: its homotopy pullback is $\Omega S^2$, with $\pi_1(\Omega S^2)=\pi_2(S^2)=\mathbb Z$, whereas the 2-pullback of the three fundamental groupoids is equivalent to a point. This elementary test, using the loop-space relation discussed in Hatcher (2002, §4.3), isolates the missing higher-homotopy information rather than refuting the torus-bundle squares on the page. It **adds a limitation to** the comparison with `ARCH_147_003-137_text.tex`, scan 82, and **agrees with** the asphericity qualifications of its scans 80 and 130; a proof here must use the actual fibration, base, and homotopy-cartesian structure.


### R19 — The inverse in the gluing map is structurally necessary

**Scan:** 107, orientations and antiisomorphisms; frozen page-file lines 6–17. **Kind:** own idea. **Confidence:** high for the torsor identity, because its independence of representatives can be checked explicitly.

For right $\mathbb U$-torsors $T_1,T_2$, define the map associated to a tensor class $[t_1,t_2]$ by $f(t_1u)=t_2u^{-1}$; the relation $(t_1v,t_2)\sim(t_1,t_2v)$ makes this well defined and gives the symmetric identification with the antiisomorphism torsor written on the page.
