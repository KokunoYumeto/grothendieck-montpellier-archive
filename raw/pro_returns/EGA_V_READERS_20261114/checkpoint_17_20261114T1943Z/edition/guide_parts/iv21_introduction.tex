\paragraph{IV 21, introductory prospectus (cote 25, sites 137--138).}
The separate contents sheet gives four subdivisions: invertible sheaves on a projective bundle and morphisms between projective bundles; representability of the divisor functor associated with an invertible sheaf and relative divisors on projective and multiprojective bundles; linear systems and morphisms to projective bundles; and linear systems with invertible Modules. Its introduction explicitly joins the local work of Chapter IV to the global constructions of Chapter II and some results of Chapter III. Grothendieck presents the language of linear systems as a way to organize both morphisms to projective bundles and invertible Modules on a relative prescheme. He then points toward Brauer--Séveri schemes as parameter schemes, explains their relation to projective bundles after a surjective étale extension, and refers their treatment to descent in Chapter V and to a planned Chapter VI. The cancelled phrase tentatively read as ``fibrés principaux homogènes'' before ``torseurs'' records a terminological revision on site 137. Site 138 singles out theorem 1.1 and its first corollaries in subdivisions 1 and 2: its typed replacement describes the central calculation in terms of the Picard group of a projective bundle and that of its base. These are explicit statements of the manuscript's dependencies and intended continuation.
