## Section 104: the closing page 393 (134-6/075)

The opening continuation says that Grothendieck does not know a suitable category M for which the cosheaf category Acal^M would be equivalent to Acal_ab for an arbitrary Acal satisfying the standing assumptions, even for a general topos and even for a presheaf topos. This does not explicitly reassign the N named on the preceding page392 to M; the earlier N/M mismatch identified by the main section reviewer should therefore remain a caution.

For Acal=Ahat, however, put B=A^o and Bcal=Bhat. Formula (**) identifies Acal^M with Bcal_M and then Bcal_ab when M=(Ab). This is the duality developed in D below, and compares cosheaves on Ahat with abelian sheaves on the dual presheaf topos Bhat. It should not be misreported as a general identification of cosheaves and abelian objects on the same arbitrary topos. The additional hypothesis A=B gives the special same-topos comparison. The page then examines the simplest case A final: Acal is Sets, Acal_ab is Ab, and cocontinuous endofunctors of Ab correspond to objects L by the functor F ↦ L tensor F. Both the external action (22) and the operation (23) become the ordinary tensor product of abelian groups under this comparison. The compatibility for literal A=A^o, described as a sum of one-object categories associated with commutative monoids, is expressly left unchecked: “I did'nt work it out myself” is a substantive reservation.

The final paragraph clarifies a useful notation distinction. The ordinary Hom(F,K) denotes an object of N; the underlined Hom(F,K) denotes an N-valued sheaf on Acal. Correspondingly, F*L denotes an object of M, while the circled-star version denotes an M-valued cosheaf on Acal. The three marginal star/circled-star signs are present. The inline circled star combines a typed star with a pen-drawn circle; its reconstruction keeps those parts distinct.

Visual revisions on this page include typed interlinear a, (we hope !), and exterior; a handwritten a over pen-cancelled more; and the resolution of the three wave/wave/bar equivalence signs. A separate blackened mark above the typed ab in (**) remains unreadable. The partly cancelled old line, clipped n, and doubtful type layers are not suppressed.

## Section 105: free completion and duality, pages 394–396 (134-6/076–078)

Proposition3 states the free-cocompletion property of the Yoneda inclusion epsilon_A:A→Ahat. For a small A and a U-category M admitting small direct limits, restriction gives the equivalence (33), Hom_!(Ahat,M)→Hom(A,M). Its quasi-inverse sends i to i_!, with i_!(F)=colim over a in A/F of i(a), formula (35). The right adjoint is i*(x)=(a ↦ Hom(i(a),x)), formula (36), which also establishes the cocontinuity of i_!. With ordinary natural transformations this assignment reverses arrows. The main section reviewer has independently checked that page385 explicitly uses an outer opposite in its corresponding Hom equivalences, so a missing-opposite qualification belongs in the guide. The reader retains (37) literally. The extra cancelled cell in corollary and the unreadable lower mark beneath Ahat are transcription matters, not mathematical amendments.

Page395 interprets (33) as 2-universality: Ahat is obtained from A by adjoining arbitrary direct limits while disregarding those that may perchance already exist in A. Perchance is typed above the line, and its placing curve does not cancel already. The corrected source restores the live word. Corollary1 gives the dual statement (38) for a category N admitting small inverse limits. The map is F ↦ F composed with epsilon_A^o; the second F and the composition circle are present, although the witness drops them.

For the topos Acal=Ahat, the left side of (33) is the category of M-valued cosheaves and is equivalent to Hom(A,M). The left side of (38) is the category of N-valued sheaves and is equivalent to Hom(A^o,N), that is, N-valued presheaves on the chaotic site A. This is presented as a special case of the familiar independence of the sheaf category from a chosen generating site.

When M admits both kinds of small limits, take B=A^o and combine the two equivalences to obtain (39), delta_A^M: Hom_!(Ahat,M)→Hom^!(Bhat^o,M). Corollary2 identifies M-valued cosheaves on Ahat with M-valued sheaves on Bhat. The word canonically was cancelled in the final sentence on395; page396 immediately explains the construction through diagram (40), via Hom(A,M)=Hom(B^o,M), and the choice of a quasi-inverse to its second downward equivalence. The latter may be chosen through small inverse limits in M. This dependency should be retained when explaining the canonical character of the comparison.

The Remark on396 explicitly invokes the tautological opposites in (6), p385. With N=M^o, the dual of delta_A^M is canonically quasi-inverse to delta_B^N. ## Section 105: the two pairings, pages 396–398 (134-6/078–080)

Case1, M=Sets, begins on396. By proposition1, the right side of (39) identifies with Bhat, giving (41): Bhat→Hom_!(Ahat,Sets). This is equivalently the canonical pairing (42), delta_A:Ahat×Bhat→Sets, separately preserving small direct limits. The opening Case label is hand-underlined and has a placing curve whose left hook was wrongly treated as a cancelled opening parenthesis in the draft.

Page397 obtains the symmetric equivalence (41') Ahat→Hom_!(Bhat,Sets). Each presheaf topos is therefore equivalent to the category of set-valued cosheaves on the other. This is the sense in which the pair is called dual. Formula (43) introduces F*G for delta_A(F,G). Twofold application of proposition3 gives (44), identifying separately cocontinuous bifunctors on Ahat×Bhat with functors on A×B. Consequently the pairing is determined by its values on representables. Under the stated convention that b^o is b viewed in the opposite category, formula (45) reads a*b^o=Hom_A(b,a)=Hom_B(a^o,b^o). Its symmetry extends to the bifunctorial isomorphism F*G≈G*F, with the operation on the right taken for the swapped pair (B,A). The two occurrences use different underlying pairings, not an undeclared symmetry inside a fixed pair of unrelated categories. Formula (47), on398, gives the representable evaluation rules a*G≈G(a) and F*b^o≈F(b), using the object-identification conventions already stated on397.

In modern explanatory terms the separately cocontinuous extension can be recognized from its representable values; the source itself does not introduce coend terminology here. Any such explanation should be presented as an interpretation of the universal property, not as additional manuscript wording.

The Remarks on398 postpone further use of this set-valued duality in order to return to abelianization and additive/abelian coefficient categories. They nevertheless sketch the opposite-variance pairing (48), more accurately a bi-sheaf, on Ahat^o×Bhat^o. It is obtained by the dual of (44) from the co-pairing A^o×B^o=B×A sending (b^o,a) to Hom(b,a). The final arrow is an ordinary arrow on the image, without a mapsto bar. Equivalently there are canonical mutually adjoint contravariant functors between the two presheaf categories.

The two pairings are then proposed as the cross-Hom sets in a category E(A), the union of the two full subcategories Ahat and Bhat^o with common A. These are described as adjoining small direct and small inverse limits respectively. The relationship between the pairings is expressed through the composition and associativity in E(A); the symmetry becomes E(A)^o≈E(B). The page sketches this organization and points forward, without supplying all composition details here. The upper pencil note reads other description (48) via a cross, cf397, with no separate of. The lower note has a cross and cf p.40[4|9]-405; its disputed digit is preserved as a scoped doubt. These later pointers should be cross-referenced if the subsequent discussion gives the promised details.
