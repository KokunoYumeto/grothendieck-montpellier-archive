## 1. R3 against his text

**His words (scan 45; checked on the scan).** He needs, for the corrected condition c'), that "pour tout sous-groupe ouvert `E'` d'un `E_K`, le centralisateur dans `E_K` (non seulement de `E'` lui-même, mais même de `E'°`) est réduit à 1 — ce qui résulte de la démonstration du th. 1, et du fait* que pour tt sous-groupe ouvert `Γ'` de `Γ = Γ_Q`, le centralisateur (non seulement de `Γ'`, mais même) de `Γ'°` dans `Γ` est réduit à (1)", margin "* à vérifier !".

**Verdict.** R3 is this starred fact, with the same quantifier (every open `Γ'`, that is every number field `k`, `Γ'° = Gal(Q̄/k(μ_∞))`). The sketch of round 2 holds. I went through it line by line; one supplement is needed and one step should be said.
- Supplement: the prime `p` must be unramified in `k` as well as split in `k'`; only then is the value group of `F = k(μ_∞)` at a place over `p` equal to `(1/(p−1)) Z[1/p]`. Such `p` exist (Chebotarev for `k'`, finitely many ramified primes in `k`).
- To be said: `a` is not an ℓ-th power in `F` (its valuation 1 is not divisible by ℓ in that value group, since ℓ does not divide `p(p−1)`), so that `F(a^{1/ℓ}) = F(σ(a)^{1/ℓ})` gives `σ(a) = a^j c^ℓ` with `j` prime to ℓ.
- The rest stands: `σ` centralizing `G_F` gives `F(σx) = F(x)` for all `x`; at a place over `𝔭` the valuations give `0 ≡ j` modulo ℓ; the same over every finite extension of `F`, each of the form `k''(μ_∞)`.

**What it settles in his text.**
- His claim for `E_K`: the image in `Γ` of the centralizer of `E'°` centralizes `Γ'°`, hence is trivial; the centralizer then lies in `π_K` and centralizes the open subgroup `E' ∩ π_K`; Théorème 1 finishes. The passage from the groups of the models to the limit `π_K` is not in his text (an open subgroup of the limit comes from a finite level, and the transition maps are onto).
- Scan 127: "(en vertu de `Centr_Γ(Γ⁰) = (1)`)" is the same fact for `Γ = 𝓔_K`.

## 2. R2 against his text

**His words.** Scan 55 (checked): "(3) `π^{Γ°} = L_i` (donc `π^Γ = (1)`, d'ailleurs) — résultat qui me paraît hautement plausible", margin "À énoncer parmi les conjectures-clefs…". Scan 61: "`π^{Γ°} ⊂ Norm_π(L_i) = L_i`".

**Verdict.** R2 is the statement of these four places, for curves over number fields, and it proves them for every section without Conjecture A: the two cases are "normalizes no loop group" and "normalizes `L_i`", not "comes from a point" and "comes from a cusp". Differences from his text:
- he has `K` of finite type over `Q`; R2 is for number fields. The extension needs the finiteness of the torsion of abelian varieties over `K'(μ_∞)` for finitely generated `K'`, which follows from Ribet's theorem by specialization at a closed point of a model (re-derived; unchecked);
- his extension (2) of scan 54 is written with "centralisateur `Z(L_i)`"; the group that maps onto `Γ` is the normalizer;
- R2 holds for germs of sections (his `Γ'` open), and for `Centr_π(s(Γ''))`, `Γ''` open in `Γ°`.

**The sketch, step by step (re-derived).**
- (1), (2): hold as written. For `z ≠ 1` in `C`, `N` characteristic open with `z ∉ N`, `H = N⟨z⟩`: the open subgroup of `Γ` acting trivially on `π/N` normalizes `H` through `s`; the class of `z` in `H^ab` is fixed by an open subgroup of `Γ°`; in the proper case `H^ab` is a Tate module without such invariants (Ribet), so `z ∈ N`, a contradiction.
- (3): holds, and the "weight criterion" it quotes can be proved in a few lines in the form needed. If the class of `z` in `(N⟨z⟩)^ab` lies in the image of the inertia groups for every `N`, then in the cyclic group `N⟨z⟩/N` the stabilizers of the cusps of `U_N` generate; subgroups of a cyclic ℓ-group form a chain, so the ℓ-part of `z` fixes a cusp of each `U_N`, hence (inverse limit of finite non-empty sets) a cusp of `Ũ`; the ℓ-parts commute, and a non-trivial element of an inertia group has that group as centralizer. This is the argument of the proof of Stix's Theorem 20.
- (4): **gap as written.** It takes an open `H ⊇ C` "with at least four cusps in which `x` and `y` are distinct". If one cusp is a `C`-translate of the other, no such `H` exists; and four cusps are not needed. Two repairs. (I) `s(Γ°)` fixes every cusp `c` with `C ∩ I_c ≠ 1` and then centralizes `I_c`; so it suffices that `s(Γ°)` fixes at most one cusp, which is the uniqueness of the cusp of a cuspidal section over the base field `F = K(μ_∞)` (Stix, Theorem 14 (1) and the end of its proof); its hypothesis holds over `F` because `A(F')` has no non-trivial divisible element (the Kummer class of a point restricts injectively from a number field to `F'`, the kernel being `H¹` of a group with coefficients in `T(A)^{G_{F'}} = 0`). (II) Directly: the set `Φ` of cusps fixed by `s(Γ°)` is `C`-stable and `C` is the union of the `I_c`, `c ∈ Φ`; if `Φ` were one `C`-orbit with more than one point, `C` would be the union of the conjugates of a proper closed subgroup, impossible in a profinite group; so there are `c_1, c_2 ∈ Φ` in different `C`-orbits, an open `H ⊇ C` separating them, with at least three cusps (automatic in genus 0; for genus ≥ 1 because `C` dies in the Tate module of the Jacobian, so there are infinitely many `C`-orbits of cusps); with generators `z, z'` of `I_{c_1}, I_{c_2}`, the classes of `z z'` and `z² z'` in `H^ab` must each be a multiple of the class of one cusp, which forces `[z] = 0`.
- (5): holds. `Γ°` is normal, so `s(Γ)` normalizes `C = I_c`; conversely a section normalizing `I_c` acts on it through `χ`.

**Where it is in the literature (texts opened today).** R2 is Theorem 20 (attributed to Nakamura), Corollary 22 and Theorem 14 of Stix's paper, applied over the base field `K(μ_∞)`: there the cyclotomic character is trivial, "cyclotomically normalized by the image of the section" means "centralized by `s(Γ°)`", and Lemma 19 of the paper says that such a field distinguishes tori from abelian varieties, by Ribet. (The lemma is worded for "a number field `k`" with `kQ^ab/Q^ab` finite; this only makes sense for algebraic extensions of `Q`; to be checked against the published version.) The paper does not state the centralizer formula. The transcriber's notes on scan 45 (B045.1) say that Stix gives the cyclotomic action on inertia "without proving that every nontrivial fixed subgroup is the entire inertia group": with Theorem 20 over `K(μ_∞)` it does. Round 2's "Novelty: none claimed" stands, and can now be said with the reference.
