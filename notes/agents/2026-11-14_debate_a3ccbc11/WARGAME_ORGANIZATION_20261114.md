### 2.3 How a companion is built so that it can absorb later results

What the user has fixed (`PLAN_RECORD.md`): one book, his text in it and built out ("You take his text, then you
build it out", Q107; "all in one file", Q93; not two parts, Q103); a development, not a literature review (Q112);
"making it clear, sort of who wrote which parts" (Q30); "a very sane fucking volume of actual readable shit"
(Q34); iterations with an end (Q117); and now "provisional, modulo new findings", able to absorb "without
necessarily looking to do so infinitely" (P5041). Not fixed by the user: the title, what "corrected" means for
notes, how his text and the development are cut into units.

My proposal, within that **(mine)**:
1. **The unit is a block attached to a passage of his text** (section and scan), with a stable id. A block has one
   shape: the statement; its status (proved, with the referee named; claim of a run, with the row; open); the proof
   or a pointer to the published return; who wrote it; the date. Long computations and certificates stay in the
   returns and are cited.
2. **The register drives the book.** The consolidated register that round 2 produces is the list of blocks. When a
   row changes state, its block is replaced and nothing else moves. A new result with a passage in this volume
   becomes a new block there; one without goes to its home and leaves a pointer.
3. **Versions with a date.** Version 1 says "state of" its day and carries a page of what changed. That is what
   makes it provisional and still readable.
4. **The stop.** Each topic ends with its open rows. A further prompt is written only for a row on that list that
   an expert marks as a main question. That is the user's "clear goal from the get-go".
5. **A status line on every statement**, so that a reader sees at once what is refereed. Only refereed rows are
   called theorems.
