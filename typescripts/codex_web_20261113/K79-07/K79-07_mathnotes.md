# Mathematical notes · cote 79, scans 97–112

## What the pages do

**Scans 97–98, (56)–(60).** For a free rank-two module over $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, with $n\ne2,4$, Grothendieck returns to the structures $P_i$ on one vertex set $S$. He takes the commutant $Z$ of the direct automorphism group. Formula (57) compares the edges $\{s,t\}$ and $\{\lambda s,\lambda^{-1}t\}$ and excludes $t=\lambda s$ when the pair is an edge. Formula (58) keeps $s'$ fixed and tests $\{s',\lambda^{-1}.t'\}$ to define the new edge set. The resulting map $\lambda\mapsto\lambda.i_0$ identifies the family $\Pi$ as a torsor.

**Scans 98–100, (61)–(67).** To recover the quotient of the units by sign from a single structure, he compares the stars of the same vertex in two structures. Each star carries a torsor action. Choosing orientations identifies the induced map of operator groups with a unit $\gamma$. Changing one orientation changes its sign. Passing to the quotient gives a candidate map $Z\to Z'_\Lambda$. The two marginal checks concern the passage to operator groups and the canonicity of the resulting map.

**Scans 100–102, (68)–(74).** The oriented structures form a two-sheeted cover of $\Pi$. A point consists of a structure and an orientation. For a unit $\lambda$, the target index comes from its class modulo sign. The two possible target orientations give opposite coefficients in (71). Equality $\lambda=\lambda_{e'}$ in (73) selects the target orientation. An oriented face then determines a point of the cover.

**D1 · Scans 103–104, VII, (1)–(9).** A triangular structure consists of a vertex set $S$ and a family $F$ of three-element subsets. T1 requires the faces to cover $S$. T2 requires each edge to lie in exactly two faces. These incidences define a strict graph whose vertices are the faces and whose edges are the original edges. Its connected components index the two partitions $F=\coprod F_i$ and $A=\coprod A_i$. Consecutive faces in a connecting chain share an edge.

**Scans 105–106, (10)–(16).** The three incidence relations pair vertices with edges, edges with faces, and vertices with faces. At a vertex $s$, the adjacent vertices and the incident faces give a combinatorial contour $P(s)$. The partitions of edges and faces induce its decomposition into $P_i(s)$. This explains why connectedness at $s$ imposes a condition on the nonempty pieces.

**Scans 106–107, (17)–(19).** T3 requires every component to cover the same vertex set and to have connected contours. The two chain conditions in (18) express reachability of a face meeting a specified vertex and connectivity by faces all containing that vertex. The data are then expressed as a family of sets of triangular faces on $S$. A marginal note singles out the tetrahedron.

**D2 · Scans 107–109, (20)–(27).** He fixes one member $P_0$ of the family, defines its repères as ordered triples over triangular faces, and introduces regularity and orientability. The subgroup $G_0^+$ acts trivially on the set of orientations. The commutant $Z'$ of this group in the permutations of $S$ is identified with a normalizer quotient after a base vertex is chosen. Two marginal revisions consider using the full automorphism group at this stage.

**Scans 109–110, (27)–(30).** The stabilizer of a vertex is described as a rank-one module. Its intrinsic description retains the orientation torsor $\mu_0$. A character $\chi_s$ records the action of $Z'$ on the stabilizer. The announced formula $\chi_s(\dot\lambda)=\lambda^2$ leads him to seek another construction of the quotient of units by sign.

**Scans 110–111, the two occurrences of (31) and (32 bis).** A combined axiom at the foot of scan 110 is crossed out. The live (31) on scan 111 asserts nonadjacency of $s$ and $\lambda s$ and gives a triangle argument using commutation with a direct automorphism. The edge equivalence becomes a separate axiom (32). He explains how the forward implication recovers the reverse and why one base repère suffices for the test.

**Scan 112, (33)–(34).** Choose $\rho$ with $\rho s_0=t_0$. An element $g$ of the normalizer represents the displacement of $s_0$, while $u$ carries the base edge to the candidate edge. Substituting $u=gz$ puts the test in terms of a stabilizer element $z$ and conjugation by $\rho^{-1}$. The drawing and upper margin specify geometric choices of $\rho$. The last sentence starts a further group-theoretic description and breaks off at “avec des”.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence · Scan 110, (30).** At $n=8$ the two unit classes modulo sign are $\{1,7\}$ and $\{3,5\}$. All four representatives have square 1 modulo 8. This finite computation gives an instance of the information lost by the square map. The identification of the manuscript character with this map is the author’s announced result A7.

**R2 · Model, high confidence · Scan 107, tetrahedron margin.** On four labelled vertices there are four possible triangular faces and 15 nonempty families of them. Checking T1 and T2 on all 15 leaves only the family with all four faces. Every pair of its faces shares an edge, so the face graph has one component. This checks the small case mentioned in the margin. The capital or lowercase reading in S4 still depends on the ink.

**R3 · Model, high confidence · Scan 111, below (32).** In the author’s reverse-direction substitution, the first restored entry is $t$ and the second is $s$. Comparing the two printed underlabels with the substituted pair confirms that order. The braces make it the same edge.

**Reading question R4 · Model, medium confidence · Scan 97 L34, (58).** The frozen wording is “structure 2-polyèdre triangulée”. In an enlarged crop the word after the hyphen appears to have a further ascender near its end. The reading “2-polyédrale triangulée” deserves consideration. The loop of the last two strokes is compressed. This question arose during the mathematical notes.
