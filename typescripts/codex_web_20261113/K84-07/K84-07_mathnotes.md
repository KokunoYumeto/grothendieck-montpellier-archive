## What the pages do

**Scans 97–98. The sign in the extension diagram.** The author evaluates $g(x)$ on the submodule $L$ and then takes $x$ in $L$. In the second calculation the induced quotient map sends $\lambda$ to $-\lambda x$. Both checks end in “OK.” He splits $E$ as $L\oplus\mathcal O.x_0$, writes the associated forms in these coordinates and boxes $Q_N(\varphi_N(x))=-N(x)\delta_N$. The arrow labelled $-\mathrm{id}_L$ is what he asks the reader to track.

**Scans 99–100. A map in the reverse direction.** The identifications involving $E_i$, $A_i$ and the duals of $L_i$ lead to $i:E\longrightarrow E_1\otimes E_2$, “une sorte de transposé” of the product. A cancelled construction on scan 99 tests compositions with inclusions. Scan 100 returns to two $\chi$-trivialised torsors. For $\chi=2$ it describes quadratic covers $X_1,X_2$ of $X$ and a map from their product to $X_1\wedge X_2$. The last lines say that the transposed module map also has meaning without the cover structures or the condition $\chi=2$.

**Scans 101–102. Construction of the trace.** The first square on scan 101 constructs the extension $A$ from an extension by $A_1\otimes_0 A_2$ and a map $\tau$ obtained from the traces of its two components. The author asks for maps whose composites agree. On scan 102 the proposed operation sends $\xi$ to $\xi+\bar\xi$, with conjugation induced by $\sigma_1\otimes\sigma_2$. On an element from $A_1$ he gets $\mathrm{Tr}_1(x_1)e_1\otimes e_2$ and writes “C'est OK.” A note then relates the image of $A$ to the fixed elements under a hypothesis on $X_0=V(2.1_X)$.

**Scans 103–104. Coordinates and the norm to be constructed.** The author computes the product of the basis generators as $U_1*U_2=U+b_1b_2$. He asks for a quadratic map on $A_1\otimes A_2$ with values in $A$. Its proposed value “serait, essentiellement, $z\bar z$, mais ‘remonté’ à $A$”. He describes its polar form using the trace. With the chosen bases he gives $b=b_1b_2$, $c=b_1^2c_2+b_2^2c_1-4c_1c_2$ and the image $2U_1U_2+b_1U_2+b_2U_1$ of $U$. The lower part of scan 104 returns to the product on the two individual factors.

**Scans 105–107. The proposition and the four conjugates.** For $A=A_1\wedge A_2$, part 1 gives a trace whose composite with $i$ is $\xi\mapsto\xi+\bar\xi$. Part 2 asserts a unique quadratic norm with prescribed values on pure tensors and prescribed polar form. Scan 106 gives its conjugation identities and the composition rules for relative norms and traces down to $\mathcal O$. For the norm rule, the author groups four conjugates of $\xi$. The two pairs become $N$ and $\bar N$ on scan 107, where the argument closes with $N\bar N=N_{A/\mathcal O}(N)$ and “OK.”

**Scans 107–109. Trace and norm as data over a ring.** A $k$-algebra $A$ is to behave like a quadratic algebra. The text explicitly allows $\alpha:k\to A$ to fail to be injective. It specifies a relative trace $t$ and norm $n$, relates $t$ to the polar form of $n$ at $1$, and defines $\tau=\alpha t$ and $\bar f=\tau(f)-f$. Scan 108 computes the double conjugate and spells out the multiplicativity required of conjugation. Scan 109 substitutes $x=y+z$ in the norm relation, then asks that the resulting addition formula for $n$ hold even when $\alpha$ is not injective.

**Scan 110. The definition.** “Récapitulons le formulaire” puts the requirements on $t$, conjugation and $n$ into five numbered conditions. The words “satisfaisant 1° à 5°” specify what the author calls an “algèbre quasi-quadratique sur $k$”. A sixth condition, labelled “facultatif”, expresses trace and norm compatibility over a further base $k_0$ when the two local ranks are $n$ and $2n$.

**Scans 111–112. Three intermediate algebras.** The data include three $A_i$ over $k$, maps $\alpha_i:A_i\to A$, and relative traces and norms $T_i,N_i$ from $A$ to $A_i$. The proposed formulation with three involutions at the foot of scan 111 is crossed out. Scan 112 takes one of six squares. It asks for the restriction of a relative trace to agree with the trace on the other intermediate algebra, and sets down the corresponding norm condition. A note beside the tensor square discusses the kernel of $A_1\otimes A_2\to A$. The author derives the restriction of $\sigma_2$ to the image of $A_1$.

## Remarks of the model

- **M108-POLY · Mine, high confidence.** The displayed identity for $2xy$ on scan 108 follows from expanding $(\tau(x)-x)(\tau(y)-y)$ and using the stated multiplicativity of conjugation. A symbolic expansion of the difference between the two rearrangements gave zero. The calculation uses commutativity and never divides by 2, which accounts for the author’s qualification in the next paragraph.
- **M108-NORM · Mine, high confidence.** At the foot of scan 108, substituting $\lambda=t(x)$ into $n(x)+\lambda^2-\lambda t(x)$ gives $n(x)$ exactly. The same clean formula for $\lambda$ appears at the right end of the line, independently of the smaller overwritten gloss below the equality.
- **M105-TRACE · Mine, medium confidence.** On scan 105 the proposition constructs the relative trace from the extension and then gives its composite with $i$. On scan 112 the square asks for compatibility with passage through $A_1\otimes A_2\to A$. This would require $t_1\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A_2}$ to vanish on the kernel of that map, as the adjacent note appears to say. The targets in the square are legible, though several words in the note are uncertain.
- **Mine, low confidence.** The crossed cyclic products on scan 111 suggest an earlier way of expressing the compatibility sought on scan 112. Both sheets use the same three indexed involutions. The cancellation leaves open how much of that formulation the author intended to retain.
