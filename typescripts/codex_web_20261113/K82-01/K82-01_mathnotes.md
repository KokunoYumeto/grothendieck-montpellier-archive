# Mathematical notes · K82-01 · scans 2–16

## What the pages do

**U01 · Duality and volume, scans 3–4.** The starting data are paired rank-three modules $E,E'$ over $S$. Wedge product pairs a vector with a bivector into the determinant line. A generating section $\omega$ of $\det E$, with its dual $\omega'$, gives the two identifications $\alpha:\bigwedge^2E\to E'$ and $\alpha':\bigwedge^2E'\to E$. The cyclic pairs $23,31,12$ specify the images of the barred basis vectors.

**U02 · From a bilinear form to a bracket, scan 4.** The triangular diagram identifies forms on $E$, maps $f:E\to E'$, and alternating algebra structures on $E'$. The direction of evaluation matters. Formula (11) pairs $y$ with $f(x)$ to give $\varphi(x,y)$. Formula (12) then applies $f\alpha'$ to $x'\wedge y'$.

**U03 · Coordinate Jacobi calculation, scans 5–7.** Scan 6 introduces $\varphi_{ij}$ and the matrix with diagonal $a,b,c$ and off-diagonal pairs $p,p'$, $q,q'$ and $r,r'$. Scan 5 gives the three cyclic double brackets, their sum, the three vanishing conditions and a description by minors. They appear in reverse order of calculation in the archive. The proposition on scan 7 takes symmetric $\varphi$ as a sufficient condition for the Lie identity.

**U04 · The example $xy+z^2$, scan 7.** The polar form has off-diagonal $xy$ entries 1 and last diagonal entry 2. With $X=e'_1$, $Y=e'_2$ and $H=e'_3$, the three bracket relations are $[H,X]=X$, $[H,Y]=-Y$ and $[X,Y]=2H$. The notes identify these as the familiar relations for the integral Lie algebra of GP(1).

**U05 · Discriminant and special orthogonal data, scans 8–9.** Equations (27)–(32) pass from the discriminant of a bilinear form to the divided discriminant of a ternary quadratic form. The pair $(q,\omega)$ is required to satisfy (33). The NB gives a sign convention in ranks $2n$ and $2n+1$ and describes the allowable choices, with varying base, as a torsor under $\mu_2$.

**U06 · The reverse construction, scans 9–10.** Interchanging $E$ and $E'$ permits another form and bracket. Starting with $f$, scan 10 actually produces the reverse map $f'=\alpha'\circ\bigwedge^2f\circ\alpha^{-1}$. Its entries are expressed by the nine minors. Symmetry of the first form gives symmetry of the second one. Formula (40 bis) writes the new bracket on $E$ directly in terms of $f(x)\wedge f(y)$.

**U07 · Compositions, ranks and rescaling, scans 11–12.** Formula (42) puts the scalar $\Delta$ in the two compositions of $f$ and $f'$. The ensuing paragraph compares $f'=0$ with the vanishing of the order-two minors. The text then varies the volume, and separately scales the form by $\mu$ with volume fixed. In the latter case the brackets on $E'$ and $E$ scale by $\mu$ and $\mu^2$, respectively. The assignments of the letters $E,E'$ in the prose change between the sheets.

**U08 · Iteration, scans 12–13.** A second application gives $f''$ and the scalar $\Delta'$. The notes first work where $\Delta$ is a unit, then appeal to polynomial identities to extend the formulas. They arrive at $\Delta'=\Delta^2$ and $f''=\Delta f$. The last paragraph singles out $\Delta=1$. Scan 14 returns to the model that can specialize to characteristic two.

**U09 · The bracket homomorphism, scans 14–15.** Formula [49] says that $f$ carries the bracket on $E$ to that on $E'$. A first approach by pairing against a section is crossed out. On scan 15 the author identifies (50) with the earlier definition (40 bis). A separate NB compares $f^{-1}$ with $\Delta f^{-1}$ under the assumption that $\Delta$ is invertible. It explains why a scalar multiple of an isomorphism of the underlying modules need not respect the brackets.

**U10 · A proposed Lie action, scan 16.** The author seeks $\rho_E(x')$ on $E$ that preserves the form infinitesimally, acts by derivations, respects the bracket in $x'$ and intertwines $f$ with the adjoint action. The rotated marginal note brings in the adjoint action of $E$ on itself and adds (56 bis). The last sentence starts a transport-of-structure explanation and continues beyond scan 16.

## Remarks of the model

**M01 · My check of the coordinates, high confidence.** Polarizing $xy+z^2$ gives the displayed matrix, and its three cyclic bracket products give exactly the three relations in (26). For a matrix with six independent symmetric entries, the coordinate Jacobi sum at the three basis vectors is the zero vector. These are direct polynomial calculations. The determinant of the model matrix is $-2$. How the author intends that sign to enter $\Delta$ is a separate, low-confidence reading question because (27) has an isolated high stroke.

**M02 · My check of the divided discriminant, high confidence.** The determinant of the matrix with diagonal $2a,2b,2c$ in (29) expands to $2(4abc+pqr-ap^2-bq^2-cr^2)$. This checks the factor of 2 between (30) and (31) and gives divided discriminant $-1$ for the model in its displayed basis.

**M03 · My check of the scope of (42), high confidence for the matrix test.** In the cyclic wedge bases on scan 4, (38) gives the cofactor matrix $C(F)$ of the matrix $F$ of $f$. The two identities of (42) hold for symmetric $F$. For the determinant-one matrix $F=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, the product $C(F)F$ has upper-left block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Thus (42) needs qualification if read for every bilinear form (**C42**). A symmetric-form reading (**R42**) agrees with scan 10’s immediately preceding discussion. I have medium confidence that the author meant to carry that hypothesis into the remarks. Independently, the polynomial identities $C(C(F))=\det(F)F$ and $\det C(F)=\det(F)^2$ hold for an arbitrary three-by-three $F$.

**M04 · My check of the last NB, high confidence for the counterexample.** Read as a claim about every endomorphism satisfying (52), the implication to (53) fails (**C52**). Reduce the displayed model $xy+z^2$ to $\mathbf F_2$ and take the identity endomorphism of $E$. It satisfies (52), because the two terms cancel. Here $[e_1,e_2]_{\varphi'}=e_3$. The left side of the derivation identity is $e_3$ and the right side is zero. The prospective assertion for the particular operators $\rho_E(x')$ (**R52**) could use additional information about those operators. The sheet ends before it gives the argument.

**M05 · My check of the two bracket maps, high confidence for the examples.** The coordinate version of [49] is a polynomial identity for a general matrix $F$. For the matrix of $xy+z^2$ over the rationals, the reverse map fails already on $e'_1,e'_2$. The two comparison vectors have third coordinates $-2$ and $4$. This directly illustrates the qualification in the NB of scan 15.
