# K135-03 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 32 and 38 · Iterating $\Omega$.** The large diagrams repeat strips of objects and maps under successive applications of $\Omega$. Each sheet encloses part of a diagram with a stepped outline. Scan 32 also sets out the chain beginning $Q\leftarrow P\leftarrow P\times\Omega Q$, several products of doubly indexed $X$'s and a row ending in a question mark. On 38 the smaller diagram with $F,G,\Phi$ and their primed neighbours precedes an indexed array that repeats three rows at each shift. The intermediate cancellations on both sheets leave individual nodes uncertain.

**Scan 33 · A non-strict Picard example.** Over a commutative ring $A$ with $2.1_A\ne0$, the objects are graded modules with a one-element basis given up to sign. The page writes the sign-stable pair as $e_L$, imposes $\varphi(e_L)=e_M$ on the graded isomorphisms, and describes the basis of the graded tensor product. Commutativity uses the “signe de Koszul”. An alternative description combines discrete $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ and a category of torsors.

**Scan 33 · A canceled torsor construction.** The lower block introduces an abelian sheaf $F$ on a topos $S$, a discrete group $\Gamma$ and a map $\Gamma\times\Gamma\to H^0(S,F)$. It places a discrete factor beside $\operatorname{Tors}(F)$ and tensors pairs by $(\alpha,P)\otimes(\beta,Q)=(\alpha\beta,P\otimes Q)$. The associativity diagram compares the two parenthesizations of three pairs. The entire proposal, including its explanation of associativity, is crossed out.

**Scan 36 · Fibres, loops and suspensions.** The upper ladder runs through $A\to B\to C\to D$, fibres marked by $\Gamma$ and its following map letters, and rows involving $\Omega$ and $\Sigma$. Two shorter ladders examine parts of this pattern. The central square has $p,q$ out of $Z$ and a diagonal $r$. A list headed “Conditions équivalentes si $S$ connexe” relates maps between fibres to a lifting condition on $T$ and to a product. The bracketed NB explicitly qualifies uniqueness. A number of trial squares follow.

**Scan 36 · Distinguished sequences and functors.** Two circled notes propose data for a distinguished sequence beginning $\Omega S\to F\to X$ and an axiomatic treatment of squares. On the right a functor out of $D$ is associated with finite limits, a map to a slice and an expression for $\operatorname{Hom}(\varphi,\psi)(S)$. The final two NBs concern values in a category with a tilde and a further Hom identity involving $i_F$. The subscript to the first Hom and some words in the conditions remain doubtful.

**Scans 34 and 37 · Other material.** Scan 34 carries a canceled French poem. Scan 37 carries the printed Five Tigers engraving and a 1974 calendar imprint. Neither sheet states a mathematical result.

**Scans 40–41 · Definition and examples.** Mme Hoang Xuan Sinh's note starts with a category having an associative and unital tensor product up to specified isomorphisms. The inserted groupoid condition, together with tensor-invertibility of each object, defines a (Gr)-category. The first example uses based loops and homotopies, with a Hopf-space variant. The second tensors torsors by Baer composition and includes invertible modules. The third composes autoequivalences and interprets an instance in terms of bitorsors or invertible bimodules.

**Scans 41–42 · The associator class.** The typescript takes isomorphism classes of objects for $\pi_0$ and automorphisms of the unit for $\pi_1$. Two maps from $\operatorname{Aut}(1_C)$ to $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$ give the action. Choosing $L_a$ and maps $\phi_{a,b}$ translates associativity into $f(a,b,c)\in\pi_1$. The pentagon yields a cocycle condition, and a change of choices yields $f+dg$. The following sheet names the resulting class $k(C)$, gives the classification statement and revisits the three examples.

**Scans 42–43 · Symmetry in a Picard category.** Strictness means that the symmetry of $X\otimes X$ is the identity for each $X$. Self-symmetries define a homomorphism $s$ from $\pi_0$ to the subgroup of $\pi_1$ killed by 2. The text uses $s$ in a classification up to Picard equivalence and describes strict Picard categories by complexes whose homology vanishes outside degrees 0 and 1.

**Scans 43–44 · Fractions and the enveloping category.** For $S$ a collection of objects of an AUC category $C$, the proposed fraction category $S^{-1}C$ universally makes the objects in $S$ invertible. The comparison between two functor categories begins on 43 and is displayed on 44. Taking all objects gives the enveloping Picard category. For finite projective modules, its first two invariants are identified with $K^0$ and $K^1$ of the ring.

**Scans 44–45 · Higher invariants.** The author proposes an interpretation of $K^i$ as homotopy invariants of higher enveloping Picard categories. The discussion extends to the relation between higher categories and simplicial objects, between higher group categories and simplicial groups, and between strict higher Picard categories and truncated chain complexes. A separate, canceled paragraph on 44 raises a fractions problem without commutativity.

**Scan 45 · Two operations.** For finite projective modules over a commutative ring, sum and tensor product induce two operations with distributivity. The page singles out categories Picard for $\oplus$, states that $\pi_0$ is a commutative unital ring and $\pi_1$ a module over it, and asks what else would classify the full structure.

**Scan 47 · English summary.** The opening of Chapter I defines a tensor category by a covariant bifunctor, gives the associator as a directed isomorphism of trifunctors and draws its labeled pentagon. The last sentence begins the definition of a commutativity constraint and ends on “is an isomorphism”.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence, scan 47.** I checked each pentagon label against the two bracketings at its ends. The upper route has two applications of the associator. The lower route first tensors an associator on the left by $\mathrm{id}_X$, reassociates at $X,Y\otimes Z,T$, then tensors on the right by $\mathrm{id}_T$. Parsing the five bracketings as binary expressions confirms that both routes go from $X\otimes(Y\otimes(Z\otimes T))$ to $((X\otimes Y)\otimes Z)\otimes T$. The equality of those composites is the axiom the page imposes.

**R2 · Model, high confidence, scans 41 and 47.** The French display places $(L_a\otimes L_b)\otimes L_c$ on the left of $\simeq$. The English definition places $X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)$ at the source of a right-pointing arrow. The first display specifies an isomorphism without choosing an arrow direction. Both expressions can be compared without reversing any arrow in the transcription.
