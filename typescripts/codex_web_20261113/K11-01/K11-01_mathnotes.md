## What the pages do

**A category of correspondences (scan 3).** Starting with a category with products and a functor to graded bivariant algebras, the writer keeps the objects and takes the morphisms from $X$ to $Y$ from the algebra of $X\times Y$. For $u:X\to Y$ and $v:Y\to Z$ in this new sense, the box defines $v\circ u$ by pulling both factors to the triple product, multiplying in the written order, and pushing to $X\times Z$. The proposed square has a functor into correspondences and another to graded modules.

**Associativity (scans 5, 9).** Two projection diagrams on $X\times Y\times Z\times T$ compare $w(vu)$ and $(wv)u$. The exchange step changes the order of pullback and pushforward. The projection formula brings the outside factor inside the pushforward. The first calculation reaches a product of the pullbacks of $w,v,u$ in that order. The last line of the second reaches the paper edge. Scan 9 singles out the kind of square used in this comparison.

**Units (scans 7, 9).** The diagonal supplies the class $\Delta_X$ by pushing forward the unit of $A(X)$. A square involving $\delta_X\times\mathrm{id}_Y$ is used to compute $u\circ\Delta_X$. The writer invokes an analogous calculation for the other order. The bracketed NB distinguishes the projections needed for composition from the diagonals and their products needed for units. The second square on scan 9 illustrates the additional case.

**Graphs (scans 9, 11).** A map $f$ gives its graph $(\mathrm{id}_X,f)$ and the image of $1$ on that graph. This defines $\varphi(f)$. Scan 11 identifies the graph of the identity with the unit construction and compares $\varphi(g)\varphi(f)$ with $\varphi(gf)$. Its larger diagram and the two coordinate paths both involve the triple $(x,f(x),gf(x))$.

**Action on classes (scans 11, 13).** A correspondence $u$ acts by $u(x)=p_{2*}(u\,p_1^*(x))$. Scan 13 treats the unit and composition separately. For composition it expands $v(u(x))$ through $Y$ and $(v\circ u)(x)$ through $X\times Z$. The underbrackets identify the pulled-back $x$ and the two displayed final products agree in their factor order.

**The functor square and transposition (scan 15).** On an ordinary arrow the comparison asks for $f_*(x)=\varphi(f)(x)$ and evaluates the graph action. The last value written is $x$. The subsequent remark asks how to realize the contravariant action. Interchanging $Y\times X$ and $X\times Y$ gives a proposed operation on correspondences which fixes the objects. Its compatibility with multiplication is marked for verification.

**Numerical function on one-parameter subgroups (English typescript, scans 14, 12).** In typescript order the proof starts on −2.30−. It compares the numerical function for a conjugated subgroup of a maximal torus with maxima of normalized linear functionals. Finitely many index sets occur. On −2.31−, homogeneity and compactness of the space of rays give a real minimizer; the writer then addresses rational attainment.

**Relative ampleness and stability (English typescript, scans 12, 10, 8).** Proposition 2.18 states that for a $G$-linear $f:X\to Y$, a relatively ample $M$ and an ample $L$, the stable locus on $X$ for $M\otimes f^*L^n$ contains the inverse image of the indicated stable locus on $Y$ for sufficiently large $n$. The proof reduces to proper schemes by equivariant open immersions. On −2.33− it combines a lower bound $-K$ for one summand with a uniform positive lower bound for the other, and invokes Theorem 2.1.

**Projective transformations (English typescript, scans 6, 4).** Chapter 3 takes projective transformations acting on $(m+1)$ ordered projective points over the integers. The first step seeks invariant affine opens. The pullbacks $L_i$ of the projective twisting sheaf acquire linearizations via the canonical isogeny from $\mathrm{SL}(n+1)$. The text states a divisibility condition for a product linearization and introduces projected coordinate sections $X_i^{(j)}$. The supplied side ends partway through Definition 3.1.
