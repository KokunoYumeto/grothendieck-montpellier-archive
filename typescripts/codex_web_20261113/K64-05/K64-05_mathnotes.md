# Mathematical notes · cote 64, scans 53–65

## What the pages do

**The six-point divisor and the four pairs of opposite faces (scan 53).** The leaf opens in the middle of a proof. It relates the normalizer of $V$, the automorphisms of $(X,\Delta)$ and the direct automorphisms of an octahedron. A nonidentity section of $V$ acts on $X$ and has an “axe”. The divisor is the sum of these axes étale locally. The second corollary passes from the octahedral configuration to a degree-four cover by pairing opposite faces. It gives the Sylow 3-subgroups as another description. The model conic is $x^2+y^2+z^2=0$, with changes of coordinate signs. One marginal note records its behavior over $\mathbb R$. The other specifies a distinguished pair of faces by their coordinates. [A53]

**A projective line with a degree-four divisor (scans 54–55).** Section 2 takes a relative projective line $X$ and an étale divisor $I$ of degree four. A distinguished subgroup $V_I$ of the permutations of $I$ makes $I$ a torsor. The proposition extends the action to $X$. The ensuing description uses the triple $J=V^*$, a projective line with its distinguished divisor of degree three, and a second torsor $K$. The manuscript expressly distinguishes $K$ from the $I$ with which it started. [A54, A55]

**The sign cover and the choice of a section (scans 55–57).** Formula (11) constructs the cover by a contracted product. The sign changes on a conic are written out in (12), with quotient (13). The upper margin of scan 56 gives trace forms for the conic and the hyperplane. The lower margin relates $K$, $I$ and the fibre over a section. After an identification with a constant four-element set, (15) supplies a fibre isomorphism and (16) expresses $K$ in those terms. [A56]

**An involution on a genus-one torsor (scans 58–59).** Section 3 assumes residue characteristics different from two. With an origin chosen locally, the involution on $E$ is $x\mapsto a-x$. The section $a$ also has an intrinsic description in the degree-two Picard torsor. The fixed scheme is given by $2x=a$ and is a torsor under the two-torsion of $E_0$. Formula (21) reconstructs $E$ and its involution by twisting $E_0$ with this fixed-point torsor. Formula (22) describes the quotient by translations and its compatibility with the two symmetries. [A58, A59]

**The branch loci and the quotient square (scans 59–62).** The quotients of $E$ and $E_0$ by their involutions give $X$ and $\Sigma$. The branch divisor of $E\to X$ gives the four marked points. To describe the other ramification locus the manuscript lifts a point to $E$ and obtains $4x=2a$ with $2x\ne a$. In the case $E=E_0$ it pairs the points of exact order four under negation. Diagram (29) labels the étale top arrow and the three ramification loci. Scan 62 explains why the square is cartesian over the indicated open and fails near $D_J$. It then specializes to the trivial torsor. [A60, A61, MC27, A62]

**A morphism determined from the marked line (scan 63).** The right-hand margin identifies (30) with the quotient of multiplication by two. With $J$ made constant, $I_t$ is $J$ together with $t$. For each $i$, an involution exchanges $i$ with $t$ and the two remaining labels with one another. Its fixed locus is denoted by $\Delta_i^t$. Formula (32) prescribes its image and (33) gives the proposed image of the four marked sections. [A63]

**The rational function and its iterates (scans 64–65).** In coordinates $J=\{0,1,\infty\}$, the manuscript computes the three involutions and their fixed points. Double zeros and double poles determine the rational function up to the factor $c_t$. Evaluation at a section squaring to $t$ gives $c_t=t$. Formula (37) is
$$
 f_2^t(z)=t\left(\frac{z^2-2z+t}{z^2-2tz+t}\right)^2 .
$$
The last paragraph identifies its iterates with the quotients of multiplication by $2^n$ and relates the inverse image of $t$ to the points killed by $2^n$. [MC34, MC35, MC36, MC37, A65]

## Slips

- **Scan 64, (35), last row [MC35, MC35-R]. High confidence.** The equation is $z^2-2tz+t=0$. The roots are written $t\pm\sqrt{t(1-t)}$. Completing the square gives $(z-t)^2=t(t-1)$, so the radicand for this equation is $t(t-1)$. Both minus signs in the equation and the order $1-t$ under the radical are clear.
- **Scan 65, paragraph after (37) [MC37, MC37-R]. High confidence.** The sentence “Pour $0,\infty,1$ on trouve $1$” concerns the unsquared ratio. At $z=1$ its numerator is $t-1$ and its denominator is $1-t$. The three values in the written order are $1,1,-1$. The squared function in (37) does have the claimed value $t$ at all four marked points.
- **Scans 53 and 60 [R53]. Low confidence as an editorial correction.** Scan 53 says “structure icosaédrale régulière” for a divisor of degree six. Scan 60 again appears to say “configuration icosaédrale”. Scan 61 explicitly calls the six inverse pairs “la configuration octaédrale”. If the same configuration is meant in all three passages, *octaédrale* is the likely intended adjective at the first two. The accent on scan 60 is uncertain.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark [MC34], high confidence.** The three projective transformations in (34) exchange the indicated pairs of points in $\{0,1,\infty,t\}$. Composing the first with the second gives the third. These checks use homogeneous coordinates over $\mathbb Q(t)$.
- **Model remark [MC36], high confidence.** Put $N=z^2-2z+t$ and $D=z^2-2tz+t$. At $z^2=t$, the ratio $N/D=-z/t$ has square $1/t$, which gives the normalization $c_t=t$. The identity $tN^2-D^2=(t-1)(z^2-t)^2$ accounts for the double fibre over 1 in (36). The resultant $4t(t-1)^2$ rules out cancellation for generic $t$.
- **Model remark [MC27], high confidence for the count, medium confidence for the meaning of the lowest brace.** Scan 61 labels four braces “degré 16”, “degré 4”, “degré 12” and “degré 2”. In $(\mathbb Z/4)^2$, twelve points have exact order four and form six pairs under negation. The quotient map on this set has degree two. Its quotient has six elements. The manuscript does not specify which degree its lowest annotation measures.
- **Model reading question [R54], low confidence.** Scan 54, L16, final NB, writes a torsor $I$ “sous $\underline V^*$”. The immediately preceding clause identifies $V^*$ with a triple, while $I$ has degree four, and the preceding paragraph says “sous $\underline V_I$”. An intended $V$ would fit those orders. The raised mark is dark in the crop, so the discrepancy between the two occurrences is recorded without emending the text.
- **Model reading question [R58], low confidence.** Scan 58, L12, (18), appears to put a small 2 over $\sigma_{E_0}$. A statement about the map induced by $\sigma$ on translations, $\sigma_{E_0}=-\mathrm{id}_{E_0}$, would fit the live formula $\sigma x=a-x$ below. The raised ink is overwritten. The text and its doubt mark are retained.
