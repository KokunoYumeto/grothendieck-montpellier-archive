# Hand log · cote 64, scans 53–65

## Scan 53

The triangular Δ has a short, level base. The underbar on Aut and on V is separate from nearby indices (upper implications). A long returning stroke under a word leads to an insertion in the line below, as at « fidèle », « schéma en » and « directs ». It is not a general rule for horizontal strokes: the uncertain « la réunion / la somme » has a stroke through its letters. The two sideways notes must be read after a clockwise quarter-turn. The bare = in « revient au = » survives.

## Scan 54

I has top and bottom bars plus a separate underline. J in the last third has a downturned hook at its upper left and a long right edge. The group symbol in (10) has an outer loop and an inner, narrow horizontal flourish. Its index and the underline of that index are distinct. The stroke above « distingué » ends at the gap before V.

## Scan 55

J has a long right vertical and an inward curl. Σ in the last display is drawn over an earlier shape; both diagonal directions can be seen, unlike the plain Σ preceding the deletion above it. The doubled left stem of D is especially clear in (D₂)ₛ. His abbreviated « rev. » has little space between the r and e. The slanted NB has its own baseline, distinct from the replacement cohomology formula.

## Scan 56

The slanted notes use different baselines. Turn clockwise by about 40° for the trace forms, and about 49° for (14). The sign over P in both side notes is a shallow hollow wedge. The dotted index in (12) lies next to a letter with a loop below the line; (13) has a plain crossing x. His « changement de signes sur les xᵢ » uses x on this line regardless of the earlier letter.

## Scan 57

A long sawtooth stroke cuts across the first (16) from its number to its closing parenthesis. The second (16) has a single continuous Σ and distinct star. The letter following the restriction bar is tall and crossed, much like t, with extra ink at the base. The only writing in the bottom third is the archive foliation.

## Scan 58

σ is a small low loop with a horizontal top stroke, including in E^σ. The left index 2 of E₀ in (20) is low and distinct from the subscript 0. Two short vertical strokes under I in (19) connect it with the set on the next line. They differ from the underline below I. The horizontal line inside Ker ends in a dot-like thickening, while (17) has a clear small arrowhead.

## Scan 59

The symbol between the two levels of (22) has a wavy left vertical and a straight right vertical. The kernel inclusion-like curve under the equals after V is still visible. A wide zigzag through the first definition affects both ₂E₀ and the equals with its raised déf. The later « droites » is written immediately above the cancelled middle of its predecessor.

## Scan 60

In the last computation ζ is a single curl that extends down below the line. At its first occurrence it could also be ξ. The other occurrences have the same long lower stroke. The low number before E₀ has a closed head in the first occurrence and is distinct from the low 4 in the deleted ending. The small (27) beneath X* belongs to that expression.

## Scan 61

Scan 61, (27)–(29). Inferior left indices 2 and 4 precede E; a short underline belongs to M and V individually. At the foot the right Sigma has a separate upper bar; on the left it is unclear whether the separate stroke is an overbar or part of the capital. The vertical inclusions under X and Sigma are narrow U shapes. The raised 6 before paires is an insertion, distinct from the handwritten degrees.

## Scan 62

Scan 62. A single long stroke crosses principal and doit être; a bent guide goes from est to the replaced verb. Similar guides put J et, et section t de Sigma* and the compressed words before V in their respective sentences. The two bars after Quand have no third stroke. A short vertical restriction mark follows X before D_J and t. Two tall f-like strokes support effet after en. The addition before V remains uncertain.

## Scan 63

Scan 63. In (32) j has a long descending hook while i has a short low stem and a dot. In (30) the label is above an arrow, and its low character is patched. In (32) and (33) the inclusion sign closes at the left into a wedge. A double stroke outside the diagonal marginal note separates it from the body. The separate NB is underlined. Do not carry the underlines of I and J from (31) into the plain capitals in (33).

## Scan 64

Scan 64, (34)–(36). A good 0 is a closed oval, a good infinity has two lobes; the latter is occasionally only an open double wave. The z in a numerator has level bars, whereas the numeral 2 has an upcurled beginning. The three Delta indices are 0, 1 and infinity. The second segment in (36) has a squared tip with no distinct arrowhead. The auxiliary Greek letter varies from a two-bend form to an almost single wavy descender. See also scans 60 and 63.

## Scan 65

Scan 65. The long stroke drawn up from the last line routes en groupes between schéma and the left torsion subscript; it is separate from the cancellation to the left. The little circle before n in the exponent is distinct from the raised n on the subscript 2. The heavy initial of Il and the retraced 2 of 2z have visible following letters and no distinct underlying word. The list in the check is written 0, infinity, 1 in that order.

## Recurring signs compared across the packet

The square-ended J on 54–56 and 61–63 differs from the two horizontal serifs of I; their independent underlines sometimes touch the lower serif. The restriction sign is upright in (14)–(16) and in X|D on 62, whereas the quotients visibly lean on 59 and 61. The wedge in contracted products recurs on 55, 57–59 and 61–62. Its lower group is recorded only when written. The roofs of Sigma and its overbars are particularly difficult on 55 and 61. In (12) the loop below the squared base has no counterpart in the plain x of (13). The sign by x+ on 60 has one principal curl; the auxiliary letter below (36) on 64 has two short upper bends. This comparison leaves separate first candidates ζ and ξ. The corresponding uncertainties remain local. The superscript of f is t in (30), (34)–(37), its subscript t in (33), and the raised circle in the iterated map on 65 is smaller than a zero. The manuscript’s hand-numbered leaves run 5–11 on alternating scans. Their numerals are independent of the lead-pencil foliation.
