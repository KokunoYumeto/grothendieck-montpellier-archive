## What the pages do

**Compatibilities and pairing (178–182, even scans).** Two diagrams lead from indexed objects to A and E. The first sheet lists three compatibility conditions. Scan 180 defines A and E using sums over intermediate tensor objects and gives component formulas for a pairing. Scan 182 applies σ to the two arguments, then sets the indexed data side by side in a ruled chart. Its last three lines are unfinished calculations.

**Quotients and components (184).** There are quotient presentations of A and E, two sequences, and a map from A × E to the underlined scalar object. The working distinguishes three types of argument. It pairs a tensor of two f’s with u, a scalar λ with a tensor of two x’s, and λ with u. The last of these values is explicitly zero. Several earlier expressions remain visible under cancellations.

**The product through the middle object (186).** A diagram places E₁∧E₂ between two copies of E₁⊗E₂. A curved arrow sends a pure tensor to the sum of that tensor and the tensor of its two barred factors. The sheet computes a mixed term and expands a product of two sums. At the foot, a descending arrow leads to an expression involving a relative trace.

**A description of the dual (188).** A candidate description of Aᵛ uses a bilinear form f on A₁ × A₂ and a scalar λ. Two earlier auxiliary maps g₁ and g₂ and their restrictions are crossed through. Two later lines give restrictions of f in terms of λ and the traces on the individual factors. The bottom sequence ends with a map labelled ψ.

**Trace, norm and a dual basis (190).** The writer asks for u in terms of the two generators, sets their product equal to λu + μ, and writes trace and norm expressions. Pairings against the displayed basis elements express the two coefficients. A diagram reverses the directions of the maps in the E row. It is followed by a transpose identity and a two-condition specification of u.

**Quadratic presentations and a special case (192).** Two monic quadratic expressions precede an expansion of the product of u₁ and u₂. The sheet considers the joint conditions 2 = 0 and b₁ = b₂ = 0. It then gives decompositions, writes the coefficients using bases of the two lines, and ends with correspondences leading to an element u of A and a quadratic expression for it.

**Six binary choices (189, footnote).** In the reused typescript the author counts the hexagrams by choosing one of two signs in each of six positions. The other seven typed sides develop the essay about action and inaction, yin and yang, in contrast with the mathematical material on the handwritten sides.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · 192 · high confidence.** As a check on the written expansion, treating u₁, u₂, b₁, b₂ as commuting symbols gives the same four terms on expansion. This checks the coefficients in that line. The algebraic construction itself lies outside this calculation.
- **R2 · 192 · high confidence.** Setting b₁ and b₂ to zero in the displayed right-hand side leaves 2u₁u₂. Its reduction in characteristic two is zero. This checks the special case even though the word before « si » is uncertain.
- **R3 · 189 · high confidence.** Counting all binary sequences of length six gives 64, as in the footnote. This concerns the count alone.
- **Reading question · 186 · low confidence.** The isolated line after the generator table ends in a subscript read as 3. A 2 would agree with the preceding product; the last small figure has two bends. The last subscript was unmarked at freeze and remains as transcribed.
- **Reading question · 192 · low confidence.** In the opening pair of quadratics the transcription has `b₁x₁` and `b₂x₂`. The two small variables could also be fast u’s. Later equations contain `β₁u₁` and a corresponding second term. The two rows use different coefficient letters, and agreement with the later notation does not identify the earlier ink.
