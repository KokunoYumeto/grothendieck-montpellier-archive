# Mathematical notes on cote 19, K19-06

## What the pages do

**Finite adjoint chains (81; P81).** Starting in mid-sentence, the manuscript puts the successive functors for \(f\), \(g\) and \(h\) in a staircase. The proposition asserts that for each integer \(n\) there are two \(\mathcal U\)-toposes and \(n\) functors alternating between them, each right adjoint to its predecessor. An insertion says that none is fully faithful. Presheaf toposes on small categories are allowed.

**A candidate for the infinite case (81–82; Q81).** The question asks for a chain infinite in both directions. Grothendieck proposes looking for \(g\) that is simultaneously left and right adjoint to \(f\). The next sheet describes a set \(A\) with a bijective \(w\), a subset \(E\) of \(A^w\), and a retraction of \(A/w\) onto \(E\). The stated functor sends these data to the triple built from \(E\) and its identity. Several words in this six-line continuation are doubtful.

**The site and its fibre toposes (84; S84).** On \(E\to B\) he puts the least fine topology making the inclusions of the fibre sites continuous. After passing from each \(E_b\) to its topos, he describes a morphism above \(f:x\to y\) by a morphism \(F\to f^*(G)\) in the fibre over \(x\). A large candidate limit formula is cancelled. The running text turns instead to sections and the topos associated to the little site at \(X\).

**Sections cartesian on vertical arrows (84–85; S85).** The condition denoted « vert-cart » applies to arrows above a fixed base object. An iterated section formula first takes cartesian sections inside a fibre and then sections over \(B\). The displayed conclusion is \(\widetilde E\simeq\underline\Gamma(B,\overline E^\triangle)^\circ\); the text explains the triangle by passage to opposite fibre categories. The final two displays compare the associated inclusions. An overwritten exponent in the last one is doubtful.

**Adjoints from the square of inclusions (87–88; A87).** The diagram relates cartesian sections and all sections before and after \(F^*\). Two three-line Hom computations produce candidates \(i_*F_*i'\) and \(i_+F_+i'\) for the left and right adjoints of \(F_c^*\) under their respective existence hypotheses. In the first computation the bracket includes the argument \(X'\). In the second it stops just before that argument.

**A fibrewise construction (88; A88).** Item ③ constructs the left adjoint of the inclusion \(i\) from inductive limits in the fibres. Its value at \(b\) is written as a colimit of \(f^*(X(x))\) indexed over objects above the displayed \(b/B\). The hypothesis is commutation with change of base. A counit arrow follows. The sloping note and its continuation at the foot state a dual construction using small inverse limits. The scratched indices at the central formulas and the last superscript in the margin do not give a reliable further convention.

**Fibre functors and strict generators (89; G89).** The first corollary gives \(b_*^c\) as a left adjoint to evaluation \(b_c^*\), subject to the stated existence hypothesis. The second transfers a family of strict generators from the section category and describes a family obtained from the fibres. Its final union ranges over \(b\in\mathrm{Ob}\,B\), with \(b_*^c\) applied to the chosen generators at \(b\).

**Generators of \(L\) (90; G90).** Small colimits in the fibres, preserved by the transition functors, give the left adjoint of \(i\) used here. The displayed Hom identification sends tests by \(X_\lambda\) in the section category to tests by \(i_*X_\lambda\) in \(L\). A small family of fibre generators and small sums enter through the reference to Deligne. The first part of the sheet also records the corresponding left adjoint of \(j^\circ\) and right adjoint of \(j\) under the limit hypothesis.

**Cogenerators (91; G91).** Passing to \(L^\circ\) changes the request for cogenerators into one for generators. The manuscript gives sufficient conditions involving \(j_*^\circ\), small inverse limits preserved by \(f^*\), and small sets of cogenerators in the fibres.

**Properties inherited from the fibres (92; T92).** Limits or colimits of the specified kinds are asserted to pass to the category of cartesian sections when the transition functors preserve them. Evaluation and change of base preserve them too. Grothendieck then considers preadditive, additive and abelian categories, exact filtered colimits, and the conditions labelled a), b), c) for a topos. The inserted clauses repeatedly specify what \(f^*\) must preserve.

**The remaining topos conditions (92–93; T93).** Size and a small set of generators are treated separately. An essentially small base deals with the first. To invoke the preceding generator argument he imposes cofibrancy and discusses the left adjoints of the \(f^*\), using \(f_!\) in the bracketed passage. The general generator argument is deferred, though the topos conclusion is asserted. Inverse limits can then exist without an evident term-by-term calculation.

**Fibrations, cofibrations and transport (95; D95).** The two definitions give contravariant transport \(f^*\) and covariant transport \(f_*\), Hom formulas, and associated pseudo-functors with different source variance. For a fibration the extra transports exist when the \(f^*\) have left adjoints. In a cofibration the converse construction requires right adjoints of the \(f_*\). The first Hom formula is an interlinear addition.

**Relative opposites (95–96; D96).** The relative opposite is introduced up to equivalence. The pseudo-functor labelled opp takes opposite fibre categories and the opposite of the transport functor, with transitivity data as well. Cofibrancy of the resulting category requires right adjoints to the indicated pullbacks. The last sentence stresses that the same pseudo-functor gives two distinct fibred categories over \(B\).

## Remarks of the model

- **M87, high confidence.** I checked the domains along the two Hom computations. Both composites begin with the primed inclusion and finish with an adjoint of the unprimed inclusion. This accounts for their direction from the primed cartesian-section category back to the unprimed one. This observation uses only the unambiguous parts of the square and the displays.
- **M90, high confidence.** The inclusion \(i\) is faithful. The Hom identification on 90 therefore suffices to transfer detection of distinct parallel arrows from the family \(X_\lambda\) to \(i_*X_\lambda\). This checks the generator step visible on 90. A stronger meaning of « stricts » on 89 was not checked.
- **M93, high confidence.** The two occurrences of existence at the end concern different things. « à rédiger en détail » follows the small set of generators. « un peu mystérieux » follows the calculation of inverse limits. Their positions identify precisely what was deferred and what remained unclear.
- **M96, low confidence.** Scan 96, second equation. The frozen mark on the argument of \(F^{\mathrm{opp}}\) allowed a circle or a prime. A small detached pen mark, without an intended exponent, also fits. The visible stroke is much less complete than the circles on the right. The witness table records all three possibilities; the notation is too uncertain for a conclusion about the variance from that mark alone.
