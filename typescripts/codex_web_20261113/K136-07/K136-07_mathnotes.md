# K136-07 · Mathematical notes

The scan numbers locate the physical sheets. The manuscripts, the English typescript and the French typescript headed IX have distinct lines of argument.

## What the pages do

**Cochains represented by a simplicial object (048).** Starting with a ring and a module, the writer considers a cochain functor with two properties. One compares its cohomology with the cohomology of the input. The other concerns limits and gives a representation of its degree-\(n\) functor by \(C_*^n\). The notes pass from evaluation on simplices to an augmentation and a resolution. A lifting diagram and a row of cohomology groups lead into a question about flasqueness. The intervening prose is substantially faded.

**Two examples and normalization (050, 052).** The first example expresses cochains using a sequence of Hom identifications. The second takes the subcomplex belonging to nondegenerate cochains and writes it by three intersections of kernels. On 052 a power indexed by simplex maps is related to an exterior power with exponent \(n+1\). A distinguished element \(T\) appears in the differential. The sheet returns to flasqueness and a possible argument using retractions.

**The minimal model, in typed page order (053, 051, 049).** Under the hypothesis of vanishing first Betti number, 053 chooses degree-two variables and then adjoins degree-three variables. Some have differential zero and others have quadratic differentials. On 051 the same construction is described inductively. New closed generators account for a cokernel, and the differentials of the others represent a kernel in the next degree. On 049 Theorem B reads the ranks of homotopy and Hurewicz, and the Whitehead products, from the model for a simply connected triangulated space.

**Integration and its uniqueness (054, 056).** A degreewise augmented, half-simplicial differential graded group is assumed to resolve \(A_0\), with degrees bounded by the simplex dimension. Proposition 1 characterizes maps to \(A_0\) by normalization, degree and a Stokes formula. The construction proceeds in order of the simplex dimension. Independence of the chosen primitive is dealt with separately in dimensions \(N\geq2\) and \(N=1\). The remarks isolate the cohomology hypotheses, draw an augmented bicomplex and invoke uniqueness to obtain compatibility with endomorphisms.

**The slice topos and coefficients (058, 060).** Sheaves on a semi-simplicial object are written as objects over it or as functors on a slice category. The product with a fixed object gives an induced sheaf. The text then passes to coefficients with extra structure, hypercohomology under a lower bound, and degree-\(n\) cochains as a product of the groups attached to the \(n\)-simplices.

**Changing variance (062).** An arrow labelled “fibration” stands above cancelled comparisons built from sums and products. The surviving text assigns chains to one variance of coefficients and cochains to the other. It compares a category with direct sums with the opposite of one with products, and discusses composition with a functor preserving the relevant operations.

**Monodromy and components (055).** The opening finishes an argument relating nondegeneracy to a separating pairing and then to a monodromy pairing. Section 11 introduces a finite étale component group and its primary components. The last display expresses the whole group using those components.

**The torus and the branches of a curve (061, 059, 057).** On 061 the kernel of the map between two Picard functors is described as an extension of a torus by a connected unipotent group. A quotient is associated to the branches over each point. On 059 the branches define a graph with the irreducible components as vertices. The page announces a canonical homomorphism from graph homology and introduces a map from branches to their incident points and components. On 057 an orientation yields a boundary given by a difference of endpoints and a map of chain complexes. Section 12.4 then writes the character group of the maximal torus as a kernel.

**The degree map and the Néron model (063).** The top of the sheet associates sections to divisors. Part c) describes the quotient \(Q=P/E\). Under the extra generic-fibre hypothesis of d), the typewritten sequence with degree map identifies its kernel \(A\). The last two displays identify the generic fibre and compare identity components.

## Remarks of the model

- **R53, high confidence.** In 053 the quadratic differential has degree four, since both \(x_i\) and \(x_j\) have degree two. This matches the degree of the differential of a newly adjoined degree-three variable. This checks the degree in the displayed construction only.
- **R56, high confidence concerning the implication.** In case a) of 056, an exact choice of difference \(d\alpha\) contributes zero after the boundary and the previous integration map. Commutation puts \(d\) on the outside, and the preceding Stokes relation then gives two successive boundaries. The source's \(\partial^2=0\) is enough for this step.
- **Q56, low confidence concerning the lettering.** In 056, line 09, the frozen middle exponent has \(N-1\) first and \(N-2\) second. The final k subscript has the same two candidates. The substitution and the preceding Stokes step favour \(N-2\) in both places. The handwriting is too compressed to infer a change in convention or a slip.
- **R61, high confidence.** For a point with exactly three branches, (12.3.1) gives a free group of rank two. The diagonal vector \((1,1,1)\) together with the second and third coordinate vectors has determinant one. Quotienting by the first vector leaves two free generators.
- **Q48, low confidence.** In the first term of the cohomology row on 048, line 26, the frozen superscript on \(H\) is 1. The tiny raised mark also looks like 0 on an enlarged view, and \(H^0\) fits the adjacent \(H^0(X_*,Z_*^1)\). This question arose after the final transcription hashes.
- **Q54, low confidence.** In the last equation of 054, the frozen subscripts are \(n\) and \(n-1\); \(N\) and \(N-1\) would continue the induction immediately above. The marks sit below the k's and have much less height than the N in the exponent on \(\omega\).
- **Q58, medium confidence.** In 058, line 07, the frozen last Hom has \(\mathbb{\Delta}^{\circ}\) as its first argument. Later on the same page the sheaf functors have a slice by \(X\) in their source. The short expression in the earlier display gives a reading question about an omitted slice. The page plainly distinguishes the hat over \(\Delta/X\) from the shorter formula to its right.
