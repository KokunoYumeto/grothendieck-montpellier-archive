# Mathematical notes on cote 105, scans 37–41 and 43–53

Line references are to the individual scan files. The English leaves are manuscript pages 15–16. A different, French sequence runs from manuscript page 6 on scan 43 to page 18 on scan 53. Folio numbers alone can therefore designate two different arguments.

## What the pages do

**Scans 37, 39 and 41.** Scans 37 and 39 list members of computer directories and carry pen annotations and totals. Scan 41 is a printer banner with six job lines. Each supplies documentary information about the paper used in the folder.

**Scan 38, corollary and construction.** The corollary starts with a small class Φ₀ of arrows with accessible sources and targets. Its other conditions concern filtering direct limits, amalgamated sums and stability of Φ. The conclusion gives factorisations with one arrow in Φ and the other in Ψ, together with a functor Σ on the arrow category and a morphism from the identity.

**Scan 38, example.** The example takes Φ to be the closure of Φ₀ shown on the page. To obtain the dotted extension, the writer considers pairs consisting of an index set and a map from a limit to X. He sketches an induction using their order. The leaf ends with a surjectivity assertion involving Hom(B,X).

**Scan 40, lemma.** The lemma collects closure properties of a left Q-saturated class. The numbered conditions concern isomorphisms, composition and cobase change, amalgamated sums, and ordinal direct systems. The insertions in the fourth condition specify the value at a limit index and the maps into the final limit.

**Scan 40, theorem.** In a category admitting small direct limits and satisfying the accessibility assumption on the page, the first part gives a factorisation condition from the listed closure properties. The second adds stability by direct factors to a criterion for saturation. A final NB isolates the preliminary smallness assumption.

**Scan 43.** A cancelled argument compares membership in W for a map and for maps on open pieces. The fresh “Bilan” presents two opens by a functor into a product of two copies of Δ¹, identifies the four fibres, and passes to the three-object category obtained by removing (1,1). It writes the opens and their intersection as inverse images.

**Scan 44.** This is page 90 of a typescript about the psyché, creation, fear and the image of reality. Its mention of mathematics occurs within that argument. It interrupts the French mathematical foliation between 6 and 7.

**Scan 45.** The two opens and their intersection are identified with slices and then with fibres of the split cofibration. A triangle compares X and its barred counterpart over Ψ. The proper direct image is evaluated fibre by fibre for a constant coefficient.

**Scan 46, manuscript page 8.** The writer takes the cohomology diagram on the three-vertex shape and includes its indexing category into the full square by adding the missing final object. He gives the resulting direct image γ and restricts it back to the original category.

**Scans 46–47, manuscript pages 9–10.** The four corners of the proposed cohomology square are written out and its value at the final vertex is compared with the cohomology of X. To reverse the implication concerning membership in W, the writer asks for a cocartesian property of the substantial square. He then considers X over the full indexing square.

**Scans 48–49, manuscript pages 11–12.** The fourth fibre is identified with X. The writer contrasts the square made from the opens with a general commuting square, and singles out the map from the integral of its three-object subdiagram to X. The ensuing “Principe” starts with a diagram of categories, its associated cofibration and an object ξ; the next leaf computes the four components of its direct image.

**Scans 49–50, manuscript pages 13–14.** “Der 6” is proposed to make the square for a cofibration cocartesian as well as cartesian. The next page introduces the primed version for a fibration, discusses duality and rewrites the condition on an object of the square-shaped diagram category.

**Scan 51.** The four sides of a substantial square are labelled. The text pairs the isomorphism tests on opposite sides and reduces an auxiliary lemma about a three-vertex diagram to an adjunction morphism. It ends with an equivalence attributed on the page to a form of Der 3.

**Scan 52, manuscript page 16.** A statement about squares in Hot, cast in terms of connected components, attracts an explicit marginal objection. The proposed “Der 8” tests a map of squares against three conditions about its four components. The writer distinguishes the implications attached to cartesian, cocartesian and bicartesian squares.

**Scans 52–53, manuscript pages 17–18.** W7bis compares membership in W for opposite sides of a square; W8bis gives three conditions on a map and its components. The next leaf proposes corollaries for smooth categories over the displayed indexing shape, with a remark about the dual proper case.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark R1, high confidence, scan 43 / 45–47.** Reading the two explicitly enumerated vertex sets as ordinary sets gives union {0, a, b} and intersection {0}. I evaluated those two finite operations. This check concerns the vertex labels alone.
- **Model remark R2, high confidence, scans 46–47.** The author attaches “à vérifier” to the passage from the cohomology square to an isomorphism test, and “il faudrait savoir” to the reverse direction. Following the directions of the two displayed implications keeps those qualifications attached to their respective steps.
- **Model remark R3, high confidence, scans 52–53.** The component conditions on a map of two squares and the tests on one square’s opposite sides are different assertions. The separate lists and diagram labels on these two sheets distinguish them.
- **Reading question, low confidence, scan 52 / 131.** The frozen first condition has a marked wedge below f. A blot beside a bare f is also possible. The next leaf clearly has bare f in its first condition, but it does not decide which mark was made on the previous leaf. This leaves the transcription of scan 52 as last hashed.
