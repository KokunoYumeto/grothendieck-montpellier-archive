# Mathematical notes · Montpellier, cote 28, scans 002–011

## What the pages do

**Surfaces and rank (002).** A table places $P_1\times P_1$, $P_2$ and $F_n$ beside their groups. An attribution to Enriques introduces the conjugacy statement for connected smooth algebraic subgroups of $\mathrm{Cr}_2$. The corollary lists properties of the groups, including open orbits and rank 2. The proposed extension to dimension $n$ singles out the difficulty with maximal tori.

**Pseudo-automorphisms and kernels (002–003).** The notes distinguish ordinary and ruled forms of Psaut, give formulas for its points and Lie algebra, and state two cases in which its unit section is a closed immersion. The next leaf gives a closed-kernel corollary. A cancelled curve proposition precedes a lemma factoring a group map for a smooth proper curve through Aut.

**A pseudo-operation (003).** The question “Que signifie” introduces a map from $G\times X$ with open domain $U$. The page puts a dominance condition beside an associativity square and spells out when successive products are defined. It finishes with $N=\operatorname{Ker}u$ and a map out of $G/N$.

**Stabilizers and transitivity (004).** The first square places $F$ under the domain $U$. The text relates $F_X$ to $G_X$ and identifies a largest constant subgroup. A second definition uses dominance of the map to $X\times X$. For an actual operation the page explains this in terms of a dense orbit.

**Split tori and rationality (004–005).** For a faithful torus operation the notes give $F=e\times X$, a dimension bound and an equality case. They relate rationality, pseudo-automorphisms and the presence of a torus. The next leaf discusses conjugacy, centralizers and normalizers, followed by a product $T\times Y$ up to the indicated pseudo-map.

**A field condition and a classification problem (005).** Two conditions compare tori of dimension $d$ in $\mathrm{Cr}_{n,k}$ with extensions $K/k$ that become pure after adjoining variables. The bracketed NB records special cases and field qualifications. The problem then fixes a split torus of dimension $n$ and looks for the surrounding groups $G$ and homogeneous spaces with an open torus orbit.

**An open product and the map $f$ (006).** The product of $T$ and $H$ sits in $G$ by an open immersion, with trivial intersection of the conjugates of $H$. Projection gives the pseudo-map $f:G\dashrightarrow T$. Three axioms describe composition, restriction to $T$, and how the values on $gt$ distinguish $g$. The written consequence is $f(tg)=tf(g)$.

**Reduction and the one-dimensional difference (007).** Centralizers of subtori and a central quotient are proposed as reductions. In the case $\dim G-\dim T=1$, the notes specify $x_\alpha$ and $\rho_\alpha$, how $T$ conjugates $x_\alpha(\lambda)$, and the value of $f$ on that element. A question mark accompanies the proposed expression for $H$. The general case begins with a decomposition of the Lie algebra by characters.

**Centralizers of character kernels (008).** For a character $\chi$, the page forms $T_\chi$ and $Z_\chi$ and lists four possibilities for the rank-one quotient. It returns to root maps, their one-dimensional Lie spaces, and the sum describing $\operatorname{Lie}Z_\chi$. Opposite roots then enter a description of two Borel subgroups and the roots of a Lévi subgroup.

**Two-by-two matrices and reflections (009).** The maps $x_{\pm\alpha}$ and $\rho_{\pm\alpha}$ occur as the images of four elementary or diagonal matrices. Two more formulas give $f$ and the action on $t$ for a general matrix. The interchange matrix defines $w_\alpha$, followed by the reflection, the coroot $\alpha^*=\rho_\alpha-\rho_{-\alpha}$, and the normalizer. The last two formulas express compatibility with the action on roots.

**Pairs of roots (010).** A criterion for commutation of $U_\alpha$ and $U_\beta$ yields two sign conditions and a list of three cases. In case c), the roots of interest have the form $\beta+i\alpha$. The binomial coefficient is tested in $k$. A conjugation product and two Lie brackets follow. The sheet ends with the beginning of a conjugacy theorem.

**Enriques systems (011).** The last leaf lists data $M,M^*,R,\rho$ and five numbered conditions. They concern finiteness, diagonal pairing, signs, opposite roots, and the binomial condition for a root string. A single existence sentence closes the manuscript.

## Questions and conjectures

- **002.** “sauf difficulté liée aux tores maximaux” qualifies the generalization. The next clause singles out subgroups of maximum rank.
- **003.** “Que signifie … ?” asks for the meaning of a map to Psaut. The following pseudo-operation and its axioms are the page’s response.
- **005.** “Pb Classifier les $G$ alg. lisses … qui contiennent un tore déployé de dim $n$.” This is posed as the classification problem.
- **007.** “il faut un peu d'attention pour 3°” singles out the third axiom under the quotient. The expression “$H=x_\alpha(1)T x_\alpha(-1)$ (?)” carries the writer’s explicit doubt. “on reconstitue …” introduces an unfinished converse.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence (009).** The written interchange matrix squares to the identity matrix. Direct multiplication gives diagonal entries 1 and off-diagonal entries 0. This checks the matrix used to introduce $w_\alpha$.

**R2 · Model, high confidence (010).** For $n=2$ in characteristic 2, the three binomial coefficients have residues $1,0,1$. In that example the nonvanishing test in (2) supplies the two endpoint indices. The implication on the page gives no conclusion at the middle index.

**R3 · Model, high confidence (010).** Interchanging $\alpha$ and $\beta$ negates the scalar in the first displayed bracket. Writing its two pairings as $a,b$, the sum of the two coefficients simplifies to zero. This checks the relative minus sign in that formula.

**R4 · Model, medium confidence (010–011).** Case c) on 010 introduces $n$ through $\langle\rho_\alpha,\beta\rangle=-n$. Rule 5 on 011 uses $n$ without restating that equality. Reading the leaves together supplies a candidate meaning for the bound in the final axiom.

**Reading questions while writing the notes (002, middle paragraph).** The frozen text gives “difficulté liée” and “ss-groupes”. In the first phrase, the last pen movement could instead leave “lié” without a separate final e. In the second, “ss-gpes” is also possible because the letters following g are reduced to a short horizontal. Both questions concern the written forms, independently of the intended meaning.
