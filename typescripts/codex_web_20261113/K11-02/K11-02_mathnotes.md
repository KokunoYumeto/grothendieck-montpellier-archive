# K11-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**016, Corollary 2.16.** Two sublevel sets of the stability function occur side by side. The weak inequality gives the set called semi-convex. The strict inequality gives the convex set used to describe which parabolics are “responsible” for the failure of semi-stability at a point.

**016, Proposition 2.17.** The typescript states a uniform bound on the absolute value of the weight. It then asserts that, for each subset of points, a pair consisting of a point and a direction attains the least value among all such pairs.

**017, the two functors.** Using the interchange of $X$ and $Y$, the manuscript compares the composite $\psi\varphi^*$ with $\omega^*A^\circ$. It then considers a final object $e$ with $A(e)=k$. An insertion says to replace $k$ by $A(e)$ otherwise. This gives the setting for the forms on $A(X)$ and $A(Y)$.

**017, a correspondence and its transpose.** A class $u$ on $X\times Y$ and the switched class $su$ induce maps in opposite directions. Two diagrams and a calculation with $p_1,p_2$ compare their pairings. The writer inserts a centrality hypothesis and boxes the qualification about signs.

**018, Proposition 2.14.** After diagonalizing the torus action on the affine cone, the typescript uses the characters of the nonzero coordinates over $\phi x$ to compute a maximum. The proposition gives a finite list of rational linear functionals. The subset entering the maximum depends on $x$.

**018, Corollary 2.15.** The weight at a product of two one-parameter subgroups is bounded above by the sum of the two weights. The short proof at the head of 016 writes the bar of a product as a sum.

**019, graded monoids.** The writer observes that the preceding calculation used multiplication without addition. He considers “monoïdes bivariants”, then “monoïdes jaugés”, with an involution in each degree, trivial in even degree. A degree attached to each graded object governs the proposed degree of direct image. The motivation is to keep track of signs in the left and right projection formulas.

**020, lines in two skeletons.** The typescript uses conjugacy of maximal tori inside an intersection of parabolics. To compare the lines they determine, it reduces the argument to the inclusion labelled (**), then to two commuting one-parameter subgroups diagonalized in $\mathrm{GL}(n)$. The last sentence continues beyond this sheet.

**021, stability criteria.** The manuscript compares closure under inverse images, direct images and products with three conditions about the action of a correspondence, external products and graph classes. The proof uses the diagonal for one direction and the projections for the other. A rewritten NB describes the corresponding subcategories when $V$ has a final object.

**022, antipodal points.** After reporting Tits’s description of geodesics, the typescript defines antipodal points using $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$. Lemma 2.12 identifies antipodality in a common torus skeleton with antipodality on its sphere.

**023, stable subfunctors.** Intersections yield the notion of a stable subfunctor generated by given data. The writer introduces $A_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and discusses classes obtained by direct image, including an example conditional on resolution of singularities. The argument of $f_*$ is a reading question below.

**023–025, operations of type $A'$.** A class on a product defines a multilinear operation by acting on an external product. The manuscript composes two such operations with a third and discusses the product of two operations with the same source and target. The calculation begun at the foot of 023 continues at the head of 025.

**024, Definition 2.7.** The typescript names the spheres $\Delta(T)$ “skeletons”. It defines $\Delta_P(G)$ by $P(\delta)\supset P$ and expresses membership by inequalities for characters. This gives an intersection of hemispheres. The semi-simple case leads to a description in terms of spherical simplices.

**025, generators of a stable system.** Given $A'$ and the $E_X$, the manuscript describes $B(X)$ using values of admissible operations on chosen elements. The definition has several interlinear revisions, including a union and “1 ou”. The closing sentence considers direct and inverse images, units and multiplication.

**026, finite kernels and isogenies.** A proof distinguishes two images by characters on tori and a parabolic subgroup. Corollary 2.11 then obtains surjectivity for an isogeny using a positive power of a one-parameter subgroup. The last paragraph describes $\Delta(T)$ as rays in the real space containing a rational point.

**027, cancelled sign computation.** A square compares the switch $A\times B\to B\times A$ before and after $f\times g$. The writer expands the signs in the associated tensor calculation, circles individual contributions and cancels exponent terms. Long diagonal strokes cancel the whole calculation above the dividing line.

**027, Poincaré algebras and their maps.** Under “A”, a functional $k_A$ defines a strictly nondegenerate pairing. The text explicitly allows algebra maps that need not preserve the two functionals. It assigns a map $f_*$ to $f^*$ by a pairing identity, then writes the composition, identity and projection formulas, ending with $k_B f_*=k_A$.

**028, quotient and properness.** The opening finishes an argument from the existence of a geometric quotient by the multiplicative group. It passes from separatedness to properness by lemma 0.5.

**028, Definition 2.3 / Proposition 2.6.** A boxed §2 introduces the dependence of the stability function on the one-parameter subgroup. The typescript defines $P(\lambda)$ by specialization of a conjugate and states its parabolicity, the position of $\lambda$ in its radical and the centralizing property of the limit. A proof begins by choosing a faithful representation and continues beyond the scan.

## Remarks of the model

**R16-EMPTY (model, low confidence about authorial intent).** Read literally, “for every subset” includes the empty set. The conclusion asks for $x_0\in S$, which is impossible for that choice. **R16-NONEMPTY** is the narrower formulation “for every nonempty subset”. This removes the empty-set counterexample. Attainment for nonempty $S$ was not checked.

**R17-27-PAIR (model, high confidence).** The role of the forms can be compared directly in the two manuscripts. On 017 they are used to ask whether two already constructed maps are transpose. On 027 a pairing identity is the definition of the map in the opposite direction. The two displayed identities make that distinction explicit.

**R27-SIGN (model, low confidence about the cancelled calculation).** Restoring the individually struck terms in the long exponent gives $xy+fx+gx+gx+fy+xy+fg$. The expression below is $f(x+y+g)$. For these two readings the parities agree on all sixteen assignments of parities to $f,g,x,y$. This check concerns only those exponents. The arguments and indices in the cancelled tensor identities remain uncertain.
