# K161-4-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U01 · Faithfully flat algebras and morphisms (scan 3).** The opening proposition tests faithful flatness of $B/A$ by flatness and surjectivity on spectra. The forward argument passes through ideals and quotients. The converse chooses a nonzero cyclic submodule and a quotient by a maximal ideal. A sideways note gives the corresponding fibre test for a module. The sheet then turns to flat morphisms and maps between spectra of local rings.

**U02 · Flat maps, closures and quotient topologies (scan 4).** The page opens with universal openness and injectivity statements. Its central theorem compares the inverse image of a closure with the closure of an inverse image, for the image of a quasi-compact morphism. An affine calculation uses a kernel. A second marginal argument uses dominance. The author applies the theorem to saturated closed sets and writes sequences for closed, open and locally closed subsets.

**U03 · Descent of properties (scan 5).** A first proposition treats surjectivity and radiciality. The next group treats quasi-compactness and finiteness or presentation conditions. The algebra lemma compares $B$ and $B'$. A parallel corollary compares a quasi-coherent sheaf and its pullback, including finite type, finite presentation and local freeness. Further corollaries concern openness, closedness, separation and isomorphisms. The bottom list goes beyond what the worked arguments cover.

**U04 · Effective descent and exact sequences (scan 6).** A cancelled reduction to commutative algebra precedes the effective-descent theorem for quasi-coherent modules. The corollaries give sequences for subobjects, subschemes and morphisms. The graph diagram accompanies the sequence of Hom sets. Quasi-coherent algebras lead to the theorem for relative affine schemes and the tests for an affine, quasi-affine, integral, finite or finite locally free morphism.

**U05 · Integrality and ampleness (scan 7).** Finiteness reduces to a module question, and integrality is tested on the subalgebras $A[x]$. The ampleness section compares a direct image with its base change. For very ampleness it writes an evaluation map and a map into $P(g_*(L))$. For the general case it uses generation after twisting. A note singles out projectivity and quasi-projectivity, and the last heading opens the subject of effectivity.

**U06 · Radiciality and openness (scan 8).** Four conditions use universal injectivity, fibres and residue fields, points over fields, and the diagonal. The next proposition combines integrality, radiciality and surjectivity. Its examples use a radicial field extension and a cuspidal curve with its normalization. A separate group relates openness to lifting generizations and singles out morphisms locally of finite presentation.

**U07 · Quasi-affine schemes and morphisms (scan 9).** For $A=\Gamma(X,\underline O_X)$, the page lists tests involving the map to $\operatorname{Spec}A$, basic opens $X_f$, and ampleness. It then treats base change and composition. A boxed diagram relates closed immersions, finite, integral, affine, projective, proper, quasi-projective and separated morphisms. The surrounding text includes results on morphisms with finite fibres.

**U08 · Relative ampleness (scan 10).** For a coherent scheme and an invertible sheaf, basic opens are compared with generation of twists of quasi-coherent sheaves of finite type. The author defines relative ampleness using an affine open cover of the target. The final proposed implication involves $L$ ample over $Y$, $M$ ample over $Z$, and $L\otimes f^*(M^{\otimes n})$ for large $n$. The horizontal implication sign has a crossing stroke.

**U09 · Conditions on a class of morphisms (scan 11).** A course plan lists properties of schemes and morphisms. A separate column tests a class $M$ by base change, composition and cancellation. Lower diagrams compare conditions on the diagonal, sections, a kernel of a pair and the graph $\Gamma_f$. Another group treats separated, quasi-separated and coherent morphisms. The last four lines sketch a possible order for the course.

**U10 · Local schemes and the revised syllabus (scan 12).** The first block revisits morphisms of finite type, immersions, separation and the stability rules. Long oblique strokes cross this block. New groups then list local schemes and their maps, types of morphisms, and topics about quasi-coherent sheaves. The “Oubli” column includes the induced reduced structure on a closed set, direct images and extension from a retrocompact open.

**U11 · Functors of points and projective space (scan 13).** The outline compares using rings, schemes and locally ringed spaces as arguments of functors. It treats $P(M)$, dependence on the base and on epimorphisms in $M$, and embeddings in projective space. The lower half relates $V(M)^*$ to $P(M)$ by a sheaf epimorphism with Zariski-local sections. A quotient by $G_m$ is displayed. Finite inverse limits and the spectrum of a quasi-coherent algebra close the list.

**U12 · Maps into an affine scheme (scan 15).** A map from a locally ringed space to $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is constructed from a homomorphism into its ring of global sections. Evaluation at $x$ supplies the point map. The preimage calculation for $U_f$ gives continuity, and localization supplies compatible ring maps on the opens. The author then identifies the role of $\operatorname{Spec}k$ in describing $k$-schemes.

**U13 · Sheaves, presheaves and ring-valued arguments (scan 15).** The large diagram relates affine schemes, schemes, locally ringed spaces and two levels of functor categories. Restriction to affines is labelled on both levels. The question underneath asks how to express the sheaf condition in commutative algebra. A lower pencil diagram compares slice categories. The final lines mention sums, suprema of subsheaves and inverse limits.

**U14 · An intersection calculation for topoi (scan 16).** The short draft compares expressions involving $C,C'$, $\widetilde D,\widehat D$ in a diamond and in an intersection. A second line places $\mathrm{Top}$ beneath each product sign. The remaining task refers to equivalences involving $\gamma$ and essential surjectivity of the associated functors. The last reformulation stops after “ou encore”.

**U15 · Representability by open subsheaves (scan 17).** The author sets up open immersions and suprema of subsheaves. The proposition asks that a functor be a sheaf and be covered by open representable subsheaves. The first corollary permits affine pieces. The next restates the problem for functors on $k$-algebras. Applications listed are $P(E)$, $\operatorname{Spec}A$, fibre products and limits of relative affine schemes. A marginal oval beside the list mentions disjoint and universal sums.
