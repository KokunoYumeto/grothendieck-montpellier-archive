# Mathematical notes on cote 84, scans 51–66

Line numbers refer to the manuscript bands in the transcription. In the explanations, \(r\) and \(\zeta\) denote the two coordinates whose letterforms remain doubtful. Statement identifiers refer to the check events.

## What the pages do

**51 · The quadratic form and the algebra [C51, B51].** Starting with \(4N=T^2-\delta\), the author substitutes \(2x=T+z\) in the homogeneous quadratic form. The surviving expression is \(z^2-\delta\), giving the desired value in the tensor square of the line. He then embeds \(\mathcal A\) in \(\underline O\times L^\vee\). Sections are pairs \((b,r)\) subject to \(b_0=r_0T_0\), and the unit has coordinates \((2,0)\).

**52 · Multiplication [C52].** The norm in these coordinates is \((b^2-r^2\delta)/4\). The modulo-four calculation explains the numerator's divisibility. Polarizing the formula for the square gives the two coordinates of \(xx'\), namely \((bb'+rr'\delta)/2\) and \((br'+b'r)/2\). The small annotations under the numerators check their reductions modulo two.

**53–54 · The dual pairing and \(Q\) [B53].** The pairing with \((\lambda,\zeta)\) is \((\lambda b+r\zeta)/2\). The norm's polar form gives \(f_N(b,r)=(b,-r\delta)\). A sequence of sign corrections leads to the boxed \(Q(\lambda,\zeta)=(\zeta^2-\lambda^2\delta)/4\). On the affine fibre \(\lambda=1\), its zero locus has \(\zeta^2=\delta\) and the prescribed reduction of \(\zeta\). Scan 54 compares two forms at \(y=T=(2,0)\).

**54–55 · Change of group and forgetting the action [C55].** The map \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)_X\to\mu_{2,X}\) gives a functor on torsors. A diagram compares this with the descriptions by pairs and triples. The suggested forgetful map takes \((L,\Theta)\) to \((L,4\Theta,0)\). Taking the product before or after this map gives coefficients \(4\Theta\Theta'\) and \(16\Theta\Theta'\). The author tests the difference on \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\).

**55–56 · The parity-dependent product [C56].** A full subcategory is specified by vanishing of the reduced trace. A pair \((n,\mathscr X)\) carries a parity, with the extra condition on the covering if \(n=1\). When both inputs are odd the modified product uses \(\delta\delta'/4\). Locally, if \(\delta=4\Delta\) and \(\delta'=4\Delta'\), it is defined as \(4\Delta\Delta'\). The calculation addresses changes of the chosen lifts by elements killed by four.

**57–58 · Quadratic coverings in characteristic two [C57, B58].** Squaring and multiplication by the trace section \(T\) give the additive map \(f_T(u)=u^2+Tu\) from a line to its tensor square. Translating a root of \(Q\) preserves the root precisely when \(f_T(u)=0\). This identifies the acting kernel \(G_T\). The theorem on 58 describes the covering by \(L\), a torsor \(P_1\) under \(L\), the section \(T\), and a section \(\alpha\) of the torsor obtained using \(f_T\). For a product, multiplication by \(T'\) and \(T\) gives maps from the two kernels to \(G_{TT'}\).

**59 · Equivalent descriptions [B59].** The corrected heading counts « Quatre ». The individual entries carry the labels \(1,1',2,3,3'\). They pass from a quadratic covering to its rank-two algebra, to a quadratic module with \(N(e)=1\), and to a quadratic polynomial on an affine line torsor. The last presentation replaces the torsor by an extension \(0\to L\to E\to\underline O_X\to0\) and a quadratic form restricting on \(L\) to the tensor square.

**60–62 · Recovering \(T\) and \(N\) from the affine polynomial [C60, C62].** Subtracting \(x^2\) leaves an affine term. For an origin \(e\) with \(Q(e+u)=u^2+bu+c\), the coefficients become \(T=2e-b\) and \(N=e^2+c-be\), with \(\psi(T)=2\) and \(\varepsilon(N)=1\). Scan 61 relates \(T\) to the polarization of \(N\) at the unit. Conversely, scan 62 asks when the homogeneous expression has values in \(L^{\otimes2}\). Writing \(T=2x_0+t\) isolates the mixed component \(x_0(-b-t)\), hence \(t=-b\). The discriminant expression is \(T^2-4N=b^2-4c\).

**63–65 · The product in local coordinates [C64, C65].** The determinant lines should tensor and the discriminants should multiply. The suggested product has \(b=b_1b_2\) and \(c=b_2^2c_1+b_1^2c_2-4c_1c_2\). Changing the two splittings by \(u_1,u_2\) changes the product splitting by \(u=u_1b_2+u_2b_1+2u_1u_2\). Scan 65 tests that two roots give a root of the product polynomial. Setting both displacements to zero recovers the earlier constant formula.

**66 · The symmetric formula and the affine digression [C66].** The opening box collects the product value as \(Q_1(x_1)\delta_2+Q_2(x_2)\delta_1+4Q_1(x_1)Q_2(x_2)\). The author then starts with a ringed base and a scalar section \(\chi\). A trivialization of a scalar pushout of a torsor is recast as a lift \(T\) of the scalar in a module extension. The final display leaves the corresponding lift for a product open.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark [R54], medium confidence, scan 54.** If \(Q'=aQ\), evaluation at \(T\) yields \((a-1)\delta=0\). Recovering the factor from that evaluation alone requires a cancellation argument. For example, modulo four the units 1 and 3 have the same product with 2. The preceding portion of the manuscript might supply an extra hypothesis.
- **Model remark [R62], high confidence, scan 62.** The stated insufficiency already occurs over \(\mathbb F_2\). For \(E=\langle x_0,\ell\rangle\), \(L=\langle\ell\rangle\), take \(\psi(x_0)=1\), \(\psi(\ell)=0\), \(T=0\), \(N=x_0^2+x_0\ell\). Then \(4N-T^2=0\) while \(Q_{T,N}(x_0)=x_0\ell\) has a nonzero mixed component. This example concerns the mathematical condition, independently of the uncertain lower margin.
- **Model remark [C66], high confidence, scans 64–66.** The symmetric expression at the start of 66 also verifies the test on 65. In polynomial coordinates it is \(Q(u)=\delta_2Q_1(u_1)+\delta_1Q_2(u_2)+4Q_1(u_1)Q_2(u_2)\). Its three summands all vanish when the inputs are roots. Direct expansion gives the same polynomial as the proposed product coefficients.
