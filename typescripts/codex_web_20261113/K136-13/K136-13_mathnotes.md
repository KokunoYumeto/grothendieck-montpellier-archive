## What the pages do

**Scan 203, typescript p. −56−.** Lemma 5.2.5 says that vanishing, and the properties of being an isogeny or an isomorphism, can be detected on the special fibre for the indicated morphisms of Barsotti–Tate pro-groups. The author applies vanishing on the toric parts to obtain a pairing on the two abelian quotients. A second application gives an isogeny. [T1]

**Scan 201, p. −57−.** Section 5.2.6 finishes the orthogonality argument in the semistable case. Equality of heights and strictness at the finite stages give the required inclusion for every prime, including the residue characteristic. Section 5.3 then forms the induced pairing (5.3.2) on the two abelian parts. [T2]

**Scans 199 and 197, pp. −58− and −59−.** The text passes from the generic-fibre pairing to (5.3.5) on the special fibre. It distinguishes the case $\ell\ne p$ from the mixed-characteristic case $\ell=p$. The biextension gives the map (5.3.7) on the abelian parts. The paragraph identifies how much of its kernel the preceding argument controls.

**Scans 197 and 195, pp. −59− and −60−.** Theorem 5.4 states that the canonical divisorial correspondence is a perfect duality. Its sentence ends on 195 by identifying each abelian scheme with the dual of the other. The proof announced there uses a construction of Raynaud. [T3]

**Scans 195 and 193, pp. −60− and −61−.** Section 5.5 introduces the character groups of the two maximal tori, first on the special fibre and then over the complete base. Formulas (5.5.4)–(5.5.9) run through the split case, finite stages, the limit and the quotients determined by orthogonality. The primes exchange sides between the two parallel formulas.

**Scan 191, p. −62−.** Proposition 5.6 collects the toric, fixed and quotient pieces over a complete trait. Section 5.6.1 relates the two splitting conditions to the isogeny of the tori and records the existence of a smallest étale extension with the required property.

**Scan 189, p. −64−.** Theorem 5.8, attributed to M. RAYNAUD, characterizes the fixed part by factorization of homomorphisms on the generic fibre, with a characteristic hypothesis at $\ell=p$. Corollary 5.9 gives the corresponding unique extension statement for ind-groups. Typescript p. −63− does not occur among the supplied scans.

**Scans 190 and 192, manuscript.** The two augmentations constrain the difference $f$ between the image of the first coordinate in degree zero and the coordinate itself. It belongs to the intersection of two divided-power ideals. The boxed expansion has positive indices in both variables. The next sheet introduces $B_i$, asks what becomes of it on exchanging $X_0$ and $X_1$, and computes the coefficients denoted by $C_i$.

**Scan 194.** A proposition seeks a unique endomorphism in each simplicial degree from the endomorphism in degree one. A note up the left edge qualifies the proposition by $2B_i=0$ for all $i\geq1$. The insertion in the corollary explicitly refers to that correction. The proof reduces compatibility with a map of simplices to its verification on generating images. [H1]

**Scans 196 and 198.** The proof handles the constant maps and the increasing surjections to $\Delta_1$. Their images give partial sums $U_r$. The next sheet recovers the $X_r$ by differences and prescribes the images of the divided powers of the $U_r$. Applying $\sigma_r^*$ to the multiplication relation checks the proposed homomorphism on these generators.

**Scan 200.** Extending the map to the differentials fixes the images of $dU_r$. The text distinguishes square-zero and anticommutation conditions from commutation with the $\xi_{s,i}$. For $s\ne r$ the marginal calculation arrives at $2B_i=0$. A lemma on homomorphisms of differential algebras begins at the foot of the page.

**Scan 202.** A divided-power expansion is rearranged using the circled index read as $\nu$. An abandoned sum precedes the retained expression for the coefficients. The page singles out the coefficient indexed by $(1,1)$ and starts another expression below a horizontal rule.
