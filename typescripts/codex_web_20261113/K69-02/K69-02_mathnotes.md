# K69-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U01 · Roots from pairs of vertices, scans 50–51.** A root is a difference δ′−δ of distinct non-linked vertices. It lies in the orthogonal complement of λ and has square −2. Relative to an ordered six, the author lists one root r₀, fifteen rᵢⱼ and twenty rᵢⱼₕ, together with their opposites. Six ordered pairs give each root. The counts are 72 roots and 432 ordered pairs. The values +1 and −1 against a root distinguish two associated sixes.

**U02 · Reflections and transitivity, scans 51–53.** A direct attempt to recognize the sixes as bases is crossed out. The replacement uses the map sᵣ(x)=x+(x·r)r. It interchanges corresponding vertices of the paired sixes and fixes the others. The author identifies the pair-index reflections with transpositions, then relates the three kinds of roots by conjugacy. He concludes that the reflection group W is transitive on roots, bases and ordered bases and is the full automorphism group of Δ.

**U03 · Simple roots and the Weyl group, scans 54–56.** From the simply transitive action on ordered bases the author obtains |W|=72·6!=51,840. Five successive differences and η−ζ₁−ζ₂−ζ₃ give the six simple roots. He shows how they generate E, expands the three-index roots into partial sums, and obtains the coefficients (1,2,3,2,1,2) for r₀, of weight 11. The displayed diagram has its branch at r₃. The full root-system automorphism group contains W with index two.

**U04 · Recovering the graph, scans 57–58.** The question is how to recognize Δ from its root system and a choice between the two long ends of the diagram. The map δ↦3δ−λ places its vertices in E at square −12. Its image Δ′ and its negative are disjoint W-orbits, interchanged by minus the identity. The text proposes recovering adjacency from the pairing and obtaining roots as differences. Both displayed criteria merit comparison with the definition on 50 and the preceding computation on 57 (S03–S04).

**U05 · Cubic graphs and their bases, scans 59–60.** Two disjoint six-element sets, the matching between them, and vertices indexed by two-element subsets describe a model Δ(I). The rules specify adjacency for the indexed vertices. The author calls the distinguished six a basic part, introduces its associated part and their bibase, and describes categories of graphs equipped with this data. Ordered bases are identified with isomorphisms out of the standard graph, giving commuting torsor actions.

**U06 · List of bases, scans 60–61.** The list has the original pair I and I′, thirty bases indexed by an ordered pair of distinct indices, and two families of twenty indexed by triples. It pairs each base with its associate. The corollaries give the number of bases and the group order, transitivity on non-linked pairs and a category description for an unordered pair. A separate, cancelled attempt studies a free triple J, the common neighbours K, a bibase and another triple J′.

**U07 · Categories and counts, scan 62.** Each row places a structure beside an equivalent category, an automorphism group, and a count. The narrow last column contains sequences of integers without a heading. The author treats ordered and unordered bases of sizes one through six, a half-star, triads and square grids. The final rows give small graphs or names without filling the other columns.

**U08 · Working sheet, scan 63.** The upright working has a bound involving δ-indices, partial sums of the rᵢ and several graphs in loops. In the other orientation the author writes the simple roots in ζ-coordinates and adds multiples of adjacent differences. Several coefficients are cancelled or overwritten. The drawings and formulas stand as calculations without a concluding proposition.

**U09 · Products and root expansions, scan 64.** A pair of sixes accompanies each type of root. Products of pair- and triple-index roots are sorted by intersections of their index sets. Six-ray figures and the final three lines put r₀, r₁₂₃ and r₄₅₆ together. Some labels in the lower figure have been written over.

**U10 · Free subsets and their embeddings, scan 67.** An n-free part is a set of n vertices, no two linked. The maximal ones are six-element bases or five-element intersections E(x)∩E(y). Embeddings of the discrete graph Lᵢ form a single orbit except at i=5. There the five-tuples that extend to a basis and the half-stars give two orbits. The projection diagram records degrees 27, 16, 10, 6 up to size four, then degrees 2 and 1 on the two branches. A regular five-tuple has one extension to size six.

## Remarks of the model

**M01 · Model remark, high confidence.** The two ways of counting to 72 agree. The list on 50 gives 2(1+15+20) roots. The list on 60–61 gives 2+30+20+20 bases. This is an arithmetic check of the two lists. Their claimed geometric correspondence is the author's statement.

**M02 · Model remark, high confidence.** The coefficient list in the box on 56 gives weight 11 and expands to 2η−Σζᵢ. With the diagonal pairing (1,−1,…,−1) on the η,ζ coordinates, the five nonzero off-diagonal entries between the simple roots reproduce the edges of the drawn diagram on 54, including the edge from r₃ to r₆.

**M03 · Model remark, high confidence for the arithmetic.** Taking the arrow degrees on 67 as given yields 25,920 ordered four-tuples. There are 51,840 regular ordered five-tuples and 25,920 ordered half-stars. Dividing by 5! gives 432 and 216 respectively. The two branches explain why a single count of five-bases on 62 does not count every free five-set.

**M04 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 55, last two displays.** The frozen last summand reads “r with subscript 2h or 3h”, with 2h first. The underbrace immediately above is r₃ₕ, and the expansion below starts the third sum at r₃. Those neighbouring formulas favour 3h as the intended summand. They do not resolve the short pen stroke.

**M05 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 63, inverted calculation beside the brace.** The frozen reading has a doubtful 2 before r₅ and a doubtful deleted 2 before ζ₅. In view of the visible right side ζ₅−ζ₆, a cancelled initial coefficient on the left is another possibility. The stroke is doubled, and it is unclear whether this was a cancellation or reinforcement.

**M06 · Model remark, medium confidence.** The rightmost column on 62 can be read as orbit sizes on the 27 vertices. For example its first row gives 1+10+16 and its six-base row gives 6+6+15. Both sums are 27. The column is untitled and row 5 totals 29, so this interpretation does not settle every cell.

**M07 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 51, line 20, oblique margin.** The frozen wording is “tel que δ soit lié à αᵢ,α′ᵢ”, with “lié” doubtful. A possible fuller reading is “non lié”. A perforation interrupts the line immediately before the end of the word group. The following “i.e. δ orthog.” would favour non-incidence. The hole prevents a letter-by-letter decision.

**M08 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 58, line 10.** The frozen verb between the two equalities is “implique”. A rising hook after its long final horizontal stroke could give the ending of “impliquent”. The written subject is a single equality, which favours the singular but does not determine the ink.
