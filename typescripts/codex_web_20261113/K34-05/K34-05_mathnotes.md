# K34-05 — mathematical notes

Written after the transcription freeze recorded in progress.log. These notes use only scans 84–99 and do not amend the frozen readings. Quoted alternatives indicate reading doubts, not alternative mathematical assertions. Confidence labels concern the observations made here.

## What the pages do

**Setup and comparison problem (84).** The exposition is headed as a geometric approach to the monodromy theorem. For X proper over S it sets up special and geometric generic fibres, coefficient systems Λν and Zℓ, and the group action on generic-fibre cohomology. It passes to a strictly local base for notation and introduces finite extensions, their normalizations and corresponding base changes. The page expresses a desired relation with invariant cohomology rather than proving that desired relation.

**Proper-base-change comparison (85, upper half).** The special fibres after normalization are compared through a radicial extension. A cohomology square and the properness assertion identify the cohomology of X and its base changes. These identifications prepare the subsequent limit of relative-cohomology sequences.

**Exact vanishing-cycle sequence (85–86).** Supported cohomology of each Xα relative to its generic open is passed to a direct limit. The resulting groups Φν^i enter the exact sequence comparing special- and generic-fibre cohomology. The manuscript names them groups of vanishing cycles in dimension i and then takes the projective limit over ν for Zℓ coefficients, invoking finiteness.

**Local-to-global spectral sequence (86–87).** A spectral sequence of cohomology with support is passed through the extension system. Its sheaf terms on X0 are denoted by underlined Φ. The calculation of these sheaves is asserted to be local on X. The analogous Zℓ passage is expressly qualified by unresolved limit and constructibility requirements.

**Smooth calculation and specialization (87–88).** Under ℓ≠p and smoothness, purity and smooth base change are invoked to calculate the supported-cohomology sheaves. Transition maps are described by extension degrees. Their limit yields vanishing of the cycle sheaves and then the specialization isomorphism for a proper smooth morphism. Several degree/twist/index irregularities in this working calculation remain literally in the transcription.

**Support and isolated contributions (88–89).** Proposition 1.17 places the cycle-sheaf support in the nonsmooth locus T. Under the discrete-locus assumption the spectral sequence reduces to stalk contributions. The next page describes the finite-locus case as a sum of purely local terms in the étale topology, while reserving examples for later.

**Inertia and a general generic open (89–90).** The text records inertia actions on the groups and sheaves and on the two comparison sequences. It proposes obtaining monodromy from local triviality statements for these sheaf actions, including a common open subgroup. The construction is then extended to an arbitrary open U of the generic fibre using Y=X−U, with supported groups, sheaves and a local-to-global spectral sequence.

**Tame and prime-to-ℓ variants (91–92).** The discussion restricts to tamely ramified extensions and uses a Hochschild–Serre spectral expression to identify tame cohomology with P-invariants. Radical extensions K(n) give an explicit indexing family. Tame versions of the exact and spectral sequences are written down. The later paragraph considers a closed normal prime-to-ℓ subgroup Q and a quotient covering the Zℓ(1) component. A marginal qualification concerns eliminating the P-invariant superscript after a finite extension.

**Leray formulation (93–95).** Section 2 replaces the relative-cohomology viewpoint by a Leray spectral sequence for the geometric generic open mapping to the strictly localized total space. It retains the action of the full π, not only inertia. Proper base change gives the underlined Ψ sheaves, and (2.8) relates them to Φ. The first inserted leaf gives the tame analogue with P-invariants. The displayed restrictions, parenthesis omissions and overwritten labels are preserved as written.

**Constructibility proposal (96).** The second inserted leaf proposes that resolution of singularities should establish constructibility of Φ (equivalently Ψ) and a constructible ℓ-adic projective limit. If available, this would produce ℓ-adic spectral sequences. Both the proposed result and the suggested reduction from the general case to a typical example remain tentative on the page.

**Normal-crossing monodromy statement (97–98).** Section 3 assumes regular X, a normal-crossing complement, ℓ≠p, and the stated purity condition. Theorem 3.2 asserts finite generation and essential unipotence, with a more precise subgroup index derived from the prime-to-p part of the least common multiple of special-fibre multiplicities. The filtration and nilpotence expression (3.3) give an echelon bound. The next page states an analytic analogue and announces further study of the canonical filtration.

**Conditional corollary and proof (98–99).** Corollary 3.5 assumes strong resolution and purity in the stated range. Its continuation claims essential unipotence for a smooth quasi-projective generic open, although the displayed live cohomology space is Y, not U. The proof reduces to a complete base and trivial P-action by references to I, chooses a proper model, and invokes resolution to reach Theorem 3.2. The last remark conditionally invokes Nagata compactification and continues beyond the supplied batch.

Later, « il est plausible que le cas général puisse s’y ramener » proposes reduction to the typical case. The typical-case reference is a doubtful overwritten 5.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark — high confidence:** the pages themselves give two comparison constructions, the relative-cohomology Φ construction and the Leray Ψ construction, then explicitly connect them in (2.8). This is a structural observation about the text, not an additional theorem.
- **Model remark — high confidence:** the finite-locus simplification and local nature of the sheaves explain why the notes turn toward regular models and normal-crossing complements. This connection is visible in the sequence of constructions and the author’s own forward references; no proof beyond the pages is being supplied.
- **Model remark — low confidence:** the notation and punctuation irregularities collected under Slips are evidence of a working draft, but they do not justify a uniform correction of indices or coefficient conventions. Several are tied to overwritten or doubtful glyphs.
- **Reading question arising after the freeze — low confidence (98, corollary heading; 99, inserted hypothesis and proof):** the frozen text places « de dim relative n » inside the cancelled U hypothesis on 98, while the live inserted hypothesis on 99 says only « soit U lisse quasi-projectif sur K, et ». The proof on 99 again inserts « de dim relative n ». One possible intended formulation is a live bound dim U≤n (or dim U=n), but whether the earlier dimension insertion was intended to survive the cancellation is a reading/state question. The cancellation prevents confidence above low. The frozen transcription has not been changed.
- **Model remark — medium confidence:** the live Y in 99’s corollary statement is inconsistent with the U-based hypothesis and proof as transcribed. U is a plausible intended replacement, already recorded under Slips; this observation does not authorize changing the clearly forked written letter.
- **Model remark — high confidence:** the final Nagata sentence is incomplete within the supplied scans. Its continuation and any eventual qualification cannot be inferred from this batch.
