# Mathematical notes · 161-2

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 4.** The first theorem starts with diagram types $d=(d',d'')$, a smallness assumption on $C$, and specified projective and inductive cones $\delta$. It seeks a $\delta$-exact functor $i:C\to\bar C$. Its universal property is an equivalence of categories of functors, induced by composition with $i$. The remark identifies two earlier problems as special cases.

**U2 · Scans 4–6.** At a successor stage the construction applies $K$ to $(C_i,\delta_i)$. The limit stage takes a direct limit of categories and the images of the earlier cones. Scan 5 lists the conditions on each transition, including the uniqueness and existence clauses for augmentations and an equivalence on Hom categories. The marginal formula $(c^*)$ describes which cones are retained. A bound involving the numbers of arrows in the indexing categories enters the choice of $\omega$.

**U3 · Scans 6–7.** The author describes $\rho$ in three steps. He takes an envelope, equalises pairs of arrows, then adjoins a mediating arrow for an augmentation. A dual construction $\sigma$ supplies three further operations. He sets $K=\sigma\rho$ and gives a differently ordered example $K'$.

**U4 · Scans 7–10.** Three families $f_\lambda^{(i)}$ specify conditions on restriction along a functor. A second theorem seeks a universal functor into $\Delta_f$. Scan 8 gives separate successor conditions on pairs of arrows, lifting a transformation, and lifting an object up to isomorphism. Scan 9 relates these to the required properties at the limit. Scan 10 records the outstanding successor construction.

**U5 · Scans 11–12.** The list of conditions on a category $C$ starts with unconditional and conditional existence of limits. It next asks when exactness for one collection of cones forces exactness for another. Restriction along $\varphi:\Theta\to\bar\Theta$ then supplies conditions involving faithfulness, surjectivity on Hom sets and essential image. Scan 12 writes two opposite inclusions for the essential image.

**U6 · Scans 12–13.** A triple $(\Theta,\delta,\lambda)$ tests a functor $F$. The functors $u:\Theta\to C$ satisfying the $\delta$ condition provide cones $u(\lambda)$ for $F$ to preserve. The discussion turns to composition, identity functors, and the possible restriction on $C$ itself. A branched diagram places the two sets of cones beside the composable functors.

**U7 · Scans 13–15.** After a cancelled plan, the notes introduce a class $\Sigma_\rho$ of functors and a class $\Delta_\sigma$ of categories. These supply the arrows and objects for $\Delta_{\sigma,\rho}$. A slanted note on scan 14 discusses “distinguished” sub-2-categories. Scan 15 lists the data again. Its third item, including the relationship between $\rho$ and the data in $\sigma$, is struck through.

**U8 · Scan 16, cancelled draft.** A limit-stage argument tries to descend a diagram, compatible families of arrows and an equality to an earlier $C_j$. Then the sheet starts another statement of the envelope theorem and restarts the transfinite construction. The diagonal cancellation runs through the proof and the new formulation.

**U9 · Scan 18, §§1–2.** A small $C$ with finite limits represents a theory $T_0$. A displayed chain passes from $T_0(E)$ to functors out of $\widehat C$ and, for a topos $E$, to the opposite of a Hom category of toposes. The next paragraph starts with a small $I$, forms its $\Delta_0$-envelope using functors of finite presentation, then applies the hatted construction.

**U10 · Scans 18 §3–19.** Given arrows $\Sigma$ in a topos $R$, the author saturates them by ST1–ST3 and writes a localisation. He relates the resulting subtopos to finer topologies on a small site, then discusses the smallness of the set of axioms and the full subtheory associated with an inclusion.

**U11 · Scan 19 §4.** Part a) characterises an algebraic $R$ by a small generating subcategory. Part b) considers $b:I\to R$ for discrete $I$ and asks for the full subcategory generated by finite products of its image to be strictly generating. The sheet announces a construction of the represented basic theory for the sufficiency direction.
