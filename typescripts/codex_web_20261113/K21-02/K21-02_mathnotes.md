# Mathematical notes · cote 21, scans 17–29

## What the pages do

**U1 · Proposition 3, scans 17, 20–22.** For a sheaf of sets on a locally noetherian spectral space, the proposition compares \(P_1\) or \(P_2\) relative to a closed subset \(Y\) with the corresponding condition at every \(x\in Y\). The restriction of sections is to be injective for \(P_1\) and bijective for \(P_2\).

**U2 · Corollary 4, scans 17 and 19.** Restrict the sheaf to an open \(X'\) and take a closed \(Y'\) contained in \(X'\cap Y\). The corollary passes the restriction property to this smaller support. Its proof factors restriction through an intermediate open set. A marginal note on scan 17 says this step does not use local noetherianity.

**U3 · Lemma 5 and its auxiliary lemma, scans 18, 19 and 21.** Lemma 5 identifies sections on \(V_{(x)}\) with a direct limit of sections on \(W\cap V\) as \(W\) runs through open neighbourhoods of \(x\). Scan 18 compares locally constructible subsets near \(x\). It then lifts a section locally to a collection of opens. Scan 21 compares the lifts on their intersections and shrinks around \(x\) to make them agree.

**U4 · Proof of Proposition 3, scans 20–22.** In one direction Grothendieck uses noetherian induction on the closed support. He compares germs to establish uniqueness, then uses the pointwise \(P_2\) condition and the lemma to extend a section near a point. In the other direction he applies Corollary 4 to \(\overline{x}\) and uses Lemma 5 twice to identify the map on the local space. In the middle display on scan 21, the limit sign appears only on the left of the comparison.

**U5 · Definition 6, scans 22–23.** For a category fibred over the opens of \(X\), the restriction from \(V\) to \(V\cap U\), with \(U=X-Y\), is required to be faithful, fully faithful or an equivalence for \(P_1,P_2,P_3\), respectively. The first two conditions are expressed on sheaves of morphisms between pairs of objects. The third also requires objects on the smaller open to come from objects on \(V\) up to isomorphism. The sideways note on scan 22 gives the conditions relative to the singleton consisting of the closed point.

**U6 · Localization of the fibred category, scans 22–24.** Scan 22 gives a pointwise condition using two pseudolimits. Scan 24 constructs the category over an open of the local space by varying the ambient open sets whose localizations contain the given open or agree with it. The displayed comparison identifies the localization of a sheaf of morphisms with the sheaf of morphisms between the localized objects.

**U7 · Proposition 7 and Corollary 8, scans 25–26.** Proposition 7 compares the relative and pointwise conditions for fibred categories. Proposition 3 supplies the first two cases through the morphism sheaves. Corollary 8 passes to a smaller closed support. The triangle on scan 26 is the calculation for its equivalence case.

**U8 · The object-extension argument, scans 26–27.** For the forward implication the author passes to isomorphism classes and takes a direct limit. Conversely, he chooses a maximal point of the remaining closed set. An object near that point agrees up to isomorphism with the given object on the overlap after shrinking. Choosing the isomorphism gives an object on \(W\cup U\), to which the induction hypothesis applies.

**U9 · Corollary 10, scan 28.** The three properties are compared with vanishing of supported cohomology sheaves in degrees \(i<1,\ i<2,\ i<3\). For the third property the page describes \(\underline H^2_Y(F)\) using the presheaf \(V\mapsto H^1(V\cap U,F)\). It compares this with \(H^1(V,F)\) in one direction and invokes the local extension argument of Proposition 7 in the other.

**U10 · Proposition 9, scan 29.** For the fibred category of principal homogeneous spaces under a sheaf of groups \(F\), the proposition compares \(P_1,P_2\) for the category and for the sheaf. The trivial object identifies its own morphism sheaf with \(F\). For an arbitrary pair the identification is local as a sheaf of sets. The last step expresses the first two supported cohomology sheaves as the kernel and cokernel of restriction. The oblique note reads « Pas d’hyp. noethérienne sur \(X\) », with the accent on the adjective uncertain.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 19 and 26 · high confidence.** The two triangles use the same order of restriction. In the categorical triangle the diagonal and the vertical leg are equivalences. The sentence below the diagram on scan 26 names the three functors and concludes that the horizontal one is an equivalence. This conclusion is independent of the shape of the initial open-set letter.

**M2 · Scans 22 and 27 · medium confidence.** The move from an isomorphism over \(W\cap U\) to an object over \(W\cup U\) uses gluing of objects. The definition on these sheets calls the input a « catégorie fibrée ». The sheets do not state a separate effective descent hypothesis here, so they leave open what earlier assumptions justify that step.

**M3 · Scans 28–29 · high confidence.** The archive order puts Corollary 10 before « Cas particulier » and Proposition 9. The latter supplies a group-sheaf interpretation of the low-degree conditions that appear in the former. The archive numbers and heading numbers can both be read independently.

**Reading question · scan 25, clause (i) · low confidence.** The first frozen candidate after « rel. à » is \(x\), with \(Y\) as the alternative. The statement introduces a closed \(Y\) and quantifies over its points in (ii). That favours \(Y\) in (i), but the crossing strokes also permit \(x\).

**Reading question · scan 24, middle and foot · low confidence.** In the middle object formula the frozen alternatives are \(U\) and \(V\). The final formula has \(\mathcal F(U)\) after naming a neighbourhood \(V\). The local argument favours \(V\) in both places. The written round-bottom forms are the reason for retaining \(U\).

**Reading question · scan 17, last line · low confidence.** In « Soit \(U\) un ouvert quelconque de \(X\) », the frozen reading is \(U\). Its bottom is rounded and wider than the preceding \(U'\). It could also be \(V\), the open-set letter at the start of the restriction triangle on scan 19.

**Reading question · scan 22, sideways note beside Definition 6 · low confidence.** The frozen text treats the note beginning « Si \(X\) est loc. » as a marginal comment. A slanted, dot-ended stroke precedes that note when it is turned upright. A similar stroke follows « équivalence de catégories). » in the body. They may direct the reader to insert the note there, immediately before « On dit que ».

**Reading question · scan 26, top side of the triangle · low confidence.** The frozen diagram shows a rightward arrow labelled \(\rho'\). Its horizontal line ends in a tiny backward stroke below the right tip. A short arrowhead is the likelier reading, though a plain line thickened at the pen lift remains possible. The prime above the line is distinct.

**Reading question · scan 28, first line of the reverse implication · low confidence.** The frozen text cancels both « On » and the word read as « est » or « sera » before « ramené ». The ink over the second word is clear. The heavy horizontal stroke through the lower bowl of « On » might belong to the word itself. In that case « On » would be live.
