# K137-01 · Mathematical notes

The scan numbers refer to the archive PDF. The English typescripts on 003 and 005 are separate excerpts. Neither bears a signature. The paragraph identifiers below connect mathematical observations to the check-event register.

## What the pages do

**002 · A projectivity criterion.** Lemme 1 makes vanishing of all $\pi_i(L_*)$, together with projectivity of the components, sufficient for projectivity of a $\tfrac12$-simplicial module. The proof passes to chains and exhibits a two-term complex with the same module in two adjacent degrees. The NB allows a projective $\pi_0$ to remain and discusses an extra summand in degree zero. The corollary names products involving $\Gamma$, $\Lambda$, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$.

**003 · The Hirsch construction.** In a principal bundle the differential on a fibre generator is specified by a representative of a characteristic class. The typescript then applies the construction one fibration at a time to a Postnikov system. The diagram distinguishes the fibre $K$ from the two successive stages. The sentence explaining which generators to add runs off the sheet.

**004 · Lemme 2 and a comparison map.** The first display concerns the vanishing of a Hom group between complexes involving two exterior powers of $\Psi$. The argument separates $q'<q$ from the equality case. In the latter the small square places $M$ opposite $L'_q$ and uses the surjection from $L'_{q+1}$. The separate Théorème asks for a morphism of cochain complexes. Its large diagram labels the vertical maps $u_0,u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_n$ and prescribes the final one through a kernel.

**005 · Canonical choices for a Riemannian model.** The tower of maps to compact nil-manifolds begins at the Jacobian. Under the new heading, Hodge decomposition picks out the harmonic representative of a closed form and a distinguished primitive of an exact form. The typescript has just introduced the choices in the map $\rho$ when the page ends.

**007 · Bounds on degrees and ranks.** Under the projectivity, finiteness and connectedness hypotheses, the lemma compares bounded normalized degrees, a finite list of elementary summands, eventual polynomial rank and a bound by $c n^N$. The proof points to an explicit formula. A separate lower-margin observation attempts to extract its leading coefficient.

**008 · Elementary factors (B08).** The proposition compares projectivity of components and homotopy with projectivity of normalized chains and their homology. Its two products give an elementary description on each side of Dold–Puppe. The chains have an adjacent pair $M_i,M_i$ and a single $N_j$ in degree $j$. The proof identifies $N_j$ with homology and $M_i$ with a quotient by cycles.

**009–010 · Finiteness and terminology (B09).** Cor 1 adds finite generation. The product for a fixed $L_n$ stops at $i=n+1$ and $j=n$. Cor 2 discusses when only finitely many indices contribute. An extensively revised paragraph introduces the special objects and the stricter free case. Its last sentence continues on 010 with extension of scalars from $\mathbb Z$.

**010–011 · Tensor products and growth (B11).** Proposition 2 reduces the tensor-product assertion to three choices of elementary factors. The text uses componentwise freeness, Künneth–Eilenberg–Zilber and a finiteness argument. The rank identity on 011 has terms $\mu_i\binom{n+1}{i}$ and $\nu_j\binom n j$. A nonzero term bounds the rank below by a polynomial of its degree. The end of 010 also preserves a cancelled formulation of the growth lemma.

**011–012 · Exterior powers.** In the bracket beginning “2°) Cas de $\Lambda^k$”, the direct-sum formula and passage to direct factors reduce a proposed assertion to powers of the elementary $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ objects. Several stages are interlinear. On 012 the discussion reaches freeness of the components and a homotopy argument for the $\Phi$ case. A question mark stands beside that step.

**012–013 · Recovering multiplicities (E13).** The Remarque subtracts the contribution of the homotopy ranks from the component-rank polynomial. The remaining binomial coefficients give the $\mu_i$. Scan 013 reverses the question and requires $\mu_0=0$ and nonnegative $\mu_i$ for $i\geq1$. Evaluation at $-1$ identifies the constant obstruction with the difference between $f(-1)$ and the alternating sum of the $\nu_j$.

**013–014 · A change of definition.** A crossed draft uses “chaîne-parfait” and the English gloss “strictly chain-perfect”. On 014 a fresh Déf 1 defines $\partial$-pseudo-coherence and $\partial$-perfection, with their strict variants, through the associated chain complex. The numbering restarts.

**014–015 · Component and filtration criteria.** Prop 1 lists conditions on normalized chains, on the modules $L_n$, and on finite successive extensions. A slanted addition at c) discusses eventual polynomial rank. Parts d) and e) introduce noetherian and regular hypotheses. Across the page break the residue-field ranks give a polynomial whose binomial coefficients are described using the normalized components. The text then discusses the variation of those coefficients with the prime.

**015 · The nonregular case and the stability note.** The NB says the preceding fibre condition remains necessary over a nonregular noetherian ring and proposes a map from a strictly $\partial$-perfect object inducing isomorphisms on the $\pi_i$. A separate slanted note lists sums, extensions, tensor products and power operations for the strict notions. The final lines ask for a structure theorem.

**016 · A principal-ring decomposition.** The theorem separates free elementary $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ factors from a third family indexed by degree and divisor. For each fixed degree only finitely many divisor multiplicities are nonzero. The final sentence seeks an ordered set of relevant divisors and breaks after “moyennant”.

## Remarks of the model

**R12 · Model remark, low confidence.** The proof of the Remarque on 012 recovers integer ranks. B08 on 008 describes the coefficient modules up to isomorphism, and B09 on 009 explicitly separates a case in which they are free. Recovering their ranks recovers their isomorphism classes in that free case. Whether the stronger assumption was meant here is unclear because the short word before “spécial” has been cancelled.

**E13 · Model check, high confidence.** In the evaluation on 013 the numerator of $P_j(-1)$ has $j$ negative factors of magnitudes $1$ through $j$. Division by $j!$ leaves $(-1)^j$, including $P_0(-1)=1$. This accounts for the sign in the alternating sum under the vertical equality.

**R13 · Model remark, medium confidence.** In the converse question on 013 a finite-support qualification on the prescribed $\nu_j$ would explain why their binomial contribution is a polynomial. Scan 009 already requires finitely many nonzero summands in the special case. For example, taking every $\nu_j=1$ gives $\sum_{j=0}^n\binom n j=2^n$. The question may carry the earlier finiteness hypothesis implicitly.

**Reading question, 008, L14.** The fragment says “complexes de cochaînes”. The earlier a′ line says “complexe de chaînes”, and the drawn two-term factors run from degree $i$ to degree $i-1$. The middle of the written compound at b′ looks long enough to admit “cochaînes”. Compare the two words on the scan before using them to infer a change of convention.

**Reading question, 014, L10.** The fragment reads “ce qui suit se … cas spécial”. On the enlarged crop the first group in the parenthesis can also look like “supposant $k$”, with “connexe” a possible start on the line below. The split across lines and the bracket arriving from the margin leave the scope of the polynomial alternative uncertain.

**Reading question, 015, L12.** The frozen first choices in the underlined passage were “Pour … tout”; “Dévis. … précis” were already alternatives. The two words head the request leading into 016, and their underlines run close to the staff. This makes a request for a precise dévissage plausible. The following article, originally read “un”, was settled as “des” during the marked-place comparison.
