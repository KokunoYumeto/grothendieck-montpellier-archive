# Cote 14 · Mathematical notes on scans 45–58

## What the pages do

**Letter, weak Lefschetz and cycles (45).** The letter resumes in mid-sentence. A′ asks for surjectivity of the direct image from cycles on Y in codimension i to cycles on X in codimension i+1 when 2i ≥ n−1. The equivalent wording describes cycles of dimension j < n/2 up to τ-equivalence and with rational coefficients. The writer discusses the field of definition required in trying to recover A.

**Letter, positivity and consequences (45–46).** For n = 2m, B concerns the form on the kernel of multiplication by ξ on middle cycles. The writer distinguishes positivity sufficient for his stated application to Weil from positive definiteness, to which he attaches further consequences. He goes on to finite generation, an injection into cohomology, semisimplicity of motives and correspondences, and an image criterion for a map induced by a correspondence. He raises the possibility of abelian parameter varieties related to odd cohomology.

**Letter, the affine question (46–47).** A″ restates A′ as the vanishing of Cᶦ(U) for 2i > n on U = X−Y. The writer asks whether the assertion extends to other smooth affine varieties and whether a stronger equivalence holds on smooth varieties generally. His geometric problem is how to deform a small-dimensional cycle to infinity. The N.B. after the signature specifies the coefficients in Hᶦ and the convention of suppressing a tensor twist.

**Cover and survey (48–49).** The cover groups abelian varieties, Picard and Albanese. The résumé associates a variety B_X to divisors and A_X to zero-cycles. Choosing one of these two constructions on each side gives four cases. A suitable cycle on X × Y acts on points. The proposed homomorphism of abelian varieties depends only on its numerical equivalence class.

**Realization and inverse (49–50).** The survey expresses the action in degree-one cohomology, using the displayed identifications for the Albanese and Picard varieties. It relates a cohomological isomorphism to an isogeny, then claims that a rational correspondence realizes the inverse on cohomology. An example is cup-product by classes of ample divisors on X.

**The four cases (51).** Case I compares the two Albanese objects, case II the two Picard objects, and III and IV mix the two. The abelian-variety maps along the bottoms point left. A margin note pairs I with II, and the two mixed cases bear the caption « Cas auto-transposé ». The remarks below the chart identify maps to invert and ask about denominators.

**Th. 1.1 and Cor. 1.2 (52).** With dim X = n and dim Y = m, a cycle Z of dimension m on X × Y is to induce a unique map u_Z from A_Y to A_X. On degree-zero cycles for which the operation is defined, the two ways of taking the Albanese value agree. The text says that u_Z depends only on the numerical class of Z. The corollary records the consequence for cycles with zero Albanese value.

**Uniqueness in 1.1 (52).** The proof first takes an open U in Y on which the action can be evaluated. It then represents points of A_Y(k) by degree-zero cycles supported in U. This determines the map on k-points.

**Existence in 1.1 (53–54).** The construction sends X to its Albanese torsor X′, pushes Z forward to Z′ and forms B × Z′. A canonical Picard class on B × X′ enters an intersection that is pushed forward to B × Y. Its numerical class yields a divisor correspondence c′. Cartier biduality identifies the target of the resulting map with A_X. The marginal request for a reference stands beside the passage from numerical divisor classes to the correspondence class.

**Cor. 1.3 and Lemmata 1.4–1.5 (54–56).** Cor. 1.3 says that the constructed map has the required action on cycles. Lemma 1.4 relates the Albanese value to evaluation of c′. The following leaf interprets the desired equality in Pic⁰(B). Lemma 1.5 chooses a divisor D in the specified linear equivalence class and gives an equality in Cyc¹(B), requiring both sides to be defined. The writer omits its intersection-theoretic verification.

**Th. 1.6 (56).** With the notation of 1.1, the cohomology class of Z acts from H¹(X) to H¹(Y). The square compares this action with the degree-one map induced by u_Z. The lower horizontal arrow goes from the cohomology of A_X to that of A_Y. The two upward arrows carry pullback stars.

**Cor. 1.7 (57).** For abelian X and Y, a degree-one cohomology map is induced by an algebraic cycle of the indicated codimension exactly when it comes from a homomorphism u from Y to X. The text asserts uniqueness of u for fixed Z and independence of the prime used for the coefficients.

**Rem. 1.8 (57–58).** The remark passes to smooth projective schemes over a base S with geometrically connected fibres and an n-cocycle on their fibre product. It states a relative map when the Albanese objects exist and sketches a more general version using a map out of X. The construction on geometric fibres refers back to the preceding one.

**Section 2 and Prop. 2.1 (58).** After a cancelled first formulation, a one-cycle on X × Y is used to state a map from B_X to A_Y. It is characterized on algebraically trivial divisors D on X for which the transposed correspondence is defined. A marginal question considers exchanging X and Y. The scan ends in the next sentence after « Cet homomorphisme ».
