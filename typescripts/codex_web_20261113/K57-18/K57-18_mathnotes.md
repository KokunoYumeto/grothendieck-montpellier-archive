# K57-18 · Mathematical notes

Source for all scan citations: Fonds Alexandre Grothendieck. (n.d.). *Picard* (cote 57, scans 287–292 and 294–301) [Manuscript notes and typescripts]. Université de Montpellier. The sheet numbers below are the PDF scan numbers. Quoted wording follows the final transcription. Open readings are identified where they affect an interpretation.

## What the pages do

**Differentials on a quotient (scans 287–288).** The triangle labels the map from $A\times_S B$ to $X$ by Γ. The first display writes the pushforward of relative differentials as invariant factors. For abelian schemes with Γ acting on $B$ by translations, the sheet compares global one-forms with first cohomology of the structure sheaf. The last line of the calculation contrasts the invariant and coinvariant tangent-space dimensions. The desired rank difference may have either sign and may vary under specialization. [U1]

**Divisibility on two fibres (scans 289–290).** Over the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, the sheet takes two finite subgroups $F,G$ in an abelian scheme $X$. It writes $A=X/F$, $B=X/G$ and factors $p\beta$ through a map $u$ from $A$ to $B$. Its argument uses the rational Hom groups and the different inclusions of $F$ and $G$ on the two fibres. The proposed realization uses an elliptic curve with different $p$-torsion behaviour on its closed and generic fibres. [U2]

**A Néron–Severi section (scan 290).** For $C=A\times B'$ with « $B'$ dual de $B$ », the relative Néron–Severi display includes a Hom factor. The section attached to $u$ has zero first and second components. Its specialization satisfies $\bar u_0=p\bar v_0$. The sheet argues that a global division by $p$ would give a section of the Hom factor, contradicting the preceding argument. [U3]

**Divisible groups and two functors (scan 291).** The sheet first classifies divisible torsion groups. For a $p$-primary torsion group $M$ with finite $\,{}_pM$, it gives three equivalent descriptions, including a decomposition $F+(Q_p/\mathbb Z_p)^\rho$. The quotient by the largest divisible subgroup $M^0$ determines $F$, and $\,{}_pM^0$ determines $\rho$. It then defines the contravariant $T_p(M)=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p)$ and the covariant $T^p(M)=\varprojlim{}_{p^n}M$, with transition map $p^{m-n}$ for $m>n$. [U4]

**Duality modulo finite groups (scan 292).** The leaf identifies $T^p(M)$ with $\operatorname{Hom}(T_p(M),\mathbb Z_p)$. It describes the effect of killing finite groups, then lists six tests for a homomorphism of divisible $p$-groups. They concern the image and cokernel on $T_p$, reduction and tensor product, surjectivity on $T^p$, a direct factor, and surjectivity on elements killed by $p$. [U5]

**First-order Picard criteria (scan 294).** Under « $X$ famille du premier ordre » in equal characteristic $p>0$, the first diagram pairs the existence of $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^{00}_{X/S}$ with $\operatorname{Im}(u\mu)\subset\operatorname{Im}(v)$. The second starts from flatness at $x_0$. It places a boundary map, the differential $d$, and a map labelled « c cup » in a triangle, followed by the displayed criterion $\rho\partial(x)=0\Longleftrightarrow\rho c(x)=0$. [U6]

**Diangles and a projective coordinate (scan 295).** The typescript attaches a diangle to a similitude carrying $D$ to $D'$. It parametrizes the classes in $G_o/H$ by $\tau(X)=\eta/\xi$ in $k_\infty$, omitting the roots of $\delta\tau^2=1$. Formula (9) gives the product law with denominator $1+\delta\tau(X)\tau(X')$. The subsequent kernel is $\{\pm1_E\}$. Proposition 2 begins a statement that $\mathbb N(X)^{-1}X^2$ is a rotation. [U7]

**Derived inverse limits and Ext (scan 296).** The first spectral sequence abuts to Ext out of a direct limit. With $B=\Lambda$ regular and the stated choice of $A_\alpha$, the vanishing region gives an isomorphism for $p\geq\dim\Lambda-k$ and completes the induction for Theorem 2. Remark 2 singles out the filtered-index and finite-type hypotheses. [U8]

**Two spectral sequences with common abutment (scan 296).** Proposition 2 places inverse limits before Ext in one $E_2$ term and after Ext in the other. Under regularity, filteredness and finite generation, the first gives $H^s=0$ for $s>\dim\Lambda$. The second yields the listed Ext vanishings in dimension one and in dimension $\nu>1$. The sentence beginning « Mais ceci » continues beyond the leaf. [U9]

**Picard groups and tangent factors (scan 297).** A landscape diagram begins with $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau_Y\simeq\widehat A_1\times\widehat B^\Gamma$ and sets the first cohomology of $Y$ above that of $X$. A second group of arrows compares the two tangent calculations and singles out $H^1(B,\underline O_B)\otimes t_A$ with an oval. Below it stand opposite arrows on tangent tensors and a chain through $\mu_2$, $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^*_Y$ and $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\Gamma_X$. The short line leading to the final $\mu_2$ has no secure arrowhead. [U10]

**Gluing two punctured planes (scans 298 and 300).** « Contre-exemples par recollement » opens with a pushout diagram. Each $X_i$ is an affine plane minus its origin. The two maps from the punctured line are given by $(x,y)=(t,0)$ and $(1/t,0)$. The text claims that $X'=X_1\amalg X_2$ is quasi-affine and the pushout $X=X_1\amalg_Y X_2$ is not. It describes global functions on $X$ as pairs satisfying $f_1(t,0)=f_2(1/t,0)$. Scan 300 continues the argument. [U11]

**Field extensions and editorial revisions (scan 299).** The typescript proposes treating regular extensions before primary extensions. For the « lemme de Zariski », an element $u=a(X)/b(X)$ algebraic over $K(X)$ has finite orbit, hence a stabilizer of finite index. The typescript passes from invariance of the fraction to invariance of its numerator and denominator [A11]. A separate argument compares two field degrees in characteristic zero to conclude that $K''=K'$. [U12]

**Taking a quotient of the gluing example (scan 300).** The sheet lets $\mathbb Z/2$ interchange the two pieces and act on the common punctured line by $t\mapsto1/t$. The quotient diagram identifies the quotient of the disjoint union with $X_1$. The text argues that the corresponding quotient of the pushout cannot be quasi-affine and sets aside an affine-neighbourhood condition for finite subsets. [U13]

**Derivations and the arrangement of a chapter (scan 301).** These editorial instructions move from the size of a $p$-basis and the field $F^{p^k}$ to derivations of extensions « du premier étage ». They request a spanning criterion for the $dx_i$ and propose restating Proposition 6 as an equivalence among separability, extension of derivations, and injectivity on differentials. [U14]

**Constructing extensions and rearranging topics (scans 301 and 299).** « Fabrication d'extensions » is to follow the treatment of cyclic extensions. The proposed examples range from Kummer and Artin–Schreier to « la wittification ». The next heading places material in commutative algebra. Its « Extensions régulières et consorts » runs into scan 299, the next page according to the typescript’s own numbering. [U15]

## Statements left without proof

**287–288 [A1].** « Nous voulons donner des exemples » introduces three aims. The difference of the two ranks should be positive, negative, or variable in a family. The decompositions precede this request; no such family is constructed on these two leaves.

**288 [A2].** The four announced consequences follow « Comme conséquence, on aura des exemples ». They concern failure of symmetry in either direction, failure of continuity for both $H^0(X,\Omega^1_X)$ and $H^1(X,\underline O_X)$, failure of flatness of $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau_{X/S}$, and absence of a « bon » abelian subscheme. The last is restated as failure of the reduced Picard construction to commute with specialization.

**289–290 [A3].** « Les conditions dites (i)(ii)(iii) peuvent être réalisées, en égales ou inégales car ». The elliptic-curve paragraph describes the two fibres and the choice of distinct subgroups of rank $p$. It does not supply an equation for a family. The opening of 290 invokes « un seul sous-groupe » on the closed fibre.

**290 [A4].** The displayed decomposition of $\underline{\mathrm{NS}}_{C/Y}$ and the assertion that a candidate $\bar v$ « nécessairement aurait ses deux premières composantes nulles » are used without supporting arguments on this leaf.

**291 [A5].** « Conditions équivalentes » introduces the three descriptions. « $\rho$ est défini de façon unique » gives the rank formula and the isomorphism class of the finite quotient. The statement that $M\mapsto T_p(M)$ « établit une antiéquivalence » with finitely generated $\mathbb Z_p$-modules is also asserted on this leaf.

**292 [A6].** « seulement exact “mod groupes finis” » qualifies the exactness of $T^p$. The text says that $T^p$ vanishes exactly on the finite groups in question, and gives an equivalence modulo finite groups with finite-dimensional $\mathbb Q_p$-vector spaces. The six tests are headed « Conditions équivalentes ». No argument is supplied here.

**294 [A7].** « Cette condition est automatiquement vérifiée en les pts » of $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\sigma_{X_0/k}$. The marginal justification is that $\mathrm{Pic}^0_{X/k}(k)$ is « divisible par $p$ ». The leaf gives no further argument for the indicated implication.

**295 [A8].** Formula (9) is followed by « dont la vérification est immédiate », first for finite coordinates and then « dans tous les autres cas ». The leaf ends after « est une rotation » in Proposition 2.

**296 [A9].** The second remark says « n'est pas valable en général si $I$ n'est pas filtrant, ou si les $M_\alpha$ ne sont pas de type fini ». No example is supplied on this page. Proposition 2 is introduced by « on démontre facilement » with a reference to methods in (²).

**297 [A10].** The top comparison « $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau_Y\simeq\widehat A_1\times\widehat B^\Gamma$ » and the cohomology decompositions are displayed without a proof. The heavy crossing through the note between the two upper rows obscures part of its explanation.

**299 [A11].** The stabilizer argument says « on en déduit que $a(X)=a^s(X)$ et $b(X)=b^s(X)$ » after imposing that the polynomials be « étrangers ». No choice of scalar for the pair is specified on this sheet. See R5.

**299 [A12].** « Il faut aussi montrer que » introduces preservation of relative algebraic closedness « (ou quasi) » after a finite algebraic extension. What follows is expressly « valable en caract. 0 ». The general version is left open here.

**300 [A13].** « Il faut cependant vérifier que tte … partie finie … est contenue dans un ouvert affine ». This is followed by « c’est facile » and a bracketed construction using a line $D$. The last expression reaches the bottom edge before a conclusion is given.

**301 [A14].** « un cor. disant qu'il existe une dérivation envoyant n'importe où les éléments d'une famille $p$-indépendante » and « donner aussi une condition nécessaire et suffisante pour que les $dx_i$ engendrent » are instructions for results to be stated. The three assertions proposed for Proposition 6 are likewise given as a plan.

## Questions and conjectures

**287–288 [Q1].** « Nous voulons donner des exemples » also calls for a family in which $A'{}^\Gamma$ « n'est pas plat sur $S$ » or has no « bon » abelian subscheme. The continuation on 288 belongs to the unfinished sentence on 287.

**294 [Q2].** After asserting the condition at the indicated points, the note asks whether it suffices for flatness: « mais il est douteux que $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0_{X/k}$ soit pour autant plat sur $S$ ». 

**296 [Q3].** « nous n'avons que des résultats fragmentaires » refers to the derived limits with $i\leq\dim\Lambda$. A handwritten question next to Proposition 2 may read « A signaler ? » or « A vérifier ? ». Its scope and verb remain uncertain.

**299 [Q4].** The characteristic-zero argument is one « qu'on pourrait essayer d'arranger ». The final paragraph requests the case of principal ideals and suggests « Le fourbi de la p.10 a l'air vrai avec l'hypothèse plus faible » that a zero divisor from $S$ in $R$ is already a zero divisor in $S$.

**300 [Q5].** The finite-subset condition is an explicit check still requested. The words « partie finie » replace an earlier « partie », and the following cancelled word may read « trajectoire ».

**301 [Q6].** « peut être ce qu’on pourra extraire de Klein ou du bouquin de Tchébotarof sur le groupe icosaédrique » is left as a possible addition to the chapter.

## Slips

**287 [S1].** In b′ the first factor on the right is $H^1(Y,\underline O_Y)$. Both its argument and subscript look like Y. The analogous degree-zero factor is indexed by B, making B a possible intended letter. Confidence in that emendation is low.

**289 [S2].** Below the cancelled bracket, « $u_0\in p\operatorname{Hom}(A_0,F_0)$ » has F in the target. The triangle on this leaf gives $u:A\to B$, so $B_0$ is the apparent intended target. The first written label reads « a) »; the later reference reads « (i)(ii)(iii) ». The overwritten subscript of $F$ in the third condition remains open. This limits any inference about the intended statement of that condition.

**289–290 [S3].** The first condition and the generic-fibre argument on 289 both give $F_1\not\subset G_1$. The opening of 290 gives $F_1\subset G_1$ with no diagonal through the inclusion. Restoring the negation would agree with both earlier occurrences and with the choice « distincts ». The transcription retains the discrepancy.

**290 [S4].** The second Néron–Severi factor has subscript $B/Y$, although the product immediately above uses $B'$. The form $B'/Y$ would track that definition; an implicit identification is also possible. Confidence in an emendation is low. The opening has « dé rang » with a visible acute where « de rang » would be ordinary French.

**291 [S5].** The phrase « espace vectoriel sur $p$ » precedes $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,M)$. The latter suggests $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ as the scalar field. The anti-equivalence paragraph begins with a square bracket, with no corresponding close visible at its end.

**292 [S6].** The gloss in (i) opens with a square bracket and closes after « facteur » with a round parenthesis. A closing square bracket would match the opening stroke.

**294 [S7].** The display writes $\rho c(x)$. The arrow marked $c$ starts at the target of $d$ in the diagram, so $\rho c(d(x))$ is a possible expanded expression. Confidence in calling this a slip is low. The author could be abbreviating a composite, and the horizontal arrow has a doubtful label.

**295 [S8].** « khânonique » where ordinary spelling is « canonique ». The typed h and circumflex are distinct. The word may be deliberate play on the earlier « canonique ». Confidence in calling it an error is low, and the letter overwritten at the beginning remains uncertain.

**296 [S9].** The final Ext vanishing has two equals signs across a line break. A handwritten equality at the right edge precedes a typed equality and zero at the left. One equality suffices to express the statement.

**299 [S10].** The degree equality has an equals sign at the end of one typescript line and another at the beginning of the next. A single sign would join the two degrees.

**301 [S11].** « les deux phrases. . » has two distinct dots in the typed line before « Dans la ». One full stop would close the sentence.

## Remarks of the model

**Model reading questions [R0], scan 287. Low confidence.** In the first triangle the final text gives a descending $p$ arrow from $X$ to $S$. Its end has a dark taper. An undirected stroke is also possible (287-p-lower.jpg). In « pour comparer », both occurrences of $\operatorname{rang}_k$ are live in the final text. The second has an independent horizontal trace through most letters, which might instead cancel « rang » (287-rank2.jpg). The first also has a long join near the $g$ (287-rank1.jpg).

**Model remark [R7], scans 289–290. Low confidence in an emendation.** The frozen third condition on 289 has $pF_{0\mid1}=G_1$, where the subscript on $F$ is obscured. The factorization just below uses the vanishing of $p\beta$ on $F$. Scan 290 writes $pF=0\subset G$. Containment $pF\subset G$ would account for that use, but the sign actually visible in the third condition is an equality.

**Model remark [R1], scan 292. High confidence in the zero-group test.** If « $H$ un $p$-groupe fini » includes a fixed $H=0$, (i bis) becomes the injection $0\to0$ for every map and cannot characterize (i). For example, the zero endomorphism of a single copy of $\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p$ fails (i). The sheet does not say whether (i bis) is to hold for every $H$ or for one nonzero $H$.

**Model reading questions [R8], scan 294. Low confidence.** The separate $V\otimes H^2$ at upper right in the lower diagram has an underlined $O$ in the frozen text. Its long lower loop could be the remnant of a previous fraktur $g$, as in the neighbouring Hom objects (294-above.jpg). Two isolated short dark uprights above the first Pic have no textual counterpart (294-top-ticks.jpg). They could be quotation-like marks or marks in the paper. They do not form an identifiable page number.

**Model remark [R2], scan 295. High confidence in the example.** The parenthesis counts two roots when $\delta$ is a square. In $k=\mathbb F_2$ with $\delta=1$, only $\tau=1$ satisfies $\tau^2=1$. The preceding paragraph explicitly qualifies its order-two assertion by « si $k$ n'est pas de caractéristique 2 ». The available page leaves open whether that restriction carries forward here.

**Model reading question [R3], scan 297, second line below the middle diagram. Low confidence.** The frozen reading has a doubtful minus immediately before $t_A$ in $t_{\widehat B}\otimes{-}t_A$. The short stroke is real. It could also be a stray lead-in to the handwritten $t$. Neither the adjacent tensor nor the circled tensor above determines its role.

**Model reading question [R9], scan 297, top diagram.** The frozen diagram puts the short downward $k$ arrow in the column containing $H^1(B,\underline O_B)$. In the facsimile its point is horizontally over the $\underline O_{A_1}$ of the preceding factor (297-midsup.jpg). Confidence is high about this visible displacement and low about assigning a mathematical target from the arrowhead alone. The oval around the separate tensor in the middle diagram also has a small gap at upper right (297-circle.jpg).

**Model remark [R4], scans 298 and 300. High confidence.** Inside $k[t,t^{-1}]$, the intersection $k[t]\cap k[t^{-1}]$ is $k$, since the only exponent that is both nonnegative and nonpositive is zero. The displayed matching condition therefore forces every global function on $X$ to restrict to a constant on the identified punctured line. This gives a precise meaning to « les pts de $Y$ ne sont pas séparés » in this passage.

**Model reading question [R10], scan 298, cancelled presentation of $Y$. Low confidence.** The frozen line has one underbrace with $Z$ beneath $\operatorname{Spec} k[t]$. A longer wavy stroke, interrupted around the $Z$, extends below the cancelled description of the punctured line. It may indicate a second, unlabelled brace or be part of the cancellation (298-stab.jpg).

**Model remark [R5], scan 299. High confidence in the qualification.** A reduced fraction fixes its coprime pair only up to common scalar. In $E=\mathbb Q(c)$ with $c$ transcendental, take $a=cX$, $b=c$. The automorphism $c\mapsto c+1$ fixes $a/b=X$ and changes both entries. A monic denominator would make the stabilizer's inference valid. This concerns the displayed step in the argument. [R5a]

**Model reading question [R6], scan 300, left margin. Low confidence.** The frozen second word after « à » reads « ré-pester | ré-percuter | rejeter ». The levelled crop also permits « ré-péter » if the small arches after the long descender belong to one vowel. A break after the initial syllable and the final tall crossed stroke are clearer than the middle. No conclusion about the mathematical status of the example depends on this verb.
