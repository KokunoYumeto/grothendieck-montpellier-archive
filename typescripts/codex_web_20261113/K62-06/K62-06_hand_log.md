# Hand log · Cote 62, scans 36–50

## Scan 36

The small knee and long baseline of s recur in the middle row and in (9). The doubled Z is particularly clear beside the two vertical arrows. A flat connecting line often swallows the middle of a French word, as in « donner » at the foot. Cancellation is by dense zigzag on short labels and by a single long stroke on the units sentence. The overprinted f-label is especially weak.

## Scan 37

The reused typescript is upside down relative to the archive foliation. The value-group symbol has the same upper serif and foot at every occurrence. The two underline strokes below Z are separate from its diagonal. Raised primes on group letters lie above the subscripts 1 and 2. The final value-group subscript in the first clause of the last proof is unprimed.

## Scan 38

In the diagram, the small crosses on u, v and h are pullback stars, while the one and two short strokes on x are primes. In the expansion, compact dark exponent marks cancel earlier powers. The prose often reduces a sequence of letters to a horizontal stroke. The straight double bar after « de » reads as the shorthand =.

## Scan 39

In (3) the typewritten i has a two-sided foot while j curves visibly left and descends. Both a-terms there carry just one Latin subscript. Bars in (5) are over the base letters. The mark after v in the last inequality of (4) has a break in its middle.

## Scan 40

A broad letter with an inner return followed by a small 3 is the group symbol. It resembles both a calligraphic S and a G. The loop of σ is low and narrow and carries 0, 1 or 2 raised ticks in the small vertical diagram. There are two separately written labels (11).

## Scan 41

The typed circumflex sits directly over a capital and leaves the subscript outside it. Both capitals on the right of the quotient arrow are hatted. In the lower paragraph A after « la topologie de » has no hat. Underlining repeatedly stops before a mathematical symbol and resumes in the prose.

## Scan 42

The inner turn of the structural-group symbol matches scan 40, including the low open 3. Above-line additions are attached by separate curved leaders (« fermé », « gal », « involutif »). The accented initial of Évidemt is distinct, though the following contracted letters merge. Single and double primes distinguish the two trisections.

## Scan 43

One long wedge over an entire quotient differs from two short wedges over its letters. The two sorts are superposed at the target of w and the source of the second map. The light pencil in the margin is in a different medium. Its final upstroke and the hook before « suite » nearly touch.

## Scan 44

The dotted invariant i has sometimes been written over a descending j. More than one letter form remains visible after « l'invariant ». Both consecutive inverse-image labels have two lobes, ∞. The separate section list also has a single closed 0. The lower dotted letter has no long descender in its final form.

## Scan 45

The hats over E and F in the quotient at the foot are individual strokes on the left of the arrow and a single spanning stroke on the right. This distinction recurs on scan 43. The centre-right separator in “identifierons/toujours” is a pen stroke through the type line; it divides the two words.

## Scan 46

The invariant has a short return to the right under its dot, especially in “quels i conviennent”. The crossbar shared by two tall downstrokes in “de tt” is the abbreviation. The elevated U belongs after “l'ouvert”. The tall loop after the abbreviation in the line above U is struck.

## Scan 47

A clockwise quarter turn makes the darker notes legible. They superimpose their own two triples and braces on the printed power-series ring, without cancelling its typed characters. Pencil loops run round the hypotheses of corollaries 2 and 3; a faint question mark sits beside corollary 2. The subscript of x in the proof of corollary 4 has a dot and a short foot, i.

## Scan 48

Look for the dot and the curved, heavy short stroke of the invariant i at the foot. It differs from the straight 1 at the head of P. A shallow rising line with an upturned end sends the bracket about a section back to the reduction to Z/2. The two words “trois sections” each have a separate low underline, contrasting with the thick line through the word to their left.

## Scan 49

Half-turn relative to the archive page. A narrow S-shaped pencil guide rises before G(A) to “un”. Lowercase i occurs both directly on a and x and nested on p under A. The congruence has three visible horizontal lines; the later large summation sign is absent from that earlier congruence.

## Scan 50

In the side notes the stem of f crosses the following letters; viewed along the writing direction both end in “-toriel”. The distinction between a single upper tick on S and the large numeral on P matters in the square. The correction on the right of sigma-dot-t leaves the horizontal bar clearly visible above sigma. Several early words on this sheet are hidden by wide, closely spaced zigzags.

## Comparisons across the leaves

The looped group sign with lowered 3 returns on 40, 42, 44, 48 and 50. The pen starts high and doubles back horizontally inside the curve. The symmetric-group reading is favoured by the same three marked sections throughout. Its outer curve alone also resembles G. The single and double primes on the two special points recur on 36, 38, 42, 44, 47 and 48. The sideways pen chart on 47 has the same pair of triples as the diagram on 36. The dark chart shares the forms of the working notes, whereas the light pencil ovals, brackets and marginal cues on the typescripts have no secure personal attribution.

## Readings settled by reinspection at the witness stage

At the five first marked group signs on 40, 42, 44, 48 and 50, the high return and inner S-shaped foot distinguish the writer’s \(\mathfrak S_3\) from his \(G_m\). The descending hooks under two dotted invariant letters on 44 are earlier j’s. The short word in the lower left margin on 50 is non, with a double underline and no i dot. The low pair on the turned chart on 47 reads x′, x″. These decisions refer to the indicated occurrences; older pen strokes at several other loci remain doubtful.
