# Mathematical notes on cote 62, scans 36–50

## What the pages do

**The map of degree two, scan 36 (C36).** The chart places \(t,s,r\) on three projective lines. On the complement of \(0\) and \(\infty\), the writer gives \(g\) the form \(\lambda t^n\). He uses the degree and the value at \(1\) to obtain \(r=g(s)=s^2\), formula (9).

**The map of degree three, scan 38 (C38).** The writer transports the two ramification points \(x',x''\) to \(0,\infty\), draws the resulting square and computes the pullbacks. He uses \(x'+x''=x'x''=1\) and \((x'-x'')^2=-3\). The resulting numerator \(2t^3-3t^2-3t+2\) is also written as \((t+1)(2t-1)(t-2)\).

**The composite and its involution, scan 40 (C40).** Squaring the expression for \(f(t)\) gives the displayed invariant \(j\) with coefficient \(-1/27\). The writer describes where the cover is unramified. In a separate diagram he follows \(\sigma\) through both quotients and gives \(\sigma'(t)=\sigma''(t)=1/t\).

**The trisection, scan 42.** A Brauer–Severi scheme \(X/S\) is supplied with an unramified trisection \(X'\). The writer describes its classification by \(H^1(S,\mathfrak S_3)\), introduces an involution and the associated \(X''\), and obtains a bisection by descent, with a qualification on the characteristic.

**The invariant of a section, scans 42, 44 and 46.** The quotient construction gives a map \(X\to P^1_S\). A section \(s\) disjoint from \(X'\) has an invariant \(i\). When \(i\) and \(i-1\) are invertible, the inverse-image construction gives the unramified Galois cover used to recover the datum. The writer then considers a normal integral \(X\) and an \(i\) not identically zero or one.

**The exceptional values, scan 48.** At \(i=0\) the structure group reduces to \(\mathbb Z/2\), and the datum is expressed by a class in \(H^1(S,\mathbb Z/2)\). At \(i=1\) the subgroup is \(\mathbb Z/3\) and the group of classes is \(H^1(S,\mathbb Z/3)\).

**The intermediate projective line, scan 48.** When \(i\) is invertible, the quotient supplies three sections used as coordinates \(\infty,0,1\). The next row sends these positions to \(\infty,0,i\). The formula below has expression \(it^2\) and the last line identifies the bisection with \(\sqrt i\).

**Equivariant data, scan 50.** The writer returns to a cover \(S'\) with group \(\mathfrak S_3\) and a section \(t\) whose values avoid \(0,1,\infty\). His two displayed conditions read \(\lambda t=1/t\) and \(\mu t=1-t\).

**The local example, scan 50.** For \(S=\operatorname{Spec}(O)\), where \(O\) is complete local with algebraically closed residue field and a stated restriction on characteristic, the writer invokes \(\pi_1(S)=0\). He describes when \(t,t'\) give isomorphic systems by the action on \(t\). The qualification above « à la donnée » is difficult to read.

**The valuation inequality, scan 39.** In document n° 272 the writer considers representatives \(x_i\) of values and \(y_\alpha\) with independent residue classes. In a putative relation he separates terms of minimum valuation and passes to the residue field to obtain \(rs\leq n\).

**Algebraic extensions, scans 39 and 37.** Proposition 2 concludes that the quotient of value groups is torsion and that the residue-field extension is algebraic. The argument passes through the filtered family of finite subextensions.

**Rank and discreteness, scan 37.** Corollaire 1 compares isolated subgroups by \(H'\mapsto H'\cap G\). Corollaire 2 extracts the conclusions about trivial valuations and rank one. Corollaire 3 treats discreteness for a finite extension. The last line breaks off after « donc » and the value-group symbol.

**Lifting generators, scan 49.** In document n° 276 the end of the preceding argument relates surjectivity of \(G(u)\) to generation by initial forms. Corollaire 1 transfers finite generation and the noetherian property from \(G(E)\) to \(E\) under the stated completeness and separation hypotheses. Its proof uses the induced filtration on a submodule \(F\).

**Two applications, scan 49.** Corollaire 2 takes \(E=A\) to get a left-noetherian criterion. Corollaire 3 asserts that finitely many elements generating \(E/mE\) generate \(E\) when \(A\) is complete and \(E\) separated for the \(m\)-adic topology. The available recto ends during its proof.

**Polynomial and power-series rings, scan 47.** After a calculation with \(\alpha_{rs}\) the first three corollaries give noetherianity of polynomial rings over a field and of the indicated formal power-series rings. For \(A[[X]]\) the writer filters by powers of \((X)\) and identifies the associated graded ring with \(A[X]\).

**The adic criterion, scan 47.** Corollaire 4 assumes \(A/m\) noetherian, \(m\) finitely generated and \(A\) separated and complete for the \(m\)-adic topology. Its argument uses the initial forms of finite generators of \(m\).

**The enveloping algebra, scans 47 and 45 (C47-LIE).** Corollaire 5 asserts that the enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra over a commutative noetherian ring is left and right noetherian. The proof on scan 45 uses a filtration and presents the associated graded ring as a quotient of a polynomial ring.

**Completion of groups and quotients, scans 45 and 43.** Section 7 recalls the canonical map into the completion and the extension of a continuous representation. For a subgroup \(F\subset E\) the writer describes \(\hat F\) inside \(\hat E\). He compares completion of \(E/F\) with \(\hat E/\hat F\) by the maps \(v,w\), and states when they are inverse.

**Exactness, scan 43.** Lemme 1 treats an exact sequence of metrizable abelian topological groups and topological homomorphisms. Its proof breaks the sequence into three exact pieces and applies the preceding special case.

**Filtered completions, scan 41.** The writer identifies \(E/E_n\) with \(\hat E/\hat E_n\). For separated \(E\) he also writes \(E_n=E\cap\hat E_n\). He next describes the filtered completion of \(A\), the isomorphism of associated graded rings and the extension of the module action to the completions.

## Remarks of the model

- **C38 and C40, high confidence.** Exact polynomial multiplication gives the cubic printed on 38. Squaring \(1/(3\sqrt{-3})\) gives \(-1/27\), the coefficient printed on 40.
- **R40, high confidence.** As a rational function over \(\mathbb Q\), the \(j\) printed on 40 is fixed by each of the substitutions \(t\mapsto1/t\) and \(t\mapsto1-t\) written on 50. Substituting into the displayed fraction and cancelling gives zero difference in each case.
- **R44, high confidence for the rational function.** Write \(P=(t+1)(2t-1)(t-2)\), \(D=t(t-1)\). The equality \(P^2+27D^2=4(t^2-t+1)^3\) relates the two marks \(x',x''\) on 36 to the fibre of \(j=1\). The displayed factors also give double zeros at \(-1,\tfrac12,2\) and double poles at \(0,1,\infty\). This calculation does not decide which symbol the author intended to replace on 44.
- **C47-LIE and R47-FIN, high confidence.** As stated on 47, Corollaire 5 lacks a finiteness hypothesis. Over a field, an abelian Lie algebra on infinitely many basis vectors has a polynomial enveloping algebra on those vectors. The ideals generated by successively larger finite initial sets of variables strictly increase. Finite generation of the Lie algebra as a module over the base is a sufficient additional hypothesis for the finite-variable polynomial-ring argument on 45 (R47-FIN).
- **Reading question, scan 38, low confidence.** In the crossed identity for the difference of cubes, the frozen last summand has \(x''\) with a doubtful squared exponent. The other possible base is \(x'\), which gives the usual factorization. The cancellation runs through both ticks. Mathematical expectation cannot establish which base was written.
- **Reading question, scan 50, low confidence.** The first condition reads \(t\in O^*\). The superscript in the second condition, on \(t(t-1)\), is transcribed as a plus with an asterisk as an alternative. The first sign is blotted and the second looks cross-shaped. The unit conditions expressed in words higher up explain why these signs deserve separate attention.
- **Reading question, scan 46, low confidence.** The noun before the tuple \((X,X^{\prime},s)\) is read as *structure*, with *situation* retained as a possible reading. Both refer back to the data introduced on scan 44.
