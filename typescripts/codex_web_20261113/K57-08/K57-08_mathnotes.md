# Mathematical notes on cote 57, scans 133–149

## What the pages do

**133 and 135 · Pullback on Picard groups.** For a surjective morphism of proper preschemes over a field, 133 studies the kernel of pullback. Two corollaries concern the Picard schemes when they exist, the injectivity of the displayed Nér Sév map, and the finite kernel between the “parties abéliennes”. The proof starts with representability and a square involving the reduced schemes. Scan 135 uses Stein factorisation to separate a finite surjection from the case \(f_*\underline O_X\simeq\underline O_Y\). The left margin qualifies a step involving schemes of finite type.

**134 · Koszul complexes and the beginning of a Čech construction.** The typed sheet passes to direct limits over powers of a sequence \(f\). The displayed homotopy gives the unit-ideal proposition and a vanishing corollary. The pen note beside it asks about the action of the individual \(f_i\) on homology and cohomology. Section 2 starts an open cover by the \(U_i\) and introduces a module of sections. Several slots for maps and inequalities in the typescript are empty.

**136 · Completion and flatness.** The opening compares cohomological codimension before and after completion and discusses how to formulate a Cohen–Macaulay lemma. A proposition tests flatness of a finite \(B\)-module over \(A\) by completing. The argument cites a criterion involving \(\operatorname{Tor}_1\). The two corollaries concern freeness after completion and a pair of dimension and cocodimension formulas. The last sentence is physically incomplete.

**138–140 · The relative Picard group and descent.** In a factorisation \(X\to\Sigma\to S\), scan 138 writes a sequence involving \(\mathrm{Pic}(\Sigma)\), \(\mathrm{Pic}(X)\) and \(\mathrm{Pic}'(X/S)\). A section over \(\Sigma\) is used to rigidify invertible modules. Scan 139 has an upright triangle and, turned the other way, a cancelled construction involving a closed \(Z\). Scan 140 treats faithfully flat base change and writes an equaliser under a local-section hypothesis. Its corollary compares two Picard functors and ends with a quotient formula.

**141–142 · A class for a curve with points removed.** A cover headed “Picard des schémas en courbes”, with the short word before “courbes” uncertain, precedes a smooth projective family of curves \(X/S\). Scan 142 removes a closed subscheme \(Y\), with disjoint sections as the example. The proper-support spectral sequence singles out a map from the row in degree 2 to the row in degree 1. The page records \(\xi_n\in H^2(S,{}_nE)\), its \(l\)-adic version and the extension of the Jacobian \(J\) by \(T\) defining \(E\).

**143 · A publisher’s letter.** The writer returns the front matter for “Huit exposés ...” with two corrections. The question for Mr Benjamin is whose name should appear in the copyright credit. The writer says that a change from the arrangement in the July letter would require consulting Bourbaki, and asks for a reply about the additional paper proposed on November 10.

**144–145 · A geometric construction of the torsor.** Scan 144 forms \(Z\) by identifying the two maps from the displayed coproduct to \(X\), sets \(E=\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0_{Z/S}\), and writes \(P=\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^1_{Z/S}\) for a torsor under \(E\). It proposes that the coboundary of the class of \(P\) gives \(\xi_n\). Scan 145 sends the class to \(H^1(S,J)\). When this image vanishes, it comes from \(H^1(S,T)\). A second exact sequence leads to a quotient of Picard groups in the split case and to a diagonal quotient when \(Y\) is a union of sections.

**147 and 149 · The linear group for a singular curve.** For a complete, reduced, connected curve \(C\), scan 147 places the local terms at the double points between the units on the normalisation and \(\mathrm{Pic}_{C/k}\). A product quotient gives a dimension formula. Several further versions are cancelled. Scan 149 instead writes \(\mu_\alpha=\mu'_\alpha+\mu''_\alpha\), relates the first part to the number of formal branches minus one, and displays separate multiplicative and additive dimensions. A marginal invariant is related in the text to the normalisation map, Weil cohomology and fundamental groups. The final sentence about an abelian variety stops at “de plus”.

**148 · An intersection estimate on an English typescript.** Page 42 compares the intersection of a divisor with a second divisor upstairs and downstairs. Two dimension formulas for the linear systems give a quadratic inequality in \(h\). The sheet concludes \(m=1\), discusses the inverse image of the general point, and begins to eliminate the remaining fundamental curves by quadratic transformations.

## Remarks of the model

**M138.1 · Model remark, low confidence as an editorial correction.** The interlinear bracket on 138 reads \(\underline L\otimes\varphi^*(g^*\underline L)\). Pulling this expression back by the section \(g\) leaves two copies of \(g^*\underline L\). If the bracket is intended to produce the indicated rigidification by cancellation, a dual on one copy would give that cancellation. The bracket is an insertion among revisions, so the precise intended formula remains a question.

**M148.1 · Model remark, high confidence for the upper bound.** In the displayed inequality valid “for all \(h\geq1\)”, comparison of the quadratic terms gives \(ma\leq a\). The sheet expressly calls \(a\) positive. Thus this comparison supplies \(m\leq1\); the stated equality uses the interpretation of \(m\) as a positive number of points in the preceding paragraph.

**Model remark, low confidence about the discarded definitions on 147.** In the cancelled paragraph, \(\mu'_\alpha\) is attached to a phrase about branches. The live definition on 149 expressly adds “diminué de 1”. Several words in the earlier passage are overwritten. The change in wording can be located more securely than the precise definition the author meant to discard.
