## Legibility

The English letter and most of the two French typewritten sheets can be read continuously. Some French typescript lines end outside the typing area reproduced, and x-type leaves only parts of the earlier letters. On scan 148 the relative heights of the stars, primes and tildes are easier to establish than whether the darker special characters were applied with a pen or special type.

The most fragile passages are the aside and final bracket on 133, the left margin of 135, the rewritten enumeration and last line of 138, and the slanted note on 140. The drawings on 139 are distinct once the lower text is turned. On 147 and 149 it is often possible to see exactly where a cancellation or addition lies while remaining unsure of the letters it covers. The low-confidence rows give partial readings of this ink. The final words “de plus” on 149 and “of” on 148 stand at the physical end of the supplied text.
