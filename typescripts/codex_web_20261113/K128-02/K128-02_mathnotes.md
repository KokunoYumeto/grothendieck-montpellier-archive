# What the pages do

**The radicial case, scans 17–19.** The notes put Ω = Δ/Δ² and separate Ω = 0 from Ω ≠ 0. In the second case the map obtained from δ modulo Δ² is an A-derivation. The author uses its action on products and invokes Nakayama to obtain the containments labelled (*). He reduces to the case of a field k and a local algebra of the form k + V with square-zero maximal ideal. Scan 19 opens the question of the dimension of V, writes out the tensor square and lists additive generators involving δ and mixed tensors. The page stops there.

**A square-zero reduction, scans 20–21.** Lemme 1 defines B and C₀ by equalizers before and after reduction modulo a square-zero ideal. Under the displayed condition on C₀, it identifies the kernel of reduction in H¹ with multiplicative coefficients with its counterpart for additive coefficients. Two short exact sequences of complexes and a diagram with vertical maps i, j and id give the comparison. Corollaire 1 translates vanishing of both reduced groups into an isomorphism and an equivalence of vanishing assertions upstairs.

**Matrices, scan 22.** Corollaire 2 passes to the matrix functor and its units. The diagonal mark at the top introduces an extra decomposition hypothesis written at the foot. The comparison of kernels preserves the distinguished element. The proof describes the actions of the two groups of coefficients on the cohomology term and says which elements act trivially.

**Free modules indexed by a set, scan 23.** Corollaire 3 puts E = A⁽ᴺ⁾. A decomposition of endomorphisms into an automorphism and a term coming from the other ring gives a comparison involving the automorphism and endomorphism functors. The bracketed paragraph interprets the first kernel using effective descent and freeness. The two-row diagram compares complexes, with Hom(E, IE) at the left of each row.

**A semilocal lifting argument, scans 24–25.** Lemme 2 gives two alternative hypotheses on residue fields and maximal ideals. For each matrix u over C it seeks a matrix v over B making u + φ(v) invertible. The proof lifts a family at the finitely many maximal ideals and tests invertibility after reduction. Scan 25 writes the analogous problem for an endomorphism of a free module, allows a set of generators, and includes a separate N.B. about k(t).

**The theorem, scan 26.** Starting from a semilocal Noetherian ring A and a finite A-algebra A′, the author forms the quotients Aₙ and A′ₙ. Part (a) compares finite-level H¹ conditions with effective descent for flat modules over B⁽ⁿ⁾ = H⁰(A′ₙ/Aₙ). Part (b) gives consequences for A′/A and makes the finite-type qualification in its descent assertion. Part (c) formulates a further descent assertion after a flat B-prescheme is introduced, with an Artinian hypothesis on A. Pencil circles and marginal comments qualify the formulation.

**Proof of (a), scans 27–28.** The induction refers back to the lemmas and to faithfully flat descent at n = 0. The text identifies the fixed-rank condition with a descent question for a free module. It reduces freeness of a descended module to the residue rings. For the reverse implication it uses base change, reduces to trivial residue extensions and a local base, and invokes the preceding corollaries. The last lemma reference is marked doubtful.

**Proof of (b) and the opening of (c), scans 28–30.** After passage to a complete base, the author writes the complex as an inverse limit and invokes M.L. to deduce H¹ = 0. For module descent he forms E from descent invariants, identifies its finite-level pieces and sets out two goals, flatness and the isomorphism after tensoring. The limit lemma is invoked for flatness. The isomorphism is obtained as a limit of the finite-level comparisons. Part (c) then reduces to X affine and A = B. The surviving argument ends with a bracketed note and a three-object sketch.

**An equivalence ideal, scan 32, other hand.** The English sheet starts with A′ = k + V and an ideal I in its tensor square. A generator F is written as two linear pieces and a bilinear piece. The inclusion for p₁₃(I) gives a relation in three families of variables. Taking its bilinear terms supplies the corrections defining F′; an inverse expression is also written. The last step uses I ⊂ Δ and ends with a difference of the same linear form in X and in Y.

# Remarks of the model

**R32.1 · Model remark, high confidence under the stated square-zero hypotheses.** The short inverse formula after “This is still set of generators” has a degree explanation. With both variable ideals square-zero, applying two corrections with linear coefficients to a generator of positive degree gives zero. Every possible bidegree of total degree three has degree at least two in one of the families. This checks the disappearance of the iterated correction in the two-variable formula. The exponent at the clipped right edge of the earlier three-variable display remains a reading question.

**Reading question, scan 23, page-file line 29.** The frozen right member is `H^1(A'_0/A_0,\operatorname{End}(E))`. Both low marks are unusually dense and joined to the feet of the A’s. They could be zeros, as transcribed, or cancellations leaving A′/A. Confidence in the cancellation interpretation is low.

**Reading question, scan 30, page-file line 8.** The frozen stroke between A′ₙ and E′ₙ is a dash. Its slight downward flick at the right could be a shallow arrowhead. The word « isomorphisme » favours an arrow as the intended map, but the stroke itself gives low confidence in that alternative.
