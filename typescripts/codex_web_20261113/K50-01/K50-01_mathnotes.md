# Cote 50 · Mathematical notes

The scan numbers follow the archive order. Statement IDs identify the claims discussed here and the corresponding entries, where applicable, in `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv`.

## What the pages do

**003 · A Picard-functor criterion (P1).** A map into `Hom(A,A*)` is followed by the effect of translation on a class ξ and a boxed expression on the product. Four conditions involve a kernel, the vanishing of that expression, membership in `Pic^τ`, and the image of an extension group. The last four lines indicate how to pass between the conditions. In the box both projections are written without stars.

**005 · Equivalence of divisors (P2).** The upper sketch puts torsion and numerical equivalence beside implication arrows. The rectangular sketch adds “algébrique”. A separate chain compares the dimensions of `A`, `Pic A` and `H¹(A,O_A)`, returning to `dim A`. The references beside individual inequalities are part of the argument. Several of the narrow arrow labels are difficult to read.

**006 · Valuation ring and value group (V1).** The surviving typescript constructs a valuation ring from v. The units have value zero, and v factors through the valuation of that ring by an increasing injective homomorphism. The next paragraph names the ring, ideal, residue field, and group of orders. It characterises equivalent valuations by an isomorphism of their ordered value groups.

**006 and 004 · The minimum in a sum (V2).** Proposition 4 gives a lower bound for the valuation of a finite sum. Scan 006 ends “si v(x) ≠ v(y), on a”; scan 004 opens with the completed equality. The proof for a unique minimum factors out the term of least value. The corollary says that a zero sum with at least two summands attains its least valuation at two distinct indices.

**004 · Translating between three descriptions (V3).** “Traductions” matches a valuation ring, a place and a valuation. Three rows give the conditions for membership in the ring, in its ideal, and in the complement of that ideal inside the ring. Proposition 5 then asserts extension to K of a valuation on a subfield F, up to equivalence on F.

**011 · Duality and the product (A1).** Items a) and b) give an additive category and an autoduality with a compatibility isomorphism. Item c) introduces a contravariantly varying subgroup of the antisymmetric forms. For a class on a product, a two-by-two matrix separates its two diagonal entries from the terms involving λ and its transpose. The bottom box takes ξ to that off-diagonal λ.

**010 · The quotient and involution (A2).** The opening relative clause says what the preceding map annihilates. “Propriétés fondamentales” identifies a quotient for a product with a Hom group. A cancelled existence assertion is followed by a conditional construction: given a rational isomorphism φξ, conjugating the transpose by φξ defines an involution on the rational endomorphisms.

**010 · Polarisation in two endomorphisms (A3).** The formula numbered (1) subtracts the two separate pullbacks from the pullback under their sum. Under φ it becomes the sum of the two mixed terms in (1′). Formula (2) inserts the identity as second argument; formula (3) rewrites the expression with primed endomorphisms.

**009 · Positivity assumptions (A4).** Item d) specifies parts of N(A), discusses their behaviour for isogenies, and lists closure under addition and positive multiples, generation, and a rational-isomorphism condition. A proposed positivity statement is crossed through. The long cancelled marginal passage relates a positive divisor to an inverse image. Several exponents were overwritten, so the precise part designated at every occurrence is uncertain.

**008 · Multilinear form and degree (A5).** Item e) assumes an integer r, a form I with values in the integers, and positivity. An equal-dimension morphism is assigned an integer governing the effect of pulling back all r arguments. The notes then express that integer for α through a chosen class ξ, call it a form of degree 2r, and expand its value at α+n1_A. The first and last coefficients are named trace and determinant.

**009 and 007 · Trace formulas (T1).** The opening of 009 continues the coefficient discussion with a formula for the trace involving I and D(α). The theorem on 007 uses the involution α↦α′ and gives an expression for `tr(αβ′)`. Its next line specialises to `tr αα′` and has `2α*(ξ)` as the last argument of I. The heading “Corollaire” stands alone.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Product and quotient, 011 to 010. High confidence.** The bottom map on 011 and the first quotient display on 010 have the same target `Hom(A,D(B))`. The opening clause on 010 supplies the vanishing on the two factor images. This explains the continuation in archive order without establishing the asserted isomorphism.
- **R2 · Coefficient degree, 008–009. High confidence under the stated homogeneity interpretation.** In total degree 2r, the term with a coefficient of degree i and a factor `n^(2r−i)` has total degree 2r. This gives the candidate correction in S7. It does not identify the source of the extra 2.
- **R3 · Mixed terms, 010, (1′). High confidence for the algebraic expansion.** Expanding the preceding product of sums, in its printed order, and removing the two diagonal terms gives precisely `{}^tβ φξ α + {}^tα φξ β`. This computation uses formal distributivity; it does not settle the individual damaged glyphs in (1).
- **R4 · Reading question, 010, (1)–(2). Low confidence as an error claim.** In the frozen reading of (1), the argument of α* is η with ξ as alternative. The long, straight descender favours η visually; the other two terms favour ξ. In (2) the left reads `Dξ(α)` and the right `φξ(α′+α)`. The preceding identities could suggest `φ_{Dξ(α)}` on the left or an implicit identification via φ. There is no basis here for choosing between those explanations.
- **R5 · How the typescript joins, 006 to 004. High confidence.** The last words of 006 give a hypothesis on v(x) and v(y), and the first line of 004 gives the equality under that hypothesis. The unqualified display at the top of 004 is consequently a continuation of the interrupted sentence.
