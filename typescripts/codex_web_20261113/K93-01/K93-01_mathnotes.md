# Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 135–136.** An extension is identified with a first-principal-parts extension on $\omega$. The note locates the indeterminacy of the operator in a tensor square. For a smooth complex curve it interprets a transcendental construction as a section of the torsor of projective connections. It asks what regularity at infinity means for that section on the compactification $\widehat X$.

**U2 · Scans 136–138.** For the reduced divisor $D$ at infinity, the note twists a short exact sequence by $\underline\lambda$ and compares the sections on $\widehat X$ to the contribution on $D$. The case $g\geq2$ includes a diagram relating regular quadratic differentials to cotangent spaces on moduli. For $g=1$ and $g=0$ it examines the cokernel of a map on vector fields. The genus-zero table leads to uniqueness for at most three holes and to dimension $\nu-3$ for $\nu\geq4$. The final construction uses $\mathbb P_X=\widehat X\times X$, its diagonal section and the trivial connection.

**U3 · Scans 139–140.** With 2 invertible on the base, a smooth group of relative dimension one acts on a torsor $X$. A filtration and a splitting express invariant connections as sections of a sub-torsor. The distinguished section has components $0+0+1$. The ensuing characterization uses reduction of the structural group, a second section disjoint from $\sigma$, and an invariance question in characteristic zero. The last NB considers a proper group with connected fibres.

**U4 · Scans 142–143.** The first-principal-parts sequence for $E$ has an additive splitting. Writing a section as $d_E^{(1)}x+\omega$ gives (4), and comparing an $\underline O_S$-linear splitting with the additive one gives (6) and the product law (7). The degree-one operator on $E$-valued forms satisfies (9). Its square is tested on $E$ in (10′), the composite called the curvature tensor.

**U5 · Scans 143–145.** For an integrable connection the resulting complex defines de Rham cohomology by hypercohomology. The spectral sequence (12) is followed by the map from cohomology of global sections (13) and the map to $H^i(S,E)$ (14). A commutative square records the inverse-image setting. In degree zero (16) identifies the horizontal sections and the canonical inclusion.

**U6 · Scans 145–147.** For an abelian-sheaf complex $K^\cdot$, a $K^0$-torsor and a section of its extension to $K^1$ describe degree-one hypercohomology. The image in the extension to $K^2$ must be the canonical section. The map to $H^1(S,K^0)$ forgets the chosen section. Applied to the de Rham complex this yields the map $d_P$ of (17), its translation law and the closedness condition in (18). The following NB treats compatible connections more generally. Scan 147 leaves the separate sentence “munis d’un” unfinished.

**U7 · Scans 148–149.** The first relation of (18) describes compatible connections. The second restricts to integrable ones. Compatible automorphisms are identified with horizontal sections. The next section introduces stratified cohomology, the infinite-principal-parts map $\delta_E^\infty$, its augmentation condition, the associated ordinary connection and the comparison (24). The lower half of 149 is a cancelled cosimplicial construction, with an abandoned first pair of arrows and a diagram extending to $C^2(E)$.

**U8 · Scan 150.** A torsor on the stratifying site is evaluated on $(U,U')$ and related to an ordinary torsor with compatible stratification. Sequence (25) embeds $E$ in its infinite jets and takes the quotient. The note considers trivializing an associated stratified torsor and identifies the corresponding jets of an ordinary torsor. The last sentence ends with “revient à la”.
