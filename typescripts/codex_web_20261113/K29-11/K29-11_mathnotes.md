# Mathematical notes · Cote 29, scans 173–187

## What the pages do

**174 · Plan for “Groupe fondamental”.** Two headings announce base-change theorems for $\pi_1$. The seven numbered items move from local and global connectedness of morphisms to statements, corollaries and a proof, then to resolution of singularities and an application to the dimension of certain fields. The adjective qualifying “dimension” is uncertain.

**176 · Coverings and cohomology.** A tower over $X$ precedes successive comparisons with cohomology and direct limits. Two spectral-sequence formulas, a quadrant of marked points and an extended row lead to formulas for the kernel and cokernel of the map from $\pi^2(X,G)$ to $\pi^2(X^h,G)$. The first exponent earlier on the sheet is shaped like a Roman I.

**177 · Descent diagrams.** The $X$ and $Y$ ladders precede the sequence from $\pi_1(\overline F)$ through $\pi_1(X)$ to $\pi_1(Y)$ and $e$. The remaining rows relate homomorphisms to $G$, first cohomology and an expression for $Y'/Y$ that ends in zero. The coefficient resembling a lower-case $z$ has an unresolved alphabet.

**179–180 · The functor of pointed descent data.** For a connected prescheme $S$ and a submersive $S'\to S$, the proposition gives conditions for strict prorepresentability in the discrete group $G$. It relates finite generation and representability to the components of the fibre products. The next leaf singles out effective data and asks when this gives the full functor.

**181 · Description by cocycles.** The ladders in degrees 0, 1 and 2 lead to a continuous map $f$ with values in $G$. The sheet writes a cocycle condition, an equivalence involving $h$, and the additional restrictions for pointed data. Several former upper indices can still be seen beside their replacements.

**182 · Contraction and pointing.** The images of $i_0(k_0)$, $i_1(k_1)$ and $i_2(k_2)$ are contracted to points. The resulting barred objects carry normalized cochains. The sheet invokes the displayed conditions on $k_*$ and $K_*$ when discussing the existence of a pointing and automorphisms of pointed descent data.

**183 · Representing a functor on sets.** The question under $F(I)\simeq C(K,I)$ introduces conditions on $F$ involving finite inverse limits, filtered families of subsets and surjectivity from an inverse limit of sets. The author proposes $K$ as an inverse limit with its limit topology and ends by comparing $\pi_0(L)$ to $K$.

**184 · Cancelled working sheet.** A branching tree, oriented paths, loops and a hatched triangle share the page with a three-factor formula involving $f$ and $g$. Among the labels are $k[[s,t]]$, the faces $d_0,d_1,d_2$, and the successive arrows $u_n,u_{n-1},u_1$. There is no continuous argument specifying how all these sketches fit together.

**185 · Connectedness and the kernel.** The opening continues the topology discussion of 183. A functor is called connected when it is isomorphic to the identity on sets. Two arrows $F^0\rightrightarrows F^1$ define the kernel denoted $\pi^0(F^*)$. The text then discusses representation by spaces and associates functors with set and group coefficients to a half-cosimplicial object.

**185–186 · Relative construction.** The map from $F^*$ to the identity supplies a pointing. A second diagram gives the more general map $\xi$ from the $F$ row to the $f$ row. In the representable case the proposed description uses the $K_i$ and then their pointed, barred counterparts. The final paragraph proposes a generalization in terms of Galois categories.

**187 · A finite-type criterion.** The revised hypotheses concern the base $Y$, the finite number of irreducible components of $X$, and a locally finite-type, quasi-finite, separated morphism $f$. The conclusion is that $f$ is of finite type. The proof chooses an affine open of an integral $X$, invokes the “main theorem”, and uses $X'$, $X''$ and the normalization of $X'$ in the final reduction.

## Remarks of the model

**R185 · Model remark, high confidence for the finite example.** The opposite arrow directions on 185 can be seen by taking $K_0=\{a,b\}$ and $K_1=\{x\}$ with the discrete topology. Let the two maps send $x$ to $a$ and $b$, respectively. Precomposition sends a function on $K_0$ to two functions on $K_1$. Of the four functions $K_0\to\{0,1\}$, exactly the two constant ones give equal composites. This finite computation illustrates the “foncteur noyau” on the sheet.
