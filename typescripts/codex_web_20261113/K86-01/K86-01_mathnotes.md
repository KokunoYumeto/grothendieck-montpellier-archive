# Mathematical notes · cote 86, scans 2–17

## What the pages do

**Scans 2–3. Incidence data.** After the title sheet, Grothendieck takes finite sets \(S,A,F\) of vertices, edges and faces with relations \(R,R'\) on consecutive pairs of ranks. He forms the ordered set of facets and discusses the induced vertex–face relation \(R''\). Conditions 1 and 2 are local counts. Conditions 3 and 3′ say that the strict interval below a face and the strict interval above a vertex are connected and nonempty. He describes each as a combinatorial polygon.

**Scan 3. The added polyhedron condition.** Condition 4 asks for suprema of pairs with an upper bound and gives a dual formulation for infima. The numbered NB examines two vertices below an edge, two vertices below a face, a vertex and a nonincident edge, and two distinct edges below a face. Uniqueness of the common upper facet distinguishes the cases. At the foot he reformulates the requirements using the common facets of two faces or of two vertices. The slanted marginal version interchanges vertices and faces.

**Scans 3–5. Describing facets by their vertices.** The map sends a facet to the set of vertices below it. The corollaries assert injectivity, recovery of the order by inclusion, and closure under intersection for pairs with a lower bound. The argument uses each facet as the supremum of its vertices. A two-sided face would have two different edges with the same endpoints. Scan 4 also expresses a face as the supremum of its edges and gives the dual assertion for a vertex. Scan 5 starts the description by sets of containing faces and stops mid-sentence.

**Scans 4–5. Recovering an icosahedral face.** On scan 4 a triple of vertices gives a face when all its pairs are edges. This identifies the automorphisms with permutations preserving the edge set, equivalently the combinatorial distance. The slanted margin names the « icosaèdre gauche » as a case where the same reconstruction fails. The different number in the NB on scan 5 is recorded below.

**Scan 6. The icosahedron as an example.** The outline gives 12 vertices, 30 edges, 20 faces, five edges at each vertex and three edges per face. It proposes studying distances, antipodes and projections. The transitivity assertion passes from vertices to incident vertex–edge pairs and then to complete flags. The page ends by contrasting the number of combinatorial regular polyhedra with the number admitting a convex realization in Euclidean dimension three.

**Scan 7. Opposite vertices.** The drawings A₁ and A₂ use two poles and two pentagons. One list assigns ten edges to the poles, ten to the pentagons themselves and ten to the connections between them. The symmetry group beside the figures has order 20. Below the horizontal line two drawings introduce a triangular view.

**Scans 8–9. Opposite triangular faces.** B₁, the construction below it and B₃ place corresponding primed and unprimed triples in different projections. A correspondence diagram on scan 9 relates the four named triangles. The edge list gives multiplicities 6, 12, 6 and 6. The symmetry group on scan 8 has order 12.

**Scan 9. Opposite edges.** Three C drawings use \(s,t,u,v\), their primed versions and doubly indexed vertices. A formula describes an identification of pairs, followed by a different formula labelled « antipodisme ». A revised edge list ends in a boxed pair and count. The group written beside this construction has order 8.

**Scans 10–11. Flags.** The second cover introduces incidence geometries and sets with operators. A nonempty totally ordered set of \(l\) facets is a flag of length \(l-1\). The three tables list individual facets, flags of two distinct ranks, and « repères », or maximal flags \(s<a<f\). The next construction uses maps that forget entries of a flag.

**Scan 12. Three involutions.** The frames are written as the fibre product \(E=R\times_A R'\). Forgetting one entry gives a two-element fibre and an involution \(\sigma_i\) exchanging the two frames. The displayed calculation exchanges the vertex and the face while keeping the edge. It gives the commutation of \(\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_2\). The presentation at the foot imposes this relation and the three square relations. The capital for this group is graphically ambiguous. Here \(G_2\) follows the first reading in the transcription.

**Scans 13–14. Reconstructing incidence from frames.** The functor takes incident triples to a \(G_2\)-set. Quotienting by the pairs of generators that leave one rank unchanged recovers respectively \(S,A,F\). The middle quotient uses the free four-element subgroup. For the outer quotients the text invokes 3 and 3′. The images of \(E\) in \(S\times A\) and \(A\times F\) recover the two incidence relations. These are the reasons given for full faithfulness and for the identification of the automorphisms.

**Scans 14–15. Connectedness and regularity.** Chains through edges, indecomposability under disjoint union, and transitivity on frames give the connectedness criteria. A frame-fixing automorphism of a connected geometry is the identity. Regularity asks for transitivity of the automorphisms themselves. The sheet compares that condition with normality of a frame stabilizer in \(G_2\), a finite cardinality equality, and lifting the effect of each generator to an automorphism at a chosen frame.

**Scans 15–16. Fixing a frame.** Evaluation at \(r_0\) identifies the isomorphisms from a fixed regular polyhedron to an isomorphic one with the frames of the target. After a cancelled diagram the notes assert an epimorphism \(g\mapsto\bar g\). The words « les \(\bar\sigma_i\) engendrent \(G\) » on scan 16 complete the assertion about generators.

**Scan 16. Cancelled orientation proposal.** The bracketed passage divides \(E\) into \(R^+\) and its complement, interchanged by the three involutions. It also compares \(R^+\) with a product indexed by \(j\ne i\). « Repères directs » and « repères inverses » belong to this cancelled passage. A second cancelled formulation concerns the automorphisms fixing a vertex. A small polygon with a filled corner stands alongside it.

**Scans 16–17. The local stabilizers.** The proposition marked « (Exer) » defines \(p,q\) using the orders of two products of involutions. It counts the incident edges at a vertex and in a face. Restriction to the corresponding polygons identifies the vertex and face stabilizers with dihedral groups. The edge stabilizer permutes two endpoints and two incident faces independently. The next exercise starts and is crossed out before a conclusion is written.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · model · high.** The incidence counts on scan 6 give \(2\cdot30=5\cdot12=3\cdot20=60\). This checks the numerical identity \(2a=ps=qf\) for the numbers written there.

**M2 · model · high.** Opposite pairs of vertices, faces and edges give \(12/2=6\), \(20/2=10\), and \(30/2=15\). Dividing 120 by those three numbers gives 20, 12 and 8, the orders written beside the three projections on scans 7–9. The arithmetic alone cannot identify the groups.

**M3 · model · high for the first two sums, low for the last assignment.** The A rows on scan 7 sum to \(10+10+10=30\). The B rows on scan 9 sum to \(6+12+6+6=30\). The live C-column entries read \(2,8,2,8,8\), which sum to 28. Counting the separately enclosed 2 as an additional class gives 30. The crossing and leader on the C list make that assignment uncertain.

**M4 · model · high.** The two-element choices from scans 3 and 12 give three counts of icosahedral frames using scan 6. Counting through each edge gives \(30\cdot2\cdot2=120\). Counting through vertices gives \(12\cdot5\cdot2=120\), and through faces \(20\cdot3\cdot2=120\). This connects the incidence table with the numerator 120 in the three drawings.

**M5 · model · low for a convention issue on scan 15.** For a left action with ordinary composition, the frame-matching correspondence in d) can reverse multiplication. In a six-element permutation example, left multiplication acts on the elements and right multiplication gives its commuting automorphisms. At the identity the two representatives agree. For two noncommuting transpositions the matching map reverses their product. The example shows where a right-action or opposite-group convention would enter the word « épi ». The sheet does not specify that convention.

**M6 · model · medium.** The intervals called polygons on scans 16–17 are written with weak inequalities, \(\Phi_{\ge s}\) and \(\Phi_{\le f}\). On scan 3 the corresponding polygon conditions use strict inequalities. In the incidence order the weak intervals include the distinguished vertex or face itself. Whether the later notation implicitly sets that element aside is a reading of the mathematical usage.

**R1 · reading question · scan 3, condition 3′.** The frozen text reads « à pfs ≥ 2 côtés ». A close reading of the cluster after the p suggests \(p(s)\). A small opening bend, an s-shaped centre and a second curved stroke appear between p and the inequality. The parallel line uses \(q(f)\). Confidence high in the proposed reading. The page file retains « pfs ».

**R2 · reading question · scan 16, cancelled b).** The frozen formula has \(=\) immediately after the indexed round letter. In the close view there is a small pair of marks before the two horizontal strokes, so \(:=\) is also possible. Confidence low because a diagonal cancellation crosses the same area.

**R3 · model · high for the two lower outlines.** Pale angular outlines behind the lower text of scan 6 correspond to the two darker drawings below the horizontal rule on scan 7. Their sizes and heights match, with left and right interchanged. I take them to be show-through. The drawings are transcribed on scan 7.
