# Mathematical notes on scans 389–404

Grothendieck, A. (n.d.). *Dérivateurs [chapitres XVII à XIX, dont sommaires de chacun des chapitres]* [Handwritten manuscript, Montpellier archive, cote 157-5, manuscript pp. 303–318, scans 389–404]. All scan references below refer to this manuscript. Statement identifiers correspond to the check register where a check or use occurred.

## What the pages do

**W01 · Definition 4.13.11, scan 389.** A $\pi_0$-$\pi$-adapted indexing category is equivalent to a small category, satisfies the condition the manuscript abbreviates as *grde devant* $\pi_0$, and admits the indicated inductive limits of cardinality at most $\pi$. An adapted inductive system takes its terms in $M_\pi$ and its indexing functor preserves those limits. A marginal note identifies the stability requirement when $\pi=\pi_0$ with stability under direct factors.

**W02 · Proposition 4.13.12, scans 389–390.** For $M$ $\pi_0$-accessible and a useful cardinal $\pi>\pi_0$, every object $x$ admits an adapted presentation. The proposed index is the slice $M_\pi/x$. The opening of scan 390 calls the verification for this canonical system immediate.

**W03 · Ordered indexing and Lemma 4.13.13, scans 390–391.** To replace the slice by an ordered set, the author chooses a small full subcategory equivalent to the slice and orders suitable subcategories $J'$ by inclusion. Each has cardinality at most $\pi$ and satisfies the condition *grde devant* $\pi_0$. The equality $\pi^{\pi_0}=\pi$ enters here. The lemma applies the construction to a small $J$ satisfying *grde devant* $\pi$ and identifies the union of an increasing family with its inductive limit.

**W04 · Lemma 4.13.13.1 and its argument, scans 391–392.** The lemma transfers the condition on the categories $J_r$ to their inductive limit when the indexing ordered set $A$ satisfies the corresponding condition. The proof uses the diagonal $J\to J^B$ for a set $B$ of cardinality at most $\pi_0$. Commutation of the indicated products with the inductive limit identifies that diagonal with the limit of the cofinal diagonals $J_r\to J_r^B$.

**W05 · Lemma 4.13.13.2, scans 392–393.** For a system indexed by $J$, the partial colimits $x_{J'}$ form a system indexed by the ordered collection of $J'$. The induced functor to $M$ preserves the stated class of inductive limits. The lemma also asserts adaptedness when $M$ is $\pi_0$-accessible and the original terms belong to $M_\pi$. The existence condition on $M$ is identified by an $L$ with a cardinal subscript. The possible additional arrow on scan 392 is recorded in the transcription.

**W06 · Corollary 4.13.14, scans 393–394.** Under the cardinal equation and the hypotheses of Proposition 4.13.12, the adapted presentation of $x$ can have an ordered indexing set $I$. If $\pi'>\pi$ and $x\in M_{\pi'}$, the corollary adds the bound $\operatorname{card} I\leq(\pi')^\pi$.

**W07 · Theorem 4.13.15, scans 394–395.** For a strictly full $\underline S\subset M$, set $\underline S(\pi)=\underline S\cap M_\pi$. Condition a combines accessibility in $M$ with stability under the indicated inductive limits. Condition a′ puts the bounded stability condition on $\underline S(\pi)$ inside $M_\pi$. Condition b detects $x\in\underline S$ by cofinality of $\underline S(\pi)/x$ in $M_\pi/x$. Condition c tests cofinality of the indices with $x_i\in\underline S$ in every specified adapted presentation. Both b and c include the bounded stability clause. The last variant restricts to ordered indexing sets. The statement presents the conditions as equivalent under its hypotheses. The starred qualification and condition labels have reading difficulties.

**W08 · Corollary 4.13.16 and its argument, scans 395–396.** The existence of suitable cardinals satisfying the preceding conditions characterizes accessibility of $\underline S$ in $M$. Given accessibility at $\pi'_0$, the author takes $\pi>\pi'_0$ with $\pi^{\pi'_0}=\pi$. He distinguishes the intrinsically defined $\underline S_\pi$ from the intersection $\underline S(\pi)$. His argument for $\underline S_\pi\subset\underline S(\pi)$ uses closure in $M$, containment of $\underline S_{\pi'_0}$ and the cited implication in 4.7.1.

**W09 · First implications in the theorem, scans 396–398.** The diagram runs from a to a′ to c, with branches from c to c′ and b. For a to a′ the author passes to the intersection in $M$. For c to b he uses the canonical presentation. In the argument for a′ to c, he sets $J=M_\pi/x$, $J'=\underline S(\pi)/x$ and $I'=I_{\underline S}$. A cartesian square identifies $I'$ with the inverse image of $J'$ under $\varphi$. The properties listed beneath it concern largeness of the index, cofinality, and existence and preservation of the specified limits.

**W10 · Transfinite construction and its intended consequence, scans 399–401.** The author reduces the remaining cofinality assertion to his condition F1, obtaining an arrow from a given $i$ to an object of $I'$. He proposes systems $i_\alpha$ in $I$ and $j_\alpha$ in $J'$, linked by $u_\alpha:\varphi(i_\alpha)\to j_\alpha$ and $v_\alpha:j_\alpha\to\varphi(i_{\alpha+1})$. At the colimit he seeks an object $i'$ receiving the map from $i=i_0$. The two compatibility squares on scan 400 induce maps in opposite directions between the colimit objects. Opposite identities are needed for the assertion that they are inverse isomorphisms. The two written identities are recorded under S11.

**W11 · Initial and inductive stages, scans 401–403.** Cofinality of $J'$ gives the first arrow to an object $j_0$. At stage $\alpha$, the induction hypothesis distinguishes the previously constructed $u_\beta$ from the $v_\beta$ requiring $\beta+1<\alpha$. Scan 402 displays four compatibility conditions. Scan 403 lists the two new objects, their transition maps, $u_\alpha$ and, at a successor stage $\alpha=\alpha_0+1$, the map $v_{\alpha_0}$.

**W12 · Compatibility at the new stage, scans 403–404.** The bounds on the previous stages are enlarged to include the new endpoint. Scan 404 displays the four resulting diagrams in the order $(3_\alpha)(\beta)$, $(4_\alpha)(\beta)$, $(1_\alpha)$, $(2_\alpha)$. The first square applies for $\beta<\alpha$. The remaining requirements are given for a successor stage. The circled labels identify arrows introduced among the data on manuscript p. 317.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 399 and 401.** The stage equations fix the order of composition. If $A_\alpha=\varphi(i_\alpha)$ and $B_\alpha=j_\alpha$, then $v_\alpha u_\alpha$ starts in $A_\alpha$ and $u_{\alpha+1}v_\alpha$ starts in $B_\alpha$. The same endpoint check gives the proposed order in S11.

**R02 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 394, 396–397.** The underline under *dans M* makes the ambient category of the closure argument explicit. The identity $\underline S(\pi)=\underline S\cap M_\pi$ then explains why the text first compares the two kinds of small object and subsequently invokes stability of their intersection.

**R03 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 402–404.** The distinction between $\beta<\alpha$ and $\beta+1<\alpha$ can be read directly from the targets of the arrows. An arrow $u_\beta$ uses objects at one stage. An arrow $v_\beta$ also uses the next $i$-object. This accounts for the extra datum requested at a successor stage.

**R04 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 401, line 17.** The frozen base equation gives $i_0=\text{0 or }i$, with 0 first. The right glyph is rounded and lacks a clearly detached dot. Scan 400 explicitly writes $i_0=i$, which supports the second reading of scan 401.

**R05 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 396, line 2.** The frozen clause reads “$\underline S$ est $\underline S_\pi$-accessible”. The surrounding proof asks for $\pi$-accessibility, which suggests “$\underline S$ est $\pi$-accessible” as a possible intended formulation. The second ruled $\underline S$ is visible on the sheet.
