# Mathematical notes · cote 79, scans 81–96

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 81–82.** Grothendieck takes an automorphism commuting with the unit action and fixing a frame. He follows what it fixes, first on multiples of the basis vectors and then on other bases, using three displayed matrices. If the units generate the ring additively, he claims that the enriched triangulation determines its module automorphisms. Equations (21)–(22) express the conditions on the vertex permutation.

**U2 · Scans 83–84.** Equation (23) proposes a presentation of the underlying additive group of $M$. Its generators are indexed by $M^*$ and its relations use admissible sums and opposites. Equation (24) states the exactness still needed for this presentation. Grothendieck distinguishes the additive conclusion from $\Lambda$-linearity and considers what changes when the individual face families are remembered.

**U3 · Scans 85–87.** Assuming $2\cdot1_\Lambda\ne0$, he divides the vertex and face sets by the antipodal involution. A quotient face has two lifts. He lists four lifts of an edge and groups them in two antipodal pairs. Equations (30)–(37) describe the determinant parameters, the indexed face families, the two faces through an edge, their orientations, and recovery of an indexed face family from its edges.

**U4 · Scans 88–89.** A vertex star is described by the affine set $L_x$ of vectors satisfying $x\wedge y=e$, with translation module $\Lambda x$. Grothendieck uses the successor on this set to discuss connectedness and vertex order. Equation (40) gives the condition $\Lambda_0=\Lambda$ for each indexed polyhedron. Equation (41) imposes the further one-index condition for $P_M$ and lists $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ for $n=3,4,6$.

**U5 · Scans 89–91.** An automorphism of the whole face family acts on its index set. Equations (42)–(44) compare this action with the primed linear groups and their actions on frames. The theorem on scan 90 identifies three automorphism groups under $\Lambda=\Lambda_0$. The scholie presents the corresponding groupoids, first with an orientation, then with an orientation up to sign, then with the family of structures on the common vertex set.

**U6 · Scans 91–92.** Items a)–f) set out a reconstruction programme. It includes axioms for the combinatorial structures, the gerbe of module lifts, reconstruction of the modules and their torsors and actions, recovery of the family from one member, and additional data over a general ring.

**U7 · Scans 93–94.** Section VI turns to the commutant on a homogeneous set. Fixing a point with stabilizer $H$ leads to the normalizer quotient in (48). For the action on $M^*$, the matrix calculation (49)–(50) gives an upper triangular normalizer. The resulting commuting permutations are the scalar homotheties.

**U8 · Scans 95–96.** The calculation is repeated after division by sign. The lower-left entry $c$ must satisfy $c^2=0$, and $act$ must be either 0 or 2 for every $t$. The exceptional case has $2\Lambda=\{0,2\}$ and allows $c=2$. Grothendieck distinguishes the index-two normalizer from the commutant, for which he writes a direct product. His last example is the antipodism of the octahedron over $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence · scans 93–96.** Over $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ there are six primitive vertex classes modulo sign. Enumeration gives 24 permutations from the determinant-one group and exactly two permutations commuting with all 24. The nonidentity one interchanges three pairs. This checks the commutant size and the fixed-point-free involution in the last example.
- **R2 · Model, high confidence · scans 93 and 96.** The margin's matrix for $\sigma$ determines the image of the base vertex through (48). Over $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$, direct evaluation of that matrix fixes the class of the second basis vector, whereas the equivariant permutation determined by the image of the first basis vector moves all six vertices. This distinction matters when interpreting the word “antipodisme”.
- **R3 · Model, low confidence · scan 88, (39).** The final character of the successor was overwritten. Moving inside $L_x$ suggests $y+x$, while the margin's coordinate in a torsor under $\Lambda$ gives $y+1$. The distinction gives a reason for both readings but does not identify the upper ink layer.

**Reading question after the freeze.** On scan 96, page-file line 17, the final transcription has “Ce cas a lieu où”. Turning the marginal text makes “Ce cas est celui où” plausible. Only the conjunction was marked at the freeze. Its neighbouring words could not be amended in the witness pass. This question concerns the French wording of the extension description.

**Reading question on the index set.** Scan 86, (30)–(32), page-file lines 10–21, renders the determinant parameter as $\overrightarrow\pi_M$ and $\pi_M$. Its straight legs and flat top also permit $\overrightarrow\Pi_M$ and $\Pi_M$. A similar ambiguity was already marked on scans 81 and 87. The issue is the handwritten alphabet, with no change of index set intended in this observation.
