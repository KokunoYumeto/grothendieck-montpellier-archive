**Three torsors and a ternary relation, scan 108.** For a commutative group \(G\), the three \(G\)-torsors and a trivialization of their product give a subset \(\Gamma\subset P_1\times P_2\times P_3\). The sheet describes it as a torsor under the kernel of the sum map. Its announced aim is full faithfulness of the functor that remembers the three sets and \(\Gamma\). Condition (1) requires the three two-coordinate projections to be isomorphisms. The author then writes the corresponding binary map \(\pi_3\) and its two families of bijective partial maps.

**The kernel calculation, scan 109.** A quadruple \((u,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\) is sent to three compositions of translations and \(u\). The kernel line identifies the three group elements and expresses \(u\) by an inner automorphism. The following products work out conjugation of a prospective kernel element. Two quotient expressions at the bottom use respectively \(\operatorname{Int}G\) and \(\operatorname{Aut}G\), with targets \(\Gamma(G)\) and \(\widehat\Gamma(G)\).

**The product-one relation and change of base point, scan 110.** In trivialized coordinates the relation is \(xyz=1\). The author solves it for each coordinate, identifies a parametrized family of triples, and replaces \(\gamma=(a_1,a_2,a_3)\) by \(\sigma(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)(\gamma)\). The three resulting maps \(\widetilde a'_i\) differ from the earlier maps by inner automorphisms. The order of the factors in the three expressions for \(x,y,z\) was checked [C110].

**Binary operations and small tables, scan 111.** The opening trial compares three equations with sum zero and ends in a cancelled calculation about \(2(x_2-a_2)\). The author gives \(x\circ y=-x-y\) and writes both ways of parenthesizing a triple; their displayed expansions agree with direct calculation [C111]. Two four-by-four tables and trials with a bar and an asterisk follow. The final lines contain \(x*y=y*x\), \(0*y=y\), \(x*0=x\) and a possible bijectivity assertion.

**Choosing three origins, scan 112.** Starting from a triple in \(\Gamma\), the sheet varies one coordinate at a time, then writes \(P_3\times P_3\longrightarrow P_3\). Several tables on \(0,1,2,3,4\) record trials and erasures. In another writing direction a triangle with vertices \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) and groups along its sides accompanies a contracted product over \(G_3\) and \(G_1\). The clean cyclic table at the foot is crossed through.

**Bitorsors, opposites and an action, scan 113.** Each \(P_i\) is paired with the adjacent two groups; the right column reverses their roles on the opposite bitorsor. Three cyclic products are compared with unit bitorsors. Condition (A) expresses invariance of a nonempty \(\Gamma\) under \((x_1,x_2,x_3)\mapsto(g_2x_1g_3^{-1},g_3x_2g_1^{-1},g_1x_3g_2^{-1})\). The author then describes a single orbit as the intended axiom. A chosen \(\gamma\) yields correspondences of groups via \(g_2a_1=a_1g_3\) and its cyclic variants.

**Double covers and a graph, scan 114.** Here \(V\) has dimension two over \(\mathbb F_2\), and \(I\) has three elements. The sheet gives double covers of the \(P_i\) and of \(\Gamma\), together with pullback identifications. It defines adjacency on the 24 lifted points, first by the involution within a cover, then using a point lying over the relation. A unique-lift statement leads to an axiom about injections into the sections of a cover. The counts 16 and 24 were checked [C114a, C114b].

**Comparisons along the fibres, scan 115.** Diagrams join \((x_1,x_2)\) to pairs with a chosen coordinate. **Counting by the position of a base coordinate, scan 116.** A point \(s\) of \(\Gamma\) gives \(p_i(s)=a_i\) and \(E=E_s\). A diagram from one fibre has arrows \(u,v,w\). The author separates \(\{s\}\), the three sets \(\Gamma_i=p_i^{-1}\{a_i\}-\{s\}\), and a remaining set. Two counts are written above the partition. A boxed sum weights the different parts. At the foot, ratios of the three maps are denoted by \(\zeta,\eta\). The arithmetic of the complement and the boxed expression is recorded under [C116].

**Stabilizers and normalizing three maps, scan 117.** Replacing \(a_1\) by \(\alpha_2a_1\alpha_3^{-1}\) gives a conjugation formula for the induced isomorphism. **Incidences and a questioned implication, scan 118.** Twelve paired branches surround the \(u,v,w\) triangle. The lists give neighbours for whole families and then for individual labels. **Multiplication of quadruples, scan 119.** After composing translated maps, the author collects their parameters. **Sphere products, curves and a projector, scan 120.** Among the drawings are products of two spheres with a diagonal removed, a second removal read as “antidiag”, and two linked vertical sequences with fundamental groups. ## Remarks of the model

- **Model [C106], high.** Seven single indices in each of the first two lists give 14 entries. The two lists indexed by \(i<h\) have 21 entries apiece. This accounts for 56 independently of the uncertain ending of the French noun.
- **Model [C110, C111], high.** Free-group cancellation confirms the order of the three inverse products on 110. On 111 simultaneous symbolic expansion gives \(x+y-z\) and \(-x+y+z\) for the two parenthesizations of the written operation.
- **Model [R113], medium.** Condition (A) specifies invariance. A single orbit also requires the ability to move between two of its points. A check with the trivial action on a two-point set illustrates that distinction. The sheets also impose torsor and projection conditions; this observation leaves their possible role in transitivity open.
- **Model [C114a, C114b], high.** Choosing compatible origins gives a four-element model for each torsor. In that model 16 triples have sum zero, with one triple over every pair. Three double covers of four-point sets give 24 points before the graph edges are assigned.
- **Model [C115, C116], high for the arithmetic and low for the restoration on 116.** The polynomial on 115 evaluates to 21 at \(n=4\). The expression above the complement on 116 simplifies to \(n^2-3n+2\). The live part of the box yields the same polynomial as 115 if its uncertain inner coefficient is 3. The notation “\(21\,/\,42\)” gives no independent explanation of 42.
- **Model [C119], high for the finite check.** Taking the two automorphisms to be conjugations by two noncommuting permutations in \(S_3\) gives agreement of the two \(\alpha\)-coordinates for all 216 choices of \(\alpha,\alpha',\alpha''\). The check uses the order of factors printed in the sheet.
