# Mathematical notes · Cote 91, scans 34–46

## What the pages do

**An outline of the Hom chapter (34).** The heading retains « Schémas Hom » and cancels « et calcul différentiel ». The list starts with the Hom formalism and a second functor whose glyph is uncertain. It moves from an affine existence result to gluing, immersions and morphisms of finite type. Simplicity, étaleness, quasi-étaleness, quasi-projectivity and Greenberg each have an entry. The changes of number and the long arrow concern the order of exposition.

**Internal Hom and direct image (35, upper half).** The diagram fixes $X\to T\to S$ and the two projections of $T\times_S T$. Five identities relate the notation $f_*(X/T)$ to relative Hom. One realizes it by a fibre product involving the Hom from $T$ to itself and $S$. Another expresses the relative Hom of $X/T$ and $Y/T$ by applying $f_*$ to their Hom over $T$.

**The descended object (35, middle).** Given a descent datum on $X$, Grothendieck proposes to obtain $X_0$ as the fibre product of two copies of $f_*(X)$ over $g_*(X\times_S T)$. A bracketed addition qualifies the existence assertion. The word describing the extra conditions is doubtful.

**The Nagata example (35–36).** The starting point is a scheme $X$ with a free action of a group of order two and no quotient $X/G$. Grothendieck takes a field $L$ on which $G$ also acts, writes $K=L^G$, and uses the diagonal action to construct a descent datum. An attempted scalar-extension argument is cancelled. The surviving argument singles out a closed orbit which no affine open contains. Its inverse image is called a closed part and then an orbit for $G\times1$. On this part the text identifies the diagonal action with that of $G\times1$. It concludes with two Hom nonexistence assertions. A further observation about $K$ breaks off.

**A second outline (37).** The first four sections separate descent of a flat object along an arbitrary morphism from descent of an arbitrary object along a flat morphism. The applications include unramified morphisms, quotients, Galois descent, Nagata and Cartier. Sections 5 and 6 introduce Hom and $f_*$, then applications to differential geometry and Greenberg. Sections 7–10 turn to representability, formal modules, a projective/proper case and Picard. The crossed diagram at the top and the instructions about moving material belong to the working plan.

**A functorial isomorphism (38).** For finite locally free $f:T\to S$ and a locally free quasi-coherent sheaf $F$ on $T$, the boxed formula identifies $f_*(\underline{\check V}(F))$ with $\underline{\check V}(f_*(F))$. The accompanying sentence records compatibility with inverse images and mentions localization.

**A least subsheaf and an intersection (39).** The base is local noetherian, $f$ is proper with flatness added above the line, and $F'_1$ is a coherent subsheaf of $F'=f^*F$. The proposed least subsheaf has pullback containing $F'_1$. Finite intersections are handled by flatness. Grothendieck then forms the intersection of a decreasing family, passes to a quotient and asks how infinite intersections behave under pullback. The marginal definition uses the intersection of the corresponding modules. The final reduction concerns the closed fibre and a local module argument ending in a question mark.

**Two forms of an open-cover criterion (40).** Proposition 2 starts with $f_*(X/T)$ already existing. Its condition on multisections over finite extensions of residue fields is used to describe a cover by the $f_{i*}(U_i/T)$. The proof appeals to closed points and finiteness hypotheses. Proposition $2^{\mathrm{bis}}$, first headed « Corollaire », proceeds from existence on the members of a given open family to existence for $X/T$. A marginal instruction says that it replaces Proposition 2.

**Gluing the representing scheme (41–42).** The direct images of the intersections $U_{ij}$ are open in the pieces for $U_i$ and $U_j$. Grothendieck glues the $H_i$, constructs a morphism $H\times_S T\to X$, and writes the test on $S'$-valued points. A long attempt at injectivity is cancelled. Starting with a section of $X/T$, he chooses an $i(s)$ and a neighbourhood $V(s)$, obtains a map to $H_i$, and leaves the gluing of the maps to be shown.

**The theorem and two corollaries (42–43).** The theorem assumes $T$ finite locally free over $S$. It asks that each indicated multisection over a finite residue-field extension be contained in an affine open of $X$. Under that condition the direct image exists and is the union of the direct images of the affine opens. Corollary 1 gives two ways to satisfy the hypothesis, including geometric injectivity of $T/S$ and a local condition on finite sets of points. Quasi-projectivity over $S$ is written in brackets as an example. Corollary 2 treats a closed subscheme $X'$ of $X$.

**Products, immersions and classes of morphism (44).** A table lists the affine, projective, proper and quasi-projective cases, with a brace, a separability qualification and an asterisk. Left exactness is spelled out in three identities, including the fibre product over $f_*(Z/T)$. The writer then considers open and closed immersions, affine morphisms, and projective and quasi-projective morphisms. The qualification about a covering by affines is important to the passage on immersions. A later sentence explicitly distinguishes the separable case for projective morphisms from the assertion « en tous cas » for quasi-projective morphisms.

**Descent and relative ampleness (44–45).** The starred note gives a descent argument to pursue for properness and projectivity. One margin invokes the lemma of Chow and asks about faithful flatness without the additional hypotheses. Another reduction asks for $S$-ampleness of the determinant of the direct image of an invertible $T$-ample sheaf. The last lines of scan 44 continue at the top of scan 45 with the determinant of the descended sheaf.

**The determinant proposition (45).** For $g:X_{/T}\to Y_{/T}$ and an invertible sheaf ample, respectively very ample, for $g$, the proposition states the corresponding ampleness of $\det f_*(\underline L)$ for the induced morphism. The proof passes through projective space with its fundamental sheaf. Commutation with products leads to $Y=T$. Locally it takes $S=\mathrm{Spec}(A)$, $T=\mathrm{Spec}(B)$ with $B$ free of finite rank over $A$. The first of two announced verifications concerns the Grassmannian, a sheaf on it and a projective immersion.

**The locus in the Grassmannian (46).** Verification B introduces an algebra sheaf $\underline A$ of rank $d$ on $G$, a locally free module $\underline F$, and a subsheaf $\underline F_1$. The question on a test scheme $G'$ concerns local freeness of a quotient over $\underline A'$. The sought subscheme of $G$ should be characterized by factorization of $G'\to G$. Local bases for $B$, $B^n$, the subsheaf and a complement are introduced. The last line announces the search for a necessary and sufficient condition.

## Remarks of the model

**My remark M1 (high confidence, scans 40–42).** Reading Proposition 2 and Proposition $2^{\mathrm{bis}}$ as successive drafts clarifies the different uses of existence. Proposition 2 begins by assuming existence for $X/T$. Its replacement assumes existence on the opens and the proof constructs $H$. The marginal « remplace prop. 2 » directly supports this reading.

**My remark M2 (medium confidence, scans 35–36).** The move from the cancelled tensor-product calculation to the orbit argument helps explain the request for a lemma. The margin aligns with the passage from a closed orbit to its inverse image and the absence of an affine neighbourhood. It does not say exactly what the desired lemma would assert.

**My remark M3 (conditional calculation, scan 46 iv).** If the first basis bound is $(n-k)d$, the two basis lengths in (iv) sum to the $nd$ entries indexed in (iii). Expanding $(n-k)d+kd-nd$ gives zero. Confidence in this arithmetic is high; confidence that the small stroke between $n$ and $k$ is a minus is only medium. The calculation does not decide the letter under $c$ or the order of the earlier primed quotient.

**Reading question (scan 43, Cor. 2).** The frozen phrase is « du thm » with « th. » also marked. Hua reads « dites » across the whole group. The d-like opening is separated by a gap from the tall crossed t. This is a possible different segmentation; the word « du » lies outside the frozen doubt mark.
