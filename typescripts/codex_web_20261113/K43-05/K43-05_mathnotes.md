# What the pages do

**Scans 64–65: rings attached to chains.** These working pages place products indexed by chains beside expressions such as `A_{[p_1,…,p_n]}`, completions, tensor products, and inverse limits. Scan 65 explicitly introduces a recursive definition and discusses a residue ring described by a total ring of fractions. Scan 64 also experiments with several topologies on one ring. The lowest prose there and some prime/completion notation on scan 65 remain doubtful; these notes do not turn the fragments into a completed definition.

**Scans 66–69: a graded algebra and path-indexed maps.** Scan 66 sets down a topological graded algebra, elements `π_x`, and a degree-one `θ` with `θ²=0` and vanishing conditions on `π_{x'}θπ_x`. Scans 67–68 explore Hom expressions for the objects `K(x)`, restricted products, composition, and differential diagrams. Scan 69 associates `H_s` with paths and ends with an ordered set, a category of composed arrows, and a functor. Several expressions are abandoned or incomplete. The many small sketches are retained separately from the formulas.

**Scans 70–74: local cohomology in the Zariski setting.** The cover introduces “Jacobiennes locales.” Scan 71 distinguishes a Čech-type theory and writes local and punctured-spectrum cohomology formulas, followed by dimension cases. Scan 72 is a cancelled, inverted draft of related formulas. Scans 73–74 give an argument reducing the groups under discussion first to a linear connected part and then to a unipotent part, with `G_m` and `G_a` calculations and a sheaf sequence. The last observation concerns replacement of `A` by its completion. This paragraph reports the manuscript's argument, not an independent verification of its hypotheses or claims.

**Scans 75–76: the proposed local relative Jacobian.** Scan 75 specifies `J_{A/k}` through a Hom functor and `H_A^i(G)`, with `i=dim A`, then describes cases in dimensions zero and one. Marginal additions discuss representability, projectivity, and multiplicative/unipotent pieces. Scan 76 completes the dimension-one discussion and gives the case `dim A≥2`, calling the object connected, unipotent, and projective. The dense marginal material on scan 75 is among the least secure readings in the package.

**Scans 77–78: the Weil-cohomology alternative.** A new heading announces cohomology of Weil, with a doubtful adverse qualification. Scan 77 contrasts its local vanishing behaviour with the earlier setting, compares a henselian local ring with its residue field, and discusses a kernel in degree one. Scan 78 gives a relative-cohomology sequence for `A` and `K`, quotient comparisons, and a bottom diagram involving `π_1`, primed `π` symbols, and `B` symbols. The bottom diagram is not supplied with an explanatory construction on these pages.

# Slips

- **Scan 67, last two lines — low confidence as an authorial-slip diagnosis.** The frozen text reads `θu+(-1)^{deg u}uθ`, then gives `θu-uθ` when `deg u=0` and `deg θ=1`. If the latter is intended as a specialization of the former, replacing the preceding plus by minus would reconcile them: `θu-(-1)^{deg u}uθ`. The preceding brace expression and an overwritten variable are uncertain, so this is only a conditional proposed correction, not an established convention. The transcription keeps the plus signs.
- **Scan 64, final prose — low confidence.** Two consecutive doubtful readings of “pour” are retained. A single “pour” is a possible intended form if this is a repetition, but the words are not secure enough to establish a slip.

No other definite correction is proposed. In particular, unfinished expressions, the isolated equals on scan 74, and changes made inside cancelled drafts are not automatically authorial errors.
