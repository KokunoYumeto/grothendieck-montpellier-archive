# Mathematical notes on scans 218–228

## What the pages do

**218 · Invariant forms and the module.** Section 16 constructs a top-degree left-invariant form from an exterior product and gives its local determinant formula (31). Right translations act on the line of such forms by a factor δ. Equations (32) and (33) relate that factor to the determinant of the adjoint action. For a locally compact base field, (34) and (35) relate the absolute value of the form, Haar measure, and the module of the group.

**219, 221 · Comparing fibre functors.** The manuscript starts with two archimedean places of a normal irreducible prescheme and asks when their integral fibre functors are isomorphic. It separately considers their restrictions to each multiplicative subcategory of finite type. Scan 219 passes to a field of constants and a finite-type model, with two drawings of the base data. The lower note gives flatness, smoothness and connectedness conditions. Scan 221 treats a countable field by a sequence of finite-type subalgebras. Points above the two places give a succession of path sets; the author uses surjective transition maps and a nonempty inverse limit. A second case, in which the places disagree on E, leads to a conjecture.

**220 · Unimodularity.** Formulas (26)–(28) give the effects of left translation, right translation and inversion on a Haar measure, on integrals, and on measurable sets. Proposition 2 uses a compact neighbourhood of the identity invariant under inner automorphisms. Its corollary lists discrete, compact and commutative groups. Two normalizations are specified, one by point mass and one by total mass. The closing lines discuss subgroups, quotients and classes of Lie groups.

**222 · Uniqueness and examples.** The top finishes a proportionality argument by comparing the functions denoted by D with two different subscripts. Equation (17) gives the factor at the identity. A corollary states proportionality for complex invariant measures. Three examples exhibit Lebesgue measure on the additive real group, dx/x on the positive multiplicative real group, and the measure on the circle obtained from an integral over any real interval of length one. The next proposition about discreteness breaks off mid-sentence.

**223 · Detecting places with integral structures.** The author proposes looking for a smooth projective X whose cohomology rings at two places are non-isomorphic; its two handwritten subscripts are uncertain. For a motive over a number field with a fixed place, he attaches a principal homogeneous space to another place. He asks which such spaces arise and writes a formula identifying integral points of a group with a group carrying the indices 1 and M. The slanting marginal note adds a comparison with a complex realization and a Hodge compatibility condition.

**224, 226 · Existence of Haar measure.** The typescript's page - 6 - on scan 226 defines a positive nonzero left or right Haar measure and states existence and uniqueness up to a constant. In part A it defines (f:g) as the infimum of the sums of coefficients in bounds by translates of g, and lists seven properties. Page - 7 - on scan 224 continues the argument for (iv), composes bounds to prove (v), deduces (vi), and uses two cutoff functions and a compact neighbourhood for (vii). It finishes by taking an ultrafilter finer than a stated filter base.

**225 · Hodge structures.** The manuscript asks what becomes of the distinction between places when the fibre functor is viewed as taking values in Hodge structures. The left margin questions which points or places should enter the definition. A diagram and successive reductions are heavily struck through. The surviving last paragraph proposes a reduction to a punctured projective space (the exponent is overwritten or star-like), and suggests an elliptic-curve motive with indeterminate invariant j.

**227 · Reconstruction and moduli.** The author asks which multiplicative functors to Hodge structures come from archimedean places, and how much of a morphism of preschemes can be recovered from the functor it induces. He connects this with constructing moduli schemes for polarized motives. He proposes a criterion involving a global polarized motive and a map to “Modules”, then considers an inverse limit of preschemes and the case of a field.

**228 · Right multipliers and inversion.** Formulas (7′)–(9′) describe the multiplier of a relatively invariant measure for a right action. The sheet passes through the opposite group and distinguishes six forms of invariance for translations on a group. Formulas (10)–(13) give the effect of inversion on functions, measures, integrals and sets.

## Slips

- **S218.1 · 218, (34). High confidence.** The typescript prints three closing parentheses after the final x of the evaluation pair. Two would close the evaluation pair and the argument of mod. In (31) the comparable expression has two. The extra parenthesis remains in the transcription.
- **S220.1 / S220.2 · 220, paragraph on normalized measures. High confidence for the literal universal reading.** “Les deux conventions précédentes ne concordent pas lorsque G est à la fois discret et compact, c'est-à-dire fini”. There is one exception. For a finite group of order N the singleton masses are respectively 1 and 1/N. The stated mismatch applies when N > 1. The original wording remains in place.

## Remarks of the model

- **R219.1 · 219, 221. High confidence.** The quantifier in a) on 219 ranges over α. The argument on 221 introduces a new compatibility requirement across i and uses a nonempty inverse limit of paths. The distinction explains why the transition maps matter in the countable case.
- **P220.1 · 220, proof of Proposition 2. High confidence.** The proof explicitly records both conditions needed to cancel μ(V) in μ(V) = Δ_G(s)μ(V). Its strict inequalities make the factor finite and positive.
- **R224.1 · 224, last two lines of the long estimate. High confidence.** Expanding the brackets shows that the last bound exceeds the previous one by 2η times the difference (f+f′:h)(h:g) − (f+f′:g). The sign is exactly what property (v) on 226 supplies.
- **R225.1 · 225, final reduction. Low confidence about the exponent.** The live text has a star-like mark above ℙ, with 1 as an alternative. The three removed points and the following elliptic family favour a projective-line interpretation. Neither consideration identifies which strokes belonged to the original exponent. No mathematical correction was made.
