# Mathematical notes on cote 62, scans 2, 4–17 and 19

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 4.** The zero section of an elliptic curve over a locally noetherian base gives a divisor and an invertible sheaf. The leaf relates its restriction along the section to the Lie line. The constant section 1 gives the maps between successive powers whose direct images are denoted by $\mathcal E_n$. The margin interprets sections as relative rational functions.

**U2 · Scan 5.** Proposition 1 gives the ranks of these direct images and distinguishes ampleness from very ampleness. Its proof uses genus-one Riemann–Roch for nonzero degree. For very ampleness the author excludes degrees 1 and 2 by the dimension of the target and reduces sufficiency to the case of an algebraically closed field.

**U3 · Scans 6–7.** Proposition 2 filters an algebra of functions by the $\mathcal E_n$. The first two terms coincide. The succeeding quotients are lines, and the author studies multiplication between them. He reduces to a field and considers functions of exact pole orders $n$ and $m$. A corollary singles out $\mathcal E_3$ as a space of generators. The last denominator or subscript in the product argument on scan 7 is uncertain.

**U4 · Scans 7–8.** Proposition 3 makes an invariant derivation act from one graded piece to the next. Its local computation tests whether differentiating a pole of order $n$ produces a pole of order $n+1$. Two attempts at corollaries on scan 8 are crossed out. A separate live corollary asserts vanishing when the positive characteristic divides $n$.

**U5 · Scan 8.** An exact sequence along the zero section gives a second description of the successive quotients. The displayed conclusion identifies $\mathcal L_{n+1}$ with $\mathcal L^{\otimes(n+1)}$ in the range written under the box. Grothendieck identifies the multiplication in Proposition 2 with multiplication of these tensor powers and proposes to change the order of exposition.

**U6 · Scan 9.** The third power of the divisor sheaf embeds the curve as a relative cubic. Its filtration distinguishes a section and a line, which the author calls an inflection tangent. The drawing shows the line meeting the curve. The leaf then chooses an adapted basis beginning with 1 and starts, and cancels, a coordinate equation.

**U7 · Scan 10.** Six monomials in the adapted coordinates form a basis of $\mathcal E_6$. Expressing $x^2$ in that basis gives an affine equation. The author introduces a degree-three form, argues that it defines the same divisor, and places a discriminant in a twelfth tensor power. The two margins ask for more detail on the comparison of equations.

**U8 · Scans 11–12.** In the reverse construction the coefficients give a relative plane cubic. The discriminant is required to be a unit at every point. The resulting parameter space $M$ is written as a localization of a polynomial ring in six variables. A chosen « base spéciale » is part of the structure that $M$ represents.

**U9 · Scans 12–13.** Upper triangular changes of adapted basis give the substitutions for $X$ and $Y$ and an action on the coefficients. The author uses $G(S)$-orbits to compare the underlying curves, then passes to the bundle of adapted bases to obtain a local presentation for a curve without a chosen basis. He calls the resulting functor $\mathcal D$-representable and specifies the class of coverings in his own terminology.

**U10 · Scans 14–15.** A nonvanishing invariant derivation fixes generators of the second and third graded lines. The remaining diagonal entries have weights two and three, and the normalized equation has leading part $x^2-y^3$. The author considers setting the discriminant equal to 1 after a finite flat extension. He leaves the power required for this operation in question.

**U11 · Scans 16–17.** Scan 16 applies $x\mapsto-x$, $y\mapsto y$ to the equation. It then describes the quotient by symmetry, the section at infinity, and a residual branch divisor of relative degree three. Scan 17 studies the action of symmetry on the filtration, assumes residue characteristics different from two in the live argument, and chooses a complementary line $L_3$. Two matrices record the residual changes of basis. A marginal proposal uses the differential of the map to $P(\mathcal E_2)$.

**U12 · Scan 19, upper half.** Two local cases distinguish whether $j$ or $j-1$ is a unit. Two correspondences pair Kummer extensions with the special values 1 and 0. The right-hand text gives candidate invariants modulo cubes or squares under conditions on $b$, $c$ and nilpotents. The margin calls for geometric verification.

**U13 · Scan 19, lower half.** The sheet tests square, cube and sixth roots of expressions in $t$. Two reciprocal power conditions carry the comment « marche pas » and a ditto mark. A separate calculation passes from a fractional expression in $b,c$ to a boxed quotient and a cubic. At the foot a Hom pairing points upward to an underlined Hom. The sheet gives no sentence connecting this last diagram to the cubic.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence · Scans 14 and 17.** The two diagonal weights in scan 14 explain both polynomial relations on the matrices. Substituting $q^2,q^3$ gives zero in $\mu^2-\lambda^3$ and in $u^3-v^2$ with the variable order of scan 17. The calculation uses only the displayed weights.
- **R2 · Model, high confidence · Scans 16–17.** Comparing the two live right-hand sides of scan 16 gives $2a=2c=0$. The conclusion $a=c=0$ follows when multiplication by 2 is injective. In characteristic two the sign comparison by itself gives no condition on $a,c$. Scan 17 supplies a residue-characteristic hypothesis for its own live argument.
- **R3 · Model, high confidence · Scan 19.** The unmarked identity $27c^2(j-1)=b^3 j$ gives the boxed ratio $b^3/c^2=27(j-1)/j$ when $j$ and $c$ are units. This check begins with the unmarked cross-multiplied line and so does not depend on the doubtful exponent in the earlier numerator.
- **R4 · Model, low confidence · Scan 19.** An unboxed $b^3/c^2=j$ occurs beside this calculation. The two proposed right sides differ as rational functions, for example at $j=2$ over the rationals. The layout gives no instruction to impose the two equations together, so the discrepancy is left as a question about the trial calculation. In the lower boxed cubic the frozen numerator begins with the marked reading $j$ or $t$. Its short crossed upright lacks the deep loop of the denominator. The calculation cannot settle that letter.
- **R5 · Model, high confidence · Scans 7–8.** In a local parameter the coefficient of the leading derivative of $t^{-n}$ is $-n$. This accounts for the divisibility test at the join of the sheets. Testing $n=2,3$ in characteristic two and $n=3,4$ in characteristic three gives the two behaviours in the text.
- **R6 · Model, low confidence · Scan 16.** The marked exponent in the running $P(y)$ remains $2$ or $1$. The separate boxed equation favours $2$, but the raised stroke in the running line has no secure foot. The two occurrences should remain distinguishable in a citation.
- **R7 · Model, low confidence · Scan 16.** Reading question at the verb before $sx=-x$. The frozen text has « se rappellant ». The alternative « se rappelant » would take the thin central upstroke as a joining stroke. The crop separates the descending p strokes more readily than the two possible l strokes. No spelling correction is attributed here.
