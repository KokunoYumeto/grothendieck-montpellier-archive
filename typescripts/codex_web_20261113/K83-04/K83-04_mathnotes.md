# Mathematical notes · Cote 83, scans 96–100 and 102–112

## What the pages do

**Scan 96.** Grothendieck considers two quadratic algebras whose discriminants vanish. He asks when they become \(k[T]/T^2\) locally. The calculation puts a unit \(\beta\) into the equation \(\beta(2\alpha+b)=0\). In characteristic two this forces \(b=0\), whereas \(b^2=0\) need not. A cancelled passage at the foot turns toward trace-zero, determinant-zero elements and their normalisers.

**Scans 97–98.** The rank-two algebras are written \(k.1+D_1\) and \(k.1+D_2\), with the two lines in the trace-zero space. The product trace \(t\) enters a Borel normaliser calculation. When the four quadratic parameters vanish, the stated criterion reduces to invertibility of \(t\). The example uses opposite matrix units, with one multiplied by \(t\). The diagonal note then asks how far the criterion extends.

**Scans 99–100.** A theorem characterises the Azumaya condition by nondegeneracy of the determinant on the span of \(1,u,v\). It gives the signed polynomial \(\Delta=-(t^2-bb't+b^2c'+b'^2c-4cc')\) and a version for two transverse rank-two subalgebras. The proof reduces to an algebraically closed field, distinguishes the étale and unipotent cases and gives a matrix calculation in the mixed case. The sufficiency paragraph ends at dots.

**Scans 102–103.** The April heading starts a discussion of a rank-three subbundle \(B\) of a rank-four Azumaya algebra and its orthogonal line \(D\). Two symmetric forms are written down, \(\varphi(u,v)=\operatorname{Tr}(uv)\) and \(\psi(u,v)=\operatorname{Tr}(u)\operatorname{Tr}(v)-\operatorname{Tr}(uv)\). Both pair the unit with \(u\) by its trace. On the trace-zero part the displayed pairings have opposite signs. This explains the equality of the two orthogonal complements for submodules containing the unit.

**Scan 104.** The varying discriminant \(\delta_B\) is treated as a section of the degree-two twist on the projective plane parametrising the hyperplanes in question. Its space of sections is identified with quadratic forms on the dual of \(A/k\). The determinant on the trace-zero part gives the proposed canonical section. The closing parenthesis singles out the quadratic form and the unit as the data used here.

**Scans 105–106.** Multiplication modulo \(B\) gives a bilinear map \(B\times B\longrightarrow A/B\). The quadratic identity for each \(u\) makes this map alternating when \(1\in B\). It descends through \(B/k\), giving \(\mu\) on its second exterior power. The determinant-line identifications put \(\mu\) and the discriminant in comparable line bundles. The sheet states that \(B\) is closed under multiplication exactly when \(\mu=0\), then compares \(\mu\) with \(\pm\delta\).

**Scans 107–109.** A proposition on scan 109 relates four conditions involving an isotropic orthogonal line, the divided discriminant, closure of \(B\) under multiplication and the Borel property. Two more conditions involve \(D\subset B\) and the ordinary discriminant. Scan 108 explains the coefficient 2 and some implications. Scan 107 discusses normalisers and records doubt about the converse over a nonreduced ring. The physical sequence is important here. For example, the end of 108, “à … une telle D”, leads into “correspondant” on 107, and the unfinished sentence on 106 leads into the displayed \(\mu\) on 105.

**Scan 110.** The corollary gives the complementary fibrewise conditions involving anisotropy, an invertible discriminant and generation of \(A\) as an algebra. With \(1,u,v\) a basis of \(B\), the quotient class of \(uv\) tests whether \(1,u,v,uv\) span \(A\). A new paragraph introduces two rank-two subbundles \(H,H'\) containing 1 and meeting in \(k\).

**Scans 111–112.** The orthogonal planes of \(H,H'\) meet in the line \(D\). Their projective lines cut the conic of isotropic lines. General position of the pair is described in terms of disjoint pairs of points in each geometric fibre. Ordering the two points in each pair produces an anharmonic ratio \(\lambda\). The marginal condition makes both \(\lambda\) and \(1-\lambda\) units. Interchanging the two entries of either pair inverts the ratio. The last sentence continues beyond the supplied scans.

## Remarks of the model

- **M96-dual · High confidence.** The ring \(\mathbf F_2[e]/(e^2)\) gives a concrete instance of the obstruction on 96. Its two units are \(1\) and \(1+e\), and both send the nonzero element \(e\) to \(e\) under multiplication.
- **M98-matrix · High confidence.** In the displayed example \(u=E_{12}\), \(v=tE_{21}\), the trace of \(uv\) is \(t\). In row-major matrix coordinates the four columns \(1,u,v,uv\) have determinant \(-t^2\). This checks generation in that split model.
- **M99-delta · High confidence for the polynomial identity.** Taking \(b,b'\) as the two traces, \(c,c'\) as the two determinants and \(t=\operatorname{Tr}(uv)\), the ternary polar matrix has diagonal \(2,2c,2c'\) and upper off-diagonal entries \(b,b',bb'-t\). Its determinant is \(2\Delta\), with the exterior minus printed on 99. This check is conditional on these parameter identifications.
- **R102-rank · High confidence for the examples.** Over \(\mathbf Q\), put \(h=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\). On \(\langle I,h\rangle\) the two Gram determinants are \(4\) and \(-4\). On \(\langle I,h,E_{12}+E_{21}\rangle\) they are both \(8\). This illustrates the difference between the arbitrary-rank wording and the rank-three application.
- **M111-conic · High confidence for the calculation.** In the split algebra over \(\mathbf Q\), the orthogonal plane to the diagonal algebra consists of off-diagonal matrices and its determinant is \(-sr\). For \(\langle I,E_{12}\rangle\), the determinant on the orthogonal plane is \(-s^2\). The two calculations distinguish a secant from a tangent independently of the doubtful word on 111.
- **Reading question · Scan 99, display for \(\Delta\).** The frozen fourth term is \(+b'^2c\). The last little character also resembles a lowered 2. Its position is below the superscript, and its curve alone does not settle \(c\) versus \(2\). The polynomial check concerns the stated reading. It does not authenticate the glyph.
