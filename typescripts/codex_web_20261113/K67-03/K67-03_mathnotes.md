# Mathematical notes on cote 67, scans 36–46 and 48–51

## What the pages do

**36, antipodism and a circle.** For $\sigma'z=-b/\bar z$, the two poles are interchanged. The calculation multiplies the two images appearing in $z\bar z=b$ to show why the circle is preserved. The text concludes that the data specify the same spherical Riemannian structure.

**36, a noncentral extension [W01].** The second part writes $\sigma z=a\bar z$ for an element outside $T$ and computes $\sigma^2 z=a\bar a z$. After imposing $a\bar a=1$ it calls the extension split. It then varies $\sigma$ by a rotation and identifies a fixed real line $\alpha\mathbb R$. The last displayed chain has a difficulty described in S1. The long margin distinguishes the information specifying $N$ from that needed to choose a spherical metric.

**37, invariant metrics on a sphere.** The proposition starts with a compact subgroup $H$ of the conformal automorphism group. Its uniqueness criterion is $X^H=\varnothing$. For a nontrivial group with fixed points, it distinguishes a two-point fixed set from a group generated by an inversion. It gives orbit descriptions of the metrics in both cases and places a verification note beside the first description.

**38, metrics on a surface without boundary.** The recapitulation proposes a canonical compatible metric $Q_X$, with the complex affine line and the conformal sphere excepted. On the line it retains a flat metric up to scale. On the sphere it specifies the set $R(X)$ by the normalization $\int_X dx=1$, then gives conditions on $H$ for a metric to be $H$-invariant and unique.

**39, curvature and the distinguished orbit.** The page associates negative curvature $-1$ with the hyperbolic case, zero curvature with complex-plane universal cover, and positive curvature with the real projective plane. The opening aside applies the sphere discussion to automorphisms of a map. The long margin describes an orbit inside the space of compatible Riemannian metrics, including a ray and a three-dimensional family in the exceptional cases.

**40, hyperbolicity with boundary.** The two assignments in the diagram pass between $X$ and a holomorphic surface $X^{\mathrm{or}}$ with an anti-involution. Hyperbolicity is defined through the double. A compactness argument uses $\chi(X^{\mathrm{or}})=2\chi(X)$, giving a criterion in terms of the sign of $\chi(X)$. The last paragraph separately discusses hyperbolicity of $\mathrm{Int}\,X$.

**41–42, descent of a metric.** The hyperbolic metric on the double descends with geodesic boundary. The text broadens the invariance assumption, considers the real projective plane, and continues across the sheet break to the case of the complex affine line with a disconnected double. In that case it calls for a distinguished metric stable under $\sigma$.

**42–43, the affine exceptional case.** After moving a fixed point to zero, the author writes an affine anti-involution as multiplication of $\bar z$ by a unit $a$. With $a=\alpha^2$ he describes the fixed line. The quotient is a half-plane with boundary. Scan 43 gives the metric up to a scalar, writes the closed upper half-plane as a model and identifies its automorphism group with the real affine group.

**44, the projective exceptional case.** An inversion on the complex projective line supplies a real projective line and gives a disk as quotient. The author asks for an interior point to select a distinguished metric. He then lists the four exceptions together, assigning three real parameters to the spherical family and two to the disk family.

**45, open inclusions and hyperbolicity.** Corollaire 3 gives exceptions to hyperbolicity for a connected open $X\subset X'$. The first row gives three bounds on a set difference; see S4 for its written order. The second treats a compact genus-one $X'$ and $X=X'$. The remark formulates hyperbolicity through the universal cover, conformal maps from $\mathbb C$ and the orientation cover. The list of quotients by fixed-point-free anti-involutions breaks off after “On trouve donc en plus”.

**46, compact surfaces and genus one.** The opening line states uniqueness for a compatible metric of constant curvature $-1$. In case 2), the oriented genus-one surface has a complex affine line as universal cover. The author describes its translation-invariant Hermitian metric up to scale and its descent. The last words are “La constante multiplicative est”.

**48, a Teichmüller isomorphism and fundamental groups.** A smooth $X$ and stable nonsmooth $Y$ with a normal propagator $N$ are related first through exterior groups, then through groups with selected base points. The distinction is emphasized by “pas ext. !”. The text says the outer isomorphism is incompatible with the stated outer Galois actions. Its closing instruction concerns conjugacy classes of homomorphisms from $\widehat{\mathbb Z}(1)$.

**49, three problems in anabelian geometry.** The first proposes an extension to singular stable curves with propagator. The second asks how the singular and marked points could be recovered from conjugacy classes of procyclic subgroups and the action of $\pi_1(K)$. The third asks for an anabelian compactification and its behaviour under specialization.

**50, presentations of groups and groupoids.** Three approaches use pants or triads, the strict automorphisms and deformation paths of pieces of Légo, and involutions. Examples involving $\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb Z)$ and $\mathrm{Gl}(2,\mathbb Z)$ accompany the last two approaches. Earlier exponents remain crossed out in the relations.

**51, group diagrams and drawings.** Two aligned rows compare groups with indices $0,4$ and $0,3$ and signs indexed by 4 and 3. Other lines contain matrix groups modulo scalars or small integers, a quotient labelled “Borel”, products of loops and a sequence with $g,\nu-1$ and $g,\nu$. A vocabulary table distinguishes groupoids, groups, multiplicities and space. The marked spheres, smaller pen and pencil figures, and printer bulletin occupy the same sheet. The sheet supplies no prose identifying the separate diagrams as steps in one proof.

## Slips

**S1 — 36, final display, first fraction. Medium.** The page has $z=\alpha\bar z/\bar z$. The preceding expression for $a$ suggests $z=\alpha\bar z/\bar\alpha$ (refinement S1r). The two barred letters in the actual fraction both look like z. For $\alpha=i$, the nonzero point $z=2i$ is fixed, whereas the written right-hand side is $i$. This observation concerns the fraction only. The subsequent denominator is itself marked $a$ or $\alpha$.

**S2 — 37, last quotient. Medium.** The right-hand member has $x^\sigma$ where $X^\sigma$ appears intended. The small crossing is visibly lower than either X on its left. In the sentence $X$ is the sphere, and the preceding quotient uses $X^H$.

**S3 — 38, last main line. Medium.** The reference to the previous proposition uses $R(x)^H$. The symbol defined several lines earlier is $R(X)$. The small x is preserved.

**S4 — 45, exception a). High.** The hypothesis is $X\subset X'$ and the expression in the bound is $\mathrm{Card}(X\setminus X')$. The likely intended quantity is $\mathrm{Card}(X'\setminus X)$, which counts points omitted from the open subset. The written difference is empty for every such inclusion. The prime on the second X is visible both in the hypothesis and in the difference.
