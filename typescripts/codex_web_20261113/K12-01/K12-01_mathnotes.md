# K12-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 2, §§1–3.** The “Esquisse” begins with the cohomology theories of smooth projective schemes. It introduces effective motives over a field, their tensor product and their grading. It lists de Rham, Hodge, Betti and $\ell$-adic functors, with conditions on the base or on $\ell$. It ascribes multiplicativity and faithfulness to the functors.

**Scan 2, §4.** The degree-one equivalence is described through the Picard object of $X$ and $h^1$ of the dual of an abelian variety. The following notes compare motives with abelian varieties and raise the question of describing smooth motives over a normal base.

**Scan 2, §5.** The passage from $M^+(k)$ to $M(k)$ introduces internal Hom and an invertible Tate object. The proposed universal property asks a contravariant functor on $V(k)$ to satisfy Künneth and send the projective-line summand cut out by $\pi$ to an invertible object. The sign in the opening “anti-Tate” heading is uncertain. The subsequent projector formula explicitly uses $Q(-1)$.

**Scans 3–4, §6.** Level at most $i$ means that, up to a Tate twist, only effective components of degrees at most $i$ are needed. Purity of level excludes nonzero submotives of smaller level. The text gives a decomposition into pure levels, relates the parity of the level to the weight, and uses a linear section to bound the dimension of varieties needed.

**Scans 3–4, end of §6.** Kernels of restriction to open subsets define a geometric filtration on $\ell$-adic cohomology. The manuscript identifies a filtration piece with the image of the largest submotive of level at most $i-2j$. It specifies a resolution hypothesis, then invokes the standard conjectures for independence of the dimensions from $\ell$.

**Scans 4–5, §7.** The tensor categories have internal Hom. The word “spéciale” is defined by the map from $\check M\otimes N$ to internal Hom. For a category with fibre functor $F$, the notes give a representation-category description, identify $G(A)$ with automorphisms of the scalar-extended functor and relate finite type and, in characteristic zero, semisimplicity to properties of $G$.

**Scans 5–6, end of §7.** A second fibre functor is expressed by twisting the first by a pro-torsor. The new group is the adjoint twist. The margin asks for a finite-extension comparison. The next sheet considers the absence of a fibre functor over the original field and proposes a gerbe description under an existence and a uniqueness assumption.

**Scans 6–7, §8.** For a regular base the proposed definition uses the generic fibre. It leads to a fully faithful functor to motives at the generic point and to a pullback along morphisms of regular schemes. A complex point supplies a Betti fibre functor. For positive characteristic the writer contrasts the problem of rational coefficients with the availability of an $\ell$-adic fibre functor away from $\ell$.

**Scans 7–8, §9.** Association with a geometric point includes a chosen isomorphism on $M^{+0}(S)$. The examples distinguish Betti, $\ell$-adic, de Rham and Hodge functors and discuss a countable algebraically closed field. A first formulation relating component groups to the fundamental group is cancelled at the foot of scan 8.

**Scans 9–10, continuation.** The live argument starts with admissible finite-rank $\pi_1$-modules and produces an epimorphism from the inverse limit of the component groups to $\pi_1(S,\xi)$. To make it an isomorphism the writer invokes Tate, or Hodge in generic characteristic zero. He then studies a connected étale cover and formulates a connectedness criterion over an algebraically closed field.

**Scans 10 and 12, §10.** With $K$ embedded in $Q_\ell$, an isomorphism between the scalar extension of $F$ and the Tate functor defines compatibility. It gives $\rho_\ell$ from the fundamental group to $G(Q_\ell)$. Scan 12 gives its left inverse, draws the square with the component-group quotient, and states a version of Tate in terms of the algebraic envelope of the image. Scan 11 is the intervening printed letterhead.

**Scans 12–13, finite fields.** The canonical Frobenius automorphism of the identity functor gives a central element. With compatible $\ell$-adic realisation it agrees with the image of the Frobenius generator. Over a finite field the writer formulates Tate in terms of the group generated by this element, then deduces commutativity and describes an extension by a torus or protorus.

**Scans 13–14, closed points.** For a closed point with finite residue field the construction produces an element of $I_G(Q)$. The scheme of invariants is compared with $T/W$ in a connected case, and the elements form a family indexed by such points. The manuscript mentions compatibility with the two maps involving the fundamental group.

**Scans 15–16, §12a–b.** Tensor-compatible gradings are described by maps from a diagonalizable group. The objectwise grading gives a central weight cocharacter. The Hodge bigrading gives two commuting cocharacters with product the weight cocharacter, and the Betti comparison gives a conjugation relation. The rank-one realisation of $Q(-1)$ gives a character whose composite with the weight cocharacter is squaring.

**Scans 16–17, §12c.** The associated graded of a filtered fibre functor is another fibre functor. Assuming a filtered isomorphism after extension that induces the identity on associated gradeds, the writer describes subtorsors, their unipotent groups, and recovery of the original functor. He asks for an explicit construction of $H(\alpha)$.

**Scan 17, §13.** The Mumford–Tate heading introduces polarisable Hodge structures, their semisimple tensor category and algebraic groups over $Q$ for finite-type subcategories. A footnote records two cocharacters, their central product and conjugation. Only part of the following line is visible.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model · high confidence · scans 10 and 12.** Once the boxed identity $\eta_\ell\rho_\ell=\mathrm{id}$ is assumed, $\rho_\ell$ is injective on the displayed groups. Applying the displayed left inverse to an equality of two images recovers the two arguments. This checks the implication only.

**R2 · Model · high confidence · scans 15–16.** The exponent in the boxed character identity is consistent with the weight assigned to the Tate line. The weight cocharacter acts on that line by $\lambda^2$, and its scalar action is exactly what the character of the line records. The check uses the written weight 2 and the boxed formula.

**R3 · Model · medium confidence · scans 8–9.** The revision appears to change the order of the argument. The first version writes an isomorphism inside a crossed-out block. The next page obtains an epimorphism first and introduces Tate or Hodge in discussing bijectivity. The physical cancellation and the later order are clear. The writer's reason for revising is inferred.
