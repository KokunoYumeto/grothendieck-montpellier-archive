# What the pages do

**Scans 178 and 180 — classification sketches.** Several branching diagrams arrange multiplicative, unipotent and discrete pieces using the labels G_m, G_a, μ_p, α_p, V, Z/pZ, Z/ellZ and Z. Additional curves and annotations distinguish connected, separable, infinitesimal and torsion conditions. Scan 180 discusses G_0 and G_réd and qualifies some assertions by hypotheses on the field. These are working sketches with cancellations and uncertain labels, not a finished classification theorem.

**Scans 181–182 — categories and a proposed gluing construction.** A ring defined using a leftward limit and the relations V^n and FV−p supports a category C_1 of discrete modules; C_2 is introduced as its opposite. The notes single out subcategories, invoke Cartier duality on finite groups, and propose a new category of triples (X,Y,u). The next page expresses compatibility by an H^0/D square and enlarges the ring to Z×A to include further groups. The final phrase about duality continues into scan 183.

**Scan 183 — extending the construction.** Passing to ind-objects and pro-objects removes the bounded-power conditions that had supported the earlier discussion. The page asks how to define the gluing and how to interpret modules over the displayed W-ring. It tentatively describes an endomorphism ring in terms of W_infinity, D(W_infinity), nilpotent Witt components and a fundamental pairing. Several crucial words and the first F exponent remain doubtful.

**Scan 184 — a cancelled ring-and-group comparison.** Exact-sequence-like rows and a classification diagram are crossed out, while other formulas and headings survive. The lower part juxtaposes the headings “Anneau de Cartier” and “Anneau de Gabriel-Dieudonné” and describes a completion for the ideal (F,V), with “maximal” inserted. The cancellations prevent treating every formula on this sheet as an endorsed final assertion.

**Scans 185–186 — affine groups, coalgebras and finite pieces.** The landscape sheet begins with tentative Hom and dual-W formulas, then compares affine algebraic groups with algebras equipped with Δ and additional properties. It asks whether the algebra is a union of finite-type pieces stable under Δ. Scan 186 compares ind-finite algebraic objects with coalgebras, adds a compatible algebra law for groups, and proposes a duality between pro-affine algebraic groups and ind-finite groups. Its finite-generation condition and hoped-for final correspondence are explicitly tentative.

**Scan 187 — topological hyperalgebras and F,V.** The page seeks an interpretation of certain groups by topological hyperalgebras with linearly compact underlying spaces. It describes Cartier duality through pairings into G_m and calls the definitions of F and V immediate in characteristic p. The lower comparison contains calculations for G_m, Z/ell and μ_p, but sweeping cancellations and a separately erased exponent must be retained when interpreting them.

**Scans 188–189 — characters and the dual object.** A homomorphism to G_m is expressed through an element f satisfying Δf=f⊗f and the counit condition. Assuming A free, the notes reinterpret these conditions using the continuous dual check A=Hom(A,k). They then isolate a missing topological/coalgebra hypothesis before describing a dual ind-affine group D(G). Five examples follow. The last, involving D(G_a) and an enveloping algebra, has a particularly uncertain inserted qualification about powers.

**Scans 190–191 — a pro-object versus its actual limit.** A pro-object is distinguished from a limit object that may exist in C. The notes compare the functors determined by these objects, give a set-theoretic example of lost information, and discuss a universal map from the limit to the pro-object and an adjoint “projection.” They then consider embedding C in a larger C′, where projective systems acquire limits, and ask when the induced functor is fully faithful.

**Scans 191–192 — approximation of affine groups.** The affine-scheme example leads to the question whether an affine group scheme over S comes from affine groups of finite type. In algebraic language, scan 192 gives a sufficient criterion involving finite-type submodules V, ΔV inside the image of V⊗V, and injectivity for A_V⊗A_V→A⊗A (the source draws a dash in that last expression). It asserts a field case and suggests a possible extension under weaker hypotheses such as A free.

**Scan 193 — elementary pieces and an extension category.** Two lists of elementary types are related through V=D(G_a). The author proposes putting the pieces together by extensions in a “présumée abélienne” category of groups on ind-affine schemes. Three slanted notes broaden or qualify this idea. The final sketch separates multiplicative, unipotent and discrete portions and appends torsion and π-action annotations. The compressed parenthesis about the affine algebra and parts of the margins are too uncertain for a precise structural conclusion.

If the memberships and diagram are retained, β(X) and α(Y) would be the domain-compatible forms. Interchanging the memberships is another possible repair. The intended correction is not unique; the transcription changes none of these symbols.

# Remarks of the model

**Model remark — high confidence.** The finite-type approximation question recurs on 185 and 191–192. This is a textual connection: both passages explicitly ask about expressing an affine group or its algebra through finite-type pieces, and both invoke compatibility with Δ. It does not prove either assertion.

**Model remark — medium confidence.** The distinction between a pro-object and its actual limit on 190–191 supplies a useful caution for reading the earlier proposed pro/ind dualities: replacing a system by a limit can lose information, according to the author's own set example. The pages do not explicitly declare this to be the reason for the earlier difficulties.

**Model remark — medium confidence.** The words “Je pense,” “sans doute superflue,” “Il n'est pas exclu,” and “présumée abélienne,” together with the cancellations, place several constructions at a tentative stage. This supports treating the sheets as exploratory notes, not as one uniformly asserted theorem. The reading of “Je pense” itself remains doubtful.

**Model remark — low confidence.** The last qualification in the D(G_a) example and the lower algebra description on 193 may materially affect the intended objects. Their marked readings are too weak to select a familiar construction or claim an error. The notes therefore leave those interpretations open.

There are no additional post-freeze reading alternatives beyond the questions and existing doubts identified above; the final hash comparison is recorded in progress.log.
