# Mathematical notes on scans 117–132

## What the pages do

**The end of 6.1, scan 117.** Grothendieck writes a morphism \(f\) as \(pi\), with \(i\) in \(TC(I)\) and \(p\) a left inverse to another such morphism \(j\). He applies \(u_!\) and concludes \(u_!(f)\in W(I')\). For part d) he turns to the inclusion \(C(I)\subset C_I\).

**Corollary 6.2, scans 117–119.** For closed parts \(U\subset V\), (6.2.1) compares the transition maps for \(X\) and \(Y\). The central square is cocartesian, and the claim concerns the remaining map to \(Y_V\), in \(C\) or \(TC\). Admitting a restriction property allows him to take \(V=I\). A functor to \(\Delta^1\) then puts this diagram in the form (6.2.3).

**The arrow-category criterion, scans 119–121.** Lemma 6.2.4 isolates the map marked \(\widetilde\varphi\). Lemma 6.2.5 gives a version for classes \(\Phi,\Psi\), using a pointwise condition and the star operation. The three maps in (6.2.5.5) are \(u(0),u(1),\widetilde u\). The note immediately after the display explains why its proof is absent.

**Corollary 6.3 and a maximal partial lift, scans 121–123.** The corollary compares \(\Phi_I\) and \(\Phi(I)\) and asserts equality under the stated order condition. For the converse inclusion he passes to dual notation and orders pairs \((U,h_U)\) by extension. He takes a maximal pair by Zorn and, if its open part is proper, chooses a minimal \(i_0\) in the complement. Extending over this one object becomes the remaining task.

**The extension square, scans 123–126.** Once the index has a final object, he describes the data by a restriction, a value at that object and a map from a colimit. The three squares (6.3.3)–(6.3.5) and the four \(\lambda\) maps make the cube (6.3.6). Its extension amounts to finding \(h_0:B_0\to X_0\) satisfying (6.3.7) and (6.3.8). The pushout \(\widetilde B\) in (6.3.9) supplies a new map \(\widetilde h_0\).

**The lift and the close of the proof, scans 127–129.** The calculations with \(r,s\) organize the faces of the enlarged diagram. The conditions on \(h_0\) become the two triangles of (6.3.14). The author invokes \(\widetilde i\in\Phi\) and \(p_0\in\Psi\) to obtain the lift and closes both 6.3 and 6.1 d) with « On est heureux ! ».

**Proposition 6.4 and corollary 6.5, scans 130–131.** Proposition 6.4 relates the classes on two diagram categories by \(u_!,u^*,u_*\). It gives three cases for the equivalent conditions in part b). In the third case the author evaluates \(u_*(X)(i')\) by a leftward limit, then writes it as a product of \(X(e_\alpha)\) over connected components. Closure under products gives the desired membership. Corollary 6.5 states the resulting pairs of conditions for a Quillen triple.

**Chapter XVIII cover, scan 132.** The topic line includes accessible categories and cardinal filtrations. The cover also indexes constructions by section and page range. The two references at the bottom share a brace.

## Remarks of the model

**M1. High confidence.** On scans 125–126 the stroke pattern distinguishes stages of the construction. The diagonal \(h'_{I'}\) already exists, while the dotted \(h_0\) is the map being sought. The author states that distinction in words at the top of scan 126. It helps identify the diagonals when several meet at \(X_0\).

**M2. High confidence.** The two columns on scan 129 correspond to testing the first equation of (6.3.15) on \(r\) and on \(s\). Their bottom lines recover the two conditions singled out in (6.3.16). The identifications of \(\widetilde i r\) and \(\widetilde i s\) are written explicitly on scan 127. The precise height of the last two prime strokes in the right column remains a reading question.
