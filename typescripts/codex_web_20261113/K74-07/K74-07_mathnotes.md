# Mathematical notes on K74-07

Scan numbers refer to the archive PDF. L numbers refer to the individual transcription files. Short identifiers link checked statements to CHECK_EVENTS.tsv. The descriptions of the manuscript report its claims. The separate computations below identify the assumptions actually tested.

## What the pages do

**121 — numerical table.** Four columns, headed by $c=0,1,2,4$, compare graph sizes, degrees, edges, vertical and transverse triangles, and vertices unlinked to a given vertex. The lower rows count embedded figures of three types. Orders of automorphism groups sit between the two parts. A further four-row block has a compressed qualifier about the preceding figures.

**122 — extension diagrams and permutations.** The initial diagram connects $V$, $\Gamma$, $\widetilde\Gamma$, $\{\pm1\}$ and indexed $E$’s. Subsequent diagrams compare the first two $E$’s with the third. A small triangle carries three $\tau$’s. The sheet ends with permutation and conjugation expressions in $\sigma$ and $\pi$, several of them unfinished.

**123 — arithmetic on a printed form.** The sideways working compares products involving powers of 2 and 3, then sets out a multiplication ending in “51.840”. The examination form has no mathematical role in the calculation.

**124 — graphs and inclusions.** A list beginning with the empty graph associates graph types with successive values of $c$. One table gives categories for a graph with a specified substructure. A second table considers longer chains of inclusions.

**125–126 — local permutations.** Three involutions are described through their effects on primed and unprimed vertices. The products of pairs are compared with $\pi$ and its inverse. Orbits, three-element sets and torsors appear beside the drawing of three ovals. On 126, lists of vertex pairs and two geometric sketches support trial restrictions of products of $\sigma$’s. The boxed identity has an explicit restriction excluding two vertices.

**128 — construction at a marked vertex.** With $a$ fixed, $I$ indexes triangles at $a$. Exchanging the two other vertices of such a triangle gives an involution. Their pairs become two-element torsors, and their product is denoted $E$. For $x$ outside $X$ and $\{a\}$, the incidence prescription gives coordinates $\varphi_i(x)$ and hence a map $\varphi:Y\longrightarrow E$.

**128–130 — the action and its two cases.** The diagonal and the augmentation for the power of the two-element field enter the endomorphism $u(x)=x+\delta\eta(x)$. The possible nonzero element of its kernel is the diagonal $z$. The notes distinguish $\nu=0$ from $\nu=1,2$ by the parity of $|I|=1+2^\nu$. Scan 130 returns to the size of the image of $Y$ in the first case. Its final sentence in the second case is cancelled.

**131–132 — incidence axioms and duality.** Triangles are three-element subsets of $X$. Conditions a)–c) refer to intersections, determination by edges, and incidence with a vertex. The inserted d) prescribes three triangles at each vertex. The relation $R$ then interchanges vertices and triangles, giving two assignments to sets of three elements. Scan 132 tests the axioms after this exchange.

**133 — the cube.** A labelled cube places pairs of indices on $\lambda$’s. An adjacent cube distinguishes open and filled vertices. The sheet relates a cube to a marked configuration and writes a comparison between the corresponding groups. The short name following the two-way arrow is read “cour”.

**134 — diagrams of types $A_2$ through $A_5$.** Four braced descriptions relate the diagrams to marked triangles, an involution on eight elements, cubes, a doublet and demi-étoile, bibases, pavés, hexagons and triads. The categories written to the right or below include products of categories of finite sets. The first description names a Weyl group.

**135 — triangular prisms and two squares.** Grothendieck follows two squares sharing vertex data through the choice of a third vertex, a triad and a configuration of type $A_5$. A half-product counts choices. The last display places three two-element factors beside a torsor category; the final underbrace label ends with “en”.

**136 — prisms and squares with a fixed centre.** A picture of three adjacent squares introduces an obstruction, followed by a statement about pentagonal and hexagonal bases. For a fixed centre $a$, a five-element $I$ and an affine torsor $T$ describe the unlinked points. A square is specified by a two-dimensional translation space generated by a chosen pair of $e_i$’s and a point of its quotient torsor. The sheet ends with a description by triples and an equivalence of categories.

## Remarks of the model

- **R123 — model, high confidence for the endpoints.** The factors $2^7\cdot3^4\cdot5$ on the exam form give 51,840, matching both its final “51.840” and the last group order on 121. This check leaves two handwritten intermediate figures open.
- **R129 — model, high confidence, conditional on the displayed definition.** Using $u(x)=x+\delta\eta(x)$ and $|I|=1+2^\nu$ from 129 L24,30 (P129), enumeration of the binary vectors for $|I|=2,3,5$ gives kernel sizes $1,2,2$. In the last two cases the image equals the kernel of the coordinate-sum map.
- **R133 — model, high confidence for the order calculation, low for the reading of the capital.** Interpreting the curled capital at 133 L44 as a symmetric group (P133) gives $3!\,2^3=48$ on the left and $2!\,4!=48$ on the right. Group order alone leaves the isomorphism open.
- **R135 — model, high confidence for the arithmetic, low for the overwritten exponent.** The half-product on 135 is 6480, also the value of $51840/2^3$ using the order on 121 L15 (P135). The clear numerical factors therefore agree across the two sheets.
- **R136 — model, high confidence, conditional on the parametrization.** The description by a pair of indices and a coset at 136 L16 (P136) would give 40 squares centred at the specified point. There are ten pairs in a five-element set and four cosets for each two-dimensional translation subspace of the four-dimensional space. The manuscript does not state this total here.
- **Q122 — reading question, low confidence.** At 122 L46 the frozen text reads $2^4/2=2^2$. The diagonal and the low hook read as “/2” could instead form a single $h$, giving $2^4h=2^2$.
- **Q126 — reading question, medium confidence.** The frozen live occurrence at 126 L9 reads $E_a$. A detached wave above this E on the top crop favours $\widetilde E_a$. It has the same position as the waves over the neighbouring $E_b$ and $E_c$. This spot had no doubt mark at the freeze.
- **Q135 — reading question, low confidence.** The second underbrace at 135 L54 ends “quasi-trilitaire en”. The last small group looks like “en”, but could also be an unfinished word. The related adjective at the heading of 131 also admits “pseudotrialitaires”. No further letters follow this particular label on 135.
