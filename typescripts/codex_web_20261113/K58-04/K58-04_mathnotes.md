# Mathematical notes on cote 58, scans 36–44 and 46–51

## What the pages do

**Open neighbourhoods and associated points (36–37).** Remarque 3 distinguishes the behaviour of $LG$ and its effective form under passage to an open neighbourhood of $Y$ from the behaviour of the indexed conditions. A locally free module on a neighbourhood need not come by restriction from $X$. A hypothesis that such modules are quotients of locally free modules on $X$ enters the proposed comparison of sections. The lemma on 37 excludes an associated point whose closure misses $Y$ under the hypothesis there. Its proof uses a nonzero section after twisting, whose restriction vanishes. The boxed conclusion places the several $LG$ conditions beside a comparison for large twists, with a straight leftward implication and additional curved arrows.

**Connected components and the fundamental group (38–39).** The first proposition on 38 passes from injectivity or bijectivity on global functions to surjectivity or bijectivity on connected components. The next proposition gives a surjection on $\pi_1$ under $LG$. Its strict version compares the fundamental group of $Y$ with the inverse limit of those of the neighbourhoods. An insertion specifies a geometric point $a$ of $Y$. In the display, the small marks after the commas in both groups are crossed out. A lemma lifts algebra structures on a locally free module from its completion. There is a cancelled clause about decompositions, a surviving connectedness assertion, and a marginal test for an algebra homomorphism. On 39, restriction of étale covers is fully faithful. Under the strict condition a cover of $Y$ extends over some neighbourhood $U$, uniquely up to unique isomorphism once $U$ is fixed.

**Picard groups and a Cartier divisor (40–41).** The proposition gives an injection from the direct limit of the Picard groups of open neighbourhoods into the Picard group of the formal completion. It also specifies injective transition maps. The comparison becomes an isomorphism under strict $LG$. For a Cartier divisor with ideal $J$, the corollary relates the inverse line bundle on $Y$ to $J/J^2$. Together with $LG$, vanishing of $H^1(Y,\Lambda^{-n})$ for every positive $n$ gives the stated injection from $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ into $\mathrm{Pic}(Y)$. Part (ii) adds vanishing of $H^2$ and strict $LG$. The lemma on 41 phrases the two vanishing assertions as full faithfulness and equivalence for invertible modules on the formal scheme restricted to $Y$.

**Permanence and existence (41–44).** A crossed-out statement on 41 concerns a projective flat morphism. The replacement theorem on 42 starts with a quasi-projective morphism. Its interlinear hypotheses concern a coherent $F'$ with proper support and, in the words of the page, « plat rel. $/Y$ ». Under $(LG)_n$ its cohomology comparison is an isomorphism for $i\leq n$ and a monomorphism for $i=n+1$. Parts (ii) and (iii) address full faithfulness for coherent modules and existence of an algebraic module on a neighbourhood. The corollaries on 43 apply the comparisons to projective flat schemes and to permanence of the conditions on the inverse-image pair. Their written order is Corollaire 2, then Corollaire 1. The remark on 44 singles out flatness and properness.

**Completion on a punctured spectrum (46).** The theorem starts with a noetherian local ring $A$, a quotient of a regular ring, complete for the $t$-adic topology. It removes the closed point from $X$ and $Y$. The family $\Phi$ consists of closed subsets of $X'$ missing $Y'$. Completion is stated to be exact with kernel the coherent modules supported in $\Phi$. The resulting functor from the quotient category is asserted to be fully faithful. The text also describes an equivalence with certain restrictions from $\widehat X$. The dimensions, signs and associated-point qualifications in the overlapping insertions are too uncertain to extract a precise further equivalence. The lower paragraph reduces full faithfulness to a comparison of Hom groups and then to one of sections.

**Dévissage and the diagram of sections (48).** The sequence involving $P',F',\widetilde F',Q'$ is accompanied by support bounds of dimension zero for $P'$ and one for $Q'$. The next sequence uses the kernel $K'$ of multiplication by $t^n$ and the quotient $G'$. Two rows compare sections of $K',F',G'$ and $H^1K'$ with their completed counterparts. The question mark belongs to the vertical comparison for $G'$. The last line has doubtful readings for both the subject and the assertion about a zero divisor.

**Modification near the closed set (50).** The sheet invokes a corollary to the affine comparison theorem and refers to conditions a), b), c′). The bracket following c′) concerns extension to a coherent module on $\widehat X$. The argument seeks an $F'$ of depth at least two at the indicated points. It considers an open neighbourhood $U$ of $Y'$, asserts that its complement consists of finitely many closed points, and examines a specialization from an associated point. The face ends at « serait de dim $1$, ».

**Fractional ideals and rank-one modules (49, 47).** The two faces of typescript n°338 read in this mathematical order, as their own pagination indicates. Page II −10− (scan 49) finishes the equivalence between projectivity and invertibility for the fractional ideal $J$, using a direct summand and a family with only finitely many nonzero terms. It deduces finite generation for rank-one projectives and defines the quotient of the invertible fractional ideals by the nonzero principal ideals. Page II −11− (scan 47) assigns a class to a rank-one projective module. It identifies isomorphism classes by equality of ideal classes and identifies tensor product with multiplication in the quotient.

**Exterior algebra and linear equations (51).** Typescript n°371 relates independence of vectors over a commutative field to their nonzero exterior product. It identifies matrix rank with the largest order of a nonzero minor. The next two corollaries characterize invertibility of a square matrix and existence of a nontrivial solution to a square homogeneous system. The latter requires a zero determinant. The sheet then adjoins the right-hand-side column to a matrix and breaks off at « on sait ».

- **40, 42–43.** « Introduire aussi les $Y_n$ » is a drafting instruction on 40. The margin of 42 singles out flatness of $G'$ in a Hom comparison and questions the need for flatness of $F'$ in words partly in doubt. - **44.** « est possible que la 'hypothèse de quasi-projectivité » and « sauf tout au plus dans cor. 2 (ii) » express the hope of dispensing with quasi-projectivity, with a possible exception for existence of an algebraic scheme giving the formal one.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model, high confidence, scans 47, 49 and 51.** Document numbers, typed page numbers and headings distinguish two reused typescripts. Their own pagination explains why scan 49 precedes scan 47 when following the ideal-class argument.
- **Model, high confidence, scans 39–41.** Three maps have distinct targets on these sheets. Scan 39 discusses the fundamental group of $X$, scan 40 first compares Picard groups with the completion, and the Cartier-divisor corollary then compares with $Y$. Their wording identifies the targets individually.
- **Model reading question, low confidence, scan 40, corollary (ii), 040-h.jpg.** The sealed text treats the $H^1$ hypothesis as live. A fine line extends right from the crossbar of $H$, across the parenthesis and $Y$, fading before the rest of the formula. An extended crossbar and a partial cancellation are both possible. The double underline under « et » and the separate $H^2$ hypothesis are clear.
- **Model reading question, low confidence, scan 43, last corollary.** The sealed reading offers both $(X,Y')$ and $(X',Y')$, in that order. The preceding morphism and the definition of $Y'$ as its inverse image suggest $X'$. The small stroke adjacent to $X$ does not decide whether a prime was written.
- **Model reading question, low confidence, scan 46, upper oblique insertion, item 9.** The sealed reading after « dim » offers $\ne 2$ and $\geq 2$. The crossed strokes beside the numeral could instead make a reference sign resembling “#2”. A similarly dark crossing occurs near the lower bracket. The surviving ink does not identify a reference or settle a dimension bound.
- **Model reading question, low confidence, scan 48, depth clause just below the first sequence.** The sealed reading places a hat on $F'$ in the local depth expression. The preceding sequence uses $\widetilde F'$. A tilde is another possible reading here, since the small upper stroke has a curved crest. No numerical bound follows the expression before « en tt pt fermé ».
- **Model reading question, low confidence, scan 48, last line.** The sealed alternatives are $G$ or $t$ and « est » or « n'est ». The preceding line calling $t$ regular on $G'$ makes the alternatives mathematically consequential. The shape of the initial letter and the brief stroke at the verb leave both questions open.
