# Mathematical notes on cote 9, scans 16–29

## What the pages do

**The question about deformation (16).** For an abelian variety $A_0/k$ in positive characteristic, Grothendieck asks for a $k$-stratification of $\mathrm{DR}^1(A/\Lambda)$ for every infinitesimal deformation. He requires compatibility with base change and functoriality in $A$. On the formal space of modules, compatibility with $F,V$ would constrain the geometric type of every fibre. He asks whether this points to ordinarity. The small inset identifies images and kernels and the text beside it invokes the maximum number of points of order $p$.

**Descending the Hodge summand (16–18; B17-flat).** In the ordinary situation he splits the extension of $H^{01}$ by $H^{10}$ using $\operatorname{Im}F$. He explains why stability of $H^{10(p)}$ leaves a further step for $H^{10}$. On the product of the formal base with itself, two sections of a Grassmannian become equal after the $p$-power base change. In the formally smooth case he invokes faithful flatness to descend the equality. He applies this on the formal space of modules and obtains a stratification-stable decomposition.

**Stratifying the two pieces (18–19).** Duality reduces the two tangent-space questions to one. The manuscript invokes the nondegenerate $p$-power structure and a discrete structural group. The margin says « à préciser ». It then writes the tangent bundles of an ordinary abelian prescheme as scalar extensions of locally constant étale $\mathbb F_p$-sheaves. Over a perfect field it relates them to Frobenius-fixed classes and duals of $p$-torsion points. Over a general base it uses the radicial and étale parts of the map from the $p$-torsion to the base.

**A polarization in the exterior square (20).** A polarization gives a section of $\bigwedge^2\mathrm{DR}^1(A/S)$. Grothendieck places it in the mixed summand of the exterior square of the decomposition. He identifies that component with the tangent homomorphism of the polarization and describes the map on the two étale sheaves. A long cancelled attempt explains the same operation by isotropic pieces and a pairing.

**The determinant and Hasse invariant (21–22).** He takes the inverse determinant of the tangent bundle, denoted $\omega_A$, and obtains a canonical section of its $(p-1)$-st power. After first describing a section on the ordinary locus $U$, he constructs it on the whole base using the determinant of $t_A^{(p)}\to t_A$. He calls it the invariant of Hasse and identifies its zero set. The determinant of the de Rham exact sequence gives an isomorphism $\omega_A\simeq\omega_{A^*}$. Compatibility with the $p$-structures and their sections is then asserted.

**Cartier duality and the proposed comparison (23–24; B23-dual).** He defines the étale Tate module by an inverse limit, writes the sequences of radical and étale factors for $A$ and $A^*$, and identifies opposite factors by Cartier duality. He seeks a canonical comparison of the étale determinant lines. An $\ell$-adic comparison motivates taking the determinant $\beta_p(\varphi)$ of the map on étale Tate modules and dividing it by $\sqrt{\deg\widetilde\varphi}$. He explicitly cancels a proposed lemma about arbitrary endomorphisms.

**Independence of the polarization and the sign (25–27).** Writing a second polarization in terms of the first and a rational endomorphism $u$, he reduces independence to a comparison of $\Delta(u)$ and $\delta(u)$. The squared equality leaves a sign. Scan 26 is an abandoned route through a sign character $\varepsilon$. Scan 27 takes up the live argument. It treats $\Delta$ as a polynomial, passes to real values, and uses squares on the positive cone of hermitian elements. The argument closes with « cqfd. ».

**The integral determinant lines (27–28; B28-dual).** For b), he reduces integrality to an equality of $p$-adic valuations. He relates the cokernel on the étale Tate module to the étale part of the $p$-primary kernel of the polarization. Self-duality pairs the étale and radical parts and gives the equality of their orders. Their product yields the square in the displayed cardinality formula. The boxed $\alpha_A$ is now an isomorphism between the determinant lines themselves.

**Naturality and unfinished comparisons (28–29).** For an automorphism $u$, he compares the determinant of $u$ with that of its contragredient and writes a product equal to $1$. An « Attention » qualifies the passage to general endomorphisms, even for an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field. The boxed formula on 29 has $\deg u$ on the right. The slanted note extends a version of that formula to homomorphisms between abelian schemes of equal relative dimension. The remaining items ask about dualizing twice, sums, and comparison with de Rham duality.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark R17-descent — high confidence.** The caution on 17 hinges on whether equality after the indicated base change descends. The two occurrences of the Grassmannian sections, before and after $\pi$, make this distinction explicit. For a simple instance of the danger, Frobenius on the dual numbers in characteristic three sends both zero and $\epsilon$ to zero.
- **Model remark R21-line — medium confidence.** The notation changes from $\omega_A^{\otimes(p-1)}$ to $L|U$ and later to $L^{\otimes(p-1)}$. An identification is being used, but these lines do not spell out what $L$ denotes at each occurrence.
- **Model remark R27-boundary — low confidence.** Scan 27, lines 12–13, moves from an element $\geq0$ to a hermitian $v>0$. If the second sign is strictly positive, the zero boundary case would need separate treatment or a weak inequality. Its two strokes are much lighter than the preceding $\geq$. This is a reading question about the sign.
- **Model remark R29-det — high confidence for the algebra, medium for its application to d).** Scan 22 displays an isomorphism of determinants. The frozen wording of d) on 29 displays the groups themselves. A determinant alone does not select a map of groups of rank two. Over $\mathbb F_3$, the identity and the upper triangular matrix with diagonal entries $1,1$ and upper-right entry $1$ have the same determinant and different images of the second basis vector. This distinction helps explain why the comparison in d) still needs checking.
- **Model reading questions noticed while writing these notes.** On 16, line 3 (page file line 6), the frozen wording is « d'avoir ». On enlargement, the letter groups after *a* look closer to « d'associer ». On 25, line 14 (page file line 23), the frozen wording is « examiner » in « Il faut encore examiner le signe ». After *ex*, the word shows a round *o*, a tight *rc* and a dotted *i*, favouring « exorciser ». 

- **Model reading question about two indices.** On 25, line 17 (page file line 26), the frozen source reads `u=\sum v.v'`. On enlargement the two v-shaped letters each have a separate small dotted stroke below and to the right. The last prime is above these strokes. Read `u=\sum v_i v_i'`. This location and the two words above had no frozen doubt mark.
