# Cote 87 · K87-03 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U01 · Facets and relative interiors, scan 29.** A polyhedral part \(C\) of an affine space is considered in its affine span \(A_C\). The manuscript starts with \(C^0\), then describes closed facets using supporting hyperplanes and affine subspaces that miss the preceding strata. For a facet of dimension \(d-i\) it takes an interior relative to \(F\). The page ends by characterizing the closed facets among unions of facets.

**U02 · Closure and order, scan 30.** The first three clauses relate a facet to its closure and its affine span, describe the facets of the closure, and give a convexity and closedness criterion for a union of facets. Clause d introduces the ordered set \(\Pi=\mathrm{Fac}(C)\), its opposite and the interval cut out by two faces. The corollary counts the strictly intermediate faces when the dimension difference is two.

**U03 · A combinatorial polyhedron, scans 30–31.** The definition starts with a finite ordered set with infima and suprema, a chain condition and a uniqueness condition for the other intermediate element of two successive covers. Clause d expresses an element as a supremum of smaller elements or an infimum of larger ones, with exceptions written into the line.

**U04 · Low dimensions, scan 31.** The entries for dimensions −1, 0 and 1 specify progressively larger orders, the last drawn as a diamond. In dimension 2 the contours have connected components with at least three vertices. The dimension-3 table puts vertices, edges and faces between the two extremes and asks for a connected contour at a vertex and at a face. The dimension-4 heading is left empty.

**U05 · Vertex, edge and face incidences, scan 33.** The two kinds of oriented edge project to \(A\). Their fibre product maps to \(S\times F\). The sheet prescribes rank two for these maps in the stated sense, then requires the incidence graph at each vertex and each face to be a nonempty connected contour. A « Version commune » introduces extreme elements and describes intermediate ranks by a sphere. The two interval subscripts in that account differ on the page.

**U06 · Involutions and orientations, scans 33–34.** On the set denoted by A with two joined arrows the author introduces \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\varepsilon\) and their commuting product \(\sigma\). The next sheet describes oriented vertices and faces by a choice of circular order, and an edge by data involving its two orientations. Two diagrams and four lines of fibre products relate the quotients to the incidence relation between \(S\) and \(F\).

**U07 · Axiom D, scan 35.** D asks for infima of nonempty bounded-below subsets, or dually suprema of nonempty bounded-above subsets. A crossed-out route passes through the number of sides of a polygon. The new account compares two edges, two faces, and an edge with a face. It ends with four incidence conditions and marginal versions expressing an edge as the supremum of its endpoints and the infimum of its incident faces.

**U08 · Order-theoretic interlude, scan 36.** For \(A\) in a poset the sheet forms the set \(B\) of upper bounds. It treats the empty set, then states \(\mathrm{Inf}\,B=\mathrm{Sup}\,A\) when the relevant bounds exist. The sufficiency argument uses the set of lower bounds of \(B\). A corollary gives the corresponding equivalences for all subsets, and for subsets subject to boundedness or nonemptiness.

**U09 · Consequences of D, scan 37.** The first corollary gives \(p(s)\geq3\) and \(q(f)\geq3\). The proof eliminates the values 0, 1 and 2 and refers the second inequality to the same reasoning. The margin singles out the weaker bound 2 as independent of D. The next corollary describes each face as a supremum and each vertex as an infimum of its incident elements.

**U10 · A rank from realization, scans 38–39.** Hypothesis (R) requires each strict lower interval to realize a sphere. The function \(\delta(x)\) is its dimension plus one. The argument compares the cone and its base and considers a maximal facet inside the sphere below a successor. The following sheet characterizes the rank by its value at minimal elements and its unit increment along covers.

**U11 · Spheres of intervals, scan 39.** The open interval \(x<\cdot<y\) is assigned dimension \(\delta(y)-\delta(x)-2\), including the empty sphere of dimension −1. The proposed lemma for an ordered set realizing a \(d\)-manifold treats an upper interval, a lower interval and a two-sided interval. The first formula has the same terminal subtraction of 2 as the third one. See S02.

**U12 · Two-step chains, scan 40.** After adjoining a least element, condition \((\Sigma)\) gives a unique alternative middle term \(y'\) in \(x<y<z\) when each step is a cover. The short argument shows why the two alternative steps are again covers. The symbol for the switch is \(\sigma_{x,z}(y)\). The rotated margin records a condition on the lengths of maximal chains towards \(x\).

**U13 · Repères and projections, scans 40–41.** An \(n\)-repère starts at a minimal element and takes successive covers. The sets \(R_n\) carry projections and involutions indexed by positions in the chain. The sheet lists commutations, absence of fixed points for certain products and compatibility with projection. The live continuation on 41 specifies which operators act trivially on \(R_p\).

**U14 · Cancelled incidence argument, scan 41.** The crossed part identifies three levels with \(S,A,F\), sketches branching chains and considers the vertices and edges incident to a face. It examines two-vertex and two-edge counts, connectivity and the local dimension near an edge incident to no face. The hatched fans and the ascending arrows accompany this abandoned argument.

## Slips

**S01 · Scan 30, L27–29.** After defining \(\Pi_{F',F''}\), the manuscript says « Alors \(\Pi_F\) ». The former is the apparent intended subscript because both endpoints specify the interval being realized. Confidence is medium. An abbreviation in the running argument is also possible.

**S02 · Scan 39, L22–23.** Part a gives \(d-\delta(x)-2\) for the dimension above \(x\). For the vertex of the triangle-boundary incidence poset used in M01, the corrected value is \(d-\delta(x)-1=0\). The ink shows −2 clearly. The example refutes part a as written with high confidence.

**S02r · Refined statement for this example.** Above a vertex of the triangle boundary, the strict upper interval realizes a sphere of dimension 0, in agreement with the expression \(d-\delta(x)-1\) in this example.

**S03 · Scan 33, L28–32.** If the realized interval is the open interval described immediately before it, the dimension suggested by scan 39 is \(\delta-2\) in place of \(\delta-1\). The two written interval subscripts differ, and the sphere letter is worked over. Confidence in attributing a slip here is low.

## Remarks of the model

**M01 · Finite example for S02. High confidence.** I enumerated the three vertices and three edges of a triangle, ordered by inclusion. Their realization has six vertices and six edges, is connected, and every vertex has degree two. At an original vertex the strict lower set is empty, giving \(\delta(x)=0\). Its strict upper set consists of two incomparable edges. Its realization has dimension 0, while the formula in 39(a) gives −1.

**M02 · Comparison of scans 33 and 39. Low confidence as an interpretation of scan 33.** In a triangle with its face included, the open interval from a vertex to the face contains exactly two incomparable edges. This agrees with the dimension-zero value on scan 39 for a rank difference of two. The count is exact. The uncertainty concerns which of the two subscripts on scan 33 the author meant to realize.

**M03 · Reading question, scan 34, L25. Low confidence.** The sealed diagram connects the middle \(\widetilde A\) to \(A\) and places the two sigma labels on that shaft. A short up-and-down sign between the labels, detached from \(A\), is also possible. The ink stops appreciably higher than the neighbouring arrows. The distinction affects one connection in the diagram.

**M04 · Reading question, scan 40, L29. Low confidence.** The sealed line has « \(x_0\) est minimal ». The first strokes before « minimal » could instead read « pt ». They form a short upright cluster without a clear e-loop. The two readings give the same intended initial condition for a repère.

**M05 · Reading question, scan 41, L40. Low confidence.** The rightmost label on the third row of the sealed arrow diagram is \(s\). A small 3 is also possible. The upper and lower lobes are compact and the neighbouring mark is similarly shaped.
