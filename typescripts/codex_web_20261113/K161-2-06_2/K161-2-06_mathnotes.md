# Mathematical notes · cote 161-2, scans 81–95

## What the pages do

**81 · Examples and morphisms.** A numbered list varies the limits, colimits and exactness conditions imposed on a category, together with the functors that preserve them. It ranges over ordered, semi-additive, additive and abelian categories, then considers topoi and essential points. Arrows in the margin move two entries within the list. The final sentence raises the size of the ambient universe.

**82 · Two constructions attached to a theory.** The upper diagram places ρ and σ in opposite directions between theories and Δ. The formulas give ρ(T) as the category of constructions and σ(R) by a represented Hom. A comparison through T(R) leads to a question about equivalence. A proposed arrow from σρ(T) to T occurs in a cancelled argument. The surviving box contains the arrow from T to σρ(T).

**83–84 · Envelopes, localization and inductive 2-limits.** Two representability conditions concern the Δ-envelope of a category and the inversion of an assigned set of arrows. To construct an inductive 2-limit, the author first takes one in Cat, then describes the full subcategory of maps out of it satisfying the Δ-condition on each term. The next sheet asks whether the arrows that must be inverted can be reduced to a small set.

**84–85 · The category of constructions.** A theory T over Δ is described by a cofibred 2-category or a 2-pseudofunctor. The distinguished theory τ evaluates to C at C. The expression Hom(T,τ) defines B_T. The author then introduces B′_T by steps and separates a size assumption from the argument that constructions inherit the specified limits.

**85–86 · Operations on structures.** The empty structure on I objects, adjoining axioms by inverting arrows, and adjoining arrows with prescribed source and target constitute steps (a), (b) and (c). In (c), an object over C acquires maps from s(h)(x) to b(h)(x). The next sheet constructs a category from the indicated arrows, takes its Δ-envelope, and imposes the remaining functoriality by inversion.

**86–87 · Combining theories.** Step (d) takes a product of theories. The variant (d′) fixes the objects of the underlying structures and uses a 2-fibred product over τ raised to I. Amalgamated sums appear on the side of the construction categories. Step (d″) proposes limits of theories with a possible restriction on the indexing categories.

**87–88 · Presentations and a transfinite construction.** For a small R, the author starts with its objects, adjoins its arrows, imposes composition, then imposes preservation of the chosen limits. The stages have names R₀ through R₃. A further construction starts from generators of an arbitrary R and alternates an envelope with the inversion of arrows becoming invertible in R. A separate line gives the rule at a limit ordinal. The question in the lower margin is whether the process reaches R.

**90 · A category generated by finite data.** For a family λ of finite diagrams the sheet introduces Σ, S and a subcategory S₀. Four assertions concern colimits in S, generation by objects of finite presentation, generation by Σ, and two enveloping-category descriptions. The question about faithfulness is attached to the comparison with the presheaves on S₀.

**91–92 · Two dictionaries.** The first dictionary pairs types and λ-categories. The second sets the second class of diagrams equal to the empty set and adds columns for the opposite category, a category with filtered colimits, and a topos. Across the two sheets, the corresponding rows treat morphisms, fully faithful morphisms, full subtheories, faithful morphisms and limits. Keeping the columns aligned matters particularly for the two directions of the limit arrows in the final row.

**93 · Rings, fields and pseudocorps.** The author asks what a limit-defined full subtheory containing fields can express. A criterion involving maps of rings, spectra and residue extensions leads to pseudocorps. An explicit map from the polynomial ring to a product is also written as a quotient presentation. The paragraph ends with questions about defining fields and the units of a ring object internally.

**94–95 · Units, zero and a classifying topos.** The universal ring object supplies a morphism involving its units and the terminal object. A base-change diagram gives the expression involving X_f and V(f). The next sheet factors the universal morphism through D and discusses localization and the intersection of zero with the units. The small case-defined functor assigns the empty set to a nonempty X and a singleton to the empty X. The concluding characterization pairs field objects with left-exact functors preserving sums, under hypotheses on the receiving category.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 87–88 · Model, medium confidence.** The small-R construction explicitly labels its last finite stage R₃ = R. For the ordinal construction on the next sheet, the corresponding equality becomes a marginal question. This distinction matters when reading the intervening sentence about large α. Its force depends on the qualifications in the NB.

**M2 · Scan 93 · Model, high confidence for the computation.** In the displayed product take the coordinate values of x to be (t,0) and of y to be (t⁻¹,0). Under that choice, each of the two written relations, (xy−1)x and (xy−1)y, has value zero in both coordinates. This checks the two relations for that assignment. The sheet explicitly gives the image of t. The choice of coordinates for y in this check is mine.

**M3 · Scans 94–95 · Model, low confidence.** The factorization suggests reading the next two paragraphs separately, one for the localization involving i and one for the disjointness of zero and the units. The heavy cancellation through the ε argument makes it difficult to tell how much of the second paragraph was intended to survive.

**Reading questions.** On scan 83, T3, the independent reading of the first relation is ⊂ in the parenthesis containing Cat with subscript d. The heavy inner stroke could also be ∈. On scan 91, T27 and T32, the large category letter in the fourth column was transcribed as script C. Its broad entrance and shallow lower curve also allow τ, especially alongside the narrower, upright C in the first column. Both questions concern the shape of the ink.
