# Mathematical notes · cote 100, scans 28–40

## What the pages do

**U28 · Orbit and stabilizer (28).** Definition 0.4 associates a map $\psi_f^G:G\times_S T\longrightarrow X\times_S T$ to a $T$-valued point $f$. Its two coordinates are the action on $f$ and the projection to $T$. Its image is called $O(f)$. Pulling back along the section $(f,1_T)$ defines $S(f)$, the stabilizer. The paragraph then adopts point notation over fields and breaks off in the sentence about $O(x)$.

**U29 · Descent of a formal abelian scheme (29, opening of 31).** The diagram arranges a base, repeated base changes and their completions. The manuscript uses a section on the residue fibre to obtain $A_0$. It then introduces $T=\operatorname{Spf} W(k)$ and a formal moduli scheme $M$ for $A_0$. The descent datum on $\widehat A'$ gives an equality between the two pullbacks of its map to $M$. Exactness of the displayed sequence is invoked to descend the map and obtain $\widehat A$.

**U30 · Illusie’s letter (30).** Illusie sends “l'Exp. IV”, asks about an apparent overlap between his analytic R-R appendix and an earlier exposé, and requests support for his CNRS renewal. He expects to send his “prénotes sur Künneth” in about a week. Two long oblique strokes cross the typed letter.

**U31a · Algebraization criterion (31).** For a complete noetherian local base, the theorem sets out three equivalent conditions on a formal abelian scheme. They are algebraizability over the base, algebraizability after a proper surjection, and an algebraizability test over normalizations of integral one-dimensional quotients. The quotient is actually written “$B$ de $A$”. The left marginal note further restricts the points to be tested by a countable family of closed sets. Its lower, separately cancelled neighbor is harder to read.

**U31b · From the proper cover to the trait test (31).** After pulling back the cover to a complete trait, the proof chooses a finite trait over it and obtains an algebraic abelian scheme there. The comparison theorem gives descent data. The writer appeals to finite faithfully flat descent and projectivity for effectivity.

**U32 · Conventions and a group pre-scheme (32).** An English typescript sets the bases for projective and affine spaces and for linear groups. It defines notation for identities, projections, products of maps, residue fields and rational points. Definition 0.1 specifies multiplication, inverse and unit over $S$. The visible square expresses associativity by applying multiplication to three factors in the two orders.

**U33 · Polarizations and the finite cover (33).** At each level $n$ the manuscript introduces an underlined NS and an open scheme $P_n$ defined by polarizations. The $P_n$ glue formally. The resulting formal scheme is described by a countable family of finite schemes $P^\alpha$ over $S$. Their images $Z_\alpha$ contain between them the points with one-dimensional closure, by condition (iii). A lemma gives a finite covering subfamily and the corresponding $P^\alpha$ furnish $S'$. The next implication begins the reduction to the reduced base.

**U35a · Gluing on a reduced base (end of 33 and 35).** After a finite étale extension and a condition on residual extensions, the discussion turns to irreducible components. The first lemma glues algebraizations over a reduced base. For $S=S'\cup S''$, their restrictions are identified over $T=S'\cap S''$ by comparison. The proof then glues $X'$ and $X''$ along that identification.

**U35b · Passing through a thickening (35 and upper 37).** The second lemma asks for algebraicity of a proper smooth formal scheme when its restriction to the reduced base is algebraic. The next sheet reduces the lifting step to an ideal $J$ and compares the obstruction groups in degree two and the indeterminacy groups in degree one before and after completion. The coefficient letter is difficult to distinguish from other cursive letters. The exponent in the initial condition on $J$ is also marked.

**U37 · A finite subcover from one-dimensional tests (37 and 39).** The proposition concerns a countable family of closed subsets of a complete noetherian local scheme. If the union contains every point with closure of dimension at most one, the claimed conclusion is that a finite subfamily covers the scheme. The manuscript disposes of the case of dimension at most one and reduces the rest to a finite extension of a power-series ring over a complete discrete valuation ring.

**U39 · Avoiding the rare closed subsets (39).** The corollary seeks a point with one-dimensional closure outside a countable union of “rares” closed subsets. On the power-series base the writer looks for a $V$-algebra map to $V$. He identifies the maps with tuples in a power of the maximal ideal, with its complete metric. For a principal closed set, membership becomes the evaluation equation $f(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_r)=0$. The page calls the solution set closed and rare. The last variable and the exponent on the ideal are overdrawn.

**U38 · The preface’s two problems (38).** The author asks when an algebraic group action has an orbit space and when a moduli scheme classifies the objects under consideration. A birational construction alone leaves a choice of model. The difficult step is to identify which orbits or objects are stable and can occur as its geometric points. Nonsingular polarized surfaces are the particular moduli problem posed here.

**U36 · Varying the characteristic (38, then 36).** The author looks for a replacement for full reducibility that works in finite characteristic. Starting with an invariant hyperplane in a representation, the conjecture asks whether for some $\nu$ an invariant line complements the indicated hyperplane in degree $p^\nu$. The preface uses the unresolved status of this question to explain the division of chapters between characteristic zero and arithmetic constructions.

**U34 · Rationality and a one-parameter subgroup (end of 36 and 34).** For $k$ perfect and not algebraically closed, the question is whether an unstable $k$-rational point admits a $k$-rational one-parameter subgroup $\lambda$ with $\mu(x,\lambda)<0$. The author suggests the Tits fixed point conjecture as an approach. The page then explains the choice of scheme language and the role of geometric points. The final example continues off the sheet.

**U40 · Letter on revisions (40).** The unsigned letter to Dieudonné discusses the placement of a condition in 12.1.1. and 12.1.3., a uniqueness restriction in 15.5.6.1., and the noetherian hypothesis in 15.6.3. and 15.6.4. It proposes using the ordered sets of open subsets and lifting generizations in the relevant arguments. It closes with a request for the second proofs. The body, date and closing are crossed by diagonal strokes.

## Remarks of the model

- **M28 · My observation, high confidence (28).** In the stabilizer square the two maps into $X\times_S T$ have the same $T$-coordinate after pullback. On a pair $(g,t)$ the other coordinate asks whether acting by $g$ fixes $f(t)$. This follows directly from the two displayed coordinates of $\psi_f$ and the label on the right edge.
- **M33 · My observation, high confidence (33, 37, 39).** The general lemma invoked after the union of the $Z_\alpha$ in 33 is recognizable in the proposition on 37. Both require a finite cover from a countable family and test points by the dimension of their closure. Scan 33 writes dimension one, while 37 states “$\leq1$”. Scan 39 returns to dimension one in its avoidance conclusion.
- **M36 · My check, high confidence (36).** Multiplying a degree-one factor from the indicated hyperplane by an element of degree $p^\nu-1$ gives degree $p^\nu$, as in the displayed target. This checks the placement of the minus one. The existence of an invariant complementary line remains the author’s conjecture on this page.
- **M39 · My inference, medium confidence (39).** Passing to a complete metric space of tuples and calling each exceptional set closed and rare suggests a category argument to choose one tuple outside their countable union. The sheet ends after the assertion about an individual closed set.
- **RQ39 · My reading question, low confidence (39, paragraph after the $V$-hom display).** The frozen text has “l'ens. des ces hom”. A possible reading is “l'ens. de ces hom”. The first short word ends in a horizontal exit that may or may not represent s. I would leave its status as a question of reading rather than assign the author a grammatical error.
