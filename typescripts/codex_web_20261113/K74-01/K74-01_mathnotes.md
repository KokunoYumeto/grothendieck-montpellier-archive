## What the pages do

**19 · A graph assembled from group data.** The sheet fixes a group $V$, a subset $I$, homomorphisms to groups $V_\lambda$, subsets $I_\lambda$ and a $V$-set $Y$. The components $X_\lambda$ enter a disjoint union with $Y$ and a singleton. Four written rules specify adjacency. The question at the foot asks when this graph is *trialitaire*. Scan 23 lays out three blocks of nine indexed symbols, joins rows and columns by lines and repeats the arrangement in a smaller triangular figure. **24 · Adjacency relative to a figure.** The upper figure singles out fixed and exchanged labels. **26–27 · Grouping and counting.** Scan 26 puts free triples, bibases, hexagons and angles between roots in correspondence. Separate columns give stabilizers, orders and numbers of objects. Below them are systems of three planes and nine opposite pairs. Scan 27 arranges the indexed points in three blocks, lists partitions into orbit sizes, and writes linkage conditions on three products formed from $E,E',E''$. Several alternative counts and configurations occupy the margins.

**28 · Three labelled hexagons.** Labels on opposite sides, two inscribed triangles, shaded corners and the bracketed pairs of roots distinguish the three figures. A claim about an index-three subgroup follows. A diagram with five vertices in a row and one branch, and a further array of the point labels, close the sheet.

**29 · A working proposition and a second set of calculations.** The proposition compares an ordered free triple with the two bases containing it and their complements. The page also lists 27 sommets, 36 bibases, 40 trihexagones and 45 triangles. Lower down, a four-element $A(i)$, a torsor $P(i)$, spaces over $\mathbb F_2$, and a diagram with dimensions 1, 2, 1 occur in much less connected notes.

**30–31 · Correspondences and tables.** Scan 30 sets hexagons, trièdres, bitriangles, blocks and planes of type $A_2$ alongside one another. Its diagram and square arrays keep track of which indexed triples go together. The calculations include 720 and 120. Scan 31 sets out nine pairs of triples in three bracketed rows and draws links labelled with the six singly indexed symbols.

**33 · A central extension and its lifts.** For a three-element set $I$, the sheet forms $\Gamma=\ker(V^I\to V)$, uses the circular orders on $I$ to define $V^\omega$, and gives the three coordinates of $\varphi_i$. It seeks homomorphisms $\psi_i$ lifting these maps to a group over $\Gamma$ with central kernel $Z$. A sum of three $\varphi$-values vanishes. The corresponding ordered product of lifts defines an element $z(x,\rho,i_0)$ of $Z$.

**34 · Orientation and commutation.** The sheet makes $z$ independent of the choice of starting index, reverses the sign when the circular order is reversed and writes the resulting functions as $z_\rho$. A proposition relates a commutator to $z_\rho(x)+z_\rho(y)-z_\rho(x+y)$. The page ends with conditions on $\lambda=z_\rho$. The last condition is substantially overwritten.

**36 · Quadratic differences.** A second difference of $\lambda$ is written independently of the base point $x$. The value at zero defines $\mu(u,v)$. The manuscript calls it bi-additive, describes the freedom in choosing a normalized $\lambda$ for fixed $\mu$, and states a criterion involving $\mu(e_i,e_i)\in2Z$ for a free module over $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. The faint last line reaches the edge.

**35, 37 · Examination papers.** Scan 35 is an unused Montpellier examination form. Scan 37 is R. Brunel’s letter inviting the recipient to the DEUG A, first-year, Série I jury. It gives the meeting date, room, and the material to be brought into account for the minutes.

## Remarks of the model

- **A26.1–A30.2 · Arithmetic, high confidence.** The numerical products can be checked independently of their geometric interpretations. On scan 26, $3!3!2!=72$ and $6^3\cdot2=432$. On scans 27 and 29, $6^4=36^2=1296$, and scan 29 also has $6!=720$. On scan 30, $36\binom63=720$ and $720/6=120$. Each evaluation is recorded separately in the check register.
- **M34 · Centrality, high confidence.** The invariance under changing the starting index is consistent with the stated centrality. The cyclic permutations of the product are conjugate to it. This check concerns the cyclic rotation in the first three lines.
- **M36 · The torsor assertion, high confidence conditional on existence.** For a fixed polar form, the difference of two normalized solutions is additive. Conversely, adding an additive homomorphism preserves normalization and the polar form. This accounts for the freedom in a nonempty set of solutions. The final criterion for existence over the residue ring was not independently established.

### Reading questions and drawing limitations

- **21, parenthesis to the right of the upper hexagon.** Frozen “ou triplet distingué”. The pen strokes could instead end “ou triplets typiques”. The long first descender of the second word in particular deserves another reading. The criticism belongs here because this place had no frozen doubt mark.
- **21, upper-left edge of the hexagon.** The frozen diagram uses an undirected edge between the left vertex and the upper-left triple. A short triangular mark at the upper end could be an arrowhead. Several little cross strokes on the broken inner lines are also less distinct in the reconstruction.
- **24, upper polyhedron.** The frozen TikZ path follows $s,r',s',r,s$. Its single arrow option produces a head on the closing segment. Short heads are also visible on intermediate sides in the ink. The paths and labels must be distinguished from this limitation in their rendering.
- **29, whole-sheet marks.** The frozen text treats the long slanting strokes through the proposition as a cancellation. They extend well outside its first paragraph and their scope is doubtful. Separate horizontal deletions are much clearer.
- **34, second inverse-product row.** Frozen `\unsure{+|-}x` in the last factor. The tip of the preceding parenthesis overlaps the horizontal. The minus in the preceding two factors supports a negative argument but does not settle this mark.
