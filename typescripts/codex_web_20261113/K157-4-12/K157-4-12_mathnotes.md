# Mathematical notes · 157-4, scans 180–195

## What the pages do

**A proposed counterexample, scans 180–181.** Grothendieck tests whether a closure argument about systems with monomorphic transition maps gives stability for filtered limits « grandes devant π ». In (5.4.) he takes the isomorphism class of an object $X_0$ with a restriction on its monomorphic endomorphisms. He works through the cofinality test, then observes that a nontrivial projector could still give a direct factor outside the class. He adds a small filtered-colimit stability hypothesis on $M^\pi$ to recover the argument.

**The lemma called 5.7, scans 181–183.** The proof seeks arbitrarily large indices at which $X_i\to X$ is monic. Grothendieck factors through the images $X'_i$, alternates primed and unprimed terms in a zigzag, and passes to the supremum of its indices. For the remaining assertion he invokes condition e) on filtered colimits of monomorphisms.

**Proposition 5.7 and Corollaire 5.8, scans 183–185.** The proposition compares stability of $M$ under filtered colimits with a bounded stability test on $M\cap\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}$ and a cofinal-subobject criterion. The added c′) describes a smallest stable part. The corollary gives two assignments in opposite directions. One takes the intersection with the filtration level, and the other tests small subobjects of an arbitrary $X$. The successive drafts alter the proposed order of proof.

**Lemme 5.8 and its application, scans 185–187.** A π-cofinal subset of an adapted ordered set is cofinal and closed under the indicated suprema. Alternating between two such subsets produces a common upper element in their intersection. The lemma is applied to subsets of the ordered set of small subobjects in order to recover condition (5.3.).

**Proposition 5.9 and Lemme 5.10, scans 187–190.** Proposition 5.9 isolates the passage from bounded filtered-colimit stability of $M^\pi$ to filtered-colimit stability of $M$, assuming π-accessibility. The sketch starts with $X=\varinjlim_I X_i$ and constructs a category $C$ over $I$. Its fibre at $i$ is described using morphisms into $X_i$. After cancelling a first proof of 5.10, Grothendieck uses a cofinality test with two conditions. The second uses uniqueness of factorization through a subobject.

**Corollaire 5.11 and regrouping the system, scans 190–191.** The corollary says that $C$ is filtered and $C\to I$ is cofinal. Its statement writes the $X_i$ in $M^\pi$. The following argument first expresses the colimit using small objects, then groups a filtered ordered system by its filtered subsets $J$ with cardinal at most π. Their partial colimits $X_J$ lie in $M^\pi$ by the stability assumption. The last line concludes $X\in M$.

**Définition 5.12, scans 192–193.** The first part defines $L$-π-accessibility by adding filtered-colimit stability to π-accessibility. The margin calls this stability $L$-stability. The second part describes $L$-accessibility through existence of a suitable π, accessibility together with $L$-stability, and generation as an $L$-envelope. The next page explains the equivalences by reference to an earlier result on cardinals.

**Proposition 5.13 and Lemme 5.14, scans 193–194.** The proposition concerns finite unions and intersections of at most π many $L$-π-accessible parts. For unions, the order-theoretic lemma says that if a filtered ordered set is covered by a nonempty finite family of subsets, one member is cofinal. A stronger-looking tail assertion and a first proof are crossed out. The live proof takes a common upper bound of the witnesses to non-cofinality.

**The intersection criterion, scans 194–195.** Grothendieck writes $X$ as a π-adapted colimit and associates a set of indices $I_{M_\alpha}$ to each part. He records closure of these sets under the relevant suprema. For $M=\bigcap_{\alpha\in A} M_\alpha$ he reduces the remaining accessibility claim to an equivalence involving cofinality of the intersection of the index sets. The supplied pages end at that display.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · scans 185–186. Model remark, high confidence.** Ordinary cofinality is too weak for the intersection conclusion. The even and odd subsets of the natural numbers are each cofinal and have empty intersection. This isolates the role of the additional closure in the lemma. Both possible residues of a cutoff were checked.
- **S14+ and R2 · scans 193–194. Model remark, low confidence as a reading of the cancelled assertion.** The struck NB appears to say that some member of an arbitrary finite cover contains a whole tail. The same parity cover refutes that reading. Every tail contains both parities. The live assertion S14 asks only for a cofinal member.
- **R3 · scans 184–185 and 192. Model remark, low confidence.** The short c′) insertion on 184 introduces $M_0$ and describes a smallest stable part without an explicit phrase saying it contains $M_0$. The phrases « l’enveloppe … de $M_0$ » on 185 and « la $L$-enveloppe de $M_0$ » on 192 favour that intended relation.
- **Reading question · scan 195, around line 8.** The frozen right-hand subscript in the equality after « D’ailleurs, comme » is `\unsure{0|\alpha}`. An α would match the family on the left and the subsequent $I_{M_\alpha}$, but the low stroke on the right resembles a closed zero. This remains a question about the writing.
