# Mathematical notes on cote 33, scans 171–181

*SGA [32] 1965. MS [Manuscrit] brouillon : notes manuscrites (s.d.)* (n.d.), cote 33, scans 171–181, Fonds Alexandre Grothendieck, Montpellier. Scan numbers below refer to the supplied facsimiles. The English typescripts do not name their authors on these leaves.

## What the pages do

**Revision of Chapter II (scan 171, typed sheet (7)).** The page gives deletion and replacement instructions for pp. 55–65 of a text on topological vector spaces. The new discussion is to start with an increasing filtered family of locally convex spaces. For the direct sum, the editor wants the neighborhoods of zero and the characterization by the maps into every locally convex $F$. A corollary presents an inductive limit as a quotient of a direct sum. Proposition 6 concerns the map from the direct sum to the product and the finite-index case.

**Closed subspaces and completeness (scan 171).** The proof of Corollaire 1 uses continuity of the projections (B171) and identifies the sum of the $G_\alpha$ with the intersection of their inverse images under the coordinate projections. The page then schedules the completeness assertion for later proof and specifies that $E$ is to be the inductive limit of the $E_n$ throughout the numbered section.

**Proposed vanishing theorem (scan 172).** The handwritten statement takes $A=Z/NZ$, with $N$ prime to the characteristic of $k$, and $\dim X\leq n$. The insertion qualifying “de type fini” and the connector between flatness of $F$ and injectivity of the stalks of $G$ are uncertain. Part (i) proposes vanishing of the sheaf Ext in degrees $i>2n$ for constructible $F$. Part (ii) assumes $k$ separably closed and writes two Ext vanishings. The first argument and the coefficient of $n$ in the first bound remain difficult. The second bound reads $i>2n$.

**Reduction to an affine case (scans 172 and 174).** The proof proposes passing to a limit, keeping the first relation of (ii), and treating $G$ with injective stalks. It considers a quotient involving $i_!(A_{X'})$, with $X'$ affine and étale over an affine open $U$ of $X$. The note restricts $G$ to $U$. Scan 174 continues with constructible $F$ and asks whether $G$ can be obtained as a filtered inductive limit while preserving injectivity of the stalks of the constructible terms.

**Adjunction, degrees and support regions (scan 176).** The sheet compares Ext out of $i_!F$ with Ext into $\mathbb R i^!G$ and writes a spectral-sequence term involving $\mathbb R^qi^!G$. It lays out degrees from $0$ to $2d$, cohomology terms, inequalities and several regions drawn against coordinate axes. The lower half returns to derived Hom, writes a nested Ext expression involving $A_X$, and starts a local-to-global expression. The last $H^{2d}(X,$ stops at the comma.

**Duality and a codimension estimate (scan 178).** A chain of derived Hom expressions passes through $DDG$ and a derived tensor with $DG$. The framed spectral-sequence line has $E_1^{pq}$ in this transcription. The later support assertion gives codimension at least $-q$ for the support of the sheaf Ext in degree $-q$. A vanishing condition follows. The sheet finishes with a proposed degree-$2d$ comparison involving $F\otimes\mathscr H om(G,A_X)$. The low marks by several occurrences of $F$ and the last inequality affect how the intervening argument can be read.

**Pairings and dimension bounds (scan 180).** Two rows of cohomology with $\Gamma_x,\Gamma_X,\Gamma_U$ run in opposite directions. Crosses and vertical lines indicate pairings with values in $A$. Below are $f^!D=Df^*$, derived Hom and tensor expressions, and a comparison involving stalks and biduality. Two downward implications take the intervals $[N,M]$ and $[P,Q]$ to $[-2n+N,M]$ and $[P-2n,Q]$, respectively, in statements about Tor-dimension and injective dimension. The last tensor–Hom line has a cancelled trial below it.

**A divisor and an affine chart (scan 175, typescript p. 11).** The opening distinguishes the point-set inverse image $f^{-1}(W)$ from the bracketed $f^{-1}[W]$ used in the subsequent assertion. The latter is said to be irreducible of codimension one and the center of the given valuation on $V^*$. Lemma 1 forms $S=R[y_2,\ldots,y_n]$ using the handwritten replacement $y_i=x_i/x_1$. It identifies $S/(y_1S)$ with a polynomial ring over $R/M$ and describes the local rings $S_P$ at maximal ideals over $y_1$, including their dimension and residue fields.

**Local rings, contraction and completion (scan 173, typescript p. 12).** The first “Proof” supplies the reference for the preceding lemma in typescript order. Lemma 2 assumes equal dimensions, inclusion of quotient fields, equality of the identified residue fields, and the hypothesis $M\bar R=\bar M$ (B173). It states approximation modulo powers of $\bar M$, contraction identities for rings and ideals, preservation and reflection of principality, and a consequence for completions. The written induction improves an approximation by replacing coefficients $b_i$ with residue representatives $c_i$. A new sentence about a minimal basis of $M$ breaks off.

**Quadratic transforms along a valuation (scan 181, typescript p. 24).** Lemma 11 asserts uniqueness of the length and chain of successive immediate transforms ending at $R^*$. Its valuation clause uses the corrected bound $q\leq m$. Definition 2 uses a valuation of $R$-dimension zero to designate the quadratic sequence along $v$. A handwritten instruction specifies which words to italicize. Lemma 12 begins with a pair of barred local data and stops while listing hypotheses.

**A parity calculation (scan 177, typescript p. 67).** Even and odd parity of $C_n^v$ leads to two integrals for the handwritten coefficient $\gamma$ with an uncertain lower index. The odd branch has the factor $i\pi$. The even branch has a minus sign and $\log x$. The text sends the reader to [18a] for evaluation.

**Local and global symbols (scans 177 and 179, typescript pp. 67–68).** Definition 4.5.1 uses the homogeneous part of $J$ and the pointwise symbol to define $\mathbb S_MJ$ on the tangent sphere bundle. The zero-symbol observation places $J$ in the intersection of the local ideals and calls $J$ completely continuous. Theorem 4.5.2 (B179) calls the symbol map an epimorphism onto the algebra of smooth functions and identifies its kernel with the ideal of completely continuous operators.

**Fredholm regularization (scan 179).** For equation (4.5.3), $J\rho=f$, the problem is to multiply by an operator $K$, obtain Fredholm type, and retain the same solutions. Theorem 4.5.4 states that a nowhere-zero global symbol is necessary and sufficient. The proof begins by choosing $K_1$ whose symbol times the symbol of $J$ is $1$. The next step falls beyond the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

- **R171 · Model, high confidence (171).** Within the direct sum, membership in every $p_\alpha^{-1}(G_\alpha)$ is exactly the coordinate condition for membership in $\sum G_\alpha$. Continuity of the projections therefore gives the closedness claimed in Corollaire 1.
- **R176 · Model, medium confidence (176, 178).** The nested Ext expression on 176 contains $F$ on the left and no explicit $F$ on the right. In the framed term on 178, $F$ is visibly a tensor factor on the right. These are two distinct stages of calculation. The comparison leaves open whether the writer meant the latter as a revision of the former.
- **R178 · Model, low confidence as a reading (178).** The frozen alternatives at the end of the support calculation are $pq$ and $p+q$. If the preceding sign is strict, $p<-q$ gives $p+q<0$. The product test already differs at $(p,q)=(0,-1)$. The algebra favours the additive interpretation, while the crop has no distinct plus.
- **R179 · Model, high confidence (177, 179).** For a nowhere-zero smooth symbol the reciprocal is smooth. Surjectivity of the symbol map, as used in the typescript, therefore gives a preimage $K_1$ of that reciprocal. This accounts for the choice in the last line. The argument about preservation of the solution set continues beyond the page.
- **R180 · Model, high confidence for the endpoints (180).** Both written interval implications lower their first endpoint by $2n$ and retain the second. The small sketch labels its horizontal axis $M,0,N$, whereas the first written interval begins with $N$. The ordering can be reported directly without assigning a numerical convention to the sketch.
