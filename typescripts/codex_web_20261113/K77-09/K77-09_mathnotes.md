# Mathematical notes on cote 77, scans 129–143

## What the pages do

**Odd rank and the determinant (129).** The opening completes a discriminant-line identification. The odd-rank section writes a model quadratic form in paired variables and one square, then gives the determinant map on the orthogonal group and its restriction to central scalars. A section-base $\omega$ of $\det E$ specifies the extra structure of index $\varepsilon$. The page records its automorphism group and the effect of changing $q$ to $-q$.

**Similitudes and twisting (130).** The central scalar acts on the value line by its square. A proposition identifies the group of similitudes in odd rank. Its corollary describes a line-valued quadratic module using a special orthogonal module and an invertible module $\Delta$. The forward formulas construct $E$ and $L$. In the reverse direction the author writes $\Delta=L^{\otimes-\nu}\otimes\det E$ and computes the determinant of the resulting $E_0$.

**Ordinary quadratic modules (131).** The value line specializes to the ground ring. A special quadratic module is accompanied by a line with a trivialization of its tensor square, also described as a $\mu_2$-torsor. Two arrow diagrams specify the functor and its quasi-inverse. The sheet then starts a section on similitudes of a form with values in a line.

**Even rank and the component group (132–133).** In rank $2\nu$ the notes take $SO(q)$ as the neutral component and write $O(q)/SO(q)\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)_S$. To produce the homomorphism they propose a degree-two étale cover. Scan 133 singles out the closed smooth locus of maximal isotropic subspaces in the Grassmannian and states the number of its geometric components.

**Isotropic flags (134).** The set $H$ specifies the ranks in a flag, with its largest member totally isotropic. Theorem 1 locates the corresponding scheme inside the flag scheme and states projectivity, smoothness and the geometric fibre count. The exceptional case is even rank with the maximal isotropic rank present. The slanted margin asks for the analogous statement for forgetting some steps.

**Lifting across a square-zero ideal (134–136).** The smoothness argument reduces the problem to lifting the last isotropic submodule. A cancelled approach tries to lift one basis element at a time. The subsequent argument varies a lift by a homomorphism and maps that variation to a quadratic form. It identifies an epimorphism and asks for an explicit identification beside its factorization.

**Orthogonal complements (137).** Lemma 1 studies $F^\perp$ for an isotropic direct factor and the pairing of $F$ with $E/F^\perp$. The argument reduces the epimorphism onto $F^\vee$ to a field. The delicate case singled out is characteristic two and odd rank.

**Reducing the flag fibre (137–138).** For a fixed $F$ of rank $d$, the next statements use $E'=F^\perp/F$ with its induced form and rank $N-2d$. Isotropic extensions of rank $d'$ give rank $d'-d$ subspaces in this quotient. The last lemma reduces the one-step count to the quadric in projective space, separating ranks at least three from rank two.

**The determinant section (139–140).** In even rank, at a maximal isotropic $F$, the pairing identifies $E/F$ with $F^\vee$. The determinant isomorphism gives $\omega_F$, which the author also writes as a wedge of a basis and its dual. Lemma 5 chooses an isotropic complement on an affine base. A discriminant computation leads to the map $F\mapsto\omega_F$ into the Arf cover.

**Fibres at fixed $\omega$ (141–142).** Theorem 2 assumes that 2 is invertible and states that this map is smooth and projective with connected geometric fibres. After a crossed change-of-basis argument, the author uses the orthogonal action for surjectivity. He reduces connectedness to extending an $(n-1)$-step with $\omega$ prescribed. Lemma 6 compares the two degree-two covers. Specialization and Stein factorization are invoked for the general case.

**Recapitulation (143).** The final theorem collects the results for maps between two flag schemes. The fibre has two components in the exceptional case $N$ even and $d'<d=n$. The author describes the Arf object as the scheme of components of the maximal-isotropic flag scheme over $S$. The composite through that object is on the sheet. Its next asserted property begins on the last line and is incomplete here.

**R2 · My remark · High confidence (138).** The displayed rank $N-2d$ preserves the parity of $N$. This accounts for the recurrence of the same parity distinction after passage to $F^\perp/F$.

**R3 · My remark · Medium confidence (133, 139–143).** Scan 133 counts two components. The determinant section on 139–140 assigns a point of a degree-two object to each maximal isotropic subspace, and 143 explicitly calls the Arf object the scheme of components. These passages give a geometric interpretation of that earlier count.
