# Mathematical notes • K157-5-22

## What the pages do

**341–342. Successor and limit stages.** To construct $X_{\alpha+1}(j'')$, Grothendieck lists the arrows it must receive. The arrow associated to an identity supplies the transition morphism. At the specified successor stages the transitions must also equalize pairs of arrows from earlier conditions. He bounds the data by $\pi$. For a limit ordinal, he asks $X_\alpha(\cdot)$ to receive the earlier $X_\beta(\cdot)$ for $\beta<\alpha$.

**343. Lemma 4.10.7.** Starting with a family $X(\cdot)$ in the product of the fibres, the lemma collects the inductive system, its initial value and conditions (i)–(iii) on manuscript p. 256. Item 3 adds the displayed compatibility for maps from a section $F$. The directed diagram and its labels are part of the statement.

**344. Proof of the additional compatibility.** For each fixed $j$, the two routes in the diagram give a pair of maps into $X_1(j)$. The proof counts the pairs and chooses $X_1(j)\to X'_1(j)$ equalizing them. Replacing the first-stage object imposes the extra condition.

**345–346. Construction of the section $G$.** Under hypothesis c) of 4.10.3, the notes take a fibrewise inductive limit over the predecessors of a limit ordinal. They use the $t_\beta(\lambda)$ to define $G(\lambda)$ on limits. The margin identifies where commutation with these limits is used. The identity and composition conditions then give a section, with maps from the initial family. Grothendieck invokes item 3 for the second of his closing conditions.

**347–348. Corollary 4.10.8.** For a cofibrant functor over an essentially small category, the corollary asserts accessibility of the section category from accessibility of the fibres and the base-change functors. Its more precise form introduces infinite cardinals $\pi_0$ and $\pi$, including $\pi^{\pi_0}=\pi$, a base-change condition and a bound on the reduced cardinal of $J$. It describes $M_\pi$ using the values of sections in the fibres. The proof uses the cofibration $N_\pi$, the ordinal condition and 4.10.1 to identify $M$ with the indicated ind-completion.

**349. Corollary 4.10.9.** The fibres are now paratopoi. Two versions of the base-change hypothesis are written into the statement, linked by a marginal warning about the term « cofibration en paratopos ». The proof combines accessibility from 4.10.8 with closure of the section category under the indicated limits. The two lower arrows under lim point the same way.

**349–350. Corollary 4.10.10.** Grothendieck begins with a fibred category having paratopos fibres and direct-image base-change functors. He invokes left adjoints to these functors and applies the preceding cofibrant case to conclude that the category of sections is a paratopos.

**350–351. Question 4.10.11.** The question asks about accessibility of sections over an essentially small base with accessible fibres and accessible change of base for a fibrant functor. The conjecture covers the cartesian-section expression as well. Grothendieck compares the problem with a result under stronger hypotheses and asks for a common generalization with 4.10.8.

**351–353. Proposition 4.11.1.** The new section examines which colimits exist in $M=\operatorname{Ind}_\pi(C)$. For a collection $\Phi$ of shapes of reduced cardinal at most $\pi$, the sufficient direction assumes the corresponding colimits in $C$ and stability for a suitable limit-ordinal shape $I_\alpha$. The converse assumes that $C$ is Karoubian. The inclusion is said to preserve the colimits under consideration. The proof uses a representation $J\times I\to C$ in one direction and the essential image $M_\pi$ in the other.

**353–354. Corollary 4.11.2 and its proof.** The corollary specializes the criterion to filtered colimits, with a parenthetical version for arbitrary small colimits. It reduces the sufficient direction to the lemma immediately following. The converse is referred back to 4.11.1.

**354. The lemma cited as 4.11.3.** Closure under filtered colimits of bounded cardinal, together with closure under colimits of the indicated size relative to $\pi$, yields closure under small filtered colimits. Interlinear additions give the corresponding variants for all small colimits and for small coproducts. Grothendieck refers to a proof on p. 97.

**354. Corollary 4.11.4.** The question here is when $M$ has coproducts. The sufficient criterion uses coproducts in $C$ of cardinal at most $\pi$ together with an ordinal $\alpha$ supplying the $I_\alpha$ colimits in $C$. The closing clause associates necessity with $C$ being Karoubian. The presence of the connective before $C$ is uncertain.

**355. Proposition 4.11.5.** The shapes are equivalent to finite rigid categories. Rigidity is written as $\operatorname{Hom}_I(i,i)=\{\mathrm{id}_i\}$. The proposed sufficient condition transfers colimits, and parenthetically limits, of these shapes from $C$ to $M$. The necessity assertion in the first case assumes $C$ Karoubian. The upper margin says the proof is unfinished.

**355–356. Lemma 4.11.6.** For a finite rigid shape up to equivalence, the proposed lemma represents every $J\to M$ by $J\times I\to C$, with the indicated largeness condition on $I$. The next sheet restates this using the canonical functor from $\operatorname{Ind}_\pi(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,C))$ to $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,M)$. The lower note on 355 questions the lemma. The NB on 356 separately asks what happens on removing rigidity.

**356. Cancelled Corollary 4.11.7.** The crossed draft compares a $\pi$-indexed representation with an ordinary indexed representation. It describes each through the essential image of a functor involving Hom and Ind. The diagonal strokes extend through the final expression. The first condition on the cardinal of $J$ had already been replaced inside the draft.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 343–344. Model observation, high confidence.** Following the two directed paths out of $F(j')$ gives the labels $t_0(\lambda)u(j')$ and $\tau_0(j)u(j)F(\lambda)$ in compositional order. These are precisely the two expressions subsequently paired on 344. Both paths end at the same drawn vertex. This identification is independent of the doubtful small mark on that vertex's $j$.

**R2 · Scans 355–356. Model observation, high confidence about the stated dependence.** The prose assigns the necessity direction in 4.11.5 to 4.11.1. It introduces 4.11.6 specifically for the first, sufficient direction. Thus the lower marginal question singles out a premise used for sufficiency. The sheets leave the truth of the lemma open.
