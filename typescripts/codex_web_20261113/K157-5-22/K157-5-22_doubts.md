## Legibility

The clearest material is the spacing of the main statements, the objects and directed edges in the diagram on 343, and the separate locations of the two cautions on 355. The endings of French words are less secure. Often a final horizontal movement is all that distinguishes two readings. The weakest areas are the overwritten successor condition on 341, the squeezed cardinal conditions on 347, the two overlapping definitions on 349, and the text under both a local strike and the long diagonal rulings on 356. The one use of the illegibility mark is the cancelled word after *Alors* on 347. It represents approximately one word. A short disputed shaft, superscript or subscript has a candidate reading at its position.
