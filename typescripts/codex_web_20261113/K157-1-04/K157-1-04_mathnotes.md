# What the pages do

**050–052 · Properness, smoothness and base change.** The opening takes up a direct image whose formation commutes with passage to fibres. Two corollaries concern contractible fibres. A note gives a limitation on cancelling a proper factor: even a section of a proper category over a base need not be proper.

**050–052 · The comparison map.** A cartesian square gives $p^*f_*F\to f'_*q^*F$. The proper case passes from fibres to localized categories and applies sections. In the smooth case a cube compares the two localized categories, using a smooth map with contractible fibres. The author then proposes converses and gives the corresponding claims for $f_!$.

**053 · Four conditions.** The list compares base change for $f_*$, commutation of $f^*$ with $g_!$, the inclusion of each strict fibre in a slice, and the fibres of a functor between colocalized categories. The arrows and the sides of the slice notation matter separately.

**053–054 · A product square.** Two maps of categories of indices, $I\to I'$ and $J\to J'$, give a square of products. To obtain the comparison for this square, the notes identify an auxiliary category $K$ with a product and specify an initial object as the reason for contractibility. Several indices in the product are overwritten.

**054 · A retraction.** After “Ouf !”, the author recalls having used full faithfulness of $f^*$ for a strict hot-equivalence and arbitrary coefficients. He restricts the claim to constant coefficients and tests the stronger assertion on the inclusion of an initial object.

**055 · Definition 1 [D1].** A $\mathbb D$-equivalence induces a fully faithful pullback on the constant coefficients in $\mathbb D(Y)$. Formula (1) spells out the Hom comparison for two absolute coefficients. Adjunction gives the cohomological test (2), and the analogous homological test is labelled (2′).

**056 · Propositions 1 and 2; Definition 2.** Proposition 1 summarizes the constant-coefficient tests. Proposition 2 gives two-out-of-three. A category is called aspherical, contractile or trivial for $\mathbb D$ when its map to $e$ is an equivalence in this sense. The text gives the unit and counit tests for this condition and a corollary when one end of a functor is contractile.

**056–057 · The HOT example.** The example expresses cohomology by $\mathrm{Hom}_A(\mathrm{hot}(X),M)$ and homology by the product of $\mathrm{hot}(X)$ with $M$. The author concludes that the equivalences for HOT are the weak equivalences. Two remarks on 056 concern a sufficient unit test and functors in a single connected component of Hom.

**057–058 · A second statement labelled Proposition 2.** For a left derivator, the notes relate equivalence after local-isomorphism base change, equivalence of the slice maps, and contractibility of the slices of $X$. A local isomorphism is defined by the induced maps on localizations. The c) to a) argument tests the unit on fibres. A marginal addition proposes further tests with a constant or locally constant coefficient.

**058–059 · The dual list and Definition 3.** The corresponding paragraph uses colocalizations. Definition 3 attaches “stricte” and “costricte” to the two tests and discusses “cofinal”. A slanted note proposes “asphérique” and “coasphérique”. Proposition 3 states composition and one cancellation law for strict equivalences.

**059–061 · Proposition 4 and Théorème 1 [T1].** A cancelled Proposition 4 on 059 precedes a fresh statement on 060. The new argument tests the cohomology comparison for every $F$ using maps from a constant object. Via adjunction this gives the counit $f_!f^*M_Y\to M_Y$, labelled (**). Its fibrewise interpretation yields the three conditions collected on 061. The operators in condition c) are written with alpha-shaped initials.

**061–062 · The axioms.** The author retains Der 1 and Der 2, gives a full-faithfulness axiom for a contractile indexing category as Dér 3, and assigns the existence of the two direct images to Der 4 and their fibre calculations to Der 5. He relates the “ancien axiome” to the preceding theorem and explains why homological hypotheses appear in his cohomological argument.

**062–064 · Théorème 2 [T2].** The theorem compares the strict-fibre-to-slice condition, contractible fibres of maps between colocalizations, and base change for relative cohomology after further base change. Scan 063 adds variants in the margin and crosses out a proposed argument for c″) to a). Scan 064 replaces it with two cartesian squares over $Y_{/y_0}$ and invokes Théorème 1 there.

**064–065 · Properness and smoothness relative to $\mathbb D$.** Corollary 1 points to the dual statement, and Definition 4 names $\mathbb D$-proper and $\mathbb D$-smooth functors. Under the contractible-fibre hypothesis Corollary 2 asserts strict and costrict equivalence, preserved by every base change. Corollary 3 [C3] gives full faithfulness of pullback on all of $\mathbb D(Y)$ and writes out both adjunction maps.

**064–065 · Stability and comparison over a base.** Proposition 5 treats composition, base change and examples. A proposed Corollary 2′ in the margin of 064 anticipates Proposition 6 on 065: for two categories proper over $S$, or two smooth over $S$, the text passes from a fibrewise $\mathbb D$-equivalence to a $\mathbb D$-equivalence. An asterisk leads to a sideways discussion of the coefficients in this assertion.

# Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence (054, 055, 065).** The quantifier changes twice. Definition 1 tests constant coefficients. Corollary 3 tests every object of $\mathbb D(Y)$ and imposes properness or smoothness together with contractible fibres. This distinguishes the final claim from the stronger recollection retracted on 054. The comparison uses the explicit hypotheses in the three passages.
- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence (060–062).** For citation purposes the chronology matters. The live calculation at the foot of 060 cites Der 4 (d). The table on 062 assigns existence to 4 and fibre calculation to 5, after “Je vais renuméroter” on 061. They can be cited accurately by identifying which list the author is using.
- **R3 · Model remark, low confidence (063–064).** The replacement proof appears to avoid the essential-surjectivity step by applying the hypothesis with a coefficient already on the slice. This compares two identifiable source categories. Part of the earlier argument is heavily crossed out, so the precise reason for abandoning it remains uncertain.
