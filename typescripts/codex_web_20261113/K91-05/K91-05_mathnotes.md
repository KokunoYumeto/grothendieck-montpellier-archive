# Mathematical notes on cote 91, scans 59 and 61–72

## What the pages do

**59 · Passage to an étale object.** The page begins in a sentence about a map \(T\to f_*^{\mathrm{ét.}}(T)\). It compares the Hom objects for \(T\) and its target \(\bar T\), then tests the claim after base change. Taking \(S'=S\times\underline I\) gives surjectivity and, with the inserted condition that \(T/S\) be finite, geometric injectivity. The converse starts with a finite flat \(T\), an étale target and a surjective, geometrically injective map. Its Hom comparison concerns unramified \(S'\). The opening words and final conclusion are among the least legible on the sheet.

**61–62 · Local functors and examples.** “De nature locale” is given three conditions. Open immersions stay open immersions, and the functor respects unions and binary intersections of opens. Base extension along \(S'\to S\) is the first example. A prescheme in commutative rings gives another, using a sheaf of local rings and a local map on stalks. The next example places a locally free algebra and a universal homeomorphism in both earlier cases. An augmented example requires a surjection onto \(\underline O_T\) with nilpotent kernel.

**62–63 · The object that represents the Hom functor.** For a prescheme \(\mathfrak X\) over \(\mathbb W(S)\), the question is whether \(T\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb W(S)}(\mathbb W(T),\mathfrak X)\) is representable. The proposed solution consists of \(X/S\) and a map \(\alpha:\mathbb W(X)\to\mathfrak X\). Composing \(\mathbb W(u)\) with \(\alpha\) must give a bijection for test objects \(T\). Locality reduces the test to affine \(T\) over an affine open of \(S\). The notation for the adjoint is \(\underline{\mathbb W}^{a}\).

**64 · Products and change of base.** The product formula identifies the object for \(\mathfrak X\times_{\mathbb W(S)}\mathfrak Y\) with the product of the two representatives over \(S\). The text extends the assertion to finite inverse limits and mentions terminal objects, fibre products and kernels. Formula (ii) compares a representative after changing the base to \(T\) with the representative for \(W_T\). The last sentence applies (i) to group and ring structures.

**65–66 · The generating ring prescheme.** Where the polynomial line has a representative, it acquires a ring structure. The notes compare it with the ring prescheme of Example II and with \(V(\check{\underline A})\) in the finite locally free case of Example I. For a closed subscheme given by polynomial sections, the representing object is obtained as the inverse image of the zero section under \(\Lambda^r\to\Lambda^s\). Scan 66 distinguishes the equation index \(j\) from the variable index \(i\). The examples include a power of the additive group and a general linear group with doubtful size.

**66–67 · Changing the functor.** A morphism \(\mathbb W'\to\mathbb W\) supplies a base change \(\mathfrak X'\) of \(\mathfrak X\). Pullback gives the Hom arrow (*). The components at \(T\) and \(S\) then give the map used in (**). Composing the arrows gives the displayed morphism between representatives, when they exist. The next sheet calls the resulting assignment a bifunctor, covariant in \(\mathfrak X\) and contravariant in \(\mathbb W'\).

**67–69 · The affine existence criterion.** On an affine open of \(S\), assume that \(\mathbb W\) sends affines to affines and that the functor of rings \(B\mapsto\mathbb W(B)\) is represented by the algebra \(\Lambda\). For a finitely generated coordinate algebra, the polynomial construction gives a closed subscheme of an affine power. The accompanying corollary counts \(sr\) generators from the two numbers \(s\) and \(r\). For a general algebra \(C\), scan 69 writes it as a direct limit of finite-type subalgebras \(C_i\). Its four-line Hom calculation leads to \(\operatorname{Spec}(D)\) as the representative. A revised corollary discusses finite type and closed immersions. Its qualifying words run into the left margin; an earlier formulation below it is cancelled.

**70 · Representing an open.** In Lemma 1 the primed open is the inverse image of \(\mathcal U\) under the diagram’s map \(\mathbb W(X)\to\mathfrak X\). Condition \((\alpha)\) asks for an open \(U\subset X\) such that, for every indicated test map \(f\), the image of \(\mathbb W(T)\) lies in \(\mathcal U'\) exactly when it lies in \(\mathbb W(U)\). The conclusion uses the restriction \(\mathbb W(U)\to\mathcal U\). The notation on the last line raises a reading question below.

**71–72 · Gluing the representatives.** Lemma 2 assumes representatives for the open pieces and uses Lemma 1 for pairwise and triple intersections. Condition \((\beta)\) asks for an open cover of each test \(T\) whose separate images land in the chosen pieces. On scan 72 the same overlap appears as \(X_{ij}\) in one piece and \(X_{ji}\) in the other. The diagram compares the direct transition with the composite across three pieces. The text identifies the restricted maps with the identity on the object representing the triple intersection, forms \(X=\bigcup X_i\), and uses the local maps to define \(\mathbb W(X)\to\mathfrak X\). The next sentence begins « La » at the lower edge.

## Questions and conjectures

- **59.** The parenthesis after the Hom isomorphism reads « Préciser » or « Précisions », followed by points. The particular clarification intended is unclear.
- **61.** The slanted note includes « cette hypothèse » and may ask about making it « de suite ». Its little hook also admits « là ».
- **63.** « se demande s’il est représentable » is the explicit question about the functor (*) introduced at the foot of 62.
- **72.** « Il faut vérifier pour cela la … commutativité » identifies the compatibility needed before gluing. The sentence « Cela tient au fait que » introduces the answer on the same sheet.

No separate conjecture is labelled on these pages.
