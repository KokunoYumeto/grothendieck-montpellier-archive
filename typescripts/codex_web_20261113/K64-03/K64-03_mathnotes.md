# Mathematical notes on scans 28–39

## What the pages do

**Scan 28, (15)–(19).** Grothendieck uses the trace pairing on \(\operatorname{End}(M_0)\) to distinguish the two summands \(M_0\) and \(\mathbb F_2^{\underline\omega}\). He passes from the torsor \(P_M\) to a pair of torsors, one for each summand. The theorem describes a free rank-two module \(M\) over \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) by its reduction \(M_0\) and these two torsors. The corollary compares their automorphism sequences and gives the congruence-kernel map \(u\mapsto\mathrm{id}_M+2u\).

**Scan 29, (20)–(22).** The first torsor and its translation space are encoded by a four-element set \(I\). A two-element set \(C\) and an \(\mathbb F_2^C\)-torsor give a combinatorial square \(Q\), with the torsor as its vertices and \(C\) as its codiagonals. The remaining datum \(k\) identifies the orientations of \(I\) with those codiagonals. The resulting triple \((I,Q,k)\) gives the Cartesian square for the automorphism group.

**Scans 30–31, (23)–(27).** Two diagrams put the general and special linear groups into exact sequences. The kernel of the map from the general linear group to the symmetry group of \(Q\) is identified with the commutator subgroup of the special linear group. Grothendieck describes the latter group’s abelianization as cyclic of order four. For the general linear group, the indicated abelianization is a two-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\). Display (27) identifies the common scalar centre \(\mathfrak z=\{1,-\mathrm{id}_M\}\).

**Scan 32, (28).** The subgroup diagram records inclusion indices and five Cartesian squares. Above it, three two-element sets are related to the characters \(\mathrm{sg}\), \(\mathrm{sg}\det\) and \(\det\). Below it, Grothendieck specifies the choice needed for the indicated product decompositions, a trivialization of \(P_M\).

**Scans 33–34, (29)–(33).** Vectors with nonzero reduction, modulo multiplication by \(\{\pm1\}\), form the vertices \(\Delta_M\). Reduction to \(J=M_0^*\) divides the six vertices into three opposite pairs. Sections of this map are faces, and partial sections of sizes two and one are edges and vertices. Incidence is extension of a section. The induced action of \(\mathrm{Gl}(M)/\{\pm1\}\) identifies it with the octahedral automorphism group. Here the manuscript gives an argument from injectivity and equal orders. It goes on to identify the central antipodism.

**Scans 34–35, (34)–(38).** The face set is a torsor under \(\mathbb F_2^J\). The nonzero diagonal element acts by antipodism. Display (36) identifies \(P_M/\mathfrak z\) with the face set. The map in (37) sends a triple of lifts satisfying the zero-sum condition to the face formed by their classes modulo sign. Display (38) compares the two automorphism sequences over \(\mathfrak S_J\).

**Scans 35–36, (40)–(44).** Three two-element sets are distinguished. \(C_\Delta\) records the two tetrahedra inscribed in the associated cube, equivalently the two face colours. The other sets record the orientations of the octahedron and those of its four face directions. Their contracted product is trivial. The manuscript describes \(C_\Delta\) as the quotient of the face torsor by \(M_0\) and relates the three sets to \(\mathrm{sg}\det\), \(\det\) and \(\mathrm{sg}\), respectively.

**Scans 36–37, the two displays (45).** Passing to antipodal pairs of faces gives four bifaces. The first display obtains the oriented octahedron \(\Delta^+\) by twisting with the orientation set and gives the reverse twist. The second describes how its orientations, face directions and face colours transform.

**Scans 37–38, (47)–(48) and the return to (23).** Recovering \(M\) from the unoriented octahedron leads to lifting a torsor through the central quotient of \(\mathrm{Gl}(2,\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)\). The square description reformulates the problem as lifting through the dihedral group of a square, given its two sets of diagonals and codiagonals.

**Scan 39, (49)–(50).** The recap lists the octahedron, its four antipodal pairs of faces and the square with vertex set \(P_M/M_0\). Grothendieck says the first two depend only on the projective line \(X_M\), while \(Q_M\) carries further information. Display (50) matches the codiagonals, diagonals and orientations of \(Q\) with the corresponding sets of the octahedron.

## Statements left without proof

- **Scan 28.** The classification functor « est une équivalence de groupoïdes ». The margin relates this to group and module isomorphisms. These sheets give no construction of a quasi-inverse.
- **Scans 29–30.** The square description yields « une équivalence can. de groupoïdes ». The subsequent proposition says that the indicated kernel « est aussi égal au groupe des commutateurs » and that the special linear abelianization is « cyclique d'ordre \(4\) ». Both conclusions are stated here.
- **Scans 31–32.** For the map in (26), « induit un isomorphisme » is followed by the formula. For (28), « toutes les flèches sont injectives, et les cinq carrés qui y apparaissent, donc aussi les composés, sont cartésiens ». Individual square-by-square verifications are not given.
- **Scans 35–36.** The identification of the face torsor in (36) is introduced as « un iso. canonique » and the underlying map is displayed. For the three two-element sets, the manuscript says « qui satisfont à un iso. canonique » before (41), without spelling out the identification of their product with the trivial torsor.
- **Scan 39.** After identifying the projective line and its point sets, Grothendieck writes « mais non pas \(Q_M\) ». He gives no example here of two squares distinguished by information lost on passing to the projective line.

## Remarks of the model

**R1. Independent finite count, high confidence (scans 33–34).** I enumerated the six labelled vertices \((j,b)\) with \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Of their 15 unordered pairs, three lie above a single \(j\) and 12 join distinct fibres. Exactly eight unordered triples meet every fibre once. These counts agree with the manuscript’s 12 edges and eight faces. The enumeration is recorded in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

**Reading question, scan 39, item (49)c, low confidence.** In the zero-sum expression the frozen readings of the last index are \(h\) and \(k\). Its ascender is clear, but a separate arm of a \(k\) is hard to distinguish from the returning pen stroke. The face construction above uses the indicated zero sum and does not decide the index.
