# Mathematical notes · cote 86, scans 18–33

The symbols below name objects in the manuscript. Quotation marks enclose its French wording. The checks labelled C1–C7 and R1–R2 are computations or comparisons made for these notes. The other paragraphs report the argument on the sheets.

## What the pages do

**Scans 19–20 · Directions and distinguished trihedra.** The antipodism identifies opposite edges. On the fifteen resulting directions the author constructs a permutation \(\rho\). Starting with an endpoint of an edge, he takes the side opposite the other endpoint in the surrounding pentagon. Starting at the other end gives an antipodal edge. The construction therefore passes to directions. The three successive directions form an orbit, called a *trièdre distingué*. The five orbits make up \(T\).

**Scans 20–21 · Orthogonality and the five edges at a vertex.** Distinct directions in the same trihedron are called orthogonal. For \(a=\{s,t\}\) the manuscript identifies four orthogonal edges and observes that none meets \(a\). Sending an edge at \(s\) to its trihedron gives \(P(s)\to T\). It is a bijection by injectivity and the common cardinal five. Rotations fixing \(s\) act on this set as on a pentagon.

**Scans 21–23 · An orientation on \(T\).** Numbering the vertices of a polygon of rank congruent to one modulo four by an épinglage gives an orientation independent of that choice. Each vertex \(s\) of the icosahedron consequently supplies a pentagon and an orientation \(\omega_s\) on \(T\). Two frame generators fix \(s\); the third fixes another vertex of the face. The action of the whole group on \(T\) is even. Equivariance of \(\omega_s\) and transitivity on vertices show that all the \(\omega_s\) agree.

**Scans 24–25 · A face model for \(T\).** For a face \(f=\{u,v,w\}\), the directions along its three sides distinguish three trihedra. The two cyclic orders of the face distinguish two others, constructed from the neighbouring faces. The five are distinct, giving \(T\simeq f\amalg\mathrm{Or}(f)\). The face stabilizer acts on the vertices of \(f\) and acts on the two orientations by its sign. This provides the factor six in the order of the image.

**Scans 25–26 · An edge model for \(T\).** For an edge \(a=\{s,t\}\), the third vertices of its two incident faces give a pair \(\varphi\). The four choices in \(a\times\varphi\) give four distinct trihedra, different from the one containing the direction of \(a\). The resulting description exhibits the action of a group of order four. The author combines this with earlier divisibility information to identify the image of the full group. See C1.

**Scans 26–27 · The group action.** The theorem gives the image of \(G=\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)\) in the permutations of \(T\) as \(\mathrm{Alt}_T\), and the kernel as the subgroup of order two generated by the antipodism. Since the antipodism reverses orientation, the author writes \(G\simeq\mathfrak z\times G^+\) and \(G^+\simeq\mathrm{Alt}_T\).

**Scan 27 · Reconstructing vertex pairs.** The new heading starts the reconstruction from the oriented five-element set. There are twelve undirected pentagon structures on \(T\), with six inducing each orientation. A pair of antipodal vertices gives a pentagon of the chosen orientation. Equivariance and transitivity give a bijection between antipodal vertex pairs and this set of six pentagons. See C2.

**Scan 28 · Reconstructing edge and face pairs.** The edge construction chooses an element of \(T\), a square on its four-element complement and an antipodal class of sides of that square. Thirty choices separate into two classes of fifteen according to the orientation induced on \(T\). Antipodal edge pairs correspond to the selected class. The manuscript also matches antipodal face pairs with the two-element subsets of \(T\). See C3 for the total of thirty.

**Scans 29–30 · Incidence in the quotient.** The vertex, edge and face objects are a pentagon \(\pi\), a marked square, and a pair \(\Omega\). Clause a) locates the square using the vertices neighbouring the distinguished vertex of the pentagon. Clause b) treats the square and the pair; the account is heavily cancelled. The NB on 30 distinguishes two square structures by their induced orientations. Clause c) makes vertex–face incidence a diagonal condition in the pentagon.

**Scans 30–31 · Flags and enumerations.** Clause d) gives the data of a flag. The marked vertex lies outside the diagonal \(\Omega\) and is adjacent to just one of its endpoints. In the inverse description \(t_0=\tau\), \(t_1\) is that adjacent endpoint, and \(\Omega=\{t_1,t_3\}\). An oriented enumeration determines the flag. The count on the sheet gives sixty choices. See C4.

**Scans 31–32 · The oriented objects.** Scan 31 asks how to recover three tilded families. Scan 32 uses an oriented set \((E,\omega)\) with five elements. Two diagrams show which parts of a flag are forgotten. A table places pentagons, squares, triangles, their marked versions and total orders against the symbols for vertices, edges, faces and flags. The lower rows match tilded objects with cyclic permutations, oriented squares and oriented triangles.

**Scans 32–33 · Generator formulas.** The three \(\sigma_i\) permute a displayed ordered list of five entries. The last sheet gives two products called \(\rho_f,\rho_s\) and a third called \(\sigma\). All three rows, including the last product's index, are retained as written. See C5–C7 and R1.

## Remarks of the model

- **C1 · High confidence.** Using the orders invoked on 25–26, the least common multiple of \(4,6,10\) is \(60\). On five letters \(|\mathrm{Alt}_T|=60\), and \(120/60=2\). This checks the numerical part of the image and kernel argument.
- **C2 · High confidence.** Enumerating the cyclic orders of five distinct letters up to reversal gives twelve pentagons. Grouping the underlying orders by parity gives six pentagons of each orientation. The enumeration uses only the oriented five-element set of 27.
- **C3 · High confidence.** Enumerating the distinguished letter, a square on the remaining four letters, and a pair of opposite sides gives thirty triples. This checks the total on 28. The enumeration did not assign an orientation to each marked square.
- **C4 · High confidence.** For each of the six pentagons of one orientation, each of its five diagonals has exactly two eligible vertices \(\tau\). Enumerating the flags meeting the condition of 30 gives sixty.
- **C5–C7 and R1 · High confidence.** Composing the explicit five-entry maps on 32 gives index orders \(4,1,3,2,5\) and \(2,3,4,5,1\) for the first two products on 33. For the third, the left and right sides disagree as recorded under Slips. Both \(\sigma_0\sigma_2\) and \(\sigma_2\sigma_0\) give the displayed right side.
- **R2 · Medium confidence.** Keeping the two descriptions of \(T\) apart helps identify the stabilizers. On 24 the natural decomposition has parts of sizes three and two, whereas on 26 it has parts of sizes one and four. This is the internal evidence for the suggested edge subscript on 26.

### Reading questions retained after collation

- **Scan 19, the two pairs with suffixed π, and scan 21, first proof.** The frozen live signs are lower-case \(\pi_{s,u}\) and its companions. The high double uprights could also represent capital \(\Pi\) with indices. The distinction matters when looking up the associated vertex names. Individual overwritten layers are marked in the transcription.
- **Scans 19 and 21, local family \(\mathscr P_{\ge s}\).** The first occurrences carry the additional candidate \(\Phi\). The succeeding unmarked instances preserve the original rendering; the loop crossing the stem is hard to distinguish from the returning stroke of a script P.
- **Scan 30, last two lines.** The frozen word after «est le» was recorded as *contingent* or *côté-gauche*. The further candidate *codiagonale* follows the prominent medial descender well, but the compressed vowels do not settle it. No conclusion about the author's terminology rests on this word.

- **Scan 30, left flag diagram in d).** The two strokes from the middle object end at the outer objects S and F. A post-collation check found the corresponding TikZ-CD arrows address empty cells immediately beside them. The audit ends with a tested, machine-applicable typesetting erratum for these two offsets. The hashed transcription was retained.
