# Mathematical notes · K157-3-19

## What the pages do

**The base-change question (282–283).** A factorization argument for maps of objects fibred over S and its dual give rise to a question in Cat. If the first map is a W-cofibration, is an ordinary cocartesian square on it W-cocartesian in the older sense? The proposed reduction factors the other map through an open immersion of Dwyer. It isolates three cases, called a), b) and c).

**The comparison category (284–285).** For b), the square is enlarged by the integral of its three-object corner. Proposition 17 tests whether a square Q is W-cocartesian by the inclusion of that integral into the integral of all of Q. The proof compares the inclusion with the map to the final corner Y. The triangle is explicitly noncommutative and carries a comparison arrow α. The reference to a lemma on page 2 enters here.

**Pasting (286–289).** The first Prop. 18 assumes W(7 bis). For two adjacent squares with the first W-cocartesian, it equates the conditions on the second square and the outer rectangle. Three maps φ and three maps ψ describe the relevant inclusions. Four asphericity checks treat the vertical comparisons. A cover by two closed subcategories treats the remaining condition. The corollary returns to the question on 282 and allows either of the two maps out of the initial corner to be a W-cofibration.

**Fibre and cofibre axioms (289–290).** The factorization in Cat leads into section 7. For a weakly saturated W, the left factorization puts a W-map first and a W-fibration second. The right factorization puts a W-cofibration first and a W-map second. The surrounding qualifications concern the final object, fibre products and the objects that are W-fibrant.

**Two interpretations of one pullback (291–292).** In a cartesian square with one structural map in Fib_W, Y represents a homotopy fibre over either of the two objects above S. For the second interpretation he factors the map from X₂ to S, forms the two cartesian squares and compares Y with barred Y. He then draws both the original fork Ψ and the enlarged diamond to express the two interpretations at once.

**Localizing diagrams (293–294).** For a category I, he localizes M(I) at the arrows that lie in W at every i. The diagram over S gives ξ, and its completed square gives an extension with a tilde. He asks for a functor i_* implementing the operation. After fixing S he introduces the localized category of diagrams in M over S, with shape Φ, and a functor that adjoins the vertex S.

**The product functor (295–296).** A pair of W-fibred objects over S is sent to its product span. The effect of a pair of W-maps can be factored by changing one component at a time. The second heading numbered Proposition 18 states existence and uniqueness up to unique isomorphism for π, with a localization square commuting up to isomorphism. Its value on arbitrary X₁, X₂ is described using barred W-fibred replacements.
