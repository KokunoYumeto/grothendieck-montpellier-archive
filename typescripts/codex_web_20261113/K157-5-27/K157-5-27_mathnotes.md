# Mathematical notes on manuscript pages 333–348

## What the pages do

**U1. The end of the proof of 4.13.15 (scans 421–425).** The opening argument factors a cofinal functor through \(\underline S(\pi)_{/x}\). A second presentation compares membership of \(x\) in \(\underline S\) with cofinality of the indices whose terms belong to \(\underline S\). In one direction, restriction to those indices expresses \(x\) as a limit of objects of \(\underline S(\pi)\). The reverse direction calls for cofinality of an inverse image. The author temporarily admits that step, then invokes 4.13.17 with \(\alpha_\pi\), the least ordinal of cardinal \(\pi\). Several implication labels are overwritten.

**U2. The cancelled corollary and its replacement, 4.13.18 (scans 425–427).** The draft on 425 gives way to a corollary using a limit ordinal \(\alpha_0\) and its ordered category \(I_{\alpha_0}\). The stability condition \(\mathrm L(\alpha_0)\) concerns \(\underline S(\pi)=\underline S\cap M_\pi\). The corollary compares accessibility and stability of \(\underline S\), stability of its small part, cofinality in the slice over \(x\), and cofinality of selected indices of an adapted presentation. The ordered-index variant \(c'\) carries extra hypotheses. The diagram on 427 locates the two implications for which the author invokes the key lemma.

**U3. Lifting stability from the small part, 4.13.19 (scans 428–429).** The lemma starts from a \(\pi\)-accessible inclusion and assumptions about \(I_\alpha\)-limits and a family of allowed indexing categories. It concludes that \(M\) and \(\underline S\) admit the indicated inductive limits and that the inclusion preserves them. The proof applies 4.11.1 separately to the two Ind descriptions. Preservation by the inclusion motivates the next lemma. The family sign is read provisionally as \(\Phi\) throughout these notes.

**U4. Extending a functor, 4.13.20 (scan 429).** A functor \(f_0:C\to C'\) between categories equivalent to small ones induces a functor between their \(\mathrm{Ind}_\pi\) completions. Under the stated hypotheses on \(\Phi\), including \(I_\alpha\in\Phi\), preservation of the indicated limits by \(f_0\) passes to its extension. A slanted note proposes a formulation starting from \(f_0:C\to N\).

**U5. Classes of limits, 4.13.21 (scan 429).** The corollary lists all small inductive limits, small filtered limits, small filtered limits with the indicated largeness condition relative to \(\pi_0\), and small sums. The corresponding hypotheses specify the indexing categories of cardinal at most \(\pi\) contained in \(\Phi\). The third case was inserted in both lists.

**U6. The general criterion, 4.13.22 (scans 430–431).** A cancelled definition and a live theorem share the number 4.13.22. The theorem introduces a family \(\Phi\) with a cardinal bound on its members and a limit ordinal for which \(I_{\alpha_0}\in\Phi\). The condition \(\mathrm L(\Phi)\) imposes stability on \(\underline S(\pi)\). Clauses \(a,a',b,c\) compare accessibility and closure with slice and presentation criteria. The NB spells out what a \(\Phi\)-\(\pi\)-adapted system must satisfy. Clause \(c'\) restricts the indexing category to an ordered one, with the qualifications given beside it.

**U7. Accessible parts, 4.13.23 (scans 431–433).** A part \(S\) of \(\mathrm{Ob}\,M\) determines a full subcategory \(\underline S\). The definition has three conditions. The first concerns the usefulness of \(\pi\) for \(M\) and the existence of limits of type \(\Phi\). The other two concern \(\pi\)-accessibility of \(\underline S\) and its stability in \(M\). The margin gives special names when a single ordinal type is used or the category sizes are restricted relative to \(\pi_0\). The author prefers clause \(a\) conceptually and clause \(c\) in practice. On 433 he singles out the direction from membership of \(x\) to cofinality of \(I_{\underline S}\).

**U8. Two sets of subcategories, 4.13.24 (scan 433).** The primed set consists of the \(\Phi\)-\(\pi\)-accessible subcategories in \(M\). The unprimed set consists of strictly full subcategories of \(M_\pi\) stable under the specified limits. The proposition restricts earlier inverse bijections to these sets. A parenthesis relates stability under \(I_{\alpha_0}\)-limits to closure under direct factors.

**U9. Intersections, 4.13.25 (scan 434).** Assuming \(I_{\alpha_\pi}\in\Phi\), the body asserts stability of the primed set under intersections of families of cardinal at most \(\pi\). The margin changes the bound to strictly less than \(\pi\) when \(\pi\) is not a limit cardinal. An NB describes how the preceding bijection respects inclusion and intersections.

**U10. Five conditions, 4.13.26 (scan 434).** With \(f:\underline S\to M\) the inclusion, the author separates usefulness for both categories, their stability under the specified limits, preservation of those limits by \(f\), \(\pi\)-accessibility of \(f\), and \(f(S_\pi)\subset M_\pi\). The last source letter's underline is uncertain on this sheet.

**U11. Increasing the cardinal (scan 435).** For \(\pi'>\pi\), the author retains conditions 2–4 and examines 1 and 5. He gives cardinal power conditions for usefulness. For the fifth condition he introduces \(\pi'_1\) using the cardinal of \(\underline S_{\pi_0}\), then \(\pi''_1=\mathrm{Sup}(\pi'_1,\pi)\). The surviving conclusion is \(f(\underline S_{\pi'})\subset M_{\pi'}\). The short qualifier after *Card* is hard to read.

**U12. Proposition 4.13.27 (scan 436).** The proposition combines the size bound and \((\pi')^{\pi_0}=\pi'\) to assert \(\Phi\)-\(\pi'\)-accessibility. It then treats \(\pi_0=\pi\), gives choices \(\pi'=2^c\), and closes with a sufficient bound conditional on the continuum hypothesis explicitly named on 435. The source of \(f\) and the word *adaptée* in the hypothesis deserve separate attention below.

- **423.** « Il semble qu'il faille ... invoquer les conditions de stabilité ». The author asks which stability hypothesis the inverse-image argument needs and takes up the issue on 424.
- **427, 431.** « mais ne ... sont peut être pas équivalentes » and « mais ne sont peut-être pas équivalentes à c') ». Without the additional restrictions, the notes retain implication towards \(c'\) and leave its converse open.
- **429.** « Il vaut mieux ... un foncteur \(f_0:C\longrightarrow N\) ». The marginal note proposes changing the formulation of the extension lemma. The verb after *mieux* is uncertain.
- **430–431.** « il me semble, la forme définitive (?) »; « Il faudrait ... la condition »; « Il y aurait intérêt à remonter cette définition (sans b) ». These concern formulation, the position of the definition, and whether b) belongs in it. Part of the right-edge note is unresolved.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1. Diagram on 427. High confidence.** The seven drawn implications agree with the later phrase « des sept implications ». Two carry an exclamation mark, \(a'\Rightarrow c\) and \(c'\Rightarrow a'\). An eighth, single-shafted stroke leads from the second mark to its conditions. Both the drawn endpoints and the encoded edges were checked. This comparison concerns the number and placement of arrows.
- **R2. Scope of the ordinal assumption on 433. Medium confidence.** The margin says « La condition \(I_{\alpha_0}\in\Phi\) n'est pas utilisée ici ». The body expressly uses stability under limits of type \(I_{\alpha_0}\) in the parenthesis about direct factors. A possible reading is that the marginal statement qualifies the bijection itself, while the parenthetical observation uses an additional assumption. The position of the note does not settle its scope.
- **R3. The last weak bound on 436. High confidence for the comparison, low for an omitted hypothesis.** Earlier on the same page the proposition quantifies over \(\pi'>\pi\). The last line gives a supremum bound with \(\geq\). If its other entry is at most \(\pi\), the weak bound by itself allows equality. The standing assumption on \(\pi'\) can account for the wording.
- **R4. The conditional simplification on 435–436. High confidence for its place in the argument.** The explicit power equation occurs in the unconditional proposition. The preceding discussion invokes the hypothesis expanded on 435 as « hypothèse du continu généralisée » to dispense with that equation. These leaves supply no independent justification for the cardinal-arithmetic implication. The abbreviated hypothesis and the qualifier after *Card* retain their recorded reading doubts.
