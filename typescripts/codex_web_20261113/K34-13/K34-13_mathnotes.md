# Mathematical notes on cote 34, scans 3–17

Grothendieck, A. (n.d.). *SGA 7 : notes manuscrites* [Manuscript], cote 34, archive de Montpellier, scans 3–17.

The scan numbers below refer to the supplied facsimiles. Section and equation numbers reproduce those leaves, including repeated numbers. Quotations retain the French. A cancelled assertion is identified as such. These notes were written after the witness comparison.

## What the pages do

**U01 · The local base and its Galois group (scan 3, §0.1).** The notes start with a trait $S=\operatorname{Spec}V$, its closed and generic points, a generator of the maximal ideal, the residue field $k$, the fraction field $K$, and a separable closure of $K$. The group $\pi$ is the Galois group of that closure. The author introduces the prime $\ell$ and writes the condition that it differ from the characteristic of $k$.

**U02 · Geometric representations (scans 3–4, §§0.2–0.3).** An integral $\ell$-adic representation is given by a continuous homomorphism on a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module. A rational version follows. The first geometric example is the inverse limit of the cohomology groups with coefficients modulo powers of $\ell$. The action is obtained by « transport de structure ». Three settings for the mod-$\ell$ finiteness hypothesis are listed, involving resolution of singularities, properness, and smoothness.

**U03 · The Tate module and degree-one cohomology (scans 4–5, §§0.4–0.5).** For a commutative algebraic group the manuscript forms the inverse limit of its geometric $\ell$-power torsion. It replaces the group by its reduced identity component and gives the rank in terms of its abelian and toric parts. The next formulas identify degree-one cohomology with the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-dual of a Tate module. For proper $X$, the text relates the group with coefficients $\mathbb Z_\ell(1)$ to the Tate module of the Picard group. At the head of scan 5 the twist is moved to the other side as $(-1)$.

**U04 · Néron models and pencils (scans 5–6, §0.6).** The notes announce two directions of comparison between the Galois module of an abelian scheme and its Néron model. For a scheme over the trait the author puts the cohomology of the total space beside those of its special and geometric generic fibres. A morphism to a regular curve leads to the example of a pencil of hyperplane sections and a result attributed to Deligne about the Picard group of a surface in projective three-space.

**U05 · Unramified extensions and strict henselization (scan 6, §2.1).** With a henselian base, the notes identify the largest unramified subextension, the integral closure in it, and the separable residue field of that ring. These supply the strict henselization and the geometric closed point. The bracketed insertion gives names for both schemes. A cancelled version below already attempts the comparison on torsion and roots of unity.

**U06 · Fibres, roots of unity and twists (scans 6–7, paragraph preceding (2.3.1)).** A finite étale scheme provides two fibre functors, compared through the strictly henselian scheme. At the group scheme of roots of unity the comparison becomes an isomorphism of torsion groups. Passing to powers of $\ell$ yields two realizations of $\mathbb Z_\ell(1)$, then their tensor powers $\mathbb Z_\ell(n)$. The comparison relates the actions through the epimorphism to $\pi_0$. A separate, unfinished display on scan 7 carries the same number as the preceding Tate-module display.

**U07 · Inertia and the scope of the seminar (scans 8–9, §2.2).** Inertia $I$ is the kernel of the homomorphism from $\pi$ to the Galois group of the residue field. In the strictly local case, $I=\pi$. The author describes the local monodromy group and draws attention to restrictions on an inertia representation when it extends to $\pi$. A separate « 2.3. » on scan 9 has no accompanying paragraph there.

**U08 · Wild and tame inertia (scans 9–10, §2.4).** The author chooses a Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup $P$ of $I$, with the characteristic-zero convention specified on the sheet. He asserts that it is invariant and gives an exact sequence for the quotient $I_t$. The fixed field of $P$ is the maximal tame extension. Over the unramified field the notes construct the fields $K_n$ by extracting prime-to-$p$ roots of a uniformizer, and describe the finite-stage Galois groups by roots of unity. Their inverse limit gives the product of Tate twists.

**U09 · The induced action and choices (scans 10–11, Remarque 2.5).** Conjugation descends to the tame quotient. The induced action factors through $\pi_0$, and the notes identify it with the action on the Tate-twisted factors. Forgetting the twist requires a choice of a coherent system of primitive roots. The final paragraph explains why the intrinsic expression matters for the action.

**U10 · A dominant morphism of traits (scan 12, (3.1)–(3,7)).** Starting from the primed trait, the author draws the towers of fields, rings, and residue fields, and then the maps between their Galois and inertia exact sequences. The residue-field map is singled out in (3.6). The last diagram displays the primed and unprimed wild, full and tame inertia groups. The several horizontal connections have different degrees of arrowhead definition in the ink.

**U11 · The finite-stage calculation (scans 13–14, (3.8)–(3.11) and Proposition 3.12).** Choose compatible radicals using $t=u't'{}^e$ and compare them via $\Theta_n=\lambda\Theta'_n{}^e$. The notes place $\lambda$ in the unramified ring on the primed side. The action fixes it and takes the primed radical to its product with a root of unity. The comparison sends that root to its $e$-th power. In the inverse limit the induced map on tame inertia is multiplication by $e$.

**U12 · Completion (scans 14–15, Proposition 3.13).** Scan 14 states that passing to the completion gives isomorphisms of the two exact sequences. A long crossed argument and a crossed remark follow. The live proof on scan 15 treats the residue and tame quotients, then surjectivity after tensoring a finite separable extension with the completed field. It concludes with injectivity via descent of finite separable extensions and the behaviour of nearby separable equations. The excellence condition on scan 14 belongs to an earlier cancelled formulation.

**U13 · Open subgroups and indices (scan 16, §3.14).** A subgroup of finite index in $\pi$ is attached to a finite separable extension and a normalized discrete valuation ring. The two displayed formulas concern $I'=I\cap\pi'$ and the $\ell$-part of its abelianization. The passage then relates the ramification index to $[I:I']$ when the residue extension is separable and records division by an inseparability factor in the other case.

**U14 · Normal subgroups and central extensions (scans 16–17, §3.15 and Corollaire 3.16).** Normality of $\pi'$ gives an action on the abelianized inertia subgroup and then on its prime-to-$p$ part. The action on the product of Tate twists is described by transport of structure. The last assertion says that for an open invariant $I'$ of $I$, the extension of $I/I'$ by the specified tame quotient of $I'$ is central.

**Q02 · Scan 16, last line.** A mark after $\pi$, immediately before « opère sur », could be a question mark. Its reading and scope are uncertain. No separate conjecture is stated on the fifteen leaves.

## Remarks of the model

**M01 · High confidence, scans 4–5.** The untwisted and twisted Picard formulas cross a physical page turn. The words « ou si on préfère » and the unchanged Picard term show that the opening display on scan 5 is another form of the final display on scan 4. This observation concerns the continuity of the manuscript.

**M02 · High confidence, scans 9–11.** The product used in Remarque 2.5 is visibly labelled (2.4.2) on scan 9. Discussing the coefficient groups as abstract free modules loses the choice of primitive roots that is explicit on scan 11. The uncertain final digit of the first reference on scan 11 remains a low-confidence reading question.

**M03 · High confidence, scans 13–14.** In the finite-stage equality, the formal monomials $\lambda(\varepsilon\Theta'_n)^e$ and $\varepsilon^e\lambda(\Theta'_n)^e$ coincide. A symbolic check of their ratio gave 1 for integer $e$ and commuting nonzero symbols. This checks the exponent in that single algebraic step. The fixedness of $\lambda$ is an input from the preceding text.

**M04 · Medium confidence, scans 5–6.** The surface statement is introduced inside a discussion of a hyperplane pencil, then worded using a degree bound and an arbitrary ground-field characteristic. These leaves do not specify a genericity condition on the surface. Inferring the full scope of the promised result would go beyond the wording of this draft.

**M05 · Low confidence, scans 16–17.** The changes of numbering, the cancelled beginning 3.16 and the later references could reflect a renumbering between two stages of the argument. The earlier numerals overlap the final ones. The order in which all revisions were made cannot be established from these faces.
