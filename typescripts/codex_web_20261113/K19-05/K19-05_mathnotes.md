# Mathematical notes on cote 19, scans 65–69, 71 and 73–80

## What the pages do

**Scans 65–66.** “Assume moreover” continues an argument already in progress. A diagram connects exactness conditions on $j^!$, $i_*$ and $\varphi$ to a surjectivity assertion. The next sheet tries to show that a kernel $N$ vanishes. The attempt is crossed through. A fresh “Question” receives an underlined “No”, followed by a diagram of modules, localization, restriction of scalars, and the submodule locally killed by elements of $S$.

**Scans 67–68.** The French opening lists quotient and fully faithful functors with additional adjoints. The English argument starts afresh with $C$ and $C'$, puts three conditions in correspondence, and proposes $j^*F=\operatorname{Coker}(i_!i^*F\to F)$. The left margin tries the construction from the opposite end. The inverted, cancelled sheet counts twelve functors. It sets elementary adjoints and composites next to two sequences involving $F$, $U$, $Y$ and $\Gamma$.

**Scan 69.** The first paragraph writes a rightward limit under the pair $(i^*,j^*)$ and beside the components of a triple. The second switches to limits with leftward arrows. The existence and preservation assertion there depends on what $\varphi:C'\to C''$ preserves.

**Scan 71.** From two functors and transformations $\alpha:\mathrm{id}_A\to gf$ and $\beta:fg\to\mathrm{id}_B$, the page puts $\varphi=fg$ and writes $\lambda=f*\alpha*g$. It draws rows built from $\varphi(X),\varphi^2(X),\varphi^3(X)$, introduces a coaugmentation on objects in the image of $f$, and gives a formula for a homotopy. The last diagrams ask what data on an arbitrary $X$ can express the possibility $X\simeq f(Y)$.

**Scans 73–75.** The heading calls transportable faithful functors “foncteurs oubli de structure”. The definition tests a functor over the isomorphisms of the base. The author compares cartesian and cocartesian arrows there and discusses replacing the functor up to equivalence. Scan 74 is a two-line pencil note. Scan 75 relates faithfulness and conservativity to the fibres, then lists four conditions involving a fibration, slice categories and a presheaf $F\in\widehat B$.

**Scans 75–76.** The text distinguishes ordered and preordered fibres and discusses uniqueness of lifting. It sends $b$ to classes of structures of a given species on $b$. Transport along isomorphisms accounts for the part over $B^{\mathrm{is}}$. To recover a category over all of $B$, the author asks for inverse images and writes a functor $\Sigma:B^o\to(\mathrm{ens\ ordonnés})$.

**Scans 77–79.** A cover headed “Liste Problèmes” precedes eleven numbered problems. Open and closed immersions lead to questions about the four functor decorations, dominant morphisms, recollement, smallness, points of topoi and ringed topoi. Some answers are brief marginal annotations. Problems 6, 7 and 11 are crossed, and parts b) and c) of 8 occupy the space above 9.

**Scan 80.** A cancelled passage asks for an endofunctor related to its adjoint. The live example proposes the constant functor with value a zero object $e$. It specifies the arrows between $e$ and $a$, writes $vu=w$, and discusses the possible square of $w$. The last sentence breaks off at “On trouve le”.

## Slips

- **67, b), low confidence.** The quotient has $j_*C'$ where the next line calls the embedded subcategory $C''$. If both phrases refer to that subcategory, the quotient would use $C''$. The English “$j^*$ exist and is exact” would ordinarily read “exists”.
- **71, opening, low confidence (M71-A).** With the two displayed transformations as unit and counit, $f$ is left adjoint to $g$. The visible wording “g adjoint à gauche de f” would then require “à droite”. The word is overwritten.
- **71, first pair of horizontal arrows, low confidence (M71-B).** For $\beta:\varphi\to\mathrm{id}$, the whiskerings $\varphi*\beta$ and $\beta*\varphi$ have source $\varphi^2$ and target $\varphi$. The shafts on the page go from $\varphi$ to $\varphi^2$. One label is overwritten. Reversing the arrows would repair these two types, but the sheet does not settle which part the author would have revised.
- **75, first fibre criterion (M75-F).** Transport over base isomorphisms alone permits parallel arrows in the total category with the same noninvertible image. The correction “assume a fibration and take fibres with at most one arrow between each ordered pair” gives the criterion in M75-R.
- **80, final two lines, high confidence (M80-S).** The alternative $w^2=\mathrm{id}_a$ forces $w=\mathrm{id}_a$ under the written relations. With $w$ an additional arrow, only $w^2=w$ is possible. The two equalities and the “au choix” remain in the transcription.

## Remarks of the model

- **M66-T · Scan 66 · medium confidence.** For the $\pi$-power torsion submodule functor on a discrete valuation ring $A$, the surjection $A\to A/\pi A$ becomes $0\to A/\pi A$. This explains the proposed failure of exactness. The identification uses the diagram's description “biggest submodule … killed by elem. of S” and interprets the single written $\pi$ as the localization generator.
- **M67-E · Scan 67 · high confidence under the abelian hypotheses.** The parenthesis “having two adjoints” does justify exactness of $i^*$. A left adjoint preserves cokernels and a right adjoint preserves kernels. Here $i^*$ is both.
- **M75-F / M75-R · Scans 73 and 75 · high confidence for the literal definition.** Take a base with one nonidentity arrow $0\to1$. Above it put two distinct parallel arrows $r,s$, with only identity arrows in each fibre. Every base isomorphism lifts and the fibres are discrete, yet $p(r)=p(s)$. For a fibration, a cartesian lift of a fixed base arrow factors every lift through the same fibre. A thin fibre makes that factor unique. Conversely a faithful functor has thin fibres. This establishes the qualified criterion with “thin” understood as at most one arrow between any given objects.
- **M75-G · Scan 75 · high confidence under transportability.** If $p$ reflects isomorphisms, every vertical arrow is invertible. Conversely, lift the image of an arrow lying over an isomorphism by an isomorphism with the appropriate endpoint. The remaining factor is vertical. Groupoid fibres therefore imply conservativity under this lifting hypothesis.
- **M80-S / M80-C · Scan 80 · high confidence.** The short check is $w^2=(vu)(vu)=v(uv)u=vu=w$. The five-arrow table using this value is associative. Every Hom set to or from $e$ is a singleton, while $\mathrm{End}(a)$ contains both $\mathrm{id}_a$ and $w$. Thus the constant functor at $e$ is self-adjoint and fails to be faithful on that endomorphism set.
