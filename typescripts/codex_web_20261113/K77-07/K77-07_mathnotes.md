**M06 · Scans 105–106.** The determinant line and $\delta'(q)$ give an equation $\omega^{\otimes(-2)}=\delta'(q)$ for a double cover $\Omega$. The left margin stresses the characteristic condition for étaleness. With the special orthogonal orientation $\omega_0$ the sign is reversed, and the allowed multiples $\lambda\omega_0$ satisfy $\lambda^2=-1$. The author identifies $\Omega$ with $S[i]$ in that case.

**M07 · Scans 106–107.** The conic at infinity is the fixed part of the completed quadric under the antipodal involution $\sigma$. The involution on $\Omega$ also acts on $X^\Omega$, with fixed part $X$. The triangle on scan 107 sets the goal of a canonical isomorphism $X^\Omega\simeq\hat\Sigma$ agreeing on $X$. Removing $X$ then identifies the two open complements.

**M08 · Scans 108–109.** After base change to $\Omega$, the two copies in $\Omega\times_S\Omega$ turn the problem into one on $X_\Omega\times X_\Omega$. The maps $f_\omega$ are required to commute with change of base, exchange the two factors on changing the sign of $\omega$, intertwine that exchange with $\sigma$, and restrict on the diagonal to the given inclusion $i$.

**M09 · Scans 109–110.** An algebra $\mathcal M$ with trace and determinant supplies the model. The conic is described by $\operatorname{Tr}u=\det u=0$ in projective space. The affine surface has $\det u=-1$ in the trace-zero part. On writing $v=u+t1$, its completion is described by $\det v=0$. The map called $F_{\mathcal M}$ relates the product of the two conics to this determinant quadric.

**M10 · Scans 110–111.** The interchange of factors acts through the anti-involution $v\mapsto v'=\operatorname{tr}(v)1-v$. In the coordinates just used, $-v'=u-t1$, giving the antipodal reflection on the projective quadric. The remaining condition b) is attributed to functoriality for $-\operatorname{id}_E$, which changes $\omega$ to $-\omega$.

**M11 · Scans 111–113.** Grothendieck asks whether this construction gives the explicit $g_q$ from § 1. A cancelled trial on scan 112 uses $\omega=i\omega_{\mathcal M}$ and $x\mapsto ix$. On scan 113 he writes $f_{q,\omega}=\theta_{q,\omega}g_q$ and uses the homogeneous-space description $G/T$ to reduce $\theta$ to the two elements represented by $N(T)/T$. The sheet ends before he chooses between them.
