# Mathematical notes on cote 64, scans 104–115

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 104–105, (15)–(21).** Inversion separates functions in $F_n$ into even and odd parts. The operator $\Theta_\delta$ interchanges the signs. The even functions are described on the quotient by inversion. The first odd piece is $F_3^-$, and multiplication by its generator describes the later odd pieces.

**U2 · Scans 105–106, (22)–(27).** Choose a tangent vector $\delta$ and a function $p$ with $\varepsilon_2(p)=\delta^{\otimes 2}$, and put $p'=\Theta_\delta p$. The bases use powers of $p$ and their products with $p'$. Restriction to the three nonzero points of order two has kernel $k p'$ in $F_3$. The proof compares the three values of $p$ after a choice of coordinate on the quotient.

**U3 · Scans 106–108, (28)–(30).** Comparing the leading terms of $p'^2$ and $p^3$ gives a monic cubic relation. Its coefficients are determined by the values of $p$ on $J$. Proposition 2 and its corollaries identify the roots, then use their sum to choose the trace-zero line $W_E$ when the characteristic is different from 3. The corresponding functions are called « fonctions de Weierstrass ».

**U4 · Scans 108–109, (31)–(37).** The cubic presents $F_\infty$ as an algebra in two generators. Inversion fixes $P$ and changes the sign of $P'$. Differentiating the cubic gives the action of the derivation. In the reverse construction a cubic with nonzero discriminant yields a cover of the projective line. The diagram on 109 factors its map through the quotient by inversion.

**U5 · Scans 110–111, (38)–(45).** A nonzero point has coordinates $x_0=p(x)$ and $x'=p'(x)$. For three distinct nonzero points, Proposition 3 equates $x+y+z=0$ with vanishing of the determinant whose columns are $1,p,p'$. A function in $F_3$ with the specified zero divisor gives the linear relation between the columns. The converse uses distinctness to rule out three points in one fibre. The marginal NB explains the repeated-point exception.

**U6 · Scans 112–113, (46)–(51).** For $x_0\ne y_0$, the determinant gives the slope and intercept of the line through the two given points. Substitution in the cubic determines the sum of its roots and hence $z_0$. The added fraction in (51) restores the intercept in $z'$. The initial calculation requires a third point distinct from the first two. The last paragraph invokes continuation to give (51) whenever the two given abscissas differ.

**U7 · Scans 114–115, (52)–(60).** Sending $0_E$ to infinity leaves an affine choice of coordinate $p$. The images $\zeta_i$ of the three nonzero points of order two give the finite branch points and the coefficients of the monic polynomial. When 3 is invertible, requiring $\sum\zeta_i=0$ leaves a scalar freedom.

**U8 · Scan 115, after (60).** Translation by $\varepsilon_i$ descends to an involution of the quotient line. It exchanges $\zeta_i$ and infinity and exchanges the other two roots. The last calculation fixes $c=1$ and gives $b=\zeta_j\zeta_k-\zeta_i(\zeta_j+\zeta_k)$.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 110, high confidence.** Two equal points give equal rows in (42), so the determinant vanishes for any third point. Direct substitution confirms this part of the marginal NB. It identifies where the converse loses information.

**M2 · Scans 109 and 114, medium confidence about the scope.** Over a field such as $\mathbb Q$, a nonzero discriminant alone does not make the three roots individually rational. For example, $T^3-2$ has discriminant $-108$ and no rational root. The statements about three specified elements of $k$ apply to a split triple. These sheets do not state the full earlier hypotheses on $k$.

**M3 · Scan 115, high confidence for the normalized expression.** For $r=\zeta_i$, the transformation $z\mapsto(rz+b)/(z-r)$ squares to the identity. With the value of $b$ at the foot, $r^2+b=(r-\zeta_j)(r-\zeta_k)$ is nonzero for distinct roots. Checking these identities explains why the last expression gives an involution.

**M4 · Scan 115, reading question, low confidence.** The frozen general fraction has $az-b$ over $cz-d$ as first readings, with plus signs as alternatives at both places. The subsequent ratios and the normalized fraction fit the plus-sign convention. In the earlier fraction both signs join the next letter in thick horizontal strokes, so the algebra cannot decide what the writer put there.

**M5 · Scan 104, reading question, low confidence.** The four targets in the middle rows of (17) were transcribed with subscript $n$. The construction below calls its derivation degree $+1$, suggesting $n+1$ as a possible intended target index. Each enlarged target shows a very short subscript. The two additional characters cannot be identified separately.
