## Cross-page comparison

The square-ended set letter recurs on 104–107, 109, 110 and 114. The lower arm of J turns left from a right-hand stem. The witness-stage enlargements give particularly clear instances in the Γ subscript on 104 and in (56) on 114. The same hand sometimes gives an arrow a pointed head (104, the lower inserted map) and sometimes ends a bare shaft in a rounded bead (105, (27), and 108, the two substitutions). A bead alone does not decide an instance. Lower-case p on 105–115 may have a long looped descender. Upper-case P on 108 normally has a straighter stem. In both forms a prime can be detached.

At the end of 108 and on 111 the writer uses two horizontal strokes for a form of même. The same shape remains equality between mathematical expressions. The detached marks in these leaves serve several purposes. A raised stroke beside a letter can be a prime, while a low dot after the end of an abbreviation belongs to the text. These twelve leaves contain no passage-length quotations.
