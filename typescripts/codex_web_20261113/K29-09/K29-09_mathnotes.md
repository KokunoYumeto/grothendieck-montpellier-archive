# K29-09 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 141–142 · Minimal pointed bundles.** For a pointed connected reduced base and a category of finite flat groups, the notes attach a class set to each group G. Proposition 4 says that a minimal class α distinguishes homomorphisms out of G by their effect on α. The proof compares two maps u and v, forms a fibre product over the object induced by (u,v), and identifies a pointed bundle for their equalizer. The two triangles on 142 compare the marked sections after a change of base.

**Scan 143 · A fundamental pro-object and two examples.** The class assignment takes products of groups to products of sets. This fact and a minimal condition on subgroups are used to describe a pro-object with epimorphic transitions. The first example recovers the fundamental group from ordinary finite groups. The second starts with a scheme over a field and a category of finite group schemes over that field.

**Scans 144–145 · A cover and a compatibility calculation.** The SGA cover bears the year 1960. On the following sheet, projections indexed by pairs among 1, 2 and 3 relate three levels of primed groups. Three equations involve inner automorphisms. Iterated inverse images of E are set alongside the inverse images under the composite maps. The bottom diagram identifies the two paths whose equality is boxed at right.

**Scan 146 · Hilbert corollary on a reused typescript.** For a polynomial P and an ideal in a polynomial ring over k, a power of P lies in the ideal exactly when P vanishes under every indicated homomorphism into the algebraically closed extension killing that ideal. The paragraph invokes the Jacobson property and a preceding proposition.

**Scan 147 · Normalizing path data.** The manuscript chooses images of points under diagonal maps and discusses taking certain paths to be trivial. A displayed equation sets an element equal to its square and then to 1. The normalized condition is labelled b′). The sketch below puts three R objects above the diagram of primed bases.

**Scan 148 · Uniform completion on a reused typescript.** The proposed completion consists of minimal Cauchy filters. An entourage V gives a relation on pairs of filters sharing a V-small set. Four numbered paragraphs verify conditions on the resulting relations. The separation argument begins by building a filter less fine than both given filters. The page ends partway through that argument.

**Scans 149 and 151 · A fibre product of fundamental groups.** The remark on 149 gives a canonical map for X and Y over S, with a proper separable X having universally connected fibres. It asserts surjectivity and gives a sufficient condition for bijectivity using a section after change of base and injectivity on fundamental groups. The field case appears below. The sketches on 151 compare kernels, the image of the fundamental group of a geometric fibre, and the two fundamental groups over S.

**Scan 150 · Bourbaki correspondence.** The mathematical secretariat asks A. Grothendieck to report typographical or scientific errors in his circulated exposé before the definitive library edition is printed. The form names exposé 221. Both printed reply choices remain visible. In the other writing direction the sheet carries a base-change sketch.

**Scan 152 · Sheaves and a group action, cancelled draft.** The sheet starts with a group π acting on sets. It relates a sheaf F to E by evaluation and describes F(U) with an equivariant Hom. The following lines treat the slice at T and a decomposition of U into orbits. They compare global sections with invariants and sheaf cohomology with group cohomology. All these lines belong to the cancelled draft.

**Scans 153–154 · Finite étale morphisms of constant rank.** The proposition compares an étale morphism of constant rank n, its trivialization after a faithfully flat base change, and its presentation as a quotient P/H of a Galois cover. Two variants specify a Galois cover and the permutation group with a point stabilizer. The construction removes the tuples with nontrivial inertia from the n-fold product. It counts tuples with distinct entries, then factors the last projection to obtain P/H → X.

**Scan 155 · Étaleness of a quotient.** A transitive action on connected components reduces the discussion to a component and its stabilizer. After the paragraph about an action without inertia, the remaining question is whether X/G is étale over Y. The sketches compare residue fields and completed local rings. The corollary below relates étaleness at x to étaleness at its image under an étale Y-morphism.

**Scan 156 · Geometric fibres and descent.** The continuation compares the geometric fibres of P/H and X. It relates the numbered conditions from 153 and uses the change of base Y₁=P and a quotient of X×G. Local freeness and separability of fibres appear in the last implication. The corollary allows a connected P when X is connected.
