# K72-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 2–3. The object and its realizations.** The cover calls the notes a “dictionnaire”. The first mathematical sheet fixes a dimension $n$, a base scheme $S$ and a universal pinned combinatorial hyperpolyhedron. Its generators with nonconsecutive indices commute. In the projective realization a maximal flag $X_*$ records which flag member each generator moves.

**Scans 3–5. Rigidification.** A list passes through projective, affine, vector and orthogonal descriptions. It specifies when a section, a line or a chosen vector enters the data. In the orthogonal vector description the chosen section satisfies $f(a_0)=1$. Two rows of a diagram record the proposed equivalences and mark where dimension and reflection hypotheses enter.

**Scan 6. Three kernels.** Two diagrams relate actions on $E$, $X$, $V$ and $C$. The writer first shows why the image group $G_0$ acts faithfully on $X$. For the kernel of the action on $V$ he writes $u=\mathrm{id}+\pi'\otimes a$, obtains $a\in V$, and expresses preservation of $f$ as $f(a)\pi'+\psi(a)=0$.

**Scans 7–8. The kernel on vectors.** Assuming $\beta$ invertible, the argument puts $a=\lambda\pi$. The equations for $\lambda$ lead to a proposition over an integral domain, with an exceptional even-dimensional case in characteristic two. A bracketed calculation starts the separate case $\beta=\rho=0$ and breaks off.

**Scans 8–10. The kernel on projective space.** An element acting by a homothety on $V$ gives a scalar $\varepsilon\in\mu_2$. The sheets distinguish $\varepsilon=1$ from $\varepsilon=-1$. Much of the first calculation on scan 9 is crossed through. The subsequent proposition names the identity and the antipodal map and gives its formula for each parity of $n$.

**Scan 11. Duality.** Invertibility of $\rho$ is related to the pairing of $V$ with $V'$. Under the additional hypothesis on $\beta$, the writer identifies the two quadrics with their ambient projective spaces. Passage to the dual reverses the order of the generators, exchanging the indices $i$ and $n-i$.

**Scans 12–13. Twisting by a torsor.** For a fixed combinatorial $\Pi$, the writer restricts to actions that factor through its quotient group and twists $X$ by the torsor $R_\Pi$ of frames. He then fixes the quotient type instead, lets the torsor vary over $S$, and states a product description of the realization category.

**Scans 14–15. From centres to lines.** One description uses strict projective pseudoreflections, their centres $h_i$ and cocentres $H_i$, and a section $s_0$. Another uses lines in a vector bundle and its dual. Successive pairings give a chain of isomorphisms starting at $L_0$. Choosing a basis section of that line rigidifies the tuple.

**Scans 16–17. A canonical basis and comparison functors.** The basis beginning with $a_0$ gives covectors $a_i^\vee$ and a normalization on adjacent basis vectors. The displayed coordinate expressions have separate rows for the two ends. The Scholie and Rescholie compare this description with the projective one. For the return from projective space the writer lifts the generators locally and uses the chosen line to remove the ambiguity of tensoring by an invertible module.

**Scan 17. Invariant forms.** With $\lambda_i=-1$, the writer sets a scalar $c_i$ beneath the value at $a_i$ in the first equation. He gives $\varphi_V(a_i,a_j)=-c_i\langle a_j,a_i^\vee\rangle$ and ends the sheet with vanishing for nonconsecutive indices. The function letter in the first equation is overwritten.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence. Scans 8–9.** In the boxed relations $(1-\varepsilon)\pi=\rho a$ and $\langle a,\pi'\rangle=1-\varepsilon$, the common scalar specializes to 0 at $+1$ and 2 at $-1$. This checks the step to $\rho a=2\pi$ in the live paragraph on scan 9. The sign marked beside the subsequent formula for $u$ is a separate question.

**R2 · Model, high confidence. Scan 16.** The final coordinate row gives $a_n^\vee=a_{n-1}^*+(\lambda_n-1)a_n^*$. Evaluating on $a_{n-1}$ gives 1, as required by the adjacent-pair normalization above. This check uses the clear endpoint row only.

**R3 · Model, high confidence in the scalar calculation, medium confidence about its scope here. Scans 5 and 17.** Over $\mathbf Q$, a nonzero quadratic equation $F$ and $2F$ can define the same hyperquadric. For $F=x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2$ the coefficient lists differ and multiplying the second equation by $1/2$ recovers the first. Any action preserving $F$ also preserves $2F$. Thus the literal one-to-one assertion P9 depends on what data the hyperquadric carries, or how the form is normalized up to a unit. The normalization $f(a_0)=1$ appears on scan 5. Its applicability to this paragraph is left open.

**RQ1 · Reading question, low confidence. Scan 7, last line of the proposition (page-file line 13).** The frozen first reading after $\mathrm{id}$ is a plus, with minus as its alternative. A minus would accord with $\lambda=-1/\widetilde\rho$ directly above. The sign is overwritten and the passage is in characteristic two. Neither observation resolves the ink.
