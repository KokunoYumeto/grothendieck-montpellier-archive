# K10-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 2–3. Covers.** The first sheet names polarisations on tensor categories. The second is marked “Photocopies”. The mathematical text begins on scan 4.

**Scan 4, §1. Adjoint.** A nondegenerate symmetric or alternating pairing $\varphi$ with invertible target $L$ gives an anti-involution on $\operatorname{End}(M)$. The author writes the adjoint using $\widetilde\varphi$ and the dual of $u$, then identifies other pairings with endomorphisms through the second argument of $\varphi$.

**Scan 4, §2. Weil condition.** The requirement is $\operatorname{Tr}(u^*u)>0$ for every nonzero endomorphism. The author draws conclusions about the endomorphism algebra in finite dimension, restriction to a subobject, and semisimplicity of a finite-length object.

**Scans 4–5, §3. Compatibility.** The direct sum of two pairings of the same symmetry type is tested by positivity on each summand and a mixed trace condition on morphisms $M\to N$. The word “compatibles” follows on scan 5.

**Scan 5, §§4–5. Changing the form.** Compatibility is reflexive and symmetric. A marginal example with both cross-Hom spaces zero shows why transitivity fails when the objects vary. On one object the author writes $\psi(x,y)=\varphi(x,\alpha y)$, compares the two adjoints and expresses compatibility by a trace inequality. He obtains equivalence classes of Weil forms on that object. A second marginal note considers adding compatible forms.

**Scans 5–6, §6. Sums and restrictions.** A pairwise compatible family of forms stays compatible on taking direct sums and passing to subobjects. The small addition below the first lines of scan 6 compares compatibility with each member of a finite family to compatibility with its sum.

**Scan 6, §7. Prepolarisation.** For an object of degree $i$, $P(M)$ is a nonempty set of $(-1)^i$-symmetric pairings with values in $K(-i)$. Condition (P1) imposes strict mixed trace positivity within each degree. The proposition characterises the unique maximal enlargement $\widehat P$ by compatibility with $P$. The corollary treats direct sum, restriction and duality with a twist.

**Scan 7, §8. Polarisation.** The degree components of a prepolarisation can be specified independently. Admissibility connects them by a tensor condition (P2) and a Tate condition (P3). The author also writes a twist version of (P3). Several arguments of $K$ and the subscript of the replacement condition have ambiguous strokes. Maximality together with admissibility defines a polarisation.

**Scan 7, §9. Operations.** The list covers direct sum, addition on a fixed object, restriction, tensor product, duality, twist and positive scalar multiples of the scalar form. The inserted items are a′) and e). A marginal note discusses which conditions already give a prepolarisation and which systems are maximal.

**Scan 8, §10. Generators.** One polarising form on each homogeneous tensor generator, together with the Tate objects, determines a polarisation. The generators can be taken in degrees zero and one. In the finitely generated case the author reduces the system to $M^0$ and $M^1$.

**Scans 8–9, §11. Passage to the reals and to finite type.** The first remark compares equivalence classes of Weil forms after real scalar extension and relates them to connected components of a space of nondegenerate forms. The second deduces a correspondence of maximal prepolarisations and finiteness in the finitely generated case. The third passes to a filtered family of subcategories and considers nonemptiness of the set of polarisations. The sentence continues on scan 9. See S2 for the inverse-limit formula.

**Scans 9–10, §12. Complex objects.** The underlying real object carries $J_M$ with square $-1$. On the conjugate object the action is $-J_M$. Rewriting a pairing against $\overline M$ gives a $J_M$-invariance condition on a real form. Hermitian and antihermitian symmetry are compared with ordinary symmetry and alternation. The sum of a real polarising form and its transform under $J_M$ gives the invariant form used on scan 10.

**Scan 10, §13. Implications and examples.** The diagram leads from a polarisation to a conjugate-duality condition for complex objects and then to a duality condition on the original category. Semisimplicity is written alongside the latter two rows. The author gives $\operatorname{SO}(\varphi)/\text{Centre}$ as an example for β without α, specifying an even number of variables and an odd number of negative squares. The example for γ without β is left open.

**Scans 11–12, §14. Criterion on generators.** For tensor words of equal degree the author gives a mixed trace inequality on morphisms between them. For sufficiency he embeds an object in a word, restricts the word’s form, takes the compatible forms and argues that the result is independent of the embedding. Tensor stability follows by combining words. The first lines of scan 12 treat twisting by changing the last index. The signs in the twist arguments on scan 11 are uncertain.

**Scans 12–13, §15. Forms from an element $C$.** The maps $G_m\to G\to G_m$ and an element $C$ with prescribed square give a construction on representations. A form $\varphi$ is tested by the symmetry and positive definiteness of $\varphi^C(x,y)=\varphi(x,Cy)$. Comparing adjoints yields a trace identity for equivariant morphisms. The author discusses tensor products, Tate twists and the scalar form, inserting the hypothesis $\varepsilon(C)=1$. Part c) retains the qualification that some $P(M)$ may be empty.

**Scan 13, §16. Existence and uniqueness.** The author asks which polarisations of $\operatorname{Rep}(G)$ come from such a $C$. He describes ambiguity from conjugation and central points of order two, then shifts the existence question to $G'=\operatorname{Ker}\varepsilon$. He calls a real affine algebraic group “hodgien” if it admits an element satisfying his polarising condition with $C^4=1$ and without a prescribed square.

**Scans 14–15, §17. Maximal compact subgroup.** The centralizer of $C$ preserves positive definite forms on a family of representations. For maximality of its real points the author reduces to showing that $C$ is central in the compact case. When its square is 1, a faithful representation splits into positive and negative summands and the orthogonal involutions on the two are determined. For the second case he considers the action on the two possible conjugates of $C$. Scan 15 excludes a negative multiplier by evaluating a positive form at a nonzero vector.

**Scan 16, §18. Comparing two polarisations.** Conjugation of $C$ transports $\varphi^C$ by the inverse group element in both slots. For the converse in the connected case, the argument compares maximal compact subgroups and reduces the difference to a central element $z$ with $z^2=1$. On a representation where $z$ acts by $-1$ the two forms have opposite signs. The author calls the set of “hodgiennes” polarisations a torsor under $\,{}_2Z(\mathbb R)$. The sentence defining the action breaks off on this sheet.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence.** The change-of-form identity on scan 5 uses the inverse of $\alpha$ on the left of $u^*\alpha$. Substitution in $\psi(ux,y)$ produces this order because $\alpha$ acts in the second slot of $\varphi$. This checks the adjoint identity under the nondegeneracy assumption.

**R2 · Model, high confidence.** The two-term sum on scan 10 is $J_M$-invariant. Applying $J_M$ to both arguments exchanges the two summands because $J_M^2=-1$ and the two minus signs cancel by bilinearity. This checks the invariance part of §12.

**R3 · Model, medium confidence.** In §7 the trace is strictly positive for $u\ne0$. In §14 the displayed test is nonnegativity for all $u$, and the proof later invokes compatibility “par hyp”. An additional reason excluding equality for a nonzero $u$ would account for the difference. These sentences give no separate such argument.

**R4 · Model, high confidence.** On scan 15 the final sign contradiction uses the same nonzero vector on the right and its image under an invertible $g$ on the left. Positive definiteness makes both corresponding diagonal values positive, so a multiplier of $-1$ is impossible.

**RQ1 · Model reading question, low confidence.** Scan 2, title line 2. The frozen reading was “galoisiennes” or “gérinales” and “graduées” was added in the witness check. A further possible reading is “générales”. There are two separate raised marks after the long initial g and a tall upright before the ending. The two marks could be acute accents. The tightly joined middle letters leave that interpretation open.

**RQ2 · Model reading question, low confidence.** Scan 13, Re NB, insertion above “il … suffit”. The frozen reading is “cf. 14”. A second reading is “f. et”, giving “il f. et suffit”. The first group has a long upright and a point, and the last stroke of the second group resembles the final t of “suffit” directly beneath it. This could change a sufficient condition into a necessary and sufficient one.
