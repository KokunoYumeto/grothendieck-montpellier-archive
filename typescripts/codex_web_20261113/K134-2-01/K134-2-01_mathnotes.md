# Mathematical notes · Montpellier 134-2, scans 83–91 and 93–94

## What the pages do

**83.** The sheet carries a single large I/1. It gives no mathematical text.

**84, appendix to Chapter I.** The writer explains how three letters to Larry Breen reached their present form. In the first two he proposed that non-strict n-groupoids model n-truncated homotopy types. The third ranges from duality and classfield theory to stratifications, étale tubular neighbourhoods and an inverse system of homotopy types. “Inverse system” replaces “ordered set” on the sheet.

**85 and 87, cancelled lists.** The first lists 24 sections of *Récoltes et semailles*. The second lists longer notes and locates them by section and note number. Its footnote explains why one long note has two named parts, which accounts for the unnumbered “Refus d'un héritage” after “Mes orphelins”.

**86, notes (12)–(14).** The author retracts two earlier assertions and narrows the universal problem in a third. The fibre products at issue exist only in the underlined C with small round subscript. These are the products the functors should preserve. There is no picture on this sheet from which to reconstruct the preceding counterexample.

**86, notes (15)–(17).** A guess about nonequivalence on groupoids leads into terminology for fibrant and trivial objects. The maps to the final object supply the conditions. The author then identifies a missing uniqueness result for functors and relates it to the existence and uniqueness of test functors into an asphericity or contractibility structure.

**86, notes (18)–(21).** He explains *gerb* through French *gerbe* and the terminology “stack of groupoids”. He rejects one assertion, doubts the proposed size of categories with subscript n, and considers carrying the “primitive” subcategory as extra structure.

**88, note (22).** A Picard category is described as a groupoid with an operation and associativity, unity and commutativity data. Relativization over a space or topos gives Picard stacks. The author relates strictness for the self-pairing of an object to the possibility of using truncated complexes of abelian sheaves in higher degrees.

**88, note (23).** For a sheaf M, X(M) is presented as a left resolution by sums of free sheaves of modules. Its generators have the form underlined Z with superscript (T), where T has the form M to the n. The purpose is to relate Ext with superscript i and arguments (M,N) to cohomology of the induced topos with coefficients in N.

**88 and 90, notes (24)–(25) and (32).** In (24), “triangulated” replaces “derived” in naming Verdier’s provisional notion. In (25), which runs from 88 to 90, the author broadens the interpretation of uniqueness: a choice of coherator enters the theory of stacks of groupoids. In (32) he identifies the problem raised about derived categories with the notion of a derivator.

**89, cancelled draft.** Contou-Carrère’s use of a general base scheme supplies a concrete counterexample to the demand to work only over the complex numbers. The writer says that even beginning over the complexes, the needs of the proofs force more general base schemes. He uses the example to argue for stating hypotheses and giving proofs. The footnote returns to results established using notions that had been left vague.

**90, notes (26)–(28).** Comments attributed to Tim Porter and Ronnie Brown identify a theorem name, relate a structure to crossed modules, and give two literature leads. The question about extending a noncommutative chain complex from length one to two is distinct from the question about tubular neighbourhoods in stratified geometry.

**90, notes (29)–(31).** (29) points to the derivator associated with usual homotopy types. (30) locates an outline of the programme elsewhere. (31) reports a negative answer via rational homotopy types: the author distinguishes the rational cohomology ring of a 1-connected type from all the data needed to determine that type.

**91, Beauville.** The printed abstract connects Siu’s result on K3 surfaces with their periods, the injectivity and surjectivity of the period map, and the cone of Kähler classes.

**91, Chaperon.** The abstract follows the fixed-point problem from Poincaré and Birkhoff through Arnold to Conley and Zehnder’s use of Morse theory. It also points to intersections of Lagrangian varieties.

**91, Dolgachev.** The English abstract announces an account of Nikulin’s non-unimodular integral quadratic forms and applications to surface singularities, automorphisms of K3 surfaces and the topology of real algebraic surfaces.

**91, Duflo.** The orbit method for nilpotent Lie groups leads into harmonic analysis on complex algebraic groups. The abstract reaches Plancherel’s formula and Vergne’s question about the Poisson–Plancherel formula.

**91, Hakim.** The abstract states the existence of nonconstant bounded holomorphic functions in the unit ball of ℂⁿ with radial limits of modulus one almost everywhere. It attributes this result to Aleksandrov and announces other constructions.

**91, Sadourny.** The abstract joins computational work on geophysical fluids with attractors, filtered equations and dynamical approaches to changes in atmospheric circulation or climate. All six abstracts belong to the cancelled seminar programme on the verso.

**93–94, letter to Daniel.** The letter recalls the Bangor papers, correspondence with Breen and a discussion of axiomatic homotopical algebra. The writer argues that foundations for tame topology and a structure theory for stratified spaces deserve priority. He then returns to what the Bangor group might contribute to topological algebra.

**94, proposed fundamental n-groupoid.** The letter associates a candidate fundamental n-groupoid to a space X, modeled at n=1 on Poincaré’s fundamental groupoid. Points, paths and higher homotopies provide its intended levels. The author sketches a correspondence of maps with classes of n-functors, proposes geometric realization in the reverse direction, and relativizes over a topos to propose coefficients for noncommutative cohomological algebra. His last paragraph explicitly calls this heuristic.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence.** The letters on 84 and 94 use the same proposed relation between n-groupoids and n-truncated homotopy types in different ways. Scan 84 calls it an intuition behind earlier letters. Scan 94 tells the reader how points, paths and homotopies would enter and then calls the whole proposal “pure heuristics”. Both qualifications are explicit.

**R2 · Model, high confidence.** Note (25) has a continuous sentence across 88 and 90. The first leaf ends “here has to be understood in”; the next English note sheet begins “considerably wider sense than I expected”. The intervening scan is the cancelled French draft.

**R3 · Model, medium confidence.** The need for an existence and uniqueness statement on 86 and the qualification about choosing a coherator on 90 both limit claims of canonicity. They concern different objects. The sheets provide no implication from one claim to the other.

**R4 · Model, low confidence.** A reading question remains at 87, index item 15. The final transcription has 45 with an overworked a-like suffix, followed on the next line by 45b. The comparison text gives 46a and 46b. The middle digit on the sheet resembles its looped 5 elsewhere, though that form can look like 6. The digit itself was outside the frozen doubt on the suffix. The preceding digit has therefore been left as transcribed.

**R5 · Model, low confidence.** On 94, a small upright with a dot and a short foot lies in the margin left of “At first sight”. The frozen paragraph begins without a marginal character there. The mark could be a fragment of i or 1, or one of several isolated traces left by the copying. It has no obvious point of attachment.

**R6 · Model, low confidence.** On 84, in “under the impetus of the … intuition”, the frozen reading keeps the second *the* live and inserts *new*. The insertion mark points into the space after *the*, but its horizontal stroke also touches the tops of the three letters. A simultaneous cancellation of the article remains possible.
