# Mathematical notes · cote 138, scans 46–59

## What the pages do

**46 · Setting up a covering.** The writer sets $X=C_2$, names a ramification divisor $D$ on $X$, and writes the complement $X^*=X\setminus D$ above an earlier version. He crosses out a sentence about an abelian universal cover. The next paragraph introduces $X_\infty$ by a family indexed by even $n$ and names its group. The family initial and final group subscript are overwritten.

**47 · A tower and its ramification.** The arrows connect three coordinate rings presented as capped Spec expressions. The sheet gives an $n$th power above the middle level and a rational expression in $z$ and $z^{-1}$ at the bottom. Beside them are the indices above $0,1,\infty$, a group expression, several drawings of fibres, and an oval with alternating marked points. Two connectors in the fibre drawing have no clear head.

**49 · Cubic coordinates.** The three primed coordinates are $z^3$, $y(y^2-3x^2)$ and $-x(x^2-3y^2)$ in the written order $z',y',x'$. The author expands their squares, retracts a proposed cube and writes a sixth-degree expression involving $z^2$. The separate equation in $y/z$ and $x/z$ supplies a condition relevant to this last line.

**49 · A rational function.** Beside a point diagram, the page writes $p(x)/q(x)=\lambda$, then $p-\lambda q$ and $p'-\lambda q'$ set equal to zero. The displayed elimination (49C) ends in $p'q-q'p=0$. A quadratic numerator over $z^2$ follows. Its last line stops after a minus sign.

**51 · Parametrization and squaring.** Double-angle expressions lead to the triple $(1-t^2,2t,1+t^2)$ and to evaluations when $y$ or $z$ vanishes. The primed triple $(x^2-y^2,2xy,x^2+y^2)$ is written out and its quadratic relation is expanded. A small diagram has two labels 2, the label $f_n$, and “$n$ impair”. The calculation in $s$ at the foot ends with an unfinished power.

**53–54 · Covering diagram and specialization.** A legend identifies a group with $\mu_n^3/\mu_n$. The larger graph contains objects indexed by $p,q,r,n$, products of groups $\mu$, and lower objects $X_4,X_2,X_1$. At the right the writer specializes to $p=q=r=1$, $n=2$. “TSVP” leads to the next sheet, where a short diagram joins $\widetilde X_2$, $X_4$ and $X_8$. The upper horizontal arrow there has a blank source.

**55 · Powers of a complex coordinate.** Starting with $X=(y^2-x^2)/z^2$ and $Y=-2xy/z^2$, the writer expresses $(X+iY)^n$ using $P_n$ and $Q_n$. He asks for rational primed coordinates, experiments with polynomials named A and B, and groups the real and imaginary parts of a cube under a brace.

**58–59 · Functors out of a groupoid.** The object function $f_0:C_0\to C'_0$ is fixed first. Part a) gives an existence criterion within each connected component of $C$. Part b) chooses $a_i$ and arrows $u_x:a_i\to x$ and assembles transport and automorphism data. A product at the edge of 58 continues as a mapping rule at the top of 59. Part c) gives criteria for faithfulness and full faithfulness. Further conditions distinguish equivalences and isomorphisms.

**52 · Letter.** David Mumford invites a contribution for Oscar Zariski’s 80th birthday. He asks for an early indication of whether the recipient could submit a paper and of its length. The possible second issue and the limit of 460 printed pages explain the two deadlines.

**48, 50, 56–57 · Other sheets.** Scan 48 is a rector’s order about provisional university administration. Scans 50 and 56 are notices of thesis defences, one on diffusion in mixed solvents and one on coprophagous scarabs. Scan 57 is the cover of the groupoid notes.

## Remarks of the model

- **49A and 49B · High for the algebra.** Squaring the three cubic coordinates gives $x^6+y^6+z^6+3x^2y^2(x^2+y^2)$. The difference between this and the author’s last line is $3x^2y^2(x^2+y^2+z^2)$. The later equality therefore holds on the all-plus conic. Confidence in assigning a slip to the handwritten sign between the sixth powers is low: the sign has a tiny right-hand cross-stroke.
- **51A and 51C · High for the algebra.** The primed triple makes $x'^2+y'^2-z'^2$ vanish identically. The rationalized expression for $s$ agrees with $-(t+i)/(t-i)$ wherever the displayed denominators are defined, when the last denominator is read with exponent 2. The ink there still allows 1.
- **55A · High for the algebra.** The real and imaginary numerators under the brace agree with the expansion of $(y-ix)^3$. The brace groups the numerator, so a missing denominator need not be attributed to the writer. This observation does not decide which sign was entered first under the blots.
- **58T · High for the finite example, low for interpreting the crossed index.** Take a groupoid with two objects and exactly one arrow between each ordered pair. Into a one-object group of order two, there are precisely two functors over the fixed object map. Only the transport to the second object is free. This illustrates why the exclusion of chosen base objects matters in the product on 58. The writing beneath that product is overwritten, and the family at the top of 59 is indexed by all $x\in C_0$.
- **58A, 59F and 59E · High for the criteria under the hypotheses on 58.** Comparing objects within a component by the chosen arrows $u_x$ reduces faithfulness to injectivity on a vertex group. The separate Isom-to-Hom condition matters because $C'$ is introduced as an arbitrary category. The last two conditions distinguish having a representative up to isomorphism from having an actual bijection on objects.
