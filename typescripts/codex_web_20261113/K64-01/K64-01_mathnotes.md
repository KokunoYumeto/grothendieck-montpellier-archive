# Alexander Grothendieck · Fourbis combinatoires

## What the pages do

**The affine plane on four points · scans 3–4.** For a four-element set $I$, Grothendieck takes the identity and the three fixed-point-free involutions as the translation group $V$. Its three nonzero elements give the partitions of $I$ into two pairs. The six two-element subsets are affine lines. Sending a line to its direction pairs it with its complement.

**Recovering the points from their group · scans 4–6.** With invertible maps as arrows, forgetting the affine structure gives an equivalence with four-element sets. To recover $I$ from a group $G$ isomorphic to its symmetric group, the notes take the set $I_G$ of Sylow 3-subgroups. This is also a set of splittings on which $V$ acts. A point indexes the unique such subgroup fixing it. Conjugation gives the comparison in the other direction, from $G$ to the permutations of $I_G$.

**Permutations, the cube and orientation · scans 6–7.** The list separates the identity, three translations, eight elements of order three, six transpositions and six elements of order four. Squaring a four-cycle pairs the two inverse circular orders above one nonzero translation. Grothendieck takes the six orders as faces of a cube and $I$ as its four diagonals. For the orientation comparison with the three-element set $J$, he specifies where the distinguished point goes in a total order. Putting it first or last gives the two choices.

**Five descriptions of the square · scans 8–9.** The incidence description uses vertices $S$, edges $A$ and their relation $R$. Either four-element set can instead be given with its antipodal pairs, or as a torsor under a cyclic group of order four. The square is recovered from the vertices by taking the pairs other than the diagonals as edges. Interchanging vertices and edges gives the corresponding construction from the edges.

**The symmetry group of the square · scans 9–11.** The group $\mathfrak D$ of order eight contains two four-element groups $V_S,V_A$ and the cyclic rotation group $\mathfrak D^+$. They share the central half-turn. Grothendieck describes the quotient actions on the opposite-side pairs, the diagonals and the two generators of the rotation group. His two tables distinguish inverse images of subgroups from inverse images of individual elements. He lists three subgroups of order four, five of order two and five conjugacy classes of elements.

**The product of the three orientation choices · scans 11–12.** The pairs of opposite sides form $C$ and the diagonals form $D$. The notes identify the orientations of $S$ with $C$ and those of $A$ with $D$. To orient a three-element set with a distinguished element $a$, a choice $x$ gives the order $(a,x,y)$. Formula $(*)$ trivializes the contracted product of the three two-element torsors $\underline\omega_Q,C,D$. The convention is expressed by $\varpi\wedge c\wedge d=0$ exactly when $\varpi c d=1$ in the symmetry group. The margin warns about interchanging factors.

**Frames and the dual square · scans 13–14.** Here $R$ denotes the frames. A frame modulo the antipode gives a pair $(c,d)$, while an orientation selects one of two complementary classes of frames. A pair of opposite sides selects the reflection in their transverse axis. A diagonal selects reflection in that diagonal. For a frame $(s,a)$, the chosen rotation sends $s$ to the other endpoint of $a$. The last drawing compares moving the vertex $s$ to $s'$ with moving the edge $a$ to $a'$. The two turns are opposite.

## Statements left without proof

- **Scan 3.** “le seul sous-groupe distingué abélien” and “il est son propre … centralisateur” characterize $V$. The quotient is identified with the full group of linear automorphisms of $V$. These assertions are used at the outset.
- **Scan 5.** “Sur l’ens de ses scindages, $V$ opère donc transitivement par autom. intérieurs”. The fixed-vector calculation follows, while transitivity of the action on splittings is invoked directly.
- **Scan 7.** “ce cube est canoniquement orienté, cf plus bas”. The page gives the choices in comparing the orientation sets of $I$ and $J$. It points forward for the statement about the cube.
- **Scan 8.** The incidence system is required to be “satisfaisant les axiomes connus”. The polygon axioms themselves are left implicit.
- **Scans 10–11.** “exactement deux éléments … d’ordre $4$” and “leurs normalisateurs sont resp. $V_S$ et $V_A$” are used in describing the square group and listing its subgroups. The marginal note names the normalizers without a separate calculation.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 4.** “suffirait bien sûr de l’énoncer pour” $\mathfrak S_4$ is followed by “?”. It is a question about the formulation of the assertion that every automorphism is inner.
- **Scans 13–14.** “Pour vérifier … si cette description … correspond à $(*)$” asks whether the geometric description selects the same trivialization. The author specifies the three group elements and then writes “On vérifie que avec ces conventions”. The apparent difficulty with the dual, “on semble obtenir le résultat opposé”, is explained by the opposite turns in the drawing. No further conjecture is stated in these scans.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark · high confidence for the computation · scans 6–7.** For each nonidentity translation $x$, enumeration gives centre $\{1,x\}$ for its eight-element centralizer. Its four-cycle roots generate a single cyclic subgroup of order four; squaring its generators recovers $x$. **Low confidence for the diagram identification.** These two subgroups fit the two sizes on scan 7. The marginal labels, the numeral after Sylow and a cancelled part of the drawing make its literal sign-by-sign interpretation uncertain.
- **Model remark · high confidence · scans 12–14.** In the symmetry group of a labelled square, four of the eight ordered triples $(\varpi,c,d)$ multiply to the identity and four to the antipode. Interchanging any two factors changes the product by the antipode in all eight cases. This gives a finite check of the marginal warning about order. In the last drawing the turn carrying the marked vertex along the top edge is inverse to the turn carrying the top edge to the right edge.
- **Reading question · low confidence · scan 8, TeX line 22.** The insertion has a doubtful crossed *côtés* followed by a doubtful live *côtés*. The writing before *opposés* is overlaid in the same small space. One overwritten occurrence, with an unreadable earlier form, is also possible.
- **Reading question · low confidence · scan 10, TeX line 22.** The struck marginal formula includes tentative divisions by $\mathbb F_2$ after the two powers with exponents $C,D$. Some obliques could belong to the long zigzag cancellation. A reading of either power without a following quotient remains possible.
- **Reading question · low confidence · scans 11–12.** The last display on scan 11, TeX line 36, reads $D$ (possibly $B$), $S$, $\underline\omega_Q$. The preceding decomposition and scan 12 suggest $C,D,\underline\omega_Q$ as the three two-element sets. The first capital is overwritten, and the second looks like $S$. In scan 12, TeX lines 16–17, the first reading of the orientation subscript is $S$, with $Q$ also possible. The main formula uses $Q$. These are unresolved readings.

## Independent recovery reading after the witness check

**Scans 3–7 and 10–14, computational check.** The supplied finite permutation certificate was rerun after the new freeze. The three double transpositions in \(S_4\) each have eight commuting permutations and two square roots; those roots generate one cyclic subgroup of order four. For the square group the centre has order two, five subgroups have order two and three have order four. Four of the eight ordered triples of quarter-turn, opposite-side reflection and diagonal reflection give the identity, and four give the antipode. Interchanging two factors switches the value. The vertex and edge quarter-turns in the last flag example are inverse. These calculations give no further information about the overwritten signs.

**Reading question, scans 10–11.** The lowered \(Q\) in the five orientation signs is attached to the underlined omega. On 11 the three brace-terminal signs admit the frozen alternatives; the finite model alone cannot resolve them. On 13–14 the looped letter denotes the flag set \(\mathcal R\), whereas 8 uses a plain \(R\) for incidence. The later formula reads \(\varpi cd=1\) in the stated order.
