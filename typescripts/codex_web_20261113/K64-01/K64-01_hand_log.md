# Reading the hand in cote 64, scans 2–14

## Scan 2

In “Fourbis” the upright is carried up out of the preceding join. In the subtitle a small final hook can stand for s. The two Zs at the end both have parallel diagonals, although the first has been thickened. The thinner (40) is distinct from the lower-corner foliation. These observations concern these occurrences only.

## Scan 3

The symmetric-group capital has a lip at upper right and a long horizontal return into the lower bowl. It looks like G even where the notation concerns permutations. I has two serifs and J a square lower hook. The opening parenthesis touches the left arm of V repeatedly in the Aut display, making (V look like W. These identifications hold across the two sequences on this leaf. The diagonal marginal writing becomes horizontal on a clockwise turn.

## Scan 4

The long lower loop of f in flèches and affines reaches into the neighbouring line. The underline below inversibles is separate. The lowercase r in Gr is a wave. Above the affine-space functor the equivalence has two wavy strokes.

## Scan 5

The ordinary G and the symmetric-group capital occur on the same line. G is open with a short lower return. The group acting on J has an extra upswept hook and a horizontal return through its bowl. The distinction recurs throughout the page. The very small + strokes over the alternating groups do have short uprights. A dot above i is visible both in the source and target of the element map.

## Scan 6

The star on V at b) has radial pen strokes. The superscripts of the two permutation cosets are simpler + and −. The four slanted notes are written at different angles. The b of cube has a tall ascender at each recurrence. The downstroke of the letter for an order-three permutation is longer than that of his dotted i.

## Scan 7

The double curl at the head of the signature comparison is the underlined orientation letter, unlike the unlined ω in its reference mark. Subscript ab consists of two very small linked letters after I and J. The long horizontal rule at the foot divides the main paragraph from the two diagrams. The bottom map to the unique element has its head at the left end.

## Scan 8

A single short underline is attached to the letter a for the antipodal operation in both quotient captions. The two down-arrows are straight, with heads at D and C. The large crossing stroke attached to the interlinear description of codiagonales is an insertion leader. In the slanting note some word interiors flatten into short straight runs.

## Scan 9

The right-angled capital Γ after torseur differs from the looped R above. The sign high to the left of R′ is shallow. At the foot the long cancellations run through earlier lines of smaller writing. Darker words about the intersection fit between those lines.

## Scan 10

The inner slash and the abutment of two coils mark the group letter; this page often reduces the letter of the preceding leaf to a single broad outer loop. The underline under a in the centre is independent of the equality stroke. In the long oblique addition, each line rises across the main text. Read after rotation. A segment of its pen run is obscured by the horizontal main argument. The diagonal bar on the separate NB crosses both fractions.

## Scan 11

The long horizontal strokes repeating ‘l'image inverse de’ are unlettered rules. D is overlaid with an angular V twice in the upper display. Three superscript blots recur in the next brace and a fourth in the parenthesis. They range from a forked 4 shape to a triangular wedge. The notation may mark a cancellation. The left-margin NB has a separate tall brace; after rotation its prose follows one direction.

## Scan 12

A three-element set has an angular capital, open on the left, with two horizontals and a straight right edge; I and J are difficult to separate for this hand. The barred sign for orientations has two lobes above a horizontal line; the element of that set in the formula looks like a lower loop hanging from a crossbar. The author changes from a product to a sum by putting the new word above the cancellation. The footnote on duality hugs the bottom edge.

## Scan 13

The lower-case c is a single open hook at each of the two horizontal edges. The paired d labels in the other drawing have distinct ascending stems. On the top square the thick horizontal edges and two filled opposite vertices are independent marks. The little o at the top right of Q in Rep can resemble zero. Superscript plus and minus in double quotation marks look like extra strokes but are inside the quotes.

## Scan 14

The short rule between the two R's ends in a slight downward thickening. It may be an arrow without distinct barbs. The small raised circle on the dual square alternates between open and closed. The equal sign before ‘relation’, ‘ensemble’, ‘groupe’ and ‘classe’ is the author's abbreviation. Three occurrences of the opposition of orientations or rotations are underlined; the middle one has only part of the word covered by the rule.

## Independent comparison of repeated hands and signs, scans 2–14

The frame-set sign on 13 and 14 begins with a returning loop and a second internal stem. Its nine appearances on 13 and eight on 14 are encoded as \(\mathcal R\). The incidence relation in the square on 8 has a plain R. The lowered Q belongs to the underlined orientation sign on 10 and 11. The tiny terminal marks of the three brace subscripts on 11 do not all distinguish a numeral from a star.

On 3 a radial mark above V at the line about vector lines differs from the earlier cross-shaped superscript. In the exact sequences on 3 and 5 a swelling at the end of a horizontal bar can resemble an arrowhead. Comparison with the open, two-stroke head on the same display decides individual occurrences. On 7 a doubled contour precedes the numeral 1 but makes only one comparison sign. The ink does not consistently separate a subscript i from 1. The shared rule above the pair of diagrams is a graphic part of the drawing.

The bottom of 10 has oblique prose crossing baseline prose. Once the sloping hand is straightened its repeated par, then the raised t, can be followed across the other writing. This rules out a putative de dévissage on the horizontal line; the two marginal endings are still doubtful.
