# Cote 56 · Mathematical notes

The handwritten quadric sheets, the corrected typescript on algebraic groups, and Antoine Delzant’s signed offprint have separate arguments. Line numbers follow the scan transcription.

## What the pages do

**A table of quadrics, scan 28.** Seven columns, $Q^0$ through $Q^6$, list powers of $\xi$, bracketed pairs involving $\lambda$, and entries written as $\tfrac12\xi^i$. A signed two-by-two array precedes the columns. The sideways sentence ends with a statement about odd dimension. Its opening remains doubtful.

**A central torus, scan 29, lines 1–3.** The passage moves from centrality of $T(k)$ in $G(k)$ to centrality of $T$ in $G$. The insertion uses a closed centralizer containing the $k$-points of reduced $G$ and cites the Nullstellensatz.

**Maximality in a chain, scan 29, lines 4–12.** Proposition 1.17 compares $T\subset H\subset G$ over $S$. The revised conditions say that $T$ is maximal in $G$ precisely when it is maximal in $H$ and the two reductive ranks agree in every fibre. An older conditional formulation is crossed out.

**The geometric Weyl group, scan 29, lines 13–28.** For a maximal torus $T$ in a smooth affine group over an algebraically closed field, the passage takes its centralizer $C$ and normalizer $N$ and forms $W=N/C$. It names $W(k)$, uses conjugacy to compare maximal tori, and starts a comparison after an algebraically closed extension. The page ends at “est canoniquement isomorphe”.

**Two cohomology rows and their classes, scan 30.** Above the diagram is a separate pencil tensor-product expression. In the ink diagram the two cohomology rows go in opposite directions. The upper row has the labels inj and surj. In the lower row the outer groups have a subscript $c$. Between them are assignments involving $\ell,\lambda_\nu,\xi^\nu,\varphi_\nu$ and a cancelled row. Below $Q^{n+1}-Q^n=U^{n+1}$, the note associates two families of maximal isotropic subspaces with $\alpha_\nu,\beta_\nu$ and writes their sum, difference and the identification $\varphi_\nu=\alpha_\nu$.

**The reductive centre of a quotient, scan 31.** After referring part of the proof back to 1.7, the typescript takes a reductive centre $Z$ of $G$ and argues that $G'=G/Z$ has the unit subgroup as reductive centre. It gives properties of $G'$, reduces to a field, and takes the inverse image $Z_1$ of a central subgroup $Z'$ of multiplicative type. The action on $G$ factors through $Z'$ and is trivial on $Z$ and on the quotient. The next deduction continues beyond the sheet.

**Even-dimensional quadrics, scan 32, lines 1–5.** For $Q^{2\nu}$ the sheet writes a map from degree $2i$ to degree $2i+2$. In the indicated range it is labelled bijective away from $i=\nu-1,\nu$. At $\nu-1$ it is injective with free cokernel of basis $\varphi_\nu$. At $\nu$ it is surjective with free kernel of basis $\lambda_\nu$. Several earlier formulations of the bounds and exceptions remain visible.

**Odd-dimensional quadrics, scan 32, lines 6–8.** For $Q^{2\nu+1}$ the range is $0\leq i\leq2\nu$. The exception is $i=\nu$, where the sheet gives an injection with cokernel $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. The argument of the final $H$ has a raised $+1$ added to the exponent of $Q$. The degree of that $H$ and the shape of the connecting stroke remain doubtful.

**Rank and maximal tori, scan 33.** Part a) ends by comparing dimensions of centralizers. Part b) extends maximality of a torus from a fibre to an open neighbourhood using local constancy of reductive rank. It compares conditions (i), (ii) and (ii bis), then introduces the subfunctor of maximal tori in $F$. Representability by an open subscheme, smoothness and a local section for the étale topology supply the remaining implication. Part c) cites conjugacy. Part d) starts a reduction.

**Bases for linear equivalence, scan 35.** In $Q^{2n}$ the sequence of powers of the hyperplane section $H$ reaches the two middle classes $S',S''$ from the two families of linear spaces. The margin gives $H^n=S'+S''$. Products by $H$ follow. For $Q^{2n+1}$ the list gives powers of $H$, then $H^{n+1}/2,\ldots,H^{2n+1}/2$, which the closing sentence associates with linear subvarieties.

**The ring of forms, scan 36.** Delzant works over a field $A$ of characteristic different from 2 with finite-rank nondegenerate quadratic modules. Direct sum and tensor product give a ring $\mathrm Q(A)$ with augmentation given by rank. Square classes define a map from their integral group algebra to $\mathrm Q(A)$. A proposition gives generators of its kernel using relations between pairs of one-dimensional forms.

**Cup products and a corollary, scans 36–37.** Delzant writes $a.b$ for a cup product of degree-one classes and interprets it as the Brauer class of a quaternion algebra. The corollary describes the kernel using equality of the displayed classes $e_{a+b},e_{a'+b'}$ and equality of the two cup products.

**Constructing the classes, scan 37, lines 5–23.** A suitable degree-one map into a graded ring induces a homomorphism into the associated filtered ring. Taking Galois cohomology gives $w$, its components $w^i(q)$, the sum formula for two forms, and explicit formulas for $w^1$ and $w^2$ of a diagonal form.

**Discriminant, Clifford algebras and functoriality, scan 37, lines 24–34.** Delzant identifies $w^1$ as the discriminant. He describes $w^2$ by the Brauer class $u(q)$ obtained by comparing Clifford algebras after a Galois extension. For odd rank he uses their even parts. The last paragraph states functoriality in the field.

**Completion and classification, scan 38.** For a number field Delzant passes to the completions and invokes Hasse’s theorem. He gives the local invariants at discrete valuations and treats the real completion using the number of negative squares and its binary digits. A commutative square relates the global and local maps. He concludes that rank and Stiefel–Whitney classes determine the class of a quadratic form over a number field.

**Closing remark and bibliography, scans 38–39.** Two questions about the second graded piece and the Brauer group precede four references. The last sheet carries the offprint’s publication details.

## Remarks of the model

**[R1] My finite check of scan 38, lines 10–11. High confidence for the 64 cases tested.** For [P38], Delzant’s statement $w^{2^{j}}=n_j$ following the binomial formula, I compared $\binom n{2^{j}}\bmod2$ with the $j$th binary digit of $n$, for each $0\leq n\leq15$ and $0\leq j\leq3$. All 64 comparisons agreed. For $n=13$ the four binomial coefficients are 13, 78, 715 and 1287. Their residues are 1, 0, 1 and 1 in order from the units bit. This computation covers the stated finite range.

**[R2] My comparison of scans 28 and 35. High confidence about the position of the pairs.** In [P28], the columns $Q^2,Q^4,Q^6$ have a bracketed pair at rows 1, 2 and 3 when the row with $\xi^0=1$ is numbered zero. In [P35], the brace under $S',S''$ is labelled “codim $n$” for $Q^{2n}$. Substituting $n=1,2,3$ gives those same positions. Identifying the individual $S$-classes with the individual symbols in the table would require further information.
