# Mathematical notes · cote 29, scans 49–62

## What the pages do

**Scans 51 and 49, submanifold inclusion.** Proposition 6 begins at the foot of typescript page 22 and continues on page 23. The map induced by the inclusion of Y in X identifies distributions supported at a point of Y with a particular subspace of the distributions at that point of X. The condition uses multiplication by functions vanishing on Y. Coordinates cutting out Y and the indices (α,0) give the proof.

**Scan 50, descent.** This is the end of a manuscript proof. It introduces Y₁ and a base change X₁, compares it with Z over the generic fibre, invokes flat descent, and finishes with an X-isomorphism. Some former hypotheses and intermediate identifications are under cancellations. The NB then displays a sequence from the fundamental group of the geometric fibre to that of X and then of Y, ending in 0. The author says the conclusion goes beyond what he had announced.

**Scan 51, coproduct.** The first pair of displayed lines evaluates the image of a distribution on a product of functions. The next display expresses compatibility of the operation P with the two induced maps in a tensor product.

**Scan 51, multiplication by a function.** Section 10 gives the pairing definition (59), restricts the action to distributions with support in an open set, and writes its local values at points inside and outside it. Equations (60) and (61) give its behaviour under products and application of a morphism.

**Scan 52, constructibility.** Lemma 2 states that the direct image of a constructible sheaf of sets under the indicated finite-type map is constructible. The stated base is locally noetherian and universally Japanese. The only ensuing justification on the sheet is « Standart ».

**Scan 52, restriction to a partition.** Lemma 3 takes a connected noetherian scheme and a finite partition into locally closed parts. The restriction map from H¹(X,G) to the product of the H¹(Xᵢ,G) has finite fibres; the author infers finiteness when the factors are finite. He appeals to gluing and Lemma 2. The inserted Lemma 4 says Hom(F,G) is finite for the indicated constructible étale sheaves of sets.

**Scans 52 and 54, a finiteness criterion.** The proposition beginning at the bottom of 52 continues with (i)–(iii) on 54. It compares finiteness over all X of finite type with conditions on smooth schemes and a restriction to an open. Condition (iv), split into (a) and (b), is offered as sufficient. Part (b) uses subgroups of G×G and is weakened when G is commutative. The proof points back to Lemmas 3 and 1.

**Scan 53, product of distributions.** Section 7 compares two maps into a space of bilinear forms. Proposition 4 specifies a unique injective map from distributions at (a,b) on the product to the tensor product of the two distribution spaces. Its image is described by the bound v(μ)+v(ν) ≤ r. A further sentence singles out nonintegral r. The proof begins with coordinate systems and paired bases; the page ends before the conditions on the product coordinates are given.

**Scans 59 and 57, first-order distributions.** Section 6 extracts coordinates of a distribution of order at most one by pairing it with the coordinate functions. Lemma 1 writes the pairing with f in terms of first derivatives of Φ. Its proof starts on scan 59 with constants, coordinates and germs in the square of the ideal. Scan 57 treats the sum of products of functions vanishing at the distinguished point, then invokes Proposition 1 to conclude.

**Scans 57 and 55, tangent vectors and coordinate changes.** Formula (26) gives the coordinate change and (27) the resulting Jacobian rule. Proposition 3 starts on 57 and ends at the top of 55: it identifies the distributions in the stated order-one space with tangent vectors via their components. Equations (28)–(30) compare applying a morphism to a distribution with its tangent map. The final remark adopts a definition of the tangent space at the exceptional value of r, whose printed glyph remains uncertain.

**Scan 56, two corollaries.** The first applies the cohomological finiteness criterion to G of order prime to the residue characteristics. The parenthesis discusses taking H=G when G is commutative. The second compares the finite-type case with a more restricted class of étale coverings of integral affine subschemes. Its NB isolates the finiteness result over an algebraically closed field used in Corollary 1 and asks how to establish it by the methods in hand.

**Scan 58, induction and a proposed extension.** The manuscript reduces to a smooth connected X, treats dimension one by a reduction to a complete curve, and reduces higher dimension using a fibration over a smaller-dimensional Y with one-dimensional fibres. A separate proposition in the lower half asks about extending a bound on coverings of curves to schemes of finite type over the same field.

**Scans 60 and 61, the prime-to-p fundamental group.** The cover pairs π₁ with an underlined Pic carrying an uncertain superscript. The following theorem states topological finite generation of the projective limit of discrete quotient groups of order prime to p. The argument reduces to affine normal X, treats curves by specialisation and a transcendental result, and in higher dimension invokes a generic curve and a surjection on fundamental groups. A separate question introduces wild inertia.

**Scan 62, a family of étale coverings.** The theorem starts with a closed part D, the normalisation and its codimension-one components, and a covering R on a product with U=X−D. The assumptions concern ramification and triviality over a point y. The conclusion is a local product expression involving R₀. The proof starts with X normal and passes to a generic curve; two diagrams compare the open sets and their products with Y. The argument breaks off in the sentence « Mais alors, on est sous les conditions excellentes ».

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 49, lines 12–22 · high confidence.** The pairs (α,0) are distinct for distinct α. Thus the stated images of the displayed Λ-basis are distinct members of the Δ-basis, which verifies the injectivity step at this point of the written proof.

**M2 · Scan 51, formula (59) · high confidence.** Applying (59) twice gives the same evaluation on any test germ h for f.(g.μ) and (fg).μ, by associativity and commutativity of multiplication of germs. This checks the module action in the displayed definition.

**M3 · Scans 55 and 59 · reading questions.** In (29) on 55 the printed x subscript admits i or 1. The summation variable and the explicit derivative Dᵢ favour i. The small circle on Δ at the start of 59 admits o or 0; a zero multiindex fits the later coordinate notation. Confidence in distinguishing the individual typed glyphs is medium.

**M4 · Scan 60, cover · reading question.** The Pic superscript may be τ or a bar above a circular sign. The short upper stroke is visibly separate in the enlargement. Confidence in choosing an alphabet from these strokes alone is low.
