# K79-11 · Mathematical notes on scans 160–174

## What the pages do

**U1 · Graphs with homotheties, scans 160–161, (1)–(6).** A graph has vertices \(S\) and an edge set \(A_0\) of unordered pairs. Grothendieck adjoins a permutation group \(Z\). For each \(\lambda\in Z\), he asks for a graph \(A_\lambda\) in which \(\{s,\lambda t\}\) is an edge precisely when \(\{s,t\}\) is an edge of \(A_0\). He also requires an isomorphism centralizing \(Z\). The family of resulting graphs is \(\pi\), with action \(T_\lambda\). An equivalence class on the same vertex set gives what he calls a *multigraphe à homothéties*.

**U2 · Strict automorphisms and stars, scan 162, (7)–(13).** \(G_0\) preserves a constituent and commutes with \(Z\). \(G\) preserves the family and commutes with \(Z\). A diagram relates \(G/G_0\) to a quotient of \(Z\), and the sloping map from \(Z\) is labelled \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda^2\). The kernel of the action on \(\pi\) is described using equality of vertex stars. The family of stars is common to the constituents, although the centre of a particular star can vary.

**U3 · Regularity and the edge partition, scan 163, (14)–(16).** Regularity calls for transitivity on oriented edges, connectedness and no isolated vertices. The writer observes that the \(Z\)-action is then free on the vertices. He treats a constituent edge set as the \(G_0\)-orbit of any one of its edges and labels the parts of the union \(A\) by a map \(\tau_A\) to \(\pi\). The index in (14) presents the difficulty recorded below.

**U4 · From a graph to triangles, scans 163–164, (17)–(20).** The triples all of whose pairs are edges form \(F_0\). The associated incidence data are \(P_0=(S,A_0,F_0,\ldots)\). The text tests whether each edge has exactly two possible third vertices and whether each vertex has a connected local contour. Strict regularity adds transitivity of \(G_0\) on the frames. Grothendieck fixes a frame \((s_0,t_0,u_0)\).

**U5 · A frame and its stabilizers, scan 165, (21)–(25).** The fixed frame gives three generators \(\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_\infty\) and products \(\rho_1,\rho_\infty,\rho_0\). The first two products satisfy the second- and third-power relations in (21). The order of \(\rho_0\) is denoted \(n\). With \(B'=G_{s_0}\), the intersection \(B'\cap G_0\) is the subgroup generated by \(\tau_1,\tau_\infty\). The page identifies \(G/B'\) with the vertices and sketches the translates of a frame at an edge.

**U6 · A pointed transitive action as extension data, scans 166–167, (26)–(34).** The writer starts over with a transitive \(G_0\)-set, a chosen point, a larger acting group \(G\), and a central subgroup \(Z\). The point gives \(\widetilde U\subset G_0\) and \(B'\subset G\). He expresses the relation between these inclusions by an intersection and two quotient identifications, then replaces the larger extension by \(B'\) over \(Z'=G/G_0\), an embedding of its kernel \(\widetilde U\) into \(G_0\), and a compatible action on \(G_0\).

**U7 · The commutant and the central section, scans 168–169, (35)–(41).** The commutant on \(S\) is expressed through the normalizer quotient of \(\widetilde U\). A subgroup of the commutant yields its inverse image \(\widetilde B''\). Compatibility with \(B'\) gives (38). The two subgroups generate \(B\). A central splitting of \(B\) over \(Z\), together with trivial action of the section on \(G_0\), is used to realize the specified action inside \(G\).

**U8 · A morphism between extensions, scans 170–172, (42)–(49).** Writing \(B\) as \(B'\times Z\) splits the upper map in (43) into two components. Its component in \(B'\) is a map \(\psi:\widetilde B''\to B'\) agreeing with the given inclusion on \(\widetilde U\). The triangle in (45) leads to two rows of extensions and the diagram \(B'\to Z'\leftarrow Z\). Formula (48) recovers \(\widetilde B''\) by a fiber product. Formula (49) requires the composite action through \(B'\) to agree with conjugation by \(\widetilde B''\) in \(G_0\).

**U9 · The two reconstructions, scan 173, (50)–(52).** Formula (50) collects the subgroups, the quotient map \(Z\to Z'\), the extension \(B'\), and the action on \(G_0\). A diagonal addition supplies a forgotten compatibility. Formula (51) extracts the data from a pointed action. Formula (52) runs the construction back and identifies \(Z\) with the image of \(g\mapsto g\psi(g)^{-1}\).

**U10 · Factorization and multiplicativity, scans 173–174, (52 c)–(55).** The last page names the map \(\varphi\) and its factor through \(\widetilde B''/\widetilde U\). Formula (55) compares conjugation by \(g\) with conjugation by \(\psi(g)\). Grothendieck introduces \(u\in\widetilde U\) to account for the change from \(gg'\) to \(g'g\). His calculation ends in a cancellation and « ok. ».

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High confidence.** The map on the left of the two extension rows in scan 171 is the identity on the common kernel. Consequently \(\psi(u)=u\) for \(u\in\widetilde U\), exactly the assertion used on 174 to obtain \(\varphi(u)=1\). This is a check of the restriction to the kernel.
- **R2a · High confidence for the scope of (36)–(37).** If (26 c) permits a central subgroup acting with a kernel, the formula on scan 168 need not recover its abstract order. Take \(S\) to have one point, \(G_0=1\), \(G=Z\) cyclic of order two, and the trivial action. All the named subgroups in (37) coming from \(G_0\) have order one, although \(Z\) has order two.
- **R2b · High confidence for the distinction; medium confidence about the author’s intended scope.** The subgroup of permutations corresponding to an abstract \(Z\) is its image in the permutations of \(S\). Identifying \(Z\) with that image requires the restriction of the action to \(Z\) to be faithful. Scan 160 starts with an actual permutation subgroup. Scan 166 explicitly postpones faithfulness in the general formulation.
- **R3 · High confidence.** The last equality on 174 is a free cancellation. Reading its two sides as words in \(g,g'\) gives \(g^{-1}g'^{-1}gg'\) on both sides. This checks the last line of the manuscript’s computation.
- **R4 · Reading question, low confidence.** Scan 160, (3), second transformed vertex. The frozen file has \(f_\lambda^{-1}\), then a cancelled \(1\) or \(\lambda\), then \(t\). Another possible grouping of the overlapping strokes is \(f^{-1}\lambda t\). The raised inverse is clear, but the lower letter’s position relative to the stem of \(f\) is difficult to establish; (4 a) gives a reason for a live \(\lambda\) acting on the second vertex. This is a reading question only.
