# Mathematical notes on cote 157-5, scans 293–308

Grothendieck, A. (s.d.). *Les Dérivateurs* [manuscript]. Montpellier, cote 157-5, manuscript pp. 209–224. Scan and line references below identify the places in the supplied images. These notes were written after the transcription was frozen.

## What the pages do

**[U01] A cardinal estimate for limits.** Scan 293, L01–21, continues an argument about $\operatorname{Filt}^{\pi}$ in $M$. Under stability of $\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi_0}C$ for limits of shape $J$, the proof bounds the size of each fibre of a limit by the size of a product. With $c$ the reduced cardinal of $J$, the bound is $\pi^c$. The hypothesis $\pi^c=\pi$ yields the desired stability. The first two comparison signs are doubtful in the ink.

**[U02] Representability and the outstanding implication.** Scan 294, L01–16, announces three implications. For $c\Rightarrow b$, the argument passes from colimits in $M$ to limits of representable set-valued functors. It invokes accessibility of their limit and its preservation of finite limits. A question mark sits over the proposed implication $c''\Rightarrow c$.

**[U03] Lemma 4.8.11.** Scans 294 L17–23 and 295 L01–12 give left exactness and accessibility as the criterion for prorepresentability on an accessible category with finite limits. The paragraph also gives a representability criterion when $M$ has small limits. The sufficiency argument refers to SGA 4 and proposes a change in the choice of the cardinal for the filtration being used here.

**[U04] Lemma 4.8.12 and its test cases.** Scans 295 L13–25 and 296 L01–21 ask whether stability of $C$ under finite limits passes to $\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi_0}C$. For $F=\lim_j F_j$ the remaining requirement is that $C_{/F}$ be large relative to $\pi_0$. The empty shape, the two-object discrete shape and the cospan are distinguished. It is the cospan that leads him to ask about simultaneous indexed presentations.

**[U05] Proposition 4.8.13.** Scan 296, L22–28, assumes $C$ essentially small and $\pi_0$ infinite with $\pi_0\geq\operatorname{card}\operatorname{red}C$. It asserts $M=\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi_0}C=M_{\pi_0}$ and describes $M$ as a Karoubi envelope. The mark over the final $C$ is uncertain. The marginal qualification has a strict $>$ where the main statement has $\geq$.

**[U06] Corollary 4.8.14.** Scan 297, L01–27, places three descriptions of $M$ together. They assert accessibility and essential smallness, accessibility with a single cardinal of accessibility for all objects, and essential smallness with stability under direct factors. The comparison uses the cardinal filtration and then 4.8.13. The separate NB concerns accessibility of both $M$ and $M^\circ$.

**[U07] Reduction of the fibre bound.** Scans 298 L01–21 and 299 L01–16 begin the proof headed 4.8.15. Lemma 4.8.15.1 asks for $\operatorname{card}F(x)\leq\pi_0$. The argument presents $F$ as an ordered filtered colimit, groups the indices by the value of $x_i$ and passes to a cofinal group with value $\xi$. It then singles out the full subcategory with one object and its endomorphism monoid.

**[U08] The monoid system and the failed attempt.** Scans 299 L17–23 and 300 L02–19 express the transition maps as $u\mapsto\alpha_{ji}u$. The reduction on 300 imposes left cancellation. The remaining issue is whether the relevant tail has isomorphisms as transitions, giving the constancy needed in the argument. Grothendieck records both the unsuccessful attempt and his continuing belief in the assertion.

**[U09] The indexed representation functor.** Scans 301 L01–26 and 302 L01–16 open §4.9. For $M=\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi_0}(C)$ and $\operatorname{card}\operatorname{red}J\leq\pi_0$, the inclusion of diagram categories extends to a fully faithful functor from $\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi_0}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,C))$ to $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,M)$. A diagram in its essential image has a presentation indexed by a common $I$, large relative to $\pi_0$. Its components have the displayed presentations $\varphi(j)\leftarrow\operatorname{colim}_{i\in I}\Phi(i,j)$.

**[U10] Proposition 4.9.2.** Scans 302 L17–22 and 303 L01–06 give two conditions for membership in that essential image. The category $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,C)_{/\varphi}$ must be large relative to $\pi_0$. For every $j$, evaluation must induce a cofinal functor from it to $C_{/\varphi(j)}$.

**[U11] Theorem 4.9.3.** Scan 303, L07–20, states two sufficient closure hypotheses on $C$ for the functor to be an equivalence. The first concerns countable filtered colimits. The second concerns colimits large relative to a smaller cardinal $\pi'_0<\pi_0$ and of cardinal at most $\pi''_0>\pi'_0$. The margin gives an ordinal version. Its first cardinal bound and part of its cancelled wording are doubtful.

**[U12] Remarks 4.9.4.** Scans 303 L21–25 and 304 L01–13 compare the countable case with 4.4.13 b). Grothendieck asks how essential the closure assumptions are, mentions $J=B_G$ for $G=\{\pm1\}$, and refers to an exercise about the comparison for ordinary $\operatorname{Ind}$. The cited counterexample concerns this neighbouring functor.

**[U13] Corollary 4.9.5.** Scans 304 L14–28 and 305 L01–08 assert accessibility of $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,M)$ for accessible $M$ and essentially small $J$. For a useful $\pi_0$ and $\pi^{\pi_0}=\pi\geq\operatorname{card}\operatorname{red}J$, the displayed equivalence uses $\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi}(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,M_\pi))$. The argument applies 4.9.3 b) with $C=M_\pi$. Its final assignment of cardinal parameters is discussed below.

**[U14] The product of component functors.** Scans 305 L09–26 and 306 L01–03 begin the proof of 4.9.3. The individual evaluation functors factor through the product of the $C_{/\varphi(j)}$. The argument needs the projections onto individual factors to be cofinal.

**[U15] Lemmas 4.9.6.1 and 4.9.6.2.** Scan 306, L04–26, treats the second projection of $I\times I'$, then a projection from a product indexed by $A$ onto the subproduct indexed by $B$. The latter is reduced to the former by separating the complementary factors. The oblique warning distinguishes the case of an infinite product of merely 0-connected categories. The hypothesis of the first lemma and the title of the second have been reworked.

**[U16] A faithful comparison.** Scan 307, L01–20, uses cofinality of the projections to reduce to the product comparison. A commutative square with $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(J,C)$ and $C^{\operatorname{Ob}J}$ shows why faithfulness enters. The two announced goals are cofinality of the faithful functor $f$ and largeness of its source relative to $\pi_0$.

**[U17] Criterion headed 4.9.6.3.** Scan 308, L01–34, compares filteredness of the source, cofinality of $f$, and conditions on categories indexed by $i'$. Its F3 condition expresses a lifting requirement by $f(v)u'=f(w)$. A long leader adds b), and b′) and b″) enlarge the list. The implication chain was written through an earlier version. The ending formulates a cardinal version $a_\pi\Rightarrow b_\pi$ and adds an equivalence when $f$ is faithful.

## Questions and conjectures

- **294 L02; 295 L13–15, L22–25; 296 L10–21.** « Reste à prouver » the implication bearing a question mark, « sous réserve que ce soit bien vrai ». Finite-limit stability leads to « la question des représentations indicielles », called « une question-clef » for accessibility.
- **297 L13.** « Ceci implique que $M$ et $M^\circ$ sont accessibles, et je conjecture que la réciproque est vraie également. » The NB is adjacent to the Karoubian characterization.
- **300 L14–19.** « Cela semble plus délicat à prouver que je ne le prévoyais, mais je crois toujours que c'est bel et bien vrai. » This follows the attempt to obtain constancy of the monoid system.
- **301 L20–22.** « Je m'intéresse à établir des cas où ce foncteur est essentiellement surjectif ». This question motivates the conditions in 4.9.3.
- **304 L01–11.** « Je présume qu'en dehors d'une hypothèse restrictive … le foncteur … n'est pas tjrs ess. surjectif ». The candidate test case has $J=B_G$, $G=\{\pm1\}$. The passage supplies a pointer for ordinary Ind.

## Remarks of the model

**[R1] Model observation, high confidence.** The order of the two lower indices in 299 L20–23 agrees with the written transition rule. Applying the displayed composition condition gives the composite transition from $i$ to $h$. This small consistency check does not settle the constancy problem on 300.

**[R2] Model observation, high confidence.** The strict inequality $\pi_0<2^{\pi_0}$ and the upper bound $2^{\pi_0}\leq\pi$ in 305 L07 agree with the live comparisons in the margin of 304 L23. The upper bound uses that $\pi$ is infinite and $\pi^{\pi_0}=\pi$. The erased comparison at the start of that margin is unnecessary for this check.

**[R3a–b] Model reading question and conditional test, low confidence for the proposed manuscript correction.** On 306 L05 the frozen reading is $I'|I$, with $I'$ first. For R3a, if only $I'$ must be 0-connected, take $I$ empty and $I'$ terminal. The projection then has an empty comma category over the unique object, and cofinality fails. For R3b, imposing 0-connectedness on $I$ gives the expected cofinal projection. This explains why the unprimed reading would fit without deciding whether the writer meant to cancel the prime.

**[R4] Model observation, medium confidence.** In 308 L12–18 the bracketed equality $f(v)u'=f(w)$ identifies the paths involved in F3 even where the preceding source index and arrowheads are unclear. It supplies no independent reading for the overwritten implication chain at L25–26.
