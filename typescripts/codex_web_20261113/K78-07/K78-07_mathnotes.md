# K78-07 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Double roots and representations · scans 89–90 (U1).** The two roots coincide precisely in the cases listed as $\zeta=\pm1$, $\alpha=\pm2$. These are the missing parameter values in the earlier construction of the canonical geometric representations of $\Pi_\infty$. The notes distinguish the absence of a finite centre when $\zeta=1$. They retain canonical coordinates to vary the intrinsic parameter and discuss working over a field or a base ring.

**The ordinary case · scans 91 and 95 (U2).** After extending the field to contain an eigenvalue, the two eigendirections give coordinates about the centre, with $s_0$ at $(1,1)$. Grothendieck returns to the model $\Pi_\zeta$, a normalized invariant quadratic function and the order of $\zeta$. He identifies this order with the number of sides when finite and says it is prime to the characteristic.

**The repeated-root calculation · scans 92–93 (U3).** The linear part is written $u_0=\varepsilon(\mathrm{id}+v_0)$ with $v_0^2=0$. The explicit formula expresses $v_0$ as a vector times a linear functional. Scan 93 works out an affine square, then tries several coordinate and sign conventions. It leaves an incomplete matrix and several cancelled computations.

**A letter from Jean-Pierre Aubin · scan 94 (U4).** Aubin writes to A. Grothendieck about the refusal to allow A. D. IOFFE to take up a professorship in France. He asks his correspondent to write to V. Semenitin, mentions petitions within the UER, and asks for copies of letters in a postscript. Its date is 10 November 1977.

**The negative repeated root · scans 95–96 (U5).** The notes identify the square of the linear operator as a transvection, discuss the orders of the linear and affine actions, and compute the affine fourth iterate in characteristic two. For the centred calculation scan 96 separates even and odd powers and plots the resulting vertex positions about $s_0$ and $-s_0$. Several signs on the odd-index line are overwritten.

**The positive repeated root · scan 96 (U6).** With no centre, the affine action is studied on a vector-space envelope. Writing $u_E=\mathrm{id}+w$, the note uses $w^3=0$ and distinguishes $p\ge3$ from $p=2$, where the square remains nontrivial.

**Invariant quadratics and normalization · scans 97–98 (U7).** Grothendieck starts with an affine polynomial of degree at most two. He imposes the two generating symmetries and obtains, apart from constants and scalar multiplication, $f_\alpha=(x^2-x)+(y^2-y)-\alpha xy$. Its zero locus contains the vertices. Its homogeneous part takes value one on a successive vertex difference. The long marginal note discusses the resulting circumscribed conic and its uniqueness in the case it specifies.

**Directions and discriminants · scans 98–99 (U8).** The binary discriminant $4-\alpha^2$ singles out the repeated roots. After adjoining the roots, the two linear factors of the homogeneous quadratic give the isotropic directions and the eigendirections. Scan 99 gives the ternary matrix and its divided discriminant $\delta'=-(\alpha+2)$.

**Two parallel lines or a parabola · scans 100–101 (U9).** For $\alpha=-2$ the conic splits into the lines $x+y=0$ and $x+y=1$. For $\alpha=2$ in characteristic different from two, the coordinates $v=x-y$, $w=x+y$ give a parabola. The displayed action advances its parameter by one. The two uncertain letters in the intervening prose equation are recorded below.

**Faithfulness and incidence · scan 102 (U10).** The proposition takes the images of the vertex and edge sets of a regular geometric realization, states regularity and describes the symmetry group as an image. Part a) begins the incidence argument. Given the two incident source vertices, it asks whether a third image vertex can lie on their line and invokes the circumscribed conic.

## Statements left without proof

- **P1 · Scan 90.** « ils sont valables pour tt corps »; the sentence also includes « tout anneau, de base $k$ ». These sheets motivate the use of canonical coordinates without developing the ring case.
- **P2 · Scan 91.** « par un argument déjà fait le cas-type » invokes an earlier argument. The assertion that the finite number of sides is « premier à la caractéristique » is stated here without a separate proof.
- **P3 · Scan 97.** « et quand on explicite l'invariance par $\sigma_0$, on trouve » introduces $F=a f_\alpha$. The coefficient calculation for that step is left to the reader. Direct invariance checks appear in the event ledger; they do not establish the classification.
- **P4 · Scan 98, sideways margin.** « On vérifie que si $\alpha\ne0,1$ … c'est la seule conique circonscrite ». The note relates this condition to a regular polygon with at least five sides. It gives no uniqueness proof there.
- **P5 · Scan 102.** « est un polygône géométrique … régulier », « Son groupe d'aut. … est l'image » and the marginal « un hom. de polygônes combinatoires » are assertions of the proposition. Only the beginning of the incidence argument falls within scans 89–102.

## Questions and conjectures

No conjecture or open question is explicitly posed. Scan 90 announces « Nous allons les étudier séparément au § suivant ». Scan 99 says of the positive-root direction « on va l'examiner plus en détail plus bas »; scan 101 returns to it. Scan 102's « il faut prouver que » is a proof obligation.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · My observation, high confidence · scans 89, 92, 96.** The fixed-point equations of the displayed affine map are $y=x$ and $(2-\alpha)x=1$. Over a field this accounts directly for the exception to a finite centre.
- **M2 · My observation, high confidence · scans 95–96.** Over $\mathbf F_2$ the affine map at $\alpha=0$ has order four, while its linear part has order two. Iterating the two maps on all four points distinguishes which order the displayed fourth iterate verifies.
- **M3 · My observation, high confidence · scans 98, 100, 102.** On a component of the split conic, the claim that a line meets the conic in at most two points needs a qualification. For instance the line $x+y=0$ on scan 100 contains more than two points of that conic over $\mathbf Q$.
- **M4 · My observation, high confidence conditional on the edge normalization · scans 98, 102.** Parametrize the actual edge by $s_{i-1}+t(s_i-s_{i-1})$. The leading coefficient of the restricted quadratic is $f_0(s_i-s_{i-1})=1$ by scan 98. Its endpoint values vanish, so its restriction is $t(t-1)$. This gives the required two-point bound for that edge, including on the split conic. The connection between these two passages is mine.
- **M5 · My reading question, low confidence · scan 96, first line to the right of the drawing.** The frozen text reads $s_0:$ before the pair of quarters. A very short equals sign is another possible reading. Both ink marks slant upward and are wider than ordinary points, but they are also appreciably shorter than the equality farther to the right.
- **M6 · My reading question, low confidence · scan 93, bottom-left two-row pair.** The frozen reading is `(X−Y,+X−Y)`. A second reading is `(X−Y,−X−Y)`. The sign after the comma is a dark wedge with a short horizontal tail. The nearby clear plus has a separate descending stroke.
