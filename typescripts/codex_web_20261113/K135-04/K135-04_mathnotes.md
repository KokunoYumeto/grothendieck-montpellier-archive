# Mathematical notes on cote 135, scans 48–59

## What the pages do

**Scans 48–49. Constraints and invertibility.** The text continues a definition with the symmetry map $c_{X,Y}$. Strictness is the condition $c_{X,X}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes X}$. It gives the unit arrows $X\to 1\otimes X$ and $X\to X\otimes 1$, the AC hexagon and the AU triangle. It combines the two conditions as ACU and defines invertibility using a left inverse object $X'$ and a right inverse object $X''$.

**Scans 49–50. Functors and their morphisms.** The structural map goes from $FX\otimes FY$ to $F(X\otimes Y)$. Separate diagrams require compatibility with associativity, commutativity and the unit. A square specifies when a natural transformation $\lambda:F\to G$ respects the tensor maps.

**Scans 50–51. Gr-categories and their invariants.** A Gr-category has a groupoid as base and invertible objects. The text associates to it the group of classes $\Pi_0(P)$ and the commutative group $\Pi_1(P)=\operatorname{Aut}(1)$. It gives the action $su=\delta_X^{-1}\gamma_X(u)$. A “preepinglage” identifies these two invariants with prescribed $M,N$, compatibly with the action. The two triangles specify the arrows between such objects.

**Scans 51–53. Pic-categories and classification.** The text adds commutativity to the Gr-category structure and states the necessary condition on $\Pi_0$ and its action. For abelian $M,N$ it writes two complexes of free abelian groups with explicit generators and boundaries through degree three. The left-primed complex enters the displayed degree-two classification group. The unprimed complex enters the ensuing statement for strict Pic-categories.

**Scans 53–56. Making objects units.** Given $T:A'\to A$, the first universal problem asks for a category $P$, a functor $D:A\to P$ and a tensor isomorphism from $DT$ to the constant unit functor. The text first introduces a quotient that sends specified arrows to identities. For nonempty $A'$ it keeps the objects of $A$ and constructs arrows of $P$ as classes $[A',B',u]$. It specifies the equivalence relation, composition, tensor product, constraints, $D$ and the components of $\lambda$. The long paths on 54–56 define the representatives used in these operations.

**Scans 56–57. Making objects invertible.** The second problem asks that the images of $FX'$ become invertible. A factorization property characterizes the functor into $P$. The proposed reduction uses $A=C\times C'$ and $TX'=(FX',X')$, together with an equivalence of categories of tensor functors. The construction yields a category of fractions, and in the specified case a Pic-envelope. An application to the stated category of finitely generated $R$-modules identifies two group invariants.

**Scans 57–58. Suspension.** The functor $S$ sends $X$ to $X\otimes Z$. The suspension category solves a universal square in which $q$ is an equivalence and the square commutes up to natural isomorphism. For pointed spaces the text describes the smash product by collapsing $X\vee Y$ and takes $Z=S^1$. A comparison functor $G$ goes from the suspension category to the category of fractions. The last paragraph draws a consequence from failure of faithfulness.

**Scan 59. References.** Sixteen entries follow the mathematical text. References [1] and [12] also appear by number in the preceding pages.

## Remarks of the model

**R1. Model remark, high confidence, scan 52.** Applying the displayed $d_1$ to each of the two displayed $d_2$ formulas gives zero. I checked this by collecting the coefficients of the formal generators for the two- and three-variable inputs. This tests the degree-two boundary identity only. The assertion about a truncated resolution in C6 requires more.

**R2. Model remark, high confidence, scans 49 and 54.** Two edges of the large diagram on 54 combine a tensor of images into the image of a tensor. The downward edge on the right uses $\mathrm{id}\otimes\breve T$. The bottom-right edge uses $\mathrm{id}\otimes\breve T^{-1}$ for the same operation. Reading the source and target of those two edges against scan 49 gives the local discrepancy in L10.

**R3. Model remark, medium confidence, scans 52–53.** The displayed presentations distinguish two complexes already in degree three. The unprimed $L_3$ has the additional summand $\mathbb Z[M]$ and a one-variable rule $d_3[x]=[x,x]$. This makes the left prime in the degree-two classification formula worth preserving when following the transition to the strict case. The pages do not compute that cohomology group explicitly.
