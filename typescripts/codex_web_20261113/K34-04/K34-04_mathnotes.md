# What the pages do

**Scans 67–68 — potential good reduction.** A lemma attributed to Serre connects a semisimple inertia action on the rational Tate module of an abelian variety with potential good reduction. The proof distinguishes the residue-characteristic cases. In the equal-prime case it uses a Néron model, a deferred reduction theorem, and two submodules whose intersection and containment force the toric rank to vanish. The opening cancelled paragraph and several local readings remain uncertain.

**Scans 68–69 — the Serre remark.** A conditional implication from solvable action to essentially abelian action is proposed as a route to potential CM. The text then discusses locally algebraic, locally rational representations associated with an abelian scheme over a number ring and compares Frobenius polynomials for two primes. The discussion includes a heavily cancelled block and ends with an unfinished choice of ℓ; it is not completed here.

**Scans 70–72 — reductions for the referenced theorem 1.1.** The notes reduce to an affine open, allow a finite extension of the base field, and arrange a geometrically normal projective ambient scheme. Fundamental-group diagrams support these reductions. A duality argument recasts the desired finiteness of coinvariants as vanishing of invariant first cohomology. The full original statement of theorem 1.1 is outside this batch; the notes do not reconstruct it. The Hom argument and one invariant-group decoration remain marked readings.

**Scans 73–74 — the curve argument.** For an open curve inside a projective curve, a localization sequence separates the boundary terms from the projective first cohomology. Twist formulas describe the boundary action. The remaining invariant group is treated by spreading out and specializing to a finite field. A cancelled Frobenius-eigenvalue argument is followed by a smaller replacement involving kernels, cokernels, and the finiteness of rational points of the associated abelian variety. The surprising signs and degrees in the written sequences are catalogued below, not repaired in the transcription.

**Scan 74 — the sideways field-conditions remark.** Remarque 2.6 isolates two properties used in the argument: an infinite cyclotomic extension and finiteness of suitable Tate-module coinvariants over finite extensions. It explicitly distinguishes the second property from mere vanishing of invariants, giving a local-field example. The small separate circled cancellation is only partly readable.

**Scans 75–78 — a generic curve and surjections of fundamental groups.** A generic projective linear section is constructed over the function field of a Grassmannian. Base points and fields are organized in diagrams. Lemme 3.2 asserts the surjectivity of the three vertical homomorphisms in (3.1.3); the argument uses connected étale covers and Bertini. Section 3.3 then transfers the curve case to the original scheme. The cancelled compositum construction is retained separately from the live argument.

**Scan 78 — finite generation remark.** Remarque 3.4 applies the curve reduction to the prime-to-characteristic quotient of the geometric fundamental group, with references to descent and to the known structure of the group of a curve. It states a finite-generation conclusion; the peculiar written wording “topologique à engendrement fini” is retained.

**Scans 79–80 — almost all primes.** Proposition 4.1 and its dual cohomological Corollaire 4.2 strengthen the preceding conclusion to mod-ℓ invariant vanishing for almost every prime. The proof again reduces to a curve. It separates an integer coming from roots of unity and an integer coming from the finite group of rational points of an abelian variety over a finite field. Corollaire 4.3 states a finite-kernel conclusion for an abelian fundamental group over a finite field, with an almost-all-primes isomorphism assertion. Its target notation is left exactly as written.

**Scan 81 — reduction of monodromy in first cohomology.** Section 5 uses finite descent, a base extension and an open embedding, then the curve construction of section 3, to reduce monodromy to a smooth projective curve. A bracketed note discusses extending a valuation. The boundary terms in the localization sequence are treated as inertia-trivial. The last written degree is H², although the announced reduction and preceding injection concern H¹.

**Scan 82 — a later unipotence statement and bibliography.** Remarque 5.4 defers an order-two unipotence assertion for a smooth separated curve to Exp III, and says the reductions yield order three generally and order two in the normal case. The first abbreviated unipotence prefix is doubtful; the second reads essᵗ. The page closes with four bibliography entries, the S. Lang entry left unfinished.
