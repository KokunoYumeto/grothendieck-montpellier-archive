# K57-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**M1 · Title and composition laws, scans 034–035.** The English title places uniqueness and associativity of composition laws together. Lemma 1 then starts with a variety, a point e, a multiplication and a self-map marked with a raised angle. Its displayed condition makes the product of x with the marked image of y equal to e precisely on the diagonal. The proof applies a two-variable expression to the axes through the unit and concludes commutativity. It starts a second argument with a three-variable associator and stops at an equals sign.

**M2 · Lifting a multiplication, scans 037 and 039.** Lemma 3 considers a simple proper scheme with a section over a local Artinian base. It seeks a multiplication lifting one on the preceding infinitesimal level. On 037 an obstruction in degree one is restricted to the two axes. Künneth enters the argument, and a degree-zero group measures the choice of a lifting. On 039 the marked section fixes that choice. The writer compares maps with the same reduction and value at the unit to obtain the unit laws, commutativity and associativity. The two corollaries at the bottom pass to an Artinian ring and then to a complete local ring.

**M3 · From a fibre to a connected base, scan 041.** Corollary 3 asserts uniqueness for a fixed unit section. The next paragraph invokes descent for existence and describes the locus of abelian fibres. To prove closedness it turns to a discrete valuation ring. The theorem below asserts that one abelian fibre suffices over a connected locally noetherian base, with the other hypotheses on G and its marked section stated there.

**M4 · Rigid groups and a proposed converse, scans 043–044.** The definition has three clauses. Alongside simplicity, it asks for density of prime-power torsion on geometric fibres and a finiteness condition on torsion over opens. In the margin the author says it is enough to require the density for one prime. A discussion of simple factors proposes a sufficient condition. On 044 a nontrivial extension of an abelian variety by the additive group in characteristic zero gives the author a counterexample to the proposed converse.

**M5 · Torsion on an open and the multiplication map, scans 044–045.** The first proposition on 044 describes an open U as the smallest closed subscheme of itself containing the indicated torsion intersections. The next asserts that the multiplication map and its kernel are unramified under the stated primality assumption, and adds an étaleness assertion when G is flat. The proof on 045 reduces to a field and identifies the tangent map with multiplication by n on the Lie algebra. The qualifier in the second proposition's initial group hypothesis is hard to read.

**M6 · Equality of finite étale subschemes, scans 045–046.** A lemma asserts that two subschemes of an unramified scheme, finite and étale over the connected base, coincide if their fibres agree at one point. The lower margin on 045 proposes their fibre product to reduce to an inclusion. The main proof passes through the complete local case. On 046 it takes the largest open on which the restrictions agree and uses a closure formula to argue that this open is closed.

**M7 · Equality of rigid subgroups and of morphisms, scans 046–047.** The rigidity theorem concludes that two closed rigid subgroup schemes are equal if they agree on one fibre. Corollary 1 applies this to the graphs of two homomorphisms with the same fibre map. The subscripts on the initial group names in the corollary are uncertain. Its formulas use G and H.

**M8 · Descent along a faithfully flat morphism, scans 047–049.** Corollary 2 descends a closed rigid subgroup H′ after a faithfully flat base change of finite type with the stated connectedness condition on its fibres. On 048 the problem is equality of two inverse images on the double fibre product. A cancelled attempt uses finite-type subalgebras. The live argument resumes at the diagonal, then uses rigidity on connected components. Its last sentence ends on 049.

**M9 · Primary field extensions, scan 049.** Corollary 3 concerns descent of a rigid closed subgroup over a primary extension. The author assumes the availability of a suitable rigid finite-type model, suggests a condition on composition factors over an algebraic closure, and refers back to corollary 2. The note in the margin requests greater precision about this step.

Scans 036, 038, 040 and 042 are leaves of a stationery catalogue.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence, scan 035.** The two restrictions of the displayed binary f to a unit axis both reduce to the same diagonal expression. This accounts for the two equalities with e before the neighbourhood argument. It uses only the unit and the displayed diagonal condition.

**R2 · Model, high confidence, scans 047–048.** The two projections from the double base agree after composition with its diagonal. This accounts for equality of the two inverse images on that diagonal. The subsequent extension to connected components is a separate step made by the author.

**R3 · Model, low confidence, scan 049, penultimate line.** The first reading of the operator before (A₁) is *hom.*; *Spec* is also recorded. The connectedness of a scheme would fit the second reading, but the tall initial stroke and the compressed middle do not give a decisive p-descender. The mathematical context cannot choose the letters.

**R4 · Model, low confidence, scan 035, display below « Pour l’associativité », TeX line 43.** The transcription reads `[(ab)c][a(bc)]` before the raised angle. The left outer strokes have long upper caps but curved feet, particularly on the first factor. A round opening parenthesis for the first factor is also possible. Both right outer delimiters are square.
