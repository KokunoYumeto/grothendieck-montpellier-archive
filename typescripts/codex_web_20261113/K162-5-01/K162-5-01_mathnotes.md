# Mathematical notes · Cote 162-5, scans 20 and 22–31

## What the pages do

**B1 · Bibliography, scan 20.** Eleven items are listed under “PUBLICATIONS OF DANIEL QUILLEN”. The list ranges from spectral sequences of simplicial groups to equivariant cohomology. It includes a book, published papers, a preprint and a work marked “To appear”.

**§1, scans 22–23.** A formal category over the scheme of objects $X_0$ is described by a strict augmented pro-ring $A$, two module structures, a coproduct $\Delta$ and unit and associativity conditions. A diagram shows $X_2,X_1,X_0$ and the face and degeneracy arrows. The conormal sheaf $\Omega_{\mathcal C}$ of $X_0$ in $X_1$ supplies the exterior algebra. The two projections define the differential on functions modulo $I^2$. A separate paragraph treats operators obtained by dualising the $A_\alpha$. Its continuation identifies a tangent object acting by derivations.

**§2, scans 23–24.** A general De Rham complex is built on an exterior algebra over $X_0$. The sheet gives maps $\delta_0$ on functions and $\delta_1$ on one-forms, a map from ordinary differentials, two product conditions and $\delta_1\delta_0=0$. It claims that these data extend uniquely to an antiderivation of square zero. The sufficiency of the displayed conditions is discussed in R1 below.

**§3, scan 24.** The dual of $\Omega$ acts on functions by $\Theta_X(f)=\langle\delta f,X\rangle$. A pairing formula defines a bracket on local sections. Its three terms involve $\delta\omega$, $\Theta_X$ and $\Theta_Y$. The author relates the bracket to the commutator of operators on functions, notes the role of $\delta_1\delta_0$, and writes the alternating and Jacobi laws in boxes.

**Jacobi calculation, scans 25–26.** After a scalar-product law for the bracket, the author pairs the Jacobi sum with a one-form and expands it cyclically in $X,Y,Z$. He rewrites the result using contractions and the Cartan formula. The crossed panel preserves an earlier expansion. The calculation ends with two expressions for $T$ or $T(\delta\omega)$ and a question about vanishing.

**§4, scan 26.** The page specifies the data for a $\Theta$-Lie algebra and its action by derivations on functions. It defines degree-$p$ cochains as a Hom out of an exterior power. The differential is referred to a known formula. A map from forms to cochains is asserted to commute with differentials.

**§§5–6, scan 27.** In the finite locally free case, the constructions from forms to Lie algebras and from Lie algebras to cochains are said to correspond. The statement headed “Th. de Quillen” passes from an adic formal category in characteristic zero to its De Rham complex, with $\Omega$ flat on both sides. A bracketed observation compares $\operatorname{Sym}\Omega$ with $\operatorname{Gr}(A)$. The divided-power variant specifies quotients $A_n$, nilpotence of $I_n$, and the filtration by divided powers.

**§7, scan 28.** A descent datum on $M$ is also called a stratification or an action of $\mathcal C$. The sheet associates a graded differential operator to it and says that its restriction to $M$ determines it. Order-one gluing is mentioned for the construction. A question distinguishes this from the order of descent needed for square zero.

**§8, scans 28–29.** An $\Omega$-connection on $M$ is introduced by a map to $\Omega\otimes M$ and a product law. The operation is extended to forms with coefficients. Its successive degree-zero and degree-one maps give the composite called the curvature tensor. Evaluating the connection against $X$ gives $\Theta_X^M$. The page writes its two scalar laws and, when curvature vanishes, its bracket law.

**§9, scan 30.** The same two scalar laws define a connection for a $\Theta$-Lie algebra. Its curvature $K$ is the action of the bracket less the commutator of the actions. Vanishing defines integrability. A curved line labelled $K'$ connects the source and target of a second construction through $\bigwedge^2\Omega\otimes M$. The formula comparing the two has an explicit warning in the margin.

**§§10–11, scans 30–31.** Local freeness of finite type gives the asserted correspondence between the two types of connection, their curvatures, integrability and their complexes. Under the earlier Quillen hypotheses the author relates descent on $M$ to $\Omega$-connections and compares De Rham cohomology with Hochschild, or Čech-Alexander, cohomology. He strengthens the comparison to a canonical quasi-isomorphism. A marginal question mentions crystals, and the sheet ends with the heading for a divided-power variant.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence in the algebraic check. Scans 23–26.** Vanishing of $\delta_1\delta_0$ on functions leaves the square on one-forms undetermined. For example, on the exterior algebra over $\mathbb Q$ on $a,b,c$, set $d(a)=a\wedge b$, $d(b)=b\wedge c$, $d(c)=0$ and let $d$ kill scalars. Then $d^2(a)=-a\wedge b\wedge c$. A sufficient condition for the claimed extension to have square zero checks its square on generators in **both** degrees zero and one. This qualification concerns the sufficiency assertion U3. The later computation can still start with a De Rham complex whose differential already has square zero.
- **R2 · Model, low confidence in any numbering change. Scans 28–29.** Both pages draw a map from $M$ to $\Omega\otimes M$. The arrow on scan 28 is labelled $\delta_M^1$, while the first label on scan 29 has a dark round superscript resembling 0. Source-degree and target-degree numbering would account for this. The ink by itself leaves the question open.
- **Reading question · Model, low confidence. Scan 22, lower heading.** Frozen reading “Suite à développer”. A small raised and returning stroke after the initial S may also support “Sorite à développer”. The enlarged image leaves the opening run closely joined.
- **Reading question · Model, medium confidence. Scan 23, bracket after “espace annelé”.** Frozen reading “de même que”. The middle mark resembles two horizontal strokes and could be the abbreviation “de $=$ que”, as at the top of scan 31.
