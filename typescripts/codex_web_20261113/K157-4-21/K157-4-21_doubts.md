## Legibility

The long descenders of \(f,g,j,p\) are often more informative than the middle of a word. The large diagrams usually preserve a clear distinction between dotted shafts, ink dots above arrows, and strokes of cancellation. Their arrow ends are less secure in the discarded left version on 324 and some edges on 327–330. The joined French word endings, the densely rewritten statement on 325, the source of the functor morphism on 329, and the slanted conclusion on 332 are the readings I trust least. One blackened word after “conditions” on 326 remains unreadable. The other places with damaged ink have local candidate readings.
