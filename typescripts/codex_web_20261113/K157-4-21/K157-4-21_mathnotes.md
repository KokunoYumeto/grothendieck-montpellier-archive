## What the pages do

**U01 · Scans 318–319.** In the continuation of an argument, the NB observes that \(\mathcal F\subset\mathcal F'\) gives \(A(\mathcal F')\Rightarrow A(\mathcal F)\), and likewise for \(B,C\). The author obtains \(f=pi\) with \(i\in W\) and a choice of class for \(p\). He invokes the path construction for the perfect class, then factors the perfect map again using the triangle over \(S\). The middle object of that triangle has a doubtful initial and argument.

**U02 · Scan 319, Corollaire 4.4.2.** For each of the six indicated sets of arrows, the corollary gives \(A(\mathcal F)\Rightarrow B(\mathcal F)\Rightarrow C(\mathcal F)\). The parenthesis refers back to diagram (4.2.1) and excludes two classes written \(\mathrm{Fib}\) with subscripts.

**U03 · Scans 319–320, Lemme 4.4.3.** The hypotheses concern base change and composition, a short doubtful abbreviation after “contient les”, maps to a final object, and factorizations \(f=pi\) with \(i\in W,\ p\in\mathcal F\). Under these hypotheses \(A,B,C\) for \(\mathcal F\) are equivalent. The remaining direction \(C\Rightarrow A\) is identified with the dual form of a lemma of K. S. Brown. The text relates the fourth condition to base change of \(\mathcal F\cap W\).

**U04 · Scans 320–321, Corollaire 4.4.4.** The author applies this result to perfect maps and names the resulting conditions \(D=D_W\) and \(D_{W_0}\). The next two displays give \(D_W\Rightarrow D_{W_0}\) for \(W\supset W_0\), then \(D_{W_0}\Rightarrow D_{W'_0}\) for \(W'_0\subset W_0\). Some subscripts in the first statement of the corollary are overwritten. The slanted NB on 320 treats a separate factorization question for \(\mathrm{Fib}_{W_0}\).

**U05 · Scans 321–323, Corollaire 4.4.5.** Three versions of \(\mathrm{Loc}(7)\) receive the names \(A,B,C\). The first assertion is \(A\Rightarrow B\Rightarrow C\). The second identifies \(A\) with \(C\) together with closure under composition of \(\mathcal F_W\), and also with \(C\) together with closure under composition of \(\mathrm{Fibf}_W\). The identity \(\mathcal F_W=\mathrm{Catfib}\cap\mathrm{Fibf}_W\) supplies the step from closure of \(\mathrm{Fibf}_W\) to closure of \(\mathcal F_W\). The converse argument invokes a comparison of the two families that is taken up on scan 327.

**U06 · Scan 323, Corollaire 4.4.6.** Closure of \(\mathcal F_{W_0}\) under composition yields equivalence of \(A_{W_0},B_{W_0},C_{W_0}\). The author gives \(\mathrm{Loc}(7)\) for \(W_0\) as a sufficient condition for closure. The NB retains the forward chain when this hypothesis is dropped.

**U07 · Scans 323–325, §4.4.7.** A diagram collects the implications for \(W\) and the pair \((W,W_0)\). The accompanying text singles out three arrows marked with a dot above them. They run from \(C\) to \(D\), from \(C_{W_0}\) to \(D_{W_0}\), and from \(D_{W_0}\) to \(F\). A dashed return from \(C_{W_0}\) to \(A_{W_0}\) carries the condition \(W_0(7)\). The sheet also has a redrawn diagram and a struck version. Scan 325 relates the first two marked arrows by specialization and the inclusion \(\mathrm{Parf}_{W_0}\subset\mathrm{Fibf}_{W_0}\).

**U08 · Scan 326.** The author reads \(D_{W_0}\Rightarrow F\) as saying that \(\mathrm{Cat}\) has enough \(W\)-fibrations. He takes \(D_{W_0}\) in the form \(A(\mathrm{Parf}_{W_0})\) and appeals to factorization through a perfect map. The two final membership statements give rise to S01 below.

**U09 · Scans 327–328, §4.4.8 and Lemme 4.4.9.** The task is to compare \(A,B,C\) for \(\mathcal F_{W_0}\) and \(\mathrm{Fibf}_{W_0}\). The lemma supposes that each \(f\in\mathcal F\) factors over its target \(S\) as \(pi\), with \(i\in W^u_S,\ p\in\mathcal G\). It transfers \(A\) and \(C\) from \(\mathcal G\) to \(\mathcal F\). The proof of \(A\) uses the grid of cartesian squares. The proof for \(C\) uses composition in \(W^u_S\). The proposed transfer of \(B\) is struck from the lemma.

**U10 · Scans 328–330, diagram (4.4.9.3).** For an arrow \(u\) over \(S\), the proposed extra datum is a compatible arrow \(\bar u\) between the middle objects of factorizations \(f=pi,\ g=qj\). Factorization functorial in the source over \(S\) supplies this diagram. A marginal note defines \(\mathcal F_S\) by the structure maps of its objects. The argument uses the localizer \(W^u_S\) in the slice to transfer the condition on \(u\).

**U11 · Scans 330–331, Corollaire 4.4.10 and application.** The extra triangle condition gives \(B(\mathcal G)\Rightarrow B(\mathcal F)\). When \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal F\) as well, the three respective pairs of conditions are equivalent. The application takes \(\mathcal G=\mathcal F_{W_0}\) and \(\mathcal F=\mathrm{Fibf}_{W_0}\), using the functorial factorization recalled from manuscript page 43. Scan 331 says this establishes (4.4.8.1) and the implications in the diagram on manuscript page 48.

**U12 · Scans 331–332, Corollaire 4.4.11.** Under \(\mathrm{Loc}(8)\), or the weaker \(\mathrm{Loc}(8\,\mathrm{bis})\) on \(W_0\) concerning limits of sequences, the inserted parenthesis says that \(A_{W_0},B_{W_0},C_{W_0},D_{W_0}\) are equivalent. The first arrowhead beside \(D_{W_0}\) is doubtful. The specialization \(W_0=W\) is explicit. To obtain the forward implication for \(A(\mathrm{Parf}_{W_0})\) and \(A(\mathrm{Fibf}_{W_0})\), the author starts to verify the factorization needed for 4.4.9. The selection ends at a reference to VI.
