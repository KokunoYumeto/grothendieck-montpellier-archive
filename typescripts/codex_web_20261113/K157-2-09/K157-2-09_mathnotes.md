# Mathematical notes · cote 157-2 · scans 131–146

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 131–133.** The opening diagram organizes categories over $S$ into what Grothendieck calls «l'étage» and «le rez de chaussée». He describes five functors drawn into the diagram. The first two send $X$ to split objects given by $s\mapsto {}_s\backslash X$ and $s\mapsto X_{/s}$. He describes $\Phi$ on the fibration side, $\Psi$ on the cofibration side, and their restrictions $\Phi_0,\Psi_0$ upstairs. Inclusion of an intermediate full subcategory has a quasi-inverse after the indicated localization. The $d$ and $g$ superscripts belong to different sides. An interlinear NB on 133 explains why $\underline{\mathrm{Parf}}\,S$ is admissible on both.

**U2 · Scans 133–135.** The four functors leave four localizations to compare. In the diamond on 134 the two routes downstairs give $\rho i=\sigma j$, induced by the inclusion $\delta$. Grothendieck recalls that this last functor gives an equivalence on coarse localizations. He describes its quasi-inverse using the fifth functor $C$, whose values lie in $\underline{\mathrm{Parf}}\,S$. Composing $C$ with the inclusion back into $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$ gives $C'$. Its displayed condition $C'(W_S)\subset W_S^d\cap W_S^g$ explains how the same construction gives the two inclusion functors $i,j$.

**U3 · Scans 135–137.** The boxed formula on 136 compares $\mathrm{Hom}_{W_S^d}(X,CY)$, $\mathrm{Hom}_{W_S^g}(X,CY)$, and $\mathrm{Hom}_{W_S}(X,Y)$. There is a cancelled sign before the first live $X$. The oblique note addresses the case $Y\in\underline{\mathrm{Fibhot}}\,S$. In the «Commentaire» he distinguishes results obtained under L1, a qualified L3, and L4 bis from the extra input needed for coarse equivalences and adjunction. Without L5 he leaves two possible localizations upstairs. Scan 137 identifies $i_{C(X)}=C(i_X)$ in the two directional localizations as a property singled out by the earlier lemma. The right-hand target of the displayed arrows is uncertain in the manuscript.

**U4 · Scans 137–140.** A factorization $X\to C(X)\to S$ suggests a question for a general pair $(\mathcal M,W)$. Grothendieck can describe the coarse localization over $S$, but asks what should define the finer ones. He compares the slice and coslice constructions in Cat with two ways of localizing a site, mentions smooth and proper topologies, and tests the analogy against infinity-groupoids. On 139 he asks whether universal $W$-equivalences retain the needed compatibility. He first sees a localization functor in one direction only, then reconsiders on 140.

**U5 · Scans 140–142.** Starting afresh, he chooses $S$, the induced $W_S$, and a class $F$ of arrows playing the role of fibrations. He asks for finite inverse limits, closure of $F$ under base change and composition, isomorphisms in $F$, and a factorization with $i_X\in W$ and $\bar f\in F$. He calls the objects with structural map in $F$ the subcategory $\underline{\mathrm{Fib}}\,S$. Its inclusion gives a functor $\delta$ after localization. A candidate in the reverse direction sends $X$ to a choice of $C(X)$. He distinguishes essential surjectivity from the problem of making this into a functor independent of choices.

**U6 · Scans 142–144.** In the first case, $C$ and the maps $i_X$ are functorial. The square on 143 is used to pass to localizations. Writing $D$ for inclusion, the two comparisons go from the corresponding identity functors to $DC$ and $CD$, with components $i_X$. On 144 Grothendieck concludes that the localized functors are quasi-inverses, then invokes a lemma from page 11 for every intermediate full subcategory. The letter naming the sought functor varies in appearance across these pages. The notation table records both readings.

**U7 · Scans 145–146.** In the second case he assumes a Quillen-type lifting property of $i_X$. Given $u:X\to X'$, the large diagram, the lemma in the margin, and the square at the foot of 145 distinguish the prescribed diagonal $v=i_{X'}u$ from the lift $\bar u$. He proposes taking the image of $\bar u$ as $\gamma(u)$. On 146 he defines $\mathrm{Cof}_0$ by its lifting property and states a lemma about the image of a choice of lift. The last live sentence still asks for independence. Below it are a cancelled pair $(v_1,v_2)$, an arrow to $C'\times_S C'$, and a cancelled square.

## Slips

- **Scan 143, below the first square.** The prose reads $i_X,i_X\in W$. The expected second component is $i_{X'}$, as written explicitly on the lower edge of that same square. The two figures of $X$ in the prose have no prime. Confidence **high**.
- **Scan 146, penultimate line of the lemma.** The sequence of marks leaves «ne» before the cancelled «dépend pas» and the inserted «est indépendant». The likely intended clause has «est indépendant» alone. Confidence **low**, because the status of the small surviving word depends on the extent of the cancellation.
- **Reading question, scan 144, opening two lines.** Frozen reading «les deux foncteurs […] comme quasi-inverses l'une de l'autre». Grammatically, «l'un de l'autre» would readily refer to the two functors. The apparent final e of une runs into the terminal pen stroke. The earlier «équivalences quasi-inverses l'une de l'autre» on 132 also supplies a possible source for the feminine. Whether this is an agreement slip remains open.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · My remark, high confidence · Scan 145.** The fine dotted arrow $\bar u$ has a different source from the heavier diagonal $v$. In the large diagram both routes from $X$ to $C(X')$ therefore read $\bar u\,i_X=i_{X'}u$, precisely the equation of the square below. I checked these two composites against the sources and targets in both drawings. The equality expresses the required commutation for a chosen lift.
- **M2 · My remark, high confidence · Scans 145–146.** The independence needed for $\gamma(u)$ concerns the images of lifts in the localization. This follows from the phrase «image de» in the definition on 146 and the specification «comme […] flèche de» the localized category in the lemma. It requires comparing two possible choices even after the square on 145 has been filled.
