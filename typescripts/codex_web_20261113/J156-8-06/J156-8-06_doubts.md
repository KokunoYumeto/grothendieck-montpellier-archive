# Legibility and limits

The larger formulas and most ordinary prose are readable, but the hand reduces many word middles to horizontal strokes. The least reliable passages are the tiny slanted notes (especially 42, 46–48), the two little words above the equality on 43, the reworked T/Z formulas on 44, the cancelled lemma's final condition on 45, the heavily corrected corollary chain on 48, and the cancelled bottom lines on 49. Repeated reading did not remove these uncertainties; the low-confidence entries are intended to remain open for a future reader.

The transcription preserves the author's deletions, insertions, surprising symbols and unfinished syntax. It does not establish mathematical correctness. No OCR, text-recognition software, outside transcription or web source was used. No `\illegible` placeholder was needed: doubtful visible material has a tentative reading in `\unsure`, including symbols beneath cancellation. A zero count of `\illegible` therefore does not imply perfect legibility.
