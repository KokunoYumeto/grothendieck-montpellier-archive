# Mathematical notes · cote 29, scans 63–76

## What the pages do

**Covers on a product (63).** The opening continues a specialization argument. A corollary considers an étale cover of $U\times_kY$ whose restriction over $y$ is trivial. It describes the cover near $y$ as an inverse image. Two subsequent corollaries give product formulas, first for the groups $\pi_1^t$ of pairs with specified closed subsets, then for $\pi'_1$ with the prime-to-characteristic restriction indicated in brackets.

**A finiteness theorem for covers (64).** The setup names a normalization $X'$, the open $U=X-Y$, and components of $Y$ and of their inverse images. The covers singled out by the word *doux* are classified by a group $\pi$ of “type galoisien”. The theorem gives finite topological generation of $\pi$. The first corollary uses the stronger wording “de présentation finie” for the indicated group of the open; the second gives finite topological type for $\pi'_1(X)$ with $X$ regular.

**Diagrams and possible surjections (65).** Separate calculations compare the fundamental group of a product to the two factors, arrange $X'',X',X$ in a tower, and compare a section, a model and an ambient projective space. “surj??” is written over the arrow out of $\pi_1(Y')$. The last two arrows into $\pi_1(X)$ form a crossed-out line.

**Reduction to a proper integral model (66–67).** For connected $X$ over an algebraically closed field, the theorem imposes a condition on each irreducible component with its reduced structure: it must be the complement of a closed subset of codimension at least two in a proper scheme. The goal is finite topological type of $\pi_1(X)$. The proof reduces to $X=Z-Y$ with $Z$ integral and proper, passes along a proper surjection, and invokes Chow’s lemma to reach a projective model. Dimension zero and dimension one are disposed of separately.

**The linear section (67).** The lemma attributed to Zariski takes a morphism to projective space and forms $X_u$ by intersecting over a field containing the generic parameters for a linear section. It asserts irreducibility. For normal $X$, the corollary gives a surjection $\pi_1(X_u)\to\pi_1(X)$ by considering what happens to a connected étale cover.

**The genus bound (68).** With $s=n-1$ the section misses $Y$ and gives a normal projective curve. Its genus $g$ supplies the stated bound of $2g$ topological generators. The next paragraph introduces projective, hence proper, models of a finitely generated extension $K/k$, with normal models available. A description of regular opens called “presque propres” is struck through.

**The inverse limit of fundamental groups (69–70).** Proper normal models are ordered by domination. Following a cancelled construction with several opens, the live text sends $\pi_1(U)$ onto the group of a model and then to the inverse limit of the pointed groups. The symbol $\Omega$ denotes an algebraically closed extension of $K$. The resulting group is named the *groupe fondamental birationnel* of $K/k$ and denoted $\pi_1(K/k;\Omega)$. The theorem asserts its finite topological type when $k$ is algebraically closed.

**The regular-model comparison (70, margin).** If there is a proper regular model, the remark identifies the group of that model with those of the normal models dominating it and calls their inverse system essentially constant. It then asks what happens for the regular locus of a merely normal model. The last formulation asks where a finite extension unramified on that locus remains unramified.

**Birational invariance of $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau$ (71).** For a dominant birational morphism $f:X\to Y$ of integral normal proper schemes, the revised statement requires $Y$ regular. The proof uses $f_*\underline O_X=\underline O_Y$ and introduces the open $U$ of $Y$ on which the inverse rational map is defined. The triangle with $U,X,Y$ is repeated for their ordinary Picard groups, with pullbacks. The calculation identifies a direct factor and asks how $\tau$-equivalent classes meet it.

**The divisor and tangent-space steps (71–72).** The difference of a class and the expression built from the three pullbacks is represented by a combination $\sum n_iX_i$ of the “diviseurs singuliers” for $f$. Vanishing of a $\tau$-equivalent combination is called a known lemma and marked for verification. The next reduction, annotated “insuffisant”, compares the tangent spaces at the origin. This leads to the claimed isomorphism on $H^1$ with structure-sheaf coefficients.

**The two Frobenius comparisons (72–73).** In characteristic $p>0$, the first comparison is written using $H^1(-,\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)$ and linked to the fundamental groups by purity. The second is written first with $\underline O/\underline O^{p^\nu}$, then, over a perfect field, with $\underline O^{p^{-\nu}}/\underline O$. The diagram marked $T$ relates the latter groups on $U,Y,X$. Its lower map has an isomorphism mark and the diagonal is described as injective.

**The characteristic-zero reduction (74–75).** The field is represented as a function field over an integral finite-type base over $\mathbb Z$. The argument spreads out the map, restricts the base, and compares the dimensions of first-cohomology maps on the fibres. The passage through the two higher direct images is overwritten. The next page explicitly concludes “Cela prouve $r=0$, et achève la démonstration.”

**Two Picard corollaries (75).** The first gives birational invariance of $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau_{X/k}$ for proper $X$ simple over $k$. The second considers a finitely generated extension admitting a proper model simple over $k$. Its direct system of Picard objects over proper normal models is said to be essentially constant. The replacement of *projectif* by *inductif* is visible in the bracket defining what is meant.

**Three proposed bounds (75).** In asking whether a model “normal sur $k$” suffices, the author lists bounded dimension of the Picard object, bounded order of the indicated Picard torsion, and bounded $\dim H^1(X,\underline O_X)$. The separate note says (i) follows from (iii).

**A uniform dimension bound (76).** The theorem asserts the existence of $n(K/k)$ bounding $\dim\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/k}$ on the stated models. The proof deals first with the case of no such model, changes the field by $K\otimes_k\bar k$, and writes the dimension as half the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-rank of the indicated primary part of the abelianized fundamental group. The last line invokes a uniform-majoration theorem. The sentence continues beyond this packet.

## Remarks of the model

- **R71 · Model remark, high confidence in the conditional diagram calculation.** With the maps labelled in the two triangles on 71, $(i^*)^{-1}g^*$ is a retraction of $f^*$ whenever $i^*$ is invertible and the triangle commutes. This identifies the direct factor meant by the calculation with $\xi$. The check uses the arrow labels and does not decide the separate question of why $i^*$ is invertible in the manuscript’s setting.
- **R73 · Model remark, high confidence in the conditional diagram calculation.** In the $T$ triangle on 73, commutativity, invertibility of $i^*$ and injectivity of $g^*$ suffice for the asserted inverse of $f^*$. These are the roles of the labels and the word *injective* in the last two lines. Whether the geometric hypotheses supply that injectivity is a separate assertion on the page.
- **R75 · Model remark, high confidence as a reading of the sequence.** The theorem on 76 has exactly the quantity appearing in (i) on 75 on its left. Its displayed bound gives neither the order in (ii) nor the cohomology dimension in (iii). The end of this packet therefore records progress on the three-part question without giving its complete resolution.
