# Mathematical notes on cote 77, scans 34–49

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 34.** The first paragraph asks when sections exist. It tests the class of the doubled affine bundle, then fixes a section underlined b and tests the class of the torsor above it. Equations (45) and (46) turn to the affine bundle associated to an extension and attempt to identify it through a symmetric square.

**U2 · Scan 35.** Section 5 bis starts again from the extension of the structure sheaf by a line L. Its fibre over 1 is F. The induced maps on symmetric powers give a sequence for every n and a decreasing filtration with pieces indexed by i. A cancelled draft proposes a map from the n-fold product followed by passage to a quotient. Its last displayed expression stops at x₁.

**U3 · Scans 36–37.** Transposing (47) gives (51). The alternating pairing of a rank-two module V takes values in its determinant, which the extension identifies with L. Equations (55), (58) and (58 bis) specify the pairings on an element coming from L. After tensoring (47) with the inverse line, the resulting extension is compared with its transpose. The diagram at the foot of 37 introduces the map jₙ to the affine function algebra of F.

**U4 · Scan 38.** Proposition 61 states that jₙ is injective and that the function algebra is the direct limit of these symmetric powers. The transition in the middle column is multiplication by the distinguished section. A polynomial function of degree at most n has a leading term modulo functions of degree at most n−1. The manuscript identifies it with a section of the negative tensor power of L and with restriction of the corresponding form to L. The closing comment separates the assertions for affine bundles of arbitrary rank from those using rank-two duality here.

**U5 · Scans 39–40.** A relative divisor D of degree n gives a submodule M_D in the functions of degree at most n. The verb specifying these functions on D is uncertain. The proposition characterizes M_D as a complement to the preceding filtration step and gives the resulting bijection with divisors. Splitting the quotient map produces the affine bundle in (62). Evaluation at the distinguished vector e_B then describes a section by a degree-n form taking the value 1 there.

**U6 · Scans 40–41.** For n=2 the theorem lists four kinds of data attached to the module B and its chosen unit section. These are a commutative unital algebra structure, a relative divisor of degree two on F, a section of the symmetric square of F, and a quadratic form q with q(e_B)=1. The identifications in (64) and (65) name the spectrum of the algebra and its norm.

**U7 · Scans 41–42.** For general n the notes replace a divisor by a finite locally free algebra Cₙ and a module map g from B. Equation (67) requires preservation of the unit and generation of the algebra by the image. The proposed n-form is the norm of Cₙ restricted along g. To check this assertion he chooses a generator t, presents Cₙ by a monic polynomial and writes a homogeneous expression from its coefficients. Several versions of the last step overlap.

**U8 · Scans 43–44.** The local calculation compares the homogeneous form with the norm of u+vt. A companion matrix gives the norm of t. A second route factors the monic polynomial after a faithfully flat extension and uses the evaluation maps ρᵢ. Their product gives (79). The calculation of the normalized form (73) is written below the horizontal rule on 43, after the formulas that call for it.

**U9 · Scans 44–45.** For a quadratic form q, its bilinear form evaluated at e_B gives the trace form underlined b(q). Evaluating again at e_B gives 2. If this trace is fixed, the difference q₀ of two possible forms satisfies (82). Such differences factor through the quotient line. Equation (83) gives their action by a section c₀ of the tensor square of L.

**U10 · Scans 46–47.** Section 7 writes the canonical involution as σ(u)=(tr u)e_B−u. The trace therefore determines the involution. A sideways definition and a cancelled computation explore quasi-reflections fixing the unit line. The next sheet states a criterion for the involution to become the identity and discusses what happens on fibres in residual characteristic two. The return to an algebra structure gives the norm identity (86) and quadratic identity (87).

**U11 · Scan 48.** Section 8 assumes 2 invertible. Half the trace selects an origin of F and a splitting of B. Its complementary line is the trace kernel. The interlinear addition calls it the −1 eigenspace of σ, and the margin identifies it as the orthogonal of the unit for the norm form. In this splitting the linear coefficient vanishes, as recorded in (90).

**U12 · Scan 49.** The discriminant in the canonical splitting is −4c₀. The theorem describes quadratic algebras or quadratic covers by a line L and a section of its tensor square. Formula (92) describes the cover inside the associated vector bundle. Multiplication by 2 takes its equation to the square-root equation for the discriminant. The looped discriminant letter has several possible alphabetic readings in the transcription.
