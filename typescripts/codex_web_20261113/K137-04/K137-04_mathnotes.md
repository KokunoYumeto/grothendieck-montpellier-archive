# Mathematical notes on cote 137, scans 45–58

## What the pages do

**U1 · Evaluation of a complex, scan 45.** A map out of \(F(x)\) into a Hom complex is displayed as (1). Its tensor form is (1′). The line marked (1″) evaluates a morphism \(x\to C\) after applying \(F\). The text invokes functoriality and additivity to reduce the isomorphism assertion to the displayed generators. On such an object the comparison (2) is identified with the identity. The opening sentence continues from an earlier sheet.

**U2 · Dold–Puppe and its four categories, scans 46–47.** Ex 3 sets the two directions marked \(DP\) and \(ND\) against one another, first for the categories marked by a lower dot or star, then for their upper-index counterparts. The central diagram relates four versions of \(\mathbb K\). Its circles mark contravariant functors. Proposition 4 runs across the page turn. There are two exterior-power expressions of degree \(i+1\), the second with a check over \(\Phi\).

**U3 · Evaluation at the starred object, scan 47.** After a cancelled corollary the manuscript sets \(C^*=DP(C^\bullet)\). A diagram compares restriction of functors and their evaluations at the two complexes. In Prop 5, for a \(k\)-additive karoubian \(B\), evaluation at \(C^*\) gives an equivalence with \(B^*\). The last sentence announces a bifunctorial isomorphism and names its variables. Scan 48 supplies no further formula.

**U4 · Exact sequences, scan 49.** The objects of \(\mathbb P_\bullet\) are chain complexes that are finite free in each degree and have finite support. A sequence is in \(\Sigma_\bullet\) when it is exact in each degree. The sheet then considers a class \(\Sigma\) in an additive category. Sex 1) expresses the kernel and cokernel conditions using the two Hom sequences. Sex 2) gives stability under isomorphism and finite sums and includes the elementary split sequences.

**U5 · Strict maps and resolutions, scan 50.** The author defines a \(\Sigma\)-mono and a \(\Sigma\)-épi by occurrence in such a sequence. The description of strictness uses a kernel, an intermediate object and a cokernel. The four stepped sequences attached to \(A\to B\to C\) place \(H\) between \(\operatorname{Ker}v\) and the terminal zero in the second row. The closing lines move from the special-complex condition to acyclicity and then to a resolution concentrated in nonnegative degrees.

**U6 · Universal property and its two clauses, scans 51–52.** The proposed theorem evaluates a functor at the distinguished complex. The left-exact version involves \(\Sigma\)-kernels of differentials. The exact version picks out complexes that are \(\Sigma\)-resolutions. In the cancelled proof draft the author writes short exact sequences involving consecutive \(\Psi^i_\bullet\). A filtration by chain degree expresses an arbitrary \(L_\bullet\) by successive extensions. Its graded pieces are concentrated in a single degree. The last square compares the two subcategories with the two categories of functors.

**U7 · The adjunction, scans 53–54.** Evaluation is called \(\rho\). The proposed opposite functor \(\sigma\) uses a Hom complex with the dual of \(L_\bullet\) as its first argument. The page works from a natural family of maps to componentwise evaluation and obtains \(u:F\to\sigma\rho F\). The calculation beneath \(\rho\sigma C^\bullet\to C^\bullet\) identifies its components with \(C^i\). It leads to the assertion that \(\sigma\) is fully faithful, and to the question about its image.

**U8 · Induction on support, scans 55–56.** The unit is evaluated on the \(\mathbb E^i\)'s and on the \(\Psi^i\)'s. The second calculation contains the cycles \(Z^i\) and the underbraced \(\Sigma\)-kernel. The induction uses the length of an interval \([p,q]\) supporting \(L_\bullet\). A diagram of complexes exhibits the truncation \(L'_\bullet\). The exact rows below express \(L_\bullet\) as a fibre product over a power of \(\Psi^{p+1}_\bullet\). The sentence concludes over the turn of the page by invoking the induction hypothesis.

**U9 · Weaker hypotheses and an example, scans 56–57.** The NB returns to the functor under weaker assumptions on kernels. In its adjoint expression \(\widehat B\) appears. Re NB relates exactness of \(F\) to the resolution property of its associated complex, with an explicit qualification in the abelian case. The example uses \(L_\bullet\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(\lambda_\bullet,L_\bullet)\). The final three manuscript lines identify this Hom with the one obtained by reversing and dualizing its arguments.

**U10 · Intrinsic construction, scan 58.** Condition a) calls for an object \(\Psi^0\) representing the dual of \(\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathbb E^0)\). A pairing by composition induces the map \(\Psi^0\to\mathbb E^0\). The argument with \(d,u',u,v\) checks its composite with the next differential. Several formulations of b) and c) are cancelled. The remaining text describes finite sums \(L+(\Psi^0)^r\), introduces a class of sequences tested with objects of \(P_0\), and requires the augmented complex to be a \(\Sigma\)-resolution. The wording of the sequence test has multiple partially legible layers.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 49–50 · High confidence.** The two Hom rows in Sex 1) run in opposite argument slots. In each row the visible zero is at the left. Applying the kernel and cokernel universal properties checks those directions; neither row places a surjectivity condition on its last displayed map.

**M2 · Scan 52 · Low confidence as an interpretation of this cancelled draft.** The stated filtration keeps precisely the components with \(j\leq i\). Two consecutive steps therefore differ only at \(j=i\). This accounts for the phrase « placé en degré \(i\) » in the graded-piece calculation. The check uses the formula for Fil and leaves the overwritten multiplicity open.

**M3 · Scans 54–55 · High confidence.** The two composites of functors do different jobs in these calculations. Scan 54 evaluates \(\rho\sigma\) on \(C^\bullet\). Scan 55 evaluates the unit for \(\sigma\rho\) on generators and on \(L_\bullet\). Following their arguments through the displayed Hom terms explains why the induction addresses the latter composite.

**M4 · Scan 58 · High confidence.** With the arrows as drawn, \(u'\) goes from the degree-one object to \(L\), and \(v\) goes from \(L\) to the degree-zero object. Both parenthesizations of \(v\circ u'\circ d\) have source and target the degree-zero object. The local inference \(vu'=0\) implies \((vu')d=0\) uses precisely the associativity recorded above the line. This checks the inference conditional on the stated vanishing.

**Reading question · Scans 45, 52 and 58.** A looped capital with an inner vertical is transcribed \(\mathbb E\) at several evaluations, while the unbarred terms in the three sequences on scan 52 read \(C\). On scan 45, line 23, an alternate curved \(C\) is specifically registered. A printed identification of these various forms would require more evidence about the handwriting; the notes preserve the distinction in quoting individual formulas.
