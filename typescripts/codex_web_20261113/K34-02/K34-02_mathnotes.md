## 1. Argument by textual unit

**Scans 34–36, §7.** The section announces the monodromy assertion as Conjecture 7.1, attributed by insertion to Serre–Tate: under the conditions of an earlier statement 0.3, the inertia action on the indicated ℓ-adic cohomology is quasi-unipotent. The text promises two relative proofs using resolution of singularities and lists characteristic-zero, degree-one, and geometric local cases. It then proposes compatibility as ℓ varies, first for finite quotient representations and traces, and then for representations of an algebraic group involving the additive group and Γ. The finite-residue-field paragraph adds a condition on the complex absolute values of eigenvalues. These are proposals, with degree-one proofs deferred. The subsequent discussion of rational models contains qualifications and a marginal warning about elliptic curves with potentially good reduction. The two headings both numbered 7.4 are retained.

**Scans 37–40, Theorem 8.1, Corollary 8.2, Remark 8.3.** The arithmetic proof is organized around expressing V as a filtered limit of local subrings containing t. The live regularity condition concerns both V_i and V_i/tV_i; several stronger or differently phrased conditions are canceled. A condition on roots of unity in finite extensions of residue fields is also imposed, although the quantifier in 8.1(b) is doubtful. The conclusion concerns proper schemes, with a tame-action alternative or discrete-valuation approximants. Corollary 8.2 gives geometric henselian cases and residue characteristic zero. Its proof descends data to smaller fields and rings and, in characteristic zero, invokes resolution to obtain suitable regular approximants. Remark 8.3 makes the broader reach conditional on resolution or local uniformization; it does not assert that such resolution has been proved in every characteristic.

**Scans 40–45, §§8.4–8.9.** Properness supplies constructibility of the higher direct image, which becomes locally constant in the manuscript's terminology “constant tordu” on a suitable generic open. Limit and base-change arguments compare the representations, followed by reduction of torsion and passage to an approximating stage. Lemma 8.5 describes the fundamental groups and tame inertia for a regular local normal-crossings situation, together with the Galois action through roots of unity. Its proof is explicitly omitted. Corollary 8.6 applies the residue-field condition to obtain quasi-unipotence of a moderate representation. Remarks 8.7–8.8 explain the single-parameter application and the cohomological condition permitting the properness assumption to be dropped. Scan 45 cites base-change and resolution results for that extension. A long proposed Corollary 8.9 is canceled; the live replacement extends 8.2 to finite-type schemes, with the stated characteristic-zero or dimension-at-most-two condition in case (a).

**Scans 46–48, §§9.0–9.4.** The discussion passes to reductions in dimension and to compactly supported cohomology. For smooth separated schemes, the displayed duality relates ordinary cohomology to the dual of compactly supported cohomology in complementary degree, with a Tate twist. The text says inertia acts trivially on that twist. An open–closed exact sequence and the lower-dimensional induction hypothesis are used to make the compact-support question birational. Assuming suitable resolution over the perfect closure and compatibility of the induction hypothesis with finite radical extensions, one reduces to smooth proper models. The nonseparated smooth case is assigned to a spectral sequence for an affine cover. Proposition 9.4 summarizes reduction to smooth affine schemes after finite radical extensions, to projective schemes under its additional hypothesis, and a consequence for ordinary cohomology of smooth schemes. Canceled drafts on scan 47 are not additional live propositions.

**Scans 48–49, §§9.5–9.6.** The author next attempts induction on cohomological degree for ordinary cohomology. After an admitted resolution hypothesis and a finite radical extension, a proper birational surjection from a smooth scheme is considered. A descent spectral sequence attributed to Deligne is used: terms with lower second degree are controlled by induction, leaving the term E₂^{0,i}, identified as a subspace of H^i of the smooth scheme. The displayed abutment degree is doubtful and its outer parenthesis is unfinished. The ensuing Proposition 9.6 has a surviving heading but a canceled body. It therefore records a draft conclusion, not a settled final statement on these sheets.

## 3. Questions, conjectures and admitted hypotheses

Conjecture 7.1 (scan 34) is the basic quasi-unipotence claim. Sections 7.3 and the first 7.4 (scan 35) ask for ℓ-independence, trace properties, and the finite-residue-field eigenvalue condition. The inserted trace sentence quantifies over I, whereas the line following (7.3.1) says π; both readings remain as written.

The second 7.4 and scan 36 ask for common rational representations, first for the additive-group/Γ construction and then for the locally algebraic semidirect product in the finite-field situation. “On peut demander” marks proposed strengthenings. The characteristic-zero transcendental proof “devrait être possible”; the nearby warning makes a blanket rational-realizability claim unsafe.

Remark 8.3 (scan 40) explicitly conditions wider applicability on resolution with the required divisor control, and says local uniformization would suffice. Remark 8.8 and its continuation (scans 44–45) condition the nonproper extension on suitable generic behavior of higher direct images, then invoke resolution in specified cases.

Section 9.0 (scan 46) assumes the lower-dimensional conjecture. Scan 47 admits strong resolution over the perfect closure and requires the induction hypothesis after finite radical extensions. Proposition 9.4(b) (scan 48) explicitly assumes smooth projective models for the stated function fields. Scan 49 again admits resolution before the descent argument. None of these conditional steps is promoted here into an unconditional theorem.
