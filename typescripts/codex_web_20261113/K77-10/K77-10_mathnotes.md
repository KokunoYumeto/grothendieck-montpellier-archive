# Mathematical notes for cote 77, scans 144–158

## What the pages do

**Flags and the Arf scheme, scans 144–145.** The opening continues an argument about the Stein factorization of a flag-space map. Part d) writes down the finite étale map of degree two obtained by retaining or forgetting the last member of a flag of dimensions $n-1,n$ in rank $2n$. Part e) sends a maximal isotropic $F$ to $\omega_F$ in a determinant line. The map from Arf to Arf′ is compared with an associated product in (7). The text records an isomorphism when 2 is invertible.

**Odd rank and a cancelled proof, scans 145–146.** Under “Application aux groupes orthogonaux” the first theorem writes $\mathrm O(q)$ as the product of $\mu_2$ and $\mathrm{SO}(q)$ in rank $2n+1$. The projection is induced by the determinant. The next sheet attempts to prove transitivity by taking a full flag and comparing adapted bases. The author crossed out the whole proof, which breaks off after “Il donne”.

**An isotropic submodule and its quotient, scans 147–148.** For a regular quadratic module $(E,q)$ and an isotropic strict submodule $F$, the map from $E$ to the dual of $F$ has kernel $F^\perp$. The induced pairing identifies $G=E/F^\perp$ and $F$ as duals. The author cancels his first account of splittings and continues on the next sheet with the regular form on $Q=F^\perp/F$.

**Changing a splitting, scans 148–149.** A splitting $\theta$ sends $G$ into $E$. The notes compute the form induced on $F\times G$ and its polar form. An affine-base argument changes $\theta$ to $\theta+u$ so its image is isotropic. The right margin gives the condition on $B_u(y,y)$. Alternating forms then act on the choices. The quotient of the isotropic splittings is compared with a torsor under $\mathrm{Hom}(G,Q)$. With one isotropic splitting chosen, the form decomposes into a paired summand and $Q_\theta$. A first corollary on completing a flag is struck out below.

**Comparing flags, scans 150–151.** Corollary 1 compares flags with isomorphic graded pieces and residual quadratic forms over an affine base. Corollaries 2 and 3 give the étale-local isomorphism and express the flag space as a quotient by the stabilizer. Corollary 4 prescribes the maps induced on the graded pieces and on $Q$. The isomorphisms inducing these maps form a right torsor under the subgroup inducing the identity.

**The scheme of splittings, scans 151–152.** The factors $\Sigma_i$ parametrize splittings of successive steps of the flag. A further factor $\Sigma_0$ concerns isotropic splittings. The model $(\bar E,\bar q,\bar D)$ is built from the graded pieces, their duals and $Q$. Corollary 5 identifies the product of the splitting spaces with the scheme of isomorphisms of the two triples respecting the prescribed maps. Stabilizers of the target and source act on the left and right. The oblique “sans hyp. $S$ affine !” stands beside the scheme statement.

**The stabilizer kernel and dimensions, scans 153–154.** Corollary 6 asserts smoothness with connected fibres. With a section over an affine base the kernel is identified, as a scheme, with a vector bundle. A Hom-to-Quad map describes one of its terms. The author counts ranks using $d_i$, their sum $d$, and the residual rank $\rho$. He passes from the kernel to the stabilizer and then to the flag space. The next sheet specializes the result to a single isotropic subspace, then to maximal dimension in even rank.

**The determinant in odd rank, scans 154–156.** The central scalar subgroup acts trivially on flags. The diagram on 155 compares the determinant on $E$ with the determinant on the residual module, followed by two rows relating stabilizers in the orthogonal and special orthogonal groups. The proof chooses an isotropic subspace of rank $n$, leaving a residual line. The homogeneous quotient and its fibres finish the argument at the top of 156. A corollary identifies the special stabilizer with $B_D(q)$.

**The sign in even rank, scans 156–157.** The action on Arf gives $\varepsilon_q$ with values in $(\mathbb Z/2)_S$, and the text defines the special orthogonal group as its kernel. A right-hand note relates the sign to the determinant. The sideways proposition treats transitivity for flags and the role of a section in the maximal case. The restriction of the sign to a flag stabilizer factors through the residual form. The exact rows on 157 describe the corresponding extension.

**Completion of the even-rank proof, scans 157–158.** Choosing a maximal isotropic $F$ gives $D=\{F\}$, $Q=0$, and a section $\omega$. Smoothness and connectedness are applied first to the stabilizer and then to the homogeneous quotient indexed by $\omega$. The argument ends with “cqfd.” and the corollary on $B_D(q)$.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model, high confidence, D153a.** The two expressions for $\dim_S\Sigma_0$ on 153 agree as polynomials in $d,\rho$.
- **Model, high confidence for the test performed, D153b.** With four independent formal graded ranks, the expression under the brace equals the sum of their pairwise products. This check concerns the stated four-variable specialization.
- **Model, high confidence in the polynomial comparison, D153c–d.** Put $t=\sum d_i^2$. Adding the displayed kernel and quotient dimensions gives a final graded contribution $(t-d)/2$. A further half in front of that contribution would change the total by $(d-t)/4$. Confidence that Grothendieck actually left that extra half live is low because a stroke passes through it.
- **Model, high confidence, D154a–b.** The general expression at the top of 154 specializes to its single-subspace example. Setting $d=n,\rho=0$ then gives $n(n-1)/2$.
- **Model, high confidence in the rank substitution, D155.** At $d=n,\rho=1$ the displayed rank relation exceeds $2n$ by exactly one. The ink status of the annotation is addressed separately under Slips.
- **Model reading question, low confidence, 144, fragment lines 3 and 7, with the same sign in later flag formulas.** The frozen transcription uses an underlined $G$. Could the very angular, nearly triangular open top be an underlined $\Delta$? The ordinary quotient letter $G$ on 147 is rounder. At the first locus the extra underline is clear, while the upper outline is harder to classify. The transcription retains its frozen reading here.
