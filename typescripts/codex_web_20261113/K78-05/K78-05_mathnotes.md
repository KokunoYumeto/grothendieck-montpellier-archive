# K78-05 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U01 · Orientations and torsors, scans 61–62.** The opening completes an argument identifying the two orientations with two orbits. The action of $G=\operatorname{Aut}\Pi$ on them has kernel $G^+$. Each element outside the kernel has order two and splits the sequence. The author identifies $G^+$ as cyclic of order $n$, calls $R$ a $G$-torsor and calls $S,A$ torsors under $G^+$. Two connections at the right of the written sequence are plain strokes.

**U02 · Recovering the combinatorial polygon, scan 62.** An orientation $\omega$ selects a generator $u_\omega$. Reversal gives $u_{-\omega}=u_\omega^{-1}$. For $n\geq3$ the orientation map is injective. Part d gives a functor to triples consisting of a cyclic group, a pair of mutually inverse generators and a torsor of vertices. Recovering the remaining data is left as an exercise.

**U03 · Linear coordinates from a pinning, scans 63–64.** With $k$ algebraically closed and $n$ prime to the characteristic, the fixed vertex barycentre supplies an origin. A starting vertex $s_0$ and a preferred rotation $u$ specify the combinatorial pinning. The involution $\sigma$ fixes $s_0$ and conjugates $u$ to its inverse. After excluding a repeated eigenvalue, the author decomposes $s_0=e_1+e_2$ along the eigenlines. In the resulting coordinates $u$ is diagonal with entries $\zeta,\zeta^{-1}$, $\sigma$ exchanges the coordinates, and $s_i=(\zeta^i,\zeta^{-i})$.

**U04 · Classification and trace, scans 64–66.** The unordered reciprocal pair is intrinsic, as is $\alpha=\operatorname{Tr}u=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$. For finite $n\geq3$ the proposition classifies regular $n$-gons, also in the pinned formulation on the sheet, by primitive $n$th roots up to inversion. Equivalently the two roots of $T^2-\alpha T+1$ must be primitive. The proposition also identifies the origin, the eigenvalues of the preferred rotations and the coordinate axes.

**U05 · Invariants of the group, scans 66–67.** Reflection invariance imposes symmetry in $x,y$. Rotation invariance imposes $\alpha-\beta\equiv0\pmod n$ on the exponents of a monomial $x^\alpha y^\beta$. The author groups terms in pairs, introduces $\varphi_{\alpha,i}$, and applies the theorem on symmetric functions to $X^i+Y^i$. The resulting ring is written $k[xy,x^n+y^n]$. The proposed basis on the way to this conclusion omits a class of monomials (S04).

**U06 · Variants of the invariant calculation, scans 67–68.** The “NB” extends the finite calculation to positive $n$ and arbitrary base rings, with an interlinear group label involving $\mu_n$. The next paragraph treats a section $\zeta$ of infinite order on every fibre and gives $k[xy]$ as the invariant ring.

**U07 · Form, conic and side length, scans 68–69.** The corollary gives $axy+b$ in degree at most two. The quadratic form $q_0$ is normalized to take value $1$ at the vertices. The curve is $xy-1=0$ in the chosen coordinates. A marginal qualification restricts uniqueness among circumscribed conics to $n\geq5$. On a side vector the same form has value $2-\alpha$. Its nonzero value allows the alternative normalization $q_0(a)=1$.

**U08 · A cancelled interpretation, scans 69–70.** The struck “Commentaires “philosophiques”” describe a fixed dihedral group, varying fields equipped with $\alpha$, and affine representations attached to pinned polygons. The paragraph ends with homomorphisms considered up to inner automorphism. Its last affine-group label is doubtful.

**U09 · Weakening regularity, scans 70–71.** The author considers affine automorphisms transitive on vertices and then assumes that the combinatorial rotations act affinely. The two eigenvalues of $u$ can have orders $d,d'$ with $\operatorname{lcm}(d,d')=n$. The orbit of $s_0$ supplies vertices and consecutive vertices give lines. The author flags a further incidence condition. He then asks what changes if a metric is present. Requiring the automorphisms to be rotations gives unimodularity and hence reciprocal eigenvalues.

**U10 · Rotation weights and their modules, scan 72.** In the explicit congruence a monomial has weight $d$ when $i-j\equiv d\pmod n$. The invariant subalgebra is generated by $W=xy$, $U=x^n$ and $V=y^n$, with $UV=W^n$. For $0<d<n$ the text gives module generators $X_d=x^d$, $Y_d=y^{n-d}$ and writes $VX_d-W^dY_d=0$. The two earlier raised groups before the explicit congruence remain doubtful.

**U11 · Degree at most two, scans 72–74.** On the six monomials $1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2$ the candidate weights run from $-2$ to $2$. For $n\geq5$ there are five distinct weights. Requiring a relative invariant for the full group leaves $a+bxy$; passing through the vertices gives $a+b=0$. At the top of 74 the author also asserts uniqueness of the circumscribed conic in this case.

**U12 · Orders three and four, scan 74.** Two tables collect the collisions among the weights. For $n=3$ the author again singles out $a+bxy$ for the full group. For $n=4$ he also discusses $ax^2+by^2$. The last lines give $xy-1=0$ and $x^2-y^2=0$ as the circumscribed examples in the two stated families. The distinction between a stable family and stable individual curves matters here (S09, R04).

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model remark, scans 63–64, low confidence as a reading.** At 063 L25–064 L1 the frozen text joins “l'un des” to “valeurs propres est stable”. An alternative for the short word is “l'ens”, the author's contraction for ensemble. Setwise stability fits the ensuing interchange of the eigenlines. The grammatical difficulty and the small strokes alone do not settle it. The transcription retains “l'un des”.

**R02 · Model remark, scan 67, high confidence.** In the finite rotation calculation each diagonal orbit contributes its monomial $(xy)^a$ once. Together with the off-diagonal orbit sums already listed, these give the refined basis in S04. This also explains how $xy$ itself enters the stated invariant ring.

**R03 · Model remark, scans 68–69, high confidence.** The two normalizations differ by the factor $2-\alpha$ on a side. Multiplication of the two written Laurent binomials gives that factor with the sign on the page.

**R04 · Model remark, scan 74, high confidence for the algebra.** In the $n=4$ case all $ax^2+by^2$ have the same rotation weight. Reflection exchanges the two coefficients. The pencil is stable and its individual reflection eigenlines are spanned by $x^2+y^2$ and $x^2-y^2$ over the field considered here. For example, the reflection takes $x^2$ to $y^2$. Both circumscribed equations at the foot of the page do vanish on the four standard vertices. The attribution in S09 remains low confidence.

**R05 · Model remark, scans 68 and 73–74, high confidence.** The bound $n\geq5$ in the margin also works for conics without an invariance hypothesis. On the parametrization $(t,t^{-1})$, multiplying a degree-at-most-two equation by $t^2$ gives a polynomial of degree at most four. Five distinct vertex parameters force it to vanish identically, leaving multiples of $xy-1$. This identifies the sense of “seule” in the continuation at the top of 74.

**R06 · Model remark, scan 72, high confidence.** The written relation $VX_d=W^dY_d$ has exponent pair $(d,n)$ on both sides. The definitions on this sheet give the companion $UY_d=W^{n-d}X_d$, with exponent pair $(n,n-d)$. Only the first relation is written in the manuscript.
