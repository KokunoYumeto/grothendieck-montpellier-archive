# Mathematical notes on scans 145–159

Equation numbers are the author's. The entry identifiers also locate the checks in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 145–146.** An orientation of a polygonal star gives a cyclic permutation of its vertices. Formula (44) relates the permutations attached to two orientations by raising one to the power $\lambda$. Independence of the chosen vertex initially requires a common orientation sign. This gives an action of $\Lambda_n^*$ in (46)–(47) and the torsor of oriented types in (48). A marginal note on 146 includes the octahedron.

**U2 · Scans 147–150.** To compare the orientation signs, the author considers the abelianization of the group generated by $\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_\infty$. For odd $n$ he identifies a unique nontrivial sign character. For even $n$ he studies the possible permutation of three such characters. He passes to a congruence group over $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ in (52)–(54). A matrix calculation and an argument about odd primes are struck through on 149. On 150 his argument goes through reduction modulo 4.

**U3 · Scans 150–151.** A rotation about $s_0$ preserves the orientation of a second polygonal structure on its star. The author uses this to identify a common subgroup $G_0^+$ in (56), completing the argument for (48). Transport of oriented types then gives $\chi$ with values in $(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^*$ in (57)–(58). Reduction modulo the two signs recovers $\chi'$, the “caractère demi-cyclotomique”.

**U4 · Scans 152–153.** Diagrams (59) and (60) relate the two characters and identify the kernel of $\chi$. The diagonal map in (60) is squaring. In (62)–(64) the author expresses $Z\cap G_0^+$ through the square roots of 1 modulo the two signs. He distinguishes the exponent of 2 in $n$ and lists the cases where the intersection is trivial. On 153 he uses (65) for that list and again for a calculation at powers of 2.

**U5 · Scans 153–154.** An oriented face determines an oriented type. The two orientations of a face correspond to the two orientations of the structure of that type. The author draws the Cartesian square (67) and the corresponding square (68) for edges. Formula (69) and the diagram with $\mathrm{Rep}(S,F)$ in (70) express compatibility when an edge has the orientation induced by its incident face.

**U6 · Scans 154–157.** Fixing $s_0$ gives the stabilizer extension (71). A choice of a $Z$-orbit $D_0$ in the star gives a candidate complement $T$, its stabilizer in $G_{s_0}$. In (75)–(78) the edge type on a ray controls the kernel of $\chi|_T$. Replacing $g$ by $g\circ\tau_\infty$ lets the author choose the sign of a lift. This supplies the splitting announced in (77). The sketch on 156 accompanies a cancelled version of the ray argument.

**U7 · Scans 157–159.** The author identifies the axioms used for the splitting, then starts with a regular triangulated polyhedron and a prescribed group $Z$ centralizing $G_0$. With (79) he constructs the classes $A_\lambda$ and their type set, allows an octahedral exception, and defines the group in (80). The extension (81) has squaring on its lower diagonal. The last paragraph introduces equivalence for structures on the same vertex set with a group of permutations.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high for the finite check. Scans 152–153.** For each $3\le n\le256$, I counted the roots of $x^2=1\bmod n$ modulo $x\sim-x$. Every count agrees with $2^{\nu(n)-1+\varepsilon}$ using the three cases in (63)–(64), and every trivial case falls in the first (65). This supports the minus reading in (64) arithmetically. The sign and the lower cases remain marked in the transcription.
- **R2 · Model, high. Scans 152, 157 and 159.** Squaring in (60) descends to sign classes because $(-\lambda)^2=\lambda^2$. The sign correction on 157 multiplies the lift's character by $\chi(\tau_\infty)=-1$. Both operations forget the choice of sign at the level of classes. The abstract diagram (81) retains the squaring map.
- **R3 · Model, low. Scan 151, L6–10. Reading question.** The frozen wording of (56) is “les caractères d'orientation”. A fresh look at the joined strokes suggests “les conventions d'orientation”, especially the pair of low v-shaped joins and the apparent ending *-tions*. The noun itself lay outside the frozen set of marks. This question is confined to the notes.
- **R4 · Model, low. Scans 152, 158 and 159.** The exceptional nonidentity element is hard to name consistently. The characters beside 4 on 152 are rounded. The symbols in braces on 158 and 159 have two short horizontal strokes and admit an epsilon reading. The shared exceptional case suggests a common referent, but the letterforms do not establish a uniform spelling.
