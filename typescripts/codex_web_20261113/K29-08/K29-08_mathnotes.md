# K29-08 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Inertia (117).** Fix $x$, its strict localization $\bar X$, a principal cover of $U$ and a prolongation with $G$-action. The “groupe d'inertie relatif à” a point $\xi$ of the geometric fibre is its stabilizer. For a transitive fibre this gives a conjugacy class. The writer specifies its dependence on the prolongation and describes a choice by normalization when $X$ is normal at $x$ and $\bar U$ is nonempty.

**Finite group schemes (126).** The letter resumes in the middle of hypothesis (v), with $H_{\mathrm{inf}}$, $H$ and $H_{\text{sép}}$ in an exact sequence. Over a perfect field the writer identifies the reduced subgroup with the quotient and cautions that “on aura seulement un produit semi-direct”.

**Pointed isomorphisms and classes (126).** The lemma gives $u=v$ for isomorphisms preserving the prescribed points. The proof reduces to trivial $P$ by twisting and invokes a description of a section of $Q$. The writer then defines $Z(S,a;G)$ using pointed $F(G)$-torsors and states compatibility with products and equalizers.

**An exact-sequence conjecture (129).** The letter asks how to relate the groups of the fibres to groups for $X$ and $Y$. With a base field it proposes an exact sequence involving the relative group, the group pro-scheme on $X$ and the pullback of the one on $Y$. The product formula for complete schemes over an algebraically closed field is introduced as a consequence.

**An abelian variety and its families (129–130).** Applying the proposed formula and the recipient's reasoning, the writer describes minimal principal covers by isogenies and writes $\pi_1(X/k)=\varprojlim {}_nX$, including the infinitesimal parts of the multiplication kernels. He passes to Cartier duals, considers the $p$-component and asks what module should play the role of the Weil module. For an abelian scheme over $Y$, he points to the finite flat kernels and their formal projective limit. A timetable for future writing closes the letter.

**A functor of pointed torsors (131).** For a proper flat $f:X\to S$ with a section $\sigma$ and the stated condition on $f_*\mathcal O_X$, $F_G(T)$ classifies $G_T$-torsors on $X_T$ trivialized along $\sigma_T$. The leaf treats automorphisms, asks about representability and records left exactness for faithfully flat quasi-compact maps. Its inverted cancelled diagram has a different orientation and its own labels.

**Rational structures (132).** The English sheet finishes a coefficient argument for a $k$-structure on a space of multilinear maps. It treats images and inverse images of rational subspaces. It then proves the graph criterion for rationality of a linear map, using the projection of the graph onto the domain.

**Conjugates (132).** Section 5 fixes a $k$-isomorphism $\sigma:k'\to k''$ and announces a map $v\mapsto v^\sigma$. The sheet ends at “such that:”, before the defining properties.

**A representability criterion (133).** Five conditions on $F$ concern inductive limits of rings, descent and localization, complete local rings, separatedness, and values on fields. The conclusion is étale, separated, quasi-finite representability. Over a complete local ring with algebraically closed residue field, the writer lifts the elements of $F(k)$ to $F(A)$ and restricts them to the punctured base $S_1$.

**Gluing local schemes (135).** Local schemes $X(s)$ come with Cartesian transition squares and the relation $\varphi_{st}\varphi_{tu}=\varphi_{su}$. A sideways addition asks for local étale realizations and compatible maps. Its last words are doubtful. The lemma asserts existence of a global $X$ and uniqueness up to unique isomorphism, then starts a noetherian induction.

**Admissible group schemes (137).** For connected reduced $S$, the page studies finite flat $G$ in a sequence with geometrically injective kernel $U$ and separable quotient $\Gamma$. The writer asserts uniqueness of $U$ and gives a base-change proposition with a faithfully flat converse.

**Sections and pointed automorphisms (138).** The corollary applies admissibility to $\mathrm{ad}(P)$. Propositions 2 and 3 relate triviality of pointed automorphisms to equality of two sections agreeing at a point. A bracketed refinement identifies $S$ with the reduction of a connected component by means of the section.

**Minimality and a comparison of homomorphisms (138–140).** Minimality is defined by a condition on reductions of the structure group. Proposition 4 asserts injectivity of $u\mapsto u_*(\alpha)$ for a minimal class. For two maps with the same class, scan 139 sets the pointed bundles over a diagram involving $G'\times G'$ and the diagonal. Scan 140 forms their fibre product $(R,\lambda)$ over $(Q,\eta)$ and ends with a comma.

## Questions and conjectures

- **126.** “essentiellement provisoires” qualifies conditions (v) and (vi), the latter being “$S$ est réduit”.
- **129.** Without a base field “il n'est pas clair cependant comment et à quels groupes fondamentaux” the fibre groups attach. With a base field “une conjecture de départ serait que” the three objects are joined by an exact sequence. “Il n'est pas dit que la conjecture envisagée soit plus difficile à démontrer” compares it with the ordinary case. An aside about dispensing with a section and a suggestion involving a complete local ring are cancelled.
- **129–130.** The $p$-component “doit jouer le rôle du module de Weil”. The writer expects a functorial Witt module and calls its division into “les trois morceaux que tu connais bien” easy to verify. He hopes for “une démonstration uniforme” and writes “J'espère arriver dans l'année prochaine à une théorie satisfaisante du groupe fondamental”.
- **131.** “Cas général??” follows the automorphism remark. “Le foncteur $F_G(T)$ en $T$ est-il représentable ??” is a separate question.
- **137.** “pour cette assertion, l'hypothèse ‘$S$ réduit’ est-elle nécessaire ?” refers to uniqueness of $U$. The shorter marginal question repeats the condition.
- **139.** The marginal words admit “Sans rigueur” and “Sens rigoureux”. Whether the writer questioned the argument there remains open.
