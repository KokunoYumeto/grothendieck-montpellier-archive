# Mathematical notes · cote 157-1, scans 132–149

## What the pages do

**Scans 132–133.** The cover reads “Cofinalité” (faint) and II. A seven-entry contents table runs from “Mise en train” to “Morphismes cofiltrants et loc. cofiltrants”. It assigns pages 1–96, with a brace joining entries 5 and 6. The index already associates cofinality with $W$-asphericity.

**Scan 134.** A cancelled diagram and a fresh diagram relate immersions, étale and coétale maps, and maps bearing the labels $W$-lisse, $W$-propre and $W$-parfait. Below them three tests involve categories over an object, under an object, and over a specified arrow. A small example with $0'$, $0$, $1$ and $2$ accompanies the circled answer “Non”. The margin identifies an empty fibre over $1$.

**Scans 135 and 137.** The first cube compares slices before and after changing the base. Under “Catégories sur $\Delta^2$”, the vertices, pairs of vertices and full triangle receive categories with labels such as “asph.”, “fib” and “cof”. Scan 137 redraws the relationships with tildes and a central “cart”. Several small diagrams then vary the arrows through $0$, $1$ and $2$ in search of examples.

**Scans 136 and 138.** The two typescript leaves belong to another text. In its own pagination, PU 50 (scan 138) assigns an orientation to a pair of terms through their yin and yang roles. An undirected affinity between them can be felt in degrees. PU 51 (scan 136) continues the sentence about working with such a graph and describes the mathematician shaping notions as a theory takes form. Section 16, dated “(25 mars)”, returns to the flower. Twelve outer yang tips meet through twelve inner yin points. The handwritten footnote explains which direction counts as “bas”.

**Scan 140.** A proposed equivalence of five conditions links “grand devant $\pi$”, the size of the set of arrows of $I$, a cofinal functor from a finite filtered category, a one-object category with an idempotent $p$, and a section over the terminal presheaf. Successive implication diagrams show the attempted order of the argument.

**Scan 141.** A rough sheet considers a fully faithful cofinal inclusion $J\hookrightarrow I$, the categories bearing the exponent $B$, and the transfer of a cardinal condition. Below the square are ordinal and cardinal calculations and a map whose fibres are to be cofinal. Some intermediate terms are struck out.

**Scans 143–144.** For a presheaf $F$ on $C$, restriction along $\alpha:C'\to C$ gives comparison (1) between two inverse limits. Grothendieck gives a sufficient condition using weakly left filtered comma categories. To obtain an isomorphism on the adjunction unit $F\to\alpha_*\alpha^*F$, he considers factorizations of $u:x_1\to x_0$ through an object $\alpha(x')$. The insertion “suffit” replaces “faut”.

**Scans 144–145.** For a triangle over $S$ the analogous comparison is between $f_*F$ and $f'_*\alpha^*F$. Slicing at $s_0$ leads to triples $(x',\zeta,u)$. Their compatibility is displayed by a triangle whose long side is $u_0:f(x_0)\to s_0$.

**Scans 145–147.** A finite ordered category with an initial object provides the link from filteredness to contractibility. Grothendieck asks what happens when derived sections replace ordinary limits. He distinguishes strictness of $\alpha$ from strictness of its opposite and asks which comparison survives for abelian presheaves.

**Scans 147–148.** Nonemptiness of the slices is used for injectivity of the restriction of ordinary sections. To construct an inverse, one takes a compatible family on $C'$ and assigns $u^*(\zeta(x'))$ to $u:x\to\alpha(x')$. The triangle on scan 148 makes the values equal along arrows. Connectedness then removes the dependence on the choice of $(x',u)$. The next paragraph interprets these values as a section of a constant presheaf and introduces $c_{x_0}$.

**Scan 149.** Two diagrams express how evaluation varies along $v:x_0\to x_1$. Taking components of the comma categories gives a presheaf $x\mapsto\pi_0({}_{x\backslash}C')$. The first box compares the limit on $C'$ with maps from this presheaf to $F$. The last box proposes a derived Hom description involving $L\alpha_!(k_{/C'})$, labelled “homologie relative de $C'$ sur $C$”.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, medium confidence · Scan 134.** Counting the elementary faces gives one way to assess the doubtful marginal number. The twelve main vertices in the uncancelled diagram form three square layers with four connecting faces in each gap. Enumeration gives $3+4+4=11$. This favours the reading “12-1=11”. The auxiliary dashed factorization is excluded from the count. The computation leaves the Cartesian property open.

**R2 · Model, high confidence · Scans 145–146.** The finite ordered hypothesis accounts for the strength of the first observation. In a nonempty finite poset, repeatedly taking a common lower bound gives a lower bound for the entire list of elements. That element is least. The later observation about connectedness concerns the limit of a constant diagram and has a different hypothesis.

**R3 · Model, high confidence · Scans 147–149.** The value at $(x',u)$ in the proof on scan 148 already defines a function on connected components. Compatibility of the family gives equality at the ends of each arrow, and hence along each finite zigzag. This explains the appearance of $\pi_0$ in the square on scan 149. The higher-degree comparison on scan 147 remains a question.

**R4 · Model, low confidence · Scans 143 and 148.** There is a direction to check when reading these sheets together. Scan 143, page-file line 25, reads $u:\alpha(x')\to x_0$ in the description of the pairs. Scan 148, line 3, inserts $x\to\alpha(x')$, the direction used in $u^*(\zeta(x'))$. Applying the latter construction to the earlier pair would require $x_0\to\alpha(x')$. The superscript and slice notation around the first occurrence are unsettled, so these pages alone do not establish an intentional change of convention.
