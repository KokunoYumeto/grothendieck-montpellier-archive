# K83-01 · Mathematical notes

The page references are PDF scan numbers. L numbers refer to the separate TeX files. Quotation marks here delimit readings from the manuscript. Some of the French remains uncertain as marked in the transcription. The identifiers connect claims actually checked in this run with CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**046 · A codiagonal described by repères.** A symmetry of paired indices in (9) is followed by a picture of a square and its diagonals. From a choice involving codiagonals the author passes to an application out of a torsor of repères. He wants a simpler description of a category currently presented by 24 arrows, opposite in pairs. The page begins in the middle of an earlier discussion.

**048–049 · Trialités and n-alités.** The data consist of a set of indices, a common object, targets indexed by that set and arrows to the targets. For each two-element subset of indices the map to the product of the two targets must be an isomorphism. The author distinguishes additive categories and sets, then specializes to sets. He sets aside empty or trivial cases and describes a family of quotients of the common set.

**049–051 · Incidence description.** The disjoint union of the targets supplies a vertex set \(S\). A relation \(R\subset S\times E\) assigns to \(x\) the vertices incident with it. The author calls these sets \(n\)-uples, and calls a pair of distinct vertices an edge when some \(n\)-uple contains it. Conditions (i)–(iv) discuss uniqueness, the equivalence relation given by nonadjacency, meeting each part of the partition, and excluding the case in which every fibre is a singleton.

**052–053 · Vertices of two complementary icosahedral structures.** The April title sheet opens a new unit. The next sheet compares the two oriented covers using separate maps for vertices, edges and faces. At a vertex the rotations form a free rank-one module over \(\mathbb F_5\). Its four generators fall into two pairs modulo sign. Doubling takes one pair to the other. Iterating this choice produces antipodism. The pentagon sits beside this construction.

**053–054 · Faces and edges.** A face is paired with its complement on the other structure. The two face maps compose to identities. The corresponding edge calculation involves two-element sets and their wedges. Choosing the required bijection for an individual edge amounts to choosing one of its elements. The author explicitly revises the edge comparison by passing to oriented edges. The resulting maps have identity composites.

**055–056 · An oriented biicosahedron and its complement.** Two icosahedral components of an oriented combinatorial polyhedron are linked by a pair of vertex bijections. Composition with antipodism exchanges the two bijections. The quotient data yield complementary structures on a common set. A second formulation uses isomorphisms to complementary objects. On the next page the author considers extending an isomorphism from one component and describes the permutation of two mutually complementary structures on twelve vertices.

**057–058 · A quotient, an action and two extensions.** After writing down a candidate quotient of order four, the author tests what two independent choices of orientation distinguish. He proposes an action of a cyclic group of order four on oriented icosahedra. Its generator passes to the complement, and its square reverses orientation. He aligns two extensions with common kernel, revisits the possibility of a map between them, and suggests a functor through the constructions with skew icosahedra and the orientation cover.

**059–060 · The congruence-map model.** A sequence with \(\mathrm{Sl}(2,\mathbb F_5)\) and \(\mathrm{Gl}(2,\mathbb F_5)\), both modulo sign, is related to the four oriented structures. The author proposes a common ambient product group \(G\) for the two extensions. The four orientations of a pair of icosahedra give a combinatorial square. In the revised wording he describes the smaller extensions by inverse images of subgroups acting on this square, including its rotations.

**060–061 · From pairs of vectors to a cone.** For a free symplectic module of rank two the author associates to the pair \(\{a,-a\}\) an endomorphism written \(a\otimes a\). He places it in a trace-zero cone of nilpotents. The note in the margin raises a field hypothesis. The next page describes the special-linear symmetry, scalar multiplication by \(\lambda^2\), and the exchange of two classes by nonsquares. The writer asks how far the description extends to an Azumaya algebra of rank four.

**062 · The finite-field case and transport of structure.** With \(p\) odd, the author considers the nonzero nilpotents of an Azumaya algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\), the two classes and the action of homotheties. He gives an index-two condition on automorphisms fixing the components. In the edge condition for a transported map both endpoints acquire the same transformation. The final observation starts with \(\lambda^2=-1\) and continues beyond the lower edge of this scan.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model, high confidence, scan 053.** In the four units modulo 5, doubling interchanges \(\{1,4\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\). Its square sends each input to its negative, as does the square of multiplication by 3. I checked the four residues. This accounts for antipodism in the displayed vertex calculation.

**R02 · Model, high confidence, scan 059.** Direct enumeration of the invertible two-by-two matrices over \(\mathbb F_5\) gives 240 classes modulo the scalar pair \(\{\pm I\}\). Each determinant occurs on 60 classes. This verifies the sizes and index in the displayed sequence. The enumeration does not test the action on the manuscript’s four geometric structures.

**R03 · Model, low confidence as a reading, scans 059–060.** The automorphism group of a four-cycle has order 8, confirmed by checking its vertex permutations. That agrees with the order in the interline note on 059. It makes the nearby \( \mathfrak S_5 \) on 060 hard to attach to this group. Its position next to the cancellation prevents a secure attribution.

**R04 · Model, high confidence for the two finite examples, scans 060–062.** In the split matrix model with the usual alternating form on a two-dimensional vector space, I checked \(a\otimes a\) for every nonzero vector over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\). The images contain 4 of the 8, and 12 of the 24, nonzero nilpotent matrices respectively. A nonsquare scalar gives the other half in both examples. The test also confirms the \(\lambda^2\) scaling law in these two fields. The ambiguous remark at \(p=3\) does not say clearly which objects it counts.

**Reading question · Model, low confidence, scan 053 L30–35.** The frozen TikZ draws the pentagon’s internal strokes as dashed segments of a star. In the ink, several interior strokes are short and disconnected. A reconstruction with isolated short marks and no completed inner path is also possible. The weak junctions leave the intended extent of the lines uncertain.

**Reading question · Model, low confidence, scan 053 L71.** The frozen right-hand term of the face bijection is an unprimed \(t\) with primed structure subscript. A prime on the face as well as on the subscript is possible. Crowded ink around the t and its index prevents a secure separation.
