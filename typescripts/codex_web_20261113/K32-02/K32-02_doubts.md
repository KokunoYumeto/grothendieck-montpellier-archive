## Legibility and attribution

The squared-paper lemma and the signed letter usually allow individual letters and accents to be followed. The quickest hand on scan 25, the doubly crossed fibres clause on scan 26, and the upside-down working sheet on scan 29 are less secure. Scan 33 needs both orientations. Tiny indices and overwritten objects on scans 34 and 36 are harder to distinguish than their surrounding arrows. On scan 35 the dark heading is clear, but the faint lower field may be transfer or show-through. Its TikZ tracing is approximate; no continuous lower text could be read. The only entire word left illegible is under the heavier cancellation on scan 26.

P. Deligne signs scan 31. Its signature supports a comparison of hands on neighbouring leaves, but only the letter identifies its writer by name. The inventory uses “unknown” for short unattributed slips and ambiguous cover marks. “Other hand” records a distinction or correspondence of handwriting, without identifying an unsigned author.
