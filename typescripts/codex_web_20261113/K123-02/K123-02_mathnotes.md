# Mathematical notes · cote 123

## What the pages do

**015 and 024 · Cover titles.** The first announces a résumé of a conjugacy theorem and a letter to Kostant. The second announces diagrams of homogeneous spaces related to Kostant and Coxeter elements and cyclic points.

**026 · Dictionary of homogeneous spaces.** A three-column table associates descriptions, short names and groups. It passes from elements to subgroups, pairs involving a torus, pairs of tori in opposition, cyclic lines and Coxeter classes. The final rows add a Borel or a normalized cyclic element. Their right-hand entries end with $\mu_h$, the centre sign, and $1$. The footnote ties normalization to a choice on the line $\Delta$.

**027 · Two comparison diagrams.** The first relates the centre, $\mu$, their product, dotted products with tori, normalizers and their intersection. The second compares the corresponding spaces. Its one- and two-starred edges have separate conditions in the right margin. A pencil outline limits the scope of the question about existence.

**029 · Intersections.** Starting with a pair $(T,T')$ in opposition, the large grid intersects torus and normalizer data with $N'$, $N(D)$ and $T'$. Its repeated rows identify some intersections; its edge labels give quotients. A smaller grid feeds into the closing observation about two extensions inside $W'$ and $W$.

**030 · Symmetry and its sign.** The first part reduces the computation of $\alpha$ to $P=Q=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)_T$ and specifies the factor to resolve. Section 2.1.9 constructs $\beta$ by exchanging $P,Q$. Both horizontal arrows in (2.1.9.2) are symmetry isomorphisms. The displayed sign relation (2.1.9.3) precedes the statement of the anticommutative-diagram lemma.

**031 · Centralizers and a determinant.** A grid centred on $\gamma$ joins fixed elements of the primed torus to a quotient written $P'/(1-c)P'(1)$. The annotations attach reasons to individual edges and to the zero under $T\cap T'$. “À prouver” introduces a normalizer assertion. The author relates it to integral combinations of $\alpha_i+\psi$ and writes a determinant. A final paragraph returns to $N(T)\cap N(D)$.

**032 · Compatibility with a change of integer.** The diamond concludes an argument about torsion. Corollary 2.1.12 gives the square for $n',n$, with $m\otimes m$ above and $m$ below. The following paragraph checks it on $x,y$. Section 2.1.13 groups the maps for powers of a prime into a morphism of projective systems. The page breaks after “Les”.

**034 · Cyclic lines and descent.** Part a) relates cyclic lines normalized by $\mu_h$ to tori $T'$ and writes $T'=\mathrm{Cent}(D)$. Part b) obtains a line $\Delta$ by descent from $D^{\otimes h}$, and also describes it using the Kostant map. In the slanted note, failure to choose $a$ over a classifying topos leads to an obstruction and two restrictions in cohomology. The final paragraph compares two characters of exterior automorphisms.

**035 · Rigidifications.** The data $(T,T',B,B')$ consist of two tori in opposition and Borels containing them. The text gives a free action, a finite étale quotient and a rank written with one slash, $w^2/\varphi(h)hz$. It constructs $T'$ as a centralizer and describes the two schemes of Borels, $Q$ and $Q'$. The subgroup $\Phi=N\cap N'$ acts on their product. The last lines discuss the action on $(Q\times Q')/\Phi$ through the negative highest-root space.

**036 · Construction of the Tor map.** The typescript introduces the canonical map from the tensor product of two $n$-torsion groups to $\mathrm{Tor}_1(P,Q)$. The corrected working hypothesis only annihilates $Q$. A derived tensor identity gives a spectral sequence. The author uses a two-term resolution to give the Tor values in degrees $0$, $1$ and $i\ge 2$, then identifies the desired map in the resulting exact sequence as an edge homomorphism.
