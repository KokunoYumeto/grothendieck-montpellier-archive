# K22-01 · Mathematical notes

The two printed notes name Jean-Pierre Jouanolou as their author. The typescript begins on its own page 24, in the middle of a sentence. Its property P and several of its references presuppose earlier sheets. Numbers below follow the visible revisions, with old numbers identified where the prose still uses them. ## What the pages do

**Scan 2, first offprint, Proposition 1.** For a regular equicharacteristic local ring with flat D(A) and a reduced quotient A/𝔞, the proposition relates regularity of the quotient to the vanishing of a first Tor group and to vanishing in all positive degrees. The A inside D(A) in the two Tor expressions carries a pen stroke referring to A/a in the margin. If D(A) is free, a fourth condition is freeness of D(A/𝔞).

**Scan 2, Corollaries 1–3.** The first corollary gives a freeness criterion for regularity of a reduced localization of a quotient of a convergent-series algebra in positive characteristic, with (k : kᵖ) finite. The second gives the criterion for a reduced « localité ». The third assumes analytic reducedness and finite type of the separated module D′(A), and uses its freeness.

**Scan 2, Propositions 2 and 3.** The note passes from regularity tests to the closedness of the singular locus. Proposition 2 concerns affine algebras over a regular equicharacteristic base with free D(A). Proposition 3 concerns schemes of finite type over a regular group scheme over a perfect field. The closing notation reserves L with subscript A for the residue field and K with subscript A for the fraction field of a domain.

**Scan 3, first lemma and sequence.** The kernel of the canonical map from the completed base change of D(A) to D(Â) lies in an intersection, taken over M for which D(A)/M is finite as an A-module. A decomposition called « semi-fini au sens de Suzuki » yields an exact sequence. The displayed middle term is printed simply (Â).

**Scan 3, Proposition 4 and its application.** For an analytically integral « localité », the fraction field of the completion is separable over the original fraction field. The following paragraph gives an analogous assertion for convergent series under the stated characteristic and finiteness assumptions.

**Scan 3, Proposition 5.** For A = k{T} over a complete algebraically closed valued field, the field K with subscript A is called « quasialgébriquement clos (C¹) ». The paragraph before the statement connects it with a difficulty in Lang's argument.

**Scan 3, Proposition 6.** A special case concerning series and rational function fields is combined with the structure theorem of Cohen. If a regular local ring contains k and its residue field is separable over k, the proposition concludes separability of its fraction field over k.

**Scans 3–4, reduction lemma and theorem.** A lemma treats a local domain obtained by localizing a finite A-algebra at a prime over the maximal ideal. The subsequent theorem gives analytic reducedness for the indicated integral localizations over a regular local A satisfying either a characteristic-zero condition on its fraction field or freeness of D(A). Pencil notes query injectivity in the lemma and the localization argument used for the theorem.

**Scan 4, two corollaries of the reduction theorem.** The first, attributed to Chevalley, treats integral localities. The second concerns integral localizations of quotients of a convergent-series algebra over a field with the stated condition (k : kᵖ) < +∞. The text invokes normalization lemmas.

**Scans 4–5, Proposition 7 and the end of the offprint.** A separability hypothesis on the completion of an analytically integral local ring is used to assert that the integral closure in a finite extension of its fraction field is a finite A-module. Applications to localities, analytic localities and regular Hopf algebras follow. The text then draws an analytic-normality conclusion and gives eleven references. Scan 5 contains the printer's imprint.

**Scan 6, second offprint, local notation and exact sequences.** A unit-preserving monomorphism A → B is accompanied by the relative module D with subscript A. The note introduces the residue fields, the ranks of the maximal ideals and the field invariant {L : K}. It writes two local exact sequences, one for separable residue extension and one with a denominator involving the intersection with A[Bᵖ].

**Scans 6–7, definition and local structure.** The note calls B « quasi-séparable » when $D_A(B)=0$. The property can be tested at maximal ideals. In the separable-residue case it implies relation (R) and a condition on the residue fields. Finite generation of the maximal ideal simplifies (R). In positive characteristic the note gives (S), again with a converse under finite generation of the module of differentials.

**Scan 7, global structure.** For a noetherian quasi-separable algebra over a field, the text gives product descriptions in characteristic zero and under extra residue-field hypotheses in positive characteristic. For a general quasi-separable algebra it discusses maximality of primes, algebraicity of the residue fields and absolute flatness of the quotient by the nilradical.

**Scan 7, theorem on free differentials.** In the local integral setting, with $D_A(B)$ finite and the stated field and rank invariants finite, freeness is equivalent to a dimension formula. There are separate denominators for the separable and other residue-field cases. The note then gives the formulas when A is a field and when B is unramified over A.

**Scan 8, first corollary.** For a locality over K whose residue and fraction fields are separable over K, freeness of D with subscript K is equivalent to regularity.

**Scan 8, Proposition 1 and its corollary.** The displayed equality compares the difference of the ranks of the maximal ideals with the difference of two field invariants. Together with the other hypotheses it gives a homological dimension at most one for $D_A(B)$. A corollary uses the preceding regular-local criterion. The L subscripts in the prose hypothesis and in the display appear in different orders.

**Scans 8–9, Proposition 2 and the separated quotient.** Freeness with the specified rank dim A + {Lₐ : K} implies regularity under the given noetherian and separability assumptions. The module D′ is formed by dividing by the intersection of all powers of the maximal ideal times the module. Proposition 2 is asserted again after that substitution. Six references close the article, followed on scan 9 by the imprint.

**Scan 10, comparison of topological algebras.** The fragment starts with closure assumptions and introduces the indexed quotients of B and C. It compares an isomorphism of topological algebras with bijectivity conditions on the modules and their associated graded objects, together with homeomorphism assertions on the zeroth quotients. Pencil ovals, arrows and a transposition change the arrangement of the conditions.

**Scan 10, argument and square.** Inverse limits account for the passage between a map and its quotient maps. Nilpotence on the quotient rings accounts for finite filtrations. The final flatness argument refers to a lemma the writer hopes is already available. A handwritten square has gr B and gr C across the top and graded objects tensored with B₀ and C₀ across the bottom. Two arrows are labelled surj and two bij.

**Scan 11, Lemma 4.3.** Tensoring the product P of a family Pᵢ with a finitely presented module M gives the product of the tensors. The written proof uses a presentation by finite free modules and exactness of products, reducing to M = Aⁿ.

**Scan 11, Corollary 4.4.** When finite-type modules over A are finitely presented, products of flat A-modules are flat. The proof tests a monomorphism between finite-type modules and invokes the preceding lemma.

**Scan 11, Corollary 4.5.** An inverse limit of a strict projective system of projective A-modules admitting a countable cofinal system is flat under the preceding assumption on A. The proof identifies the limit with a product of projective modules. Earlier formulations with extra finite-type and countability language are struck through.

**Scan 12, Theorem 4.6.** With a preadmissible base A, noetherian quotients Aᵢ and the revised hypotheses on B, the theorem first asserts formal simplicity. The added condition b) concerns B₀ over A₀. Given an isomorphism on zeroth quotients, the second part produces a continuous lift to C and lists equivalent conditions involving formal simplicity, P, flatness of indexed modules and topological isomorphism. The typewritten B's in the numbered conditions have been retained.

**Scans 12–13, proof of Theorem 4.6.** The proof cites earlier results for several implications, lifts the inverse of the residue-level map, and applies the comparison lemma with the two rings exchanged. A replacement sentence on scan 13 supplies the last implications involving condition 5). The writer then proposes simplifying the exposition by restricting to noetherian hypotheses.

**Scan 13, Definition 4.7.** For a continuous homomorphism of noetherian preadic rings, a topological Cohen algebra is specified by separated completeness, condition P and formal simplicity. The added wording expresses the last condition on the reduction modulo an ideal of definition.

**Scans 13–14, Corollary 4.8.** For a fixed residue algebra B₀ over A₀, an existing Cohen lift is unique up to an isomorphism compatible with the specified reductions. For an adic B with that reduction the conditions include being a Cohen algebra, formal simplicity, P, flatness, and flatness on every quotient by Iⁿ⁺¹. The opening of scan 14 continues the list from scan 13.

**Scan 14, flatness, base change and the existence question.** The equivalence of conditions 3) and 3 bis) is referred to « la platitude supérieure ». The next paragraph calls stability under base change by completed tensor products a triviality. The existence discussion reduces the problem to a discrete base with a square-zero ideal, records that the general problem is open for the writer, and announces a special case.

**Scans 14–15, cancelled theorem and replacement Corollary 4.9.** A hatched draft theorem includes residue fields k and K and a separability condition. The surviving end is « pour sa topolo- ». The replacement sets out a local criterion. With the usual topologies, being a Cohen algebra is compared with flatness over A, completeness and the Cohen property, or formal simplicity, of the closed fibre over k.

**Scan 15, proof of the local criterion.** The argument reduces to the Artinian base case, lifts a regular system of parameters of B₀, and considers ideals generated by the componentwise powers. It asserts flatness of the quotients B/Jₙ and then projectivity. Two pen passages and a longer typed NB carry cancellations. The NB discusses the radicial-multiplicity assumption and requests a check of a cited result.

**Scan 16, Corollary 4.10.** A separable residue extension K/k and a complete local Cohen k-algebra B₀ give existence and uniqueness up to isomorphism of a Cohen A-algebra lifting B₀. The argument writes B₀ as a power-series ring and separates B₀ = K from B₀ = k[[T₁,…,Tₙ]]. One case invokes a result of 0 with subscript III; the other gives the lift using Â.

**Scan 16, Corollary 4.11.** Under the local conditions cited as « 3.9. » and a handwritten finite radicial-multiplicity qualification, a further equivalent condition is completeness and formal simplicity over A. To obtain flatness the proof changes the base by a finite-type free local extension with a suitable radicial residue extension, uses the separable existence case upstairs, and concludes flatness downstairs. A marginal qualification is linked to the separability step.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Model, high confidence.** The finite free case used in the proof of Lemma 4.3 on scan 11 checks directly for a fixed finite rank n. Both sides of its comparison map keep n coordinates for each member of the family. This check covers the reduction case M = Aⁿ.

**M2 · Model, high confidence.** The forward implication from flatness in 3) to the quotient-flatness in 3 bis) on scan 14 agrees with base change along A → A/Iⁿ⁺¹. The converse is the substantial direction referred to « la platitude supérieure ». Only the forward implication was checked here.

**M3 · Model, low confidence.** The pencil objection on scans 3–4 may connect the finite-algebra lemma's injectivity question with the later note « C ⊃ A !!! ». The two annotations concern compatible hypotheses. The pages contain no written counterexample and the verb in the first note is partly compressed.

**M4 · Model, low confidence.** The added fifth condition on scan 12 and the swapped B,C in the proof give a reason to consider a C-reading for the *intended* subject of some earlier conditions. The typed text says B. The revisions leave the intended final formulation unsettled.

**M5 · Model, low confidence.** On scan 15, proof line at « voisinages », the frozen reading is Iₙ with Jₙ as an alternative. The typed quotient on the next line uses Jₙ. The equality of the intended ideals is plausible, but the pen letter's pronounced crossbars make its spelling a reading question.

**M6 · Model, low confidence, reading question.** On scan 2, right margin beside Corollary 2 (page fragment L32), the frozen article in « contenu dans le critère jacobien » is le. On enlargement its first stroke makes a low loop. It could also read ce. Either article fits the phrase.
