# Mathematical notes · K157-1-12

## What the pages do

**The list (3.7)–(3.8), scans 182–183.** The three familiar functors on presheaves associated with $u:I'\to I$ are interpreted using $\mathrm{HOT}_{W_0}$ and the derivator attached to sets. A $W_0$-equivalence induces a bijection on $\pi_0$. Asphericity asks for each $I'_{/i}$ to be $0$-connected, and coasphericity asks the same of each $\,{}_{i\backslash}I'$. The proper and smooth conditions concern the inclusions of fibres into those categories. “Parfait” means both proper and smooth. The continuing brace distinguishes universal equivalences, fibrations and weak fibrations. Its margins give examples.

**Change of base (3.9)–(3.10), scans 183–184.** A cartesian square gives two comparisons for presheaves. The direct-image clause takes $u$ proper or $p$ smooth, and the clause for $u_!$ takes $u$ smooth or $p$ proper. The short predicates on scan 183 are difficult to read. The next sheet takes up coefficients in $M$ when the indicated direct images exist. It gives $W_0$-equivalence $\Rightarrow\mathbb D_M$-equivalence and mentions fully faithful embeddings of a small $M$. A marginal qualification excepts both notions of fibration. Proper and smooth functors are also characterized by what base change does to coaspherical and aspherical arrows, respectively.

**Proposition 3.1(a,b), scans 185–186.** Part (a) tests universal $W_0$-equivalence on the pullbacks of arrows $\Delta^1\to I$. Each such category must be $0$-connected. The alternative separates the fibres and the nonidentity arrows. Part (b) tests a $W_0$-fibration on the maps from connected components of $I'$ to their corresponding components of $I$. Each map must be a universal $W_0$-equivalence.

**Proposition 3.1(c) and its examples, scans 186–187.** A weak fibration must induce a bijection from the components of the fibre at $i$ to those of $\,{}_{i\backslash}I'$, and the latter components must form a locally constant presheaf. For $W_0$-smooth $u$, the conditions suffice. Étale coverings illustrate the distinction between weak and strong fibrations. In the $0$-connected case considered, the strong condition would force the covering to be an isomorphism. A separate observation interprets smoothness and properness as openness and closedness on the underlying spaces, using nonempty local fibres.

**Proposition 3.2, scans 187–188.** The first comparison is a colimit comparison for $F:I\to M$. The second compares limits of an $M$-valued presheaf on $I$ and its restriction to $I'$. Testing the second with set-valued presheaves suffices. The equivalent condition on $u$ is $W_0$-coasphericity. The right-hand NB on scan 188 suggests additional tests. The long note at the left relates the question to morphisms of topoi.

**The argument (3.2.3)–(3.2.5), scans 188–189.** The text passes from a comparison using the projections to the point to the map on Hom from the terminal presheaf. By adjunction, this map comes from $u_!e_{I'}\to e_I$. Its value at $i$ compares $\pi_0({}_{i\backslash}I')$ with the one-point set. The grade on the first $H$ of (3.2.3) has competing readings in the transcription.

**Definition 3.3, scan 189.** The conditions of 3.2 define a cofinal functor. The opposite functor supplies the dual notion, with the test on $I'_{/i}$. The text then applies the terminology to subcategories by way of their inclusions. Some of the wording of this last sentence is cancelled.

**Corollary 3.4, scans 189–190.** F0 is the bijection on components. F1 supplies an arrow $i\to u(i')$ for every $i$. F2 postcomposes a parallel pair $i\rightrightarrows u(i')$ with the image of an arrow of $I'$ that makes the pair equal. The corollary gives necessary conditions, sufficient conditions when $I'$ is weakly filtered, and equivalences when $I'$ is filtered. Part (d) treats fully faithful $u$ and prefiltered $I$, where F1 suffices. The marginal versions distinguish local from global filteredness.

**Proof of 3.4, scans 191–193.** For (b), F1 makes the coslice nonempty. The diagram shows how to find a common upper bound for two of its objects by passing to a common target in $I'$ and then applying F2. For (c), the map from the coslice to $I'$ is called coétale. The author uses local filteredness and $0$-connectedness to obtain filteredness of the coslice. The equalities on scan 192 give F2, and a second parallel pair gives the remaining filteredness condition for $I$. The first lines of scan 193 close this argument and dispose briefly of (d).

**Section 4, scans 193–194.** “Foncteurs biasphériques” asks when restriction $u^*:M(I)\to M(I')$ is fully faithful. Either available adjoint gives an isomorphism test. The author calls the property $M$-connexe or $M$-biasphérique. He passes to $M(S)$ in (4.3), considers a fully faithful embedding of coefficient categories in (4.4), and tests all small full subcategories in (4.5). Formula (4.6) states that set coefficients suffice for all $M$. The two raised marks on the target-side $M$ in (4.4) are uncertain.

**Cofinality with coefficients (4.7)–(4.8), scans 194–196.** An isomorphism on projective limits defines $M$-cofinality. Cofinality of every $I'_{/i}\to I_{/i}$ with those coefficients suffices for $M$-connectedness. For the converse the issue is whether a presheaf on $I_{/i}$ comes from $I$. The text singles out ordered $I$ and then set coefficients. The suggestion on scan 195 about a conservative right adjoint is crossed out, including its continuation on scan 196.

**Proposition 4.1, scan 196.** The list compares full faithfulness for all $M$, its adjunction versions when the requisite limits exist, and the corresponding tests for sets. Clause (c) uses representables for the left-adjunction test. A proposed (c′) is struck. The three final clauses test cofinality on slices, cocofinality on coslices, and $0$-connectedness of the category of factorizations over a specified $\alpha:i_0\to i_1$. The extract ends at that last condition.
