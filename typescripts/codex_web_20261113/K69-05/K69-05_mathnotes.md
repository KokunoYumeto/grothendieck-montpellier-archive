## What the pages do

**003–004. The graph criterion and its involution.** The theorem characterises a “graphe cubique” by 27 vertices, ten neighbours at each vertex and exactly one common neighbour for each adjacent pair. Fix a triangle $T=\{u,v,w\}$. Lemma 1 partitions the other 24 vertices into the three eight-element sets $E(u)^*,E(v)^*,E(w)^*$. The corollary to lemma 2 introduces $\sigma_T$, fixing the triangle and exchanging four pairs in each set. Lemma 3 describes how a vertex in the first set meets one point in each pair of the second. The two corollaries on 004 make $\sigma_T$ a graph automorphism.

**004–005. Intersections of sections.** The neighbours of $x$ in the $v$-class select a section over its four pairs. The difference of two sections is the function $\delta_v(x,y)$. Lemma 4 gives its support size as two when $x,y$ are unlinked vertices in the $u$-class. Lemma 5 completes two links to triangles in the third class. The proof of lemma 4 uses these triangles to exclude a third common choice.

**005–008. Partitions and their lifts.** Corollary 1 associates the partitions of type $(2,2)$ of $P_u$ with those of $P_v$. Corollary 2 describes the corresponding bijection on pairs of unlinked vertices. Lemma 6 states $c_{wv}c_{vu}=c_{wu}$ on partitions. For the lifted maps the displayed formula has an additional $\sigma$. The four little triangles used in the proof and the primed points on 008 keep track of that distinction.

**008–009. The set of triangles as a torsor.** The three partitions give a set of size three and an associated two-dimensional vector space $V$ over $\mathbb F_2$. The pages count 32 triangles outside the fixed triangle and 16 orbits under $\sigma$. Lemma 7 places the quotient in the 64-element product $P_u\times P_v\times P_w$. Its image is a torsor for the kernel $\Psi(V)$ of the sum of three coordinates. The proof establishes injectivity and then stability using the coordinate kernels. The final “NB” distinguishes this quotient data from the double covers still to be recovered.

**010–011. Pinning and uniqueness.** A second triangle and choices of order give the names $\alpha_i,\beta_i,\gamma_i$ and their primed partners to the 24 exterior vertices.
