# Mathematical notes, cote 136, scans 282–296

## What the pages do

**Semi-stable reduction and inertia, scans 284 and 282.** Proposition 3.5 relates reduction of an abelian scheme over a trait to its Tate module, the toric part and inertia acting unipotently with a two-step filtration. The proof invokes an orthogonality theorem. Corollary 3.8 uses a finite-index subgroup and connectedness of a unipotent algebraic group in characteristic zero. Scan 282 also identifies first cohomology with a Hom on the Tate module, refers back to 3.7 and begins a base-change assertion about a Néron model. It ends at “le morphisme canonique”.

**Pairing and its inputs, scan 296.** Section 9 introduces the monodromy pairing of two twisted constant character modules. It identifies their ℓ-adic versions and relates the pairing to Barsotti-Tate groups, their generic fibers and, for ℓ different from p, a Galois module. The case ℓ = p carries an explicit qualification.

**The inertia action, scan 290.** The typescript writes the displacement of x under g as u(g).x. It describes a transvection parallel to the toric part, adds the corresponding homomorphisms under composition and obtains (9.2.4), a homomorphism from I to a tensor product involving U/V and its orthogonal target.

**From the Tate twist to a bilinear form, scans 288 and 286.** Equations (9.2.5) and (9.2.6) give a map out of a Tate twist and an element of a twice-twisted tensor product. Equations (9.2.7) and (9.2.8) identify the toric factors using character groups. The beginning of scan 286 expresses the resulting invariant element as a Hom and identifies it with the pairing announced in (9.1.2).

**Extensions panachées, scan 286.** Section 9.3 fixes three objects P, R, Q in an abelian category, gives a filtration of X with specified successive quotients, and names E and F. Equation (9.3.2) lists two ordinary extensions to be used in the classification. The choice of outside factors in this display is discussed below.

**Equalizing two maps, scan 283.** The sheet sets P beside two parallel maps out of C⊗C′, writes a kernel and asks how to define a compatible map out of P. It continues with a chain of subobjects, an intersection called P∞ and a boxed product for a proposed morphism of coalgebras. Several arrows are tentative, and the underlined heading near the foot is abbreviated.

**Bilinearity and the coalgebra maps, scan 285.** The upper square relates a coproduct on C to an operation on V(C). A proposed bilinear map into V(C″) is followed through diagrams involving scalar action, Spec(A), Sym and several coactions. The two final tensor composites are written alongside another square.

**Graded modules and cutoffs, scan 287.** The notes introduce a nonnegatively graded ring, its degree-zero part, the grading shift t and the subcategories denoted by a low or high N. They describe how the cutoffs vary with N, how the shift moves them, and how the full family is related to the cutoffs at zero.

**Inclusion functors and adjoints, scan 289.** Relations (9)–(9.3) organize the inclusions for successive cutoffs. Proposition 1 lists four successive adjoints. The first construction keeps Pᵢ for i ≥ N and puts zero below N; an inclusion into P carries the annotation “hom d'adjonction”.

**Components and adjunction maps, scan 291.** The next functor is described using graded Hom from a truncated copy of S. The module action is obtained by transposing multiplication. Two adjunction maps and a triangular diagram follow. The final paragraph describes q_N(P) as a quotient by the submodule generated by the components of degree below N.

**Exactness, limits and localization, scan 293.** The corollary records preservation of limits, full faithfulness and localization properties for the functors. Its last lines identify the thick abelian subcategory associated with τ_N by a cutoff with upper index N−1.

**Coefficients in an abelian category, scan 295.** The author revisits the cutoff functors for graded modules with coefficients in C. He asks for a right adjoint using the representability of a Hom functor for fixed Q. The page then tries to describe a component of the representing object with an underlined graded Hom and a shifted copy of S.

**Partial sums and periodic sequences, scans 294 and 292.** The sheet headed “Problème MP 2” asks about a decreasing positive weight and a complex sequence with bounded partial sums. Part b) separates a periodic sequence into constant and zero-sum parts. Part c) constructs sequences supported at two residue classes and asks for a basis statement. The opening of 292 continues that exercise and asks for another demonstration using an alternating series.

**Geometric, trigonometric and periodic examples, scan 292.** The exercise sets uₙ = zⁿ, asks about bounded partial sums, convergence and uniform convergence, and studies the resulting function near z = 1. It then applies the conclusions to trigonometric series and roots of unity. Many operator positions remain blank in the typescript. The final sentence is cut short.

## Slips

- **S284, scan 284.** Proposition 3.5(ii) prints V ⊂ V⊥, and the last inference calls V⊥ ⊂ Uᴵ = V the same condition. Scan 290 uses V⊥ ⊂ V for semi-stable reduction. The written lines give opposite containments. The local correction would require choosing one direction consistently, and the first part of the proof would also need review.
- **S286a, scan 286.** In (9.2.9) the opening bracket before the first checked M is already closed before the scalar extension, leaving the close before I unmatched. A further opening bracket would group the whole tensor product.
- **S286b, scan 286.** The line after (9.3.1) clearly ends “munie d'une filtration”, with a final e. If its antecedent is “objets X”, “munis” supplies the agreement. The displayed filtration belongs to X.
- **S286c and S286r, scan 286.** The stated identifications give the refined pair 0 → Q → F → R → 0 and 0 → R → E → P → 0 (**S286r**). Equation (9.3.2) prints P as the subobject of F and Q as the quotient of E (**S286c**). The adjacent phrase “de R par P et de Q par R” follows the printed sequences.
- **S288, scan 288.** The first line has “appliqué a la fois”; later “s'interprèter” has a grave on the second e. The corresponding standard forms are “à la fois” and “s'interpréter”. In (9.2.7) an extra close appears in the first evaluation at k̄. The second line has an unprimed Tₒ and no vee over M′. The preceding reference to T′ₒ and the duals at the top of 286 make these plausible omissions. They have been retained.
- **S290, scan 290.** The generators are written gx − x “(u ∈ U, g ∈ I)”; (9.2.1) then uses x ∈ U. Substituting x for u in the first parenthesis would make the bound variable agree. The next sentence calls g ↦ u(g) a homomorphism from U. For fixed g it is u(g) that has source U in (9.2.2). The spelling “Plaçons nous” on the same page lacks the ordinary hyphen.
- **S294a, scan 294.** The introductory period condition ends “pour tout r”. Once r is fixed the equality should range over n. In the summation hint the printed cᵢ would have to read uᵢ, since the partial sums give Sᵢ − Sᵢ₋₁ = uᵢ. These corrections follow from the definitions on the sheet.
- **S292, scan 292.** The superscript in f) reads ix in cₙeⁱˣ. The immediately following cosine and sine have argument nx, suggesting inx for the exponential's exponent. Part g) has “et et b)” and prints c zⁿ without an index on c. The neighbouring series would suggest cₙ. The first letter of the later word read “dauf” or “sauf” is overwritten. It remains a reading question.

## Remarks of the model

- **M290, model remark, high confidence.** In the situation explicitly stated on 290, the operators measuring displacement have image in V⊥ ⊂ V and vanish on V. Consequently their pairwise composites vanish. This accounts for the addition law after (9.2.3).
- **M287, model remark, low confidence on the sign in (8).** The component formula (4.1) sends an object supported in degree 0 to one supported in degree −1. The direction of the first inclusion in (8) therefore deserves attention. The inclusion is overwritten, and two zero indices in the second inclusion have been corrected. This limits any claim about the author's convention.
- **M294a and M294r, model remark, low confidence as an interpretation of the missing operators.** If the bounds intended in a) are |Sₖ| ≤ M and |Rₙ| ≤ Mcₙ₊₁ (**M294a**), the second bound can fail. With M = 1, u₁ = −1, u₂ = 2, all later u zero, and cₖ = 1/k, the tail after n = 1 is 1 while Mc₂ is 1/2. With the same hypotheses, summation by parts gives the refined bound 2Mcₙ₊₁ (**M294r**). The page shows blank positions at the relevant relations.
- **M292, model remark, low confidence as an objection to g).** If the indices of (zⁿ) start at 1 and “de période r” allows a period other than the least one, z = 0 gives a constant zero sequence of period r with zʳ different from 1. For nonzero z and positive exponents, r is a period of zⁿ exactly when zʳ = 1 (**M292r**). The index relation before 1 is blank.
- **Reading question, scan 295, last equalities.** The final transcription reads “justifier”. “Justif…” as an unfinished instruction remains possible because the last strokes run together. This affects whether the equality was explicitly flagged by the author.
