## What the pages do

**Incidence description of a polygon, scan 2.** For an affine plane $E/S$ and $n\geq3$, a displayed set lists vertices $s_i$ and lines $d_i$. The incidence conditions run through the last and first vertices together. An attempted second set description has a cancelled predicate. The drawings show what the tuple can record when consecutive vertices or lines coincide.

**Difference operators and closure, scans 4–5.** Scan 4 places equations in $L_j(\lambda)$ beside the differences labelled $M_j$, then expands $M_1u_1$ and $M_2u_2$. Scan 5 identifies cyclic endpoints under individual factors. Its lower calculation obtains successive $u_i$ from $u_0,u_1,u$ and the multipliers. The last two lines, marked “relation entre”, identify $u_n$ with $u_0$ and $u_{n+1}$ with $u_1$.

**Quadruples of wedge products, scan 6.** For the oriented quadrilateral, the consecutive determinants are labelled $\mu_0,\dots,\mu_3$. The displayed relation is $\mu_0+\mu_2=\mu_1+\mu_3$ [Q06a]. On the locus $\mu_1\ne0$, the author writes $u_2=\alpha u_0+\beta u_1$, determines $\alpha,\beta$, and gives $u_2,u_3$ explicitly. The vanishing of $u_2$ yields the boxed pair $\mu_2=\mu_3=0$.

**Four distinguished points, scans 6–7.** The text associates a quadrilateral with a null side to one of four points. It exhibits three coordinate vectors and their rank-three matrix [P07], then identifies $\mathbb M(\Pi)$ for a combinatorial quadrilateral $\Pi=(S,A)$ with “l’enveloppe projective de $A$”. Five unlabelled sketches follow.

**Sequences of tests, scans 9–11.** Scan 9 compares $\mathcal F_i$, $T_i$ and powers of $\widetilde\Omega$ by projection and writes incidence conditions for the lower-case $f_i$. Scan 10 gives the successive choices explicitly, from $\mathcal F_1\subset\Omega$ through maps on the tilded families, and calls the sequence a “crible de longueur $\nu$”. Scan 11 supplies the interpretation. An operation $\omega$ gives a map on configurations $C$, and an effected operation singles out a fibre. A sequence singles out an intersection of fibres. The last lines evaluate operations at one configuration to produce a section over the space of virtual tests.

**Equations for critical polygons, scan 13.** The author introduces a common $u$, the cyclic recurrence for the edges, and the quantities $\mu_i=u_{i-1}\wedge u_i$ and $\alpha_i=u\wedge u_i$. The first displayed relation between these quantities is $\lambda_i\mu_i+\lambda_{i+1}\mu_{i+1}=\alpha_i$ [C13]. A column lists nonvanishing conditions. Below, the author writes a map involving two projective spaces and starts a reconstruction in the basis $u_0,u_1$. The exclusion after “$n$-polygônes critiques (mod Aff)” is difficult to read.

**The differential and elimination of multipliers, scans 15–16.** Scan 15 differentiates $f_i=x_{i-1}y_i-y_{i-1}x_i$ and collects the coefficients in $\sum\lambda_i\,df_i$ [D15a]. It expands successive $\lambda_i$ using the $y$ coordinates. Scan 16 places the general $x$ and $y$ formulas together, eliminates $\lambda_i$ to obtain a relation linear in $\lambda_0,\lambda_1$, and tests the cyclic endpoint. The resulting identity is labelled “OK”.

**Triangle and quadrilateral specializations, scans 16–17.** The bottom of scan 16 reduces the coordinate expressions for a triangle and compares the wedges of $u,v,w$ with $w=-(u+v)$. Scan 17 groups factors into $2\times2$ determinants, twice boxes $\lambda_0+\lambda_1=0$, and places each box beside a condition “non aplati” at a labelled corner. The last lines express $u_3\wedge u_0$ by using closure.
