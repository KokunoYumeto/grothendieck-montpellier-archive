# Mathematical notes on scans 428–442

## What the pages do

**Choice of paths, 428–429.** A path type has an initial and a terminal sign. Adding just one end step on either side can destroy reducedness or give the wrong end signs. Grothendieck therefore adds a pair of steps at each end. The diagram on 429 gives the end paths $\xi_u$ and $\eta_v$, the resulting paths $\widetilde c,\widetilde{c'}$, and the comparison between them. The two choices of $t_n$ on 428 are written differently. A sign in the second one remains uncertain.

**Comparison functors, 429–430.** The functor $\varphi$ sends $(c',w')$ to the padded path and its comparison map. In the opposite direction $\psi$ uses $\rho_c$ to compose a comparison back to $c$. The displayed map $\psi\varphi\to\mathrm{id}$ gives the stated homotopy. The author concludes smoothness and passes to the opposite category and the opposite system of types for properness. A lemma is still outstanding at the page turn.

**Factorization, 431–432.** Given $f\colon X'\to X$, Grothendieck pulls the path construction for the diagonal of $X$ back to $X'$. The large diagram identifies $C(f)$, the section $i_f$, and the composite $\overline f$ to $X$. He argues that the projection is proper and smooth (“parfait”) and that the section is a Hot-equivalence, using a perfect map with aspherical fibres.

**Relative 1-types, 433–434.** The chapter cover names integration of relative homotopy 1-types, with stacks in groupoids in parentheses. For a category fibred over $S$, the next sheet assigns the fundamental groupoid $\Pi(X_s)$ to each fibre and writes the resulting fibred category as $\Pi(X/S)$. A fibrewise Hot-equivalence induces an equivalence of the resulting fibred categories. The distinction between this and an isomorphism matters for the functor he leaves to be defined.

**Fibred replacement and direct image, 435.** An arbitrary $X\to S$ supplies a fibred category $\widetilde X$ with fibres $\,{}_s\backslash X$. When $X$ is already fibred, the maps $i,j$ on the fibres give $ji\simeq\mathrm{id}$ and a homotopy $ij\to\mathrm{id}$. The latter arrow need not be invertible. For $f\colon S\to T$ and a groupoid fibration $G$ over $S$, the direct-image construction assigns $\Pi({}_t\backslash G)$ to $t$.

**Transitivity, 436–437.** Grothendieck describes $f_!$ as a 2-functor preserving fibrewise equivalences. For $p\colon X\to S$ he writes the boxed comparison $f_!(\Pi(X/S))\simeq\Pi(X/T)$. Applying direct image to the trivial groupoid over $X$ prompts the proposed formula $(fp)_!\simeq f_!p_!$ on arbitrary fibred groupoids. The small handwritten capitals in the argument positions vary in shape.

**The two adjunction arrows, 437–439.** The boxed statement (P1) identifies the Hom category from $G$ to $f^*H$ over $S$ with the one from $f_!G$ to $H$ over $T$. Grothendieck constructs the arrow out of $G$ fibrewise. For the arrow to $H$, he changes the construction on 438 and composes through $\Pi(H/T)$ using a $T$-quasi-inverse on 439. That is where he stops the adjunction verification.

**Base change, 439–440.** Using direct image covariantly and inverse image contravariantly (P2), the square with horizontal maps $q,p$ and vertical maps $f,f'$ gives the comparison $f'_!q^*G\to p^*f_!G$, written as a leftward arrow. Grothendieck gives its adjoint form using transitivity and a commutativity map. He then proposes an equivalence for a cartesian square with invertible comparison cell if $f$ is 1-smooth or $p$ is 1-proper.

**Degrees of fidelity and asphericity, 440–441.** The three degrees of fidelity of $f^*$ require that its functor on Hom categories be faithful, fully faithful, or an equivalence, respectively. The marginal note relates this to taking $G=e_T$. The following sheet defines $i$-asphericity via $S/t$, including nonemptiness in degree $-1$, and names the derivator of relative $i$-types. The proposed terminology “3-fidèle” at the foot of 440 and the first theorem formulation on 441 are cancelled.

**Descent criterion, 441–442.** The live “Théorème ?” takes $i\in\{0,1,2\}$. Its alternatives are $(i-1)$-proper and $(i-1)$-aspherical, or $(i-1)$-smooth and $(i-1)$-coaspherical. The first conclusion is $i$-fidelity of $f^*$. In degree two the proposed test for descent of $G$ asks whether its restrictions to the fibres $S_t$ are equivalent to constant fibred groupoids. The last clause treats constancy over a 1-connected category. The corollary expresses the test through equivalences induced on groupoid fibres by vertical arrows of $S$.

## Remarks of the model

**R1. Model remark, high confidence about the arrow types (437, TeX lines 22–31).** In the boxed Hom expression, substitute $H=f_!G$. The identity on $f_!G$ corresponds in type to $G\to f^*f_!G$. Substituting $G=f^*H$ likewise gives the type $f_!f^*H\to H$. These are the source and target pairs displayed just below the box. This check concerns only the directions of the two arrows.

**R2. Model remark, high confidence about the bases (439, TeX lines 31–35).** Starting over $S$, the route $q^*$ then $f'_!$ ends over $T'$. The route $f_!$ then $p^*$ ends over $T'$ as well. This checks the common target base for the displayed leftward comparison. Invertibility is the separate question at the foot of the page.

**R3. Model reading question, low confidence about intention (437, TeX lines 38–39).** The frozen right-hand expression reads $\Pi({}_s\backslash G)$ after a fibre indexed by $f(s)$. Writing $\,{}_{f(s)}\backslash G$ would follow the $T$-index of the definition on 435. The enlargement shows a small $s$ and a short oblique, with no discernible $f$ or parentheses there. An implicit use of $f(s)$ is possible. The manuscript reading remains $s$.
