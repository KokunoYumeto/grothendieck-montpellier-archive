# Mathematical notes on cote 10, scans 185–198

## What the pages do

**The additive group, scans 185–186.** Grothendieck asks for polynomial matrix functions satisfying \(P(x+y)=P(x)P(y)\). For an irreducible representation he passes to the commutant and its centre. A crossed-out passage extends scalars. The surviving argument compares degrees \(n\) and \(2n\). He concludes that an irreducible representation has dimension one and states the triangular form with ones on the diagonal.

**Translation in positive characteristic, scan 186.** Composing with Frobenius and its iterates gives an operation on representation classes. Translation on the polynomials \(\lambda X^{p^h}+\mu\) gives two-dimensional examples \(E_h(y)\) whose upper-right entry is \(y^{p^h}\). He contrasts their number with the characteristic-zero case.

**Passage to a nilpotent operator, scans 187–188.** Proposition 2 identifies the characteristic-zero classification with that of finite-type \((X)\)-primary modules. The indecomposable \(E_n\) is represented by \(k[X]/X^n\). He records self-duality, \(d(E_n)=n\), and the unit \(E_1\).

**Multiplication in the additive-group ring, scans 188–189.** Setting \(\xi=E_2\), he realises a tensor power as a quotient by the squares of the variables. Its operator is multiplication by their sum. The top nonzero power has one-dimensional image. He uses triangularity to give the polynomial-ring presentation, then writes a two-column table through the fourth power. The last remark singles out the order structure as less explicit.

**Characters, scan 190.** A Laurent expansion of \(P(\lambda)\), compared at \(\lambda\lambda'\), gives orthogonal idempotent coefficients. For an irreducible representation a single character remains. Grothendieck records character multiplication and duality.

**Weight decomposition, scans 190–191.** With operator-valued coefficients, the sum of the projectors is the identity. He deduces complete reducibility and describes the class ring by the character group. He briefly records the differential and passes to split tori with a character group of rank \(n\).

**The semidirect product, scan 192.** The multiplicative factor acts on the additive factor by \(\lambda^p\). In characteristic zero the Lie bracket is \([Y,X]=pX\). Writing the space as a sum of weight spaces for the multiplicative factor shows that \(X_{ji}\) can be nonzero only when \(j-i=p\).

**From weights to a chain, scan 193.** If all components of \(X\) vanish, an indecomposable is one-dimensional. Otherwise he splits the weights into a residue class modulo \(p\) and its complement. Indecomposability leaves a single class. Consecutive spaces are joined by maps \(u_i:E_i\to E_{i+1}\).

**The two lemmas, scans 193–195.** The injectivity argument starts with a kernel vector and the line \(D\) it spans. A complementary hyperplane \(F\) gives two families \(E'_j,E''_j\) and a proposed decomposition into stable summands. Several draft versions of that construction remain on 194. The next lemma invokes the contragredient representation for surjectivity. The conclusion calls the maps bijections and the spaces one-dimensional.

**Models for the indecomposables, scan 195.** The models \(e_{n,k}\) are character twists of a truncated polynomial model. \(X\) acts by multiplication by \(Z\), while \(Y\) acts on \(Z^\alpha\) with weight \(\alpha p\). The page records the extreme weights, dimension, pairwise inequivalence and dual.

**Restriction and the coefficient ring, scan 196.** The arrows between the class rings go from \(A(H)\) through \(A(G)\) to \(A(F)\). The first map identifies characters with the one-dimensional classes. Restriction to \(F\) sends \(e_{nk}\) to \(e_k\). Grothendieck sets \(\Lambda=A(H)\) and introduces \(\xi=e_{02}\) as a proposed polynomial generator.

**The leading coefficient, scans 196–198.** He restricts an expansion in the \(e_{0i}\) to \(F\) and compares the augmented coefficients with the additive-group calculation. Positivity makes the leading coefficient a character \(\sigma_\alpha\). Two weight bounds give \(\alpha\leq0\) and \(\alpha\geq0\). The final proposition states \(A(G)=\Lambda[\xi]\), gives \(d(\xi)=2\), and identifies \(\check\xi\) with \(\sigma_{-p}\xi\).

## Statements left without proof

- **Scan 186.** “peut se mettre sous forme … triangulaire, avec des 1 sur la diagonale.” The page calls this a corollary and proceeds to characteristic \(p\).
- **Scan 186.** “sont inéquivalentes deux à deux” and “En car. 0, il n’y en a que deux, au contraire.” He gives the matrices for the first assertion. The classification on the following page supplies context for the second.
- **Scan 187.** “correspondent à certaines représentations lin. de l’algèbre de Lie abélienne” and “il résulte de la … prop. 1”. The passage to the associated infinitesimal representation is invoked here. The module classification is stated as “sont ceux qui sont du type”. Self-duality is introduced by “voit aussitôt que le dual”.
- **Scan 189.** “N. B. Le calcul explicite donne” introduces the two columns without the intermediate products.
- **Scan 191.** “Généralisation aux … tores” leads directly to the rank-\(n\) character group and \(A(G)\simeq Z(\Gamma)\). The short differential calculation also has no derivation on the sheet.
- **Scans 194–195.** For Lemme 2 the directions are “de façon analogue” or “appliquant le lemme 1 à la repr. contragrédiente.” After the bijectivity statement, “les \(E_i\) sont de dim. 1” appears without a separate argument. The dual formula at the foot of 195 is stated.
- **Scan 196.** “forment une telle base” invokes the \(\Lambda\)-basis of classes \(e_{0i}\) from the preceding classification before he proves that the powers of \(\xi\) form a basis.

- **Scan 190, left margin.** “\(k\) infini (?)” stands beside the comparison of coefficients as an identity in two variables. The proposition has “\(k\) corps infini” in its parenthesis.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Scans 185–186 and 190 · Low for the interpretation of the margin.** The question about infinitude could concern equality on field-valued points versus equality of polynomials. As a small check, on the two elements of \(\mathbf F_2\) the upper-right entries \(y\) and \(y^2\) in the matrices on 186 agree. The expanded polynomial formulas still distinguish the two powers. The marginal wording supplies no explicit link to this example.
- **M2 · Scans 192–193 and 198 · Low for a missing hypothesis.** Choosing the *least* weight and advancing by \(p\), then calling the weights \(0,p\) positive, reads naturally with \(p>0\). With \(p=-2\) the two displayed model weights would instead be \(0,-2\). The first parenthesis reads “non nul” and has a change of parameter name. The uncertain subscript beside “petit entier” also bears on the interpretation.
- **M3 · Scans 195 and 198 · High for the weight comparison.** Negating the weights \(n,n+p,\ldots,n+p(k-1)\) gives the progression beginning at \(-n-p(k-1)\) when read in reverse order. In the two-dimensional case with \(n=0\), the weights become \(-p,0\). This agrees with the last line \(\check\xi=\sigma_{-p}\xi\).
- **M4 · Scan 188 · Low as a notation issue.** The iteration is written with \(h\) where the factorial and some small suffixes look like \(k\). A formula using one iteration parameter throughout would have the same suffix on the last variable and on the factorial. Two of those suffixes remain marked, so the individual pen forms do not establish a deliberate convention change.
- **M5 · Scan 197 · Reading question, low.** The first restriction calculation uses the frozen reading \(d\) for augmentation. Two later evaluations use \(\varepsilon\). One possibility is that the heavier, retraced first sign is also an epsilon. The clear upper upright there favours \(d\), as used in \(d(\xi)\) on 198. The comparison does not require separate augmentation maps.
