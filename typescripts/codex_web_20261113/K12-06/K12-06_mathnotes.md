# K12-06 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**A curve and its puncture (82).** For a smooth proper curve $C$ over a finite field and an abelian scheme $A$ over $C$, the page passes from the whole curve to $U=C-\{x\}$. A sequence relates $H^1(U,A)$ to cohomology supported at $x$. The proposed vanishing in degree one for the complete curve matters for the displayed isomorphism. The next line compares the supported group, $H^1(K_x,A)$ and a dual of local sections. Several of its relation signs are cancelled.

**The multiplicative group (84).** For $X$ of finite type over the integers, the sheet puts $H^i(X,\mathbb Q_\ell(1))$ alongside groups with coefficients in $G_m$. In degree two the two pieces of an extension are ringed and the higher-cohomology piece is linked to “coBrauer”. The degree-three example and the final question distinguish possible values from a general size formula.

**Forms and their tensor products (86–87).** For a pure semisimple motive $M$ of weight $p$, a pairing has target $\mathbb Q(-p)$. The symmetry imposed on it depends on the parity of $p$. The notes give a map on forms induced by tensor product and decompose forms on $M'\times M''$ into two diagonal pieces and a mixed term $B(M',M'')$. The space of forms is represented here by the looped sign transcribed first as $\Phi$.

**The positive cone (87–88).** The margin calls forms in the cone pseudo-ample and those in its interior ample. Conditions (i)–(iv) concern interior, nondegeneracy and restriction. An ample form restricted to a subobject gives an orthogonal splitting. Conditions (v)–(viii) cover direct sum, tensor product, the weight-zero vector-space case and the normalization on $\mathbb Q(p)$.

**Twists and isotypic components (89).** The twist isomorphism is said to preserve the ample cones. A weight-zero ample form gives a positive-definite form on $\Gamma_k M$. The text then reduces the study of positivity to isotypic pieces and describes a piece by $I\otimes_A V$, with $I$ simple and $A$ its endomorphism ring.

**Endomorphisms and polynomials (90).** The starting objects are presented both as modules and as pairs $(V,f)$. Over a field, simple modules are related to monic irreducible polynomials. The construction $k[t]/Pk[t]$ leads to $P(t)=\det(t\,\mathrm{id}-f)$ and its reverse $Q(t)=t^dP(1/t)$. The last formula has constant coefficient one.

**Rational functions and traces (91–92).** The first displayed assignment uses the reciprocal determinant. A margin specifies rational functions of value one at zero. A second map takes a logarithmic derivative. Its coefficients are traces of powers of $f$. The notes discuss recovering the function by integration and exponentiation and the change in positive characteristic.

**Cohomology recovered by weights (92–93).** The Lefschetz–Weil–Grothendieck formula relates the $L$-function to an alternating cohomology class with compact supports and Frobenius action. With the stated weight assumptions, the note separates contributions by the absolute values of zeros and poles. For a complete smooth $X$ and a twisted constant coefficient of weight $p$, the first formula identifies the piece of weight $i$ with degree $i-p$ cohomology. The exponent in the next occurrence is overwritten.

**Removing a lower-dimensional locus (93–95).** A cancelled relative draft contains a diagram and functions attached to inverse images of open sets. The live text restarts with opens $U$ of $X$ and compares $L(U,F\mid U)$ up to rational factors of bounded weight. The continuation singles out the difference of the two top compact-support classes, interprets them by duality and discusses the components and Albanese of $X$ for trivial coefficients. Weight together with level is proposed as a finer comparison.

**The generic fibre (95).** For $f:X\to Y$, the final question compares $L(X,F)$ modulo the functions for $f^{-1}(Y')$ with the birational invariants of the generic fibre over $K$, the function field of $Y$. It asks how much smoothness, properness and the condition on $F$ are needed. Scan 96 is a title sheet.

- **S87 · 86–87.** The initial target is $\mathbb Q(-p)$, whereas the displayed mixed term uses $\mathbb Q(p)$. A minus would make the targets agree if both use the pairing of 86. The discrepancy of the written signs is clear; the proposed correction is conditional.
- **S89 · 89, line 5.** The second reference is “(vi)”. The positive-definite vector-space condition is (vii) on 88; (vi) concerns tensor products. The intended reference may therefore be (vii).

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · The zero endomorphism (high confidence for the example, low for diagnosing the intended domain).** With the finite-length $k[t]$-modules described on 90, take $k=\mathbb Q$. The zero space and the one-dimensional space with $f=0$ both have $L=1$ and $\Lambda=0$. Their dimensions distinguish their additive classes. Thus U7, read as injectivity on all those classes, fails even in characteristic zero. The one-dimensional test does not depend on reading the overwritten letter in $R$.

**R1a · Refined statement (high confidence for finite-dimensional modules over a field).** For the reciprocal determinant on the Grothendieck group of these modules, the kernel is generated by the simple module $k[t]/(t)$. This follows by comparing the reciprocal polynomials of the simple factors: $t$ gives $1$, and the other monic irreducibles give distinct nonconstant factors up to units. In characteristic zero the logarithmic derivative has the same kernel on these classes: a unit series of constant coefficient one and zero derivative is identically one. This is a qualification supplied by the model.

**R2 · Which assignment the product denotes (low confidence as a claim of a slip).** With $f=1$ on a one-dimensional space over the rationals, the displayed $Q$ on 90 is $1-t$ and the first displayed $L$ on 91 is $1/(1-t)$. In the later product written $L=\prod_Q Q^{c_P}$, a single simple factor with multiplicity one gives $1-t$. The interlinear reciprocal labelled “additif” makes it unclear whether $L$ there denotes the initial or the composed assignment. The scalar calculation does not decide the intended convention.

**R3 · Reading questions (low confidence).** On 86, line 7 and later, the weight transcribed $p$ has a loop and descending stem that could also represent $\rho$. On 93, line 6, the frozen exponent was $i$ followed by a cancelled, doubtful $-q$ or $-p$; the witness also suggests $-\rho$. The crossing prevents reliance on it as a second independent instance of the degree formula. On the same scan, lower left, the frozen words “s’ent / sont pour la bonne filtration” could instead contain “n’est pas la bonne filtration”. The rotated crop does not settle the polarity. No inference about the choice of filtration here rests on that phrase.
