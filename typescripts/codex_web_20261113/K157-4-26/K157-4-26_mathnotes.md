# Mathematical notes on cote 157-4, scans 394–400 and 402–409

## What the pages do

**U01 · 394.** A continuation about fundamental localizers says that $\mathrm{Loc}(6'\mathrm a,8,9)$ makes $\mathrm{HOT}_W$ a derivator. An appeal to the recovery of $W$ leads into Corollaire 7.7.2. The marginal qualification on that recovery and the cancellation of $3\ \mathrm{bis}$ in the conclusion are essential to reading the corollary.

**U02 · 394.** Corollaire 7.7.3 restricts the domain of diagrams to finite ordered sets. It lists derivator axioms obtained from a model category associated with a category with cofibrations, invokes duality, and states a conclusion when $W$ admits both structures. The raised signs on the final three numbers are small and the last numeral is uncertain.

**U03 · 395.** The opening finishes a base-change comparison. The displayed arrow starts at $p^*f_!$. The other side is $f'_!$ followed by an inverse-image symbol whose letter may be $g$ or $q$. The author gives separate reasons in the $W$-smooth and $W$-proper cases.

**U04 · 395–397.** Section 7.8 collects three implications from localizer axioms to derivator axioms. The following prose asks which additional axioms enter the argument. It separates the role of $6\mathrm a$ and its primed variant from opposition, non-vacuity and fibrations. Two contrasting examples are a doubly written W with an uncertain numerical subscript, and the class of all arrows in $\mathrm{Cat}$.

**U05 · 397–398.** The author proposes calling a fundamental localizer satisfying $\mathrm{Loc}(6'\mathrm a,8,9)$ “spécial”. He associates $D=\mathrm{HOT}_W$ to it, then describes recovering $W$ as the class of $D$-equivalences in $\mathrm{Cat}$. The slanted note on 398 explicitly limits the recovery argument to the case with $\mathrm{Loc}(3\ \mathrm{bis})$.

**U06 · 398–399.** For a least localizer inside $\mathbb W_\infty$ satisfying the indicated axioms, two questions follow. One concerns $\mathrm{Loc}(9)$ and accessibility. The other asks whether the resulting $W$ differs from $\mathbb W_\infty$. The optional stronger hypothesis is written above the last lines on 398.

**U07 · 399–400.** Section 7.9 returns to a closed Quillen structure on $\mathrm{Cat}$ corresponding to $W$. Thomason's construction supplies the stated example for $W=\mathbb W_\infty$. The author considers extending it, then distinguishes its influence on his confidence in $\mathrm{Cat}$ as a model for $\mathrm{Hot}$ from the technical use he made of Dwyer maps.

**U08 · 402.** A working sheet headed “XV p. 119” arranges consequences of several combinations of axioms, first as implications and then by conclusion. The four bottom entries concern variants of $\mathrm{Loc}(4)$. A separate question about recovering $W$ occupies the right-hand side.

**U09 · 403.** A cancelled two-part draft compares a square built from $X_0,X_1,X_2$ with a $W$-homotopy amalgamated sum. Part b) relates its map to $Y$ to the canonical map out of that sum. A final diagram with $X'_1$ and $X'_2$ lies below the two long cancellation strokes.

**U10 · 404.** A map $\mathrm{Cat}\longrightarrow\Delta^1$ heads an example organized around empty and nonempty categories. The sheet tests maps over $S$ on their slices. In particular, a nonempty $Y$ has a nonempty slice at some $s$ (**A01**). It then draws a coproduct decomposition, squares and a cube. One component map bears a question mark. The subscript attached to the initial double-stroked W remains unclear.

**U11 · 405.** The starting diagram compares $U,\bar U,X,Y$ and their primed versions. The equation $\bar U\cap Y=\dot Y$ locates a common part of the comparison. Further diagrams use cofibration and fibration labels, and the lowest diagram compares two spans with arrows labelled $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ and their primed versions. A marginal note calls for an addition elsewhere.

**U12 · 406–407.** One sketch puts a winding letter, transcribed $\xi$ with $\zeta$ as an alternative, beside $A$ and its two outgoing arrows. Closed and open immersions, limits of Hom expressions, and component sets occur in the adjacent diagrams. Scan 407 asks about filteredness, writes a fork and a possible commutative completion, then gives Hom expressions involving a vertex reached from $b$ and $c$.

**U13 · 408–409.** A condition involving H and a 1-like stroke is placed beside an uncompleted diamond. The sketches then use $A_1,A_2,B'$ over $a,b$ and list elements over $b$. The $B'$ row pairs that object with the identity on $b$ (**A02**). Scan 409 gives two rows with $B',C'$ and the identities on $b,c$, a component expression and a value involving $j_!$. A long diagonal cancels the left calculation and reaches the lowest diagram. The sheets supply no connected proof joining all these sketches.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model remark, high confidence · 404.** The step “Si $Y\ne\varnothing$, $\exists\,s\in S$ tel que $Y_{/s}\ne\varnothing$” checks under the usual slice interpretation. An object of $Y$ and the identity of its image give the required object of the slice. The corresponding slice of an empty $X$ is empty.

**R02 · Model remark, medium confidence · 408–409.** The entry ending $B',\mathrm{id}_b$ on 408 can be matched to $x_1=b,\ s_1=B',\ u_1=\mathrm{id}_b$ on 409. The object, base and identity agree. The proposed identification concerns this entry alone; the manuscript gives no explicit instruction to join the two sheets.
