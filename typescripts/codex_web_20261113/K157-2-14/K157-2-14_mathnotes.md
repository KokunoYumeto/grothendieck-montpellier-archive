# Mathematical notes on scans 211–226

## What the pages do

**Products and direct image, scan 211.** The sheet begins at parts b) and c) of an earlier argument. When both categories are smooth over $S$, the class of their fibre product has an interpretation as a product over $S$. When just one is smooth, Grothendieck gives the corresponding formula with $\mathrm{multhot}$. Corollary 2 identifies $f_!f^*$ with multiplication by $S'$ and specializes the formula when $S'$ is smooth over $S$.

**The fibre formula, scans 211–213.** Der $4'$ asks for left adjoints to inverse image. To calculate a fibre of $f_!$ in Der $5'$, Grothendieck uses the category written ${}_{s\backslash}X'$. An arbitrary representative $X'$ over $S'$ need not be smooth over $S$, so its ordinary fibre would give an unjustified substitution. The local change of base gives the same $\mathrm{hot}_W({}_{s\backslash}X')$ on both sides of the comparison.

**The homological form of Der 3 b, scans 213–214.** For $f:S'\to S$ in $W$, the comparison on a constant object becomes the product of $\mathrm{hot}_W(f)$ with that object. Grothendieck collects the axioms in Proposition 3. At Der 2 he inserts « (pour mémoire) » and writes « pas prouvé » in the margin.

**Locally constant objects, scans 214–216.** The next assertion is that $f_0^*$ gives an equivalence on locally constant objects when $f\in W$. One possible approach to full faithfulness would use internal Hom. The calculation actually attempted uses the two adjunction maps. One involves the projection $X\times_S S'\to X$. For the other, Grothendieck first takes $C_S(X')$, then constructs $X'\to C_S(X')\times_S S'$ from the two maps into the pullback. He invokes L 5 for the projection out of that pullback.

**Change of base, scans 217–219.** Theorem 4 considers a cartesian square with vertical map $f$ and base map $p$. For smooth $f$, both composites have the representative $Z\times_S S'$. For proper $p$, Grothendieck first requires that ordinary pullback preserve the localizers $W_S^d,W_{S'}^d$. This would allow him to calculate on an arbitrary representative $Z$. He then considers obtaining the compatibility from a dual theorem.

**Detecting an equivalence, scans 219–220.** Proposition 5 characterizes a $\mathrm{HOT}_W$-equivalence $f$ by invertibility of $\mathrm{hot}_W(f)$. Strong saturation identifies this last condition with $f\in W$. Adjunction turns the test on morphisms between two constant objects into a test on $s'\times a\to s\times a$. The argument ends by using the unit of multiplication.

**A criterion over a base, scans 221–222.** Corollary 1 says that change of base for every $f_!$ over $S$ implies compatibility with the relative localizers. Its lemma tests $u:X'\to X$ on the direct images of every constant $\xi$. For sufficiency the text passes to fibres by Der $5'$ and uses strong saturation and Proposition 5. For necessity it represents $\xi$ by $T$ and identifies the resulting local comparison with the product of ${}_{s\backslash}u$ and $T$.

**A direct argument for proper change of base, scans 223–224.** Writing $s=g(s')$, Grothendieck reduces the comparison to a change of base $Z'\to Z$ between the two local categories. He invokes properness and asphericity of the fibres at this step. The concluding lemma treats a universal $W$-equivalence. In its upper square, $g_X$ and $g_Y$ belong to $W$, and the author obtains the equivalence between membership in $W$ for $f$ and for $f'$.

**Revising the hypotheses, scans 225–226.** The section « Nouvelle version de la théorie de » $\mathrm{HOT}_W$ begins with saturation, localization and the final-object axiom. A further axiom tests a $W$-morphism between $W$-fibrations on each fibre. A weaker version adds a perfectness assumption on $X,Y$. The small index in the last line of 225 is uncertain. Scan 226 defines $W_\omega$ using $W(1,2\,\mathrm{bis},3)$ and $W'_\omega$ using $W(1,2,3,4\,\mathrm b)$. Grothendieck considers both $W'_\omega$ and its strong saturation as replacements for the earlier lower bound $W_\infty$, and leaves the formulation of Der 3 open.

## Remarks of the model

- **[R1] My check of the unit argument, scan 220. High confidence.** The premise [B1] is the author's statement that multiplication in $\mathrm{Hot}_W$ has a unit. Under that premise, the test on $s'\times a\to s\times a$ includes the unit as a value of $a$. Conversely, product with each $a$ preserves an isomorphism. This explains the particular use of the unit in Proposition 5.
- **[R2] My check of the square, scan 224. High confidence.** The two composites from $X'$ to $Y$ are $g_Y f'$ and $f g_X$. Universality puts both $g_X,g_Y$ in $W$ [B2]. Under the two-out-of-three property [B3], the asserted equivalence of membership for $f,f'$ follows. The geometric assertion about $Z'\to Z$ enters before this square.
- **[R3] My reading of the scope of the direct argument, scans 221 and 223–224. High confidence.** The unfinished step on 221 concerns detecting isomorphisms in $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S)$ by fibres. The lemma ending « cqfd » on 224 establishes preservation of $W$ under a specified change of base. This distinguishes the question answered on 224 from the broader one left open on 221.
- **Reading question, scan 213, line 21. Low confidence.** The frozen first reading is $f'_!$, with $p'_!$ as the alternative. The structural map named just below is $p'$. The initial stroke of the label permits both letters, so the mathematical role alone cannot settle it.
- **Reading question, scan 224, line 9. Low confidence.** The frozen superscript on the target base $Z$ reads $+$, with a prime as the alternative. A prime fits the target named in the following lines. The isolated superscript has an extra short stroke.
