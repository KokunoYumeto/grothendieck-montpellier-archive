# Mathematical notes on scans 357–372

## What the pages do

**U1 · Corollaire 4.11.7, scan 357.** The closing corollary of §4.11 transfers a stability condition from \(C\) to an Ind category. Both directions of the limit arrow appear, with \(J\) finite. In the case discussed, the condition is also necessary when \(C\) is Karoubian. The connecting word between the two limit notations and the subscript of Ind are uncertain.

**U2 · Proposition 4.12.1, scans 357–358.** For an accessible functor \(f:M\to N\), Grothendieck chooses infinite cardinals \(\pi_0,\pi_1\). For \(\pi\geq\pi_1\) he writes \(f(M_\pi)\subset N_{\pi^{\pi_0}}\). At a fixed point of the cardinal power this gives \(f(M_\pi)\subset N_\pi\). The choices imposed on the cardinals are revised several times at the top of 358.

**U3 · Proof of 4.12.1, scans 358–359.** He restricts \(f\) to \(M_{\pi_0}\), introduces \(\pi'=\pi^{\pi_0}\), and reduces to the fixed-point assertion. The final computation expresses \(f(x)\) as a colimit of \(f(x_i)\) and uses closure in the target. The written fixed-point calculation and the domain of the \(x_i\) give rise to S1 and S2 below.

**U4 · Corollaire 4.12.2, scan 359.** Under the additional conditions on \(N\) and the cardinal bounds, \(f\) is identified, up to equivalences of categories and functor isomorphism, with \(\mathrm{Ind}(f_\pi)\). Here \(f_\pi\) goes from \(M_\pi\) to \(N_\pi\).

**U5 · Proposition 4.12.3 and its construction, scans 359–361.** If \(C\) is equivalent to a small category and \(N\) satisfies \(L_{\pi_0}\), restriction from \(\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi_0}C\) to \(C\) gives an equivalence of functor categories. The quasi-inverse first applies Ind, then composes with the colimit functor to \(N\). Grothendieck calls the canonical functor from \(C\) “2-universel” and points to variants with other kinds of limits.

**U6 · Corollaire 4.12.4, scan 362.** At a sufficiently large cardinal \(\pi\) useful for \(M\), the category of \(\pi\)-accessible functors from \(M\) to \(N\) is accessible. The proof replaces the source by its \(\pi\)-Ind presentation, applies restriction, and cites accessibility of the resulting functor category.

**U7 · Cardinal estimate and Corollaire 4.12.5, scans 362–363.** A bound on \(\operatorname{Card\,Fl}M_\pi\) leads to \(\pi'=2^\pi\). When \(\pi_0\) is useful for both categories, the corollary describes the fixed-\(\pi\) functor category as \(\pi'\)-accessible and presents it as a \(\pi'\)-Ind category. The upper formula and the formula of the corollary differ in the target of the inner Hom.

**U8 · Remarque 4.12.6, scans 363–364.** Grothendieck distinguishes fixing \(\pi\) from taking all accessible functors. He states that suitable colimits are computed argument by argument. He then considers what a small generating subcategory of all accessible functors would imply for a common accessibility cardinal, and proposes \(M=N=\mathrm{Ens}\) as a case where no such cardinal exists.

**U9 · Définition 4.13.1, scan 365.** A class \(S\) of objects determines the full subcategory \(\underline S\). The definition requires strict fullness, accessibility of \(\underline S\), and accessibility of its inclusion into \(M\). The manuscript distinguishes the object class from the category by an underline.

**U10 · Comparison of cardinals, scans 365–367.** With a cardinal useful for \(M\) and another for \(\underline S\) and its inclusion, the author obtains the equality \(S_\pi=M_\pi\cap S\) at the indicated threshold and fixed point. The passage marked “facultatif” compares \(\pi'_0\) with \(\pi_0\) and \(\pi'_1\) with \(\pi_1\). **U10a** is the implication there from the fixed point for the larger exponent to that for the smaller exponent.

**U11 · Proposition 4.13.2(a–c), scans 367–369.** Part a) characterizes accessible classes by colimits of objects in a full subcategory of \(M_\pi\), or by the essential image of its canonical Ind extension. Part b) gives cardinals for which one can take the intersection with \(M_\pi\), closed under direct factors. Part c) describes a least eligible \(\pi'_0\) and proposes a choice of \(\pi'_1\). The last argument in the formula for that choice is among the least legible in the batch.

**U12 · Proof of 4.13.2, scans 369–370.** The preceding comparison supplies the necessity and the cardinal bounds. For sufficiency, the Ind presentation gives accessibility of the full subcategory. The inclusion into \(M\) is \(\pi\)-accessible by the canonical functor in (4.13.2.1) and the earlier restriction result.

**U13 · Définition 4.13.3, scans 370–371.** Fixing an infinite \(\pi\), Grothendieck defines a \(\pi\)-accessible part of \(\operatorname{Ob}M\) by its presentation as an essential image. He allows a replacement of the presenting subcategory of \(M_\pi\) that makes it strictly full and Karoubian. The written name of the operation is abbreviated and overwritten.

**U14 · Recovery of the presenting subcategory, scan 371.** The Karoubian choice identifies \(\underline S(\pi)\) first with the \(\pi\)-accessible objects of \(\underline S\), then with \(\underline S\cap M_\pi\). Two successive displays give these different identifications. The subsequent reference shares their numerical sequence.

**U15 · Proposition 4.13.4, scans 371–372.** The two collections are the strictly full Karoubian subcategories of \(M_\pi\) and the subcategories of \(M\) that are \(\pi\)-accessible in \(M\). The upper arrow takes the essential image of the Ind extension. The lower arrow takes the intersection with \(M_\pi\). They are asserted to be inverse bijections for an infinite \(\pi\). The final paragraph explicitly removes the requirement that \(\pi\) be useful for \(M\).

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 363–364.** The counterexample Grothendieck seeks would address a change in quantifiers. Each accessible functor may have its own cardinal. His hypothetical small generating family would yield a single cardinal applying to them all. The manuscript explicitly says « tout foncteur accessible » after introducing a single \(\pi'\).

**R2 · Model remark, medium confidence, scans 370–372.** Taking the intersection after the Ind image is precisely the operation the second equality on 371 prepares. Going the other way uses the essential-image presentation from the definition on 370. This explains how the two inverse-map assertions use different parts of the preceding pages. It depends on the recovery and presentation statements the author invokes.
