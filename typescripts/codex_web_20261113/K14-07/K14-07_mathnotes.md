# Mathematical notes for cote 14, scans 87–100

## What the pages do

**The graph of a homomorphism (87, 89).** Scan 87 places divisor classes, homomorphisms to the dual abelian variety and the cohomology of a graph in a diagram. A diagonal labelled “graphe” bears the qualification “pas additive!”. Below it, the sheet distinguishes the maps with a lower and an upper star. Scan 89 argues that knowing the cohomology class of the graph determines a homomorphism and makes a parallel assertion for an abelian subvariety. It isolates a mixed \(H^1\)-tensor factor of \(H^2(A\times B)\) and ends with an unfinished question about algebraicity.

**Acyclicity on a site (88).** A reused typescript completes a vanishing argument by passing from a covering family to its image under a continuous functor. It then distinguishes acyclicity relative to a site from an intrinsic condition on a ringed topos. Only the first condition of Proposition 3.4 appears here.

**Cartan–Leray (90).** This typescript applies an earlier proposition to the sieve generated by a family. Corollary 2.5 writes a spectral sequence over an object and one for presheaves. It gives the ranges in which the resulting maps are isomorphisms or monomorphisms. The proof continues past the foot of the sheet.

**Cycle classes (91–92).** The cover introduces a chart for a nonsingular projective variety over an algebraically closed field. Scan 92 connects numerical, algebraic and homological classes with the groups labelled pic, alb and lin and with groups built from \(K\). Braces distinguish the questions about finiteness from the assertions about surjectivity and isomorphisms modulo torsion. An earlier position of the homological column is crossed out. A faint note linked to the chart mentions characteristic zero.

**Ampleness (93–95).** The cover and theorem sheet introduce Mumford–Nakai. For an invertible sheaf on a projective scheme, scan 94 lists tests using Euler characteristics and dimensions of spaces of sections. Some apply to coherent sheaves, others to integral subschemes. Two later formulations are cancelled. Scan 95 sketches implications by induction on dimension. Its first lemma gives higher-cohomology vanishing under ampleness on smaller subschemes. Its second concerns the map defined by sufficiently high powers of the sheaf. Both point to a letter of Mumford.

**Cones and the surface criterion (96).** The sheet compares \(P,Q,R\), their interiors, closures and polars. It specifies conditions on a class \(x\) using multiples represented by curves, and boxes a criterion attributed to Nakai involving \(x.P>0\) and \(x^2>0\). A drawing labels three nested cones. The last paragraph considers what happens if the self-intersection vanishes. Several superscript crosses have been retraced.

**Lifts and the Cohen draft (97).** A reused typescript finishes a lifting construction and proposes a new formulation of Corollary 2.2 in terms of three conditions. A handwritten insertion adds a lifting formulation. The next paragraphs suggest how to order corollaries on coefficient fields in complete local rings and discuss the wording “cas d'égale caractéristique”.

**Tests in complementary degree (98).** A cohomology expression stands alone above a divider. Below it, two boxed conditions connect positive intersection tests for a divisor with membership in cones in degrees \(1\) and \(d-1\). The next chart places \(\alpha_i\) and \(\alpha_{d-i}\) in parallel rows. An isolated \(c(E)\) with difficult indices appears underneath.

**Derivations and extensions (99).** A second reused typescript opens “Différentielles et dérivations.” Proposition 1.1 describes differences of lifts across a square-zero kernel by a linearity condition and the product rule. Definition 1.2 names the derivations. Corollary 1.3 describes the set of lifts as empty or principal homogeneous under the derivation group.

**The top power and the cubic example (100).** After the induction hypothesis through degree \(d-1\), the sheet asks about \(D^d\). It writes the product \(D^iD^{d-i}\) with the degrees indicated beneath each factor. Three boxes compare positivity of the pairing and images under the two \(\alpha\) operations. A cubic example expands \((D'+nD)^3\), with strokes connecting terms to the final box \(D^3\geq0\).
