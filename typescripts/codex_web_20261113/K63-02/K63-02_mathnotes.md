# Mathematical notes on cote 63, scans 73–77 and 96–101

Page and line references use the scan numbers and the lines of the separate TeX files. Short quotations retain the language of their sheets. Identifiers beginning with R refer to the calculations in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**An involution on a sum of lines (73).** The sheet writes $V=L+L^{-1}$ and an involution from its conjugate to $V$. It assigns two components, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, to the map out of the conjugate of $L$. Under the boxed assumption $\beta\ne0$ it writes elements using $L$ and its image and computes a formula for conjugation on the other summand. The expanded expression has several overwritten coefficients and signs. A pairing calculation ends with $\beta+\bar\beta=0$, glossed as « purement imaginaire ».

**Bibliographical leaf (74).** A cancelled list cites work on homological algebra, Galois cohomology, categories and functors, Analysis Situs and abelian categories. It ends with a reading suggestion for the seminar. The short handwritten name underneath the list is uncertain.

**The two differential forms and their volumes (75).** The author evaluates $\beta(\omega,\omega)$ by integrating $\omega\bar\omega$ and relates it to $-2i\rho$ for positive $\rho$. He describes $\gamma=-\alpha\beta^{-1}$ as the object that changes the « algébrique » splitting into the « splitting transcendant ». A closed representative $y_0$ of the class of $y$ enters the definition of $\sigma$. The page ends with $4\rho\sigma=1-\alpha\bar\alpha$.

**Seminar results and programme (76).** The crossed typescript attributes results on vanishing cycles and the Picard inequality to Igusa, and results on cohomology and duality to Ogg and Chafarevitch. Its outline proceeds from sites and topoi to étale cohomology, torsion coefficients, base change, compact supports and duality. The manuscript layer revises the ranges of exposés and adds a comment on the importance of base change.

**The argument of α (77).** The volume formula is restated between inequalities for $\alpha\bar\alpha$. To discuss the argument the author chooses $\omega^{12}=\Delta$. The multipliers written beside the twelfth and sixth powers have distinguishable but similar shapes. The page calls $(\arg\alpha)^6\in U$ a canonical invariant and links it to $j$.

**Divisors on the modular site (96, upper part).** The sketch places $\Delta=0$, $j=0$ and $j=12^3$ relative to the fibres over 2 and 3. Opposite it, two braces give the positive and negative parts of the divisors of $j$ and $j-12^3$, and classes written as $\omega^4,\omega^6,\omega^{12}$. The drawings label automorphism-group orders 24 and 12 at the special intersections, and 4 and 6 on the two divisors away from those characteristics.

**The loci M′ and M″ (96, lower part).** For $c_4=0$ the page describes, after inverting 6, torsors of isomorphisms to the curve $y^2=x^3+1$. The displayed cohomology group is used to count 72 curves. The corresponding $c_6=0$ entry names $B_{\mu_4}$ and gives a count of 32, with a small doubtful sign before the number. The two sets of fibre multiplicities at 2 and 3 are respectively 4, 2 and 6, 3. Ramification and regularity are discussed separately.

**Letter (97).** The writer thanks Grothendieck for approaching Verdier and Deligne and for sending papers from the seminar. He says he will speak with Deligne after reading the Hartshorne seminar. Operations denoted by an angular sign with superscript $i$ have interested him. The letter gives no definition of those operations.

**Four components and their fibres (98, opening and middle).** The sheet says that $M_3\cap V(c_4)$ has four irreducible components which can meet only over 2, and gives their union one connected component. It relates a geometric rank-one fibre above the point with residue field $\mathbb F_4$ to automorphisms of a curve in characteristic 2 and to their orbits on bases. It then discusses good reduction, identification of each component over the indicated base, and different 3-rigidifications of the model. A marginal addition addresses how the fibres are identified where the components meet.

**The tangent question (98, lower part).** Over $\mathbb F_4$ the page considers whether the four branch tangents exhaust the nonvertical directions. It asks how $G'$ acts on the tangent space, notes the invariant map from $T_a$ to $T_b$, and uses a subgroup $B'$ that leaves each component invariant. The matrix in the margin has bottom row $(0,1)$. The argument invokes transitivity and ends mid-sentence.

**Local parameters and the completed ring (99, upper part).** The list proposes $j$ on one open set and $1/j$ or $1/(j-12^3)$ on another. It gives $c_4,c_6$ near the respective special loci away from the small characteristics, and $a_1,b_2$ in characteristics 2 and 3. The question about the completed local ring is accompanied by $W[[t]]$, a residue-field annotation and the two choices of $t$. The requested equations of the divisors are left open.

**A flag and the level-three locus (99, lower part).** An invariant flag for the automorphisms of the curve reduces the structural group $G$ to a Borel subgroup $B$. The sheet describes a model over the primitive cube roots with an adapted basis, the two basis vectors’ pairing, and the different rigidifications. It presents $M_3[1/6]\cap V(c_4)$ as four copies of the indicated base and ends with a product over $B$ carrying a $G$-action. A margin table gives orders for the groups, quotients and intersections.

**Equal tangents and the action on the vertical line (100, opening).** The page opens with the assertion that the tangents at $a$ are equal. It separates the tangent space into vertical and horizontal lines and says that $G'$ acts trivially on the horizontal line. It records a surjection to $\mathbb F_4^*$, and a likely order-eight kernel. Determining the map « au signe près » still leaves a choice.

**Which actions extend (100, Attention).** A double underline emphasizes that the action of $\mu_3$ on the modular curve does not extend in the indicated way. The live argument says an extension would commute with $G'$ on the special curve and would put $\mu_3$ in its centre. It invokes the noncentrality of a 3-Sylow subgroup. The final lines relate this obstruction to joining the components and identify an automorphism group with $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. A substantial intervening calculation is crossed out.

**Passage to graded rings and specialization of cycles (101).** In the cancelled typescript, $Z_s$ comes from schematic closure and the author asks about descent of a homomorphism to a quotient. Sections 0 and 1 of $P^1_S$ give the two evaluation terms in the starred displays. The text relates the image of a cycle on the $t$ fibre to a difference on the $s$ fibre, subject to a definability condition. The operation in the first display is overtyped. The formula labelled (7.11.2) contains blank spaces where a map name and an arrow might have gone.

## Remarks of the model

- **R77, high confidence.** As algebra, the two versions of the volume relation in the display on 77 are rearrangements. I compared their polynomial residuals with opposite signs. This checks the rewriting only.
- **R96, high confidence.** The three cyclic factor orders displayed on 96 give $6\cdot6\cdot2=72$. The arithmetic accounts for the stated number conditional on the displayed cohomology identification.
- **R98, high confidence.** In the direction count on 98, removing one vertical direction from the stated five leaves four. The subtraction applies under the author’s hypothesis of distinct tangents. Scan 100 gives a different conclusion about those actual tangents.
- **R99a, R99b, R99c, high confidence.** With the orders in the margin of 99, $48/12=4$, $24/6=4$, and $12/6=2$. These are checks of the listed indices from the stated orders.
- **R100, high confidence in the arithmetic.** The order 24 assigned to $G'$ on 99 and the asserted order 8 for $P'$ on 100 give $24/8=3$. This is consistent with the displayed index. Identifying the actual kernel is the author’s qualified step.
- **Reading question, 99, L42 (low confidence).** Frozen reading $e_1^0,e_2^0,\zeta^0$. The other reading is a small circle in place of 0. The superscripts are tiny rounded marks, whereas subscripts 1 and 2 have visible feet. The argument does not distinguish the two typographical choices. The frozen reading was left in place.
