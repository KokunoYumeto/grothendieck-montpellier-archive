# Mathematical notes on cote 85, scans 16–29

The scan numbers are those of the supplied images. The identifiers link these notes to the check-event register. Quotations are from the transcription. A crossed calculation is identified as such whenever it enters the discussion.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Faithful dihedral representations, scans 16–17.** For an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\), the proposition asks when \(\mathbb D_i\) embeds in \(\mathrm{GP}(1,k)\). It treats \(i=0\) as the infinite dihedral case. In positive characteristic this case depends on whether \(k^*\) has an element of infinite order. For \(i\ge2\) the surviving finite-index criterion is \(p\nmid i\) or \(i=p\). The proof distinguishes the orders coming from semisimple and unipotent elements. A proposed corollary about an additional factor of order two is crossed out.

**U2 · The invariant and the torsor, scans 17–19.** Two involutions \(\tau_0,\tau_1\) give \(u=\tau_0\tau_1\). The condition \(u_s^2\ne1\) is imposed in every fibre. The ensuing category of representations in forms of \(\mathrm{GP}(1)\) is related to pairs \((\alpha,R)\), with \(\alpha+2\) invertible and \(R\) a torsor under \(U(2-\alpha)\). Scan 18 writes a group law in the coordinate \(\lambda\), then constructs a twisted representation. Scan 19 starts the inverse construction, taking \(\alpha\) from \(u\) and describing \(R\) using \(H_1\setminus\{c\cup d\}\).

**U3 · Automorphisms, scans 19–20.** The coordinate condition \(\lambda(2+\lambda\beta)=0\) leads into the identification of the automorphisms with the two-torsion of \(U_\beta\). For invertible \(\beta=2-\alpha\), the sheet identifies the image as the unique maximal torus containing \(u\), with two-torsion \(\mu_2\). For \(\beta=0\) it distinguishes the case where 2 is invertible from the case of characteristic 2. Scan 19 contains a heavily cancelled draft of these cases; the labelled proposition and corollaries are on scan 20.

**U4 · A commuting factor and finite order, scans 20–22.** A representation of \(\mathbb D_\infty\times\gamma\) is described by adding a homomorphism from \(\gamma\) into the two-torsion group to the previous pair. Scan 21 compares exact fibrewise order with factorization through a finite dihedral subgroup. It introduces \(\Psi_n(\alpha)=0\) and calls \(\Psi_n\) a “\(\frac12\) cyclotomique” polynomial. The coefficient conditions immediately above it contain cancellations. Scan 22 applies the discussion to an even index and the factor \(\mathbb Z/2\). Its proof breaks off in characteristic 2.

**U5 · The invariant of a section, scans 23–25.** A title sheet opens a new set of notes. On scan 24, \(G\) is a form of \(\mathrm{GP}(1)\) or \(\mathrm{Sl}(2)\), and the invariant \(A\) is characterized on a copy of \(G_m\) by \(A(i(\lambda))=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}\). The identity and certain involutions give \(2\) and \(-2\). Scan 25 uses the affine group and the function \(f(\lambda,t)\) to compute the value on unipotent sections. The last paragraph gives a different numerical value from its preceding calculation (S4).

**U6 · Local conjugacy and its exceptional fibres, scans 26–27.** With \(A(u)-2\) and \(A(u)+2\) invertible, the conjugacy question reduces to the two roots of \(t^2-\alpha t+1\), exchanged by inversion. At the exceptional value corresponding to \(\lambda=-1\), scan 27 tests a lift \(\lambda'=\lambda+\mu\) with \(\mathfrak m\mu=0\). Equality of invariants leaves more possibilities for \(\mu\) than equality or inversion does. The sheet concludes that one can make the proposed relation fail when the residue field is not reduced to two elements.

**U7 · Power maps, projective automorphisms and involutions, scans 28–29.** A homomorphism out of \(G_m\) gives a polynomial \(G_n\) through \(\lambda^n+\lambda^{-n}=G_n(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\). The examples pass to a central quotient and to the projective action of a rank-two automorphism, where \(A(u^C)=(\mathrm{Tr}\,u)^2/\det u-2\). The next sheet evaluates this expression using the two eigenvalues and then for a pseudoreflection. It states an involution criterion and a converse over a reduced base. The last case of the proof ends mid-sentence.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scans 24–25, high confidence.** My check gives \(1+1^{-1}=2\) and \((-1)+(-1)^{-1}=-2\). Both torus values agree with scan 24. The first also explains the proposed correction in S4.
- **R2 · Scans 26–27, high confidence.** My check gives a concrete instance of the failed lift. In \(\mathbb F_3[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), take \(\lambda=-1\) and \(\lambda'=-1+\epsilon\). The inverse of \(\lambda'\) is \(-1-\epsilon\). The two invariant values agree, and \(\lambda'\) differs from both \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^{-1}\). This uses precisely the first-order situation studied on the sheet.
- **R3 · Scans 28–29, high confidence.** My check of the eigenvalue expression uses only the trace and determinant given on the page. Substituting \(x+y\) and \(xy\) yields \(x/y+y/x\), agreeing with the displayed formula when the eigenvalues are units.
- **R4 · Scan 28, L12, low confidence.** My reading question concerns the digit in the frozen \(\mathrm{Sl}(1\mid2)\) below the quotient diagram. The centre \(\mu_2\) and the rank-two calculation favour 2, while the small dark stroke also permits 1.
- **R5 · Scan 28, L18–19, low confidence.** My reading question concerns the frozen \(A(u')\) between \(A'(p(u))\) and \(A(u)^2-2\). Could the raised stroke on this particular \(u\) be a 2? The immediately preceding torus example involves squaring, while the stroke here looks short and prime-like. The transcription retains the prime.
- **R6 · Scan 28, L01–03, low confidence.** My reading question concerns the small words surrounding the revision of the hypothesis on the homomorphism. The frozen text has « pour … nous supposons ». On the enlarged line, the first word could be « pas », and the addition above the cancelled verb could be « mono » rather than « nous ». The two short horizontal groups have almost no letter detail. This matters for the scope of the hypothesis.
- **R7 · Scan 17, L23–24, medium confidence.** My reading question concerns the frozen « quelques bons invariants » after « munies de ». The enlargement shows two long descenders in the first word and a tall initial in the second. They suggest « quadrique lisse invariante », which would also account for the quadric associated to the representation at the start of scan 18. These three words had no frozen doubt mark. Their alternative is recorded here.
