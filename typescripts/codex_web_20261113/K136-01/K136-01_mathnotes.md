## What the pages do

**328 · Central extensions and cohomology.** Diagrams compare $G$, $G'$, their versions indexed by $d$, and their classifying spaces. The coefficients change between the integers and a group identified at upper left with $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. The bottom diagram relates $E'$ and $E$ on $T'$ and $T$. A separate calculation proposes two values involving $g$.

**329 · Three descriptions of the data.** For $F_A(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,M)$ the sheet sets a compatibility with $\Gamma^p$ beside maps $u_p:A\to\Gamma^p(A)$ and a map out of $k\{T\}$. The two equations for the $u_p$ use different target degrees, $p+q$ for multiplication and $pq$ for iteration.

**330–331 · Operations and a cancelled approach.** Scan 330 sets out $\gamma$, $\beta$, $\alpha$ and the arrows for tensor products and $\Gamma^p$. Below “OK OK” a crossed draft defines $u_n$ using $1/n!$ times a product and draws a multiplication square. The short fragment on 331 begins a Hom formulation in the variable $M$. Its outgoing arrow has no written target.

**332 · Transport of a divided power.** The page starts with $u_p:\Gamma^p F(X)\to F(\Gamma^p X)$, compares its value on $\gamma^p(x)$ with $F(\gamma_X^p)(x)$, and follows the effect of taking a further $q$-th power. The factorial coefficient is written under $\alpha_{pq}$. The last calculation turns to a product of powers of several $x_i$.

**333 · Reduction to generators.** An expansion of the $q$-th divided power of a sum leads to individual summands and then to products of divided powers. The lower half repeats the question for $u:A\to B$ and ideals $J,K$. The test on generators is explicitly conditional.

**334 · A pair $J\to A$.** After asking about $F(J)\to F(A)$, the page lists multiplication, the action of $A$ on $J$, the map $i$, and the operations $\gamma^p$. Its six displayed rules cover degrees zero and one, addition, scalar multiplication, products of divided powers, and iteration.

**335 · Testing the rules.** The notation $x^{(p)}=u_p^X(\gamma^p(x))$ introduces calculations for a sum, a scalar multiple and a product of two divided powers of the same element. A question about preservation of divided powers and a square involving $\Gamma^q\Gamma^p F(X)$ remain at the foot.

**336–337 · A title and a rank calculation.** *Gribouillis* occupies scan 336 by itself. Scan 337 opens with a grid in $\Phi_*,\Psi_*$, exterior powers and $\Gamma^2$. Two binomial calculations lead to a formula for the rank of $\Gamma^p\Phi_{[N]}\otimes\Lambda^q\Psi_{[N]}$. The operator $(\tau P)(n)=P(n+1)$ then produces expansions through $\tau^3$, followed by expressions with variable exponent.

**338 · The two indices on $C$.** The sheet distinguishes a simplex index $[n]$ and a chain or cochain degree. The diamonds relate the objects obtained by changing a star to a bullet on one or both indices. A separate condition defines $\operatorname{Hom}^{!}$ by the image of an increasing map. The lower half works with the free module on $X_{[n]}$, its dual and the normalized part.

**339 · The functor of a simplicial set.** The object $X_*$ defines $F_{X_*}$ by Hom. Evaluation on $C^i_*$ gives nondegenerate cochains. The sheet specializes to a simplex, then compares transformations $F_{X_*}\to F_{\Delta^n_*}$ with maps between cochain complexes. The lower diagram has a separate passage from a free module to its double dual.

**340–341 · Duals and normalization.** Scan 340 sets cochain and chain versions of Hom next to exterior-power models. A triangle at the foot carries the warning “pas linéaire !”. On scan 341, read after a half-turn, the diagram places interior product by $\overline T_n$ opposite $d_i$. The following paragraph takes the normalized part in a Hom construction, and ends with two degree tables.

**342 · A pair of arrows and a tensor comparison.** The upper diagrams place $K(M_*)$ before a pair of arrows and give a map $u_M$ to $M_{[n]}$. The right-hand calculation identifies $K(M_*)$ first with degree-zero cycles and then with Hom expressions. Below, two evaluation maps are tensored and compared with evaluation on $M_*\otimes N_*$. A Hom-to-tensor map and a diagonal on $M$ close the page.

## Slips

- **L1 · 332, lines 12 and 14. Medium confidence.** The first right-hand side reads $F(\gamma_X^p)(x)$, the second $F(\gamma_X^p)$. Its left-hand side again evaluates at $x$. An omitted final $(x)$ would reconcile the two lines.
- **L2 · 332, line 29 and 334, line 49. Medium confidence.** Scan 332 attaches $(pq)!/((p!)^q q!)$ to a $q$-th power taken after a $p$-th power. Scan 334 writes the same coefficient beside $\gamma^p(\gamma^q(x))$. Keeping the latter order suggests $(pq)!/((q!)^p p!)$ there. At $p=2,q=3$ the two coefficients are 15 and 10 respectively. Exchanging the two operation labels is another possible repair.
- **L3 · 335, lines 25–27. Medium confidence.** The first equality ends $\lambda^p x^p$. The calculation directly below ends $\lambda^p x^{(p)}$. The local definition in line 5 favours restoring the parentheses on the first right-hand side.
- **L4 · 338, lines 22–23 and 38–44. Medium confidence.** The column listing gives the upper degrees $n,n+1$ for positive $n$. The later cases give $C_n^m=k$ for $n=m,m+1$. Holding $n=2$ fixed gives upper degrees $2,3$ by the first listing and $1,2$ by the second. If these are the same entries, $n,n-1$ would agree with the cases.
- **L5 · 338, lines 38–47. Low confidence.** The line for $n=m+1$ equates $C^m_{m+1}$ with $\Lambda^{m+1}k^{m+2}=k^{m+2}$, although the cases just above give a single $k$. Brackets around the lower index, $C^m_{[m+1]}$, would let the adjacent diagonal refer to the copy of $k$ inside that module. Small bracket strokes at this location are hard to exclude.
- **L6 · 341, lines 41–43. Low confidence.** The lower case assigning $k$ reads “$j\ne1,i+1$”. The parallel case above reads “$j=i,i+1$”. Changing the lower inequality to equality and the first numeral to $i$ would agree with the displayed duality. The preceding case also contains a doubtful $i-1$ or $i+1$.

## Remarks of the model

- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence for the numerical examples. Scan 337, lines 12–24.** At $N=5,n=3$, the two sides of the first binomial equality each give 60 and those of the second each give 105.
