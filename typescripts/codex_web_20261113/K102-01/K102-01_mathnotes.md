# Mathematical notes · Montpellier 102, scans 2–7 and 9–14

## What the pages do

**A class of covers, scans 2–3.** The cover says *Recollements*. The typescript starts with a site $S$ and a class $M$ of arrows. The first three conditions concern base change, identities and composition, and locality on the target. Condition d) tests an arrow using a covering family on its source and the composites with that family. The margin considers putting saturation later in the construction.

**Extension from the site to the topos, scans 3–4.** The proposition characterizes a class $\widetilde M$ in $\widetilde S$. Condition $\tilde e$ compares the two classes on arrows of the site. Condition $\tilde f$ asks for covers by site objects for the source of certain arrows to a site object. For uniqueness the proof first assumes that the diagonal is representable. In the general case it compares $Z_{ij}=X_i\times_F X_j$ with $Z'_{ij}=X_i\times_X X_j$ and returns to the first case.

**Objects obtained by gluing, scans 4 and 6.** The definition of $\widetilde S_M$ selects the sheaves $F$ admitting a covering family $X_i\to F$ of the specified kind, with $X_i$ in $S$. A crossed-out proposition at the foot of scan 4 continues in the two crossed lines of scan 6. The latter break off at $F_i$.

**Stability, scan 5.** The five parts concern representable sheaves, passage along arrows in $\widetilde M$, a covering map and its first projection, sums, and finite-limit constructions available in $S$. In the sum argument, covers of the fibre products $X_i$ supply the families $X_{ij}$ covering the summands. The corollary describes closure under sums and quotients by equivalence relations with first projection in $\widetilde M$. It then changes the presenting site to a subcategory $S'$.

**Examples and the slice site, scan 7.** Open immersions between affine schemes give the scheme example. Étale morphisms give the example attributed to Artin. The following proposition adds a cancellation condition on $M$, equips $S(X,M)$ with its induced topology, and identifies a full subcategory of the slice topos by restriction. The sheet preserves an earlier, struck formulation as a composite of functors.

**Descent questions, scan 9.** The four questions move from effectiveness of descent for proper objects to descent of varieties, detection of representability and descent of small étale sheaves. The author links 1° and 3° conditionally on 2°, and considers how a version of 2° with a weaker condition on the diagonal would bear on 4°.

**Exercise on valuations, scan 10.** A different typescript defines sets $D_E(\lambda)$ by a valuation bound. It asks for an eventual equality for polynomial values and a criterion in terms of zeros in an intersection over an algebraic closure. The two cases distinguish whether that intersection is empty. One takes an element of least degree in the nonempty case and a valuation on $K(X)$ in the empty case. Part e) asks for a criterion for linear compactness in terms of immediate extensions.

**Plan for morphisms of sheaves, scan 11.** Eight numbered topics cover representability, finiteness, formal and infinitesimal conditions, immersions, separation, properness, surjectivity and radicial morphisms, and connectedness. A pen arrow reorders the middle of the list. The final paragraph asks that definitions agree with the accepted scheme definitions when a morphism is representable. It writes the test $X\times_G F\to X$ for $X\to G$.

**Reduced fibres and a Hilbert functor, scan 12.** The sheet opens in mid-argument with a radicial extension and reduction. A set-theoretic section of $M=\underline{\mathrm{Hilb}}_{X/Y}$ chooses the reduced subobject of each fibre. The image $Y'=\varphi(Y)$ is asserted to be closed. The passage then considers it with the induced reduced structure and obtains a closed subscheme $Z$ of $X'$. The last lines compare the fibres after a residue-field extension and identify $Z$ with $X'_{\mathrm{réd}}$. Some adjectives in that last step remain uncertain.

**A criterion for open immersion, scan 13.** The diagram places $G\to F$ over a locally noetherian base. The hypotheses concern sheaves, commutation with two kinds of limits, and an infinitesimally étale monomorphism. The conclusion calls the morphism an open immersion. A separate assertion concerns surjective open immersions of functors. An epimorphism condition in the draft has a line through it.

**Boxed plan, scan 14.** The sheet puts the formal and infinitesimal conditions in parallel columns. Other boxes group immersions, separation, properness, connectedness and finiteness conditions. The small family arrow and the base-change square stand to the left of the group marked with a circled A. Several shared boundaries and a brace are part of the drawing.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 5 · High confidence about the type of the projection.** For the two arrows $X\to F$ and $F_i\to F$, the projection with target $X$ has source $X\times_F F_i$. This accounts for the proposed arrow in S3. It does not determine the writer's reason for putting a membership sign there.

**M2 · Scan 10 · Medium confidence about the letter of the valuation.** The introduction assigns $v$ to $K$ and $w$ to $E$. Evaluating at an arbitrary $z$ in $E$ calls for a valuation on $E$. The second case presents a similar issue for $X$ in $K(X)$. The notation could abbreviate restriction and prolongation.

**M3 · Scans 11 and 13 · High confidence about the textual connection.** The bracketed example following the radicial-morphism topic on scan 11 reappears as a separate assertion between two horizontal rules on scan 13. This identifies one specific overlap between the plan and the later working sheet.

**M4 · Scan 12, final fibre argument · Low confidence.** The frozen readings are *normales* for the fibres and *normal* for $Z$. The alternatives include *réduites* and *réduit*. The middle letters are too faint to use this passage as evidence for a normality hypothesis. The last comparison with $X'_{\mathrm{réd}}$ is clearer.
