# Mathematical notes · cote 58, scans 2–10

## What the pages do

**Cover, scan 2.** The title ends « à π₀ ». The group of strokes before « à » permits a singular, plural or abbreviated form of *Application*.

**Lemme 2 and its proof, scans 3 and 5.** For a local ring A the author sets X = Spec A, removes its closed point to obtain X′ and puts Y′ = X′ ∩ V(f). The heavily revised hypothesis concerns the dimensions of components of X′. The conclusions still visible are that the components of Y′ have dimension at least 1 and that Y′ is connected. The proof compares the dimensions of Z′ and Z′ ∩ Y′, reduces to an irreducible case and passes to the normal closure. On scan 5 the reduction ends with a left-pointing comparison of two groups of sections H⁰.

**Lemme 3 and its proof, scans 5 and 7.** The two hypotheses concern components of X′ of dimension at least k+1 and connectedness of X′ in dimension at least k. The author introduces a finite set Φ and the condition V(f) ∩ Φ = ∅. The asserted conclusion is connectedness of Y′ in dimension at least k−1. The proof reduces to an irreducible, then normal, case. It links the choice of Φ to minimal points in the bracketed passage about the Z′ᵢ, and invokes (S₂) and connectedness in codimension 1.

**The theorem and the beginning of its proof, scan 9.** Condition aₖ is a dimension bound of k+2 on the irreducible components of the spectrum of the completion. Condition bₖ is connectedness of X′ in dimension at least k. For 0 ≤ ℓ ≤ k the author forms Y′ by intersecting X′ with the loci V(fᵢ) and asserts the conditions with index k−ℓ. For connectedness he supposes there is a disconnecting Z′ of dimension less than k−ℓ and chooses further elements with common zero locus disjoint from Z′. The last line stops during the resulting inference.

**Changing and shifting a grading, scan 10, examples 5 and 6.** A homomorphism ρ of grading monoids gives new gradings compatible with multiplication and module action. The author singles out the product grading Δ₀ × Δ₀ and its partial and total gradings. He next defines the shift by M′λ = M_(λ+λ₀) and writes the shifted module M(λ₀).

**Examples and conventions, scans 10 and 8.** Examples 7 and 8 grade a polynomial ring by degree and a Clifford algebra by its two components, C⁺(Q) and C⁻(Q). The first remark gives the usual index sets for graded, bigraded, trigraded and positively graded objects. Remark 2, on scan 8, identifies graded Z-modules over the trivially graded ring with graded commutative groups.

**Graded homomorphisms, scans 8 and 6.** Definition 4 requires a ring map to preserve each homogeneous component. A module map of degree δ takes Mλ into M′_(λ+δ). The discussion covers uniqueness of degree under a regularity hypothesis, composition, inverse maps and morphisms of degree zero. Examples 9 and 10 illustrate the identity from a shifted module and multiplication by a central homogeneous element.

**Graded submodules, scans 6 and 4.** Proposition 2 equates three conditions on N. It is the sum of the N ∩ Mλ, it contains the homogeneous components of each of its elements, and it is generated by homogeneous elements. A double sum in the proof expands a linear combination of homogeneous generators. The two corollaries on scan 4 address components of a generating family and finite generation.

**Ideals, subrings and quotients, scan 4.** The typescript defines one-sided graded ideals, describes a graded subring and gives the degrees of the canonical inclusions. The last paragraph constructs the quotient grading with pieces (Mλ + N)/N and records the degree of the canonical map M → M/N.

## Remarks of the model

- **R-SHIFT · high confidence.** The direction of the shift in example 6 on scan 10 agrees with the degree of the identity in example 9 on scan 6. With M′λ = M_(λ+λ₀), that identity from the shifted module to M takes an element of degree λ to degree λ+λ₀. Both the plus sign and the direction « sur M » are legible.
- **R-ORDER · high confidence.** Reading the typescript by its own folios reconnects « les homomorphismes gra- » on scan 8 to « dués de degré 0 » on scan 6. Scan 6 then ends « muni de cette graduation, » and scan 4 opens « est un sous-module gradué de M. » These joins give direct textual evidence for the order 10, 8, 6, 4.
- **R-IDEAL · reading question, low confidence.** Scan 9, interlinear insertion in the proof, reads *fraktur m A* in the frozen text. The alternative *fraktur r A* deserves consideration because the upper stroke looks much like the radical sign at the marked occurrence earlier on the sheet. The interlinear occurrence itself carried no frozen doubt mark and retains its original reading.
