## What the pages do

**M1 · A final object in the fundamental groupoid, scans 327, 329, 331.** The proposed criterion starts with two objects $b,c$, their product $a=b\times c$, and nonempty sets of arrows from every $x$ to each of $b,c$. The argument compares pairs $(\beta,\gamma)$ using the two projections. It then treats a zigzag from $x$ to $a$ by choosing arrows from its vertices to $a$ and comparing successive triangles. The surviving conclusion is « $X$ est 1-connexe ». The note beside the original assertion of asphericity reads « Faux, cf plus bas ».

**M2 · The two-object category, scan 331.** The author takes $b=c$, writes $\mathrm{Ob}\,X=\{b,b\times b\}$, and sets $G=\operatorname{Hom}_X(b,b)$, an arbitrary monoid. The two projections give arrows written $u\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$ and $u\circ\mathrm{pr}_2$. He introduces the full subcategory $S$ of sets with the two objects $\{0\}$ and $\{0,1\}$, then sets $T=S^\circ$. A first calculation using $S$ is struck out. In the surviving display the arrow points from $H^p(T,R^qf_*(M_X))$ towards $H^n(X,M)$.

**M3 · Degree two, scans 331, 333.** The calculation uses a provisional formula for cohomology on $T$. The two values of the coefficient presheaf are written $H^q(G,M)$ and $H^q(G\times G,M)$. In degree two the manuscript obtains the cokernel of $u\mapsto(u,-u)$ on $H=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{mon}}(G,M)$, then writes $H^2(X,M)\simeq H$. The resulting criterion concerns the vanishing of the abelian group enveloping $G$. The author singles out a nonzero abelian group as a case where $X$ fails to be 2-connected.

**M4 · The fork and the terminal presheaf, scans 333, 335.** The fork with arrows from $a_0$ to $b_0,c_0$ is redrawn without subscripts inside $X$. The assertion concerns a diagram whose amalgamated sum in $X^\wedge$ is $e_X$. The author says that asphericity of $X$ need not follow, then leaves open a question about 1-connectivity. The overwritten name of the fork is clearest as $\Psi$ on 335.

**M5 · Changing the left side of a square, scans 335, 337.** In the diagram marked $(\alpha)$, $U=\Psi$, $T=\{t\}$, and adjoining a final object $t$ gives the square pictured in the margin. For $U'$ the author takes the two-object construction. The map out of $\Psi$ sends the fork to the two projections $b\times b\to b$. He writes $p_!(e_U)=e_{U'}$, identifies a final object in $X'$, and chooses $G=\{\pm1\}$. He states $i\in W$, with « et même asphérique » beside it, and $i'\notin W$.

**M6 · Adjoining another final object, scans 337, 339.** The inclusion $p':U'\to U''$ adjoins a final object. The author identifies the direct image of $e_{U'}$ with $U'$ viewed as a presheaf on $U''$. A second square has $p''=p'p$ and the maps $i'',q''$. The obstruction cited for $X''$ is the slice written $U''_{/t''}\simeq U'$. The sentence invoking the slice criterion carries the marginal qualification « pas encore démontré ! ».

**M7 · A four-arrow candidate, scans 339, 341.** The author seeks a simpler functor from the fork and a nonaspherical direct image of its terminal presheaf. In the candidate $Y$, two arrows go from $a$ to $b$ and two from $a$ to $c$. The calculation gives singleton values at $b,c$ and identifies the elements named by $\beta_0,\gamma_0$ at $a$. The last lines describe the fibres above $b,c,a$. The diagram, calculation and fibre description are crossed through. The introductory proposal preceding them remains live.

Scans 328, 330, 332, 334, 336, 338 and 340 carry a different text on personal experience, faith and the psyche. Its independent section numbering and dates are recorded in the notes on the sheets.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · High confidence for the cases checked.** I computed the cokernel of the displayed antidiagonal map on cyclic groups of orders 2, 3, 4 and 6. The resulting quotients have 2, 3, 4 and 6 classes respectively, and addition of the two coordinates gives a distinct residue for each class. This checks the sign and quotient in the degree-two calculation for those inputs. The spectral-sequence premises remain provisional on the sheets.

**R2 · High confidence for this finite computation.** For $G=\{\pm1\}$ and additive coefficients $\mathbf Z/2$, there are exactly two monoid maps $G\to\mathbf Z/2$. They agree at $+1$ and differ at $-1$. Thus the right-hand side of the degree-two formula on 333 is nonzero in the example chosen on 337, if that formula applies.

**R3 · Low confidence as an interpretation of the cancelled draft.** Imposing just the written identification on four arrow names leaves three classes. Their names are the common class of $\beta_0,\gamma_0$, the class of $\beta_1$, and the class of $\gamma_1$. This accounts for the count in the last sentence. The crossing strokes and uncertain final label limit what can be inferred about the proposed counterexample.
