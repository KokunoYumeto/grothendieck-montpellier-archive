# Mathematical notes on cote 57, scans 164–179

## What the pages do

**164 · Divisors, Picard classes and tangents.** An inclusion $Y\subset X$ stands beside a map from $\mathrm{Div}$ to $\mathrm{Pic}$. The sequences compare $\underline L$, its restriction to $Y$, and the ideal $\underline J$, with $\underline L=\underline J^{-1}$. Three captions align the relative tangent of $\mathrm{Div}/\mathrm{Pic}$, the tangent of $\mathrm{Div}/S$ and the tangent of $\mathrm{Pic}/S$ with successive terms. Separate notes under the next two terms relate universal vanishing to simplicity along a section.

**165 · Completion of a topological module.** The typescript extends scalar multiplication by continuity from $A\times E$ to $\hat A\times\hat E$. It retains the scalar identities and, in the unital case, the identity element. This supplies the completed module.

**165 · Passing to separated objects.** Without assuming separation, the typescript takes the closures $N,F$ of zero in $A,E$. It passes the scalar action to $B=A/N$ and $L=E/F$. It calls $L$ the associated separated module and $\hat L$ the separated completion.

**166 · Schematically dense opens.** The definition uses closed subpreschemes and the equality $\overline U=X$. The writer then gives a local test in terms of a section vanishing on $U\cap V$. He records locality on an open cover and the behaviour of two schematically dense opens under intersection.

**166–167 · Pseudomorphisms and pseudo-sections.** The proposed morphisms are equivalence classes on schematically dense opens. The domain formula describes how a domain of definition changes on restriction. Scan 167 applies this formula to a sheaf of pseudo-$S$-morphisms with separated target and takes $X=S,\ Y=X[t]$ to obtain a sheaf of algebras. An assertion of quasi-coherence at the bottom is crossed out.

**169 · Divisor of a function.** To a prime divisor $V$ the sheet associates its local discrete valuation ring and its valuation on $K=k(X)$. It forms $\operatorname{div} f$ by summing the orders times $V$. Finiteness is reduced to the affine normal noetherian case. The theorem relates $\operatorname{div} f\geq0$ to $f$ being defined everywhere, using an intersection of local rings.

**170 · Exceptional locus and the graph proof.** The corollary describes the undefined locus of a function by the negative valuation terms. The theorem then considers a rational map from a normal $n$-dimensional variety to a complete variety. The first demonstration uses the graph, a proper birational morphism, and the dimension of its positive-dimensional fibres. The invocation of the “Main th.” locates an isomorphism off that locus.

**171 · Valuative proof.** The second demonstration tests the map at an irreducible hypersurface $V$. The author asks for a local ring on $Y$ dominated by $\underline O_{(V)}$ and invokes the valuative characterization of completeness. This is the other argument for the dimension bound on scan 170.

**173 · Two preliminary Picard functors.** The unprimed functor assigns $\operatorname{Pic}(X')$ to a base change $X'=X\times_S S'$. The primed one takes global sections of the relative first cohomology sheaf of units. Under the degree-zero hypothesis, Proposition 1 maps the quotient by the absolute Picard group of $S$ into the primed group. A section makes this an isomorphism. The corollary formulates a related full-faithfulness statement for locally free sheaves.

**174 · Low-degree sequence and obstruction.** The sequence begins $0\to\operatorname{Pic}(S)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\mathrm{Prépic}'(X/S)$. The next discussion describes the last image by an obstruction in $H^2(S,\underline O_S^*)$. A longer display identifies the terms coming from the Leray spectral sequence, with the final map drawn downwards.

**174–175 · Trivialization along a section and gluing.** An object of $\underline C$ is an invertible sheaf $\underline L$ equipped with an isomorphism $\underline O_S\to s^*\underline L$. Corollaire 2 states that its automorphisms are trivial. The next page chooses local sheaves and trivializations. The uniqueness of the isomorphisms $\varphi_{ij}$ preserving them supplies the compatibility used to glue.

**175–176 · Faithfully flat change of base.** Proposition 2 asserts injectivity for the primed groups and, when there is locally a section on $S$, exactness of the comparison with the two maps over $S''=S'\times_S S'$. The corollary on 176 tests whether a locally free sheaf comes from the base. Its argument uses the direct image, the evaluation map and behaviour under flat change of base.

**177 · Descent for the primed functor.** The proof treats two matching images over the double overlap. After passing locally to a section it appeals to faithfully flat descent for invertible sheaves with a marked section. Corollaire 3 specifies that the degree-zero identity persists on every base change and adds a local section hypothesis.

**178 · The relative Picard functor.** The author defines it as the “enveloppe exacte” for the specified descent and immediately qualifies which morphisms should be used. A diagram labels the two maps from unprimed Prépic through primed Prépic to Pic by $\alpha$ and $\beta$. The discussion identifies the kernel of the first with the absolute Picard functor under the universal hypothesis. It also gives a section hypothesis for surjectivity.

**179 · The second map and representability.** The universal degree-zero condition gives the stated monomorphism $\beta$; the local section hypothesis adds surjectivity. A nontrivial Severi–Brauer variety over a field is the example named for failure of surjectivity. The projected obstruction for $\beta\alpha$ takes values in a degree-two group. The last paragraph distinguishes representability of the three functors and postpones the positive cases for Pic.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, low confidence, scan 164.** The last superscript reads 1. The degrees in the preceding terms and the short exact sequence above would instead lead to $H^2_f(\underline O_X)$ at this point in a long cohomology sequence. The term meets the cropped right edge. This is a question about the intended sequence.
- **R2 · Model, medium confidence, scans 173–175 and 177.** A trivialization along a section distinguishes a line bundle from its twists by pullbacks from the base. The quotient by $\operatorname{Pic}(S)$ on 173 gives context for the normalization on 175. An associated reading question on 174 is whether the intended functor in “Tout … élément de Prépic $(X/S)$” is primed. The frozen reading is unprimed. The proof uses local representatives, and small prime marks are easy to lose in this hand.
- **R3 · Model, low confidence, scan 173, boxed note.** The frozen ending is “leurs propriétés etc”. An alternative from the blind reading is “imm[ersions] projectives etc”. The short word after “inversibles” begins with a narrow detached stroke. In the word on the next line a low descender precedes a tall letter. These strokes leave the wording open.
- **R4 · Model, low confidence, scan 166, opening definition.** The frozen word is “schématiquement”. The last part may have been struck, leaving “schémati.” as the live abbreviation. The heavy horizontal stroke coincides with several joined letters, so it is difficult to distinguish retracing from cancellation.
