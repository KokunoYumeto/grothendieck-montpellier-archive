# Hand log · cote 57, scans 242–256

**242.** The supply hand loops the capital L leftwards at the baseline, then rises far above the typed body. The upright, sometimes hooked 1 in the list of functions has no bottom serif. These forms distinguish the two L alphabets and the first numbered coordinate. The correction above *linéaire* has a broad undulating ending. The vertical pen stroke beside the fixed-component paragraph spans four lines. Hand attribution remains open.
**243.** In the working hand the upright of a k may be almost all that distinguishes it from h. The n at the foot of X is a low wave; m can be longer, though the lengths overlap in the crossed paragraph. Pic has a very narrow P, and its underlining is significant in the two live formulas. The long diagonals and frame cancel the whole lemma. They cross several perfectly readable words. The copy supplies little independent evidence for the endings after *rappelle* and *connexes*.
**244.** Capital I and lowercase l are distinguishable here by the top and bottom serifs of I. In the first W exponent the 1 is a single upright with a short upper return. The two short accents on THÉORÈME and the oblique accent on donné are distinguishable from the keyed letters. In the final underline the second m is a separate raised type impact. The pencil ? beside the theorem has a separate faint dot.
**245.** The short waves for n and m differ most clearly beside the top inequality. The page distinguishes underlined and plain Pic even in places where the same distinction is unexpected. The marginal diagram uses very small turned membership signs, different from the vertical arrows. The long curved stroke carrying *le* joins the space before *soit*. The writing at the extreme right reaches the edge of the image.
**246.** The salutation and the block at the opposite edge have opposite orientations. The upper-right dark mark is the outline of a paper clip. Its reverse impression accounts for the three strokes at the upper left of 245. The remaining extensive pale writing is reversed show-through. In the cancelled question, the tall q descender in the adverb is clear; its suffix is reduced.
**248.** The ascenders of adjacent letters merge in *fondamental*. The abbreviation for isomorphism has one short ending (*isom*). The underlining on *lim* in the bracket is straight; under the displayed limit there is an arrowhead. A separate strip of ink just right of the map has no continuation.
**249.** The type is upside down in the archival view. The lighter roman sorts have ordinary serifs, including the r in “élément minimal”, despite the z in the next line. In the dark underlining, a gap around a letter is meaningful: the mathematical variables were supplied in reserved spaces. The pen-shaped 3 and 5 differ from the keyed 0.
**250.** A dot distinguishes the superscript i from a prime. The f with the small hook below the line recurs in both displays. The E in the support condition has a long lower return, unlike the raised ink over the F in item (i). The star-shaped lower hook alone does not always define a complete asterisk.
**251.** On the reverse typescript the alternation of two and three stops is visible between the low tails of the bounding commas. These tails distinguish commas from the central stops. The capital T in the image of the ring has full-height serifs and a top bar; all nearby t's have short crossbars.
**252.** The transverse stroke of the handwritten Z is doubled. Alpha in a superscript is narrower than the low Greek beta (two loops). A crossed suffix remains attached to the Z immediately above it, never to the group below. The long strokes beside the EGA citation and *et β* are insertion leaders. The rightmost parenthesis runs into the edge.
**253.** The looped initial and two tall strokes in the marginal “Grothendieck” are in darker ink than the title. The person's name is readable; the corner inscription alone does not identify who wrote it. In the proof, the faint small letter in the contractions has a right arch, while q has a descender.
**254.** The large pen letters can be corrected over one another. In the proof the crossed first draft of the title lies below “Proposition”; the longer horizontal stroke under both is an underline. A small hook or dot on a long horizontal stroke may be the entire written ending of a word.
**255.** The short descender and dot at the open end of the letter are retained as “q.” The fuller hand here separates letters much more clearly than the cohomology notes. The two long faint diagonals cross several words and the salutation.
**256.** The top panel combines pale running words above a darker diagram, and a broad saw-tooth pen stroke cancels the entire third horizontal row. The two oblique marginal paragraphs require different rotations. The edges of the black loops at lower left cannot always be sorted into skeletons, corrections and delimiters. The last 7 lies above another loop in the theorem number.

## Cross-page collation

The narrow dotted superscript i is repeated on the R and H of 250, 252, 254 and 256. The opening degree-one specialization on 256 has a rounded form instead and remains doubtful. The lower n of the system (243, 245, 250, 252) may be only a short horizontal wave. In the lower left of 256 one suffix has a much sharper upward return and may be U. The crossed descender in the final adjective on 243 favours **finis** on a fresh isolated crop. The underlined and un-underlined instances of Pic on 243 and 245 were compared individually, including two underlines where the surrounding argument might have suggested a plain absolute Pic. The light lower-case sorts on 249, 251 and 253 contrast with keyed body letters, but the same standing n, r and q recur in the arguments about prime ideals. The numerals 293 above those pages have the same three pen shapes. The keyed 227 on 242 and 244 uses repeated impressions of the same 2. On 249 and 251 the lemma numerals are supplied in pen, while the 1 of Lemme 1 on 253 has keyed serifs.
