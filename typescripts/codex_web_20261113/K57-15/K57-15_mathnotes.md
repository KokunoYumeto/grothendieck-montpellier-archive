# Mathematical notes on cote 57, scans 242–246 and 248–256

## What the pages do

**242, rational map of a linear system.** In typescript 227 a basis of the module derived from a divisor gives projective coordinates for a rational map from the complete variety \(V\). The text compares changes of basis, changes of divisor used to derive the module, and removal of a fixed component.

**242, base points.** A point belongs to the base locus when it belongs to every divisor of the system. The typescript announces a criterion for the map to be defined at a point. The last sentence breaks off after « mais ait ».

**243, comparison of Picard groups.** A displayed isomorphism carries over from an earlier argument. In the general case the notes choose a finite extension \(k'/k\) and display an exact sequence from \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)\) through the \(k\)-points of the relative Picard object to \(\check H^2(k'/k,M_n)\). The text describes \(M_n\) by the units of \(H^0(X_n,\mathcal O_{X_n})\), then discusses the Mittag–Leffler condition for this system. Several qualifiers at the foot are doubtful.

**243, cancelled lemma.** A boxed and struck argument asserts torsion-freeness for a Picard cokernel under the conditions of an earlier corollary. It reduces to \(m=n+1\) and points to a subgroup of \(H^2\) with coherent coefficients. Its characteristic-zero qualification and parts of its notation are uncertain.

**244, hypersurface cycles.** Typescript 227 defines the multiplicity of a prime divisor \(W\) in \(Z(f)\) by taking the order along \(W\) of a homogeneous equation divided by an auxiliary coordinate to the same degree. For two equations of the same degree it gives \((f/g)=Z(f)-Z(g)\). It identifies the supremum of the polar divisors of the ratios with fixed denominator \(f_0\) and states that the resulting system has no fixed component.

**244, Théorème 2.** The divisor inequality \((\varphi)\geq-Z(f)\), with \(f\in R_n\), leads to a representation \(\varphi=g/f\) with \(g\in I_n\). The second assertion concerns completeness in sufficiently large degree for normal \(V\). The proposed definition of \(I\) just above is struck through.

**245, stabilization and projectivity.** An isomorphism between Čech groups at two sufficiently large indices leads to a comparison of the images of the ordinary and relative Picard systems. An element diagram records the lifting argument. An adjective describing the images remains doubtful. A separate corollary makes projectivity over the base equivalent to projectivity of every \(X_n/Y_n\).

**246, salutation and cancelled question.** « Ma chère » occupies one end of the sheet. In the opposite orientation, a struck passage asks about a torus \(S\) in \(H\subset G\) and its containment in a maximal torus. The clause involving a conjugate of \(T\) has a cancelled first formulation.

**248, Lemme 1.1.** A closed subset \(Y\) has a fundamental system of paracompact neighbourhoods in \(X\). The lemma identifies the direct limit of the cohomology of these neighbourhoods with the cohomology of the restricted abelian sheaf on \(Y\).

**248, Corollaire 1.2.** For a continuous map corrected to « fermée » and a locally compact target, the displayed comparison is between a higher direct-image stalk and fibre cohomology. The notation for the fibre varies within this passage. The overwritten first subscript on the right of the display is unresolved.

**249, conductor calculation and Lemme 5.** Page 6 of typescript 293 constructs \(\mathrm r'=\mathrm o[t]+u\mathrm r\), identifies its conductor relative to \(\mathrm o[t]\), and uses a polynomial relation and primality to obtain a contradiction. Lemme 5 then compares the local rings at a maximal prime and its contraction, stating a condition for a strictly smaller prime above the contraction. The proof begins on this sheet.

**250, Lemme 1.3.** For a proper morphism of complex analytic spaces and a coherent sheaf, the notes give coherence of higher direct images, the Mittag–Leffler condition on the cohomology of the thickenings \(X_n\), and comparison with the completed stalk.

**250, Corollaire 1.4.** An isolated point of the support gives the comparison with the stalk itself. The coefficient letter in the support condition has two possible readings. The coefficient in the subsequent stalk formula is \(F\).

**251 and 253, Lemme 1 and the opening of Lemme 2.** Page 2 of typescript 293 continues its opening sheet, which occurs later in scan order. Lemme 1 seeks a proper subprime of a given maximal prime containing its contraction. Its proof reduces to an integrally closed base, uses algebraically independent indeterminates, and compares contracted prime ideals. Lemme 2 starts an argument over a local domain. Its polynomial hypothesis breaks off on 251.

**252 and 254, passage to an inverse limit.** Starting from the short sheaf sequence on \(X_n\), the writer forms the long cohomology sequence, compares the integral groups, and examines the images of the transition morphisms and the kernels of \(\alpha^{i+1}\). The continuation uses finite generation and torsion-freeness. The last clause about ranks is hard to read. Proposition 1.5 states a Mittag–Leffler conclusion for unit-sheaf cohomology and gives a sequence with completed direct-image stalks.

**254 and 256, diagram (D) and Corollaire 1.6.** A second exact sequence on \(X\), evaluated at \(y\), provides the comparison rows of (D). The notes invoke the five lemma, discuss injectivity and equality of the images in the individual cohomology groups, and give an isomorphism assertion under an isolated-support condition. The sheet retains a third cancelled row, a cancelled argument in a box, and two oblique marginal discussions.

**255, letter draft.** The cancelled draft addresses « Madame ». It mentions a letter of 24 June and begins to discuss the kind of work for which the recipient may be suited. It breaks off at « q. ».

**256, opening of Th. 1.7.** After the phrase ending in \(=1\), two Picard groups are defined. The first uses the direct limit of \(\operatorname{Pic}(f^{-1}(U))\) over neighbourhoods of \(y\) and is identified with a stalk. The second uses the inverse limit of \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)\). The supplied sheet ends with the second definition.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence.** The sign in \((f/g)=Z(f)-Z(g)\) on 244 agrees coefficient by coefficient with the multiplicity convention immediately above it. **B1** is the usual additivity of order on a quotient. With the same auxiliary coordinate for \(f\) and \(g\) of the same degree, its two contributions cancel. Only this sign comparison was calculated.

**R2 · Model remark, high confidence.** The two Picard limits at the foot of 256 depend on different operations. In the first, the neighbourhood \(U\) shrinks around \(y\). In the second, the power of the maximal ideal defining \(Y_n\) increases. The comparison of the two displayed indexing sets with the definition of \(X_n\) on 250 establishes which arrow accompanies each symbol. The continuation of Th. 1.7 lies beyond this leaf.

**Q1 · Reading question, low confidence · 256, page-file line 28.** At the pre-witness freeze the character before \(=1\) was transcribed as \(\nu\) or \(i\). The final text also admits Latin \(v\). When comparing the notes, the pen form also resembled the boundary sign \(\partial\) at 254 line 15, with an oblique entry and rounded foot. On 256 the two ends remain visibly apart, and the following Picard definitions favour a degree. This is a question about the shape of the character.

**Q2 · Reading question · 256, page-file line 22.** At the freeze the opening mathematical mark in the cancelled box read \(\beta\) or \(\delta\), followed by \(=0\). The witness review added \(\xi\) for that single character. In the newly isolated detail the left character has two offset horizontal turns before a descender, giving \(\xi\) greater visual support than the first candidate. Immediately to its right, an open curved stroke might be a membership sign. Confidence is medium for \(\xi\) and low for the adjoining sign. The latter was outside the original mark.
