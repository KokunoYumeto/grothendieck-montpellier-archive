# Mathematical notes on scans 051–066

## What the pages do

**U1 · Lengths of paths (051).** The opening continues a question from the preceding sheet. The quantity is the minimum length needed to connect fₙ and gₙ in an internal Hom between two indicated K's. He considers whether these lengths can grow without bound and whether a Thomason hypothesis would alter the answer.

**U2 · Comparing path theories (051–053).** He separates a sequence of path categories from its inductive limit. A proposed theory would assign such a system to M, Σ and two endpoints. The displayed comparison on 052 connects an ordinary Hom after localization to π₀ of an internal Hom into a path object. His objective is a transitive system of isomorphisms between the outcomes of different choices. He also suggests an approach through a dérivateur, or prédérivateur, first on finite categories.

**U3 · Finite homotopy types as probes (053–054).** He forms the full subcategory Hot₀ defined by finite categories, then writes the functor that sends X to Hom restricted to these probes. He calls it conservative, discusses the spheres as tests, raises a base-point difficulty, and asks whether the functor could also be fully faithful.

**U4 · Cover and index (055–056).** The cover marks a new unit. Its index distinguishes a definition of equivalences over S, conditions on a fundamental localizer, two theorem-scholie entries, diagrams and a later version of the theory. The entries point beyond this extract.

**U5 · Fundamental localizers (057–059).** Starting with M = Cat and W a class of arrows, he lists saturation conditions, interval and asphericity conditions, localization and fibration axioms. A diagram relates several strengths of L2 and L3. He singles out L1,L3,L4, discusses duality, then records three supplementary conditions about components and finite sums and products. The two bounds W∞ ⊂ W and W ⊂ W₀ reappear as a single chain on 059.

**U6 · Defining HOT_W(S) (059–060).** He localizes the category of diagrams on the opposite of S at maps whose components lie in W. He describes the diagram category using split fibrations, spells out strict transitivity of the chosen pullbacks, and identifies the resulting presentation of HOT_W(S). He distinguishes the displayed isomorphism from the equivalences sought for other presentations.

**U7 · A comparison diagram over S (060–062).** After a comment about the 2-category structure and internal Hom, he draws categories of split and unsplit fibrations and cofibrations, smooth, proper, perfect and Hot-fibred categories, and Cat over S. His gloss identifies objects of Parf as both smooth and proper. He corrects the stated number of exceptional vertical arrows above the line on 062.

**U8 · Base change and local systems (062–063).** The four subcategories with small round suffixes have a condition on base change or cobase change. The square on 062 relates an ordinary diagram F to a barred functor on the localization of the base; the corresponding discussion for F′ and the system on S continues across the page turn. He distinguishes a Hot-valued system from one obtained with values in Hot_W and explicitly invokes W∞ ⊂ W.

**U9 · Equivalences of presentations (063–065).** He assigns a class W_S to each category, fibre by fibre except in Cat over S, and states his theorem about the induced equivalences on localization. His proposed method is to find functors in the reverse direction and relate their composites to the identity functors by arrows in W_S. He names Φ, Ψ and a third canonical functor and writes the proposed composites Φ₀ and Ψ₀.

**U10 · Revised corollary (066).** A three-legged diagram maps split fibrations and cofibrations with suffix 0 and the perfect objects to Cat over S. In the revised statement a full subcategory through which one of p,q,r factors carries an induced class of arrows. An earlier formulation with a category, a class Σ, f and g remains under cancellation. Most of the lower proof is crossed through; the last case on this sheet ends at “f=p,”.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High confidence, textual observation (051–054).** The original question about one arrow between homotopic objects and the later question “pleinement fidèle ?” have different subjects. The intervening sheets first develop a comparison of path theories and then introduce the restriction to Hot₀. Treating the two question marks as one proposition would lose that change of subject.
- **M2 · High confidence, textual observation (059–065).** The qualifier “on l'espère” on 060 occurs after the author has called the split-fibration presentation an isomorphism. The subsequent diagram specifies which additional presentations receive his weaker equivalence claim.
- **M3 · Low confidence, figure reading (061–064).** The angular W in the earlier diagram and the four zeroes in the following prose occupy comparable positions. They might record the development of the notation. The differently shaped symbols and the overwritten count do not establish a change of convention.
- **M4 · Low confidence, figure reading (064–065).** Parts of the Φ₀ and Ψ₀ tracks can be followed below the Parf baseline in 064. The frozen diagram draws them upwards from the Fibhot level. Reading one or both from Parf is also possible because hatching and a solid vertical overlap the two paths. The formulas on 065 identify composites without resolving the drawn starting point.
- **M5 · High confidence, textual observation (066).** The corollary was rewritten in the spaces between struck lines. The version with an induced class on a full subcategory and the earlier version involving f, g and Σ can be read separately. The long oblique strikes cross the ensuing demonstration, so it gives no retained completion of the revised proof on this sheet.
