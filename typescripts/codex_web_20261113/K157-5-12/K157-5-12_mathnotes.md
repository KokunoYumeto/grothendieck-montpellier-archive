# Mathematical notes on scans 181–196

## What the pages do

**U1 · The indexing set and Corollaire 4.4.14, scans 181–182.** For a small category $J$, Grothendieck orders by inclusion the subcategories $J'$ with at most $\pi$ arrows. He calls the resulting set $I(J)$ « grand devant $\pi$ ». Given $\varphi:J\to M$, he assigns to $J'$ the colimit of its restriction. Corollaire 4.4.14 uses this construction to pass from colimits of the two specified sizes or types to arbitrary small colimits.

**U2 · Cor. 4.4.14.1, scan 182, left margin.** The marginal corollary gives the functor version. If $M$ satisfies 4.4.14 and $f$ preserves colimits of cardinality at most $\pi$ and those indexed by ordered sets large relative to $\pi$, then $f$ preserves small colimits. Scan 188 explicitly sends the reader back to this margin.

**U3 · Corollaire 4.4.15 and its proof, scans 183–185.** The strictly full subcategory $\operatorname{Ind}'_\pi(C)$ of $\widehat C$ is stable under the indicated large-index colimits, and its inclusion preserves them. To test the colimit $F$ of the $F_i$, he examines $C_{/F}$. First a family of objects receives a common upper bound. Then the arrows of a family $f_\alpha$ have equal composites with a further arrow. The diagrams distinguish the maps into $F_i$ from their composites into $F$.

**U4 · Corollaire 4.4.16, scans 185–186.** The canonical inclusion $\alpha:C\to\operatorname{Ind}_\pi(C)$ has a 2-universal property. Composition with $\alpha$ gives an equivalence from the indicated category of functors preserving small colimits to that of functors on $C$ preserving colimits of size at most $\pi$. These are the subscripts $!$ and $!\pi$ on the two underlined Hom signs. Grothendieck calls the larger category an « enveloppe ».

**U5 · The quasi-inverse, scans 186–190.** Starting with $f$ on $C$, he extends it to $Lf$ on $\operatorname{Ind}(C)$ and restricts the extension to $\operatorname{Ind}_\pi(C)$ to obtain $g=\psi(f)$. For a diagram of bounded cardinality he writes $\Phi:I\times J\to C$ and compares the two orders of taking colimits. Scan 189 concludes essential surjectivity. The diagram on 190 locates $\psi$, the extension $L$ and the restriction $R$ among the Hom categories.

**U6 · Definition 4.5.1 and the new setup, scans 190–191.** In section 4.5 a category-$\mathcal U$-paratopos is an accessible $\mathcal U$-pseudotopos. The cardinal-specific definition refers to a small generating subcategory of $\pi$-accessible objects and to a presentation of the form $\operatorname{Ind}'_\pi(M_\pi)$. For the filtration he then takes $M=\operatorname{Ind}'_{\pi_0}C$ inside $\widehat C$, where $C$ is equivalent to a small category.

**U7 · Proposition 4.5.2, scans 191–193.** For $\pi\geq\pi_1=\sup(\pi_0,\operatorname{card}\operatorname{Fl} C_0)$, the proposition compares six conditions on $F$. They bound arrows or objects in $C_{0/F}$, bound the individual $F(x)$, give a bounded presentation by an inductive system, or assert $\pi$-accessibility in $M$ or $\widehat C$. The implication diagram is on 192. For $a''\Rightarrow a$, scan 193 counts the arrows over $\alpha:x\to y$ by the choices in $F(y)$. The converse from accessibility in $M$ carries a handwritten qualification.

**U8 · Corollaire 4.5.3, scans 193–194.** Grothendieck uses condition a) to define $\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}M$ and proposes a cardinal filtration. For the stability step b) he calculates $F(x)$ at each $x$ and bounds its cardinality by $\operatorname{card}\operatorname{Ob} I\cdot\pi$. An annotation questions condition c) of a cardinal filtration. This c) belongs to the definition being cited, whereas the c) in Proposition 4.5.2 concerns the accessibility of one object.

**U9 · Corollaire 4.5.4 and the discrete calculation, scans 194–195.** The bracketed corollary would give stability under all colimits of cardinality at most $\pi$. The attempted argument uses $I=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(J,C)_{/\varphi}$. For discrete $J$, the slice becomes a product of slices. In the example Grothendieck obtains $\pi^{\pi_0}$ for the number of arrows, where he wanted an upper bound of $\pi$. He ends the bracket after « il n’y a pas moyen de faire mieux ! ».

**U10 · A second approach to the filtration, scans 195–196.** After referring to p. 76, he starts again with $F\in M$ and $J=C_{/F}$. He considers subcategories $J'$ of cardinality at most $\pi$ that are large relative to $\pi_0$, and assigns $X_{J'}=\varinjlim_{j\in J'}x_j$ to each. He identifies their colimit with $F$. The last line begins the argument that the poset of such $J'$ is large relative to $\pi$ and breaks off after a family indexed by $A$ and a struck « est ».

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · High confidence, scans 188–189.** In `(*)` the $J$-colimit is outside the $I$-colimit. In `(****)` the $I$-colimit is outside the $J$-colimit. The underbrace identifying $g(\Phi(i,j))$ with $f(\Phi(i,j))$ makes it possible to compare the two expressions directly. The questionable index on a different row of 188 does not enter this comparison.

**R2 · High confidence for the product count, scan 195.** With one $\pi$-element factor for each object of a discrete $J$, the product counts choices of one element in each factor and has cardinality $\pi^{|\operatorname{Ob}J|}$. Taking $|\operatorname{Ob}J|=\pi_0$ gives the exponent in the example. The calculation concerns the size of this particular indexing category. It leaves the general stability assertion of 4.5.4 open on these sheets.

**R3 · Low confidence, scans 191 and 193.** The choice of $C_0$ could account for the unindexed C in the later count (S7). The low subscript occurs in the definition of $\pi_1$, while the displayed estimate is written with C. The pages leave the change of representative implicit.

**R4 · Reading question, scan 192, display 12–13. Low confidence.** The frozen label reads (4.5.2.1), with (4.2.2.1) as the alternative. The first would duplicate the label on the preceding sheet, but the second digit group is too compressed to use the duplication as evidence for a different final digit.
