# Mathematical notes · cote 130, scans 037–050

## What the pages do

**Descent (037).** An argument already in progress ends with a formula for a quasi-Galois extension. The hatted group over \(X/K\) is identified with the \(\pi\)-invariants of the group on the right. The sentence also names radicial extensions.

**Two uses of boundedness (037–038).** For a set \(E\) over the places of \(\overline K\), the author bounds \(|\varphi(\xi)|\) by a divisor evaluated at the place of \(\xi\). He describes this by vanishing outside finitely many places of \(K\), together with bounded values. He then defines bounded subsets of \(X(\overline K)\times M_{\overline K}\) by finite unions of affine pieces. Coordinates generating the affine ring give the displayed lower bound on the infimum of their valuations.

**Admissibility and divisors (038–039).** A function is admissible if it is bounded on every bounded part in the stated sense. Applying this to a single geometric point over all places gives a divisor-valued map. A point defined over a finite extension leads to the corresponding group of divisors over that extension. The two properties listed are compatibility with the Galois action and an order bound on the divisors obtained from a bounded set of points.

**The example from a unit (040–041).** A global unit \(f\) gives \(\varphi_f(P,v)=v(f(P))\). The first proof using affine generators is crossed out. The surviving argument reduces to the multiplicative group and its coordinate \(x\). The conditions on \(E(M,S)\) bound \(|v(x)|\) at the places in \(S\) and give \(v(x)=0\) off \(S\). The calculation ends on 041 with \(\varphi_f(x,v)=v(x)\).

**Sheafification (041–042).** On an open set \(U\), the proposed sections are the Galois-invariant admissible functions. After asserting separatedness, the author isolates the difficulty in gluing. A bounded part of the ambient space should lie in a union of images of bounded parts from the covering opens. The next sheet accepts this assertion provisionally.

**Quasi-functions (042–043).** Units map both to admissible functions and to invertible rational functions. The proposed sheaf of quasi-functions is their amalgamated sum. A second presentation uses a quotient of a product. Its defining pair contains \(-\varphi_f\) and \(f\). The handwritten transposition strokes and the cancelled exponent on \(f\) are visible in the transcription.

**Divisors and kernels (043–045).** One sequence maps quasi-functions to Cartier divisors. The other takes a quotient of the admissible-function sheaf by the image of units. Computing the first kernel gives the underlined assertion that quasi-functions with zero divisor are admissible functions. For the other kernel the author asks which rational functions come from a unit \(g\) with \(v(g(P))=0\) at every point and place. He argues that nonconstancy on a geometric component would give too many values. For geometrically integral \(X\) he identifies the kernel with the constant sheaf attached to the \(M_K\)-units in \(K\), and writes the two exact sequences together.

**Global sections and the Picard group (045, 047, 049).** The common diagram is followed by a diagram of sections and a term \(H^1(X,\underline{\Phi}_X)\). The text uses flasqueness and identifies the first cohomology of the unit sheaf with \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\). In the quasi-projective case the author phrases the proposed replacement of two right-hand terms by zero as surjectivity of a map onto \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\). Passing to a difference of two very ample classes reduces this to the class of \(\underline O_X(1)\) on \(\mathbb P^r_K\). The supplied continuation ends there.

**Cancelled draft on rational points (046, 048).** These leaves use \(X(K)\) and \(M_K\). They attach a divisor to each point, order-bound the divisors attached to a bounded part, and question sufficiency. A globally bounded function is given a name read tentatively as “équivalente à zéro”. The draft also puts the maps of sets of places for \(K'/K\) alongside two real-valued functions and says that one defines the other.

**The reused typescript (050).** A separate argument concerns functions on a space with an \(H\)-action. Properness and compactness give a compact set \(L\) of possible group parameters, allowing an integral over \(H\) to be taken over \(L\). The sheet describes equivariant functions with saturated support and their positive cone. Proposition 2 calls for one nonnegative \(h\) such that \(g=(hg)^\rho\) for all the specified \(g\) with support in \(KH\). The proof begins with a positive function on \(K\), an averaged \(f'\) and open sets in \(X/H\). It breaks off during the choice of \(F\).

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · model · high.** The wording on 047 gives a precise stopping point for the Picard-group argument. It specifies surjectivity onto \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\), then singles out a very ample class. Scan 049 identifies that class. These leaves give no lift of the hyperplane class and no separate calculation of the full cohomology group.
- **R2 · model · low.** The order of the factors on 042–043 can be read in two stages. The curved pen stroke crosses both the product and the comma, and the later kernel pairs place the rational function first. This may record a change in the written order. The cancelled exponent leaves its relation to any sign convention uncertain.
- **R3 · model · low.** The last statement of 046 and the opening “Cela signifie aussi” of 048 read consecutively if the two cancelled leaves are put together. Their common \(K\)-point notation supports that connection. The delivered transcription preserves archive order.
- **Reading question · 044, line 13.** The frozen reading keeps “cela doit être une” with être live. At the baseline of être a dark horizontal stroke could instead cancel the word. Its overlap with the last pen stroke makes the two states difficult to distinguish. This affects the sentence’s grammar.
