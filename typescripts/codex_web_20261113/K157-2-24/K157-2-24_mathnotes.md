# K157-2-24 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 368–370.** The opening comparison identifies three sets of components, \(\pi_0(C)\), \(\pi_0(C_\infty)\) and \(\pi_0(H_C)\). The author asks what survives for maps from a general \(S\). He exhibits the category obtained by base change along \(\mathbb N^\circ\to T\), with one arrow to \(C\) and a localization arrow to \(C_\infty\). In the case \(T=\mathbb N^\circ\) he writes down the direct comparison \(C\to C_\infty\). In the general discussion, inverting the localization in Hot produces a comparison in the other direction, \(C_\infty\to C\).

**U2 · Scans 370–373.** Setting \(C=T\) turns the question about the projection into a question about asphericity of \(T\). The author considers whether \(C\) has a useful meaning apart from its fibration. The dated entry takes this up again. An isomorphism \(C_\infty\to C\) would give a way of showing that the image of \(C_\infty\) in Hot is independent of the choices made in its construction.

**U3 · Scans 372–375.** The path categories \(\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}_\Sigma(X)\) and \(\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}_\Sigma(X;a,b)\) carry composition. The difficulty is finding a corresponding multiplication on \(C_\infty\) with the required meaning and uniqueness. For \(a=b\), the author considers the nerve of a category with an associative, unital law and its relation to \(\Omega(X,a)\). A long later note in the margin challenges the argument. The diagram on 375 places \(C_t\), \(H_T\), \(C_\infty\) and \(C\) together and names the categories in which its arrows are defined.

**U4 · Scans 376–378.** The stagewise couplings concatenate paths of two types, or paths at two numbered stages. The author says they are compatible with transition maps up to homotopy, and describes the resulting coupling on the \(H_T(a,b)\). Under the stated condition on \(\Sigma\), reversing a path gives an inverse. Testing with \(Y\) gives the arrow sets of a groupoid with vertices \(\mathrm{Ob}\,X\). A marginal qualification concerns smaller \(\Sigma\). The end of 377 and beginning of 378 relate these arrows to the localization of \(X(Y)\) at \(\Sigma_Y\), evaluated on the constant objects \(a_Y,b_Y\).

**U5 · Scans 378–379.** The choice is made explicit as a cofinal functor \(\underline t:\mathbb N^\circ\to T\). The objects \(C_\infty(\underline t)\) vary contravariantly. Sending transitions to isomorphisms in Hot gives the stated factorization through the fundamental groupoid. The author distinguishes pairwise isomorphism, which connectedness would give, from a transitive system of comparisons. He considers simple connectivity as one way of obtaining the latter.

**U6 · Scans 379–381.** A multiplication \(t*t'\) with functorial maps to each factor gives a common source for pairs of systems. With the terminal object as a two-sided unit and associativity up to isomorphism, the author asserts transitivity of the indicated comparisons. For three systems he draws a six-arrow diagram through the two adjacent products and applies the contravariant functor to it.

**U7 · Scans 381–382.** Returning to paths, he calls \(C_\infty(a,b)\) canonical in Hot and writes the comparison from the canonical ind-object \(H_T(a,b)\). He describes its image using an inductive limit and a map of Hom sets. The next page treats the transition functors themselves and the maps they induce on finite tests, first in Hot, then in Cat hot. The excerpt ends after “les deux membres s'identifiant alors à”, before the two sides are identified.

At the head of 378 the frozen reading is \(H_T(a,b)\) opposite a Hom set that still depends on \(Y\). If the same defined set is intended, the expanded left side would be \(H_T^Y(a,b)\). ## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model · high confidence, conditional · scan 381.** For the six displayed arrows, if their images are invertible and the two paths through the middle vertex \(t'\) commute, the two adjacent comparisons compose to the comparison through the triple system. I checked this particular consequence by cancelling inverse arrows. The drawing has no \(t*t''\) vertex, so that direct pair comparison would require a further identification.
- **R2 · Model · high confidence · scans 378–382.** Two questions are easy to conflate here. Scan 380 asks about independence from an arbitrary morphism of systems. Its later transitivity claim refers specifically to “les iso. (canoniques !) … qu'on vient d'indiquer”. The wording gives the two questions different scopes.
- **R3 · Model · low confidence · scans 373–374.** The location of “C'est une erreur” and the repeated \(\Omega\) and \(C_\infty\simeq C\) in the margin tie the note to the loop-object argument. Precisely which sentence he meant to retract is harder to determine because the diagonal text overlaps the body.
