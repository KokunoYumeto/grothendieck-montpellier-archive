# Mathematical notes · Cote 161-5, scans 17–29

## What the pages do

**Two filtrations on inertia (17).** The last page of Serre’s paper compares divisibility of \((g-1)(T)\) by \(p^n\) with membership in the upper ramification group \(I^n\). He asks how the resulting filtrations \(v,w\) differ. The page closes with nine references.

**A representation and its weights (18–19).** The Annuaire cover introduces Serre’s course. Over a complete discretely valued field \(K\) with algebraically closed residue field of characteristic \(p\), a continuous representation acts on a finite-dimensional \(\mathbf Q_p\)-space. The completed algebraic closure \(C\) and the cyclotomic character \(\chi\) give the weight spaces. A representation is called a Hodge module here when these spaces span \(V_C\).

**The image and its algebraic envelope (19–20).** The course distinguishes the image \(G_V\) from its algebraic envelope \(H_V\). Serre states openness in the rational points, obtains a cocharacter \(h\) from the decomposition, and describes the neutral component in the solvable case. The margin beside the proposed generalization names Sen.

**Varying the representation (20).** The envelopes form an inverse system. Serre describes the resulting proalgebraic group, its category of representations and its components. Its connected part has a largest commutative quotient \(\Theta\), described using the tori \(T_E\) for finite extensions \(E/\mathbf Q_p\).

**Weights zero and one (21).** The weight dimensions are denoted by two indexed \(n\)’s. The case \(n_1=1\), under a simplicity assumption, gives a field of endomorphisms and an openness assertion. For \(n_1>1\) the course ends with an attempted classification using a one-dimensional torus with only two weights.

**The seminar report (21–22).** Raynaud moves from finite group schemes to \(p\)-divisible groups and the full-faithfulness theorem. Tate treats lifting an abelian variety via its \(p\)-divisible group. The report distinguishes his Woods Hole construction from the later sheaf-theoretic approach. The ordinary case gives a canonical lift. Publications and visits follow.

**The Hodge–Tate categories (23).** The manuscript sets out the field and ring tower, two Galois groups, a category of Hodge–Tate sheaves and its effective objects. Barsotti–Tate groups up to isogeny map to the part with degrees zero and one. A tensor subcategory generated by these objects, a fibre functor \(T_p\), its automorphism group and the image \(G_M\) for each object are written down.

**Comparison data and their extension (23–24).** A three-row table puts cocharacters, torsors and groups over \(\Omega\) alongside descended data. It gives an expression for \(T_{\rm Hdg}(M)\). The list continuing on the next page records a vector space, an F-isocrystal structure, a filtration and an isomorphism on the associated graded. The author asks how much of this package can extend beyond the original Barsotti–Tate objects.

**A first set of group and torsor data (25).** Numbered boxes specify \(G\), a continuous \(\rho\), a cocharacter and a descended torsor \(Q_L\). A cancelled formulation of the possible actions of \(\Gamma\) and their conjugation is followed by a right torsor and an equivariant map to operations on \((\Omega,Q_L,G_L)\).

**The diagonal action and a descent axiom (26).** A second formulation starts with \(G,\rho\), a diagonal action and an invariant cocharacter. A rank-one module and its multiplicative-group torsor enter a twist. The graded fibre functor has summands involving \({\rm gr}^{i}_{j}(M_\Omega)\otimes S_\Omega(-i)\). Descent of \(Q_\Omega\) is called an axiom. Below a rule, the ordinary and diagonal operations on two fibre functors are compared using a cocycle.

**A filtered functor and descent to \(K\) (27).** A right torsor under \(U''_L\) contracts with \(Q_L\) to give \(P_L\); the filtered functor is written using a second contracted product with \(T_p(M)_L\). Items 5 and 6 specify descent of \(P_L\) and an isomorphism on \(P_K\). The text reformulates the two requirements as an action prolonging an existing one. The raised symbol in item 6 is uncertain.

**Extending sections (28–29).** A section of a group extension gives an action on its kernel. Relative to a chosen section the other sections correspond to 1-cocycles. The author next fixes a section on a subgroup \(\pi\) and isolates the invariant group that can act on its extensions. Scan 29 writes out the cocycle identity with trivial restriction to \(\pi\) and asks for conjugacy of the extensions.

**A torus and a Néron model (29).** Below the separate \(H\) the author draws sequences involving \(g,N,\widetilde N,\mathbb Z\), with field and ring indices. A leader names the Néron model of \(g_K\). The right panel compares groups of sections, \(K^*\), the unit group and \(\mathbb Z\). No prose identifies this calculation as an answer to the question above it.

## Questions and conjectures

- **17.** « Quelle relation y a-t-il entre v et w ? » The proposed bound is \(w=e_Kv+O(1)\). The following question extends the comparison to \(p\)-adic Lie groups which occur as Galois groups. The pencil attribution has no proof on the sheet.
- **20.** « Il est probable que cette caractérisation … vaut sans supposer … résoluble ». Serre gives a sufficient implication, that weight zero alone force finite image. The separate annotation names Sen and *Annals*.
- **21.** « conformément à ce qu’avaient conjecturé Lubin et Tate » identifies a conjecture the report presents as obtained. « Il est possible que sa démonstration … puisse être adaptée » leaves the equal-characteristic case open. Tate’s alternative method has difficulties « qui n’ont pas été entièrement résolues ».
- **24.** « Peut-on prolonger ces données » and « A-t-on unicité … à isom unique près ? » ask for extension of the comparison package, with tensor and Hom compatibility, and uniqueness of \(T_{\rm cr}\).
- **26.** « Du moins si » leaves the hypothesis attached to the note about \(j\) unfinished. The sheet supplies the graded functor and a descent axiom, without a general proof of uniqueness of \(j\).
- **28–29.** « Est-il vrai que les α qui prolongent α₀ sont … conjugués »? The proposed sufficient condition concerns restriction \(H^1(\Gamma,G'_S(S))\to H^1(\pi,G'_S(S))\). The words « i.e. noyau unité » isolate the fibre over the distinguished point. This appears in a passage traversed by a long stroke.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence (28).** The invariant subgroup’s action on extensions of a *fixed* partial section can be checked directly from conjugation. The pertinent action on the kernel is the one induced by that partial section. This verifies the scope of S28b and leaves the existence and conjugacy questions open.

**R2 · Model remark, high confidence for the group-action distinction (29).** A transitive action on the allowed extensions can have stabilizers. In the split example with central kernel \(C_2\), \(\Gamma=\pi=C_2\), and the section already specified on \(\pi\), there is one allowed extension and two stabilizing kernel elements. This explains the mathematical difference between « torseur » and « espace homogène ». Confidence about the author’s intent for the entire cancelled passage is low.

**R3 · Model remark, medium confidence (23–27).** Scans 23–24 pose the extension problem in terms of functors. The repeated cocharacters, contracted products and descended torsors on 25–27 give a possible way to formulate the needed data. The numbering restarts and the field letters are reworked, so the sequence alone does not establish that the proposed axioms solve the question on 24.
