# K154-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**046 · The structural map.** The author describes the map from the surface with its graph $K$ by its behaviour on faces, edges and vertices. The local terms are “folding” and “double folding”. He identifies $L$ with the inverse image of the union of the $D_i$, and relates the image of a point on $K\cap L$ to the condition that the point be critical.

**046–047 · Changing the boundary of a face.** At selected critical points of $\partial F$, a small bulge gives an enlarged face $F'$. The pair $(F',F'\cap L)$ describes the associated affine system. The text next distinguishes stable and unstable exceptional edges. The long drawing on 047 shows several neighbouring intersections at once.

**047 · Symmetry and reconstruction.** Items m) and n) propose to index the sections of $L$ on each face and reconstruct the underlined sigma from $K$ and numerical data. The marginal calculation makes the index of an exceptional edge depend on vertex multiplicities. The right column introduces neighbourhoods $T_a$ and involutions on overlaps near incident edges.

**048 · Subdividing and signing a face.** The heading « Structure de $K$ » introduces new vertices of order two and two classes of vertices. On a face boundary they give dual subdivisions. The side towards which an edge points determines its sign. Three rules compare signs across a face boundary or at a common vertex. The edge symmetry fixes the endpoints of the shared edge and extends along its large edge.

**049 and 051 · Two drafts of the vertex construction.** On 049 the author adds intersection points and, in one case, a midpoint. A last rule uses the order $\nu(s)$ of a critical point and breaks off at « si ». On 051 he instead specifies conditions on $S'_K$ and $S''_K$. The number of selected points between two neighbours $t',t''$ is tied to $\nu(s)$. Both formulations have extensive cancellations.

**050 · Critical arcs.** The subset $C_K$ carries weights. The marginal definition assigns amplitude $2\nu(s)$ to an arc centred at $s$. The main text relates the sign $+1$ to the criticality of the antipodal point on a face and gives a lower bound on the number of critical boundary points.

**052–053 · Transfer between adjacent faces.** An edge symmetry transports critical points between two arcs. Their signs agree except in a specified case at $s$. For the complementary open arc the number of critical points can be zero, one or two. The summary separately treats $s$, its antipode $t$, and the interior of the arc. Scan 053 carries related configurations drawn across a computer listing. It has no written mathematical conclusion.

**054–055 · Propagating signs.** The set $A_{F,a}$ collects the large edges whose images are again large edges. The proposition divides the complementary arc in two ways and draws three possible arrangements. Two corollaries pass from the sign distribution on one face to its neighbour, and then, by connectedness, to the other faces. The author explicitly sets aside the exceptional-face case.

**056–057 · Composing reflections.** Coordinates in a cyclic group turn an elementary transition into $\sigma_i(t)=l_i-t$. The square on 056 compares this formula with the maps of polygon vertices. The author multiplies successive transitions, follows the alternating signs and obtains $\lambda(t)=t-(n-1)$ for $p=2n$. On 057 a separate calculation ends in the box $2\nu(n-2)$. The marked circles and arcs share the sheet with a printer job banner.

**058 · Image of the transport.** The half-turn translates by $n-1$ up to sign. A repeated half-turn gives the translation by $2(n-1)$. The page considers what these elements tell us about the image in a dihedral group, its cyclic subgroup and its quotient. A later marginal objection qualifies the proposed use of a cycle.

**059–060 · Parity and a loop.** One count of elementary operations pairs the sweeps and adds one departure and one arrival. A second count gives twice a vertex count plus the order of $s$, with a resulting distinction between odd and even orders. The full turn on 060 is the square of the half-turn and preserves the polygon orientation. A later paragraph claims surjectivity onto the dihedral group and begins a construction near a supersingular position.

**061 · Transport along a path.** The first, cancelled passage continues the construction by following a half-turn and returning to $D$. The entry headed « 4.1. » describes transport along a combinatorial path as a composite of reflections. For a loop, its effect on orientation is a character with values $\pm1$. The page also names vertex and edge characters and associates them with the two choices of inscribed or circumscribed $n$-gons.
