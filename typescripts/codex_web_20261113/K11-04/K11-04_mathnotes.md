## What the pages do

**Coefficient rings and examples · scan 44.** The writer discusses Poincaré duality over a coefficient ring, then gives Chow groups, quotients of cycles, and $K(X)$ with its Euler-characteristic functional as examples for bivariant rings. He distinguishes the simplicial structure from additional Poincaré properties of its components.

**Action of a correspondence · scan 44.** A class $C\in A(I\sqcup I')$ acts from $A(I)$ to $A(I')$. The construction pulls back along one inclusion, multiplies by $C$, then pushes forward along the other. The margin asks which side $C$ should multiply on.

**Composition · scan 46.** On the triple product the writer multiplies the two pulled-back classes and applies $p_{31*}$. He calculates the action of $v\circ u$ and the composite of the two actions. The diagrams identify where transitivity, the projection formula and the exchange of pullback and pushforward enter.

**Diagonal · scan 48.** Pushing $1$ forward along the diagonal gives $\Delta_I$. The displayed calculation ends at $p_{2*}\delta_*(x)=x$. The closing lines distinguish this identity on elements from the unit property still to be established for composition.

**Tautological classes in small powers · scan 51.** For $X$, $X^2$ and $X^3$ the page lists generators, relations and bases by degree. The two-factor relation $\delta^2=\chi y_1y_2$ introduces the Euler characteristic. In three factors the pair diagonals multiply to the small diagonal.

**Generators for $X^k$ · scan 53.** The writer pads a class with units and takes its transforms among the factors. The list advances from the $k$ copies of $y$ through classes on $r+1$ factors to the full diagonal. The last “relations” heading has no entries below it.

**A partition with a distinguished part · scan 55.** The data $(R,J)$ include the empty part and a chosen $J\in R$. The coordinate equations impose equalities within each part and fix coordinates in the chosen part. For two such loci the writer takes the partition generated by $R,R'$, then examines what happens to the two chosen parts.

**Products and codimensions · scans 55 and 57.** After the reduction to a single generated equivalence class, the underlying intersection is the diagonal. Scan 57 compares its codimension with the sum of the other two. The cases $s+s'=k+1$, $s+s'=k$ and $s+s'<k$ lead to different proposed products.

**Operations on the classes · scan 57.** The lower half treats external products, inverse images and direct images under projections. Deleting an index splits the calculation according as $i\in J$ or $i\notin J$. The boxed line names a generator and the diagonal classes under external products.

**Determinants and charts · scan 49 (−3.3−).** Determinants of the projective coordinates define opens by nonvanishing. Definition 3.2 requires each new set of an ordered $R$-partition to meet its predecessors’ union in exactly one index. Definition 3.3 uses the partition to select the determinants defining the chart.

**Trivialization and normalization · scans 49, 47, 45 and 43.** Proposition 3.1 identifies the chart with the product of the projective group and affine space. Scan 47 describes a coordinate section. Scan 45 writes the two components of the map in the reverse direction. Corollary 3.2 restricts the product description to invariant opens. Scan 43 illustrates it with a projective frame and then five plane points.

**Criterion in terms of weights · scans 58 and 56 (−3.14− and −3.15−).** Scan 58 relates an interior point of $C_x$ to the existence of sections $P_i$ and formulates a test using ordered weights with two constant blocks. Scan 56 uses the first point escaping each successive linear span to select a nonvanishing determinant of minimum weight. It then invokes the extreme weights.

**Stable quotient · scans 54 and 52 (−3.17− and −3.18−).** The product of the $P_i$ cuts out an affine open around a stable point. Theorem 3.8 describes the stable quotient as the base of a locally trivial principal bundle and asserts ampleness on that base. The proof descends a power of the invariant section, obtains an affine nonvanishing open downstairs, and closes by introducing notation for the stable locus.

## Remarks of the model

- **R46 · Model remark, medium confidence · scan 46, lines 32–37.** The indented projection-formula line starts with $p_{31*}$. Its target still includes the first and last factors. To identify the result with $p_{3*}$ on the next line, the outer $\beta''_*$ from the preceding line must still apply. The indentation permits reading this line as a calculation inside the preceding brackets.
- **R57 · Model remark, high confidence · scan 57, lines 5–15.** Subtracting the diagonal’s codimension from the sum of the other two gives $n(k+1-s-s')$. For $n>0$ this recovers the boxed inequality. The calculation concerns degrees and leaves the queried product coefficient open.
- **R58w · Model remark, high confidence · scans 58, lines 21–25, and 56, lines 31–34.** The two constant weight blocks have lengths $\beta+1$ and $m-\beta$. Their contributions to the sum cancel exactly. This checks the normalization used in the lemma.
- **R58z · Model remark, high confidence for the displayed conditions · scan 58, line 15.** A separating functional needs a nonzero condition somewhere in the argument. Literally, $\ell=0$ satisfies both $\ell(1,\ldots,1)=0$ and $\ell(d)\geq0$ for every generator. The sentence on this page does not say “nonzero”. Earlier standing assumptions are outside the selection.
- **RQ57 · Reading question raised while writing the notes, low confidence · scan 57, line 65.** The frozen reading after “on trouve” is $\delta_I$. An alternative is $\delta_{\bar I}$. A short, heavy horizontal sits above and slightly to the right of the upright. It could be the upper stroke of the capital $I$ or a separate bar. The preceding projection removes $i$, which makes this small distinction worth recording.
