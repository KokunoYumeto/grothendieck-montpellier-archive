# Mathematical notes · Cote 77, scans 114–128

## What the pages do

**U1 · Comparing two triples, scan 114.** The page starts in the middle of a calculation with $(E,q,w_0)$ and $(E,-q,w)$, where $w=iw_0$. The change $\theta=i\,\mathrm{id}$ takes the two vectors to $\alpha=iu-v$ and $\beta=iu+v$. The calculation ends with a minus sign in front of the commutator divided by the trace.

**U2 · A map from two cone factors, scans 115–116.** The two sections must be distinct in every fibre. The sheet expresses that condition by the nonvanishing of $\operatorname{tr}(\alpha\beta)$. It lifts the normalized commutator to $F_M(\alpha,\beta)=\operatorname{tr}(\alpha\beta)\,1-[\alpha,\beta]$. The discussion of the quotient by homotheties is crossed out. The trace-and-determinant condition on $\alpha,\beta$ is used to assert that the image lies in $D_M$.

**U3 · Removing the trace-zero assumption, scan 117.** The writer sets $u=\alpha+s\,1$ and $v=\beta+t\,1$. The commutator stays the same. He expands the trace and both determinants and frames a relation in $u,v$. The plus signs in (3) are consequential for that relation. See S1 and S2.

**U4 · The affine quadric as projective data, scans 118–119.** The data are a projective bundle $P$, a smooth quadric $Q$ and a hyperplane $H$. The affine bundle $E$ is realized as $\pi^{-1}(1)$ in an extension with kernel $V$. The notes introduce the orthogonal line $D=V^\perp$. When the quadratic form takes values in a line $L$, the calculation reaches $D\simeq L$.

**U5 · Nontangency and normalization, scans 119–121.** The condition on $H$ is expressed using $q$ on $D$. Combining (5) and (6) gives the trivializations in (7). The proposition introduces $a$ and $q$, with $q(a)=1$. The start of scan 121 returns to (10) by putting $x=a$ in (9) and using $\varphi(a,a)=2q(a)$.

**U6 · Rank and characteristic, scans 121–123.** For even-rank $T$ in residual characteristics other than two, the splitting gives $q(x,t)=q_0(x)+t^2$ and the affine equation $q_0(x)=-1$. In characteristic two the writer places $D$ inside $V$. He treats odd-rank $T$ separately using the divided discriminant, the isomorphism $D\to\mathcal O_S$, and the normalization $\pi(a)=1$. This time he obtains the affine equation without a restriction on characteristic.

**U7 · Construction of the reflection, scans 123–124.** After a scalar adjustment, the lift fixes $V$ and has the form $\mathrm{id}+v$ with $v(x)=\pi(x)c$. Imposing preservation of $q$ yields (19). The choice $c=-a$ gives the reflection. A separate marginal proposition writes its minus sign clearly and calls the reflection unique. The text records its square in (21).

**U8 · A determinant example, scan 125.** On the triangular fragment, $T$ consists of two-by-two matrices and $a$ is the identity. The writer sets down the determinant, a bilinear expression, the functional $\pi$, and the matrices with diagonal entries $a,-a$. The reuse of $a$ as a matrix entry can be distinguished by its position. The line « attention au signe ! » is beneath the trace.

**U9 · The automorphism scheme, scan 126.** Formula (22) gives the reflection for odd-rank $T$. The next section identifies automorphisms of the triple with affine automorphisms preserving $Q\cap E$. It describes one parity by $\operatorname{Aut}(V,q_0)$ and a sequence involving $G^+$. In the other parity it announces a product of $G^0$ with a constant group of order two generated by the reflection.

**U10 · Cover, scan 127.** The cover groups quadratic forms, flags and orthogonal groups under one title and carries a count of 31 pages.

**U11 · Regularity criteria, scan 128.** A new section lists equivalent conditions on $q$. They concern the divided discriminant, the smooth quadric in projective space, the open orbit of a fibre form, the dimension of its automorphism group and local equivalence to a standard form. The dimensions written for the open orbit and stabilizer are $n(n+1)/2$ and $n(n-1)/2$.

**U12 · Forms with values in a line, scan 128.** The final NB passes from $E\to\check E\otimes L$ to determinants. The last expression of (1.2) is $\operatorname{Hom}((\det E)^{\otimes2},L^{\otimes n})$. The exact grouping immediately after $\Gamma$ is doubtful.

## Slips

**S1 · Centred determinant, scan 117, (3). High confidence.** The manuscript statement **S1a** gives $\det(u-s)=s^2+\det u$, and the second has the corresponding plus for $v,t$. With $s=\operatorname{tr}(u)/2$, the corrected identity **S1b** is $\det(u-sI)=\det u-s^2$, and likewise for $v$. Taking $u=I$ gives $0$ on the left of the manuscript equality and $2$ on the right.

**S2 · Framed identity, scan 117. Low confidence about the manuscript sign.** In the framed expression **S2a**, the first mixed-determinant sign is a blot. The second is a written minus. In the corrected identity **S2b**, obtained by substituting the corrected determinants in (0), the two mixed terms have positive coefficients, $+(\det u)(\operatorname{tr}v)^2+(\det v)(\operatorname{tr}u)^2$. The coefficient of $(\det u)(\det v)$ is still $-4$. An exact polynomial expansion confirms that version, independently of what is under the blot.

**S3 · Translations, scan 118, after (1). Medium confidence.** The phrase is « des translations de $T$ ». The affine object identified with $\pi^{-1}(1)$ is $E$, whose translations lie in $V$. This suggests « des translations de $E$ ».

**S4 · Quotient letter, scan 119, (4). Medium confidence.** The sheet gives $(E/V)^\vee$. The later explanatory bracket has $T/V\simeq\mathcal O_S$, the quotient in (1). This suggests $T/V$ in (4). The separate numerator in the tensor product on the same scan remains doubtful.

**S5 · Degree and rank, scan 121, penultimate paragraph. High confidence.** The sheet reads « forme de degré $2\nu-1$ ». Its immediately preceding description uses $2\nu-1$ for the rank of $V$. The expected word is *rang*.

**S6 · Polarization, scan 125. Low confidence about the intended alteration.** The displayed bilinear expression at $I,I$ gives $-2$. Polarizing $q=\det$ with the convention $\varphi(a,a)=2q(a)$ used on 121 gives $2$. Keeping $q=\det$ would reverse the sign of the displayed bilinear expression. The handwritten warning and the reworked signs leave open which expression the author intended to change.

**S7 · Spelling, scan 128, e). Low confidence.** The terminal stroke of « standart » may represent a final t in place of the expected d in *standard*. Both readings remain in the text.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark on the two coefficients, scans 120, 122, 124 and 126. High confidence.** Both clear versions of the reflection square to the identity for the value of $\pi(a)$ given in the same rank case. For $x\mapsto x-k\pi(x)a$, the coefficient left over on applying it twice is $k(k\pi(a)-2)$. The pairs $(k,\pi(a))=(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ both make this zero. For the first map this check uses the clear marginal version of (20).

**R2 · Model remark on characteristic two, scans 120–122. High confidence.** Formula (10) puts $a$ in $\ker\pi=V$ in characteristic two. Since $a$ generates $D$ in that discussion, this explains the inclusion $D\subset V$ on scan 121. The choice $\pi(a)=1$ in (15) gives a section outside the kernel.

**R3 · Model reading question about the group sequence, scan 126, lower third. Low confidence about its intended scope.** The final text has $G^+$ acting trivially on $\det V$ and displays $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ as quotient. The frozen crossed product immediately above read $G\simeq G^+\times\mu_2$. Its last factor also admits $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Over $\mathbb F_2$ the coordinate interchange preserves $q_0(x,y)=xy$ and has determinant $1$. In this example even this nonidentity symmetry lies in the stated determinant kernel. The example raises a question about the role of characteristic two in the displayed sequence. A field-point calculation alone does not determine the quotient of group schemes.
