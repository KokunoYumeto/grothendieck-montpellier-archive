# K16-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**The corollary and its question (17).** A homomorphism from the model graded ring sends the distinguished element to L and gives projectors defining the grading. The corollary asserts uniqueness and gives an existence condition involving elements wᵢ. A second formulation uses a faithful graded module but breaks off. The question at the foot asks what happens without a prescribed grading. The author answers it using n = 1 and the small subring generated by L.

**Lefschetz elements and the converse (18, 20, 22).** Section 8 introduces terminology for the ring, its element L and the associated Λ. The note compares nonvanishing of the two extreme components, projectors and powers. Scan 20 tries expressions for Λ in terms of L and tabulates graded pieces and relations. Scan 22 compares two Lefschetz elements in the extreme degrees, then asks about a converse. At the bottom, the algebra on two degree-two generators has L′³ = L³ even though L′ fails the Lefschetz condition there.

**Local Picard groups (19).** The cancelled typescript expresses Pic(U) as an extension of NS(U) by the identity component of Pic(U). It uses the intersection matrix of the irreducible components over x to identify a finite cokernel. The symmetric pairing takes values in Q/Z. The next paragraph starts the prime-to-characteristic divisibility argument and ends at « donc ».

**Cohomology and the Brauer group (21, 23).** Sequence (8.3) relates finite torsion, cohomology with twisted coefficients, and a Tate module. The Kummer sequence gives (8.7), with an NS term on the left. For a proper smooth connected surface, the text compares the cup-product with the intersection form and describes the orthogonal complement over Qₗ. It then gives a discriminant condition for integral values and self-duality. Section 8.3 turns to the finite quotient by the divisible part and relates it to torsion in H³. The last sentence on 23 continues beyond this selection.

**Multiplication in order two (25).** The five graded components are k, V, kξ + W, the dual of V, and kξ². The table constructs their products using an alternating form φ, a W-valued alternating map ψ and a symmetric form Q on W. The four-variable identity is the associativity condition. The boxed tests at the foot attach nondegeneracy of φ to the Lefschetz condition and nondegeneracy of Q to the remaining Poincaré pairing.

**Corrections to S.G.A.D. (26).** The crossed typescript lists edits for Exposé XV. They include adding a vertical arrow to the diagram on p. 43, changing a paragraph on a Cartan subgroup on p. 81, changing C′ to T′ on p. 115, and changing the group in a dimension formula on p. 123. The heading names three exposés. The actual entries on this leaf are under XV.

**Fourfold products and extension data (27).** In the special case (xy)(zt) = 0 the image of the exterior square is isotropic. The note describes a choice of isotropic line D. The general case defines an alternating four-form χ by the coefficient of ξ² in xyzt. Multiplication of exterior squares gives the map through which the form factors. The list a)–d) records V, χ, a space carrying a symmetric form and a compatible map, and an element ξ with Q(ξ,ξ) = 1.

**The orbit example (29).** After a cancelled attempt, the author starts from a nondegenerate χ′ and takes α to be the identity on the exterior square. The rest distinguishes a bivector’s square in the exterior algebra from a tensor construction mapping to that square. Using the form defined by χ′ gives an endomorphism u. The last sentences concern its orbits and the spaces they generate as the dimension varies.

**Bibliography (28).** Fourteen entries cover homological algebra, class field theory, abelian varieties, group schemes and local fields. Reference check R28–30 pairs the English proof’s [26] and [27] with the entries by Shatz and Tate here, and [21] with Rosenberg and Zelinsky.

**Vanishing and a complex (30).** The English proof first treats degrees above 3 and then reduces degree 3 to vanishing over a field using duality and Tate’s result. A new paragraph introduces tensor powers of a purely inseparable extension K/k. Their face operators induce maps on cohomology with multiplicative-group coefficients. The resulting complex C(s) is the subject of Proposition 15.
