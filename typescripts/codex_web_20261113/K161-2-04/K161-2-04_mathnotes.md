# Mathematical notes · 161-2 · scans 51–53, 55–65

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 51.** The page asks whether an image constructed from an intersection commutes with change of base topos. In the case of rings with full support, it considers the local choices of a family of idempotents. Permutations of the choices give a pseudo-torsor. The bottom half describes a torsor \(P\), a product decomposition of \(A_P\), and compatibility of the action with the factors. The qualification about base change is written alongside this discussion.

**U2 · Scans 52–53.** Flatness is tested first with \(N\mapsto M\otimes N\), then with the injectivity of a map built from finitely many sections. A universal family and two small change-of-base diagrams follow. Faithful flatness occupies a separate circled question. Under « Algèbres » the writer lists properties from finite presentation to being a universal homeomorphism. The next sheet asks when a ring embeds in a field, using the zero section and the total ring of fractions.

**U3 · Scan 53.** The second half places freeness, projectivity, finite presentation and being monogenic beside a category of finitely presented modules. For an invertible module it writes the evaluation morphism with \(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_A(M,A)\). A cancelled calculation then starts from \(M\otimes N\simeq A\), compares the two composite tensor functors, and tries to pass to one finitely generated stage. The evaluation morphism above the calculation is live.

**U4 · Scans 55–56.** The first theorem gives several descriptions of a \(\pi\)-accessible category, including presentation by an \(\mathrm{Ind}_\pi\)-category and a strict generation condition. Its corollaries treat accessible functors, restriction of functors, representability and adjoints. The bottom of 55 treats a fibred category and functor categories. Scan 56 sets out working definitions, preservation properties, partial converses involving kernels and strict epimorphisms, examples and the conditions for a cardinal filtration.

**U5 · Scans 57–58.** Starting with \(\varphi:C\to E\), scan 57 writes two Hom comparisons, the associated functor into \(\widehat C\), and the extension in the other direction by a colimit. It places density, full faithfulness, localisation and a fully faithful restriction functor among the conditions for strict generation. The last live condition concerns a subtopos. Scan 58 has a live inclusion \(E\subset\widehat C\) and two heavily cancelled expressions. Their arguments and indices are too uncertain to use in reconstructing a further assertion.

**U6 · Scan 59.** Three conditions relate a strictly generating small category, small colimits and an embedding into a presheaf category admitting a left adjoint. The writer asks whether these give accessibility. A diagonal note offers the opposite of sets as a counterexample. The lower columns discuss structures defined by small limits, left adjoints for change of structure and preserving functors, and a category of bifunctors preserving limits separately.

**U7 · Scans 60–61.** A lemma on \(\mathrm{Ind}_\pi(C)\) describes its \(\pi\)-accessible objects by direct factors and studies the slice \(C/X\). The converse starts with an abstract \(E\). A further filtration bounds the cardinality of a cofinal set of indexing objects. Scan 61 uses a partially ordered set \(J\) of filtered full subcategories \(I'\) to express \(X\) as a colimit of the \(X_{I'}\). It records the bound \(\operatorname{card}J\leq(\pi'')^{\pi'}\), then crosses out another proposed argument about \(E_{\pi'}\).

**U8 · Scans 61–62.** A corollary characterises the Ind presentation in terms of a small strictly generating category of accessible objects. « Dém » on 62 takes a cardinal appropriate for the objects of \(C\), factors an identity through a stage, and obtains a direct-factor description. The final paragraph reduces the closure issue first to direct factors and then to countable filtered colimits. The middle boxed argument has substantial cancellations.

**U9 · Scans 63–64.** The first « Compléments » problem starts with a discrete \(I\) and \(I\to R\) in \(\Delta\). It seeks a transfinite description of presentations of algebraic theories. Successor stages change arrows, and a limit stage takes the colimit of the preceding stages. The marginal note proposes a generation criterion. NB 1 considers the possible size of the construction. NB 2 proposes adjoining axioms after choosing a small subcategory containing the objects and the endpoints of added arrows. The unfinished sentence continues at the start of 64.

**U10 · Scan 64.** The second problem asks for intrinsic conditions on an algebraic theory. Commutation with projective 2-limits is proposed. The third problem, associated with Schanuel, asks for a generalised exactness condition attached to \(\rho\to\sigma\). Restriction \(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_\Delta(\sigma,C)\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_\Delta(\rho,C)\) is to satisfy one of several conditions, including equivalence and full faithfulness.

**U11 · Scan 65.** The fourth problem considers \(\Delta\subset\Delta'\). Restriction appears at two levels in the left diagram. A second diagram relates the eight vertices labelled by theory categories, their algebraic counterparts and the two choices of \(\Delta\). A functor in the reverse direction and the notation \(\rho_{\Delta',\Delta}\) appear below. Part of the description preceding them is uncertain.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 51 · High confidence for the finite calculation.** The two equations \(e_i^2=e_i\) and \(e_i e_j=0\) explain the role of the nonzero condition independently of the uncertain connective on the page. In the residue rings modulo 2, 4, 6, 12 and 30, an enumeration found respectively 0, 0, 2, 2 and 12 ordered pairs of nonzero orthogonal idempotents. No pair had equal entries. This finite check concerns the idempotent calculation only.

**M2 · Scan 59 · High confidence for the singleton case.** The singleton part of S9 has a short independent check. The set of maps from any set to a singleton is itself a singleton, so the relevant comparison of Hom sets over a filtered diagram is a bijection. This verifies that a singleton is accessible in the opposite category. The exclusion of all other sets in the marginal assertion was not independently checked.

**M3 · Scans 60–62 · Medium confidence.** Two index sets enter the argument at different stages. \(I\) indexes the original colimit and \(J\) indexes smaller subcategories. This accounts for the change from a bound on the size of \(I\) to the separate exponential upper bound on the size of \(J\). It also explains why the question about equality in the upper margin of 61 deserves its own entry.

**M4 · Scans 63–65 · Medium confidence.** The intrinsic condition in problem 2 and the map between Hom categories in problem 3 both test a theory on varying target categories \(C\). Problem 4 instead varies \(\Delta\) itself. The shift of variable helps identify what the restriction arrows connect. It does not settle the direction or decoration of any faint stroke in the diagrams.
