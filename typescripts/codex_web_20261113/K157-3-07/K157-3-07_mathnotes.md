# K157-3-07 · Mathematical notes

Scans are the archive numbers. The corresponding manuscript leaves are 47–62. Quotations are short readings from these leaves. The observations about the two-point order below were made after the page fragments were frozen.

## What the pages do

**098 · A fibre inclusion.** The author begins with a category of the form $Z\times S$ over $S$. He treats the map from its fibre as a product of two arrows and then compares fibre inclusions for $X_0$ and $X_1$ by a square. A marginal note asks which form of W(2) the bottom arrow needs.

**098–099 · Obtaining a chain of actual arrows.** An isomorphism in the localized category does not immediately supply the chain the argument uses. The homotopy from the identity to $fg$ gives intermediate functors and pullbacks of $X$. The author emphasizes “dans” the category of fibrations.

**099–101 · Proposition 9 and its attempted proof.** The proposition passes from a left W-fibration to a stronger fibrewise conclusion under base change. In the proof the author replaces $X$ by $\Phi_S(X)$, discusses when inverse image has the required interpretation, and reduces to the split case. The last leaf identifies the transpose of the comparison map, assuming the adjunction formula, and invokes the previously cited equivalence for pullback.

**102 · Théorème 3.** Under W(1,2,3) the page arranges eight conditions in four pairs. They use contractible bases, initial or final objects, slice constructions, and pseudofibrations with a smoothness or properness condition. A ninth condition, d), is first a consequence. The final parenthesis makes it equivalent to the others under strong saturation.

**103 · Definition, three corollaries and a marginal proposition.** The definition names the W-fibrations. The corollaries specialize the criterion to proper or smooth maps, to Cat-fibrant maps and their duals, and to maps that are both W-prépropres and W-prélisses. The sideways Prop. 10 compares a map with its images on fibres and uses a second diagram involving $\Phi_S$. A separate, cancelled Théorème 4 begins below.

**104–105 · Variants of W(4).** Section IV lists conditions about base-change squares, maps on fibres and aspherical fibres. Several variants restrict the maps to perfect ones or assume split fibrations. A diagram records implications among a, a′, b, b′, b″, c and c′. The marginal comments qualify the arrow from a′ to b.

**105–107 · Proposition 1 and the corollary on locally constant coefficients.** For a cartesian square the proof seeks membership of $q$ in the strong saturation. It interprets the associated arrow as a map on homology, using direct images along the structure maps to $e$. The corollary compares the Hom sets into an arbitrary $y$ and invokes the pullback equivalence for locally constant objects under W(4b).

**107–109 · Fibrewise smoothness, properness and fibrancy.** Item 1° distinguishes a fibrewise condition from smoothness of the total morphism, with a special result for split fibrations and a compatible functor. Item 2° asks for a corresponding statement about perfectness. Proposition 2 uses the category drawn as a fork to explain the obstruction to properness. Proposition 3 asserts fibrancy for a strictly cartesian functor with fibrant fibre maps when W satisfies W(4a).

**110 · Open and closed pieces of a category.** Section V starts with an open $U$ and its closed complement $T$ in $X$. A bracketed note describes the preorder on objects from nonempty Hom sets, the opens and closed sets of the associated topology, the least open neighbourhood of a subset, and the largest closed subset contained in it.

**111 · Gluing from a bifunctor.** The two categories $U,T$ and the sets of mixed morphisms $H(u,t)$ recover the triple. Proposition 1 identifies a category of open–closed triples with one described by bifunctors. A morphism includes two functors and a compatible morphism on $H$. The directions and opposite-category mark are part of that description.

**111–112 · An induced triple.** Given functors from $U'$ and $T'$, the construction composes the original bifunctor with the pair of functors in its two variables. The author gives a representability statement, then proposes an internal-Hom version. A small note at right singles out fully faithful functors and the cases where one piece stays fixed.

**112–113 · The topos version.** Subsection 1.2 compares this construction with gluing a topos from an open and its closed complement. The gluing functor is given by $j^*i_*$. The author mentions left exactness, accessibility, and preservation of arbitrary inverse limits in the case of topoi coming from categories. The last sentence has only its opening words in this set.
