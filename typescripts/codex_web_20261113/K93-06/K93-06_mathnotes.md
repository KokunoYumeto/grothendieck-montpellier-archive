# Mathematical notes on cote 93, scans 207–220

## What the pages do

**207, curvature and the Picard functors.** The first sequence maps $P^c_{X/S}$ by curvature into the sheaf of closed 2-forms and places $P^{ci}_{X/S}$ at its kernel. A second sequence compares $P^{ci}$ with the relative Picard functor. A bracket substitutes another last term in the non-smooth case.

**207–208, comparison with an Albanese torsor.** With $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0_{X/S}=B$ an abelian scheme, the page compares differentials on $X$ and on the torsor $A^1$. The Lie-algebra identifications at the foot of 207 accompany the assertion that the resulting vector extension is universal. On 208, equality between direct images of 1-forms and closed 1-forms, together with representability assumptions, leads to (2.6). The observation $\mathrm{Hom}(B,V)=0$ enters the argument.

**208–209, the $p$-power condition.** Section 3 introduces $P^{cip}$ inside $P^{ci}$ and places the $p$-curvature map $K$ after $P^{ci}$ in (3.1). Scan 209 first asks about a tensor for a general module $E$. In the Picard sequence (3.2) a brace identifies the “faisceau de Cartier” as the failure of exactness in the middle of a three-term expression. Formula (3.3) gives $K_0$ on closed 1-forms. An insertion says it is additive, and the text distinguishes its $p$-linear and linear contributions.

**209–210, descent and Verschiebung.** Local $p$-bases lead to a description by descent to $\Lambda$. The author forms a ringed space and writes $P^{cip}$ as the sheaf associated with a Picard presheaf. Under the further hypothesis on that space, (3.4) identifies the functor with a twist of Pic. The canonical map is then called $V$. Formula (3.6) describes its kernel using differential forms satisfying a Cartier condition, modulo logarithmic differentials.

**210–211, the abelian case.** The comparison moves among $X$, the Albanese torsor and its abelian scheme. The three terms involving $P$ in (3.7) are identified with the twist of $B$. The author writes $\alpha_B=\mathrm{Ker}(V_B)$, considers its dual and the kernel of a Frobenius, and relates the extension by $\alpha_B$ to the universal vector extension.

**211–212, the nilpotent condition.** Section 4 lets $p$ be nilpotent on $S$ and introduces $P^{cin}$. The author compares its values on reduced test schemes with those of $P^{cip}$. On 212 he describes an inverse image under the tensor-valued map. Formulae (4.1)–(4.3) retain the overline on the formal part of its target. The first exponent in (4.1) presents a difficulty recorded below.

**212–213, a kernel and a completion.** A kernel $U$ is introduced in (4.4), then related to the Cartier sheaf in (4.5). Restricting to the inverse image of $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0$ gives (4.6) under a divisibility hypothesis. The following NB describes $P^{cin}$ by formal completion. In the abelian case the specified locus in the vector extension $E$ is $B^{p/S}$. The last sentence compares this information with completion along the unit section.

**214–215, the obstruction to an integrable connection.** A cover introduces $c^1(L)$ and the Cartier operation. The next sheet compares two spectral sequences using the complex indicated by ${}^1\Omega$ and the de Rham complex. The diagram places $c(L)$ above a hypercohomology group. Below it the author separates the vanishing of the Hodge class from a second obstruction in $H^2(H^0(X,\Omega^*))$. He marks a further possible vanishing “à vérifier”.

**215 and 217, changing a connection.** When the obstruction vanishes, the choices differ by a closed 1-form $\omega$. The bottom of 215 evaluates the change in compatibility with $p$th powers on a vector field. Scan 217 writes the change intrinsically and takes a quotient by the image of closed forms. A later comparison uses $\gamma$. The marginal “pas linéaire !” points to its arrow. The final hypotheses concern flatness and the kernels and cokernels of differential maps.

**216 and 218, cancelled category calculations.** The first draft considers diagrams $S\leftarrow U_0\to U$, products and functors carried by them. Its marginal note distinguishes the absence of a final object from the availability of fibre products. The later draft writes a functor on test diagrams using a morphism $T\to U$ with compatibility at $T_0$, then considers infinitesimal neighbourhoods and ind-representability. The last two lines, below the cancellations, specialize to $U=U_0=S$.

**219–220, outline for the canonical Gauss–Manin connection.** The title sheet is followed by an unfinished formula and a numbered agenda. It lists compatibilities as $S,T,E$ vary, truncated de Rham complexes, Hodge–de Rham, the transversality claims associated with Griffiths and Deligne, and stratified and crystalline variants.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · My comparison, high confidence.** The expressions on 209 (3.3) and 217 both begin with the tensor power of the added closed form and subtract a pullback of a Cartier expression. On 209 the C carries a tilde. On 217 a tilde also appears on $\omega$ in a different presentation of the difference. The comparison concerns the two terms and the intervening sign.
- **R2 · My comparison, medium confidence.** The product $U_0\times_S V_0$ on 216 and the compatibility $\psi_0u_0=\varphi_0$ on 218 both single out the vertices mapping to $S$. This explains why the base $S$ appears on the first product in the ordered pair. Both sheets are cancelled. Their contents suggest a shared construction, although neither sheet identifies itself as the continuation of the other.
- **R3 · My reading of the comparison, medium confidence.** The final paragraph of 213 specifies two loci for completion. One is the copy of $B^{p/S}$ in $E$. The other is the unit section of $E$. This gives a concrete reason for the closing phrase “plus précise”. The connective wording before the first locus remains difficult.
