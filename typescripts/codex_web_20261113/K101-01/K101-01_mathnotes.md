# Mathematical notes · Cote 101, scans 2–15

## What the pages do

**Cone and twists (2–3).** A cover announces a relation between local and projective cohomology. The next sheet starts with a graded algebra over a base \(Y\), sets \(X=\operatorname{Proj}(S)\), and describes a punctured cone \(X'\) over \(X\). For a closed part \(Z\) and an open \(U\) of \(X\), it compares Ext with support upstairs to a sum indexed by \(n\in\mathbb Z\) with coefficients \(G(n)\) downstairs. The changes of support are written below the Ext and Hom symbols. The right-hand braces attach separate hypotheses to \(F\) and \(G\).

**Localisation and completion (4).** The author writes a relative comparison over \(Y\). In a cancelled argument he sets \(U=X\). An upward arrow compares Ext for hatted and barred objects. Five margin sketches successively isolate the intersection or point under discussion. The lower calculations bring in cohomology of the complement of a barred or completed locus and invoke the five lemma. A final bracket asks for a base-change lemma.

**Two spectral sequences (5).** Two pairs of lines compare Ext with cohomology of sheaf Ext. The support \(T\) moves from the inner functor to the outer cohomology in the second comparison. The left margin connects the two comparisons. The sheet then announces the special case \(F=\underline O_X\) and leaves it at that.

**The formal affine comparison (6–7).** The second cover names local duality for modules that need not be quasi-coherent. On an affine formal scheme \(\operatorname{Spf} A\), Proposition 1 compares Ext of the modules on the space with \(\operatorname{Ext}_A\). Corollaire 2 gives the sheaf version, with a small completion sign. The bracketed argument connects the two by the spectral sequence and vanishing of cohomology. The author also gives an argument using a resolution by finite-type free modules and one using the flat map to \(\operatorname{Spec} A\). He crosses out “finie” before “de \(M\)”.

**An inverse-limit question (8).** For two coherent modules and a closed subset of a formal prescheme, a spectral sequence has sheaf Ext on the cohomology side. The question is when Ext with support can be written as a projective limit. The last sentence begins another observation about the terms indexed by \(n\) and breaks off.

**Pairings on a scratch sheet (9).** Three preliminary equations use \(U/k\), \(S/k\) and \(Y/k\); the latter two labels exchange positions between the two sides. Below, two vertical strings are paired. One pairs terms involving \(M\) with Ext or Hom into \(I\). An inclined note compares \(\operatorname{Hom}(K,I)\) with \(K\). The page ends with a comparison involving \(H_Y^i(X,M)\). Several strokes between objects lack a clear arrowhead.

**Fine sheaves (10, reused typescript).** The upper paragraph finishes a construction of a section \(s_\mu\) with prescribed support and checks its sum with \(s\) at two kinds of point. Section 6 defines a fine module sheaf in terms of its endomorphism sheaf. A local condition asks for an endomorphism that is the identity near \(F\) and zero near the complement of \(V\). The last proposition asserts that a fine sheaf is soft.

**The proposed local-duality map (11).** For a complete regular noetherian local ring of dimension \(n\), the author pairs \(H_x^i(F)\) with an Ext group in degree \(n-i\), landing in \(I\). He qualifies the resulting isomorphism claim, then reduces it to sheaves of the form \(i_!(\underline O_U)\) on principal opens. In dimension zero he calls the result a tautology. In dimension one he writes \(\operatorname{Hom}(K,K/A)\simeq K\).

**Modules as triples (11–12).** A sheaf in the one-dimensional calculation is described using \(M_0\), \(M_1\) and a map \(u\). Local cohomology is the kernel and cokernel of \(u\). A resolution of the structure sheaf yields a two-term Hom complex, with the terms explicitly labelled 0 and 1. The two comparison maps go to the duals of the cokernel and kernel, respectively. Parts a)–c) discuss the vanishing of maps to \(A\), lifting through \(K\to K/A\), and extending maps into \(K/A\).

**Adelic scratch calculations (13).** The sequence labelled “résolution de \(A\)” contains a coproduct of \(K/A_p\). In the next line a product of hatted \(K_p\)'s maps to \(\operatorname{Hom}(K,I)\). Other pieces of Hom, Ext and short sequences occupy the middle of the sheet. A small square near the foot compares a row beginning with \(A\) with one beginning with \(K\). The two labels opposite it are uncertain.

**Localisation of modules (14, reused typescript).** The corollary gives a criterion for equality of two fractions. Proposition 4 constructs the induced map on localised modules when \(f(S)\subset T\). Formula (3) concerns composition and (4) addition. The first remark identifies an induced map with \(1\otimes u\); the second begins the transition map for an inclusion of multiplicative data and stops at “telle que”.

**Dimension two (15).** With \(U=X_f\) and \(X'=X-x\), the calculation reduces \(H_x^2(F)\) to \(H^1(X',F)\). A sequence involving \(G\), supported on \(Y'\), gives the first horizontal diagram. In the second diagram the arrows run left after dualising. A map from \(A_f\) has components written \(d_g\). The author concludes that \(\varphi\) is injective and fails to be surjective. His last words turn to topological duals.
