# Mathematical notes · cote 157-2, scans 179–194

## What the pages do

**179–181 · Pullback and localization.** Grothendieck compares three conditions on change of base, b), b′), b″), for Liss, Fib and Fibsc. A square compares localization at the finer class with localization at W. A second square uses smaller categories on which the classes agree. In trying to go back from a), he distinguishes two conclusions about a pulled-back arrow: it becomes an isomorphism, or it belongs to the specified localizer. He stops at the first. Corollaire 1 treats a Hot-fibration under the hypothesis « L 6 bis ».

**182–183 · Computing inverse images.** For a Hot-fibration the manuscript uses the ordinary fibre product. For a general object it proposes first passing to C(X), then pulling C(X) back. The discussion turns from contravariance of HOT to the stronger demand that the locally constant version also be a derivator.

**183–185 · Transformations between pullbacks.** He seeks to realize $u:f\to g$ by $u^*:g^*\to f^*$ on categories over a base. He considers smoothness of X, a Hot-fibration, and properness of the base maps. Taking the source base to be $e$ turns the proposed map into an arrow between two fibres, $X_t\to X_s$. The difficulty of choosing that arrow canonically leads him to restrict the model to fibred categories.

**185–187 · Der 1, Der 2 and arbitrary sums.** A coproduct of bases gives a product of categories of fibred objects. The resulting question is whether localization commutes with that product. He considers the path categories and their connected components. For an infinite index set he identifies a restriction to families of bounded length. The starred margin on 185 separately qualifies his assertion about Der 2.

**187–188 · Der 3 and constants.** The minimum requirement he considers is full faithfulness on constant objects under pullback along a Hot-equivalence. He rewrites it in homological language and recalls a heuristic expression for $p_!p^*(a)$ using the class of S and a product with a. He connects it with the assertion about $f\times\mathrm{id}_X$ once the direct-image formalism is available.

**189–191 · A direct image on the three localizations.** For $f:S'\to S$ he uses postcomposition $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S'}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$. To descend the functor he considers base changes by local coisomorphisms. The argument passes to a square of colocalizations. Théorème 1 gives the three induced vertical functors, and Déf 1 names them « foncteurs images directes homologiques » or « foncteurs d’intégration ».

**192–193 · Localization, inclusions, composition.** The direct images commute with the localization functors in the diagram. He questions the corresponding assertion about the fully faithful inclusions, calling attention to the case of Fibhot. He records transitivity for the ordinary, locally constant and opposite versions, then returns to one fixed f.

**193–194 · The first adjunction map.** To construct the component of $\mathrm{id}\to f^*f_!$, he views X′ over S, applies the looped functor transcribed provisionally as $\Phi_S$, and pulls the result back to S′. Ordinary categorical adjunction turns the desired arrow into one over S. He identifies it with the canonical $i_{X'/S}$ and records its functoriality. This is the last construction in the extract.

## Questions and conjectures

- **181–182.** « je devrais fabriquer un contreexemple » and « Je ne me sens pas capable d’exhiber un foncteur autre qu’une Hot-fibration ». These concern preservation of the finer class and the range of compatibility with localization.
- **182–185.** « il faudra m’en assurer expressément », « que ça doit clocher », « sous réserve de la validité de la conjecture page 122 », and « Y … a-t-il moyen de le définir bel et bien ». He doubts the existence of the desired adjoints for the locally constant system. For the transformation on slice categories he expects fibred models to work. He writes « je ne doute pas en effet que ça marche ».
- **185.** The margin says « je ne sais si c’est … vrai même en supposant W … fortement saturé ». In the body he asks whether Der 1 extends to « des sommes quelconques de catégories ».
- **186–187.** « est-il toujours une équivalence ? » becomes « Je soupçonne que c’est vrai ». The proposed route would need paths of bounded length, « disons … de longueur deux ou trois ». He reserves the question for later.
- **188–189.** « je n’ai aucune chance de prouver Der 3 a′ … ni même Der 3 a) » without control of the $p_!$ formalism. He has « bon espoir » for this formalism and says the question of $p_*$ « semble beaucoup plus délicate ».
- **192–193.** « ce cas doit être regardé avec soin ultérieurement » points to the Fibhot condition. For the locally constant version of the adjunction he writes « on s’occupera … plus tard ».

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High confidence, 187, 189, 193.** The source and target in the ordinary case put the unit on $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S')$ and the counit on $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S)$. This follows by composing the two typed functors in the order drawn. The subscript on the displayed identity on 193 agrees. This check concerns only the domains of the comparisons.
- **M2 · High confidence, 186–187.** A finite collection of finite path lengths has a common finite bound. Allowing an arbitrary collection loses that immediate bound. This elementary distinction explains the force of « longueur bornée » and leaves open whether the same arrows admit shorter representatives.
- **M3 · Low confidence, 187, last display.** The word « iso » identifies the intended property. Because the frozen relation is a line with a doubtful right-hand tick, I cannot use its arrowhead to infer a variance convention.
