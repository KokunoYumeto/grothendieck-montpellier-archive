# K85-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Working list and finite maps (002–003).** The opening sheet lists representations of $G_0,G_1,G_2$ by degree of degeneration. Alongside them are questions about ramification, the resulting maps and their genera, covers of the three-punctured sphere, and elementary operations. Scan 003 passes from a presentation with three generators and product one to finite, regular three-weighted maps. It points to 44 types of hyperpolygons and to a finite quotient covering them. A marginal instruction adds degenerate cases.

**The two invariants (004–005).** A title sheet introduces the nondegenerate case. Over a scheme $S$, the theorem attaches two sections $\alpha_0,\alpha_1$ to a group form and a homomorphism. Its hypotheses involve the squares and commutation of two fibrewise elements. Its target is a discrete category described by three invertibility conditions. Full faithfulness is stated first. An insertion attaches equivalence to the specified presentation of $\Gamma=G_2$. The three consequences concern centralizers, isomorphism of two objects, and existence from prescribed sections.

**The angle calculation and rotation lemma (006–007).** A regular, parametrized, flagged $2$-polyhedron supplies a representation. The note computes $4\cos^2\theta_i$ from values of the quadratic and bilinear forms; the underbrace gives the double-angle expression for $\alpha_i$. A separate lemma computes the invariant of the induced rotation using coordinates with $f(x,y,z)=x^2+yz$. The sketch distinguishes the angle between two rays from the larger arrowed arc. Scan 007 questions the scope of the angle argument and invokes prolongation.

**Specifying the orders (007–008).** The corollary adds relations on the two adjacent products of generators. It asks for exact orders in every fibre and describes a corresponding condition on each invariant by $\Psi_{\nu_i}(\alpha_i)=0$. The treatment of invertibility and prime orders has several cancelled versions. Scan 008 ends by claiming the equivalence of the resulting categories.

**Orders $(3,3)$ and $(4,3)$ (009–010).** A cancelled remark considers emptiness in residual characteristic dividing an order $2p$. Example A then separates characteristic two from the case in which two is invertible, using the group written with the coiled $S_4$ sign. Example B adds a central factor of order two, writes down orders four and three, and considers factorization through a dihedral quotient. The second part asserts uniqueness up to unique isomorphism under the stated fibre condition.

**Orders $(3,5)$ (011).** Here $\alpha_0=-1$, and the remaining parameter satisfies $\alpha^2+\alpha-1=0$. Two norm computations exhibit the required units. The theorem compares the category of pairs with the discrete category of such sections. The restriction to $A_5$ is required to be nontrivial on each fibre. A marginal revision proposes dropping the central factor from the statement.

**Degenerate representations (012–014).** A second cover introduces $G_1$ and degenerate projective representations of $G_2$. The proposition compares an invariant divisor of degree two, an invariant projective line, containment in a torus normalizer or a Borel subgroup, and commutation or factorization conditions on the image. Old and new versions of C coexist on 013. The argument runs through stabilizers, a semidirect product and a fixed-point divisor before reducing the remaining implication to a question about quotients of $\mathbb D_\infty\times\mathbb Z/2$.

**Subgroups and quotients of the infinite dihedral group (015).** With $u=\sigma_0\sigma_1$, the lemma distinguishes $\langle u^i\rangle$ from subgroups generated by $u^i$ and $\sigma_0u^{j'}$, where $j'$ represents a class modulo $i$. It specifies normality and which conjugacy classes of reflections occur. One corollary lists quotient types of the dihedral group, a second considers its product with a group of order two. The final line has reached the case of a kernel mapping onto the second factor.

## Remarks of the model

- **M09 · High.** The product displayed on 009 L23 is $(-1)(-1)(-1-1)=-2$. The manuscript explicitly writes « au signe près » beside 2. This arithmetic therefore supplies no reason to alter the sign in the text.
- **M11 · High.** In the ring with $\alpha^2+\alpha-1=0$, the products $(\alpha+2)(1-\alpha)$ and $(\alpha-1)(-\alpha-2)$ both reduce to 1. This checks the two reciprocals on 011 L3–5. It checks only the scalar computation in the example.
- **M15 · Low.** The second quotient list on 015 L21–22 may omit the unit group. The quotient of the full group by itself is trivial, and Cor 1 mentions « gpe unité ». The connective immediately before the list in Cor 2 is doubtful, and this passage is heavily revised. Its intended scope remains open.
