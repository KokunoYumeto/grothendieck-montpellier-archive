# Mathematical notes · K67-05

## What the pages do

**Scans 69–70 · Disks and tangent metrics.** A sentence continued from the preceding leaf asserts a bijection between admissible systems of conformal disks at marked points and systems of standard disks in their tangent spaces. The proposed bundle over Teichmüller space has one positive scale per marked point. A canonical section would single out a Euclidean metric on each tangent line. Grothendieck asks how to reconcile that consequence with genus zero, where he sees no canonical metric on the surface.

**Scans 70–71 · A disk with a slit.** Section 5 recasts local lemmas 2 and 3 as a global uniformization problem. In Lemma 4 A, a rectifiable Jordan curve and an interval meet at an endpoint. The proposed disk map is holomorphic on the interior. One boundary arc parametrizes the Jordan curve away from the attachment point. The other two arcs each run along the slit. The two drawings label the attachment and the corresponding boundary points on the disk.

**Scans 72–74 · The inverse boundary problem.** An ordered triple of boundary points fixes the normalization. The two copies of the slit give a rectifiable identification of boundary arcs. Part B starts with such an identification and asks for its normalized holomorphic realization. The Scholie puts four kinds of data in correspondence. These are an endpoint-preserving homeomorphism of an interval, a rectifiable segment in the disk, a continuous disk map with the listed properties, and a convergent power series with a prescribed boundary extension.

**Scans 74–75 · Cutting, capping and making nodes.** Starting with a bordered surface and its cutting curves, §6 cuts to obtain X′ and fills boundary components to obtain X″ with marked disks. Certain disks are paired by anti-isomorphisms. Identifying their centers gives a nodal curve X‴ with normalization X″. The unpaired centers become marked points. At a node the construction supplies a nonzero element of the tensor product of the tangent lines on its two branches. This is the “structure de propagation normale” used in the target groupoid.

**Scans 76–77 · Two maps on moduli.** The first triangle sends cut bordered surfaces to stable curves with normal propagation and a system of disks. Both vertices map to the combinatorial diagram with its toric data. The second triangle omits the multidisk structure and uses the underlying combinatorial type. Grothendieck assigns real dimensions in the first case and complex dimensions in the second. The leading digit of the first exponent is doubtful in the manuscript.

**Scans 78–80 · A proposed inverse.** At a pair of branches the propagation datum gives a duality pairing. Choosing a conformal disk on one tangent line gives its polar disk on the other. The proposed realization of these disks on the normalization would identify their boundary circles and allow the surface to be glued back together. He then considers scaling one by λ and the other by its reciprocal and explicitly questions the resulting argument.

**Scans 80–82 · A test with two pairs of pants.** The test case has genus zero and four boundary components. The source description gives two combinatorial triples, the seam along which they are joined, lengths and a gluing parameter θ. The target description gives a pair of generalized pants, punctural boundary components, parameters λᵢ and an identification. In general he proposes to examine fundamental groups for a fixed cutting type, then the tangent map to test whether (*) is étale.

**Scan 84 · Six distinguished points on a projective line.** A four-element set I yields the set J of two-plus-two partitions and a plane V(J) over the field with two elements. Its nonidentity elements act by double transpositions. For four marked points on a projective line, Grothendieck extends the action to the line and collects the fixed points. In coordinates the three transformations are −z, 1/z and −1/z. The fixed points give the six vertices of the octahedral configuration. He compares the normalizer extension with the extension defined by the action on I.

**Scans 84–85 · Splittings and orientation.** For a trivial torsor the discussion passes from an isomorphism of extensions to a splitting. An antipodal involution on the six vertices defines the faces. A face has three elements and, together with its antipode, exhausts the six. The text says the stabilizer of such a triple realizes a splitting. The distinction between a triple and its antipodal pair matters here (S85-face). The edges are the two-element subsets of faces. An oriented octahedral structure singles out the normalizer action. The choice of orientation is related to the choice of a square root of −1. The leaf ends after introducing the notation for the octahedron, its pairs of faces, and its normalizer.

## Remarks of the model

- **M84-fixed · Model, high confidence.** The three displayed matrices have exactly the three fixed-point pairs written alongside them. I solved the fixed-point equations in the affine coordinate and checked infinity separately. The three pairs are disjoint. This checks the coordinate example on scan 84.
- **M85-pairs · Model, high confidence.** In the six-point model one can choose one vertex from each of three opposite pairs in eight ways. Grouping these triples with their complementary triples gives four unordered pairs. I enumerated the choices. This checks the counts on scan 85.
- **M85-pair-splitting · Model, high confidence.** I enumerated the 24 orientation-preserving signed permutations of the three axes. Exactly six preserve the pair of opposite faces. Their permutations of the axes are all distinct, so this subgroup maps isomorphically onto the order-six quotient. Only three preserve a chosen face. This checks the proposed refinement of S85-face.
- **M73-endpoint · Model, medium confidence.** Scan 73, condition 2, reads f(0)=0. On scan 72 the normalization places the tip a of the slit at 0. In the forward description the two copies of the slit meet at the source boundary point 1. That comparison suggests f(1)=0 as a possible intended boundary normalization. The zero inside the parentheses on scan 73 has a small rounded outline. I do not infer a different reading from the geometry.
