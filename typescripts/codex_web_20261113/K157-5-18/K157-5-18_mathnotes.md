# Mathematical notes on Montpellier 157-5, scans 277–292

Alexander Grothendieck, *Les Dérivateurs*, undated manuscript, Montpellier, cote 157-5. Scan numbers below refer to the archive PDF. Block numbers refer to the accompanying inventory. The manuscript applies *utile* and *régulier* to a cardinal **for a filtration**. The supplied run begins after the start of 4.8.2 and ends within a lemma. Greek π is the first reading of a recurrent cardinal symbol that can also resemble κ or k.

## What the pages do

**4.8.2 and Corollary 4.8.3 (277).** The continuation of 4.8.2 treats strict generation under passage from \(i\) to \(i'\), and the effect of enlarging the family of all arrows \(i(x)\to X\). A starred margin note weakens one hypothesis to condition a) of \(1^\circ\). The corollary applies the passage to a full subcategory \(C'\) containing a strictly generating \(C\). An instruction beside an overwritten number concerns the order of the clauses.

**Proof of 4.8.2 (278–281).** To the family \(i(x)\to X\), the author adjoins arrows \(p_\alpha:X_\alpha\to X\). The three forks distinguish a pair from the original family, a mixed pair, and a pair from the added family. Compatibility on the original family supplies \(u:X\to Y\). To obtain \(up_\alpha=u_\alpha\), he tests after the arrows \(q_y:i(y)\to X_\alpha\) and uses the mixed fork. The last paragraph uses the enlarged-family result and the full faithfulness of \(i'\) for \(3^\circ\). The changes between \(u\) and \(v\) in the individual formulas are recorded under Slips.

**Proof begun under 4.8.1 (281–282).** The displayed chain is \(c'\Rightarrow c\Rightarrow b\Rightarrow a\). The argument for \(c\Rightarrow b\) uses an Ind presentation and essential smallness of its source. The reason offered for \(b\Rightarrow a\) is closure under the specified inductive limits and strict generation by the objects of \(C\). The latter point becomes a separate lemma.

**Lemma 4.8.4 (282–283).** The lemma says \(C\) strictly generates both \(\mathrm{Ind}\,C\) and \(\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi_0}C\). The cancelled argument would identify a slice-indexed colimit with \(F\) by a cofinality assertion. The live argument invokes the full faithfulness of \(M\to C^\wedge\) and a criterion from 4.8.2 [B01]. The line « ce foncteur est cofinal » belongs to the cancelled argument.

**Reconsideration of \(a\Rightarrow c'\) (283–284).** A small strictly generating \(S\) of \(\pi_0\)-accessible objects yields a functor \(\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi_0} S\to M\) described as fully faithful. The difficulty is essential surjectivity. Saturating \(S\) under representable colimits of bounded cardinality and under direct factors is considered. Even with \(C=M_{\pi_0}\), the author cannot see that \(C_{/F}\) is filtered. He changes the assertion he undertakes to prove to \(b\Rightarrow c'\).

**Corollary 4.8.5 (285–286).** For \(M=\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi_0}(C)\), with \(C\) equivalent to a small category, the corollary asserts that \(\pi_0\) is useful and regular for the canonical filtration. The proof of usefulness proceeds through a composite isomorphic to the identity. For regularity it appeals to a bound involving \(\pi^{\pi_0}\). Part \(2^\circ\) identifies \(M_{\pi_0}\) with the direct factors of objects of \(C\) in \(M\). One inclusion uses closure under direct factors and the other writes \(F\) as a suitable filtered colimit and obtains it as a direct factor of one term.

**Marginal Corollary 4.8.5.1 (285, B5).** When \(C\) is Karoubian, the right-hand addendum identifies \(M_{\pi_0}\) inside \(C^\wedge\) with the representable functors. Its final arrow identifies \(C\) with this level. The identification recurs in the argument on 292 [B02].

**Marginal addendum 4.8.5.2 (285, B4).** For \(u:C\to C'\) the long oblique note gives seven lettered conditions, a), a′), b), c), d), d′), d″). They concern the Karoubi envelopes, full faithfulness and retracts in the essential image, the two Ind constructions, and three presheaf functors. The last three formulas distinguish a lowered exclamation mark, a lowered star and a doubtful raised sign.

**Questions 4.8.6 (286–289).** Four numbered problems seek a separation of a) and b), additional assumptions sufficient for b), stability of \(\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}\) under direct factors, and examples of cardinals failing usefulness or regularity. The qualification « et probablement faudrait » occurs inside the proposed reduction of the third question to a problem about cofinal categories.

**The attempted example for question 3 (287–288).** The author first crosses out an ordinal construction. In the replacement he adjoins a terminal object to \(D_0\) and considers a particular retraction \(D\to D_0\). The generalization uses \(\alpha:e_{D_0^\wedge}\to x_0\). A one-object subcategory \(I_0\), with endomorphisms \(p\) and \(\mathrm{id}_{x_0}\), supplies his reason for abandoning this route to a counterexample [R01, R02].

**Definition with doubtful final numeral, apparently 4.8.7 (289–290).** The text calls \(M\) \(\pi_0\)-accessible when it is equivalent to an \(\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi_0}(C)\) of the indicated small kind. It cites the equivalent conditions b), c), c′) of 4.8.1, then defines accessibility by existence of a cardinal. A bracketed observation gives larger admissible cardinals under an exponent condition. The next sheet restates accessibility in terms of a filtration.

**Comment 4.8.8 (290).** The author gives the older terminology of SGA 4 I 9.4 the qualifier tentatively read *fbt.* He distinguishes a generating subcategory from a strictly generating one. Strict generation by accessible objects is necessary in the setting under discussion. He doubts its unqualified sufficiency and invokes an earlier result when \(C\) has the indicated closure under inductive limits.

**Proposition 4.8.9 (291–292).** The list relates a condition on the category (the word following *catégorie* is doubtful), closure under small inductive limits, closure under inductive limits of bounded cardinality, and closure under small projective limits. The bracketed c′) and c″) involve finite projective limits at a cardinal level and products or filtered projective limits. Two implications receive explicit attention, \(b'\Rightarrow b''\) and \(c\Rightarrow c'\). The first uses the Karoubian identification [B02]. The second passes through representable limits in \(C^\wedge\) and the filtration class \(\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}\). A marginal NB reserves the full comparison with the bracketed conditions for later.

**Beginning of Lemma 4.8.10 (292).** From stability of \(\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi_0}(C)\) under finite projective limits, with a qualification about a specified type, the lemma asserts corresponding stability of \(\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}\) above \(\pi_1\). It mentions \(M_\pi=\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}\) when \(\pi=\pi^{\pi_0}\). The descriptor of \(C\) in the definition of \(\pi_1\) is uncertain. The supplied text breaks off at « Plus ».

## Remarks of the model

- **R01, high confidence (288, B6 and B9).** The indicated endomorphism \(p:x_0\to e_{D_0^\wedge}\to x_0\) is idempotent. The middle return in \(p^2\) is an endomorphism of the terminal presheaf, hence its identity. This checks the closure under composition of the displayed two-element list, allowing the two entries to coincide.
- **R02, high confidence (287–288).** Failure of cofinality for the particular retraction \(D\to D_0\) leaves open the existence of a different small cofinal functor \(I_0\to D_0\). The arrows in the two assertions have different domains. The last paragraph explicitly switches to such an \(I_0\). This explains how the example and the author's last exclamation can both stand.
- **R03, high confidence (289, B2).** The explicit bases \(\pi'\geq2\) give the exponent identity required by the bracketed sufficient condition when \(\pi_0\) is infinite. The arithmetic check is \(({\pi'}^{\pi_0})^{\pi_0}={\pi'}^{\pi_0\cdot\pi_0}={\pi'}^{\pi_0}\). This checks only the cardinal exponent in the example. The transfer of accessibility is the manuscript's categorical assertion.
