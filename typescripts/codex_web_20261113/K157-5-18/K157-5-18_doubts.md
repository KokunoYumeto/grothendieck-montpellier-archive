## Legibility

The straight lines of prose generally give enough separated letters to follow the argument. The same degree of assurance does not extend to the small connecting words, to an occasional accent, or to old words beneath a heavy horizontal strike. The weakest readings are the renumbering on 277, the targets and subscripts in the compatibility diagrams on 278–280, the cancelled proof on 282–283, the crowded left addendum on 285, the last lines on 288, and the two angled notes and size qualifier on 291–292. The repeated cardinal letter can resemble π, κ or an italic k. The notation table gives local comparisons. Where only part of a cancelled word could be traced, the transcription records a tentative reading in its place. A count of zero wholly illegible-word marks measures the choice of notation and does not establish certainty.
