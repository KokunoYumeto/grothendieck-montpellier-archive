# Mathematical notes · cote 157-1, scans 114–131

## What the pages do

**M1 · Proposition 8 and its corollary, scan 114.** For a map from X to Y in Diag, Proposition 8 tests a D-fibration by pulling back maps from Y″ to Y′ over Y. Its hypothesis on the map downstairs is 1-completeness. The small square identifies the corresponding map q upstairs. The corollary treats the case where every D-equivalence in Diag is 1-complete. The writer says he knows only HOT with this property.

**M2 · Proposition 9, scans 114–115.** Condition a concerns the comparison from the fibre over y to the category with subscript /y, the maps associated to arrows of Y, and persistence under base change. Condition b concerns the direct image of a locally constant F and calculation “fibre par fibre.” Conditions c and c′ reformulate the comparison in terms of a map over Y, with final-category hypotheses in c′. The proof gives the order a ⇒ b ⇒ c ⇒ c′ ⇒ a.

**M3 · Definition 5, scan 115.** The preceding conditions give the name “D-fibration cohomologique.” Dual conditions give the homological name. The adjective “forte” requires both. A cramped addition speaks of stability under composition.

**M4 · Cancelled Proposition 10, scans 115–116.** The crossed draft considers 1-complete and 2-complete equivalences for fibre comparisons. It includes a dual condition using categories written on the left of X, a test by base change, and special cases involving final or initial objects. Some subcase labels and discarded beginnings are obscured.

**M5 · Possible stronger notions, scans 116–117.** The author weighs the convenience of the strong fibration against variants requiring 1- or 2-completeness. An internal-Hom case leads to a diagram relating cohomological, 1-complete and homological fibrations. A further proposed test asks that a pulled-back 1-complete equivalence itself remain 1-complete. He calls this version stable under composition but reports difficulty even giving nontrivial examples.

**M6 · Coefficients and Der 3, scans 118–119.** To verify a cohomological equivalence he writes a comparison of two H expressions with locally constant coefficients. He then introduces c₁ and c₂, asserting respectively cohomological and homological equivalence for each D-equivalence, and gives c′ and c″ for 1- and 2-completeness. The little implication diagram and the following NB record what he expects for HOT.

**M7 · Proposition 10 and Corollary 1, scan 119.** The standing Proposition 10 assumes Der 3 c₁. Under it D-fibration coincides with cohomological D-fibration and is stable under composition. An interlinear addition supplies a test involving p and q. The corollary gives a fibre criterion for a D-proper map. A parenthetical smooth variant has been struck from this corollary.

**M8 · Corollary 2 and the change of approach, scan 120.** With c₁ and c₂ together, the corollary identifies the fibration, cohomological fibration and homological fibration conditions, then treats a proper or smooth f. The paragraph following it explains why the author wants a direct, “élémentaire” treatment of Hot-fibrations while constructing HOT on all of Cat is still pending.

**M9 · Functor diagrams, scan 122.** The upper diagram links the looped H applied to A and B, expressions on their hatted versions, and underlined Hom expressions. An integral with subscript A labels an arc across it. The lower part gives the Hom comparison involving f! and f*, says Y is cofibré over B, and writes A ×_B Y. Its last box repeats the Hom comparison with the script H categories as subscripts.

**M10 · Implication charts, scan 124.** A small diagram relates “fibré parfait,” “propre,” “fibrant,” “lisse” and the two prefibration labels. A second has two arrows ending at D-équiv. The larger “Le Ciel” diagram arranges cohomological and homological conditions on the two sides. The legend identifies F′ and F″. The sheet ends with questions about the two prefibration conditions and Der(k-Mod).

**M11 · Short covering letter, scan 125.** On headed paper bearing Alain LAMBIN DOSTROMON’s name, the correspondent refers to an enclosed or accompanying letter and offers encouragement. The subject name after “à propos” and the autograph are uncertain.

**M12 · Variants of properness, scan 126.** A landscape sheet organises conditions on the maps i_y. It places coasphericity, equivalence, cohomological equivalence and completeness beside tests involving p and q. It also gives two base-change diagrams and a cohomology comparison with nested annotations. The oblique group experiments with “faiblement” and “quasi”; individual terms in this group and in the central diagram are uncertain.

**M13 · Letter to Jean Coulardeau, scan 127.** The writer on Guy Desmichelle’s letterhead acknowledges news of the Tribunal du Puy, expresses a hope about changes in legislation and administration, and mentions a personal connection to the Chazot and Vey families.

**M14 · Coasphericity and a local question, scan 128.** For a coaspherical f the sheet asks whether the map from f* f_* F to F is an isomorphism. It mentions locality on Y and Der 3 a′. An adjacent implication diagram has a heavily crossed inner portion and a surviving D-équiv. at its foot.

**M15 · Copy of a letter to Mr SOLE, scan 129.** The two panels carry a single letter to the Directeur Adjoint CPAM. The writer discusses a vaccination campaign, presents his own medical views and experience, asks for a reply, and adds a postscript. The panels reproduce the letterhead twice.

**M16 · Question for Thomason, scan 130.** A line relates cohomological equivalence, 1-complete equivalence and homological equivalence. A short RHom gloss is attached to its first relation. The reverse implication from homological to cohomological equivalence is posed as a question for Thomason.

**M17 · Letter and signature sheets, scan 131.** A form letter addressed to the prefect concerns the proceedings against Monsieur COULARDEAU. Two copies of the closing appeal ask the prefect to intervene. Each is followed by its own handwritten list of names, addresses and autographs.

## Questions and conjectures

- **114.** The condition imposed in the corollary “paraît quasiment caractéristique de HOT.” The wording and the stated limit of his examples leave open the possibility of other derivators.
- **116–117.** The qualification “que je sache !” follows a concern about composition. The author calls the stricter version “néanmoins inutilisable” in the general setting and writes that he cannot give “exemples non triviaux.”
- **118–120.** “Je ne sais vérifier un énoncé de cette nature … que si” describes a limitation of his method. For HOT, “sous réserve d’écrire la théorie … il doit être vrai” introduces c″. Scan 120 names the construction and proof still needed.
- **124.** “faut il fibrant coh. et hom ?”, “Est-ce tjrs le cas ? J’en doute.” and “Est-ce équivalent … Der (k-Mod), k un anneau ?” are three separate questions or reservations.
- **126.** The upper test ends “alors q est D-équiv. ??”. A lower oval asks about 1-completeness of q. The NB asks whether coaspherical maps are cohomological equivalences, adds “oui”, and goes on to ask about completeness. The exact final strengthening is difficult to read.
- **128.** “iso ?” is followed by a short local answer. The separately underlined assumption reads “On suppose Der 3 a′”.
- **130.** “Mais a-t-on” introduces the implication from homological to cohomological equivalence. “lui poser la question !” follows the reference to Thomason.
- **Letters, 127, 129, 131.** The first says “on peut espérer qu’un jour la législation évoluera”. The second asks “Pourriez vous avoir l’amabilité de me répondre.” The petition includes the rhetorical question beginning “Doit-on, en république française”.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High confidence, document structure (115–116, 119).** There are two occurrences of the heading Proposition 10. The diagonal cancellations run through the first occurrence and its continuations. The later occurrence introduces a statement under Der 3 c₁. Treating both as standing propositions would lose the visible revision.
- **R2 · High confidence, diagram reading (119–120).** In the small tree the path from c″ passes through c′ and then branches to c₁ and c₂. Corollary 2 on the following sheet names the pair (c₁,c₂) explicitly. The subscripts at the ends of the branches matter when following this reference.
- **R3 · Low confidence, possible connection (116, 130).** The internal-Hom remark on 116 might explain the RHom gloss attached to the first equivalence on 130. The phrasing is compressed in both places and there is no explicit cross-reference. This is a possible reading of the juxtaposition.
- **R4 · Low confidence, terminology (126).** The succession of crossed “faible” and boxed “faiblement” and “quasi” suggests an experiment with naming. The sheet does not settle whether these were intended as permanent terminological replacements. Several of the adjoining strokes are uncertain.
