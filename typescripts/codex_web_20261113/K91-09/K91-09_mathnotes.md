# Cote 91 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Constructible maps (114).** The opening formula defines the value at $Y$ of the functor associated to $(X,E)$ by a set of constructible maps. The next formula identifies maps of pairs with maps of the associated functors. A four-object diagram places the construction between affine objects, pairs, an Ind-category and a category of functors. The small qualifications on its arrows are substantially harder to read than the formulas.

**Partitions of an affine scheme (115).** Starting with a commutative ring $A$, the author forms $A^{\mathrm{cons}}$ from the rings $A_{\mathscr P}$ indexed by partitions of its affine scheme $X$. The pieces are locally closed and affine; the formula for a piece uses its reduction. He passes to $\widetilde X=\operatorname{Spec}(A^{\mathrm{cons}})$, describes its underlying points by a limit and identifies them with those of $X$. He then compares the topology with that of $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$.

**Topology defined by constructible pieces (116, entire draft cancelled).** The two conditions on an open $U$ involve retrocompactness and a finite-subcover property for covers by constructible parts. The draft introduces a topology whose opens are unions of locally constructible parts. It relates the $T_0$ axiom to separation by complementary opens and interprets its second condition as quasi-compactness in that topology.

**Programme and the Hom formulas (118, 120).** Six headings on 118 range from compact and locally compact préschémas to Néron spaces. A cancelled diagram and its replacement arrange functors between préschémas, systems of schemes and reduced compact schemes. The Hom formulas below compare a map out of $K$ with a map out of the image of $K$ under a primed functor. Scan 120 draws barred versions of the functors for the locally compact setting, writes successive limits in $\lambda$ and $\mu$, and ends with a fully faithful arrow out of $(\mathrm{Cons})$.

**Exterior algebra of a direct sum (119, typescript nº 371, p. 90).** The page first establishes $(u(x))^2=0$. It uses the graded sign for different components and the square-zero property within a component. The induced map $h$ is inverse to $g$, as tested on generators. Corollary 1 identifies the homogeneous degree $m$ piece with the direct sum of tensor products indexed by $m_1+\cdots+m_n=m$. Formula (7) begins a description of the map on an exterior product using a function from the positions of its factors to the summand indices. The description continues beyond the leaf.

**Naturality, summands and nilpotence (123, nº 371, p. 92).** A commutative square expresses the compatibility of the graded tensor-product description with a direct sum of linear maps. Corollary 3 takes a split injection and describes the complementary graded ideal in the exterior algebra of the target. Corollary 4 asserts nilpotence when the scalar component vanishes. Its proof begins by locating an element in a smaller exterior algebra.

**Intersections and an exterior basis (121, nº 371, p. 93).** The first lines finish the reduction to a finite-type submodule. Corollary 5 identifies the exterior algebra of $N\cap P$ with the intersection of two exterior subalgebras under the stated direct-factor hypotheses. Proposition 8 assigns an ordered wedge $e_H$ to each finite subset $H$ of a totally ordered basis index set. The proof treats a finite basis by splitting it into rank-one summands. The infinite-basis argument breaks off after “il est clair que $M$ est limite”.

**A compact scheme (122).** The heading specifies quasi-compactness and topological separation. Part (i) describes neighbourhoods that are at once open and closed. In part (ii), a finite affine cover is subdivided using $X'_i=X_i-X_i\cap\bigcup_{j<i}X_j$, and the resulting disjoint pieces are used to conclude that $X$ is affine. Part (iii) identifies constructible parts with open-and-closed parts and ends at a fresh “Donc”.

**Conditions on a point in a limit (126, 128).** Scan 126 starts with an essentially affine projective system. The first condition for a point to be *épais* uses maximality of its images in the relevant fibres. The primed condition uses a nonempty open $U_i$ in $\bar x_i$ whose inverse images lie in later closures. The double- and triple-primed versions use constructibility and a nonempty open part of $\bar x$. Scan 128 attempts to relate these conditions by arrows, then places locally closed pieces beside an ideal-theoretic calculation involving $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ and $g$. Several notes at the local ring do not survive clearly enough to recover a conclusion.

**A cohomology exercise (127, nº 316, p. 145).** Given a finite-dimensional irreducible representation and a condition involving $\dim h$ and the characteristic of $K$, the text asks for the vanishing of $H^p(g,M)$. The hint directs the reader to an automorphism of cochains, its action on cycles and boundaries, and a formula of the form $d\varrho+\varrho d$. Its operator letter and the hand-filled lower-case symbol both have alternatives.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · My remark, high confidence.** The proof at the bottom of scan 123 resumes at the top of scan 121. The typed numbers are 92 and 93 of the same document, and “appartient à une sous-algèbre” leads directly into “$\Lambda(N)$, où $N$ est un sous-module de type fini”. This gives the reader a route through the proof while preserving the order of the scans.
- **R2 · My remark, high confidence.** The β pair is a useful link between the two crowded diagrams. On 118 the bottom formula has $K$ and $\beta\mathscr Y$ on one side and $\beta'K$ and $\mathscr Y$ on the other. The first Hom line on 120 repeats this arrangement with bars, before passing to the indexed limits. Comparing the positions avoids having to decide the name of the separate long-arrow functor.
- **R3 · My remark, medium confidence.** Scan 128 can be read beside the list on 126 by counting the primes. Each of the single-, double- and triple-primed names has a counterpart. This correspondence helps locate the question marks on 128; the overwritten arrow condition remains too uncertain to infer an equivalence from it.

- **Q120a · My reading question, 120 L8, medium confidence.** The frozen target of the last Hom ends in $(\operatorname{Cons})$. The source of the same arrow has a clearly looped *o* after the larger C. The terminal word lacks that loop and could be *Ens* or *ens*. Its initial is more rounded than the E of *Ens* on 115 L10–11. This question concerns the target alone.
- **Q120b · My reading question, 120 L4–7, medium confidence.** The uppermost Hom to the right of the two limits has $\beta(\overline{\mathscr Y}_\mu)$ in the frozen text. The scan shows two closing parentheses after $\mu$. Between the beta and the left side of the script letter I cannot distinguish an opening parenthesis. A literal reading is $\operatorname{Hom}(\bar K_\lambda,\beta\overline{\mathscr Y}_\mu))$.

The first occurrence has one tall stroke and a peaked shoulder, also compatible with $h$. The two shorter subscripts have no distinct crossing stroke to decide between them.
