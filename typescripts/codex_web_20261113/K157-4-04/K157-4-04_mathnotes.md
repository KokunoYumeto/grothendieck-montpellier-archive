## What the pages do

**U01 · Evaluating diagram arrows (050).** The opening continues an earlier argument. Grothendieck defines C(I) by testing an arrow at every object s of I. He identifies TC(I) with C(I) ∩ W(I), then writes F(I) and TF(I) using a subscript operation on TC(I) and C(I). A corollary invokes homotopy fibres, cofibres and long exact sequences when M has a neutral object.

**U02 · Change of indexing category (051–052).** Section 4 chooses a closed weak Quillen structure exact on the left. A functor u from I′ to I gives restriction in the opposite direction. Under the stated limit and accessibility assumptions, restriction has ordinary left and right adjoints. Proposition 1 lists what restriction preserves, what the right adjoint preserves, and two equivalences concerning the left adjoint.

**U03 · An adjunction criterion (053–054).** Proposition 2 transports a relation between two classes of arrows across an adjunction. Its corollary gives the version for a right adjoint by interchanging the categories. The following sheet substitutes specific pairs of classes to obtain (2) and (3) of Proposition 1. The overwritten class in the second pair is uncertain.

**U04 · Discrete slices (054–055).** Corollary 1 assumes that each category written i∖I′ is discrete. Evaluating u! at s gives a colimit over s∖I′. In the discrete case this becomes a coproduct, so a map in C(I′) or TC(I′) gives a sum of component maps in C or TC. The argument uses closure under such sums. A marginal variant allows an initial object in each connected component.

**U05 · What a pointwise test detects (055).** Corollary 2 gives a necessary condition for a map to belong to F(I) or TF(I), and for an object of M(I) to be fibrant. Grothendieck singles out necessity with an underline and questions the converse for maps.

**U06 · Exactness and the choice of functors (055–057).** The scholium discusses which Cartesian or cocartesian squares, and which long exact sequences, the functors preserve. Grothendieck then questions part of his reasoning. The ordinary adjoints need not respect the localizers. The proposed adjoints between the homotopy categories have an extra W on their labels.

**U07 · Fibrant arrows (057).** A slanted Proposition 3 treats X₀ ← X₁ as an object of M(Δ¹). Its condition involves the two endpoints in M_f and the arrow in F. An adjacent corollary applies this to a monomorphism in I. A second, bracketed corollary asks about the induced map between limits for an open immersion.

**U08 · Quillénisable (057–058).** Section 5 defines the term by the existence of an exact weak Quillen structure. The relative definition requires the property for every shape I in Diag. He describes the value of keeping the definition autodual, sketches the closure properties he would like for Diag, and returns to the example furnished by §§ 3–4.

**U09 · Complexes (058–060).** For cochain complexes in an abelian category A, W consists of quasi-isomorphisms and the category of fractions is D(A). The text distinguishes the bounded variants, diagram categories for finite I, and fixed degree bounds for arbitrary I. It attributes two one-sided results to Quillen, with enough injectives or enough projectives respectively. The slanted qualification on 060 concerns infinite filtered colimits in K⁺(A).

**U10 · Bounds on direct images (060–061).** The positive-bound display gives a right adjoint to restriction. A negative-bound display proposes a left adjoint. Grothendieck questions the extension to unbounded categories and raises finite cohomological dimension as a possible hypothesis on u.

**U11 · The prederivator and its axioms (061–063).** The scholium introduces the symbols HO and a double-stroked D for the prederivator. An insertion weakens the hypothesis for defining it. Suspension and cosuspension enter the subsequent exactness statement. Der (1) is written for a finite family of shapes. Der (2) is asserted under strong saturation and quillénisabilité. For a proposed strong form of Der 3, equivalences of shapes in a chosen fundamental localizer would induce equivalences on the parts marked l.c. The general verification remains open here.

**U12 · Uniform structures (064–065).** Section 6 specifies how a single structure on M determines the classes for every I in Diag. Part (a) starts with pointwise classes and obtains the other two by the subscript operation. Part (b) adds exactness on the left. Grothendieck says which earlier propositions still apply under uniformity alone and announces applications to homotopy direct images for certain u. The last sentence continues beyond scan 065.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model remark, high confidence (054–055).** The discrete-slice calculation retains the arrow from s to u(s′) as part of a summand's index. The two-line subscript gives both s′ and that arrow. Counting only choices of s′ would lose part of the formula. The doubtful letter naming the arrow does not affect this observation.

**R02 · Model remark, high confidence (055–057).** Grothendieck's qualification concerns a change of category as well as the direction of adjunction. The arrows in Proposition 1 have endpoints M(I) and M(I′). The later diagram has endpoints HO_W(I′) and HO_W(I), and explicitly puts an extra W on the functors.

**R03 · Model remark, high confidence (058–060).** The finiteness qualification changes with the kind of bound on a complex. On 059 the signs +, −, b are followed by the underlined « si I est fini ». The following pair of formulas for a fixed bound is introduced by « pour I quelconque ». This distinction can be read without resolving the doubtful vanishing conventions.

**R04 · Model remark, medium confidence (057, 065).** The reference to « la prop 3 (p. 50) » helps place the slanted text on 057 in the author's numbering. The page number and proposition heading agree. One neighbouring corollary also has a question mark, so the citation does not establish the status of every marginal assertion.
