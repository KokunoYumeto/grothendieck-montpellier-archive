# K157-4-18 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**273 · Weak and strong fibration axioms.** Grothendieck gives two forms of Loc(3′) for categories fibred over a base S with a chosen splitting. The weak form tests a projection from its fibres. The strong form tests a cartesian functor on each fibre and requires compatibility with the splittings. Both include a clause about the comparison from X to $\Phi_S(X)$.

**274 · A cancelled typescript.** The narrative connects Weil’s conjectures with the prospect of using cohomological arguments for spaces of an arithmetic kind. Its three footnotes identify the constructions in view, name four themes of the author’s own work, and discuss Zariski and Serre. The text begins and ends mid-discussion on a page numbered P 30.

**273, 275–276 · From Loc(3) to the fibration tests.** The proof compares the fibre $X_s$ with ${}_{s\backslash}X$. The inclusion has a right adjoint. A separate added passage uses a left adjoint for the comparison of X with $\Phi_S(X)$. The square at the top of 276 relates the map on fibres to the map ${}_{s\backslash}X\to{}_{s\backslash}Y$.

**276–278 · Converse implications.** The projection from Δ¹ × X to X supplies the homotopy argument for Loc(2). For Loc(3), the author compares X over S with a fibred category with fibres ${}_{s\backslash}X$. A cancelled argument on 277 attempts to use passage to opposites. The resumed argument uses the fibres of $\Phi_S(X)$. The stronger implication ends on 278 with the square comparing X and Y to $\Phi_S(X)$ and $\Phi_S(Y)$.

**278–279 · Loc(4) and direct sums.** The condition for a sum of maps to be in W is divided into two implications. Loc 4a concerns taking the sum of maps already in W. Loc 4b concerns restriction over a summand that is both open and closed. The corollary gives a finite-family version in terms of every X being “W-cofibrant”. The last class name in that formulation is written $\mathrm{Cof}_W$.

**279–280 · Products and sums after localization.** Rappel 2.3 recalls stability under finite products for a fundamental localizer. The next proposition states that finite products exist in $\mathrm{Hot}_W$ and that localization preserves them. The corresponding assertions for arbitrary or finite sums have extra sum hypotheses. The author also states distributivity over products.

**280–281 · The product and adjunction lemmas.** Lemme 2.5 treats products of the specified sizes in a category M and in its localization. Its last sentence treats a final object. On 281 the author applies the diagonal/product pair and invokes duality for sums and initial objects. Lemme 2.6 passes a pair of adjoint functors compatible with W and W′ to the two localizations.

**282, 285 · Loc(5).** A map in W must induce a bijection on components. Proposition 2.7 relates this condition to factorization of π₀ through the localization and to conditions on the localized category and its nonempty part. The slanted NB gives additional criteria under saturation and under an axiom with a difficult subscript. The referral at the foot of 282 resumes at the head of 285.

**283–284 · A strict initial object and maps out of the final object.** The inserted argument treats a category with strict initial object $\varnothing_M$ and final object $e_M$. A sideways hypothesis asks for a map s in M satisfying an equation after localization. The lemma asserts strict initiality in the localization and surjectivity on maps from e to a nonempty object. For the first assertion the author works backwards along a zigzag ending at $\varnothing_M$. For the second he shortens a zigzag starting at e. The subsequent verification of the hypotheses breaks off at “donc”.

**285 · Two consequences for connectedness.** The first corollary says that W-aspherical objects are 0-connected under Loc(5). It also gives a converse under strong saturation and an added alternative involving fibrations. The second corollary tests Loc(5), under the stated sum hypothesis or its 4b part, on maps in W whose target is 0-connected.

**286 · The image of a set.** A set I is sent to its discrete category and then to $I_W$. Formula (2.7.2) expresses the discrete category as I copies of e. Under Loc(4a), preservation of sums gives the corresponding description using the final object $e_W$ in (2.7.3.).

**286–287 · The two adjunctions.** Under Loc(5), Proposition 2.8 places π₀ on the left of the functor taking I to $I_W$. The text on 287 also places π₀ on the right. In the two displayed bijections the arguments of Hom appear in opposite orders. The corollary takes I to be a singleton. Commentaire 2.10 relates the first formula to decomposition into connected components and begins a further remark on the reverse adjunction.
