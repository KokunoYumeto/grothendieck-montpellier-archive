# Mathematical notes on cote 10, scans 171–184

## What the pages do

**171 · Functions on the group of characters.** The sheet begins mid-sentence. The map from $A$ to $k$-valued functions on $G$ respects the unit, diagonal and augmentation. Its injectivity is expressed by saying that $G$ is total in $\underline U$. Grothendieck relates this to the Gelfand transformation and, for a finitely generated algebra over an algebraically closed field, to the absence of nonzero nilpotents. He tests a tensor-square condition and gives $k[X]/X^p$ in positive characteristic.

**172–173 · Reversing the construction.** A hyperalgebra gives a group $G$ and a point-separating algebra $A_0$ of functions. The stated conditions include $f(xy)\in A_0\otimes A_0$ and that characters of $A_0$ come from points of $G$. In the other direction he starts from the permitted $G$-modules. Separation gives injectivity of the map to $G(\underline U)$. For surjectivity he reduces to the coefficient map $A\to A_0$.

**173–174 · Coefficients and translations.** For a unital associative algebra $\Omega$, the coefficients of finite-dimensional representations form a space $A$. Multiplication in $\Omega$ gives a coalgebra operation on $A$ and leads to a weak completion of $\Omega$. A form $f$ is a coefficient when its translates span a finite-dimensional space. The displayed expansion of $f(uv)$ explains how finitely many forms span the left translates. A marginal note identifies subcoalgebras with subspaces invariant under both translations.

**175–177 · Recovering a class from its coefficients.** A subcoalgebra $A_0$ selects finite-dimensional modules whose coefficients lie in it. Conversely one takes all coefficients of a class closed under equivalence, subobjects, quotients and direct sums. A sum of coefficients is realized on a direct sum. To recover the class, the first lemma embeds a representation into a finite direct sum using the maps indexed by a basis of $V'$. The second reduces to cyclic representations. The identity $u*f_{x,x'}=f_{ux,x'}$ supplies the final quotient, followed by « CQFD. ».

**177–178 · Products, duals and Tannaka duality.** For a group, closure under tensor product corresponds to multiplication of coefficients. Passage to the contragredient gives the condition involving the fork-shaped involution, and the trivial representation supplies the constant $1$. The structural group is the character group of $A_0$. The stated Tannaka condition is bijectivity of the map from $G$ to that group.

**178–179 · Finite generation and a linear realization.** The question about a linear algebraic group leads to finite generation of $A_0$. Finite algebra generators occur as coefficients of representations $V_i$. Their sum supplies a single $V$. In the other direction the coefficients of tensor powers are expressed in terms of those of $V$. The notes in the slanted margin distinguish the coefficient-product formula from the assertion about representations contained in quotients of tensor powers.

**179–180 · Coordinates and closedness.** A faithful representation realizes $G$ as a closed subgroup, with coordinate functions $g_{ij}$ and the inverse determinant. The argument invokes regularity of the inverse and restriction of regular functions. The diagram on 180 places the pseudo-algebraic and algebraic cases below the correspondence with hyperalgebras. The accompanying margin asks about the field hypotheses for the condition on nilpotents.

**181–182 · Indecomposable classes.** For a finite-length abelian class, $I$ indexes the indecomposables. The positive cone in the free abelian group $Z(I)$ corresponds to isomorphism classes with direct sum. Further structure gives the distinguished $e$, the dual index $\check i$, and $n(i)=\dim M_i$. The text records $\check e=e$, $n(e)=1$ and $n(\check i)=n(i)$.

**182 · Morphisms and tensor multiplication.** Specifying representatives on $k^{n(i)}$ leads to subgroups of linear maps with a composition condition. Tensor product makes the ordered group into a ring. Dimension is multiplicative, $e$ is the unit, and $i*\check i$ has a nonzero component along $e$. He calls the resulting $I$ an « hypergroupe discret ».

**183 · Characters and the torus.** For completely reducible representations the page identifies the positive cone with characters of representations. Dimension becomes evaluation at the identity. For a complex semisimple algebraic group, the algebra is described by regular functions on $T/W$, with $T$ a maximal torus and $W$ the Weyl group. Identifying its irreducible characters intrinsically remains a question.

**183–184 · Two examples.** For $G=k^*$, the indexing set is identified with $Z$ and the algebra with the group algebra of $Z$. For $G=k$, the classification goes through nilpotent operators and elementary divisors. The indecomposables are indexed by positive integers, the neutral element is $1$, and the involution is the identity. The multiplication is left open.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence, scans 182 and 184.** The additive example has the unit dimension required on 182. At $i=1$, the cyclic module in the displayed family consists of polynomial classes represented by constants, so its dimension is one. This comparison uses the exponent and the identification of the unit with index $1$. It is independent of the case used for the indeterminate.

**R2 · Model, high confidence, scans 175–176.** The direct-sum calculation accounts for the addition of coefficient functions. Evaluating the functional $(x',y')$ on $(ux,uy)$ gives $x'(ux)+y'(uy)$. This checks precisely the addition step in the construction of $A_0$.

**R3 · Model, high confidence, scan 183.** The torus paragraph distinguishes two levels of information. The algebra of regular functions is identified before Grothendieck asks for an intrinsic way to recognize the members of $I$ inside it. The transition to « il faudrait essayer » is the basis for this reading.
