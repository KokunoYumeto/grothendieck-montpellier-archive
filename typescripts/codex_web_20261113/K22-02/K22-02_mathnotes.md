# Mathematical notes · Cote 22, scans 17–30

## What the pages do

**Cohen algebras, scan 17.** Corollary 4.12 concerns a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings with r(B)=r(A)B and residue extension K/k. It puts three descriptions of B side by side: complete and formally simple over A, complete and flat with separable K/k, and a Cohen algebra over A. It adds existence over a prescribed separable residue extension and uniqueness up to A-algebra isomorphism.

**Completeness and editorial choices, scans 17–18.** The author considers replacing completeness for B’s own topology by completeness for the IB-adic topology. He identifies earlier numbers requiring changes and leaves the decision to the addressee. The next sheet proposes reordering existence, descent and uniqueness.

**A fibre criterion, scan 18.** Corollary 4.13 compares formal simplicity with flatness and universal regularity of the fibre B⊗ₐk. The hypothesis on the residue extension is finite “multiplicité radicielle”; a handwritten “ou” introduces the radical condition. The argument passes to the completion and appeals to earlier numbered results.

**Open questions about topology, scans 20 and 22–23.** The first question asks whether universal regularity implies formal simplicity. The second asks whether the topology can be changed from the usual one to the discrete one. A later sheet treats a presentation by formal power series in positive characteristic using a finite p-basis. The characteristic-zero discussion tests k[[t]] and turns to differentials.

**Open loci, scans 20–21.** Question 3 concerns the points of Spec B whose fibre local ring is formally simple over the residue field. There is a separate question about localization, an appeal to Nagata and a discussion of resolution of singularities. Question 4 asks for openness of the flat locus. Its reduction singles out a pair of primes and an open subset after forming the primed rings. Some of the qualifications are in rapidly written diagonal notes.

**A proposed obstruction, scans 23–25.** The map from differentials of the coefficient field, tensored up to the power-series ring, is tested for a left inverse. Scan 24 states a proposition for K/k with an infinite-dimensional differential module and a positive number of variables. The proposed derivation is reduced to the case Dtᵢ=0. Scan 25 expands into components indexed by a basis and proposes a series whose components would not have finite support.

**A field example, scans 27 and 29.** The square starts with k′=k∩Kᵖ. The writer compares p-independence over that intersection with p-independence after passage to K, and records a possible failure of injectivity for a map of differentials. Below the square he builds fields from x, y and coefficients aᵢ. The two defining lines give a=xᵖ and express a₀ using y and the terms with positive index. Scan 29 compares two coefficient expansions, then revises the conditions on the generators.

**Extension and norm of divisors, scan 30.** Formula (31) extends a place with its ramification multiplicities. Formula (34) uses the residue-degree factors for the norm. The typescript relates both operations to principal divisors and towers, then records the two degree identities (38) under its product-formula assumptions.

**Extension, trace and norm of repartitions, scans 30 and 28.** The last paragraph of typescript page 26 (scan 30) continues on typescript page 27 (scan 28). The local entries of an extension are specified first. Formula (40) defines trace by a sum of local traces and norm by a product of local norms. The text then identifies their restrictions to principal repartitions and the effect on idèles.

**Product topology, scan 28.** Section 6 introduces a family of topological groups and open distinguished subgroups. The last sentence starts to describe a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of the identity and breaks off at “for-”.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 17.** “Je t’en laisse donc le choix” applies to the change of completeness hypothesis. On scan 18, “peut-être plus service” introduces the proposed alternative exposition.
- **Scan 20, point 1.** “Est-il vrai que A … simple sur k si A est … régulier sur k” is followed by its proposed consequence for a local homomorphism. The short marginal answer is “OK”.
- **Scans 20 and 22, point 2.** “Est-il vrai … pour sa topologie discrète? Cas où A est complète?” On 22 the question returns with “???”. Scan 20 suggests that “la réponse soit affirmative en car p>0, négative en car 0”, then considers the relative case over Λ. The margin has “Faux, cf …”, “Je suis pessimiste”, and a qualified “Peut-être y-a-t-il de l’espoir …”.
- **Scans 20–21, point 3.** “est-il ouvert?” concerns the locus in the fibres. The next page asks whether the relevant set is “stable par localisation?” and mentions residual extensions of finite degree. A slanted passage asks for a locus to be “ouvert (ou au moins constructible)”.
- **Scan 21, point 4.** “Lieu des pts de Spec B en lesquels B est plat sur A est-il ouvert?” The writer follows with “vérifier que … ∃ un ouvert non vide de Spec B′”.
- **Scan 23.** “Je présume que si dim A>0, A n’est jamais … simple sur k pour sa topologie discrète.” The text also says “je doute qu’il y ait …” relative to the prime field and asks whether the differential map “admet un inverse à gauche?”. The abbreviation in the conjecture is uncertain.
- **Scans 27 and 29.** “peut ne être pas injectif” and “il peut arriver” introduce a proposed counterexample. The second sheet compares presentations and gives “Conditions essentielles”.

## Remarks of the model

- **R25, medium.** The condition that the individual coefficient differentials be nonzero, taken alone, permits repeating a single coefficient. All the resulting differentials can then occupy the same finite set of basis positions. The distinction matters because scan 25 asks for infinitely many nonzero components. **Low confidence** that a step was inadvertently omitted: the more specific component conditions are crossed out, and the manuscript ends with dots.
- **C30.35, high.** Using the definitions (31) and (34) on a single place P′ gives the coefficient ∑e(P)f(P) at P′. The sheet identifies this sum with n, which recovers (35) by additivity. This checks the displayed consequence conditional on the local degree sum used on the page.
- **Reading question, scan 20, relative version of point 2.** The frozen reading has “sur” followed by a cancelled group and then Λ. There appears to be a separate small **de** immediately before Λ. It may be a live word outside the cancelled group. The final text retains the frozen boundary.
- **Reading question, scan 27, start of the field example.** The frozen text gives “Voici un exemple”. The group after “un” has a long join and a tall final stroke. An abbreviated **contre-ex.** is also plausible. This word was outside the marked span before Voici.
- **Reading question, scan 29, after the cancelled explanatory sentence.** The frozen line has “p-indép. sur F”. A small oval below and to the right of F might be an index **0**, or the lead-in to the hooked addition. The field later in the same paragraph is written with a plain double-stem F.
