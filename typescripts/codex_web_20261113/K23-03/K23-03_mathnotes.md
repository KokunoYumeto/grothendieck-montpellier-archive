# Mathematical notes · cote 23, scans 51–65

## What the pages do

**51–52 · Two reference lists.** The first collects five later references in chapter IV under 11.2.9. The second brings together sections on regular sequences and regular immersions and points to two letters from Deligne. Its cancelled question asks whether the immersion notion works in the non-noetherian case.

**53–54 · Regularity for Riemann–Roch.** The postscript distinguishes regularity of an ordered sequence from the proposed condition on its exterior-algebra complex. He says that reversing two generators can destroy regularity, without specifying the ring. He wants a condition that passes to an ideal, behaves under localization and descent, and can be used for immersions. He proposes an explanatory remark and a footnote in the current proofs, leaving a change of terminology for a later edition.

**56–57 · Separation and the infinitesimal-neighbourhood objection.** Deligne uses the power series algebra $B=A[[T]]$, with $M=B$, to discuss quotients of submodules viewed over $A$. He distinguishes these from the $B$-module quotients and submodules used in the proofs. In the second PS he takes one affine line, and then an affine line with an additional disjoint point. With $J=0$ he questions the argument concerning infinitesimal neighbourhoods. He separately questions an inference about the kernel of $A\to B$ when $B$ is finitely presented over $A$.

**58–59 · Comments on EGA IV 19 and the plan of EGA.** Deligne objects both to the strength of the separation condition and to an example which only tests graded submodules. He proposes testing a kernel after tensoring with $M$. For 19.5.5 he says the implication $b)\Rightarrow c)$ needs no separation hypothesis and refers to the case $n=1$. He then comments on where the topology of the spectrum enters the EGA I draft and on a proposed account of intersections.

**60 · A compactness obstruction.** In 19.3.12 the proposed parameter set must consist of algebra homomorphisms lifting a specified map. Deligne questions the claim that this is a linear variety without a square-zero assumption. The family $(x_\infty-u)y_u=1$ over an infinite field shows what goes wrong with the compactness argument for an infinite index set.

**60–61 · Depth and projective dimension.** For a nonzero finite module of finite projective dimension over a noetherian local ring, the letter relates projective dimension to the difference of depths. A derived Hom–tensor formula leads to a Tor–Ext spectral sequence. The shaded strip supplies a lowest possible total degree and the displayed corner term accounts for the proposed equality. The next sheet discusses a formulation using Tor dimension.

**61 · Tor and completion.** The proposition compares the inverse limit of Tor for $N/m^kN$ with the completion of Tor for $N$. Deligne also formulates the comparison as an isomorphism of pro-objects. He sketches the flat case using Artin–Rees and explains why he wants a suitable flat algebra mapping onto the complete target.

**61–63 · Construction of the auxiliary local algebra.** For a local map with complete target $B$, the proposed algebra $C$ is flat over $A$. Its special fibre is regular, and the map on associated graded special fibres is to be bijective in degrees 0 and 1. The construction adjoins lifts of tangent-space generators, treats a transcendence basis and the separable part of the residue field, then writes successive relations for a purely inseparable extension. A boxed infinite sum gives the pattern for the finite-level relations (1). Flatness is reduced to finitely many of these relations and a regular-sequence test on the residue field.

**63–64 · Criteria for a regular sequence.** The letter relates the symmetric algebra of $I/I^2$ to the associated graded algebra and its exterior algebra to Tor. For minimal generators in a local ring, it adds regularity, acyclicity in the resolution sense of the written condition (iv), and an Ext-vanishing condition. The proof compares a Koszul complex with a projective resolution and uses Nakayama. For the version with coefficients in $M$, the author reduces along a regular element or uses the stated avoidance lemma for a union of ideals and primes.

**64–65 · Flatness and formal projectivity.** In the local noetherian setting with the indicated adic topologies, the proposition relates flatness of $M$ over $A$ to formal projectivity. The proof reduces to an artinian local base, passes from an Ext ind-object to a dual Tor pro-object, and applies Artin–Rees to relate the vanishing to flatness.

**65 · Corrections to individual passages.** The closing sheet discusses changes of base and formal smoothness, a semi-exact functor, three places requiring the word “local”, the sign of $dx$, an integrally closed hypothesis, and an erroneous completion formula. Deligne returns to the Tor argument on his own page 2 and states the special case for which he would like a direct argument.

## Remarks of the model

**M60.1 · Scan 60 · High confidence.** I checked the finite-intersection example directly. For a finite set of indices, there is a field element outside that set; for the full family the index equal to $x_\infty$ gives an impossible equation. This checks the example over the stated infinite field.

**M60.2 · Scan 60 · High confidence.** I checked the lower boundary in the drawing against the total degree in the displayed spectral sequence. On the strip $-d\le p\le 0$ and $q\ge a$, the minimum of $p+q$ is $a-d$, at the single corner $(-d,a)$. This check concerns the support calculation.

**M61.1 · Scans 61 and 65 · High confidence.** The “pg 2” revisited in the last paragraph can be identified within the packet: authorial page 2 contains precisely the comparison between the Tor of quotients and the completed Tor module. This identification links Deligne's admission of difficulty to the earlier argument.

**RQ56 · Reading question after the freeze · Scan 56, opening paragraph · Low confidence.** The frozen reading is “réjète”; “répète” is also possible. The medial descender is thin and its return stroke does not decide between j and an open p. The surrounding counterexample still voices the objection. No conclusion about withdrawing the objection should rest on this verb. The transcription retains the frozen reading at this unmarked place.
