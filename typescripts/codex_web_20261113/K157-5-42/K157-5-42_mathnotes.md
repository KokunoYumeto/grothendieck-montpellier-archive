## What the pages do

**U32 · Proposition 3.2 · scan 648.** Starting from a localizer $W$ associated with a cofibration structure, Grothendieck lists derivator axioms on three domains of ordered sets. He starts with finite ordered sets, enlarges the domain by a noetherian condition on the associated space, and finally assumes transfinite Cof 4. The annotation about subdivision points back to K.S. Brown.

**U33 · Corollary 3.3 · scans 648–649.** Functoriality of the factorization $f=pi$ lets him apply 3.2 to the diagram category $M(I)$. He singles out functors of the form $\mathrm{id}_I\times v$. A handwritten question mark follows the list Der 1, 2, 3.

**U34 · Extension to diagrams · scans 649–650.** He distinguishes the objectwise class $C(I)$ from the extended class $C_I$. He recalls the hypotheses for the cofibration structure on diagrams over an ordered set and sets out the intersection formula (3.4.1) and the pushforward inclusion (3.4.2). He wants versions for arbitrary diagram categories.

**U35 · Proposition 3.5 and the proposed definition · scans 650–651.** For a left $Q$-closed class $\Phi=\Psi^*$, he expresses Anderson’s extension using the functor $v$ from the discrete category of objects. Formula (3.5.2) supplies a definition for arbitrary $I$. For a class assumed only transfinitely $Q$-saturated, he proposes a different construction in (3.5.3) and explicitly leaves its agreement with Anderson open.

**U36 · Question 3.6 · scans 651–652.** The desired equality is $C\langle I\rangle\cap W(I)=(TC)\langle I\rangle$. One inclusion is identified in a margin. He recalls hypotheses involving the two factorization pairs of a closed Quillen structure, then asks whether factorization for the pair involving $TC\langle I\rangle$ suffices.

**U37 · Proposition 3.7 · scan 653.** Under that last factorization hypothesis he factors $f=pi$, obtains a lift $q$, and exhibits $f$ as a retract of $i$. Closure under retracts gives the required membership. The triangular diagram supplies the three objects and the return map used in the argument.

**U38 · Corollary 3.8 · scans 653–654.** A $Q$-speciality assumption on $TC$ gives the intersection equality for every $I$. Grothendieck then distinguishes obtaining this equality from establishing all the factorizations he wants to construct $u^W_!$. His method for the latter still uses speciality of $C$.

**U39 · Search for examples · scans 654–655.** He asks for a closed Quillen structure for which a derived left or right adjoint sometimes fails to exist. He suggests a terminal target and a one-object group category $B_G$. He also asks how much accessibility and existence of small colimits one could retain in an example.

**U310 · Example 3.10 · scans 655–656.** With isomorphisms as $W$ and all arrows as $C$ and $F$, the question passes to ordinary adjoints of restriction. The margin adds the finite-limit and finite-colimit qualification. For terminal target he states the condition in terms of limits of the indicated diagram shape.

**U311 · Example 3.11 · scans 656–658.** He chooses bounded cochain complexes over $k$ with quasi-isomorphisms. For $I=B_G$ and $u:I\to e$, he formulates existence of the adjoints as boundedness of derived group functors. This becomes eventual vanishing of homology and cohomology for each module. He proposes the group of order two over $\mathbb F_2$, then remarks that his $M$ lacks arbitrary small colimits.

**U312 · Example 3.12 · scans 658–659.** Starting with a localization that has a left adjoint, he identifies the localized diagram category with diagrams in the localization. He tries to arrange for small limits or colimits available in the original category to fail in the localization. He observes that the inverse limits exist there and identifies conditions that force small colimits as well.

**U313 · Question 3.13 · scan 659.** He asks for a category relative to a universe that has all small colimits while some small limits fail to exist. The NB gives restrictions on such a category.

**U314 · Question 3.14 · scans 660–661.** He questions Anderson’s argument for existence of $u^W_!$ under transfinite Cof 4. He distinguishes the absence of an example known to him from his expectation that examples exist, and records how extra accessibility assumptions affect that expectation.

**U315 · Question 3.15 and the end of §3 · scans 661–662.** He asks when a suitably saturated accessible class of arrows has a small left $Q$-generating part. He also records a more basic gap in his examples of pairs $\Phi\leftrightarrow\Psi$. A systematic study of factorization would, he says, take weeks.

**U41 · Pseudo-toposes · scan 662.** Under the heading « Paratopos et L’opération $M\boxtimes N$ », he defines a pseudo-topos by small colimits and a small strictly generating subcategory. A parenthesis specifies generation by strict epimorphisms. He asserts existence of small limits and points to groups as an obstruction to the same property for the opposite category. The qualification about Theorem 2.6 continues beyond the last image.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · High confidence · scan 653.** In the arrow category, the two squares for the asserted retract have components $(1_X,q)$ and $(1_X,p)$. The manuscript gives all three identities needed to check their types, commutativity and composite. The choice of name for the dashed arrow does not affect this check.

**R02 · High confidence · scans 655–656.** For the ordinary example, a diagram map whose components are isomorphisms has a natural inverse given by their inverses. The identity functor can thus serve as the localization at $W(I)$, accounting for the equality in (3.10.3).

**R03 · High confidence · scan 657.** The proposed $G$ of order two over $\mathbb F_2$ already fails the vanishing test on the trivial module. A calculation on the four elements of $\mathbb F_2[G]$ gives equal image and kernel for multiplication by $1+g$, with that kernel also equal to the augmentation kernel. On the trivial module the induced maps vanish. This confirms nonzero homology and cohomology in every positive degree for this particular example. The general bounded Quillen-structure question in Q05 remains open on the page.

**R04 · High confidence · scans 651–654.** Equations (3.5.5), (3.6.1) and (3.8.1) put the same two classes on the sides of the equality. The first two put a question mark above it. The last one has the hypothesis on $TC$ in its corollary. This identifies exactly which instance of the earlier question the corollary addresses.

**R05 · High confidence · scan 658.** The lower shaft takes $M$ to the script category and is labelled $v^*$. The expression $(v^*)^{-1}(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathscr H))$ therefore has the indicated type, a class of arrows in $M$. This check uses only the direction and label of the shaft and does not decide the style of the capital H.
