## What the pages do

**003 · Forgetting labels and a distinguished line.** A branching diagram starts with configurations indexed by $I$. The map $\alpha$ forgets the labelling except for $\Delta_{i_0}=\Delta$. Its two successors forget the distinction on $\Delta$, or erase $\Delta$ itself. The first fibre is described by $\Sigma/G_{X,\Sigma}$, the second by relative positions. The examples for $n=5$ concern these different fibres.

**004–005 · A disk with a labelled boundary.** Cutting along the distinguished pseudo-line gives a disk with a stable system of chords. The boundary vertices form a two-sheeted polygon over $I_0(\xi)$. Given a polygon $S$ of order $2n$ and its antipodism $a_S$, the notes set $I_0=S/a_S$. They identify the fibre over a configuration with fixed polygonal structure $\pi_0$ by a triangle of polygon isomorphisms over $I_0$. There are two lifts, interchanged by antipodism.

**006–007 · Forgetful maps and orbit sizes.** The inclusion of the polygon automorphisms in the permutation group relates the fibres of $\alpha^!$ and $\alpha$. The factor $n!/(2n)$ counts the possible polygonal structures on the labelling set. Writing $N$ for the order of the automorphism group of the distinguished arrangement gives $2n/N$ for the intermediate fibre. The two-sheeted map gives $4n/N$ for the composite.

**007–008 · Small cases and résumé.** For $n=3$, the sheet gives an automorphism group of order six and an orbit of order two. For $n=4$, it gives an automorphism group of order two and a set of order eight. The $n=5$ entry ends after the cardinality six. The résumé distinguishes the quotient by the full group $D_S$ from the quotient by the antipodism subgroup and asks about the stabilizer identification.

**010 · Polygon counts.** A large circular drawing carries labels for lines, intersections and regions. Two smaller hexagons single out the central crossings. A triangle, a quadrilateral and a pentagon stand beside three counts ending in 7, 24 and 6.

**011 · A universal property and base change.** For a steinienne arrow over $Z\to X$, the page asserts the existence of a unique $X$-morphism $X'\to\widetilde Z$ and restates this using an initial object. A separate question asks which squares remain Cartesian when a Cartesian square of the underlying maps is factored through the two intermediate spaces.

**012 · Spatial drawings and arithmetic.** A sphere with marked crossings, a branching tree, a pointed solid, two cubic frameworks and a darker faceted drawing share the sheet with factorial and product calculations. The positions of the equations are clear. No sentence assigns every factor to a particular edge or branch.

**013–015 · Three line-arrangement studies.** Scan 013 numbers the regions around a central pentagon. Scan 014 contains two perspective sketches and a labelled line arrangement beside inequalities in $a,a',b,b'$. Scan 015 labels five curves $D_0,\ldots,D_4$, with hatched sectors on either side of their crossings and several smaller studies on the right.

**017 · Sign data of a real hyperplane family (D3).** Each hyperplane gives three parts, the hyperplane itself and its two open half-spaces. The point’s position in all these partitions defines a map $\varphi$ from the space of directions into their product $E$. The nonempty fibres are the cells indexed by $\Sigma=\varphi(S)$. The text records the antipodal operation on fibres.

**018 · The order on the coordinates (D3 continued).** The hyperplane has value zero. The two other values lie above zero and are incomparable. The coordinatewise order restricts to $\Sigma$, whose lower topology has the downward-closed subsets as closed sets. The sheet expresses the closure of a cell as the union of the cells below it and introduces $Z_A^*$ for a union of cells.

**019 · Unions, strata and bounds.** The first display extends the closure formula to subsets of $\Sigma$. The next paragraph describes a finite stratification of the sphere. A crossed paragraph considers common lower bounds and intersections of closed facets. The live paragraph at the foot asserts upward closure of $\Sigma$ in $E$ and makes assertions about suprema and infima.

**021 · Five configurations.** Four numbered drawings lead into a proposition about one-dimensional arrangements and their realizations in dimension two. For the configurations called “non spéciales”, the text lists three possibilities for the intersection of two closed faces. The final note introduces a fifth arrangement of seven pseudo-lines, described by a quadrilateral and its three diagonals.
