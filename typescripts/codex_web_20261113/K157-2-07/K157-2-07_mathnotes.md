## What the pages do

**U1 · Fibrewise equivalence and base change (99–100).** Scan 99 starts mid-argument. The inclusions from a fibre into the two slice constructions give the implications drawn between a, b, c and c′. The converse a) ⇒ b) becomes L5. A degree-$n$ covering of the circle illustrates the obstruction for a localizer associated with modules. L6 asks for preservation of a $W$-equivalence after base change along a perfect morphism. L6 bis replaces that hypothesis by a Hot-fibration.

**U2 · Components and local equivalence (100–101).** L7 treats sums of maps and maps in $W$ with connected target. The lemma rewrites the condition in terms of a bijection on $\pi_0$ and a $W$-equivalence on each corresponding component. L4 bis passes from a map that is locally a $W$-equivalence over $S$ to membership in $W$. Scan 101 collects saturation, the inclusion of $W_\infty$, the local condition and the component criterion as requirements on a fundamental localizer. Proposition 2 states invariance under passage to opposite categories.

**U3 · Which equivalence to use (102–103).** The local and colocal equivalences over $S$ are called fine left and fine right. On the six “internal” categories the distinctions collapse under the conditions being discussed, with L5 stated on 103. On the left edge, the relevant fine equivalence is the right one, tested using ${}_{s\backslash}X$. On the right edge it is the left one, tested using $X_{/s}$. On the whole of $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$ he distinguishes the coarse equivalence and both fine equivalences.

**U4 · Three localized categories (104–105).** He assigns the coarse localization the name $\mathrm{HOT}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W(S)$, with “lc” glossed by “localement constants”. For the fine right and fine left localizations he writes $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S)$ and $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S^o)^o$. To specify individual categories he chooses split fibrations, split cofibrations and, in the locally constant case, perfect objects. The powers on the two central lines of 105 are retouched.

**U5 · Comparison functors (106–107).** Passage $X\mapsto X^o$ relates the perfect objects over the two bases. The dotted inclusions from the indexed split categories suggest maps $i,j$ out of the locally constant category. He proposes choosing the necessary quasi-inverses using the restrictions $\Phi_0,\Psi_0$ on $\mathrm{Parf}\,S$. The next leaf asks whether $i,j$ are fully faithful.

**U6 · The obstruction for split structures (107–109).** For coarse equivalence he first tries reverse functors for $r,p,q$. The identity comparison is available on the $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$ side. For the proposed $\Phi',\Psi'$ the comparison on the split side is the difficulty. Allowing non-Cartesian or non-co-Cartesian maps gives the arrows at the top of 108. The square on that sheet then compares split and underlying fibrations, with and without the subscript 0. A diagonal annotation suggests comparing directly with $\mathrm{Fibhot}\,S$. On 109 the functorial map $X\to\Phi(X)$ gives two comparisons with the identity. The map still sought goes from $\varphi p$ to the identity.

**U7 · The retraction and the proposed adjunction (110–111).** For a fibrational $X/S$, a pair $(x,u)$ in the left slice is sent to $u_X^*(x)$ in $X_s$. He writes the resulting map $\Phi(X)\to X$ as a retraction of $i_X$. Its name is transcribed $\sigma$, with alternative glyphs in the doubts file. After choosing inverse-image functors he considers obtaining the two natural arrows instead from a Hom formula for $\Phi$ and the forgetful $\pi$.

**U8 · Reducing the verification (111–112).** A revised count gives five cases, three up to symmetry. Three diagrams place each pair of functors beside its two comparisons of composites with identities. The author says the indexed comparisons are restrictions of the unindexed ones. After using symmetry he retains $(r,C)$ and $(\lambda,\Phi)$.

**U9 · Fine right equivalence (113–114).** For the four categories $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$, $\mathrm{Liss}\,S$, $\mathrm{Fib}\,S$ and $\mathrm{Fibsc}\,S$, he again takes $\Phi$ in the reverse direction. The first test identifies $f\in W_S^d$ with $\Phi(f)\in W_S^f$, using the fibre at $s$. The second asks whether $X\to\lambda\Phi(X)$ is fine right. He narrows the proposed base-change argument to local co-isomorphisms and ends with the isomorphism of the indicated left slices. The third of the announced tests is beyond these leaves.
