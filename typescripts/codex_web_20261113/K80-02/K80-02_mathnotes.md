# K80-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Autobiographical writing, scans 22, 24, 26, 30.** Two sheets each survive in two exposures. On his page 3 the writer weighs a chronological account against reading his correspondence and considers which language to use. His page 2 proposes starting with a period for which he has substantial material. He mentions the correspondence with Hanka since 1942.

**C1 · Classification, scan 23.** The first division is whether a bigonal pseudo-line exists. The list describes a pencil with a transversal, two pencils with the line through their centres, two pencils without that additional line, and simple arrangements on four or five pseudo-lines. Small sketches and marginal counts accompany the cases.

**C2 · Exceptional arrangements, scan 25.** A theorem singles out the six-line tetrahedral arrangement and the seven-line arrangement of a quadrilateral with three diagonals. The NB discusses arrangements without bigons and connects three listed types with a condition on intersections of closed facets.

**C3 · Closed facets and adjacency, scan 27.** The first proposition gives a criterion for the lattice condition. The next adds a pseudo-line D, names its intersections with the old lines, and relates adjacency of two such vertices to a closed face containing them. The diagram identifies s, s′ and T. A third proposal at the foot is crossed out.

**C4 · Ordering the lines, scan 29.** A lemma orders D₁ through D_N and names the successive arrangements K_i. It specifies a four-line start, with a six-line case in parentheses, and a condition on later D_i. A brace groups three cases; the note calls them mutually exclusive.

**Administrative notice, scan 28.** The reused sheet announces a doctorate in plant nutrition. Its abstract gives the thesis length and summarises a proposed nitrate-transport model. The notice has no pseudo-line construction.

**C5 · Cube, octahedron and quotient, scans 32–33.** A family of three two-element sets Φ_i encodes the six cube faces in opposite pairs. Partial sections over one, two or three indices encode faces, edges and vertices. Reversing incidence yields the octahedron. The simultaneous interchange within each pair gives the antipodal involution; its quotient octahedron is related to three projective lines.

**C6 · Edge sets and orientations, scans 34–36.** Diagram (5) relates oriented cubes and octahedra to their “gauche” counterparts. Formula (6) combines the three Φ_i with the orientation set of I. The succeeding formulas introduce the two vertices S_i, two edges A_i and orientations Ω_i of a bigon; they relate their wedge products and the orientation of a three-element index set.

**C7 · Recovering data from the graph, scans 36–38.** The proposition starts with a combinatorial map with three vertices, six edges and four triangular faces. From its 1-skeleton and three distinguished pseudo-lines, it recovers the two-element sets and the isomorphisms in (15). Formula (15 bis) and the source map give a trivialisation of a two-element torsor. The functor in (16) incorporates that trivialisation. Diagram (17) arranges the octahedra and their graphs, with and without orientations.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High.** Counting the partial sections in (2) gives 6, 12 and 8 for domains of size one, two and three. Pairing every section with its simultaneous opposite gives orbit counts 3, 6 and 4. This independently checks the quotient-octahedron counts written on 33.
- **R2 · High for the counts only.** Distinct pair intersections in the simple four- and five-line cases give 6 and 10. For two pencils with distinct centres and no extra coincidences, the cross-intersections contribute pq further vertices. This checks the marginal counting pattern on 23 under its incidence assumptions.
- **R3 · High for the elementary check.** Under (7), changing the label on either of the two factors defining A_i flips that edge label. Across the three A_i each original label occurs twice. All eight possible simultaneous relabellings preserve the total parity, in agreement with the trivialisation in (10). This tests that particular identity.
- **R4 · High for the written arithmetic.** The marginal additions on 25 give 4+3=7 and 6+3=9. These sums alone do not establish the theorem classifying the arrangements.
- **R5 · Low, reading question.** On scan 27, above “2-dim”, the frozen reading is a question mark. A loose closing stroke associated with the preceding II₄ is also possible. It stands midway between the two lines and has no definite dot. No mathematical conclusion here relies on it.
