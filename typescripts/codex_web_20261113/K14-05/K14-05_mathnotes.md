# Cote 14 · mathematical notes on scans 59–71 and 73

## What the pages do

**U1 · Characterization and construction of $u$, scans 59–60.** A square relates $u^{(1)}$ on $H^1(A_Y)$ to the action of a cycle on $H^1(Y)$. The proof seeks a homomorphism $u:B_X\to A_Y$ satisfying $u(\operatorname{cl}(D))=S_Y({}^tZ(D))$ where the latter is defined. The author constructs a correspondence $C$ on $Y\times B_X$ by intersecting with the canonical class $\Theta$ and pushing forward along $\pi$. The evaluation at $(b)-(0)$ gives the required value of $u(b)$.

**U2 · Cohomological comparison and variant, scan 61.** The author invokes 1.6 for compatibility with cohomology and 1.1 for the consequence of numerical equivalence to zero. The variant uses the two divisor correspondences $\Theta_X,\Theta_Y$ and forms $\Theta_Y\circ Z\circ{}^t\Theta_X$ on $B_X\times B_Y$. The following corollary, asserting that a homomorphism comes from a cycle, is crossed out in its entirety.

**U3 · Transposition, scan 62.** The opening corollary compares the transpose of $u_Z$ with the homomorphism of the transposed correspondence, and evaluates it on a divisor on $Y$. Proposition 2.3 gives a related formula for divisors on $X$. Both formulas have marginal cautions about signs. A homomorphism of Picard schemes is suggested as another route to 1.1.

**U4 · Proposition 2.4, scans 62–64.** A cohomological isomorphism from degree 1 on $Y$ to degree $2n-1$ on $X$, with twist $n-1$, is related to $u_Z$ being an isogeny. The inverse is represented by a divisor with rational coefficients and corresponds to $u_Z^{-1}$. The text asks when the divisor can have integral coefficients, then discusses denominators using the order of the kernel as a group scheme. Several proposed wordings of the denominator restriction are superimposed on scan 63.

**U5 · Corollary 2.5, scans 64–65.** The integral-coefficient version compares the condition that the isogeny's kernel order $N$ be prime to $l$ with bijectivity after passage to the target modulo torsion. An insertion treats the extra condition that the cohomology group itself be torsion-free.

**U6 · Corollary 2.6, scan 65.** For a smooth connected projective $X$ of dimension $n\geq2$, the author takes the classes of $n-1$ ample bundles. Their product $\eta$ acts by cup product from $H^1(X,Q_l)$ to $H^{2n-1}(X,Q_l)(n-1)$. He asserts that the inverse $L$ is represented by a rational divisor correspondence, whose class is unique and independent of $l$.

**U7 · Polarization and restriction, scans 65–66.** The proof turns to $u:B_X\to A_X$ and strengthens the finite-kernel assertion to one about a polarization of $B_X$ using $A_X\simeq B_X^*$. A cancelled approach passes through successively cut varieties and a section $Y$. It labels the restriction and injection maps and places $\lambda$ between $B_Y$ and $A_Y$.

**U8 · Correspondence carried by a curve, scans 66–68.** For $v:C\to X\times Y$, the maps to $X$ and $Y$ give pullback to $B_C$, duality on the curve, and pushforward from $A_C$. When $X=Y$ and the components agree, the text lists conditions involving a polarization, an isogeny, injectivity and surjectivity. It reduces the argument to surjectivity of $A_C\to A_X$ for a curve cut out by very ample divisors.

**U9 · Proposition 2.7, scans 68–70.** Starting in the other direction with a divisor $D$, the author associates $u_D:A_Y\to B_X$. For an isogeny, a rational one-cycle $Z$ is to induce the inverse on cohomology and $u_Z$ corresponds to $u_D^{-1}$. The square on 69 reduces the construction to algebraicity of the inverses of two comparison maps. The polarized square on 70 expresses these inverses using $\lambda$, $\mu$, $\varphi$ and $\psi$.

**U10 · Integral inverse questions, scan 71.** The first question asks for an integral cycle realizing the inverse of $H^1(A_X)\to H^1(X)$. The second asks the corresponding question for $H^{2n-1}(X)(n-1)\to H^1(B_X)$. The proposed steps involve a rational point on a generic fibre and a reduction to abelian varieties.

**U11 · Torsion and duality, scan 73.** The separate sheet begins with $H^1(X,\mu_N)$ and $N$-torsion in the Picard group. A sequence separates the connected Picard part and Néron–Severi torsion. The author passes to a Tate module and introduces $B_X$ from the Picard scheme and $A_X=B_X^*$. Under smoothness and pure dimension $n$, duality gives a sequence in degree $2n-1$, followed by a limit over powers of $l$. The last sequence identifies the $l$-primary component and labels an algebraic homomorphism. Several Tate twists have been crossed out or rewritten.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scan 70 · High confidence about the calculation.** Reading the square from $H^1(A_X)$ to $H^1(B_X)$ gives $\mu=\psi_X\lambda\varphi_X$. With that identification, the sources and targets match in both displayed inverse formulas, and cancelling the adjacent inverse factors reproduces their written orders. This checks the compositions conditional on invertibility.
- **R2 · Scan 65 · High confidence about the degree count.** The product line runs from the first divisor class to the $(n-1)$st. Counting two degrees for each of those classes gives $1+2(n-1)=2n-1$, the degree in the displayed target.
- **R3 · Scans 62–65 and 71 · High confidence about the distinction in wording.** The rational divisor result explicitly allows denominators and then discusses an integral representative under an isomorphism hypothesis. The later questions ask for an integral cycle inducing each particular inverse. The word « entier » is independently underlined in both questions.
- **R4 · Scan 73 · Reading question, low confidence.** In the finite sequence the frozen reading of the cancelled twist after the dual of the Néron–Severi term is $(−n)$, with $(+n)$ as an alternative. The horizontal arrow cuts through the sign. This term is too heavily worked over to infer a change of twist convention from it.
