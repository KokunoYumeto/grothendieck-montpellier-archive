# Hand and notation, cote 60, scans 25–35

Observations are local to the indicated line or group of lines. A habit supplies a comparison for the next leaf and still requires a check on that leaf.

## Scan 25

The top bar of the capital E can detach and resemble a superscript. Its three strokes are especially clear in the two L subscripts. The f extends below the baseline, the compactification bar is a separate horizontal. The double upright on R is visible in the opening equality but varies within the calculation. The thin downstroke under the first argument points to an empty pair of parentheses.

## Scan 26

On the typed face the narrow uppercase I has both serifs. The isolated pen stroke through the initial of « la suite » rises above the type. The three accents on the capitalised titles have continuous dark oblique strokes against broken type. The hand responsible for these accents cannot be identified.

## Scan 27

The crossed χ of the top line stands beside a much smaller rounded epsilon. They recur together in this leaf. The short pair of strokes after « De » is an equals sign. In the central brace several heavier letter shapes contain parts of a previous character. The parenthesis below the box has a cancellation of its own. The diagonal from the middle bottom box leads to the note below, away from the exponent.

## Scan 28

Read the typed face after a half turn. The lower-case o sorts in the corollary have x-height, while the adjacent zero at the end of the sequence reaches cap height. The second d in « additivement » has a faint broken bowl. The printed long ell is identical in both class formulas. The underlines below F, T and p belong to the letters themselves.

## Scan 29

The rising tick on delta in the penultimate product clears the following E. The sign in the definition has a short upper zigzag and a long descending curve. Its compressed repetitions at the foot do not give a separate identification. The cancelled word on line 3 has a cross-stroke lasting its full length.

## Scan 30

The low bar below an ideal letter is often pale. It persists below the descending stroke of small p. Uppercase B sometimes loses much of its left upright in this typescript. The accents on the two instances of où in the last paragraph curve sideways above a regular typed u. Attribution of the added accent strokes is open.

## Scan 31

The initial on the boxed line has the same narrow bends as the sign in scan 29. In the definition below it, the large, heavily inked two-lobed letter with a prime is repeated in a much thinner hand. The ink after A in the box is visibly higher than the baseline. The lower strokes of the cancelled letter before X do not give its top bar clearly.

## Scan 32

The three accents above the u of où on this typescript have the curved stroke also seen on scan 30. The first p below E in part e has visibly blank paper below it, while the following p below A has a pale underline. The serifed capital Lambda in d and 23 has horizontal feet at both sides. The three subparts use Greek letters on the baseline.

## Scan 33

Two rising leaders in the first description locate courbe after X and the bracketed word after projective. The letter for the generic point has a pronounced descending right stem, close to y or eta, in both text and subscripts. The tiny dual mark above E is open upward. After the colon in the map to X the horizontal stroke ends bluntly.

## Scan 34

The single vertical type on the K after “pour tout” in 12a is capital-height with two diagonal arms. The exponents in the same sentence are all lower-case k. The crossed Greek chi differs from the upright X in the polynomial ring. The baseline underlining of ideal letters stops before their subscripts. The two accent strokes over u repeat the curved marks on scans 30 and 32.

## Scan 35

The narrow double curve in the boxed function name is retraced on the left but thin on the right. Horizontal links in the sequences vary. Before Q both arrowhead strokes are clear. The lower outline attached to Q encloses the coproduct and crosses the text below its index. In the bottom corner a sweeping arc marks off two cohomology groups. Several digits, primes and strokes of the last twist are written over one another.

## Comparisons across the sheets

The labels ξ/ζ on 29, 31 and 35 share an elongated lower curve. The number of small upper turns is variable, so the name stays open where first used. The small y/η in 33 and 35 has the same long right descender in full-size text and in the subscript. Both readings are recorded at the first occurrence on each sheet. On 25, 27, 29 and 31 the weight ρ has a long right descender unlike the shallow first hump of n. On 33 and 35 the first exponent summand is 1. The stars on L, χ and δ on 33 and 35 are raised even when the subscript is immediately adjacent.

The typed exterior-power character on 32 and 34 has two baseline feet. At one subscript height the typed o on 28 and 34 is shorter than an adjacent keyed 1. The accents over the u of où on 30, 32 and 34 are small, separately applied curls. They are recorded individually. The C and E of the mixed headings on 26 and capitals in the roman operators on the other typed sheets show no offset second upright. The handwritten field letter on 33 does.
