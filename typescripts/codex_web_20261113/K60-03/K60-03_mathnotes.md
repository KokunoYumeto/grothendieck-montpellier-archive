# Mathematical notes on cote 60, scans 25–35

## What the pages do

**Boundary duality (25).** The manuscript compares the ordinary $L_E$ with a function at infinity. A chain marked “dualité locale” rewrites the dual of the boundary direct image. The first box ends in $[1]$, with a marginal query about its sign. The weight and dimension hypotheses lead to a second box involving the twist $n+\rho$, followed by a reciprocal product formula for $L_E^\infty$.

**Injective modules (26).** The typescript defines injectivity by exactness after applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-,I)$ and gives an equivalent extension property for maps along an injection $F\to G$. With $D(F)=\operatorname{Hom}(F,I)$ it explains one implication. The converse begins by arranging three short rows around the middle objects $S,E,T$.

**The constants (27).** Equality of the two Euler characteristics gives $\chi_\infty(E)=0$. The sheet expresses $\delta_\infty$ as a ratio, rewrites it as $\varepsilon(E)^{-2}$ and specializes the reciprocal product. Three lower boxes relate $\delta,\delta',\varepsilon,\delta_\infty$ to $q^{\chi(E)}$. The middle box raises the algebraic question S27 below.

**Lengths and torsion classes (28).** Proposition 10 gives additivity of $\chi$ in a short exact sequence, invariance under a pseudo-isomorphism, and its vanishing criterion. The proof uses the local lengths $\ell_{\underline p}$. The corollary gives an alternating sum for a longer exact sequence. Proposition 11 introduces an additive map on classes of torsion modules and stops on its first hypothesis.

**Reciprocity and normalization (29, 31).** With $q=p^{n+\rho}$, scan 29 applies $t\mapsto1/(qt)$ twice to $L(1/(qt))=B(t)L(t)$. It expands the resulting product and absorbs $t^{\chi(E)/2}$ into the function denoted by a sign resembling $\xi$. Scan 31 boxes the transformed equation, defines $A(t)$ and $\varepsilon'(E)$ and gives their values for complete $X$. The small exponent on $A$ in the box remains open.

**Degrees of ideals (30).** In a finite extension of fraction fields the exercise follows height-one primes through intermediate rings. It defines the degree of a prime by the extension of fraction fields of the quotient domains, extends the degree to primary components, and adds their contributions. In an intermediate ring $C$, pseudo-invertibility is tested by comparing that degree with the coefficients of $\operatorname{div}(\underline a B)$. The hint reduces to a discrete valuation ring and compares lengths.

**Decomposition and flatness (32).** Parts c–e connect a principal ideal with a cyclic direct summand, compare two families of determinantal or annihilator ideals with a cyclic decomposition, and give a freeness test using a residue module and a localization. Exercise 23 uses the annihilator of a highest exterior power to formulate a flatness assertion for quotients, then applies it modulo the radical.

**A curve and its generic point (33).** The heading is “Cas d'une courbe alg.” The writer starts with a constructible sheaf on the generic point, written $y$ or $\eta$, takes $E=i_*E_y$, and records $D(E)=i_*(\check E_y)(1)$. The starred $L$-function and the starred invariants $\chi^*,\delta^*$ are defined using this direct image. Under $\check E_y=E_y(\rho)$ the lower box gives a reciprocal equation with $q=p^{1+\rho}$.

**Determinantal divisors (34).** The opening polynomial-ring example asks for four determinantal ideals. Exercise 11 defines the divisor of one lattice relative to another, forms its successive differences, and extends invariant factors to a lattice in a subspace by intersecting with that subspace. Exercise 12 applies exterior powers to an inclusion of equal-rank torsion-free modules and relates the classes of the resulting cokernels by a binomial coefficient.

**The local correction (35).** The starred normalization produces another reciprocal equation. The writer then objects that the starred expression is not additive in $E$. The second sequence has an extra term $Q$. The definition involving a kernel of two first higher direct images is struck through. Its replacement is indexed by ramified points. The final formula includes $L_Q(t)^{-1}$ and writes the correction as a product of $P_{Q_x}(t)$. A separate marginal duality formula relates $H^2$ and $H^0$; its twists are uncertain.

## Remarks of the model

**M27. Model, high confidence.** As a check on S27, take $q=4,\chi=1,\delta=1,\delta'=4$. The product box holds, while the two expressions in the middle box are $1/2$ and $2$. This is a numerical test of the implication between the written equations. Realizing the chosen values by a sheaf is a separate question.

**M29. Model, high confidence.** The reciprocal substitution in scan 29 has order two. For example, with $q=4$, $t=3/2$ goes to $1/6$ and then back to $3/2$. Taking $L(t)=1+t$ gives reciprocal multipliers $7/15$ and $15/7$, whose product is $1$.

**M35. Model, high confidence for the displayed factor.** In the last line the quotient of $L^*_{E_y}$ by the product of the primed and double-primed factors is precisely the product indexed by $x$. Thus the writer's objection can be located in a specific part of the formula even though several words describing the support of $Q$ are difficult to read.

The short horizontal has an upward lump and no separately visible lower arm (033-q.jpg). The corresponding definition on scan 35 has a distinct crossing (035-defqsign.jpg).
