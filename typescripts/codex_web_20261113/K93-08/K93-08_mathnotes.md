# Mathematical notes

The scan numbers below refer to the supplied images. Formula labels such as S73-square also identify checks in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv. The quoted French is the manuscript or typescript wording. Plain mathematical notation in the accompanying English is used to discuss it.

## What the pages do

**Scans 61, 63, 65 and 67. University research.** Four reused typescript faces form a separate report. In printed order, pages 5, 6, probably 7, and 8 ask what research missions teaching establishments should have, enumerate four objectives, estimate resources attributable to research, and give annual operating and equipment figures for 1971–1976. The pen corrects punctuation and a figure in the 1976 total.

**Scan 62. Local systems and Euler characteristics.** A lemma connects an alternating sum of homology ranks for a local system of rank \(b\) with \(b\chi(Y)\). The opening refers to available covers of \(Y\), their intersections and a condition whose symbol is hard to read. The next paragraph returns to this hypothesis.

**Scans 62 and 64. Locally conical spaces.** A recursive definition starts with the empty space and describes conical neighbourhoods. A margin gives low-dimensional examples and a reservation about triangulability. The argument distinguishes compact spaces from locally compact spaces completed by a point. Removing the interior of a smaller cone leads on scan 64 to the boxed \(\chi(X)-\chi_!(X)=\chi(S_\omega)\). The arrow from \(S_\omega\) identifies the contribution from the local structure at infinity.

**Scan 66. Axioms and range of validity.** The theorem lists properties of ordinary and compactly supported Euler characteristics, including agreement on compact spaces, a closed-subspace formula, an inclusion–exclusion expression, two different invariance conditions, normalization at a point, product formulas, a fibration formula and a compactification formula. The marginal reservations qualify the last part of the discussion. The sheet ends with a cellular alternating sum called « formule d'Euler ».

**Scan 68. Topic list.** Orientation(s), embedding(s) and homology with \(\chi\) precede three items joined by a brace. These concern coverings, the fundamental group, Galois theory and complex-variable functions. « Conj. de Weil » stands below the brace.

**Scans 69 and 70. Names and address.** A sheet of signatures and a separate two-line address intervene. They contain no mathematical statement. The right-hand handwritten heading on the names sheet is difficult to decipher.

**Scan 72. A differential invariant in two local coordinates.** Part I seeks a quadratic differential \(G(f,g)\). The conditions address dependence on jets, addition over three coordinates, invariance under three transformations of \(g\), and agreement of coordinates from initial data. A marginal condition requires an epimorphism. Another marginal line introduces projective coordinates modulo a group \(H\) and a torsor. Part II varies the curve with étale maps and reduces the ambiguity from a unit on \(X\) to a nonzero constant.

**Scan 73, left column. Explicit formula and torsor.** The author presents the coefficients of a rational jet expression as constants, writes \(G\) as a difference of two third-derivative expressions, and gives a map \(c^X\) from projective coordinates to a torsor. Its displayed difference is \(c^X(g)-c^X(f)\). The quotient map is said to be an isomorphism.

**Scan 73, part III. Varying the base.** The construction is required to respect change of base and étale maps. The formula uses divided derivatives \(D_t^{(i)}\). The notes choose a normalization over the integers, describe the two signs and evaluate the normalized expression at \((t,t^2)\) and \((t,t^3)\). The distinction between characteristics 2 and 3 motivates the change of normalization.

**Scans 73–74, remarks 1 and 2. The recipient and the torsor.** The author asks about replacing quadratic differentials by a sheaf \(\mathcal F\), and describes a factorization \(G'=uG\). The argument then compares the torsors for \(G\) and \(\varphi G\), together with multiplication by \(\varphi\) on the quadratic differentials. The « choix “le plus fin” » is associated with \(G_0\).

**Scan 74, remark 3. Fixed characteristic.** There is a proposed characterization of \(G_0\) up to multiplication by an element of \(\mathbb F_p^*\). The discussion also treats a base scheme and a fibrewise nonvanishing condition. The separate « faux ?! » makes any claim of a settled fixed-characteristic result unsafe.

**Scan 75. Translations.** The projective case motivates an analogous question for the translation group. On a smooth curve the example is \(A(f,g)=dg-df\). The page records invariance under adding a constant to \(g\), a local uniqueness condition when two values agree at \(x\), and an epimorphism condition. The margin proposes changing the domain sheaf. The closing remark calls the torsor trivial in this example.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model, high confidence, scan 73, S73-scale.** With divided derivatives, the ordinary first, second and third derivatives correspond to \(a,2b,6d\). Substitution in the displayed ordinary expression gives exactly 12 times \(-d/a+(b/a)^2\). This checks the factor in the normalization for each term.
- **Model, high confidence, scan 73, S73-cube.** Substitution of \(t,t^3\) into the displayed definition gives \(2/(3t^2)\,dt^2\), as in the live second evaluation. The proof of existence or uniqueness does not enter this check.
- **Model, low confidence, scans 72–73, R72-order.** The sideways NB on scan 72 appears to give \(c(f)-c(g)\), while the clear display on 73 gives \(c^X(g)-c^X(f)\). The relation sign and part of the marginal writing remain doubtful. This is a reading question about the comparison, not a finding that the author changed conventions.
- **Model, low confidence, scan 74.** The slanted « faux ?! » and the bound read as « d'ordre \(\leq3\) » are near the uniqueness statement. The intervening words are too compressed to tell whether the author is giving a counterexample or adding a hypothesis.
- **Model, low confidence, scan 75, last line.** The frozen phrase « contient toutes les / tous les qui en sortent » may contain a highly compressed noun. The ink does not settle an expansion or an agreement correction. No reading was changed in preparing these notes.

- **Model, low confidence, scan 62, opening class and proposition.** The frozen form is `\mathcal E_{\chi!}`. An alternative is `\mathcal E_{\chi_!}`. The small exclamation often begins well below the crossing of χ. Its placement relative to the long descending stroke of χ is clearer in the proposition than in the opening line. This is a question about index depth.
