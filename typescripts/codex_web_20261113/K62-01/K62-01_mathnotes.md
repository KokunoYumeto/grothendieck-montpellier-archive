# Mathematical notes on cote 62, scans 051–067

The scan numbers identify the supplied sheets. The three pages of typescript read in mathematical order as 055, 053, 051. Scan 059 opens a different group of notes. The symbols p, ζ, σ and sn below follow the transcription. Editorial tests have IDs that also appear in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**Completion and flatness (055).** For a homomorphism of finite A-modules the text compares the quotient topology on its image with the topology induced by its range. It deduces exactness of completion. A two-term finite free presentation then gives a diagram comparing tensoring with the completed ring with completing the module. The last paragraph uses the free case for the vertical maps and proceeds to flatness.

**Submodules and the Zariski-ring hypothesis (053, 051).** The continuation identifies the completion of F inside the completion of E, compares the powers of the ideals and states a corollary on sums, intersections and transporters. Its last paragraph begins an editorial aside, completed on 051. The writer prefers collecting the argument into one theorem and observes that the tensor description itself did not use the radical-containment hypothesis.

**An affine coordinate and its barycentre (052).** The distinguished section is taken as infinity. Its complement carries the action of the group associated to an invertible sheaf. The notes use the sum of a trisection and invertibility of 3 to obtain a barycentric section. This gives a global identification with the group and leads into 054.

**A pointed anharmonic system (054).** The underlined formulation replaces the system by an invertible sheaf and a trisection of its associated line space having sum zero. The insertion “non ramifiée” points to the trisection. The final lines turn to other elementary symmetric functions. Several words of the slanted note about a covering of degree three are cancelled.

**Scaling the cubic (057).** The page compares a cubic in \(u_\xi,D_\xi u_\xi\) at \(\xi\) and at \(\lambda\xi\). The calculation puts \(\rho=\lambda^2\) and writes weights 2, 4 and 6 beside \(u,b,c\). A separate filtration of the \(E_i\) has successive terms labelled with \(L_2,L_3\) and their products; hatching cancels several attempted identifications. The bottom of the page places \(b,c\) in negative powers of a triangularly written symbol.

**Generation and independence (058).** The sheet passes from tensor products involving B to ones involving L. The \(x_i\) form a basis over the residue field in one line; the next invokes Nakayama for generation over A. For independence, a relation \(\sum\lambda_i x_i=0\) is tensored with 1. The last lines use the coefficients \(1\otimes\lambda_i\) and the map back to B. The diagrams above the calculation give its geometric and ring notation.

**Periods and one period parallelogram (059–060).** The cover announces the transcendental theory. The next sheet defines elliptic functions, includes constants, and divides the plane into parallelograms. It counts the representatives of an interior, edge or vertex point and explains how to count zeros or poles on the boundary with their multiplicities.

**Counting and uniqueness (061).** The opening relates the values taken on one parallelogram to values in the whole plane. The theorems give the residue sum, count zeros and poles, and define the order. Equal divisors determine two functions up to a factor; equal principal parts determine them up to an additive constant. The two concluding statements compare sums of zeros and poles modulo a period and assert algebraic dependence.

**The function p (062).** An order-two elliptic function is normalized by its double pole at zero and by the value of its regular part there. The parity argument leads to a Laurent expansion, a cubic equation for its derivative, and an inverse integral. Half-period values give roots of the cubic. The sheet next expresses higher derivatives in terms of p and its first derivative.

**The function ζ and its periods (062–063).** Integration of the regularized p gives the displayed definition of ζ and the relation \(\zeta'=-p\). Its period differences introduce the constants η. A contour is cited for their relation with the ω's. The substitution \(Z=pz\) then expresses an integral with numerator \(Z\,dZ\) in terms of ζ.

**Hermite decomposition (063).** For prescribed principal parts at representatives \(a,b,\ldots\), the page writes an elliptic function as a constant plus terms in translates of ζ, p and derivatives of p. The coefficient of the general derivative is examined in S3.

**The function σ (063–064).** The logarithmic integral of ζ motivates σ. The following page writes a lattice sum for p, another for ζ, and a product for σ. It identifies the zeros and the logarithmic derivative of σ, then gives formulas for translating its argument.

**Jacobi's quotient and addition (065).** Poles and zeros give two lists whose sums differ by a period Ω. Three expressions for \(f(z)\) move Ω between quotient factors and an exponential. Three further identities give addition formulas for σ, ζ and p.

**Legendre's sn (066).** A quartic integral leads to a differential equation with \(Z(0)=0\) and \(Z'(0)=+1\). The first substitution yields a fractional expression in \(p(z-z_0)\), a triple of e-values, and values of p and its derivative at \(z_0\). The notes use the expression to see the existence of sn and to relate its periods to integrals.

**Two more expressions and a shift (067).** The second and third substitutions express \(\mathrm{sn}^2z\) as a shifted p expression and as a reciprocal. Equating them gives a constant product. The explanatory passage relates the constant to σ, η and a root of the cubic. A final choice of \(\omega_1\) and k fixes the sign in the unsquared shift formula.
