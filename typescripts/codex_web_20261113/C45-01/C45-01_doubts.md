# Legibility

The bibliography (43) is mostly clear. The algebra typescripts (63, 65, 80) are more faded, with small lower indices and occasional paper scratches; their printed i/1 forms and heavy interval brackets require particular care. Scans 72–75 are darker and generally readable, but numerous typed-over cancellations and short handwritten replacements are substantially less secure than their surrounding prose.

The least secure readings are the damaged initial module letter on 65; the cancelled remnants on 72–75; the abbreviated replacement before injectif and inserted d'anneaux augmentés on 73; the last target exponent on 73; the overwritten penultimate letter of automatiquement on 74; and the overlapping first factor and final orthogonal spelling on 75. One cancelled word on 75 is physically unreadable and remains illegible{1}; no other word has been omitted on that account. The alternative alphabet readings for ruled Z and the three heavy H forms remain explicit. The archive number cancelled before 79 on scan 80 is unreadable and is excluded from the text under the archive-number rule.

All whole pages and all supplied thirds were viewed directly, followed by the recorded second readings and supplementary checks. Enlargements used the supplied images only. No OCR, text layer, published text, or web source was used. Handwritten additions are attributed to “unknown” in the inventory: these pages alone do not establish their writer securely. Handwriting style has not been used to assert an author. The inventory's handwritten insertion-guide rows refer to the carets/attachment strokes encoded by the corresponding inserted macro.

The cross-page comparison preserves rather than erases visible differences: plain Z on 63/80 versus ruled Z on 72–75; ordinary H versus the three overstruck candidates on 73; lower-case w before upper-case W on 73. Wedges, interval brackets, looped varphi and lambda forms were checked across their recurring pages. No script capital or bold mathematical alphabet has been supplied from convention.
