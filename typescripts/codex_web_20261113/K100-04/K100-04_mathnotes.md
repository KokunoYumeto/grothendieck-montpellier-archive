## What the pages do

**041 · Polarizations and algebraization.** The remark starts with a proper flat formal scheme over a complete local base and the condition $H^0(X_0,\mathcal O_{X_0})\simeq k$. It imposes flatness on the connected Picard terms, forms a quotient denoted NS, and takes its ample part $P_n$. The resulting formal scheme is written as a coproduct indexed by $\alpha$. Four proposed equivalent conditions test algebraicity and projectivity after a proper surjection, on normalized bases of dimension at most one, or after suitable finite covers. The sloping NB qualifies the last conditions by a smoothness assumption. The qualification matters to how the list can be used.

**042 · EGA IV 15.6.** This reused sheet redistributes the noetherian, finite-presentation and quasi-separated hypotheses in the cited statements. Its proposed “autocritique” credits Raynaud’s use of henselization, discusses passage to projective limits, and objects to using “non ramifié” in place of “net”. The last sentence continues beyond the sheet.

**043 · Descent of a proper flat scheme.** An NB asks about the abelian case and gives a second, uncertain adjective after “ou seulement”. The main remark asks when a descent datum on $X'$ is effective for a proper effective epimorphism $S'\to S$. It passes to local rings and their completions. With hypotheses on infinitesimal automorphisms, or enough sections stable under descent in the smooth locus, it invokes formal moduli to construct a formal object descending the given one.

**044 · EGA corrections on valuation rings and depth.** The opening fragment discusses separability for the fraction field of the completion of a discrete valuation ring. Later entries correct references, insert hypotheses and set out a local argument comparing depth with a condition on Ext. Several relation signs were never entered in the typescript. The lower entries concern local finiteness of irreducible components and the wording “présentation finie”. The annotations cancel whole passages as well as individual words.

**045 · Projectivity and the pair with an invertible sheaf.** Part d) adds a surjective morphism and fibrewise hypotheses on Picard schemes and infinitesimal automorphisms. It states an effective descent result over a finite étale extension of a completed local base and discusses projectivity over its components. A separate remark gives the pair $(X',\underline L')$ its own descent datum and assumes the geometric obstruction expressed as “pas d'autom. infinitésimal non nul” on each indicated fibre. “Argument par modules formels…” is the proof sketch.

**046 · EGA errata and coherent quotients.** The list questions a field identity, corrects references and refines a statement on associated points under a quasi-compact, quasi-separated map. Its proposed corollary associates a coherent quotient to a discrete set of points with finite-length quotient modules. It describes the kernel of a composite, then starts a bijection statement. The last line breaks off after “de”.

**048–049 · Extension to a Néron model.** The typescript asks for a unique prolongation of a generic-fibre morphism from a $p$-divisible group. Lemma 1 reduces extension from a finite flat group $M$ to extension from its connected part. The proof uses the product with $G$, the map $(m,g)\mapsto u_\eta(m)+g$, and the schematic closure $P$ of its generic kernel. Lemma 2 asserts existence and smoothness of the quotient. The exact sequence (1) and the étale quotient of $M$ are used at the top of 049. [B1]

**049 · Two cases of the theorem.** After henselization and passage to the connected part, case a) uses an étale quotient in the semi-stable case. Twice it cites Tate to extend a morphism. In case b) the author passes to a finite flat extension of traits, uses the semi-stable case there, and recalls a construction using restriction of scalars, schematic closure and the “lissifié”. The marginal question “V.A. seulement !” bears on this reduction.

**050 · Lifting and Lemma 3.** A map to $G_1$ has been obtained. Lemma 3 factors a power map on $G_1$ through $G$. [B2] The diagram uses the exact first column for the $p$-divisible group and a dashed map to $G$ to exhibit the required lift. The ensuing remarks distinguish the two uses of Tate, the characteristic-zero qualification and the asserted multiplicative case. [R1]

**050–051 · Corollary on good reduction.** The corollary compares “bonne réduction” for a generic-fibre extension of an abelian variety by a torus with extension of the associated $p$-divisible group over $S$. The change from an $S$-group to an $\eta$-group is visible at the foot of 050. The residual-characteristic condition and the request to explain the quoted phrase are both on 051. [R2]

**052 · A factorization lemma.** Starting with $\varphi=(\varphi(n))$, the author takes the centralizers of the level images. The descending sequence becomes stationary; he then factors through the centre and reduces to a connected commutative group. The case $p=\operatorname{car} K$ uses the subgroups $p^\nu G$. The other case invokes a perfect field and the exclusion of an additive subgroup. The conclusion seeks a subgroup that is an extension of an abelian variety by a torus. [B3]

**053 · Application over a trait.** The opening continuation is cancelled. The live argument treats a generic map $M_K\to G_K$ for a Néron group over a trait. In residual characteristic $p$ it invokes the preceding lemma to factor through $H_K$, takes a Néron model $H$, and appeals to Raynaud to extend the map into $H$. The last line reads “On gagne !”. The arrow labelled $i$ has the manuscript target $K$. [S6]

- **041, 043 and 045.** “Cette condition n'est bonne telle quelle que si …” qualifies the descent criterion. The very compressed “Conditions …” note beside c) may question the necessity of those conditions.
- **042 and 046.** “Nous espérons présenter une version entièrement refondue” is an editorial hope on 042. The “?” beside “qui est manifestement faux” on 046 calls attention to the proposed correction of III 4.3.6.
- **048 and 049.** The annotations “[ ] inutile”, “G commutatif?”, “donc” / “abusif” and “? Cas où … est une V.A. seulement !” identify distinct questions about assumptions and inference. The last note points specifically to case b).

- **052.** “à dégager en lemme” asks for a separate statement about $p^\nu G$ over a field of positive characteristic.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · 050, high confidence.** Following the arrows from the upper wavy H to the middle G gives $g\circ u_1$ along the top and $u\circ[p^n]$ through the lower H. From the lower H the bottom horizontal and the path through G both end at the bottom $G_1$. This identifies the dashed lift used in the next sentence. Its existence is the manuscript’s mathematical argument.
- **R2 · 050–051, high confidence.** Two pen leaders have different endpoints on 051. The upper insertion qualifies $S$ by its residual characteristic. The left leader ends at the quoted phrase “bonne réduction”. This is why the corollary and the request for clarification can be reported separately.
- **R3 · 052–053, medium confidence.** The case distinction on 053 mirrors the field hypotheses of the lemma on 052. The text’s passage through $H_K$ explains where the theorem attributed to Raynaud enters the extension argument. The cancelled opening on 053 is a distinct attempted continuation.
