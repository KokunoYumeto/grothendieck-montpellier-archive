# Mathematical notes on cote 62, scans 020–026 and 028–035

## What the pages do

**Induced topology (020).** For a finite module \(E\) over a commutative noetherian ring, Theorem 1 identifies the induced topology on a submodule \(F\) with its \(\mathrm m\)-adic topology. Artin–Rees gives \(\mathrm m^pE\cap F=\mathrm m^{p-k}(\mathrm m^kE\cap F)\) for sufficiently large \(k\) and \(p\geq k\). The proof begins by forming a graded ring and module inside \(A[X]\) and \(E[X]\).

**Derivative values of a cubic (021).** A product of root differences is expressed as \(\prod_i f'(x_i)\). The right margin computes the quadratic coefficient \(3u+a\) after translation. Setting \(a=0\), the author writes \(j=-27c^2/\Delta\) and works toward a formula for \(\Delta\) in \(b,c\). The box ends with a question mark.

**Completeness of filtered modules (022).** A bound on \(d(x_p,x_q)\) by successive distances gives Proposition 4. Completeness is equivalent to convergence of every series whose terms tend to zero. Corollary 1 applies the criterion to closed submodules and quotient modules.

**Finite modules (022, 024).** Corollary 2 represents \(E\) as a quotient of a finite free module. The proof reduces to a finite product of copies of \(A\), comparing the product filtration with the product topology.

**Cubic coefficients (023).** The three factors \(2x_i-x_j-x_k\), the squared product of root differences \(\Delta\), and the three identities in the left margin lead to the box \(j=(27c+2a^3-9ab)^2/(-27\Delta)\).

**Power series (024, 026).** Proposition 5 transfers separatedness and completeness from \(A\) to \(A[[X]]\) with the \((\mathrm m,X)\)-adic filtration. The proof assembles sums of coefficient series into a limit. The corollary gives separatedness and completeness for series in \(X_1,\ldots,X_r\) in the variable ideal.

**Cross-ratio (025).** Two cubic models precede three diagrams. The first sends \(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\) to \(\infty,0,1,t\). The others group the four objects and reorder them. Setting \(x_4=\infty\) gives \(t=(x_3-x_1)/(x_3-x_2)\), followed by expressions for \(t-1,t+1,2t-1,t-2\).

**Filtered morphisms (026).** The condition \(u(E_n)\subset F_n\) induces a \(G(A)\)-linear map \(G(u)\). Proposition 6 reaches the hypotheses of a complete source, a separated target and surjective \(G(u)\). Above the typescript is a separate pen drawing labelled 59, 60 and 59/60.

**Permutations of three sections (028).** Formulas (1)–(5) give the two cycles and three transpositions of \(0,1,\infty\), their products and their conjugates. The quotient by the cyclic subgroup of order three has order two. Each transposition gives a splitting. The margin proposes new names for the transpositions.

**Residues of valuations (029).** The three corollaries lift simultaneous residues to \(B\), produce \(x_i\) with \(v_i(x_i)=0\) and \(v_j(x_i)>0\) for \(j\ne i\), and place one of the \(A_i\) in each valuation ring containing \(B\).

**Independence and approximation (029).** Two valuation rings in \(K\) are independent if no proper subring of \(K\) contains both. The rank-one example precedes simultaneous approximation to \(a_i\) with lower bounds \(\gamma_i\).

**An additional involution (028, 030).** The map \(\sigma(t)=(t-2)/(2t-1)\) sends \(0,1,\infty\) to \(2,-1,1/2\). The author lists the three transposition-fixed pairs. The cycles and \(\sigma\) have the fixed equation \(t^2-t+1=0\) away from characteristics 2 and 3. He describes the stabilizer of this degree-two scheme as an extension of \(\mathbb Z_2\) by a twist of \(G_m\).

**Three sections of the quotient (032).** The diagram maps \(\mathbb Z/3\) into the twisted multiplicative group and identifies the order-two quotients. Its middle column has no vertical arrow. Under the stated characteristic restrictions the author describes a tame action. The two triples and one pair give three sections of the quotient by \(\mathfrak S_3\).

**The intermediate quotient (032, 034).** The two triples give sections on the order-three quotient. Over \(S'=S(\sqrt{-3})\), the two roots give \(x',x''\). Once zero and infinity have been chosen, using one root section as the unit gives the other value \(-1\). The author relates the quotient to an invertible sheaf, a symmetric unramified bisection and a nondegenerate quadratic form.

**Extension of places (035).** The opening finishes a rank-one argument for \(\Gamma_{v'}\). Proposition 3 carries algebraicity from \(L/K\) to the image fields of a place. Proposition 4 extends \(g:S\to k\) to a place of \(K\), for algebraically closed \(k\).

**The maximal-pair argument (035, 033).** The lemma seeks a place of an extension fixing \(k\). A transcendental \(X\) would extend a maximal pair \((F,\psi)\) via evaluation at zero on \(F(X)\). Once \(L/F\) is algebraic, an extended place has image \(k\). An editorial aside discusses where to put the proposition.

**Extensions of a valuation (033, 031).** Proposition 5 bounds their number by \(1\le r\le [L:K]\). Their rings intersect in the integral closure \(B\) and are its localizations at the distinct maximal ideals. The proof excludes containment by an argument on value groups. It then seeks too many independent vectors in \(L\) and breaks off just after normalizing one coefficient.
