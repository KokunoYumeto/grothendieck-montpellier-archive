# What the pages do

These notes were written after the transcription freeze. They use only the supplied pages. Descriptions follow the first candidates in the frozen transcription where a glyph is marked unsure; they do not resolve those doubts or repair the text.

**149–150, §5.1 and theorem 5.2.** The discussion applies local Lefschetz statements to a vanishing problem for the underlined Phi notation. A hypothesis on strict generisations is treated as an induction hypothesis. The page relates the desired stalk vanishing to local cohomology of the schemes denoted X(h), and then to an étale-depth bound. Theorem 5.2 separates the generisations lying outside a chosen subset Z from those in Z and states a local acyclicity conclusion and a proper-specialisation consequence.

**150, cancelled corollary 5.3.** A wholly cancelled draft introduces a dimension bound d and competing depth hypotheses. Its formulas and proposed proof are still present in the transcription, including internal deletions. They should not be read as an additional settled version of the following corollary.

**151–153, corollary 5.3 and its proof.** The replacement corollary gives two ways to obtain condition b) of theorem 5.2. Case I uses étale-depth inequalities; Case II assumes flatness and uses geometric depth. The proof compares dimensions along generisations and invokes local Lefschetz statements. It ends by reducing the geometric-depth argument to the two assertions of lemma 5.3.3.

**153, lemma 5.3.3.** Part (i) states a lower bound for geometric depth after localisation at a prime. Part (ii) states an additive lower bound for a flat local homomorphism, with an auxiliary hypothesis involving a formally smooth local algebra over the residue field. The page asks for proofs and separately questions the need for that auxiliary hypothesis.

**154, corollary 5.4.** A complete-intersection hypothesis on the special fibre leads to displayed degree ranges for the underlined and non-underlined Phi expressions. The page specialises to d=0, states the corresponding comparison-map properties, and records a triviality assertion for the inertia action. The displayed ranges and their indices have been preserved without harmonising them.

**154–155, §§5.5–5.6.** The discussion turns to noncommutative cohomology and local 1-asphericity for a set of primes. It gives a comparison for finite groups and a vanishing condition on the local schemes, then states theorem 5.6 under flatness, a codimension bound on the non-smooth locus and local complete-intersection hypotheses. In the proper case it states a specialisation isomorphism for H¹ with suitable finite-group coefficients. The revised sentence leading into the theorem remains uncertain in several places.

**155–156, remark 5.7 and conjecture.** The author proposes replacing a complete-intersection assumption by a geometric-depth condition. The next page gives a broader conjecture for flat strict-local schemes, with a qualification about excellence, bounds on singular loci after finite residue-field extensions, and conclusions about local acyclicity and 1-asphericity. Several signs and cancelled hypotheses are unsettled in the manuscript.

**157–158, §§6.1–6.2.** The discussion assumes vanishing away from the chosen point and compares four local schemes. It introduces an equivariant morphism u and a distinguished triangle. Forgetting the operators and applying the derived invariants functor produce further triangles. The page describes how these data recover cohomology and splits a resulting morphism into components alpha and beta. A margin asks for a diagram to be moved or revised and commented on.

**159, proposition 6.3 and corollary 6.4.** The proposition identifies a local-cohomology object with a shifted and twisted coefficient object and relates a canonical triangle’s boundary map to beta. The corollary identifies the triangle with a mapping-cylinder construction, explicitly qualified by signs and shifts. The separate “signe ?” is an unresolved caution in the source.

**160, proof of 6.3(a).** After a cancelled draft, the page draws a base-change diagram and writes two chains of derived-functor comparisons. The second ends in a direct-sum expression with a twist and shift. It then refers to the usual relations between local cohomology and higher direct images. Compatibility b) is left to the editor(s).

**161, §6.5.** With n defined by the local-ring dimension, the author uses results of §2 and the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence to pass from a vanishing bound on the geometric generic fibre to one on the generic fibre. A long exact sequence from the earlier triangle is then used to express the conclusion through a map i_* and its isomorphism/epimorphism ranges. The source and several overwritten indices in the final formula are low-confidence readings.

**162, §6.6.** Results of §5 give comparison statements in low degrees under complete-intersection and equidimensionality hypotheses. A four-line display lists ensuing cohomological vanishing ranges. The inserted direct-limit expression and repeated H⁰(V_s) in the second row are retained as written.

**162, §6.7.** A stronger maximality condition, now stated in X, leads to a heavily revised Poincaré sentence. With an additional complete-intersection condition, the page lists smaller sets of possibly nonzero degrees and adds degree-zero/top-degree identifications at the right. The rewritten wording and one twist are unresolved; these notes do not turn them into a settled duality theorem.

**164, new section and §1.1.** A new heading introduces monodromy and the fundamental group. The introduction contrasts the preceding cohomological discussion with the action on a geometric fundamental group. The text defines the maximal prime-to-p quotient of a profinite group, describes it using p-Sylow subgroups and begins discussing functoriality. The final sentence continues beyond the supplied page and is not completed here.
