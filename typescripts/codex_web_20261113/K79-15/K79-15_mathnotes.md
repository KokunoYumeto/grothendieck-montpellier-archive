# K79-15 · Mathematical notes

These notes follow the manuscript labels, including repeated numbers. “Ray” and “coray” translate *rayon* and *corayon*. The small superscript on a quotient set is graphically uncertain, so it is called the orbit set in prose. The statements below concern these fifteen scans.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Transport of a gauge, scan 220.** A gauge is a map from a vertex set \(S\) to its partner \(S^\vee\). An automorphism \(g\) transports it by applying \(g_{S^\vee}\) after the map and \(g_S^{-1}\) before it. Equation (87) also transports its graph. For a \(Z\)-gauge the notes compare this action with \(T_g\), obtaining the square \(g^2\) in (90) and (92). Part of the intervening calculation is cancelled.

**U2 · The isomorphy condition, scans 221–222.** A commutative subgroup \(Z\) of the automorphism group of \(\Gamma=(S,S^\vee,R)\) acts on a family of gauges. The lemma tests whether transport preserves a chosen sub-torsor \(\pi\) by looking at the graph of one member, then of every member. Its group diagram leads to (98): the isomorphy condition asks for surjectivity onto \(Z\). Below it, a cancelled draft considers transitivity and an orientation-preserving subgroup for a 2-polyhedron.

**U3 · Association, quasi-gauges and gauges, scans 223–224.** Two maps \(S\to S^\vee\) are associated when the incidence test with one map agrees with the reversed test with the other, (3). Separation on the \(S^\vee\) side gives uniqueness when an associate exists. A gauge is self-associated and a strict gauge is bijective. The manuscript relates a strict gauge and its inverse to an isomorphism with the dual pregraph.

**U4 · The graph and its stars, scans 224–225.** A gauge gives a graph on \(S\), allowing an edge with one vertex. Its stars are drawn from the subsets specified by \(S^\vee\). Surjective gauges give the entire prescribed set of stars. Equivalences (15) and (16) distinguish graphs in general from graphs in which the star determines its vertex. The pregraph in the latter equivalence is separated on both sides and has a strict gauge.

**U5 · Homotheties, scans 225–227.** The notes identify pregraph automorphisms through permutations preserving the star data under separation. The homothety test in (20) applies \(f^{-1}\) to one endpoint and \(f\) to the other. Equations (21)–(23) express that test on a gauge; for a bijective simultaneous gauge, the product again satisfies the test precisely when its two factors commute. The action \(T_\lambda\) on gauges and on their edges follows. The subset \(G_{\varphi_0}\) in (29) is explicitly distinguished from a subgroup.

**U6 · Choice of a sub-torsor and passage to the dual, scan 228.** The datum singled out in (30) is a \(Z\)-sub-torsor \(\pi\) of bijective \(Z\)-gauges. Formula (31) relates the vertex and covertex sets by contracted products. Inversion of the bijections sends \(\pi\) to the corresponding family for the dual pregraph. Formula (33) records the opposite \(Z\)-action.

**U7 · Orbits and incidence fibres, scans 229–230.** Orbits in \(S\) are rays; orbits in \(S^\vee\) are corays. The gauges induce a common identification of the two orbit sets. The incidence quotient maps to their product, and that map can have nontrivial fibres. With free actions and chosen base points, (39)–(40) describe a fibre by those \(z\in Z\) for which \((zs_0,s_0^\vee)\in R\). Changing the base points translates the describing subset of \(Z\).

**U8 · Incidence of rays and the type of an edge, scans 230–232.** Condition (41) asks for injectivity of the incidence-quotient map. With the stated freeness, incidence between a linked ray and coray then gives a bijection of torsors. For two rays in a graph it gives an anti-isomorphism. After forming the union of the edge sets \(A_i\), the notes describe \(\pi\)-incidence and attach to an edge its unique type \(i\). The transformation law (46) leads to the contracted-product isomorphism (47).

**U9 · Intersections, scans 232–233.** An underlined NB distinguishes stars, which come from the pregraph, from rays, which come from \(Z\). Distinct rays are disjoint. The possible intersections of two stars receive a separate research suggestion. In (48) the useful mixed criterion is that a star and a ray meet in at most one vertex.

**U10 · Relative torsors, scans 233–234.** Sheet 119 bis describes \(S\) and \(S^\vee\) as torsors over their orbit sets. Over the incidence relation between rays and corays, (48.4) identifies their pullbacks. Diagram (48.5) recovers the original incidence from this identification. A strict gauge then involves both a bijection on orbit sets satisfying an incidence symmetry and an isomorphism of torsors, (48.6).

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Model, high confidence.** The freeness conclusion on scan 227 concerns the action on *bijective gauges*. “Supposons pour simplifier que Z opère librement” on scan 229 instead concerns the actions on \(S\) and \(S^\vee\). The two statements have different underlying sets.
- **M2 · Model, medium confidence.** Formula (40) usefully retains its content when the base-point pair is not incident: the author says both sides may be empty. The extra incidence hypothesis is what puts \(1\) in the subset of \(Z\). This explains the two successive qualifications in the paragraph.
- **M3 · Model, low confidence.** Reading question, scan 231, source lines 36–39. The frozen (43a,b) read \(s'^{q}=\varphi^{q}(s^{q})\) and its reversal, where \(q\) stands here for the small quotient sign. In view of the identification after (35), it is unclear how these two equalities distinguish incidence of two rays from equality of their images. A possible interpretation would use the induced *relation* between the orbit sets. The ink here shows equations with \(\varphi\). The upper, cancelled (43) also contains disputed strokes.
- **M4 · Model, low confidence.** Reading question, scan 220, source line 51. The frozen right-hand factor in (91b) is \(g_{S^\vee}^{-1}\); \(g_S^{-1}\) is another possible reading of that factor. The inverse is clear. A separate check above the little S is less clear here than in the parenthetic gloss immediately below. The suggested correction in S1 therefore does not identify both subscripts with certainty.
- **M5 · Model, low confidence.** Reading question, scan 223, source line 43. Before “telle que”, the frozen alternatives are “i.e.” and “i.e. est”. On enlargement the initial mark has a long descending stroke and the next group appears to end in t. A compressed “φ est” is a further possibility. This affects the prose introducing (4).
