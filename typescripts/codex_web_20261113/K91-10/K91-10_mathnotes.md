# K91-10 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Restricted enveloping algebras, scan 129.** Part a) states a universal mapping property for the tilded \(U\) and a tilded map whose letter is doubtful. Part b) passes from an ordered basis of \(g\) to ordered monomials with exponents less than \(p\). Its parenthesis separates each exponent into a remainder and a multiple of \(p\), then introduces \(T_\alpha\) and the central elements \(\phi(b_\lambda)\).

**Derivations, scan 129.** Exercise 7 defines a \(p\)-derivation, gives inner derivations as examples, asks about derivations of an associative algebra, and states an extension criterion using the restricted enveloping algebra.

**Greenberg points, scans 131–132.** A discrete valuation ring \(V\) has perfect residue field \(k\) and fraction field \(K\). The notes relate \(\bar X(k')\) to \(X(V_{k'})\). For \(Y\) proper over \(V\), a map of generic fibres gives a set map \(\bar X\to\bar Y\) by passing to a perfect closure and taking the point described on the page. Proposition 1 treats an «épais» point, its residue field and a map of finite index. The first corollary on 132 expresses functoriality in the generic fibre.

**Constructible sets, scan 132.** Proposition 2 starts with a constructible \(S\) and a boundedness condition whose notation is difficult to read. It asserts constructibility of the image and a finite partition into irreducible constructible pieces on which the map has finite index. The final corollary singles out the collection \(\operatorname{Cons}(\bar X)\).

**Units of Laurent series, scans 134–135.** Scan 134 presents \(A\mapsto A((t))^*\) by stages that keep coefficients of a series and its inverse. The relation is their product being 1. It identifies the zero stage, forms a product with the integers, and writes a comparison map. Scan 135 describes the component map by multiplication by \(t^\nu\). Its theorem distinguishes the extreme components from the intermediate ones, then follows the induced stages indexed by \(i\).

**Limits of schemes, scan 136.** A diagram arranges \(\operatorname{Pro}\), \(\operatorname{Pro}'\) and \(\operatorname{Pro}''\) of schemes over \(S\). Eventual affineness, quasi-compactness and finite presentation appear in the conditions. Two Hom comparisons send a map of systems to one of their limits. The closing remark concerns projective systems used by Serre and Néron.

**Evaluation of coordinate series, scan 138.** The page writes \(f_i\) as a constant term, a linear part with matrix \((b_{ij})\), and terms indexed by \(p\) of weight at least 2. It imposes a determinant condition and evaluates the tuple \(f\) on elements of an ideal. Infinitesimal order \(N\) gives a boxed expression through degree \(N\). At the foot, the powers of the ideal are bracketed into successive levels \(V_i\).

**Two formal-group exercises, scan 139.** The typescript finishes a dimension calculation involving transposed matrices, then asks for the symplectic formal group and the matrix description of its Lie algebra. Exercise 27 gives a two-coordinate law in positive characteristic and asks for different commutativity conclusions for the group and its Lie algebra.

**Truncated coordinate changes, scans 140 and 142.** Scan 140 writes an augmented-algebra automorphism group of a truncated polynomial algebra, names its Greenberg group, and starts composing transformations on tuples. Several trials break off. Scan 142 gives three affine-space expressions with tensor factors \(V_1,V_2,V_3\), revisits the polynomial coordinate changes, and composes two quadratic polynomials. Only the terms through degree two lie inside its box.

**Brackets, homomorphisms and the linear group, scans 141 and 143.** Formula (3) relates the antisymmetric part of a formal law to brackets, up to terms of degree at least three. Part d) defines a formal homomorphism by its coordinate system and asks for the induced map on invariant derivations and its matrix of constant partial derivatives. Exercise 25 defines a matrix law in shifted coordinates. Its formula for the invariant derivations continues on 143, followed by the identification with matrices and the stated \(p\)-powers.

**The orthogonal group, scan 143.** Exercise 26 uses a symmetric form and a diagonal Gram matrix \(R\). It writes the transformation between an orthogonal matrix \(U\) and an alternating matrix \(S\), with determinant conditions. Three families of formal variables give \(S,S',S''\); the product \(UU'\) is taken back to \(S\)-coordinates. The sentence about the series \(f_{ij}(S,S')\) continues beyond this sheet.
