# Mathematical notes · 157-3, scans 342–356

## What the pages do

**342, 344 · Reused typescript.** Page 41 distinguishes accepting a desire from acting on it. Page 44 distinguishes reactive violence, retaliation and displaced aggression. The two fragments have their own pagination. Scans 354 and 356 are uncompleted Avignon education-office forms.

**343 · A four-object example.** Two parallel arrows enter \(a\), and one arrow leaves \(a\) for each of \(b,c\). Their two composites to each target agree. The page identifies the amalgamated sum of the representables \(b,c\) over \(a\) with the terminal presheaf (A1). A proposed retraction onto the parallel-arrow subcategory would give the example the homotopy type of a circle (A2). The last diagram adds a final object.

**345–346 · Total asphericity.** The small outgoing fork \(\Upsilon\) and the larger category \(Z\) play different roles. The former indexes the diagram of representables. The latter supplies an example where the pointwise sum is terminal although \(Z\) is not weakly contractible. A comma category for the closed inclusion has a four-edge graph of circle type. A slanted note rejects the separate retraction of \(Z\) and identifies \(Z\) as a suspension. On 345 the author revises the connectivity claim to « 1-connexe forcément, mais non 2-connexe ».

**347–348 · An intended application to pushouts.** The author seeks a square in which pushing along an open immersion fails to preserve a weak equivalence. He singles out the boundary of \(\Phi_n\times\Phi_m\). The cancelled draft on 348 tries to specify a local category \(V\) and a functor out of it. Its five-position zigzag contains the smaller fork as the two central edges.

**349–350 · The boundary inclusion.** In the proposition, most of the indicated slices have a final object. The four corner slices have a different shape. Deleting the four distinguished objects produces an open Dwyer immersion. The proof isolates one corner, its closure \(\overline V\), and the nine-object open neighbourhood called \(W\). The five slices in that neighbourhood are the assertion A4. Here the letter \(W\) names a category in the grid as well as a class of maps in the surrounding discussion.

**350–352 · The proposed counterexample to the cofibration property.** Extending \(V'_0\) by a final object gives \(V'\). The author pushes \(V\to V'\) along the inclusion into \(\overline V\), then extends the square to \(X\). He factors the map through \(U\) and ends with \(q_U\in W\) and \(q\notin W\). The argument uses the Dwyer property (B1) of the closed inclusion and the asserted failure of asphericity of a slice. The specific fundamental-group assertion on 351 is discussed below.

**353, 355 · Gluing an open category to its closed complement.** The category \(\widetilde X_{01}\) consists of arrows from \(X_0\) to \(X_1\). Source and target give the upper half of a square with a drawn 2-cell. The author asks whether that square is \(W\)-cocartesian. He constructs the comparison \(\varphi\) from the category obtained by integrating the span to \(X\). The proposed one-dimensional conclusion (B2) is expressed in terms of categories of coverings and their 2-fibre product. The last page asks how far this extends through higher gerbes.

## Questions and conjectures

- **353.** « Ce diagramme est-il \(W\)-cocartésien ? » The subsequent « je n’ai pu prouver » concerns the additional hypotheses on \(i\) or \(j\), \(X\), and \(W\). The asterisk leads to « Je suppose que \(W\) satisfait Loc(6) ».
- **353.** « Pas non plus si \(X_0\simeq e\) … » introduces a further unsuccessful special case. « Mais j’ai présumé du moins … » turns to the comparison (**).
- **355.** « me semble devoir s’étendre aux dimensions supérieures, à coups de \(n\)-gerbes » is followed by « les fondements sur la théorie des \(n\)-gerbes … manquent ». The first pronoun has an alternative reading in the transcription.
- **355.** « Aussi je conjecture que \(\varphi\) est … au moins dans \(\mathbb W_\infty\) ». The author restates this in terms of (*) and a condition on \(W\) involving \(\mathbb W_\infty\) and a primed Loc(6) reference. In the slanted note he reports « une esquisse de vérification pour \(n=2\) » and writes \(\varphi\in\mathbb W_2\). His further words about \(\mathbb W_\infty\) overlap the main text.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High confidence for the displayed presentation, scans 343, 345–347.** I evaluated the pushout of the two representables over the third on each of the four objects. The cardinalities, in the order source of the parallel pair, its target, and the two branch tips, are \(1,1,1,1\). This checks A1 without choosing a name for the first oval.
- **R2 · High confidence for the displayed presentation, scans 343, 345–346.** I enumerated functors to the full parallel-arrow subcategory that fix it pointwise. There are none. This verifies the content of the marginal objection for that explicit category.
- **R3 · High confidence for the displayed presentation, scans 350–351.** The two defining composition relations are also visible in the diagram on 351. Its normalized integral nerve chains have ranks \(4,6,4,0\) in degrees zero through three. The boundary ranks are \(3,3\), and the greatest common divisor of the maximal minors of the second boundary is 1. Thus \(H_1=0\) and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z\). This detects failure of weak contractibility and explains the difficulty with A3. The calculation alone does not identify the entire homotopy type.
- **R4 · High confidence, scan 350.** I read the nine-object grid as a product of two three-position diagrams. For the five named positions outside the closed corner, the comma categories have respectively \(2,2,1,1,1\) objects in the order \(a',b',a'',b'',c\). Each has exactly one initial object. This checks the specific five-slice assertion (A4).
- **R5 · Medium confidence, scans 353 and 355.** The marginal \(n=2\) sketch concerns the same comparison arrow \(\varphi\) that appears in (**). Its explicit \(\mathbb W_2\) conclusion can be distinguished from the longer \(\mathbb W_\infty\) conjecture even where the connecting words are difficult to read.
