# Mathematical notes · cote 157-3, scans 2–17

## What the pages do

**Title and starting data, scans 2–3.** The cover announces a return to categories of fractions and a comparison with Quillen. Section 1 starts with a category $M$, an admissible collection of arrows $\Sigma$, and interval objects indexed by $n$. It asks how the external Hom $\mathrm{Ch}^{\Sigma}_{\infty}(a,b)$ depends on the endpoints in $M\Sigma^{-1}$.

**Composition and units, scans 3–5.** A pairing of paths from $a$ to $b$ with paths from $b$ to $c$ induces composition on $\pi_0$. Grothendieck also writes the map from $\mathrm{Hom}_M(a,b)$ to these components. He describes a two-sided unit by the two endomorphisms it induces on the external Homs. On components this gives identity arrows for a category he calls $M_\pi$.

**The two variables, scans 5–6.** Fixing $a$ gives a covariant assignment in $b$. Fixing $b$ gives a contravariant one in $a$. A square compares the two orders of composition by $u$ and $v$. The stated corollary is that invertible changes of endpoints induce an isomorphism of external Homs.

**Operations in a category with a product, scans 7–8.** For a category $\mathscr H$ with product $*$ and unit $1$, the manuscript puts $\pi_0(\xi)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr H}(1,\xi)$. A pairing $P*Q\to R$ gives an action on each factor by an element of the other. For three factors Grothendieck draws an associativity square and writes out one of the resulting identities, $c(\beta*_{u_2}\gamma)=c(\gamma)c(\beta)$.

**The general axioms, scans 9–10.** On a set $M_0$ he specifies external Homs and their composition. The two axioms are an associativity square and the existence of two-sided units. The resulting category $M_{0\pi}$ has a canonical external-Hom bifunctor. Applying $\pi_0$ recovers its ordinary Hom bifunctor. Scan 10 stresses that the identification is functorial in both arguments.

**Two constructions and their comparison, scans 10–12.** For $(M,\Sigma)$ the two cases are $\mathrm{Ch}^{\Sigma}_{\infty}$ in $\mathrm{Hot}$ and $\mathrm{CH}^{\Sigma}_{\infty}$ in $\mathrm{Ind}\,\mathrm{Cat}\,\mathrm{hot}$. The diagram on scan 11 relates their target categories. Scan 12 gives a comparison from $\mathrm{CH}$ to $\mathrm{Ch}$, labelled $(\alpha)$, and identifies the colimit in $\mathrm{Hot}_0^\wedge$ with the image of $\mathrm{Ch}$, labelled $(\alpha\alpha)$.

**The difficulty with explicit paths, scans 13–14.** Using $C_\infty$ for either construction, Grothendieck asks how an arrow changing an endpoint could act on the displayed inductive system of path categories. He then interprets paths as infinite zigzags that are essentially finite in both directions. Paths degenerate on one side form the two one-sided subcategories.

**Composition with one-sided paths, scans 14–15.** An element of $C_\infty^{-}(a,b)$ and one of $C_\infty^{+}(b,c)$ can be composed into $C_\infty(a,c)$. For associativity in the $\mathrm{CH}$ case, scan 15 compares two maps from a triple product of finite stages to a common stage $C_N(a,d)$. Grothendieck records separately how far he has got with $\mathrm{Ch}$.

**Quillen’s classes, scans 16–17.** Section 2 recalls the sets $\pi_1(a,b;f,g)$ for parallel arrows in a “model category”. Ordinary arrows $a\to b$ are the objects of the groupoid described here. Grothendieck proposes identifying its morphisms with morphisms in the fundamental groupoid of the path category, after placing each arrow in an infinite zigzag.

**Pointed paths, scan 17.** If $0_M$ is both initial and final, the zero arrow $0_{a,b}$ supplies a distinguished path. Grothendieck asks for composition and its laws in $\mathrm{Hot}_{\bullet}$. The sheet stops as he writes the two zero paths to be composed.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 7.** “Voici l’axiomatisation définitive (?)” and “commutatif (?)”. The margin adds “On ne doit pas avoir besoin de la commutativité”. The reservation concerns the need for commutativity of $*$.
- **Scans 11–12.** “j’ignore ce qu’il en est de” the second horizontal functor and its Ind version. The next sheet leaves a possible exception to loss of information “si … est pl. fidèle”. The cross-shaped label itself is doubtful.
- **Scans 13–14.** Endpoint functoriality “n’est pas évidente du tout sur la définition directe”. The proposed composition has a direct definition “pas moins problématique”.
- **Scan 15.** For $\mathrm{Ch}_{\infty}$, “je n’ai pas trouvé de démonstration”. He asks “Faute seulement d’avoir essayé sérieusement de m’y mettre ?” and suggests that the functor from $\mathrm{Hot}$ to $\mathrm{Hot}_0^\wedge$ might be fully faithful.
- **Scans 16–17.** “Je suspecte que l’on a” introduces the proposed fundamental-groupoid formula for $\pi_1(f,g)$. Its extension to arbitrary essentially finite paths is qualified by the raised “si c’est bien ça”, whose last word has an uncertain initial.
- **Scan 17.** “Le problème, c’est de définir les flèches … dans $\mathrm{Hot}_{\bullet}$”, with “associativité (et les unités)”. A doubtful question mark follows the statement about the faithfulness of the forgetful functor. The page leaves pointed composition open.

## Remarks of the model

- **R8.order · my observation, high confidence.** In the boxed expression $c(\gamma)c(\beta)$, the displayed action of $\beta$ has endpoints $P_0,Q_0$ and that of $\gamma$ has endpoints $Q_0,R$. Thus reading the right-hand side from right to left gives composable arrows from $P_0$ to $R$. This checks the order and endpoints only.
- **R16.path · my observation, high confidence.** The zigzag places $f$ specifically from $a_{-1}$ to $a_0$. The values written beneath these vertices are $a$ and $b$. Each neighbouring identity joins two vertices with the same written value. This fixes the position of $f$ in the comparison even though the prose word introducing the diagram is hard to read.
