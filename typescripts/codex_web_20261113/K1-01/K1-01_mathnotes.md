# Mathematical notes on cote 1, scans 51–64

## What the pages do

**U1 · Vector-valued square-integrable functions, scan 51.** Theorem 1 identifies the Hilbert tensor product of $L^2(\mu)$ and $E$ with $L^2_E(\mu)$ in (11). One corollary gives square-summable $E$-valued families, with inner product $\sum_i(x_i,y_i)$. The other gives $L^2(\mu\otimes\nu)$ and recovers an earlier proposition by specializing both measures to the sequence-space case.

**U2 · From tensors to operators, scans 51–53.** Formula (14) sends $\bar a\otimes b$ to the operator $x\mapsto(x,a)b$. Pairing with $y$ in (15) bounds its operator norm by the Hilbert tensor norm. The extension to the completion is injective because its coefficients are the entries of its operator matrix. Definition 2 calls the resulting maps Hilbert–Schmidt. In orthonormal bases the square of the new norm is the sum of the squared absolute matrix entries. Finite-rank approximation gives compactness.

**U3 · Composition, adjoints and singular values, scans 53–55.** Composing on either side with a bounded operator preserves the class and gives the two bounds in (17). Passage to the adjoint preserves the norm and reverses the entries in the scalar product in (18). Writing $h=\sqrt{u^*u}$ and comparing $u$ with $h$ by partial isometries reduces (19) to the diagonal case. There the Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the square root of the sum of squared eigenvalues of $h$.

**U4 · The manuscript’s Fredholm class, scans 55–56.** Th. 3 uses the completed tensor product, the norm $\|\cdot\|_1$ and summable singular values. Projection onto the first $n$ eigenvectors bounds their partial sum. Conversely the diagonal series bounds the norm. This gives (20), then the rank-one expansion (21) using orthonormal families on the two sides. A trace of the positive modulus and a supremum of $|\operatorname{Tr}(Au)|$ over contractions express the same norm in two displays both numbered (22). The sideways (23) returns to the Hilbert–Schmidt criterion for $u^*u$.

**U5 · Trace of a product, scans 56–57.** Th. 4 bounds the Fredholm norm of $vu$ by $\|v\|_2\|u\|_2$ in (24). For $u,v$ with the same source and target, (25) identifies $(u,v)$ with both $\operatorname{Tr}(uv^*)$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(v^*u)$. The proof reduces by continuity to finite rank, calculates on two elementary tensors, and invokes commutation under the trace.

**U6 · Two converse criteria, scan 58.** The first corollary recovers $\|u\|_2$ by testing $\operatorname{Tr}(vu)$ on the Hilbert–Schmidt unit ball. In the converse, the closed graph theorem gives continuity of $v\mapsto vu$. Testing its associated functional on $\bar x\otimes y$ identifies the representing operator with $u$. The second corollary factors a Fredholm operator on one Hilbert space as $vw$. The proof takes $w=\sqrt h$ and $v=Uw$, and obtains equal Hilbert–Schmidt norms for the factors.

**U7 · Several Hilbert tensor factors, scans 59–60.** The numbering restarts at (1). The inner product on two elementary tensors is the product of the component inner products. The side note regroups the factors and permutes them. Orthonormal bases give a basis indexed by a Cartesian product. To bound the tensor product of maps in (2), the manuscript first lets just one map differ from the identity, then uses the two-factor Hilbert–Schmidt estimate. A coordinate-space approach is crossed out.

**U8 · Exterior Hilbert power, scans 60–62.** The signed permutation average $a_n$ in (3) is a continuous Hermitian projector. Scan 61 describes the quotient and identifies it with the image of the projector, multiplying the scalar product by $n!$. The algebraic exterior power has dense image. Evaluating this scalar product on two decomposable wedges gives the mixed Gram determinant in (4).

**U9 · A basis on the slip, scans 62–63.** The theorem on the supporting sheet refers to $e_J$. The slip supplies its definition for $i_1<\cdots<i_n$. Formula (4) gives $(e_J,e_{J'})=\delta_{J,J'}$. The span is dense by applying the natural multilinear map to the span of the original orthonormal basis. The scan exposes the lower part of the supporting sheet again.

**U10 · Determinant bounds, scans 62–64.** Setting $b_i=a_i$ in (4) gives the Gram formula for the wedge norm. In coordinates the Gram matrix is $AA^*$, with determinant $|\det A|^2$. A choice of basis making $A$ triangular gives Hadamard’s inequality (6). The corollary bounds $|\det((a_i,b_j))|$ by the product of all the vector norms, using Cauchy–Schwarz on the exterior power. Scan 64 applies this to the previously defined coefficient $k_n$.

**U11 · Exterior products of maps, scan 64.** The manuscript places the tensor product of the $u_i$ between a map out of the source exterior power and the natural map onto the target exterior power. The following norm calculation shows the factors $\sqrt{n!}$ and $1/\sqrt{n!}$ explicitly. Estimate (9) bounds the norm by $\prod_i\|u_i\|$ and extends the map to the completions. The next special case begins at the foot and continues outside these scans.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence, scans 53–58.** “Fredholm” here has the summable-singular-value, trace-class meaning. The criterion in (20), the convergent expansion in (21), and the factorization on scan 58 all describe this same class.
- **R2 · Model, high confidence, scans 56–57.** With the first entry of the Hilbert product linear, finite complex matrices give a check sensitive to the order of the adjoint. In the recorded example both traces and the entrywise scalar product equal $3-i$. The example checks the calculation only for those matrices.
- **R3 · Model, high confidence, scans 60–63.** For two orthonormal vectors the squared tensor norm of their averaged alternating tensor is $1/2$. Multiplying the scalar product by $2!$ gives squared wedge norm one, agreeing with the basis on the slip.
- **R4 · Model, low confidence, scan 64, line 10.** The frozen coefficient is a cancelled $n!$ or $1/n!$, and the label reads (8) or (7). The reciprocal square-root factors below explain the norm estimate without settling the cancelled coefficient or the label.
- **R5 · Model, medium confidence, scans 62–64.** Orthogonal unit vectors give equality in the determinant bound. This supplies the extremal example suggested by “égal à 1”. The definition and range of dimensions used for $k_n$ in the earlier chapter cannot be checked here.
