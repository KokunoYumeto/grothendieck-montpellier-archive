# K74-03 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Two operations on triangles of triads, scans 56–57 (§14).** The two indices of a vertex in the first hexagon belong to a three-element set and a two-element set. Two vertices are linked when both indices differ. Ordered pairs of distinct elements of Λ give triangles of triads. The bar on a triangle exchanges its first two indices. The tilde exchanges the last two. The manuscript identifies the resulting operations with two transpositions.

**Coorientation and symmetry, scans 57–58 (NB and §15).** From a cooriented bitriangle the NB forms a union of opposite triads. It also considers the other coorientation on the original bitriangle. Section 15 describes a triangle by a subset whose projections to the two factors are bijections. The bijection identifies a pair of fibres, and the associated permutation exchanges their corresponding elements. The author describes the group generated by these symmetries and a subgroup fixing one part.

**Containing graphs and flags, scans 58–59 (§16).** Adjoining an orbit outside a bitriangle X gives a larger graph containing X. The text describes a chain of three nested graphs by data in a product of categories of finite sets, then adds a fourth object t to the chain. For this longer chain it lists three finite sets, with respective sizes 3, 2 and 2.

**Bibases, scan 59 (§17).** The complement in S of a graph of type T₁ is called a bibase. Taking complements is said to give a bijection between the T₁ subgraphs and bibases. The resulting category is compared with the product of the categories of six-element and two-element sets.

**The six-element model, scan 60 (§18).** The vertices of the graph attached to B are the two-element subsets of B. Adjacency is disjointness. To recover B the author takes the discrete sets of five vertices, and sends each vertex to the pair of such sets containing it. A partition of B into two triples describes a bitriangle. The last sentence asserts a canonical envelope.

**Orbits of triads, scans 61–62 (§19).** The group preserving a partition acts on triads and bitriangles. Four orbits of triads are listed. One lies within a part, and three are distinguished by drawings marked I, II and III. A second hexagon writes the vertices as pairs of letters drawn from three named fibres. The two sheets differ in the distribution of I and II around the hexagon.

**Intersections and enumeration, scans 62–64.** The diagrams distinguish a doublet, a pair of triangles meeting in a vertex, and a single triangle as intersections. Scan 63 associates a bitriangle to a vertex a and an orbit choice u. With X fixed, the table gives counts for X itself, the disjoint bitriangles, and the three intersection shapes. Scan 64 returns to the type III case by a system of isomorphisms between the fibres and a drawing of the intersecting triangles.

**A triad and its hexagon, scans 64–66 (§20).** Scan 64 describes the bitriangle containing a given triad τ and deletes τ to obtain a hexagon. Scan 65 describes the inverse correspondence. It sorts the vertices outside τ by how many elements of τ they meet. The four classes are described as a hexagon, a bitriangle, an opposite hexagon and an opposite triad. Scan 66 writes a six-term equality of associated bitriangles and identifies the two triads in each of the two hexagons.

**Pointed configurations and counts, scan 67.** A labelled hexagon and two smaller diagrams accompany product counts for hexagons and bitriads. The words *bitriangle épinglé* are underlined in the prose. The following displayed equivalences among A₄, A₅ and P₆ end with the different words *bitriade épinglée*. Cancelled and replacement calculations lead to counts of pointed triads, bitriangles with a chosen triangle, and bitriangles.

**Programme, scan 68.** A separate typed Bourbaki programme lists six talks over three days in June 1976 and the dates of the next three meetings. Its speakers and attributions are recorded under Pointers.

**The shadow of a vertex, scan 69 (§§1–4).** The page introduces *pavé* with the gloss “anciennement : bitriangle”. For x outside X its shadow is the set of vertices of X linked to x. The text says this is a triad and that the shadow map is onto. Opposite and parallel triads are described by their incidence with a chosen triad. A final hexagon uses solid sides and dashed connections.

**The two complementary pavés, scans 70–71 (§§5–8).** The two families of parallel triads lead to two pavés partitioning the complement of X. Opposite triads in the remaining families are paired. Their three pairs define I₀, identified with the three diagonals of a hexagon. The canonical map to I₀ has fibres described as unions of opposite triads. Adjoining X to a fibre gives a containing T₁; the same fibres are described as orbits under symmetries.
