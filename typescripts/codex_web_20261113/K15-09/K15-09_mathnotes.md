# Mathematical notes on cote 15, scans 207–217

## What the pages do

**207a · Archimedean fibres (207).** As the complex point $x$ varies, Grothendieck asks whether the integral and real fibre objects distinguish archimedean places. An encircled marginal « faux » qualifies the assertion. He then says that the complex objects are isomorphic, without a canonical isomorphism. In positive characteristic he proposes the eventual emptiness of $T_\alpha(\mathbb R)$ and asks about points over totally real extensions.

**207b · Motivic points and paths (207, 209).** A point with values in a field or ring $k$ is introduced as an object of $T(k)$. This gives a meaning to the fibre of a motive at a point. The notes call the fibred category a torsor for the fpqc topology. The sentence continues across the intervening typescript to an interpretation of isomorphisms as « classes de chemins ». The page then discusses automorphisms, Galois action and principal bundles after choosing a point.

**209a · A list of realizations (209).** The first brace groups the $\ell$-adic and transcendental rows. They specify coefficient rings and fields and attach extra structure to the transcendental case. Separate rows name Hodge–de Rham points over $k$ and Witt points with values in $W_p(k)$. The small addition says that $k$ has characteristic $p$.

**211a · Comparisons by characteristic (211).** For a point over $k$ of positive characteristic, the first question proposes a motivic point over $W(k)$, possibly its fraction field, with $F,V$. A wavy arrow leads to generalized motivic points over $k$ with filtration. The characteristic-zero case proposes filtered motivic points over $k$ itself.

**211b · Other coefficient objects (211).** Complex points modulo conjugation are placed in correspondence with motivic points over $\mathbb R$, over $\mathbb C$ with $f_\infty$, or over $\mathbb Z$. The sheet then gives $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and $\mathbb Q_\ell$ entries, and singles out points over a finite field with $W(\mathbb F_q)$ and $(F,V)$.

**213a · The discrete motivic fundamental group (213).** For $\xi\in T(\mathbb Z)$ the note sets $\pi_1(X,\xi)=\mathrm{Aut}(\xi)=G_\xi(\mathbb Z)$. A finiteness assertion for a fixed $\alpha$ leads to a finite number of possible discrete fundamental groups. The ensuing question concerns their association with places.

**213b · Fibres over a ring (213).** The next display takes $\xi\in T_{(\alpha)}(k)$ and a motive $M$, with fibre $M_\xi=\xi(M)$. The two writings of Aut, one underlined, accompany $\pi_1(X,\xi)$ and $G(X,\xi)$. A further line compares the discrete group with $G(X,\xi)(k)$. The small marks on the two discrete-group expressions have different states of cancellation.

**213c · A Hodge realization and its image (213, 215).** For a connected $X$ over $\mathbb C$ and a point defined by a complex point of $X$, the functor has a filtration. The text discusses full faithfulness with Hodge structure and an action of $\pi_1$. The next handwritten sheet invokes weak forms of the Hodge and Tate conjectures, qualifies the condition on $X$, and asks for the image. It also mentions $k\subset\mathbb C$ algebraically closed, with $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ as example.

**215a · Real points (215).** Following the Serre heading, a closed point in the real-base case gives a fibre $\xi_a$. The condition under which these fibres are isomorphic has been rewritten. At a real point the text assigns an automorphism $\mathrm{frob}_\infty$ of order two. Together with the connected component it is said to generate $G(X,\xi)$. A functor equipped with Hodge structure and a real structure defined through $\mathrm{frob}_\infty$ is called fully faithful. The first component of its displayed formula remains difficult to read.

**215b · Variation (215).** The concluding problem asks how the Hodge structure varies with a complex point, in terms of the datum at the indicated point $a_0$. The word describing the datum is uncertain.

**217a · Maps to a moduli variety (217).** The page proposes that a morphism into the indicated polarized, rigidified moduli variety is determined by its value at one point and the induced application on fundamental groups. It asks how far that property might itself identify moduli varieties. The noun naming the objects being parametrized is doubtful.

**217b · A pointed construction (217).** The final paragraph proposes a motivic point with values in a ring $k$ as part of the structure of a scheme. Morphisms are to induce the indicated connections between $G(X,\xi)$ and $G(Y,\eta)$ and between their $k$-points, identified in the last display with the respective $\pi_1$’s.

**212a · Programme for root systems (212).** The Borel introduction describes finite root systems and Euclidean reflection groups as a framework for applications to Lie groups, Lie algebras and algebraic groups. It gives a restricted itinerary through the chapter for those applications and credits seminar notes of Jacques Tits.

**210a · Complex semisimple case, I (210).** The typescript starts from a Cartan subalgebra, defines roots and their rational span, and states the classification and automorphism assertions. It locates the exponential kernel between two lattices for the inverse root system. Their quotients describe the centre and fundamental group, and it relates the associated lattice in $V$ to representation weights.

**208a · Real semisimple case, II (210, 208).** A maximal compact subalgebra, its orthogonal complement for the Killing form, and a maximal abelian subalgebra of that complement produce the restricted roots. The continuation on 208 allows a nonreduced system and describes its Weyl group by induced automorphisms.

**208b · Algebraic-group case, III (208).** Characters of a maximal split torus and the weight spaces for its adjoint action define a root system. The text gives a condition for reducedness, describes the Weyl group as a normalizer quotient, and compares the complex and real instances with I and II.

**208c · Compact case, IV (208).** Characters of a maximal torus give the root system and a lattice $X$ with $Q(R)\subset X\subset P(R)$. The text states the two endpoint cases. This sheet breaks off after « L'algèbre de ».

**216a · Definition and consequences (216).** A marked manifold receives an inversion map and a multiplication defined on an open subset of $G\times G$. Conditions (36)–(38) prescribe inverses, identity and associativity wherever the requisite products exist. The following computation gives $\vartheta(e)=e$ and $\vartheta(\vartheta(x))=x$. The fourth domain condition and one parenthesis in the computation have visible typescript slips.

**216b · Examples (216).** A group manifold is an example. For a partial multiplication satisfying the last two conditions, the second example invokes an earlier lemma to obtain inversion near the marked point and restricts the product to a suitable open subset of $G\times G$.

**214a · Morphisms and convolution (214).** A morphism preserves the marked point, inversion, admissible pairs and their products. The sheet uses a neighbourhood of the marked point to define convolution on $T_e^{(r)}(G)$, denoted $U(G)$ with this multiplication. A morphism induces $f_*$ between the corresponding $U$’s.

**214b · Tangents (214).** The tangent maps to multiplication and inversion and the point $0_e$ give a group nucleus on $T(G)$ of class $C^{r-1}$. The projection to $G$ is a morphism. Several domains in this paragraph are visibly blank in the typescript.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High confidence, 213.** The words « nb fini de groupes » count possible groups as the point varies for fixed $\alpha$. The sheet gives no order for an individual group. The quantification in the preceding sentence makes the distinction explicit.
- **R2 · High confidence, 216.** The first equality $\vartheta(e)=e$ follows from (36) and (37). For double inversion, the intermediate products used in the written chain have the needed domains under the pair interpretation in S216.1. This checks the short computation while keeping its transcription errors visible.
- **R3 · Medium confidence, 207, 209, 213.** The passage from isomorphisms between points to automorphisms at one point explains the successive appearances of « classes de chemins » and a discrete fundamental group. The sheets provide that sequence without a formal construction joining the two passages.
- **Reading question · Low confidence, 215, functor line.** The final text starts with a wavy sign and offers $\xi(M_\xi)$, $\xi(M)_\xi$ and $\xi(M_{\mathbb C})$ for the first component. The overlap of the descender and the closing parenthesis prevents deciding the placement or identity of the smaller letter. No convention about realizations follows from that component.

- **Reading question · Low confidence, 211, caption above Pts banaux.** The transcription includes the whole underlined caption in the cancellation. The heavy loops principally cover its beginning. The final $p$ meets the underline but has little other ink through it, so the end of the old caption could have remained live.
