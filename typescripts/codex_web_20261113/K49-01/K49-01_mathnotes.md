# Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U1 · The Picard theorem, scans 003–004.** For a projective abelian scheme $A$ over $S$, the theorem identifies $\mathrm{Pic}^{\tau}_{A/S}$ with $\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{A/S}$. It asserts that this is an abelian scheme, open and closed in the relative Picard scheme, of the same relative dimension as $A$. The proof invokes torsion-freeness of the fibrewise Néron–Severi groups. The long left note proposes an argument using simplicity along $\mathrm{Pic}^0$ and flatness of a morphism φ.

**U2 · The translation map, scans 003–004.** An ample module L gives a section s of the Picard scheme. The manuscript writes φₛ(t) as the class of a translate of L tensored with its inverse. An abandoned construction with P = s + Pic⁰ precedes the surviving map to A′. On the fibre, the finite kernel and the inequalities involving the dimension of A′₀, the first cohomology group, and A₀ give equality of dimensions.

**U3 · A local flatness lemma, scan 004.** Grothendieck sets up X and Y over a locally Noetherian S and inserts a map G → F over φ. Flatness on the source and a condition on sections of the special fibre give flatness of G at y. He points to the associated graded criterion, then applies the lemma to the structure sheaves on A and A′. The statement contains several corrections, particularly in its hypotheses.

**U4 · Cohomological flatness and the exterior algebra, scans 005–006.** Corollary 1 asserts cohomological flatness of the structure sheaf and identifies its higher direct images with the exterior algebra on the locally free first direct image. The proof compares this algebra with fibre cohomology in a square. Surjectivity gives cohomological flatness. The map of locally free modules is an isomorphism on reduced fibres, completing the argument in the first line of scan 006.

**U5 · Projectivity, scan 006.** In a separate remark he asks whether Corollary 1 remains valid without the projectivity assumption. He connects that question with the existence of the relative Picard scheme and its simplicity along the unit section.

**U6 · Ampleness and components, scan 006.** Corollary 2 describes the morphism attached to an ample L as surjective and faithfully flat, with finite flat kernel. A small interlinear word may qualify the morphism itself as finite. The margin requests a necessary and sufficient condition on L. Corollary 3 asserts that Pic⁺ is open and closed and gives a fibrewise surjectivity argument. Corollary 4 states existence of an object whose name begins with underlined Nér. The short sign after it is uncertain.

**U7 · The homomorphism and its kernel, scan 007.** The homomorphism from Pic to the scheme of group homomorphisms from A to A′ has evaluation given by translating ξ and subtracting ξ. Its kernel is identified with A′. The boxed formula characterizes sections in the kernel by equality of the pullback along the group law with the sum of the two projection pullbacks. The corollary gives a closed immersion from the Néron–Severi quotient. A marginal equation writes that quotient as Pic/Pic⁰.

**U8 · Reduction to fibres, scans 007–008.** The proof considers additivity and the restrictions of the induced morphism to products with the identity section. It then reduces the assertion about the kernel of the quotient map to its fibres, using the unramified condition. The word before *réduites à un pt* reads *ensemblistement*. This leads to the case of an algebraically closed field.

**U9 · A divisor-class lemma, scan 008.** For an abelian variety over an algebraically closed field the lemma says that a translation-invariant divisor class is algebraically equivalent to zero. The author explicitly postpones its proof.

**U10 · The pullback identity, scans 009–010.** To characterize the vanishing of $\varphi_\xi$, the author makes the base change $S_1=A$ and uses the universal section $t_1$. Translation followed by the second projection gives the pullback along the addition law. The resulting difference of three pullbacks has zero relative Picard class. On the unit section two composites are labelled id and the third $\varepsilon_{A/S}$. The last sentence concludes that the sheaf in question is trivial.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · My remark, low confidence about the intended converse (003, 006).** In the displayed translation formula, replacing L by its inverse negates the corresponding Picard class. Negation of a homomorphism preserves its surjectivity. The parenthesis after *alors* and the marginal request for a condition on L leave a question about which hypotheses should distinguish ampleness. The sign calculation alone does not settle the intended statement.
- **R2 · My remark, high confidence (009–010).** The normalization attached to L, “trivial sur la section unité”, explains the final zero. The two identity pullbacks cancel, and the remaining term is the negative of the trivial unit restriction. This checks the last displayed cancellation only.
- **R3 · My remark, high confidence about the written order (007–008).** The product at the foot of 007 is A′ × A. After listing the two restrictions, 008 twice writes A × A′. Interchange of factors may be understood. The text gives no explicit interchange map.
