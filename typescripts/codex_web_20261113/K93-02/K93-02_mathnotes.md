# Mathematical notes · Cote 93, scans 151–164

## What the pages do

**151 · A stratification on a torsor.** The proposed datum is a map λ from the torsor P into a quotient of the infinite principal parts of E. Formula (27) prescribes how λ changes on translating a point of P by a section of E. Formula (28) requires horizontal values. The author puts both requirements together in (26′) and records functoriality in E and compatibility with change of base.

**152–154 · Passing to a connection.** The additive isomorphism (29) represents a class modulo δE by a positive principal part. In this representation λ becomes λ⁺, and its translation law becomes (31). Reduction to order one gives the coefficient-valued form in (32). The corollary on 153 asserts a bijection between compatible stratifications and compatible integrable connections under the stated characteristic-zero and flatness hypotheses. Taking a trivial torsor on 154 relates horizontal positive jets to closed one-forms. The exact-form example writes out the image of a section.

**154, 156 · Extending the torsor and forming its jet.** The author starts with an exact sequence of modules and an E′-torsor P′. He expressly allows E′ to be a submodule that does not inherit the stratification. Extension of the structural group gives the E-torsor P in (34). The two versions of (35)–(36) express its stratification in terms of P′. The sequence of principal parts in (37) then produces a section φ in (38) and a horizontal form of it in (38′). Some denominator strokes in these last two expressions are overwritten.

**157–158 · Picard–Fuchs operators and the Manin section.** The opening of 157 names φ the Picard–Fuchs jet. A differential operator D from E″ to F is represented by a continuous linear map on principal parts. The condition that this map vanish on vδ(E) makes it factor through the quotient in (41). Applying that factor to φ gives the global section (42), called the « section de Manin » on 158. The small universal-equation paragraph recovers φ as a particular case.

**158–160 · The connection version and its comparison.** Equations (43)–(44) give the translation and closedness conditions on a map from P′ to E-valued forms. Operators Δ in (45) kill changes of local representative. Applying one gives the section (46). The author exhibits a universal target, then composes Δ with the connection on E and passes to E″ in (47). He identifies the resulting sections in (48) and marks the equality for verification. On 160 an affine factorization lemma is used to obtain the comparison in the opposite direction under additional hypotheses.

**155, 161 · Physical break.** Scan 155 carries the printed institute letterhead. The damaged cover on 161 separates the torsor calculations from a fresh numbered section. Neither sheet contains a mathematical argument.

**162 · Filtering the de Rham complex.** For X → S → T, the sequence (1) of relative one-forms induces a filtration of the de Rham complex of X/T. Formula (2) gives its p-th graded piece using forms pulled back from S/T and the complex of X/S shifted by −p. The interlinear addition explains how the pulled-back coefficient acquires a relative differential. Flatness motivates the derived version (2′), with a qualification about its meaning.

**163 · The Gauss–Manin connection.** The author applies filtered derived direct image and a projection formula to obtain (3). The first differential between graded pieces is written in (4)–(6). Its p = 0 instance is called the Gauss–Manin connection on the derived direct image of the relative de Rham complex. The text uses (7) to claim integrability, and the marginal warning singles out the compatibility with the differential on the base that this use requires.

**164 · Cohomology sheaves and a spectral sequence.** Under the stated flatness condition the maps on cohomology sheaves take the form (8′). At p = 0 they give connections on the relative de Rham cohomology sheaves. The author sets K equal to the filtered direct image of the complex for X/T. He identifies its derived global sections in (10) and writes the filtered-complex spectral sequence (11), whose coefficient complex is indexed by α. The page ends by announcing the result of combining (3) and (10).
