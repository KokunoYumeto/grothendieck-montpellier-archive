# Mathematical notes · cote 83, scans 63–78

## What the pages do

**63–64 · Orientation and a universal map.** The opening continuation distinguishes the non-oriented icosahedral structure at \(p=5\) from the biicosahedron. A marginal NB asks how central homothety by \(-1\) acts. The main text describes a triangulated map through generators \(\sigma,\rho_f,\rho_s\), with determinant and trace conditions. Reduction modulo \(n\) leads to a claimed factorization through a quotient of \(\mathrm{SL}_2\). The margin identifies vertices with unipotents and describes an edge using a conjugate pair and trace \(-1\).

**65–66 · A line and its square class.** For an Azumaya algebra of rank four presented as \(\operatorname{End}(E)\), the manuscript starts with a nondegenerate square-zero \(u\). It associates lines \(L,D_L\) and compares \(D_L\) to \(L^{\otimes2}\otimes\underline{\omega}^{-1}\). The choice of a basis \(e\) of the determinant line gives values \(\varphi_L^e(x\otimes x)\) for generators \(x\) of \(L\). These define \(D^*(e)\), and their union over the lines defines \(C^*(e)\). The effect of \(e\mapsto\lambda e\) is recorded explicitly.

**67–68 · Classes and change of basis.** A disjoint union presents the cone as classes indexed by determinant generators modulo squares. Homotheties permute the classes. The map from \(E^*\) sends \(x\) to the class represented by its tensor square. The writer distinguishes the action induced by conjugation on endomorphisms from the action on \(E\). A determinant factor appears in the displayed comparison. The margin interrupts the account to ask what has actually been proved about two neighbours belonging to one class.

**69 · Non-emptiness.** The relation \(\operatorname{Tr}(uv)=-1\) defines the graph even before the restrictions on the algebra and on \(\operatorname{Pic}(k)\). The writer distinguishes existence of a vertex from existence of an edge. The former is connected with \(E=k\oplus L\); the latter is said to characterize the matrix algebra \(M_2(k)\). He invokes conjugacy of a standard pair.

**70–71 · Orienting a triangle.** A cancelled tensor calculation precedes the construction of an operator cycling \(x,y,z\). The sum of their three rank-one tensors commutes with it. Its trace and determinant lead to an expression in \(1-2\rho\), up to sign. The author then seeks to choose between \(\rho\) and its inverse for the unordered triangle of endomorphisms. The writer puts the verification request beside the final, live equation. See R1 and R2 below.

**72–73 · Fibres, edges and cyclic order.** The diagram compares the map to \(C^*(e)\) with the projection onto scalar classes. The fibre relation involves \(\mu_2(k)\); under its stated identification with \(\{\pm1\}\), the lower induced map is called bijective. The next page transports the combinatorial structure \(I_e\). An edge is recognized by \(x\wedge y=\pm e\), and a chosen sign orients it within a triangle. An interlinear NB states the number of triangles through an edge. The last paragraph describes a contour, an integer action and a further additive action of \(k\).

**74 · Transvection and trace.** The operation \(T_\lambda\) on neighbours is written using \(y+\lambda x\). A composition of two rank-one endomorphisms is then expressed using an exterior product. This gives the displayed negative square for \(\operatorname{Tr}(u\circ v)\) and identifies adjacency with trace \(-1\). A triangle is described by three pairwise trace conditions.

**75 · Cover.** “Azumaya rang 4” and “avril 1986” introduce a new group of sheets.

**76 · Four-letter words and a normalizer.** Three ways of grouping \(xyzt\) give three expressions in terms of a proposed polynomial \(Q\). A small diagram joins matching three-letter words in different rows. The author anticipates two relations between ten quantities. He then tries to express \(\delta(\tau h)-\delta(\tau)\) through commutators when \(\tau\) normalizes a commutative \(H\). The oblique note challenges the proposed hypothesis. See R3.

**77 · Reused typescript.** The cancelled sheet numbered “PU 93” lists oppositions under “Evolution” and “Hauteur”. One footnote compares “naissance - mort” with “vie - mort” in group II. The other distinguishes literal and figurative senses of “élévation - profondeur” and “hauteur - largeur”.

**78 · Relations in a projective matrix group.** A heading describes \(A\simeq M_2(k)\) in terms of its units modulo scalars. \(x*y\) is written with lifted representatives. The scalar \(\delta\) is expressed by a discriminant divided by the determinant. The page collects relations for powers and products, questions a product-and-inverse identity, and asks whether a three-letter value is a polynomial in six shorter values. For four elements it counts ten single and pair values against nine parameters. See R4–R6.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 70–71 · High for the computation.** For \(R=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) over \(\mathbb Q\), direct multiplication gives \(\det R=1\), \(\operatorname{Tr}R=1\), \(R^{-1}=I-R\), \(\det(I-2R)=3\) and \(R^3=-I\). These values localize the tension to the order assertion if the displayed normalization is kept. They do not decide which choice of lift was intended.

**R2 · Scans 71–72 · High.** In \(k=\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q\), \(b=(1,-1)\) squares to 1 and is neither of the two constant signs. The later hypothesis \(\{\pm1\}\simeq\mu_2(k)\) supplies exactly the restriction needed for the earlier inference.

**R3 · Scans 76, 78 · High for the example.** Use the discriminant on 78 and take the diagonal subgroup of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)\), \(\tau=1\), \(h=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\). Then \(\delta(\tau h)=1/2\) and \(\delta(\tau)=0\). This gives a specific instance of the objection “en prenant \(\tau\in H\)” to the broad proposed identity.

**R4 · Scan 78 · High.** With \(X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), the two matrices do not commute. In the questioned formula the left side is \(2\), and the right side is \(0\), using the leaf’s discriminant. Thus the displayed relation fails in general. This example does not establish its full range of validity.

**R5 · Scan 78 · High.** Put \(D=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\) and use the \(X,Y\) of R4. The triples \((X,Y,D)\) and \((X^{-1},Y^{-1},D^{-1})\) have the same six inputs, in the order of the boxed \(Q\): \((0,0,1/2,5,1/2,1/2)\). Their three-letter values are respectively \(17/2\) and \(4\). Over the stated projective matrix group, these six values therefore do not determine \(\delta(xyz)\) for every triple, even as a single-valued function.

**R6 · Scan 78 · High for the arithmetic.** The square rule is consistent in the scalar test \(D=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\): \(\delta(D)=1/2\), and both \(\delta(D^2)\) and \(\delta(D)^2+4\delta(D)\) give \(9/4\).
