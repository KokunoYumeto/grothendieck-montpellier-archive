# Mathematical notes · 157-2, scans 83–98

## What the pages do

**The construction and its landing category, 083.** Section III sets $\Phi_0(X)=\Phi rC(X)$ and $\Psi_0(X)=\Psi rC(X)$. The point of the lemma is the low zero in the two target categories. For $Y$ perfect, or merely Hot-fibred, the proposed test for $\Psi(Y)$ compares its fibres over an arrow $s\to t$ of $S$. The comparison is identified with the map from $Y_{/s}$ to $Y_{/t}$.

**Compatibility with weak equivalences, 084.** Having drawn $p$ and $\Phi_0$ in opposite directions, the author invokes the conditions of the lemma on manuscript page 11, and names $q,\Psi_0$ as the other case. For a map $f$ over $S$ he passes to $rC(f)$. He reduces the remaining verification to a comparison over each $s$ for a morphism $g$ between Hot-fibred objects. The functor letter in the last comparison is overwritten.

**The comparison with the identity, 085–086.** The two arrows $i_X$ and $\Phi(j_X)$ give a composite $\alpha_X$ from $X$ to $\Phi C(X)$ and a transformation from the identity to $p\Phi_0$. For the other composite the same underlying arrows must be read in a category of fibrations. The square over $u:s\to t$ exhibits a commutation morphism that the author explicitly says need not be invertible. That is where his attempt to use $i_X$ as a cartesian morphism fails.

**The packaging theorem, 087.** Passing to $\mathrm{Fib}_0\,S$ changes the morphisms available for the comparison. The author writes a $\beta$ with target $\Phi_0p_0$ and reinterprets $\alpha$ with target $p_0\Phi_0$. His « Théorème-emballage » concerns a full subcategory of $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$ containing any one of the three displayed full subcategories and equipped with the induced gross localizer. Its inclusion is said to induce an equivalence upon localization.

**The obstruction for split objects, 088.** An arbitrary relative homotopy type over a connected $S$ need not give a local system of fibre types. The example to keep in mind on the page is $S=\Delta^1$, where the author wants to allow an arbitrary arrow in $\mathrm{Hot}_W$. He contrasts this with the locally constant objects associated with the other categories in the diagram on manuscript page 8.

**Variance and the proposed comparisons, 089–091.** The two sides produce functors into $\mathrm{Hot}_W$ from $S^{\mathrm{o}}$ and $S$, respectively. The author singles out local coefficients and distinguishes two proposed results. One concerns the localization of the functor forgetting a splitting between the zero-subscript categories. The other concerns full faithfulness of the localization of the inclusion from the zero-subscript split category into the unrestricted split category. On 090 he considers what the intervening forgetful functors would give as retractions onto locally constant objects. On 091 he restates the two tests in the fibred case.

**The triangle and its localization, 092–093.** In the solid triangle, $i$ takes $\mathrm{Fibsc}_0\,S$ into $\mathrm{Fibsc}\,S$, while $j$ and $k$ go to $\mathrm{Fib}\,S$. A dashed arrow returns from the bottom vertex. It is identified with $\Phi_0$. The localized triangle has objects $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S)$, $\mathrm{HOT}^{?}_W(S)$ and $\mathrm{HOT}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W(S)$. The question mark is the author's notation for the category he cannot yet interpret. His conjectures concern the equivalence of $\bar k$ and full faithfulness of $\bar i$.

**What survives of the conjecture, 094.** The displayed comparison is $\bar k\bar\varphi\simeq\mathrm{id}$ on the bottom category. The author records consequences on objects and morphisms. He then allows for the possibility of discarding the question-mark category. The remaining comparison has a primed return functor defined by the composite $\bar i\bar\varphi$, and its full faithfulness becomes question 3°.

**The missing homotopy, 095–096.** The proposed equivalence leads the author to look for a homotopy between an identity endofunctor and $\varphi k$. He reconsiders the detour through $C(X)$ and suggests using $X\mapsto\Phi(X)$ on a suitable source. For variable $X$ in $\mathrm{Fibsc}_0\,S$, he asks for intermediate split fibrations depending functorially on $X$, with cartesian links in $W$. The fibre comparison ends in three question marks.

**Four tests for a map over a base, 097.** In the entry dated 15.11 the author distinguishes gross, fibrewise, local and colocal $W$-equivalence. The tests use the underlying map, its ordinary fibres, its right slices and its left coslices, respectively. The added glosses on the last two items describe changes of base by local isomorphisms and by their duals. Separate marginal notes qualify stability with respect to other base changes.

**Proposition 1, 097–098.** Part 1 gives implications from the local and colocal tests to the gross test. Part 2 considers proper, smooth and Hot-fibred objects and asserts a universal conclusion from fibrewise equivalence. In the smooth case the square on 098 compares $f_s$ with the map on the left-indexed categories, giving the equivalence of the fibrewise and colocal tests. The proper case is called dual. The proof takes up the Hot-fibred case at the last line of the final scan.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · 093–094 · high confidence in the formal consequence.** For any pair of functors $K,P$ with $KP\simeq\mathrm{id}$, $K$ is essentially surjective, $P$ is faithful and injective on isomorphism classes, and $K$ surjects on morphisms between objects of the form $P(A),P(B)$ after the indicated identifications. This uses only the displayed order of composition. The exact shape of the small Greek letter does not affect the formal statement.

**M2 · 094 · high confidence in the finite example, low confidence about the scope of the manuscript phrase.** The composite alone does not give surjectivity on Hom for arbitrary pairs of objects in the domain of $K$. For example, take $K$ from the discrete category on two objects to the terminal category and let $P$ select one object. Then $KP$ is the identity, but the Hom between the two distinct source objects is empty. The phrase « sur les Hom(-,-) » leaves its object arguments unstated. This example bears only on what follows from the displayed composite.

**M3 · 097–098 · high confidence in the diagram comparison.** Both the smooth and proper implication diagrams contain a path from b to a. In the smooth diagram the return arrow to b comes from c′; in the proper diagram it comes from c. These are the two distinctions the text makes in naming the colocal and local tests. A finite reachability check used only the drawn arrows and makes no independent assertion about the axioms of a localizer.

**M4 · 095–096 · low confidence in the exact subscript and arrow count.** The frozen reading in line 3 of 095 is $W_f$, with $W_S$ as the alternative. The low stroke is too small to decide between them. The need for cartesian arrows in $W$ is explicit on 096, but it does not determine that earlier subscript or the number of links drawn under $h$.
