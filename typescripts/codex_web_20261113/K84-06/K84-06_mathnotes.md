# K84-06 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**[U1] Trivialisations and involutions, scan 82.** With 2 invertible, the writer identifies the category of 2-trivialised torsors with that of modules. A splitting fixed by the involution gives $b=2x_0-T=0$. The sheet then compares applying the bar to either input of $x_1*x_2$ with applying it to the output. The computation uses $b_1\otimes b_2$ to express the latter.

**[U2] The surjectivity question, scan 83.** The writer asks when $E_1\otimes E_2\longrightarrow E_1*E_2$ is surjective and when differences under the joint involution generate its kernel. For surjectivity he identifies a cokernel with $\operatorname{Coker}(\pi_1)\otimes\operatorname{Coker}(\pi_2)$. After choosing splittings, he writes $\operatorname{Im}\pi_i=\mathcal O_X b_i+2L_i$ and applies a pointwise criterion.

**[U3] The kernel, scans 84–86.** Invertibility of 2 gives a decomposition into summands on which the joint involution acts by the two signs. In characteristic two the writer computes the differences on three kinds of tensors. If $b_1$ generates an invertible $L_1$, the image contains $L_1\otimes L_2$ and one further generator. The rank comparison with the kernel gives $n+1$ when $L_2$ has rank $n$. The proposition states a generation condition on $T_{10}$ or $T_{20}$ over $X_0=V(2\cdot1_X)$.

**[U4] The Hom criterion and dual algebras, scan 87.** The corollary expresses the coinvariant property by testing maps to every module $M$. Dualising gives a map from the algebra of the product to the tensor product of the two dual algebras. The displayed isomorphism identifies its target there with the invariants under the dual involutions. The writer qualifies the converse by change of base.

**[U5] The construction where the scalar vanishes, scans 87–89.** A trivialisation there is described by a section $T$, with constant map $\pi(x)=-T=b$. The product map kills $L_1\otimes L_2$. Its source quotient is identified with the fibre product $E_1\times_{\underline O}E_2$ and with the extension attached to $P_1\times P_2$. The diagram sends $(u_1,u_2)$ to $u_1\otimes b_2+b_1\otimes u_2$. Pushing the extension, or the torsor, along this homomorphism gives the product.

**[U6] Admissible forms, scans 89–90.** The oblique note on 89 specifies how a form restricts to $L$. In a splitting its value on $\lambda x+u$ is $u^2+\lambda bu+\lambda^2c$. Once $b$ has been fixed by the trivialisation, choosing the form amounts locally to choosing $c$. The main text applies this description to two quadratic covers and writes their discriminants $\delta_i=b_i^2-4c_i$.

**[U7] Coefficients of the product form, scans 90–92.** The theorem characterises a unique admissible $Q$ by its value on $x_1*x_2$. In the product splitting the boxed formulas give $b=b_1b_2$ and $c=c_1\delta_2+c_2\delta_1+4c_1c_2$. Substituting gives $\delta=\delta_1\delta_2$. The margin on 90 shows the extra augmentation factors in the formula for general sections of the envelopes. On 92 the writer returns to how the local construction is to be checked.

**[U8] Product of quadratic covers, scans 92–93.** The writer introduces an operation on covers and a map from their fibre product. He lists symmetry, associativity and a unit, describes twisting when a factor is étale, and records étaleness and flatness assertions. The opening lines on 93 finish the exception to faithful flatness at points where the two covers are both non-étale. Long diagonals cross the subsequent calculation.

**[U9] Cancelled calculation in two splittings, scan 93.** The algebras are decomposed in terms of a splitting and a dual summand. The writer starts to express the trace and norm of $u'_1$ in terms of $b_1,c_1$, writes a quadratic equation and expands the norm of $u'_1+\lambda e_1$. One expansion replaces another above the line. The calculation is struck and its last formula breaks off.

**[U10] The norm in one splitting, scans 94–95.** For a single quadratic algebra $\mathcal A$, $x_0$ identifies the dual summand with the kernel of evaluation at $x_0$. The writer pairs $b=2x_0-T$ with $u$ to obtain $-\operatorname{Tr}(u)$ and expresses $N(u)$ by pairing $u^{\otimes2}$ with $c$. Expanding $N(u+\lambda)$ gives $N=x_0^2-x_0b+c$. An evaluation translated by $v$ is then related to the equation $v^2+bv+c=0$.

**[U11] Exterior pairing and the extension, scans 95–96.** The rank-two isomorphism $E\simeq E^\vee\otimes\det E$ comes from $y\mapsto x\wedge y$. The extension identifies $\det E$ with $L$. The form obtained in this way is compared with $\psi(x)y-\psi(y)x$. Taking a local basis of $L$ and a lift of 1 determines the proportionality scalar as 1. The final two diagrams display the transposed extension and its tensor product with $L$.

## Remarks of the model

**[M1] Model, high confidence. Scans 90–91.** The boxed product formula for $c$ is compatible with multiplicativity of the discriminant over the integers. I expanded $(b_1b_2)^2-4(c_1b_2^2+c_2b_1^2-4c_1c_2)-(b_1^2-4c_1)(b_2^2-4c_2)$ as a polynomial in the four independent coefficients. Every coefficient is zero. This check is specific to that algebraic identity.

**[M2] Model, high confidence. Scan 92, lines 2–4.** The qualification on division by 4 matters even at the coefficient level. In $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$, take $b=0$. The choices $c=0$ and $c=1$ give the same $4c$ and the same discriminant. Those two values alone cannot recover the chosen coefficient in that ring.

**[M3] Model, high confidence. Scan 85, lines 21–26.** The final $b_1\otimes b_2$ changes from a positive term in the three-line list to a negative term in the characteristic-two brace. The coefficients $1$ and $-1$ have equal residue modulo 2, so that difference does not alter the stated reduction.

**[M4] Model, low confidence. Scan 95, line 3.** The frozen reading is $Q(v)=0$. A possible expanded argument is $Q(x_0+v)=0$. Scan 94 introduces $x=x_0+v$, and the polynomial on scan 95 contains the constant term $c$. The author may simply be evaluating in the translated affine coordinate $v$. This remains a reading question.
