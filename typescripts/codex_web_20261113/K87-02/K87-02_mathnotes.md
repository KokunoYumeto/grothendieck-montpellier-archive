# Cote 87 · K87-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**G15.6 · Scan 015, Cor. 6.** For a face \(F\) of a convex polyhedron, every integer from \(-1\) to \(\dim F\) occurs as the dimension of a face below \(F\). A cancelled induction is followed by the chain argument from Cor. 5. The bracket chooses the empty face and \(F\) as endpoints.

**G15.7 · Scan 015, Cor. 7.** Between two comparable elements of a strict combinatorial polyhedron whose dimensions differ by two, there are exactly two intermediate elements. The interval is identified with a one-dimensional polyhedron, and the argument uses the two ends of a segment.

**G18 · Scans 018–019.** Conditions a)–d) describe an ordered family of affine subspaces. It has bottom and top, and its proper part is controlled by maximal members in opposite pairs. For a nonempty member, the maximal members above it give a partial section of the orbit map \(B\to I\). A dimension condition compares the combinatorial dimension, the rank of \(E\) and half the cardinality of \(B\). The note above the last lines distinguishes an order-theoretic infimum from actual intersection. The quadrilateral on 019 illustrates the distinction. Condition e) requires opposite hyperplanes to be disjoint.

**G20 · Scans 019–020.** Taking the directions \(H_i\) of parallel hyperplanes leads to one-dimensional quotients \(T_i\) and affine torsors \(E_i\) with distinguished pairs \(B_i\). For one pair, the kernel and the fibre of \(\varepsilon\) at 1 recover the linear and affine pieces. For the family, the corresponding map is labelled « tr », with a handwritten « trace » gloss. The final construction with \(J\subset I\) breaks off.

**G22 · Scan 022.** Over a commutative ring in which 2 is invertible, one category has direct sums of lines \(E_i\), each with a two-element subset \(B_i=-B_i\) whose elements are bases. The other has \(2n\) elements with a free involution. The two constructions take the union of the pairs and reconstruct the lines by contracted product with \(k\). A circled sequence and the formula \(\lambda_{-b}=-\lambda_b\) in the opposite margin give two descriptions of the resulting module.

**G23 · Scan 023.** Multiplication \(xy\) gives the pairing on each line. The sheet writes the orientation object of the sum as \(\omega_E\simeq\omega_I\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in I}B_i\). It invokes associativity and identifies \(B_i\) with \(\omega_{E_i}\) using the map from distinguished bases to orientations. A tilted note asks to drop the index \(i\).

**G24.ranks · Scan 024, upper half.** Closure inclusion orders the faces. The supremum of lengths of ascending chains ending at a face gives its combinatorial dimension. The successive ranks and the relations \(R_i\) between neighbours recover the order. The opening exclusion of the maximal face raises an indexing question below.

**G24.faces · Scans 024–025.** Nonempty cube faces correspond to partial sections over \(J\subset I\). A selected element of an orbit fixes a coefficient at \(+1\); its partner then has coefficient \(-1\). The distinction between closed and open faces is expressed by weak and strict inequalities on the remaining coordinates. The next sheet tests when two points admit extensions past both ends of their joining segment inside the cube. This gives equality of the saturated coordinates and their signs.

**G25 · Scan 025, corollary.** Extension of partial sections reverses the order of faces. The empty section gives the maximal face; a section on one orbit gives a submaximal face. Two distinct submaximal faces fail to meet precisely when the corresponding elements belong to the same orbit.

**G26 · Scan 026.** An exercise generalizes the construction to a surjection \(p:B\to I\). Maximal partial sections are full sections. The elements just above the minimum identify with \(B\). For distinct \(b,b'\), the sets of full extensions \(C_b,C_{b'}\) are disjoint precisely when \(p(b)=p(b')\). This recovers the quotient \(I\) and the projection up to isomorphism. The corollary phrases the recovery in terms of categories and explicitly says « pour les iso ».

**G27.grp · Scan 027.** The sheet defines the real coordinate cube and its affine copies. It describes their connected groupoid in terms of torsors under \(W_n\). A proposition identifies the affine symmetry group of the cube with the linear symmetries of \(B_n=\{\pm e_i\}\) and then with the permutations of \(B_n\) respecting the involution. The sideways box describes the ambient space as odd real-valued functions on \(B\) and the cube by the bounds on those functions.

**G27.eq · Scan 027, corollaries.** The category of convex \(n\)-cubes is related to the category of their « épures ». The last corollary gives the oriented version. Both statements revise an earlier wording involving « combinatoires ».

**G28.rel · Scan 028, upper half.** After a cancelled endpoint condition the sheet describes faces through open intervals in a convex set. Its relation on \(x,y\) allows equality or extension of their line segment in both directions. It asserts that the resulting classes are convex faces and partition \(C\). The segment and the labelled triangle accompany the argument for equivalence.

**G28.poly · Scan 028, lower half.** For a convex polyhedron the propositions give finitely many faces, a unique maximal face equal to the relative interior, and a decomposition of the closure of each face into faces. The face itself is the unique one of maximal dimension in its closure. Item ④ asserts existence one dimension down. Item ⑤ describes \(C\) in terms of the convex hull of its extreme points. Item ⑥ mentions a barycentre; the conclusion is too crowded to state reliably. The corollary says that equal closures determine equal faces.

## Remarks of the model

- **M25.square · High.** As a finite check on G24.faces and G25, the standard square has nine nonempty faces. Its nine coordinate patterns with entries free, \(+1\) or \(-1\) give the opposite inclusion order in all 81 pairwise comparisons. This tests the stated correspondence in dimension two.
- **M26.fibres · High.** For a surjection with fibre sizes \(1,2,3\), the six full sections satisfy the recovery criterion on all 36 ordered pairs in \(B\). The test includes a singleton fibre, relevant because the initial lower bound on fibre size is struck through.
- **M27.square · High.** In the square, \((1,0)=\tfrac12(1,1)+\tfrac12(1,-1)\). The other three signed coordinate vectors are likewise edge midpoints. The four choices of corner signs give the extreme points. This refutes the extreme-point identification already at \(n=2\).
- **M28.rank · High.** Under the stated nonempty convention, item ④ has a meaningful lower-rank conclusion for \(d\ge1\). The singleton test detects the missing endpoint qualification.
- **M24.top · Low.** The insertion excluding the maximal face on 024 sits beside a hooked mark that might be a question. The later coproduct includes rank \(n\). The notes may be trying both conventions.
- **M28.interval · Low.** The phrase « tt intervalle ouvert … qui rencontre F est contenu ds F » alone does not isolate a single class. In the closed unit interval the set consisting of both endpoints meets no open segment contained in the interval. The partly cancelled « ss / ssi / si » could make the preceding formulation stronger. This observation applies only to the displayed one-way test.
- **M28.halfspaces · Medium.** The side note « int. finie de demi-espaces fermés… » may need a boundedness qualification if intended as equivalent to the finite convex-hull description. A closed ray is a half-space in a line and is unbounded. The terminal dots leave the qualification open.
- **Reading questions arising in the notes.** On 019, line 13, the frozen \(H_i\) has a reinforced upright which also permits \(\mathbb H_i\). On 020, line 6, the frozen conjectures « ens orienté / squelette / ens orient. » share the position of a deep q-like descender. « maquette » is another possible reading of those strokes. On 024, line 2, the frozen insertion ends at « maximale »; a hooked upstroke before the comma may mean « maximale ? ». These are low-confidence questions about the ink.
