# Mathematical notes on cote 51, scans 13–23

T-numbers refer to lines of the individual LaTeX fragments. Quoted French retains the manuscript’s abbreviations. The short identifiers connect the observations to `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv`.

## What the pages do

**The subgroup example, scan 13.** Two finite subgroup schemes have trivial intersection in the primed formula and nontrivial intersection in the unprimed one. An elliptic curve supplies the example. The author maps B diagonally into the product of its two quotients. An oblique addition considers equal characteristics using the p-th power on Lie(B).

**Extending subgroups, scans 13–15.** In characteristic zero, a closed subgroup of the generic fibre is related to an abelian subgroup over the base. Two classifying schemes supply local properness and unramifiedness. Under a relative Hilbert-scheme hypothesis, the theorem on 15 asserts unique multiabelian extension over a connected normal base. The margin considers weaker hypotheses.

**Hom and rational sections, scan 16.** The Hom analogue is proposed without a characteristic restriction. A bracketed lemma extends rational sections of finite unramified morphisms over a locally noetherian unibranch base. Its proof passes to an integral birational situation and counts geometric points of fibres.

**Generating maps, scan 17.** A rational map from a projective model’s generic fibre generates an abelian variety. The author uses a generic curve, its Picard variety, and a surjection onto the target to obtain good reduction. The argument about injectivity in the middle is cancelled.

**Pic and Alb, scan 18.** The author identifies Alb as the dual of a primed subgroup of Pic. After an unramified extension a section gives a map to Alb. The preceding proposition applies, followed by an isogeny argument for Pic. The superscript-like correction on the first Pic is unresolved.

**The relative Picard scheme, scan 19.** The theorem seeks a unique abelian subgroup with prescribed underlying set P. Properness of P leads to two questions about the relative Picard scheme. The author defines a functor of closed subgroup schemes over varying bases. The margin limits the proof to characteristic zero.

**Representability, scan 20.** The Hilbert scheme of P/S supplies a parameter space. The subgroup, abelian and underlying-set conditions are discussed separately. Rigidity enters the description of the geometric fibres. Properness of the projection is reduced to a valuative criterion.

**The valuative argument, scans 20–21.** A rational section over a discrete valuation ring supplies a generic abelian variety. Koizumi’s theorem gives good reduction. Murre–Chow enters the extension of a morphism to A→P. The dimension argument for its image carries a marginal warning.

**The kernel, scan 22.** Having identified the image as P, the author studies the kernel. Its special fibre is asserted to be a p-group. A first argument, with a small triangle, is cancelled. The surviving prime-to-p argument finishes the characteristic-zero case and leaves the other case open.

**A regular base, scan 23.** Items a)–d) concern projectivity, a translation group and its homogeneous space, extension from the generic point and the codimension of the exceptional set. The cohomology display compares global and local quotients. A question about purity for good reduction closes the page.

The union of all connected components exhausts each fibre, while the enclosed note specifies Pic⁰. Confidence in an omitted qualifier is medium. The sheet does not supply the extra word.

**Reading questions.** On scan 16, T18, the frozen reading is “fini non ramifié”. In the enlarged word there is a small loop before the tall f-like stroke, so “affine non ramifié” is another possibility. The lemma explicitly says fini earlier. On scan 17, T12, the frozen first reading V in “courbe générique dans V” may be X, the source of the rational map. On scan 19, T33, the transcribed G might stand for the ambient relative Picard group after base change. These are reading or reference questions of low confidence. No error of French agreement is assigned to the author.

## Remarks of the model

**R13 · Model remark · high confidence.** The choice of the product of the two quotients explains why the two intersections are written down. The kernel of the diagonal map into that product is the intersection of the two quotient kernels. The evidence is the paired quotient formula immediately before “OK.”

**R18 · Model remark · high confidence.** The phrase “par la proposition précédente” on scan 18 identifies the role of the generating-map statement on scan 17. The map actually named on 18 has source `X_K` and target `Alb⁰(X_K)`, and the next line says it generates that target. The later isogeny sentence is a separate step.

**R22 · Model remark · high confidence.** Two limits on the proof belong together. Scan 19 says it has only been proved in characteristic zero. Scan 22 identifies the image, then asks about the kernel and explicitly closes `p=1`. Its assertion that the special kernel is a `p`-group still permits a nontrivial kernel when `p>1`. This accounts for the qualification on 19 without treating the struck argument on 22 as a proof.

**R23 · Model remark · medium confidence.** The two uses of “th. de pureté” play different roles. An oblique line links the first to d), about a codimension-one complement. A brace puts the second inside the final question about abelian schemes. The intended analogy is visible; its precise hypotheses are unstated.
