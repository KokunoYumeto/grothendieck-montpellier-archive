# Mathematical notes · K93-13

## What the pages do

**U1 · Operators on closed cochains, scan 221.** An arrow from a quotient of the sheaf of sections of \(G\) lands in a quotient of degree-one cocycles. The sheet then factors a differential operator \(D\) from \(\underline g\) through \(c=d^0\) and a lifted operator \(\widetilde D\). The last diagram starts with \(g^{-1}dg\) and circles the label on the composite. The question in the left margin stands beside the factorization.

**U2 · Integrals between two sections, scans 222–223.** For a smooth analytic map, the page chooses fibrewise arcs from \(a(s)\) to \(b(s)\) and integrates a closed relative one-form. A local primitive gives holomorphic dependence on \(s\). Changing the arc adds a period on \(Z_s\), as in (1). A family \((D_i,\omega_i)\) defines a Picard–Fuchs equation when the sum in (3) vanishes for every continuous family of cycles. Under that condition (4) defines \(\mu(a,b)\) independently of the paths. Equation (5) is the addition law for three endpoints.

**U3 · A sufficient condition and the endpoint calculation, scans 224–227.** The quotient \(\Phi\) in (6) receives the classes \(\omega'_i\). The proposed condition (7) is \(\sum D_i\omega'_i=0\). In the presence of an integrable connection on \(X/S\), lifted operators give a closed form \(\bar\omega\), locally of the form \(d\varphi\) under (7). The argument passes from cycles in (11)–(12) to horizontal endpoints in (13). The two sides of the latter can be evaluated using a local primitive. Equation (14) expresses \(\mu(a,b)\) for horizontal sections as \(\varphi(b(s))-\varphi(a(s))\). The long cancelled calculation (8)–(9) on scan 224 precedes a new (8) outside the cancellation.

**U4 · Periods of classes and the converse, scans 227–229.** A further proposed vanishing formula is first written for paths with horizontal boundary and then with endpoint values. The argument returns to cycles \(Z_s\). A boundary has zero integral around a cycle, so the expression on scan 228 can be considered on classes. Boxed (15) relates the derivative of a period to the action on the class. The author explicitly admits (15), then invokes the de Rham theorem on the fibres for the necessity of (7).

**U5 · Calculation with moving endpoints, scans 229–230.** For nearby endpoints the notes propose choosing a local connection that makes the chosen sections horizontal. For general sections, introduce horizontal \(a_0,b_0\) through their values at \(s_0\). The decomposition of \(\mu(a,b)\) separates the horizontal pair from the terms for \(b_0,b\) and \(a_0,a\). In the next display the operators act on those two integrals before evaluation at \(s=s_0\). A crossed-out continuation contracts the differential of a section with a pulled-back relative form to obtain a one-form on \(S\).

**U6 · Differential operators and exactness, scans 231–232.** A formally exact sequence of first-order operators is followed by two sequences of sheaves of differential operators. One composes on the left and one on the right. For locally free stratified \(E\), the formal Poincaré lemma is assumed for its relative de Rham complex. The ensuing leftward sequence compares operators of successively lower order with linear homomorphisms. The kernel at \(\mathrm{Diff}^i(E,P)\) is named \(\mathrm{Diff}^i_+(E,P)\). Scan 232 gives a quotient presentation and places a question mark at a proposed map into additive homomorphisms from \(\ker d_E^1\).

**U7 · Principal parts and the quotient by a submodule, scans 232–233.** For \(N\subset E\) and \(M=E/N\), the notes ask which differential operators on \(M\) become constant-coefficient operators on \(E\). They characterize them by linear maps out of principal parts of \(M\) killing the image of \(\rho_i:E\to p^i(M)\). The displayed quotient \(\Phi_i\) represents this condition for variable target \(P\). The pages describe a strict inverse system and the limit map \(\rho_\infty\). Its composite with augmentation is required to be an epimorphism. Further integrability conditions are mentioned.

**U8 · Two liftings and a homotopy, scan 234.** A new numbered section starts with two smooth prolongations of \(X_0/T_0\) over a “1-nilpotente” immersion. The sheaf of germs of isomorphisms between them is a right torsor under the sheaf of automorphisms of the second. The latter is expressed using relative one-forms and the pulled-back ideal \(I\). The page records the resulting maps on de Rham complexes. Interior product supplies a degree-minus-one map, and the closing formula reads \(v(g)-\mathrm{id}=d k(g)+k(g)d\).

**U9 · Passage to a morphism in the derived category, scans 235–236.** For two complexes on a ringed space, the data now include an abelian sheaf, a right torsor and three maps into Hom complexes. An extension of \(\mathbb Z\) by the abelian sheaf has the torsor as its fibre over 1. The extension supplies a two-term complex. The square on scan 236 describes its map to the Hom complex, with components in degrees \(-1\) and \(0\). Tensoring with \(\mathcal O_X\) and applying derived sections gives the boxed arrow from \(\mathbb R\Gamma_X(\mathcal O_X)\) to \(\mathbb R\mathrm{Hom}(K',K'')\). The last lines pass to degree zero and a morphism in the derived category.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 223 and 230 · High confidence.** The signs of the three terms in the decomposition on scan 230 agree with (5). Writing each term as the difference of its two formal endpoint values gives zero net coefficient at both intermediate endpoints. This is a check on that line alone.

**R2 · Scan 236 · High confidence for the domain comparison, medium for the proposed correction.** In the bracket the restriction along \(i:G\to\bar G\) has source \(G\). The same bracket assigns \(v\) to \(G\) and \(u\) to the torsor. The written \(u\) in \(\varphi^0 i=u\) therefore raises a concrete question about the label, independently of the partly overwritten tensoring formulas below.

**R3 · Scans 227–228 · Low confidence.** The lower endpoint display on scan 227 ends in zero. Earlier on that sheet, (14) gives a difference of primitive values for horizontal endpoints. The page does not state an equality of those two values there. The word following “1-” is doubtful and affects how to read the boundary condition. I would leave the relationship between these two assertions open.

**R4 · Scans 235–236 · Low confidence.** The annotations at the foot of 235 appear to put \(G,\bar G\) at \(\mathcal L^0,\mathcal L^1\). The square on 236 puts their images in \(\mathscr H^{-1},\mathscr H^0\) with correspondingly labelled vertical maps. The first degree label on 235 is faint and the marks beside it are doubtful. The sheets do not explain a shift. This is a question about the reading of the degrees.

**R5 · Scans 234–235 · Low confidence.** On 234 the left side of the homotopy formula reads \(v(g)-\mathrm{id}\). The abstract version on 235 starts \(u(g)\) and the second coefficient looks like \(h\) or \(k\). The pages do not settle whether the author is changing notation here. Both lines are retained as read.
