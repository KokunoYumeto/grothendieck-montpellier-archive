# K111-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**W1 · The functors \(S\) and \(T\), scans 2–3.** The author associates a semi-simplicial set \(S(C)\) to a category. He describes local systems on it as functors that invert every arrow, and their cohomology by derived inverse limits. This gives a cohomological test for \(S(C)\to S(C')\) to be a homotopism. He reads the same test in the topos associated to \(C\). In the other direction \(T(X)\) is the category of simplices over \(X\). Quillen’s comparison of the composites with the identities gives quasi-inverse functors on the two localized categories.

**W2 · Passage to the opposite, scans 3–4.** A second comparison identifies \(C\) with \(C^o\) after localization. On a local system it keeps the values at objects and inverts the values on arrows. The application is a sheaf interpretation of cohomology with a contravariant local system. A pencilled query beside the cochain formula and a separate “Si !” qualify the discussion.

**W3 · A derived criterion, scans 4–6.** The question is when pullback gives an equivalence of the bounded derived categories whose cohomology sheaves are locally constant. The proposed extension to topoi imposes a local triviality condition on both sides. The proof sketch passes through local systems, fundamental groups, and a spectral sequence for Ext. The author lists three formulations, a), b) and a′), and asks separately how far abelian coefficients suffice. The continuation considers enriching the derived category by its local systems, cohomology and tensor product.

**W4 · Multiple categories, scans 6–7.** A category internal to categories is described by its objects, arrows, two projections and composition. Its bi-semisimplicial description imposes an exactness condition along rows and columns. The example uses the categories of strings of arrows in \(C\). Exchanging the two directions gives a symmetry. The description specializes to 2-categories when the category of objects is discrete.

**W5 · Picard categories, scans 8–9.** The commutative case of the construction attributed to Dold gives length-one complexes. Objects of the associated Picard category come from \(C_o\), and an arrow from \(x\) to \(y\) is specified by \(z\) with \(dz=y-x\). The pages distinguish strict group objects, choices of associativity isomorphisms, and the data that survive homotopy equivalence. The qualifications to the commutative assertion in the left margin are difficult to read.

**W6 · From bicomplexes to higher group objects, scans 9–10.** Applying the construction in two semi-simplicial directions produces a bicomplex supported on the small square drawn on scan 9. The author identifies the resulting strict abelian group objects in 2-categories with complexes of length two. He asks for a description of homomorphisms in terms of RHom and proposes a corresponding statement in length \(n\).

**W7 · Candidates for \(K_i\), scans 10–11.** Starting with an associative tensor product, the author proposes a universal Gr-category and then higher Gr-categories whose homotopy groups would define \(K_n(C)\). He discusses the extra information wanted for an abelian category. An additive category illustrates how the naïve \(SC\) can be homotopically trivial. The handwritten postscript asks how semi-simplicial homotopy groups compare with the groups read inside a Gr-\(n\)-category.

**W8 · A universal pair for bordism, scans 12–14.** A covariant functor and a contravariant one agree on objects and satisfy a formula for transverse fibre products. Bordism of manifolds over \(X\) gives addition by disjoint union and multiplication by transverse fibre product. A handwritten note supplies the relative-dimension grading. The author takes \(B(X\times Y)\) as the morphisms of a correspondence category. To factor another pair, he assigns to a span the composite through its middle object. Doubling a bordism and comparing its two fibres over the circle is the argument for invariance.

**W9 · Coefficients, twists and internal Hom, scans 14–15.** The coefficient algebra is \(\Lambda=B_\bullet(\mathrm{pt})\). The manuscript compares bordism with cohomology over the field of two elements, discusses a lift, and describes a category of graded free modules. The circle supplies the twist \(\Lambda(-1)\). Making the twist invertible leads to internal Hom and to all finite-type graded free modules. A further passage to a generated abelian category is proposed.

**W10 · A homological version, scans 15–17.** The author considers changing the equivalence relation on maps of manifolds. After cancelling a construction with all cohomological correspondences, he takes \(S(X)\) to consist of homology classes of submanifolds and uses \(S(X\times Y)\) for correspondences. Stable cohomology operations, their graded dual, and an affine group scheme \(G\) enter the description. The condition on \(x\) is expressed both as invariance and as annihilation by \(A^+\). The author asks what part of the representation category this recovers.

**W11 · Proper maps and orientations, scan 17.** In the extension to manifolds that need not be proper, the covariant operation is restricted to proper maps. A stable orientation is described by a homotopy lift involving \(H\) and \(\mathrm{BO}\). Morphisms are represented by oriented spans with a proper map to \(Y\). The last lines mention supports proper over \(Y\), a Thom-style description of bordism groups for compact \(X\), and an exposé by Karoubi.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 9–10 · High confidence.** I counted three positions labelled by \(C\) in the surviving bicomplex on scan 9. They carry \(oo,1o,11\) as read in the diagram. The first sentence on scan 10 identifies the complexes as length two. This comparison establishes which diagram the sentence picks up. The categorical equivalence is the author’s assertion.

**R2 · Scan 12 · High confidence.** I followed both directed paths in the diamond. The left path goes from \(X'\) to \(X\) under \(g'\), then to \(Y\) under \(f\). The right path goes from \(X'\) to \(Y'\) under \(f'\), then to \(Y\) under \(g\). The edge labels can be read independently of the ambiguous punctuation-like mark in the formula below.

**R3 · Scan 14 · High confidence.** I followed the two interlinear qualifications to their insertion points. The doubt about multiplication attaches to the lift in the opening sentence. “Quillen ignore si” belongs to b), which concerns Gysin. The intervening assertion about the tensor-product isomorphism has its own line.

**R4 · Scan 14 · High confidence.** I compared the degree bounds in the last paragraph. The first has the single stroke \(<0\). The later condition on free modules has the extra lower stroke \(\leq0\). Between them the text explicitly mentions a single degree-zero generator for connected \(X\). This is a difference of ink and wording. Its mathematical resolution is left open.

**R5 · Scan 15 · High confidence.** I checked the two successive descriptions against their verbs. The text says “on trouve maintenant” when it reaches finite-type graded free modules after inverting \(\Lambda(-1)\). For the category generated under abelian operations it instead says “on s'attend à trouver”. The handwriting above “type” supplies “présentation”. That distinction matters when quoting the two identifications.

**R6 · Scan 17 · High confidence.** I checked the order of the two spans against “le deuxième étant propre” and the ensuing phrase “propres sur \(Y\)”. Both refer the properness requirement to the map ending at \(Y\).
