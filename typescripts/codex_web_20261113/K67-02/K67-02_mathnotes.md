## What the pages do

**19–21 · A letter about conformal pants.** The writer asks whether the discs removed from a sphere to make a pair of pants are determined by their radii. He divides the sphere into three “orange slices” centred at \(0,1,\infty\) and takes a concentric disc \(\lambda_i Q_i\) in each slice. Nondegenerate discs have \(0<\lambda_i<1\). The model would give \(r_i=\lambda_i\rho_i\) and a product description of the parameter space over \(\widetilde{\mathrm M}\). Its relation to the three hyperbolic boundary lengths is the question put to the recipient.

**24–25 · Data on a sphere.** After the cover comes a list. Its entries combine compatible real structures of opposite types, a spherical metric and a great circle, a circle with extra structure, a compact torus and one of its orbits, and a pair of points with an orbit of the torus fixing them. There is no connecting argument on the sheet.

**26 · A separate calculation.** The coefficients of \(u_+\) and \(u_-\) are \(x+\tfrac12 y\) and \(\tfrac12 y\). Below are the five objects \(0,\Pi,V,E,0\), a cohomology comparison starting at \(H^1(\gamma,E)\), and the quotient \(\Pi^\gamma/(1+\sigma)(\Pi)\). The second cohomological degree looks like 2 and remains marked. The exponential map stands apart at the bottom.

**27–28 · Hyperbolic type.** Scan 27 characterises a hyperbolic surface by its universal cover, by maps from the complex line, and, in the finite-puncture case, by \(2g+\nu\geq3\). It lists the sphere, affine line, multiplicative group and elliptic curve as exceptions. Its corollaries concern open subsets and interiors of surfaces with boundary. Scan 28 names the real projective plane with or without a puncture and quotients of real genus-one curves without real points. A new heading introduces the hyperbolic metric of curvature \(-1\).

**29–31 · Flat and spherical metrics.** Translation invariance and total area one single out the flat elliptic metric. On \(\mathbb C^*\) the author instead fixes the length of the unit circle to one. He discusses descent of both metrics to nonorientable surfaces. For the complex affine line, the translation-invariant compatible metrics form a class up to positive scale. On the real projective plane an antipodism supplies spherical data on the orientation cover. On the complex projective line the full automorphism group leaves no Riemannian metric invariant even up to scale, according to the text.

**32–33 · Prescribing symmetries.** A compact subgroup of conformal automorphisms supplies an orientation-preserving subgroup \(H^+\). Its lift preserves a positive Hermitian form. When that form is unique up to scale, the normalised spherical metric is invariant under the normalizer. In the case of two fixed points, the proposed parameter is an orbit of the circle group \(T\) away from those points. The selected orbit is the great circle with the two fixed points as poles.

**34–35 · Antiholomorphic symmetries.** The text distinguishes an involution with fixed points from an antipodism, then considers extensions of a circle by a group of order two. The last page uses coordinates with the two poles at zero and infinity. For the interchanging maps \(z\mapsto b/\bar z\), it identifies the ambiguity between an inversion and an antipodism by changing the sign of \(b\).

## Questions and conjectures

- **19.** “My expectation is” concerns uniqueness from radii and admissibility under individual bounds. The writer says agreement with his earlier operation would require infinite limiting radii. His orange-slice model uses finite ones.
- **20.** “This model, if correct” qualifies the proposed inclusions between pants. “Maybe it is unreasonable to expect” concerns whether \(\ell_i\) depends only on \(\lambda_i\). The writer asks whether the spherical model is “likely to hold, or is defintely false”.
- **21.** “what about the relation” asks how the hyperbolic metric changes when an open collar is removed. “it would be nice to have an explicit expression” asks for a metric in the multipliers.
- **28, 33.** Scan 28 opens the question of canonical metrics “sans bord pour commencer”. Scan 33 places “à vérifier” beside the comparison of metrics invariant under \(H^+\) and \(T\).

## Remarks of the model

**R35 · My calculation, high confidence, scan 35.** Composing the pole-exchanging map with itself gives multiplier \(b/\bar b\). When \(b\) is real and nonzero, its fixed set satisfies \(x^2+y^2=b\). The positive sign gives a circle of radius \(\sqrt b\). The negative sign gives an empty fixed set. These checks use the displayed formulas, independently of the doubtful abbreviation following “involutif”.

**R30 · My reading question, low confidence, scan 30, approximately line 11, after the orientation cover.** The frozen reading is “et on trouver encore”. Alternatives are “et on trouve encore” or “et en trouver encore”. The final short stroke can belong to an r or to the exit from e, and the preceding little word consists of two joined humps. The intended grammar cannot decide either detail.
