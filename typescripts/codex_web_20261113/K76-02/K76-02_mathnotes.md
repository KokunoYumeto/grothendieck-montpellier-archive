# Mathematical notes · cote 76, scans 19–32

## What the pages do

**19–20 · A flag geometry and its cells.** The degree sets $D[n]$ give a quasi-simplicial set. Distinct injections of a smaller standard interval into a larger one act distinctly on a fixed simplex. The last diagram relates this description to orders on flags. On the next sheet Grothendieck forms $X=|D|$, assigns a closed simplex to each $d$, and groups simplices by the supremum of their vertices to form the pieces $|F|$ and $|F|^\circ$.

**21–22 · The ordered set of simplices.** Condition 1 expresses when the category fibred in discrete sets over the injection category is an ordered set. The closed piece below $d$ has the face pattern of the nonempty subsets of an interval. Its vertices form $I_d$. Compatible total orders on the $I_d$ encode the ordering of the vertices in each quasi-simplex. The closing diagram singles out the maps from degrees 2 and 1.

**23–25 · Recovering a single vertex order.** A directed edge defines a strict comparison of vertices. Condition 2 bis lifts a composable pair of edges to a triangle. The calculation follows its third edge, giving transitivity. A marginal argument uses condition 1 to exclude a loop. The converse starts with an ordered set of faces and an order on its vertices and recovers a quasi-simplicial set by restricting the vertex order. The remark reverses that order and compares the two realizations.

**26 · Flags in an ordered set.** In « Ex 1 », the faces are finite totally ordered subsets, ordered by inclusion. Singletons recover the original vertices. The added condition $d=\mathrm{Sup}(I_d)$ leads to the description by simplices of a « maquette simpliciale ». The last, heavily revised paragraph compares this with existence of bounded finite suprema.

**27–29 · Orbits and gluing.** In « Ex 2 », $N_H$ is generated by the generators whose indices lie outside $H$. Inclusions of index sets give restrictions between the orbit sets $D(H)=X/N_H$. Grothendieck asks whether two compatible two-index faces lift to a three-index face. His lemma proves surjectivity for two overlapping index sets whose intersection is final in one and initial in the other. The proof cuts at the last index of the first set. Generators from opposite sides commute, which gives the stated product of subgroups.

**30 · Two questions in general.** Part a asks for hypotheses on the ordered data that imply the gluing surjection. Part b asks whether a cell decomposition, with conical structure, recovers the set of faces and the two orders.

**31–32 · Flags with finite types.** A finite nonempty subset $A$ of the natural numbers is a type. The flags $D(A)$ of each type have restriction maps. Their disjoint union $\Theta(D)$ is ordered by restriction and graded by $|A|-1$. The construction has a monotone map to the set of finite types. Under the finiteness assumption the realization has a finite triangulation. Finally an increasing injection of standard intervals, followed by the increasing enumeration of $A$, selects a subset $B$ and gives a restriction $D(A)\to D(B)$. At the foot of scan 32 the definition continues into a clipped restatement.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Model, high confidence (23–25).** Condition 2 bis is used in one direction in the displayed proof. It supplies a triangle for two compatible edges; scan 30 a) asks when such lifts exist more generally. No equivalence between transitivity alone and all the gluing surjections is asserted here.
- **M2 · Model, medium confidence (27, compared with 22 and 31).** Reading the coproduct in 27 as indexed by *all* subsets creates an empty-type component. For the trivial group on a one-point set with $n=0$, the element of type ∅ lies strictly below the singleton-type element. The manuscript’s assertion about minimal elements therefore needs a convention omitting the empty type on this interpretation (M2-nonempty). Scan 31 explicitly adds $A\ne\varnothing$.
- **M3 · Model, high confidence within the finite check (29).** I checked the split of the generator indices for every admissible ordered pair $H,H'$ with $0\le n\le7$, 4,608 pairs. The left piece lay in $K'$, the right piece lay in $K$, and every index in one piece was at distance at least two from every index in the other. This checks the set calculation over that range. The manuscript supplies the group argument.
- **M4 · Model, medium confidence (25, 9–20).** The remark calls the reversed order $\omega^\circ$, while the final realization carries $\omega'$. The homeomorphism is introduced just after the two orders are compared, which suggests that the prime refers to that reversed order.
- **Reading question · Model, low confidence (32, last line).** The final source has `(B : u_A σ(Δ_n)` with n doubtful. One could read an equals sign, as in the full formula higher up. At the edge only the beginning of the last small index survives and the closing punctuation cannot be checked there.
