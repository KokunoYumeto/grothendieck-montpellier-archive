# What the pages do

**Affine frame and involutions (083–084).** A flag R and points s₀,…,s₃ are related to three involutions. Two sketches accompany the coordinate frame eᵢ=sᵢ−s₀. The action of σ₁ on the fourth point introduces λ, μ and ν. The next page expands the involution condition, considers its two field-valued possibilities, and chooses ν=1 when σ₁ is to be a reflection. It then introduces α and α′ through λ=−(1+α)=α′. Some intermediate signs and the slanted marginal argument remain doubtful in the frozen reading.

**Products and a three-cycle (085–086).** The notes set u=σ₀σ₁ and v=σ₁σ₂, calculate s″₃ and the images of s′₃ and s″₃ under u, and record a three-cycle and u³=id. They state that v⁵=id is equivalent to α²+α−1=0. A presentation of G and an identification with the automorphism group of a combinatorial icosahedron follow. The question then becomes whether the affine orbit of a flag has exactly the intended incidences.

**Counting vertices (086–089, including the short continuation on 087).** Assuming G has order 120, the stabilizer argument makes the number of vertices divide 12. Six coordinates are displayed and compared, excluding the degeneracy α=−1 on a nonempty base. The remaining possibilities are six and twelve. The six-vertex possibility is related to a stabilizer containing the antipodism, motivating calculation of the opposite vertices and of a centre.

**Root identities (090).** Six rows collect identities involving α and α′, including their sum and product, reciprocal expressions, and products of shifted factors. The sheet contains no accompanying proof or prose.

**Centre and invertibility (091).** The fixed-point equations of the three generators are written out, several centre proposals are cancelled, and formula (15) gives the surviving coordinates when 2 is invertible. A note rewrites the quadratic equation as α(α+1)=1 and discusses invertibility of further factors. The characteristic-two centre is tentatively described as a point at infinity.

**Antipodism and characteristic two (092).** A proposition identifies antipodism with symmetry about the centre when 2 is invertible and gives a formula intended to cover all characteristics. Its characteristic-two description is followed by a discussion of the quadratic field over F₂ and of an F₄-structure on S. The field-name revisions in the NB are among the least secure readings; the quotient F₂[T]/(T²+T+1) is considerably clearer.

**Antipodal coordinates (093–094).** Formula (18) lists images of the six known points under the underlined a on music-ruled stationery. The next sheet contains another six-row calculation with the same label, wholly crossed out. Both the live table and the abandoned calculation are preserved, including small local cancellations.

**Position and incidence tests (094–095).** A proposed assertion about three and four vertices is heavily qualified by insertions and by the marginal objection about characteristic two. Three line tests are then considered: through s₀ and its antipode, through s₀ and s₁, and through s₀ and s″₃. The following page declares the tests true. It restricts the harder plane question to four distinct points of which three form a face, and expresses that case as the nonvanishing of the other vertices’ z-coordinates.

**Invariant quadratic functions (096).** Starting with a general quadratic polynomial, the page uses invariance under σ₂ to identify coefficients and expands substitution by σ₀. Crossed invariant summands, coefficient comparisons, inserted corrections and abandoned rewrites accompany the calculation. The surviving final expression is f(x,y,z)=ax(x+y+z−1)+b(y²+z²)+uyz+μ(y+z). No further condition from σ₁ is computed on this sheet.
