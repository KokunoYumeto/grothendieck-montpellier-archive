# Mathematical notes on scans 325–340

## What the pages do

**Scans 325–326, manuscript pp. 241–242.** The excerpt opens inside a proof about the categories $i'\backslash I$. For a family of objects indexed by $A$, with $\operatorname{Card}A\leq\pi$, the author chooses a common target in $I'$, then uses conditions on $f$ to obtain arrows $w_\alpha$ in $I$. The displayed equality makes $f(w_\alpha)u'_\alpha$ independent of $\alpha$. A second argument starts with parallel arrows, uses an equalization in $I'$, and invokes faithfulness of $f$ to conclude $vu_\alpha=vu_\beta$. The equalizing arrow is then interpreted in $i'\backslash I$. Paragraph 4.9.6.10 records the ordered case.

**Scans 327–328, pp. 243–244.** Section 4.10 recasts the representation theorem for a cofibration $\mathscr D\to J$. The fibres $M_j=\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi}(\mathscr D_j)$ form the category over $J$ denoted here by $\mathscr N$. The author passes from the inclusion of the original fibres to a functor between categories of sections. In the constant case he writes the comparison as $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(J,C)\to\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(J,M)$. Fibrewise computation of the stated limits and $\pi$-accessibility of the objects coming from the source give a fully faithful functor $\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi}(\underline{\Gamma}(\mathscr D/J))\to\underline{\Gamma}(\mathscr N/J)$.

**Scans 328–329, pp. 244–245.** Theorem 4.10.1 asserts that this last functor is an equivalence. The conditions include a cardinal bound involving the reduced $J$, a limit ordinal $\alpha$ with $\operatorname{Card}\alpha\leq\pi$, and representability in the fibres of inductive limits indexed by $I_\alpha=\{\beta\mid\beta<\alpha\}$. The displayed transition functors are required to commute with those limits. On scan 329 the letter $\alpha$ also labels a morphism of $J$.

**Scans 329–331, pp. 245–247.** Corollary 4.10.2 adds $\operatorname{Card}J\leq\pi$. For a section $\varphi$ of $\mathscr N$, the author states that the faithful comparison from $\underline{\Gamma}(\mathscr D/J)_{/\varphi}$ to the product of the fibrewise slices is cofinal and that its source is “grde devant $\pi$”. He introduces $\mathscr D_\varphi\to J$, describes its fibres and transition functors, and identifies its category of sections with the slice of the section category. The resulting comparison takes a section to its family of values.

**Scans 331–332, pp. 247–248.** Corollary 4.10.3 formulates this comparison for $\mathcal E\to J$. The hypotheses bound $J$, require the fibres to be equivalent to small categories and large relative to $\pi$, and impose an ordinal-indexed colimit condition preserved by base change. A bracketed passage gives two ways the latter condition can arise. One uses countable filtered colimits. The other uses $\pi_0\leq\pi$ and $\pi'_0>\pi_0$, with colimits large relative to $\pi_0$ and of cardinal at most $\pi'_0$. The conclusion again concerns both cofinality and the size condition on the source.

**Scan 333, p. 249.** Lemma 4.10.4 says that $C\to M=\operatorname{Ind}_{\pi}C$ preserves existing inductive limits of cardinal at most $\pi$. For $x$ presented as such a limit and $X$ presented by an $I'$-indexed system, the written Hom calculation interchanges an inductive limit over $I'$ and a projective limit over $I$. The citation “SGA4 I 9.8” sits over the equivalence making that interchange. The final expression is the projective limit of the $\mathrm{Hom}(x_i,X)$.

**Scans 334–335, pp. 250–251.** Lemma 4.10.5 uses conditions a) and b) of 4.10.3. Condition F1 asks for a section $G$ and arrows $X(j)\to G(j)$ for a given family of fibre objects. F′1 adds a section $F$ and maps $F(j)\to X(j)$, and asks that the composites define a morphism of sections $F\to G$. The lemma reduces the desired cofinality and source condition to these two statements. The slanted marginal note on 335 also compares them with unindexed F3 and ordinary filteredness.

**Scans 335–338, pp. 251–254.** The direction developed in the proof is $\mathrm F1\mathbin{\&}\mathrm F'1\Rightarrow\mathrm F3_\pi$. Starting with fibrewise maps $F_\alpha(j)\to F(j)$, the author draws naturality squares marked with an exclamation point. For fixed $j'$, he bounds their number by $\operatorname{Card}\operatorname{Ob}J_{/j'}\times\operatorname{Card}A$. Postcomposition with $v(j')\colon F(j')\to X(j')$ makes the required comparisons agree. An application of F′1 gives maps $w(j)\colon X(j)\to G(j)$. The triangle on 337 and the diagram on 338 finish the argument for the composites to $G$.

**Scans 338–340, pp. 254–256.** Paragraph 4.10.6 starts the construction needed for F1 and F′1. The author temporarily sets aside the optional $F(j)\to X(j)$, puts $X_0(j)=X(j)$, and introduces maps from $X_\alpha(j')$ to $X_{\alpha+1}(j)$ above an arrow $\lambda\colon j'\to j$. On the last sheet, (i) compares the identity-arrow case with the transition at $j$. In (ii) a further transition equalizes two routes across the marked square. In (iii) another boxed relation compares the route for $\mu\lambda$ with the successive routes for $\lambda$ and $\mu$. The supplied pages end at this equation.

## Remarks of the model

**R1. Model remark, high confidence about the endpoints.** In the triangle on 337 the two dashed legs pass through $X(j)$. Reading the component arrows immediately above, the target of $v(j)$ is the source of $w(j)$, and the outside endpoints are $F(j)$ and $G(j)$. This checks the typing of the written $w(j)v(j)$. The separate assertion that these components form a morphism of sections comes from the application of F′1.

**R2. Model remark, high confidence about the diagram on 338.** The vertical arrows are attached to the $F_\alpha$, $F$ and $G$ columns. The two $X$ objects have no vertical map between them. This matters when reading “comm.”: the author can compare the routes from $F$ to $G$ without first specifying a transition on the family $X(j)$.

**R3. Model remark, medium confidence.** Paragraph 4.10.5 explicitly sets aside hypothesis c) “pour l’instant”. The next construction is indexed by ordinals, but scan 340 still concerns successor stages. The limit stage is a plausible place to look for the role of c). Its use is beyond the end of the supplied argument.
