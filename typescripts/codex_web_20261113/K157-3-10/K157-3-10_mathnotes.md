# What the pages do

**146 · Factorization.** The sheet opens inside an argument. Grothendieck compares the relative component objects for $f$ and for its composite with the lower local isomorphism α′. A triangle gives the canonical factorization of $f$. Its dashed leg continues from V′ to U′. The final equation factors α′f through the same intermediate object.

**147–148 · An attempted converse.** He proposes forming U′ as the amalgamated sum of U and V′ over V. The problem is whether the new map remains a local isomorphism. His example uses a group of order two, a map out of $E_G$, and the quotient G′/H′. The induced comparison fails the stated bijectivity test on objects. He then asks how to separate an asphericity condition on a presheaf from a condition on its category of elements.

**148 · Proposition 9a and its corollary.** He considers a category with a final object and representable presheaves. The proof factors a map from V through its relative component object. Its corollary identifies the image of a final object under the first factor as final. An oblique second argument uses an inductive limit and the functor $f_!$.

**148–150 · Proposition 9b and its rectification.** The proposed gluing assertion starts with opens V′ and V″ covering V and assumes total asphericity for both opens and their intersection. He asks what the direct image would have to preserve for the argument to work. On 149 he tries imposing retractions. The square on that sheet distinguishes the inclusions and the four return maps. Scan 150 carries the construction through direct-image and slice squares. Its margin gives an objection.

**151 · Corollary 2.** He applies the proposed gluing argument to the category with two arrows leaving one object. The two open subcategories are represented by intervals and their intersection by a point. The later “Faux” annotates the conclusion of total asphericity. The longer slant reopens the question of which categories can furnish examples.

**152–153 · A criterion for a counterexample.** He seeks a map $f:X\to Y$ satisfying the relative-component condition a), with X aspherical and Y failing asphericity. Étale coverings give a restriction on the fundamental group. He therefore looks for nonzero higher homotopy. Scan 153 discusses some small target categories and then takes the category of paths in Y with fixed initial point $y_0$.

**153–154 · Proposition 10.** He compares the fibre over $y_0$ with the loop space Ω(Y,y₀). He takes Y to be 1-connected with higher homotopy and illustrates the choice by a six-object ordered category for the two-sphere. He records the resulting distinction between asphericity and total asphericity of the path category. The second note on 154 gives a shorter, fork-shaped example.

**155 · Returning to an open immersion.** The diagram has U→X and U′→X′ in its two rows, with singleton complements on the right. By arranging final objects in X and X′, the preceding example gives the requested difference between i and i′. Grothendieck then changes the question to p and q.

**156–158 · Producing p ∈ W and q ∉ W.** He again makes t final in X, imposes asphericity on U and U′, and seeks failure for X′. He explicitly assumes a corollary from p. 85. He then reduces the construction to a local isomorphism Y→U′ and builds U′ by adjoining a final object to Y. Proposition 11 recapitulates the example with W∞.

**158–159 · §2.4 and Theorem 3.** The new section treats open Dwyer immersions. For the cocartesian square it imposes a condition on the presheaves $i^*(t)$, also expressed with the slice categories U/t. In the final-object case he relates this to a right adjoint and to Dwyer’s open immersions. The two displayed conclusions compare membership of i with i′ under W(7 bis), and p with q under W(7).

**159–160 · Corollary 1.** He calls the qualifying open immersion a W-cofibration. The statement spells out the effect of a further change of base on a map lying in W. The proof applies Theorem 3 to the induced immersion and says that its hypothesis persists.

**160–161 · Definition 2.** Part a gives terminology for functors in terms of presheaves and slice categories. Part b applies it to open immersions and uses opposites for closed immersions. The adverbs in both parts have been revised in the manuscript. Parts c and d define W-cofibrations and trivial W-cofibrations, the latter also called “W-équivalence couniverselle”. Two notes record stability properties.

# Questions and conjectures

- **147–148.** « Mais je crains que ce ne soit pas vrai en général » introduces the attempted converse. The question turns into a search for an example « où elle est satisfaite » while the indicated slice fails total asphericity.
- **148–149.** « Sans doute faux tel quel » and « je doute fort à présent que l’énoncé b) soit vrai, sans hyp. supplémentaires » concern the two-open gluing assertion. « Mais peut-être faux également » is written beside the amendment.
- **150–151.** « faux déjà dans le cas où … » and « Faux » dispute the proposed application to the fork. The lower note on 151 ends « Je conjecture … que ce sont les seules ! ». The intervening cancellations make the scope of “les seules” uncertain.
- **151–152.** « il faudrait quand même s’assurer » that asphericity alone does not always give total asphericity. The next sheet records the time spent finding the example.
- **154.** « On peut se demander si Y … asphérique implique que … » ends « J’en doute fort ! ». The unverified implication concerns total asphericity of the path category when Y is already aspherical.
- **155–157.** « On aimerait aussi trouver un exemple » where p ∈ W but q ∉ W. The ensuing construction ends « On a gagné ! ».
- **159.** « Je ne connais pas d’autre cas où le th. … » qualifies the stated range of examples. The slant also mentions categories with a final object, filtered categories and p. 112. The relation between several of these phrases remains a reading question.

# Remarks of the model

- **M151-fork · High confidence.** Relabelling the fork in the first drawing by 0↦1, a↦0 and b↦2 gives exactly the two arrows in the parenthetical drawing on 151. This identifies the drawings independently of the doubtful handwritten category letter.
- **M154-count · High confidence.** In the six-vertex diagram on 154, each of the levels 0, 1 and 2 occurs with both signs. Counting the six distinct labels agrees with « à 6 sommets ». The count concerns the labelled vertices.
- **M159-wording · Low confidence.** In the horizontal conclusion on 159, the frozen word after « elle est » is « totale|cohér ». The strict/total terminology on 160 makes a tot- beginning plausible there, but the crossing on 159 prevents a choice. This is a question about the reading of that sentence.
