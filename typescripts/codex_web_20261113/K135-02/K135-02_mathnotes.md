# Mathematical notes on cote 135, scans 017–024 and 026–030

References give scan numbers. Line numbers count the lines following the transcription comment in the individual page files. The notes follow the order of the manuscript, including its change of subject at 026.

## What the pages do

**017 · An obstruction in degree three.** The page starts in mid-sentence with a Picard category and a splitting condition. Grothendieck changes the auxiliary presentation ending in $M$ and passes to a universal situation over $B$. He places $w(\sigma)$ in an $\operatorname{Ext}^3$ group and concludes that it vanishes. The construction of $B$ is outside this extract.

**018 · A Picard 2-category.** For sheaves $M,N$ on $X$, Baer composition organizes the objects under discussion. An exact sequence describes their equivalence classes. The next two groups concern self-equivalences and automorphisms of the identity functor. A final construction uses locally isomorphic pairs $(L,L')$ and a quotient by a diagonal $N$.

**019–020 · Splittings of a Gr-category.** A diagram asks about a correspondence with 2-gerbes on $B_G$. The proposed gerbe assigns to a $G$-set $X$ a 2-category, giving the splittings of $C$ at the final object. A cancelled choice of representatives and cochains precedes the explicit reduced split category $C_0$. The next sheet describes splittings as a torsor and computes classes and automorphisms in cochain notation.

**020, lower half · A category over a $G$-set.** The proposed 0-objects include $L(x,g)$ and an isomorphism from $L(x,g)\otimes L(gx,g')$ to $L(x,g'g)$. The two margin sketches record the projection, action and succession of points. Higher arrows of this particular construction do not appear here.

**021 · Extensions by a group.** An arrow $u$ of $\Gamma$ receives an $M$-bitorsor $P_u$. A pair of composable arrows receives a product isomorphism. Three arrows give a pentagon. The sheet proposes invariants involving the action and a noncommutative $H^2$. It then starts the analogous definition with $M$-gerbes in place of bitorsors.

**022 · Cancelled calculations.** A draft juxtaposes two diagrams of successive face maps, a list $H^2,H^1,H^0$, and triples $(L,M,\varphi)$ equipped with a multi-input pairing. The triangles and two columns of products track different compositions. The diagonals across the sheet make the entire calculation provisional.

**023 · Coherence for 2-extensions.** The pentagon now carries a 2-cell $\alpha_{u,v,w}$. Four composable arrows require another compatibility. The text asks that the isomorphisms built from the $\varphi$’s and canonical associativities form a transitive system. A note allows a Picard 2-category as coefficient data.

**024 · Arrows between extensions.** With $\alpha$ fixed, compatible families $\lambda(u)$ form the 1-arrows. The ratio of two families obeys a twisted cocycle equation. The sheet specifies 2-arrows using $m$ and writes a proposed coboundary formula along $x\xrightarrow{u}y$.

**026–027 · Items 1, 2 and 2′.** A programme on higher categories introduces $i$-fidelity and $n$-equivalence. Hom objects valued in presheaves lead to equivalent-looking functor descriptions. Evaluation at a final object gives the identity condition, and composition suggests the maps in (b) and (c). The continuation discusses homotopies, isotopies, Kan objects and a semi-simplicial description of strict higher categories.

**027–028 · Items 3–6.** Grothendieck proposes studying fibred $n$-categories and $n$-stacks on sites and topoi, including passage to the associated topos, stackification and inverse image. He then lists depth along a closed subset, base change and Lefschetz-type statements in étale and complex-analytic settings.

**028 · Item 7.** Locally constant $n$-stacks test a morphism of topoi using the maps on sections. Taking all $n$ and retaining the passage between neighboring levels suggests a way to encode the homotopy type of a topos. The author asks about completion and constructibility.

**028–029 · Items 8–9.** The list adds Gr-, Picard and strict Picard structures and their behavior under direct and inverse images. It relates strict Picard $n$-stacks to complexes with cohomology in $[0,n]$, mentions truncation for direct image and internal Hom, and asks about modules over a ring and derived tensor products.

**029–030 · Items 10–11 and the final question.** Semi-simplicial groups are suggested as examples for higher Gr-stacks. The next item assigns the sheaves $\underline K_i(A)$ as invariants of an enveloping Picard $n$-stack. The final paragraph singles out non-strict Picard $n$-stacks and distinguishes the known-to-the-author $n=1$ case from $n\geq2$.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High confidence, 021 and 023.** The two pentagons have corresponding objects and boundary maps. The second includes an $\alpha_{u,v,w}$ between the two composites, making visible where the 2-extension adds one level of data.
- **M2 · Medium confidence, 028 and 030.** Item 9 explicitly inserts «stricts». The last question singles out «pas stricts». The restriction explains why the proposed derived-category description in item 9 leaves the last question open.
- **M3 · Low confidence, 019 and 020.** The tensor formula with second entry $m+m'$ may suppress a group action in this split model. An action is mentioned elsewhere and reappears as $gm-m$, but the hypotheses connecting the two calculations are abbreviated. I propose no change of sign or convention.
- **Reading question · 028 L39 / 029 L1.** The final transcription joins «pleine des / la catégorie dérivée». The last stroke on 028 could instead close «de», giving «pleine de la catégorie dérivée». That reading would resolve the grammar. The two source files retain their final hashed text.
- **Reading question · 019 L3, L41 and 020 L3.** The final reading is «splittings». In the longer cursive forms the ending can also look like «splittages», especially the descending loop before the final letters. These places were unmarked at freeze.
