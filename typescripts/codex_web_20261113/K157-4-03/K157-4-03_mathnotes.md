# What the pages do

**Definition 1 (scan 34).** Grothendieck fixes a pair \((M,W)\) and four classes of arrows \(C,TC,F,TF\). He gives lifting, factorization and stability conditions, followed by inclusions relating the four classes to \(W\), \(\operatorname{Cof}_W\) and \(\operatorname{Fib}_W\). Exactness adds \(W=TF\circ TC\). The separate left and right conditions are \(TC=C\cap W\) and \(TF=F\cap W\). A diagonal NB discusses passage between these last conditions under hypotheses about direct factors. Part of its conclusion is overwritten.

**Saturation, closure and perfection (scans 35–36).** Either one-sided exactness condition is said to imply exactness. Remark b) discusses saturating the classes while retaining the axioms. Définition 1 bis calls a structure exact on both sides a structure de Quillen, and gives four lifting-class equalities for a closed structure. “Parfaite” combines biexactness with closure. The next sheet gives a sufficient condition for perfection and associates it with strong saturation of \(W\).

**Fibres, cofibres and localization (scans 36–37).** The Scholie treats the \(TC,F\) pair as a starting point for homotopy fibres and gives a dual discussion of cofibres. It associates the weak structure with the equivalence \(M_{cf}W_{cf}^{-1}\longrightarrow MW^{-1}\), where the bracket identifies \(M_{cf}\) as the full subcategory of objects both fibrant and cofibrant. The next page asks when each arrow between such objects in the localization is represented by an arrow in \(M\). It attributes surjectivity of the Hom map (a) to M2 bis.

**One-sided homotopy and fractions (scans 37–38).** Under left exactness, the discussion uses right Q-homotopy, a fully faithful right adjoint to localization and a calculus of left fractions. The dual paragraph uses left Q-homotopy, a fully faithful left adjoint and right fractions. The upper qualifier on \(W\) in the first discussion and the corrected lower qualifier in the second occupy different positions in the ink.

**Tests using a cylinder or path object (scans 38–39).** For a biexact structure the two homotopy relations are said to coincide. The manuscript allows a cofibrant source and a fibrant target and emphasizes “n’importe quel” in both the cylinder test at the source and the path-object test at the target.

**Exact squares and diagrams of a fork (scans 39–41).** Grothendieck first distinguishes cartesian from cocartesian squares under one-sided exactness. He then brings in the category drawn with arrows from \(0\) to \(1\) and \(2\), assuming weak exact structures both for \(M\) and for diagrams of that shape. The text relates this hypothesis to representing morphisms in the corresponding localization and to long exact cosuspension sequences. The dual paragraph treats cocartesian squares and, for pointed \(M\), suspension sequences. These observations motivate treating all diagram categories \(M(I)\) together.

**Constructing the classes for diagrams (scans 42–43).** Right closure prescribes \(F(I)=(TC(I))_*\) and \(TF(I)=(C(I))_*\). Left exactness prescribes \(TC(I)=C(I)\cap W(I)\). Formula (5) defines \(C(I)\) by evaluation at each object and (6) records the corresponding rule for \(TC(I)\). The next sheet organizes the remaining requirements under (i) factorization, (ii) stability, and (iii) inclusions involving the lifting classes.

**Lemma 1 and the reduction to (7) (scan 44).** Objectwise membership in \(\operatorname{Cof}_W\) or \(\operatorname{Fib}_W\) suffices for the indicated global membership. For \(W(I)\), the manuscript underlines “f et s”, giving both directions. It then reduces the remaining inclusions to a question about evaluating the right lifting class of a class of diagram arrows.

**Lemma 2 (scans 45–46).** The composite \(\alpha_t^*\alpha_{s!}\) is described by a coproduct of copies indexed by \(\operatorname{Hom}_I(t,s)\). Part a) gives the evaluation inclusion for a class stable under the requisite coproducts, assuming these coproducts exist. Part b) applies it to the two classes used in (iii). It treats finite Hom-sets first and then gives a small-coproduct hypothesis for the unrestricted case.

**The outstanding factorization (scans 46–48).** The text singles out factorization for the diagram pairs. It invokes the theorem of n° 1, considers accessibility and ordinal colimits, and adds small systems of generators in the margin. A functorial factorization in \(M\) suggests a simultaneous one, but defining all four classes objectwise leaves a lifting problem. The text says that \(TF(I)\) and \(F(I)\) must be made smaller.

**Proposed theorem (scan 49).** The assumptions include weak saturation of \(W\), small inductive limits and finite projective limits in \(M\), stability of \(W\) under filtered inductive limits, and accessibility of \(M\). Conditions a), a′), a″) and b) successively discuss a one-sided exact and one-sided closed structure, small generating classes, and inclusions between lifting classes. The last parenthesis draws a consequence of b). No conclusion relating all four conditions appears before the packet ends.

# Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 37.** “Il semblerait que la relation de Q-homotopie à droite …” introduces a marginal question about its being an equivalence relation under the weaker hypothesis. A second “il semblerait” concerns its comparison with left homotopy and the choice of cylinder. Both the object subscript and the cited name have doubtful letters.
- **Scan 39.** “je doute qu’il en soit de = pour les carrés W-cocartésiens” questions the other kind of square. The marginal “Il semble bien … le cas” refers back to manuscript page 30. Later, “il semble qu’on ne peut en conclure l’exactitude” concerns exactness without either one-sided hypothesis.
- **Scan 40.** “je doute que … résultat … soit exact” belongs to an oblique, apparently cancelled note. The live paragraph calls representation of morphisms “une propriété apparemment très délicate”.
- **Scan 41.** “je ne sais comment vérifier l’exactitude” introduces the practical difficulty of obtaining exactness except through a left or right condition.
- **Scans 46–48.** “Il reste, pour être heureux, à vérifier la condition de factorisation”; “je vois peu de chances”; “je ne vois guère d’autre moyen de s’en tirer”. The issue is passage to diagram categories and the strength of the stability hypothesis on \(W\).
- **Scan 49.** “encore un peu conjectural” qualifies the heading itself. The adjacent mark can be read as a question mark or as 2.

- **R1 · Remark of the model · high confidence about the membership calculation.** In scan 42, (6) combines the objectwise tests for \(C\) and \(W\) with the intersections in (3) and (4). A finite check on zero through three objects gave the same result for “every component belongs to both classes” and “every component belongs to \(C\), and every component belongs to \(W\)” in all 85 assignments. This check concerns the membership calculation alone.
- **R2 · Remark of the model · high confidence about the order of arguments.** Scan 45 writes \(\operatorname{Hom}_I(t,s)\) in the large formula, in the small explanation underneath it and in Lemma 2(a). The repeated order supplies a direct reason for retaining \(t,s\). A claim about the author’s variance convention would require evidence beyond that repetition.
