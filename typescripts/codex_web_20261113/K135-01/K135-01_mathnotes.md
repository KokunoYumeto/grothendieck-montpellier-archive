## What the pages do

**Product and twist (scans 3 and 5).** The author regards a group Γ as a discrete category and forms its product with a Gr-category C. He gives the two homotopy groups of the product and the pullback of its class k along the projection. For a Picard category he varies the commutativity constraint while holding the associativity data fixed. The corresponding form φ has diagonal Σφ(x) = φ(x,x). In item 5 he twists Γ × C using a form on Γ. Its value on ((α,x),(β,y)) is φ(α,β), as the diagram expresses.

**The diagonal lemma (scan 5, item 4).** Given Σ from M to the 2-torsion of N, the page constructs an antisymmetric bilinear form with diagonal Σ. In the vector-space reduction the author chooses a basis, sets the off-diagonal coefficients to zero, and sets the diagonal coefficient to Σ(eᵢ). The inserted corollary applies this to changing the commutativity constraint and making a Picard category strict.

**Representations of a semi-simplicial complex (scan 8, item 1).** Objects are assigned to 1-simplices and tensor-product isomorphisms to 2-simplices, subject to a pentagon on 3-simplices. The induced α and the action on π₁ give a local system. With K, the category and α fixed, the author describes the representation 2-category as a 2-torsor with acting invariants H², H¹ and H⁰ of K in those local coefficients.

**The gerbe attached to a Gr-category (scans 8–9, items 2–3).** For a G-set X, the author uses the category with objects X and arrows G × X, together with the projection to G. Its representation category gives the value of a 2-gerbe on X. He states the covariance in the Gr-category and an equivalence of 3-categories for fixed group and module. The categories on one side have specified invariants, called “épinglées” on the page.

**Morphisms and the relative version (scans 9–10).** Compatible homomorphisms on the group and module specify the allowed tensor functors. The author compares their 2-category with maps of 2-gerbes after change of coefficients and base. He places the obstruction to an object in degree three and writes it as a difference of images of classes. Over a topos S the gerbes are relative to S, with a trivialization there.

**Mac-Lane’s “noyaux” (scan 11, item 5).** Starting from a map to the outer automorphisms of F, the author uses the Gr-category of F-bitorsors and an inverse image to construct a Gr-category with π₁ = Z(F). He relates its trivializations to extensions of G by F realizing the prescribed map. The obstruction is the degree-three class he associates with Mac-Lane.

**Classification with fixed invariants (scan 12, c–f).** For Picard categories with specified M,N, the comparison uses their maps σ from M to the 2-torsion of N. The page gives Hom(M,₂N) as the classification group. It describes equivalences as a torsor and gives Ext¹ and Hom in the marginal addition. Under Baer multiplication it describes the 2-category at fixed σ and its relation to strict Picard categories. Part f) begins the passage to triples.

**Picard structures on an underlying category (scan 13, item 6).** The author first compares the 3-categorical description with a 2-category obtained by retaining the first two levels, taking the next up to isomorphism and forgetting the last. For strict Picard categories he points to Deligne and to truncated derived Hom. He then relates existence of a Picard structure to splitting and to the vanishing of k, and distinguishes the torsor of structures from the sub-torsor of strict structures. The suffix of the first Bil group has been overwritten.

**Triples and their morphisms (scan 14).** The triples are (M,N,σ). The author compares isomorphism of functors with equality of the induced homomorphism of triples. For a given homomorphism, he describes a groupoid of lifts as a torsor under a Picard category constructed from derived Hom. A marginal NB singles out the groupoid structure.

**Picard stacks and lifting a map (scans 14–15, item 7).** Over a topos, two functors with the same map of triples need no longer be isomorphic, even locally. The page distinguishes the global and local Ext terms and describes the stack of lifts as a pseudo-torsor. The obstruction to finding a functor is in Ext² globally. The local obstruction is written as a section of the sheaf Ext².

**Realizing a triple (scans 15–16).** At fixed M,N,σ the author takes a difference of two Picard stacks and places its invariant in Ext². He then asks when a triple comes from a stack. An antisymmetric bilinear lift of σ gives a sufficient condition. In the construction using a quotient L → M, restriction to the kernel R yields a degree-two class considered modulo an image. Its image in Ext³(S;M,N) is named above the line. The supplied sheet ends inside the proposed condition for its vanishing.

Scans 4 and 6 carry the English song “LONELY ROAD”. They interrupt the mathematical sequence between scan 3 and scan 8.
