# K157-3-11 · Mathematical notes

The references below use archive scan numbers. U, S and R identify passages for the check-event register. The cancelled draft on 171 and the overwritten figures on 173–176 are identified where they affect interpretation.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 162.** Corollaire 2 treats an open W-dewyérienne immersion. Under the indicated W(7) hypothesis, the conditions for a trivial W-cofibration are expressed first as membership of the immersion in W and then as membership of the inclusion from dotted T to T. The author refers sufficiency to theorem 3 a).

**U2 · Scans 162–163.** Two open questions follow. The first asks whether a W-cofibration among open immersions must satisfy the dewyérienne condition. A special case involving T isomorphic to the final category leads to a criterion using the immersions obtained by the construction of proposition 7 b). The second question, continued at the top of 163, seeks a notion applicable to all immersions.

**U3 · Scans 163–164.** The proof of theorem 3 compares the test presheaf at t′ with the direct image of the one at t. A lemma then passes total W-asphericity through a direct image. The parenthetic variant concerning a category over a presheaf runs onto the next sheet.

**U4 · Scan 164.** Corollaire 3 applies the result to changes of base of an open or closed immersion. A bracketed note distinguishes the assertion itself from the equivalences marked with circled a and b, for which W(7bis) and W(7) are named.

**U5 · Scans 164–165.** Stability under change of base leads to a discussion of composition and Dwyer immersions. The author wants workable conditions for an immersion to be a W-cofibration. For later applications he wants a class of open immersions that also admits a specific construction with the endpoints of an alternating interval. He tests this demand on an inclusion into Δ¹.

**U6 · Scan 166.** Section 2.5 begins with a W-proper category over a base with a final object. Proposition 11 asserts that total W-asphericity of the final fibre implies that of X, with the connectedness axiom W(5) stated. The proof is postponed while the functors assigning homotopy types and then connected components to fibres are introduced.

**U7 · Scan 167.** Proposition 12 identifies the set of components of X with the directed colimit over I of the components of its fibres. The maps from the fibres to X induce the displayed bijection. A slanted note proposes a W₀-proper hypothesis.

**U8 · Scans 167–168.** Passing to opposites leads to a presheaf description. The proof writes the structural map to the final category as a composite, expresses the components of a category over a presheaf by a direct image, and computes the direct image of an arbitrary presheaf on I by a colimit.

**U9 · Scans 168–169.** For an additional functor p from X to Y, the author computes the presheaf obtained by applying p! to the terminal presheaf. The three cartesian squares on 168 identify the relevant fibres over i after fixing y. Corollaire 1 assembles the resulting fibre presheaves into a functor on I and gives a canonical map from their colimit to the presheaf on Y.

**U10 · Scan 170.** For a split cofibration the transition along α from i to j is made explicit. A transformation from pᵢ to the composite of pⱼ with transport along α gives the first map in the final composite. A bracket under its target supplies the map towards the terminal presheaf on the j-fibre. The author flags the first arrow for fuller explanation.

**U11 · Scan 171.** Corollaire 2 specializes the result to a base with a final object i₀. The final-fibre direct image maps isomorphically to the direct image on X. The author immediately uses this to return to proposition 11.

**U12 · Scan 171.** Corollaire 3 gives the product example. If Z is totally W-aspherical and I has a final object, the same property is asserted for Z × I. A subsequent draft about the product as a cofibration is cancelled, including its last displayed assignment.

**U13 · Scan 172.** Section 3 starts with two maps out of X₀. The opposite indexing shape is drawn explicitly with arrows from 1 and 2 to 0. The author describes a split fibred category over that index and writes an integral around the original span. He proposes regarding its total category as a homotopy amalgam or homotopy inductive limit.

**U14 · Scans 173–174.** The class of the total category is identified with the direct image of the diagram object under the absolute projection. This motivates the comparison with an inductive limit, even though ordinary inductive limits in the homotopy category are said to exist only in particular cases. The qualification continues at the top of 174.

**U15 · Scan 174.** The canonical functors give a diamond explicitly labelled “non commutatif !”. Two natural maps lead from the composites around the diamond to the inclusion at 0. The author uses them to identify the composites in the homotopy category.

**U16 · Scans 175–176.** The two closed pieces of the index category have object sets {0,1} and {0,2}. Their inverse images in the total category intersect in X₀ and cover the total category. A primed square of closed immersions is described as cartesian and cocartesian. The comparison with the earlier diamond uses the identity at the outer vertices and inclusions at the two others. This is the author’s support for the homotopy-amalgam interpretation.

**U17 · Scan 176.** The next application begins by displaying the original span and the associated diamond again, with a reference to manuscript p. 123. The supplied sheet ends after the diamond. The proposition number is overwritten.
