# Mathematical notes on scans 252–266

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 252–253.** Grothendieck compares the two bracketings of a ternary product. Each of the three binary subproducts gives one equality to check. Each individual factor gives another, by passing through either of the two pairs containing it. He asks for a unique map from an ordered partial product with $1\leq m<n$ factors into the whole product, using associativity and the arrows $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

**U2 · Scans 253–254.** He locates a two-sided unit in the larger category of endofunctors equipped with an arrow from the identity. The unit gives the two families of arrows he needs in the full subcategory $\mathcal C$. He then tests the proposed conclusion about $1$-connectedness against the example $B_G$.

**U3 · Scans 254–257.** The composite on manuscript p. 204 first localizes $\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$ at $W_S$, then takes a quasi-inverse to the functor induced by inclusion of the fibrations. Choosing $C$ and $i_X:X\to C(X)$ in $W$ gives a way to calculate the composite before localization. Grothendieck wants to compare two such choices through the same intrinsic composite.

**U4 · Scans 257–259.** The new section starts with a category $M$ admitting pullbacks and a class $W$. A margin specifies weak saturation. He defines a class of $W$-fibrations by a condition on base change and defines $W_X$ by forgetting the map to $X$. Localizing the whole slice at $W_S$ gives $\mathrm{Ho}^{!}_W(S)$. He asks for inverse image along every $S'\to S$. When a terminal object $e$ exists, evaluating at $p:e\to S$ would give the fibre of $X/S$ in $\mathrm{Ho}_W$.

**U5 · Scans 260–261.** The second construction localizes the full subcategory of $W$-fibrations over $S$. Its class $W_S^u$ consists of maps that belong to $W$ after every change of base. He explains how pullback preserves the fibrations and these classes and hence induces inverse image on $\mathrm{Ho}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W$ for arbitrary base maps.

**U6 · Scans 261–263.** He considers using the universal class in the whole slice and gives practical objections. Two diagrams then show how to evaluate a fibre at an object $S'/S$ by changing base and forgetting $S'$. His test for local constancy is that an arrow $S''\to S'$ in $W_S$ induce an isomorphism from the fibre at $S''$ to the fibre at $S'$. In the two numbered conditions on 263, the first specifies arrows in $\mathrm{Cat}$ and the second specifies arrows in $M_{/S}$.

**U7 · Scans 264–265.** The first construction assigns a type to every object over $S$. The second permits every base change for the types it assigns to fibrations. Axiom (a) asks that the comparison from the second localization to the first be an equivalence for every $S$. Axiom (b) asks that, on fibrations over $S$, every map in the induced class $W_S$ stay in $W$ under every base change.

**U8 · Scan 266.** Grothendieck considers how (a) and (b) might be established together, then discusses $M=\mathrm{Cat}$ with a fundamental localizer. Returning to general $M$, he formulates the requirement of enough fibrations to represent every object of a slice up to $W_S$-equivalence. His next sentence breaks off at “on”.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence (scan 252).** Enumerating the three pairs and the two pairs containing each of the three single factors accounts for the author's $3+3$. This checks the number of comparisons. Whether the six pairs of maps agree remains open on this sheet.
- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence (scans 260, 265).** The identity $S\to S$ is one of the base changes in the definition of $W_S^u$, so universal membership gives ordinary membership on the fibration subcategory. The direction singled out in the last paragraph of (b) is the converse. This follows from the quantifier on 260 and the direction of the implication written on 265.
- **R3 · Model remark, high confidence (scans 259, 262–263).** There are two meanings of a point in these pages. On 259 it is a map from a terminal object. On 262 an arbitrary object $S'/S$ is itself called a point, and 263 compares fibres at two such objects. This explains why the second construction can be discussed in the original setting, where pullbacks alone were assumed of $M$.
