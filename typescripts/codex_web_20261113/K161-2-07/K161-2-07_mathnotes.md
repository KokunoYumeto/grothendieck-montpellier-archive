# Cote 161-2 · mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**M1 · A universal field and regular elements (96).** The opening paragraph describes sheaves for the Zariski topology and identifies their structural sheaf as the universal field. The writer then asks how to isolate, in a ring object O, the elements acting regularly. He tests the proposed O′ on the universal ring and on its fibres. An attempt in the middle of the sheet is struck through. His last comparison puts $O'_p$ and $(O_p)^*$ side by side.

**M2 · Types of structure and their bases (98–99, §§1–2).** A pair λ specifies shapes of limits and colimits. The objects of the resulting 2-category have those limits and colimits, and its functors preserve them. A type T assigns the category T(C) of structures in C. For structures on an I-indexed family there is an additional map to the type with value $C^I$. The triangle on 99 records the comparison over this base, including an isomorphism of commutation.

**M3 · Constructions on a structure and representability (99–100, §§3–4).** A morphism from T to the one-object base theory gives a construction on T-structures. Evaluation at a structure ξ in C gives a functor from the category of constructions to C. The margin rewrites this evaluation in both arguments. A representing category $R_T$ comes with a universal T-structure. The substitutions in (4.3)–(4.6) compare $R_T$ with the category of constructions and evaluate the representation at sets.

**M4 · Four formation rules (100–101, §5).** Start with the type having only its indexed base objects. Impose the invertibility of specified arrows, adjoin arrows between specified objects, or combine a family of structures over their common base. For adjoining arrows the display on 101 writes out a structure together with the new arrows. For combination it gives a 2-fibre product. The definition asks for the smallest full sub-2-category closed under the four operations.

**M5 · Do the rules characterise the representable types? (101–103, through 5.7).** The question at the foot of 101 is divided on 102 into a free construction, a universal inversion of arrows, a universal adjunction of arrows, and a universal construction for a family indexed by J. Generation by the distinguished objects is asked for in each case. The next question proposes a test using natural transformations. The following sheet takes up the converse and considers what can be said after forgetting the choice of I.

**M6 · Changing λ and taking opposites (103–104, §§6–7).** Enlarging the classes of diagrams suggests a functor on types and an enveloping category on the category side. The examples involving Pro and Ind both carry question marks. Passing to the opposite exchanges the two families of diagrams. The displayed equivalences on 104 compare the corresponding types, their indexed versions, and the categories which represent them.

**M7 · Limits in categories of structures (104–105, 8.1–8.1.1).** In the case with no specified colimit diagrams, the writer uses the description of T(C) by functors to identify limits computed argument by argument. He then allows general λ and gives a condition on a diagram type d in C. Its limits must exist there and commute with the specified colimits. Evaluation by a construction is said to preserve these limits. A slanted addition concerns changing C and changing T.

**M8 · Fully faithful functors and presheaves (105–106, 8.2.1–8.2.11).** Applying T to an embedding of categories gives a comparison square over the I-indexed base objects. The text describes its essential image in terms of the images of those objects. It then applies the square to the canonical embedding in the presheaf category. Evaluating presheaves gives the map α from structures in the presheaf category to functors from $C^o$ to $S_T=T(\mathrm{Ens})$. The second square on 106 uses the component base functors out of $S_T$ to express a representability condition.

**M9 · Recovering the representing category (106–107, 8.2.12–8.2.17).** In the intrinsic formulation the test uses all the constructions β. Evaluation embeds R in a functor category. Scan 107 relates this to $R^o\subset S\subset\widehat{R^o}$ and singles out a full subcategory Σ of S by a representability condition on composites. The two final functors have sources $\Sigma^o$ and T(C). The sentence announcing their consequence ends after « une ».

**M10 · A classifying topos and inverse image of a champ (109–110).** The universal structure in the presheaf topos is the inclusion of C. A structure in E determines a morphism from E to the classifying topos. Display (1) compares maps to an inverse image with maps between two set-valued structures. Scan 110 calculates the comparison for an object of C. It then describes restriction to a site, a variant with a subtopos and a topology τ, extension over Pro(C), and formation of the associated champ.

**M11 · Structures on fixed objects (112).** Objects $E_i$ in a fixed topos X are to be the inverse images of the base objects $b_i$ in B. The diagram sends X and B to the same base topos. The candidate modular topos is their 2-fibre product. The writer then specialises to a finite-limit presentation and starts describing the champ of structures with the chosen identifications. He explicitly leaves open whether the proposed construction works.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence (102–103).** The choice of *objects* as generators matters to the writer’s question. His parenthesis in 5.6 explicitly asks whether the arrows must also be taken into account. The preceding injectivity test is worded in terms of objects of S.
- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence (105–106).** The two squares answer different questions on the page. Scan 105 describes when a structure comes from C under a fully faithful embedding. Scan 106 adds a test on its underlying presheaves via the maps $b_i$. The qualification on the colimit part of λ is visible beside the latter square.
- **R3 · Model remark, medium confidence (109–110).** Crossing out *bijection* before (1) matters for the scope of the next calculation. The following page restores that word under the explicit restriction $X\in\mathrm{Ob}\,C$. Nothing in these two pages states the same result for every X in the classifying topos.
- **R4 · Model remark, low confidence (99).** The interchange of upper and lower labels in the sideways note could be a change of notation. Its relation to the main line is clear from the repeated evaluation expression, while the rest of the marginal note is hard to read.
- **R5 · Model remark, high confidence (112).** The proposed fibre product is tied to the specified identifications $f^*(b_i)\simeq E'_i$. The sheet explicitly records the absence of a proof that it works. The category named in the final marginal note remains uncertain.

The stroke beneath it has no unambiguous arrowhead in this line. This would make the parenthesis qualify the category by its limits. The hashed transcription retains the frozen wording at these unmarked letters.
