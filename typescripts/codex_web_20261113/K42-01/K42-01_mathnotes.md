# Mathematical notes · Cote 42, scans 2–7

## What the pages do

**D1 · Definition and morphisms, scan 2.** A *réseau d'anneaux locaux* has an ordered set of points and an inductive system of local rings. For comparable points the ring map induces a localization isomorphism. A marginal insertion makes the points above a fixed point correspond bijectively to the prime ideals of its ring. A morphism comprises an increasing map of points and compatible local homomorphisms in the reverse direction. The page names the two categories and draws the functor from preschemes to networks.

**T1 · Relative full faithfulness, scans 2–3.** Over a locally noetherian prescheme S, the functor restricted to preschemes locally of finite type over S is declared “pleinement fidèle”. Two crossed passages try to pass from networks to maps of neighbourhoods. The first appeals to continuity and specialization; the second introduces an S-relative Hom map. The marginal question about finiteness is outside those cancellations.

**P1 · Sections from stalks, scan 4.** The proposition concerns a sheaf F on a locally noetherian space in which each irreducible closed subset has exactly one generic point. Around each x it requires the maps from the stalks at points of the closure of x to the stalk at x to be injective. With the indicated order on points, the stalks form a projective system. The proposition identifies global sections with its inverse limit.

**L1 · The agreement locus, scan 4.** Lemme 1 takes a section on U and a compatible family of germs. It asserts that the points where the two agree form an open set. The lines underneath use points generalizing a given point, constructibility, and agreement on a nonempty open part of a closure. The proof of the proposition is then dismissed in a short closing sentence.

**C1 · The structure sheaf, scan 5.** Corollaire 1 identifies the global sections of the structure sheaf of a locally noetherian prescheme with families of elements of the local rings compatible under generization. “Modules” is written beside the corollary and again beside the lemma below.

**L2 · Injective localization maps, scans 5–6.** For a noetherian ring A and a prime p, Lemme 2 chooses an f outside p for which the map from A localized at f to the local ring at p is injective. It also treats the further localizations at primes over p in that neighbourhood. The proof first names the kernel of the map from A, uses its finite generation and chooses a denominator annihilating it. The revised last lines on scan 5 and the argument resuming on scan 6 both follow individual fractions.

**C2/L3 · Morphisms from compatible germs, scans 6–7.** Corollaire 2 uses the sheaf of germs of S-morphisms from X to Y and identifies an S-morphism with a coherent family of such germs. Lemme 3 states that this sheaf meets the hypothesis of the proposition. The next sheet reduces the issue to equality of two maps from an affine scheme once their restrictions to the local ring at p agree.

**C3 · Return to networks, scan 7.** Corollaire 3 formulates the equivalence in terms of an S-homomorphism of networks when X is locally noetherian, Y is locally of finite type over S and S is locally noetherian. The parenthesis questions the last condition. The first network argument is written with a smaller letter than the second.

**E1 · Example, scan 7.** The remark returns to the extra condition in the proposition. On the affine line over an algebraically closed field, the writer first considers a finite set of closed points and strikes out a construction indexed by that set. The live formula is a coproduct indexed by the points of X. He chooses a distinguished element called “unité” and describes the resulting family as coherent. The last line appears to say that it is not a section. Both the fibre notation and parts of this last phrase remain doubtful.

## Slips

- **S1, scan 2, definition.** “[donc si …)” has a square opening and a round close. A matched pair would be expected. The extent of the parenthesis is clear.
- **S2, scan 5, proof of Lemme 2. Low confidence as a slip.** “l'annulateur de A−p” glosses the kernel of localization. A more precise reading of the operation here is “elements annihilated by some member of A−p”. If it meant annihilation by every member, it would include annihilation by 1. The phrase may be shorthand for the former operation.
- **S3, scan 7, Corollaire 3. Low confidence.** The smaller first argument in “ρ(x)” may stand for X. The sentence starts with schemes X and Y, and Y is the second argument. Capitalization of the first remains doubtful.
- **S4, scan 7, remark. Low confidence.** The letter in “la condition imposée à S” looks like S. F would refer back to the sheaf subject to the extra condition in the proposition. The letter itself carries a doubt mark.
- **S5, scan 7, example.** The opening parenthesis before k has a square closing bracket after “alg. clos”. A matching closing parenthesis would be expected.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · My remark, scans 4–6. High confidence.** The direction of the local-ring map in the affine calculation agrees with the direction of the sheaf condition. For primes p contained in q, the complement of q is contained in the complement of p. Thus the displayed map runs from the localization at q to the localization at p. This checks its direction alone.
- **R2 · My remark, scans 3 and 7. Medium confidence.** The margin on scan 3 and the parenthesis on scan 7 both ask about finiteness hypotheses. Only the latter securely identifies S. The damaged final letter on scan 3 prevents identifying the two questions with each other.
