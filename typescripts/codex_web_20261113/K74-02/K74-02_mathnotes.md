# Mathematical notes on cote 74, scans 38–55 (selected sheets)

The scan numbers are those of the PDF. Quoted French retains the wording of the transcription. A cancelled formulation is identified as such. The finite calculations in the last section have corresponding entries in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**Scans 38–39. Extensions and a universal coefficient group.** A map $z_\rho$ from $V$ to a commutative $Z$ satisfies two previously stated relations. The author attaches an extension of $\Gamma$ by $Z$, together with lifts indexed by $i$. He constructs a universal $Z$ as a quotient of $\mathbb Z^{(V)}$ by those relations. In the sideways note he changes $\rho$ to $\bar\rho$ and obtains $z_{\bar\rho}(x)=-z_\rho(x)$. The main sentence continues across the page turn to $V+\Gamma^2_{\mathbb Z}(V)$. The slanted insertion singles out a subgroup generated by $x+x^{(2)}$.

**Scans 39–40. A quadratic example in characteristic two.** On scan 39 the difference $\lambda(x+y)-\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)$ is denoted by $\mu(x,y)$. Substituting $y=x$ brings in $2x=0$ and yields $\mu(x,x)=-2\lambda(x)$. Cyclic factors of orders four and two occur at the foot of the sheet. Scan 40 gives the function equal to zero at zero and one elsewhere. It attaches a central extension and writes a product of three indexed lifts equal to the nonzero element $z$. The specification of the order of the indices is overwritten.

**Scan 42. Polygonal structures on five elements.** Below the accounts and address, in the opposite orientation, the author associates a combinatorial dodecahedron and hence an icosahedron to a five-element set. He identifies its faces with the twelve polygonal structures on that set. Scan 43 carries a number only.

**Scans 44–45. Drawings and counts.** Scan 44 places products involving $\tau$, $\varepsilon$ and $\omega(\tau)$ beside hexagon drawings and maps with degrees 2 and 3. Several object names are too compressed to identify securely. Scan 45 places pairs of digits around polygons, gives $15\cdot6\cdot4\cdot2\cdot1=720=6!$, and writes $10P=720$, $P=72$ and $P_0=12$. Two drawn pentagons give different cyclic arrangements of the same five digits.

**Scan 47. Relations on discrete sets of vertices.** A discrete set has no internal edges. In a bidiscrete graph the vertices split into two discrete sets, with non-linkage across them giving a bijection. The author discusses the exceptional four-vertex case and names three relations on discrete parts: *liées*, *biliées* and *opposées*. The margins and insertions are important here because the first version of a name is crossed out.

**Scans 47–48. The hexagon of a triad.** For a cubic graph $\Delta=(S,A)$, the proposition puts a graph structure on its triads. The author says the linked components are hexagons. The two neighbours of $\tau$ partition $L(\tau)$, and the vertices linked to every element of $\tau$ give its opposite $\bar\tau=E(\tau)$. The bars, primes and double primes in the drawing distinguish all six vertices. For the relation *biliées* the components are triangles. Passing to the opposite triangle gives an involution.

**Scans 49–50. Two coordinates on a bitriangle.** Starting with a triangle of triads indexed by $I$, the author constructs $J$ from tuples of pairwise non-linked vertices and projects $J$ bijectively to each triad. Their union $X$ has nine elements. The two families of fibres identify it with $I\times J$; choosing one family gives a coorientation. Scan 50 forms the opposite $\bar X$ and the complement $\widetilde X=S-X-\bar X$, again of size nine.

**Scans 51–52. Symmetries and projections.** The symmetries $\sigma_t$ give an action. The discussion distinguishes three orbits of size six and six orbits of size three under an even subgroup. The mark on the first acting group is uncertain. For three mutually disjoint bitriangles $X_\alpha$, the author attaches a three-element $I_\alpha$ to each. Identifying each $X_\alpha$ with the product of the other two index sets gives a disjoint-union description of $S$. Point c tests linkage within one component by comparing coordinates, and point d compares projections to the remaining coordinate for elements in different components.

**Scan 53. Two-element partial sections.** The points of $S$ are recoded as two-element subsets on which $p$ is injective. For distinct $A,B$ with identical image, linkage requires empty intersection. For different images, it requires nonempty intersection. The page then describes cubic graphs with three distinguished bitriangles through sets partitioned into blocks of sizes $(3,3,3)$. It also discusses the category of pairs $(\Delta,X)$ with just one bitriangle distinguished.

**Scans 54–55. Indexing the eighteen triads.** For $p(i)\ne\alpha$, the triad $\tau_{i,\alpha}$ consists of two-element partial sections containing $i$ with the indicated image. The text labels opposite, linked and bilinked triads and groups six of them by a fixed $p(i)$. A cancelled item counts pairs of a bitriangle and a coorientation. Scan 55 begins “le couple” and stops after the comma in the subscript of $\tau$.

## Remarks of the model

**Model, high confidence in the arithmetic (C45.1, C45.2, C50.1, C53.1).** Multiplication gives $15\cdot6\cdot4\cdot2\cdot1=6!=720$ and hence $720/10=72$. Treating a polygonal structure as an unoriented cyclic order, enumeration of the orders of five labelled elements gives 12 up to rotation and reversal. The complement calculation gives $27-2\cdot9=9$. The four permutation-group factors on scan 53 give $(3!)^4=1296=3^4 2^4$. This last calculation concerns the order only.

**Model, high confidence for the finite model specified on scans 52–53 (C47.1, C47.2, C48.1, C48.2).** I enumerated the two-element partial sections of three labelled three-element fibres and applied the manuscript's two intersection rules. The 27 vertices contain 720 discrete triples. Joining triples when their union is discrete gives 120 components, each a six-cycle. Joining disjoint triples when non-linkage between them is bijective gives 240 components, each a triangle. The complements of the 120 six-cycles are distinct nine-element sets. Each contains six discrete triples, with exactly two ways to partition its nine elements into three of them. These counts help explain the two families on scan 49. The individual strokes in the handwritten marginal counts on scan 47 remain uncertain.

**Model, high confidence in the coordinate enumeration (C54.1).** In the same graph, the 18 pairs $(i,\alpha)$ allowed on scan 54 give 18 distinct triads. They exhaust the triads lying entirely in one of the three coordinate bitriangles. Holding $p(i)$ fixed gives three groups of six, each a linked hexagon of that graph.

**Model, low confidence, reading questions.** On scan 51, L9, the frozen group superscript is $\circ$ or $0$; an alternative is the full $\widetilde G$ with the mark arising from a retracing. Point c later assigns the size-three orbits to $\widetilde G^\circ$, whereas this line assigns orbits of size six. On scan 53, L3 and L12, the frozen base-set letter is $A$; $\Lambda$ would distinguish the base from a two-element subset also called $A$. On scan 53 the base of the drawn projection and the base-set letter to the right of the equality have no clear crossbar. The corresponding letter after $p(A)=$ on scan 54 also lacks a bar, whereas the nearby name for a two-element subset has one. Neither reading question changes the transcription.
