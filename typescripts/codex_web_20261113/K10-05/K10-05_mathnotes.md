# Mathematical notes on cote 10, scans 155 and 157–170

L numbers refer to lines in the audit. The quotations are taken from the transcription. Where the manuscript was crossed out, the status is given with the quotation.

## What the pages do

**Restriction to a maximal torus (155).** For a linear algebraic group $G$, a maximal torus $T$, its normalizer $N$, and $W=N/T$, the first map restricts from $G$ to $T$. The actions on the two sides give a map into the $W$-invariants in the integral group ring of the characters of $T$. The author labels it (5). A second map, (5 bis), uses a field of coefficients and puts the tensor product inside the outer $C$.

**Recovering an algebra of operations (157–158).** An abelian class of structured modules over a commutative ring $k$ comes with an exact underlying-module functor and a set of representatives $A_i$. An endomorphism of the functor amounts to a family of operators satisfying the commutation square on 157. The product of the operator rings gives these families a topology. The resulting algebra $U$ acts on the original modules. Scan 158 calls actions satisfying the stated continuity condition “permis”.

**The comparison problem (158–159).** Once the objects are restricted to finite generation over $k$, the author asks for two things. He wants to recover the structures on an underlying module bijectively and to identify the allowed morphisms with the $U$-homomorphisms. The cancelled lower part of 158 tries to express reconstruction using a commutant and a functor. Scan 159 describes neighbourhood ideals and their finite-dimensional quotients. Its margin compares such algebras over a field with coalgebras, and the boxed conclusion describes the permitted modules as finite-dimensional unitary comodules.

**Augmentation and maps (159–161).** A privileged structure on $k$ leads to a continuous augmentation. Scan 160 sends a continuous algebra map to a functor that commutes with passage to the underlying module. Conversely it recovers a map on rings of natural endomorphisms from such a functor. Scan 161 adds the condition that the trivial module be preserved.

**Duality (161–162).** The proposed functor gives the ordinary linear dual $V'$ a permitted module structure. The square on 161 records its effect on underlying modules. The double operation is required to be the identity. Passing to the opposite algebra expresses the operation by an involutive anti-automorphism. Preserving augmentation amounts to preserving the trivial module under duality. The last formula on 162 compares restricting a dual module with dualizing the restriction.

**Tensor product and the diagonal (163–165).** A bifunctor is to put a permitted action on the ordinary tensor product. Its structure square is followed by crossed attempts using two commuting actions, annihilators and indecomposable summands. The live end of 164 introduces a diagonal from $U$ to its completed tensor square. On 165 the author constructs its finite-quotient components using a condition about expressing operators on a tensor product as sums of tensor products of operators. The margin names a semisimple case.

**Compatibility axioms (166–167).** Associativity, interchange of the factors, the trivial module and passage to the dual each impose conditions on the diagonal. The evaluation maps in both orders lead to the two underlined identities with product $P$. Symmetry relates the two requirements. The lower part of 167 asks when the functor attached to a continuous map preserves tensor products.

**Collecting the data and dualizing (168).** A large brace groups the topological algebra structure, the coalgebra structure, their compatibility, the augmentation and the involution. The product identities are repeated at the foot of the brace. A parenthesis discusses multiplication on the completed tensor square. A second brace begins the dual description on $A$, exchanging the side on which commutativity occurs.

**Group-like elements (169).** The author considers invertible $s$ with $\Delta s=s\otimes s$. He relates them to automorphisms of the underlying functor compatible with tensors, and to maps from the algebra associated to the additive group of integers. The margin deals with the trivial representation and the contragredient one. The augmentation takes the value 1 on these elements, and the involution gives their inverses. A totality hypothesis is used in a uniqueness assertion. At the bottom, evaluation at $s$ acts on the commutative algebra $A=U'$.

**Coefficients, units and primitives (170).** Continuous linear forms on $U$ are described as coefficients of finite-dimensional continuous representations. The units of $U$ give automorphisms of the underlying functor. The next entry describes a primitive element by its action on a tensor product, $A\otimes1+1\otimes B$. The manuscript calls the resulting object a complete topological Lie algebra and mentions a $p$-operation in positive characteristic.

## Questions and conjectures

- **155 L14–16.** “Mais c'est sans doute une bijection s'il n'y a pas de torsion.” The qualification is an interline addition on the claim about (5).
- **158 L4; 159 L6–9.** “conditions pour que ce soit … un isomorphisme”, followed by “On veut que cet hom. soit bijectif” and the requirement on homomorphisms. These are the reconstruction questions.
- **164 L2; 165 L10–16.** The crossed “Montrons qu'il existe un …” attempts the uniform finite-module step. The live argument later proceeds “pourvu qu'on fasse la supposition” about tensor-product operators. The margin gives a sufficient case.
- **169 L19–22.** “Si G est total dans U” makes uniqueness conditional. Whether this totality holds is left open on these pages.

## Slips

- **S157, 157 L20.** “sous-classe … fermé” would ordinarily take *fermée*. Low confidence as a slip because two words between the noun and the adjective were struck.
- **S159, 159 L1–5.** The opening names $K(W)$ and $K'(W)$. The arrow then reads $K(V)\longrightarrow K'(W)$. Consistently using the same argument would match the stated comparison. Low confidence because some V/W letters are doubtful or overwritten.
- **S162, 162 L3.** The masculine article *un* remains before the inserted *application*. *Une application* would give agreement after the revision. Low confidence because of the overwritten construction.
- **S165, 165 L8.** The last $L_k$ term closes with a parenthesis whose opening is missing before $W$. The apparent correction is to insert that opening parenthesis.
- **S167, 167 L15–19.** The functor is written from the category with exponent $V$ to the category with exponent $U$, for an algebra map $V\longrightarrow U$. The module construction on 160 runs from $U$-modules to $V$-modules. The immediately following operation on 167 also gives a $V$-structure to a tensor of $U$-modules.
- **S168, 168 L1–2.** “les données et tous les axiomes … réunies” would ordinarily take *réunis* for the combined subject.
- **S170, 170 L3.** The coefficient formula pairs $u.a$ with $b'$, then places $a,b'$ in $V$. Writing $b'\in V'$ would type the second argument as a linear functional. The sheet specifies no identification of $V$ with its dual.
- **S170n, 170 L16.** The primitive-elements item follows “1)”. The retraced numeral could have been intended as “2)”. Low confidence.

## Remarks of the model

- **M169a — high confidence.** The conclusion $\varepsilon(s)=1$ in 169 L14–16 uses both idempotence and invertibility. An invertible idempotent in the coefficient ring equals 1.
- **M169b — high confidence.** In 169 L18 the deduction from $\breve s\,s=1$ to $\breve s=s^{-1}$ uses precisely the invertibility included in the definition of $G$.
- **M163 — low confidence.** The cancelled argument across 163 L11 and 164 L1–2 tries to pass from finite quotients to a finite list of indecomposables. These leaves give no live justification of that step. Its intended hypotheses are particularly hard to recover under the enclosing strokes.
- **M167 — low confidence.** The digit 1 in the two product identities on 167 L7 and 168 L10 deserves to be left literal. The application to invertible group-like elements on 169 fixes what happens on those elements. It does not by itself determine how the symbol 1 was being used for maps on the whole algebra.

- **Reading question of the model, 170 L8 — low confidence.** The frozen heading begins *Classes remarquables*. The first word could also be *Choses*. An upright immediately after the capital could belong to either l or h, and the remaining letters occupy a single low stroke. Both readings introduce the ensuing list.
