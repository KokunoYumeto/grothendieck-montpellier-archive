Scan numbers refer to the PDF. B numbers identify the blocks listed in the inventory. ## What the pages do

**117, even orders and the square.** The page continues an argument relating order \(2p\) to order \(p\) by changing the sign of the variable. It gives the invariant \(-2\) in characteristic \(p\), presents a modular ring with 2 inverted, and treats \(n=4\) separately. At the square, the variable in the quotient is zero and the resulting ring is identified with \(\mathbb Z\). The prose attaches existence, uniqueness up to unique isomorphism, and faithfulness over a field to this calculation [U1].

**118–119, reduction of a cyclotomic polynomial.** Write \(n=p^rn'\) with \((p,n')=1\). The proposition compares \(\Phi_n\) modulo \(p\) with the \(\varphi(p^r)\)-th power of \(\Phi_{n'}\). The induction is on \(r+n'\). A divisor is written \(p^s d\); the proof multiplies the lower-index relations and uses the sum of the \(\varphi(p^s)\) to cancel a power of \(T^{n'}-1\). The rewritten factor in (2) and the unprimed index at the end are visible stages of the calculation.

**119–120, order of the polygon.** The corollary starts from \(\Psi_n(\alpha)=0\). In an extension field the author writes \(\alpha\) as a root plus its inverse and applies the proposition. The three orders he gives are \(n'\) when \(n'\geq3\), \(2p\) when \(n'=2\), and \(p\) when \(n'=1\) [U2].

**121, low-degree cases.** The tables sort prime powers by their totients, assemble the possible orders, and list the corresponding rational, quadratic and cubic invariant values or equations. The last two cubic entries, for 18 and 14, are lower and to the right of those for 9 and 7. The long curved stroke connects the two tables.

**122, a separate tensor calculation.** The starting equation moves \(u\) from one tensor factor to its transpose on the other. Two expansions in primed and unprimed basis vectors yield conditions on the coefficients \(u_{ij}\). A partly filled matrix, two angular figures and short formulas written in other directions accompany the cancelled work. The surviving lines give no explicit statement tying these coefficients to the preceding table.

**123, a working polynomial list.** The author places the minimal polynomial of a primitive root next to that of its sum with its inverse. For \(n\geq3\) he relates them by \(T^{\varphi(n)/2}F_n(T+T^{-1})\) and lists examples through \(n=10\). A separate calculation introduces \(S_0=1\), the positive-index power sums, and a recurrence for expressing \(U^n\) in terms of them.

**124–125, multiplicities.** The first two identities relate indices \(p\) and \(2p\). The main calculation then separates the ordinary case from \(n'=1,2\). It gives multiplicity \(\varphi(p^r)\) for the first case and the half exponent in the exceptional trace case. There are several changes of polynomial initial, an abandoned \(n=4\) case, and a final pair of specializations whose linear terms differ from the preceding cases.

**126, the cyclotomic prime.** The rings generated by \(\alpha\) and by \(\zeta\) are written as polynomial quotients. After describing their behaviour over primes, the author expands \((\zeta-1)^{p-1}\), takes a norm, boxes \((\zeta-1)^{p-1}=pu\) with \(u\) a unit, and identifies the residue field of the ideal generated by \(\zeta-1\).

**127–128, norm of the trace difference.** The two pages work with \(\alpha-2\). A factor \(2-\alpha_i\) is compared with the product of the differences of two inverse roots from 1. This leads the author to \(F_{p^r}(2)\), \(\Phi_{p^r}(1)\) and, for \(p\) itself, the boxed question below. Other lines try quotient presentations over \(\mathbb F_p\) and a comparison of \(A_{n'}\) and \(A_n\).

**129, tensor product and involution.** For coprime factors the author writes a tensor-product isomorphism and a product of groups. A quadratic presentation sends \(T\) to the root and \(\alpha_n\) to its sum with its inverse. The substitution \(T\mapsto U-T\) is applied to \(F+GT\); a later diagram describes \(A\) as free over \(B\) with basis \(1,T\). Other diagrams place the primitive-root locus beside its quotient by inversion.

**130, ramified coverings.** Two covers of \(S\) have distinct designated ramification points. Diagrams relate their fibre product, fundamental groups of punctured bases, and arrows between groups. Lower on the page a subgroup maps to both factors and a component \(Z\) maps to \(X\) and \(X'\), with separate ramification labels on its branches. The sheet closes with a fragment about \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^*\).

## Questions and conjectures

- **Q1 · 128 B4.** « \(F_p(2)=p\) ? » The arithmetic \(8+4-4-1\) sits beside the box. For an odd prime, inserting \(T=1\) in the identity on 123 gives \(F_p(2)=\Phi_p(1)=p\). The ledger also records the direct evaluations for 5 and 7.
- **Q2 · 128 B6.** « iso? » appears over the map \(B[T]/(T^2-\alpha T+1)\to A\).
- **Q3 · 127 B10.** « mais peut-être pas à \(p-1\) … ! » qualifies the coprimality discussion.
- **Q4 · 130 B6.** « \(X\times_S X'\) est … ? » The predicate may concern normality or connectedness. The question is not answered on the sheet.
- **123 B10.** « donner aussi les cas \(n=12,14,18\ldots\) » is an instruction to add examples.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence.** On 118, the exponent belongs to the prime-power part of \(n\). As a check of the indices, \(\Phi_{45}-\Phi_5^6\) reduces to zero modulo 3. This is one test of the general statement.
- **R2 · Model, high confidence.** On 123, \(S_0=1\) is a separate initial value. In the displayed binomial sum the central term for even \(n\) therefore carries the full binomial coefficient. Substituting \(U=T+T^{-1}\) checks the written formula at \(n=2,4\).
- **R3 · Model, medium confidence.** The isolated \(T^{2n}=T^n\) on 129 makes sense in the cyclotomic quotient, where \(T^n=1\). Which of the several rings on the sheet is intended at that particular line is left open.
- **R4 · Model, low confidence.** On 130 the upper and lower product labels end with \(X\), while the projection on the right ends at \(X'\). A missing prime is plausible. The unprimed vertex is preserved, and the doubtful word in the associated question cannot establish which geometric property is at issue.
