# Mathematical notes on cote 83, scans 113–128

## What the pages do

**U1 · A symmetric invariant, scans 113–114.** Exchanging the two entries of either pair replaces the ratio $\lambda$ by its inverse. The sum $\rho=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}$ gives the quadratic $\lambda^2-\rho\lambda+1=0$. In the position under discussion, $\rho_0=\rho-2$ is invertible. Grothendieck uses it to describe simultaneous local conjugacy of the pair of étale subalgebras $(H,H')$. He identifies an order-two stabiliser away from an exceptional value. The note at the foot of 114 qualifies that last assertion at $\rho=-2$.

**U2 · An isomorphism torsor, scans 114–116.** The nontrivial involution acts on each rank-two algebra by $x\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(x)1-x$. The isomorphisms between two pairs form a torsor. Grothendieck compares it with the covers attached to $H$ and $H'$, then compares two bisections with coefficients $(b,c)$ and $(\bar b,\bar c)$. When both linear coefficients vanish in characteristic different from two, the ratio invariant agrees for the two bisections, while an isomorphism entails a square-class condition on $\bar c/c$. He singles out this example as exceptional and asks a further local-existence question.

**U3 · A polynomial and two discriminants, scans 117–119.** In the basis $1,u,v$ of $B=H+H'$, the five scalars are two traces $b,b'$, two determinants $c,c'$, and $t=\operatorname{Tr}(uv)$. Scan 117 writes $\rho_0=P/Q$ and gives the trace matrix on $B$. Scan 118 expresses the discriminant on $H$ as a section of a negative tensor power of its determinant line. The two rank-two discriminants and the discriminant on $B$ can then be compared in one line. The boxed ratio on 119 is $\delta_H\delta_{H'}/\delta_B$. The sign issue is recorded below.

**U4 · Boundary values, scans 119–120.** The ratio extends as a projective-line value wherever its two defining sections do not vanish together. The vanishing of $\delta_B$ gives the value $\infty$ in the stated étale case. A non-étale member gives the value $0$ where the ratio is defined. Grothendieck describes the remaining field case using a Borel subalgebra, a line $D$, $H=k\oplus D$, and a second plane in the Borel. He relates the conjugacy type to a Borel and maximal torus.

**U5 · Realising five prescribed scalars, scans 121–123.** Starting with $(b,c,b',c',t)$, he seeks generators in an Azumaya algebra that realise these values. He first fixes the individual quadratic algebras. The pair thereby obtained has a product trace $\tau$, to be compared with the prescribed $t$. The equality of two values of $Q$ puts $t$ and $\tau$ among the roots of one quadratic. In the unequal-root case he uses $u'=b-u$. His trace computation on 123 gives the required product trace without changing the trace or determinant of $u$.

**U6 · A canonical algebra and an omitted case, scans 123–124.** The algebra $M(b,c,b',c',t)$ has the indicated formal basis $1,u,v,uv$ and a previously described multiplication. Grothendieck reduces its Azumaya property to geometric fibres, with a marginal caution about associativity. He then returns to $\delta\delta'=0$, identifies the dual-number algebra in the non-étale case over the algebraically closed field, and separates the two-idempotent, mixed and two-square-zero cases.

**U7 · Rank-one endomorphisms, scans 125–126.** He formulates three existence assertions for $\operatorname{Tr}(uv)=t$. Writing $A=\operatorname{End}(V)$ with $\dim V=2$ makes the trace the product of two pairings. For the first two cases the chosen basis gives $y_1y'_1=t$, with $y_2y'_2=1-t$ or $-t$. He discusses the two families at $t=0$ in the first case. In case 3 he changes the covector to $e'_2$ and ends with $y_1y'_1+y_2y'_2=0$ and $t=y'_1y_2$. The page stops there, with dots.

**U8 · Norm, trace and the unit, scan 127.** The opening NB observes retrospectively that $\rho$ was unnecessary in the verification. The new exposition starts from two rank-two algebras with norm, trace and unit maps. The polar form and the trace-of-product form add up to $(u,v)\mapsto T_H(u)T_H(v)$. The oblique NB uses evaluation at the unit to relate the two forms, the trace and the norm.

**U9 · Amalgamating the units, scan 128.** A quotient of $H\oplus H'$ identifies the two units and gives a module $B$ with $H\cap H'=k1_B$ and $H+H'=B$. The two maps into $P=H\otimes_kH'$ agree on $k$ and factor through $B$. A marginal map $T_B:B\to k$ restricts to the two traces because their values on the unit agree. A separate marginal warning concerns which object carries an algebra structure.

## Questions and conjectures

- **115.6–9.** “lequel torseur peut (je présume) être non trivial”. The proposed nontriviality concerns the smaller isomorphism torsor.
- **116.13–20.** “Il serait intéressant de voir si tout $\rho_0$ inv. peut être réalisé loc. Zar”; “Je soupçonne que oui”. The suggested construction sets $H'=\operatorname{int}(u)H$.
- **117.19–21.** “Je voudrais donner à cette formule … une forme intrinsèque”. The determinant-line discussion on 118–119 pursues this aim.
- **121.5m–6m.** “Sans doute inutile de supposer $\delta$ inversible”; “on trouvera en fait une façon canonique”. These are marginal qualifications to the realisation argument.
- **123.6m, 11m and 124.4–6.** “Il manque le cas où $\delta\delta'=0$”; “Attention au cercle vicieux”; “j’ai oublié de faire la vérification”. The missing case is taken up on 124–126. The caution about associativity remains explicit.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Scan 117. High for the polynomial calculation.** With $G$ the displayed three-by-three trace matrix, symbolic expansion gives $\det G=-2Q$ over the integers in the five coefficients. This identity can be stated without dividing by two. The status of each crossed sign remains as recorded in S4.
- **M2 · Scans 122–123. High.** A direct polynomial check gives $R(t)-R(\tau)=(t-\tau)(t+\tau-bb')$. This accounts for the two alternatives in the field argument after 121. It does not settle the shape of a retraced letter.
- **M3a–c · Scan 124. High for the substitutions.** Substituting the three displayed coefficient rows into the polynomial on 121 gives $t(t-1),t^2,t^2$, respectively. The ambiguous handwritten right side of $v^2$ was not used as input.
- **M4 · Scan 125. High.** Multiplying two generic rank-one two-by-two matrices gives $\operatorname{Tr}(uv)=\langle x,y'\rangle\langle y,x'\rangle$ with the final tensor order shown in the text. The earlier overwritten order is still unresolved.
- **Reading question · Scan 113.18. Low.** The frozen reading is “dans $A$,”. The short mark immediately to the right of $A$ may be a retraced comma or a cancelled subscript preceding the comma. It has a second downward stroke. The longer leader for the insertion begins farther right.
- **Reading question · Scan 128.18. Low.** The frozen lower diagram reads the two inner curls as arrow ends at $H,H'$, with arrows from $B$. They can also be read as hooked tails on the inclusions $H,H'\to B$. The latter interpretation fits the scalar identification and the factorisation described in the adjacent sentence. The weight of the strokes on the inner diagonals leaves their direction uncertain.
