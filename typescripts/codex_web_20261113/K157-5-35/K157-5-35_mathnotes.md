# Mathematical notes on manuscript pages 454–468

Grothendieck, A. (n.d.). *Les Dérivateurs* [Manuscript, cote 157-5, pp. 454–468]. Montpellier archive. All scan numbers below refer to faces 543–557 of the supplied facsimile. Statement identifiers also identify the corresponding entries in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**W01. A relation detected on a cofinal set (543–544, pp. 454–455).** The first leaf continues an argument that produces $v\in N_{i'}$ with $u=v\alpha_{i'i}$. For $u\in N_i$, the relation $R_i$ induced by $\alpha_i$ on $M$ is the relation $R_u$ induced by $v\mapsto uv$. There are at most $\operatorname{card}M\leq\pi_0$ such relations. The author passes to a cofinal $I'$ on which a single $R_u$ suffices. The assertion beside « Si » and the display on p. 455 have distinct left-hand labels.

**W02. The quotient and the key lemma (544–546, pp. 455–457).** Put $E=M/R_u$. The transition maps descend to injections of $E$. The author uses the image $\overline{M'}$ of the submonoid preserving the relation and inducing injections. Its cardinal is at most $\pi_0$. Lemma 4.15.8.1 asks for eventual isomorphisms for a system in the one-object category $B_M$ of a left-cancellative monoid. Applied to the quotient system, it would identify $F(\xi)$ with one stage and bound its cardinal by $\pi_0$. The converse implication from 4.8.13 motivates the next lemma.

**W03. A strict increase in the cardinal of a limit (547, p. 458).** Lemma 4.15.8.2 assumes that beyond $i_0$ the sets have cardinal $\pi_0$, the transition maps are injections, and above every stage a later transition fails to be an isomorphism. For an indexing set « grand devant $\pi_0$ », the conclusion is $\operatorname{card}E_\infty>\pi_0$. The leaf links this assertion to the equivalence of 4.8.13 and the key lemma.

**W04. An ordinal family witnessing growth (547–549, pp. 458–460).** Corollary 4.15.8.3 selects an increasing family $i_\alpha$, beginning at $i_0$, indexed below the first ordinal $\alpha_0$ of cardinal greater than $\pi_0$. The main condition concerns successor transitions. The note « mieux » strengthens it to nonsurjectivity of the map from the preceding colimit into the current stage. The proof chooses $\xi_\alpha$ outside the union of preceding stages and uses the resulting injection to bound $\operatorname{card} E'_\infty$ from below. The manuscript's two differing subscripts are recorded under S03 and S04.

**W05. Reduction to one ordinal (549, p. 460).** Corollary 4.15.8.4 fixes $M$ and $\pi_0$. It suffices to test the key lemma on the ordered set $I_{\alpha_0}$. Its last formulation excludes a system on that set whose transition element is noninvertible for every strict comparison $i<j$.

**W06. Five versions of a stabilization question (550–551, pp. 461–462).** Theorem 4.15.9 assumes $C$ small and $\pi\geq\operatorname{card}C$. Condition a) identifies $\mathrm{Ind}_\pi C$ with $\mathcal M_\pi$. In a′), with $C$ Karoubian, large inductive systems are essentially constant. In a″), a cofinal reindexing makes all objects a single $x$ and all strict transitions one projector of $x$. Conditions b) and b′) concern left-cancellative quotient monoids of $\mathrm{End}_C(x)$. The former writes eventual transitions as $1$; the latter excludes an ordinal chain of noninvertibles. The theorem asserts equivalence and adds a conjecture about truth.

**W07. The strict cardinal bound (551–552, pp. 462–463).** Corollary 4.15.10 establishes the conditions for $\pi>\operatorname{card}C$. The author takes $\pi_0=\operatorname{card}C$, its successor $\pi_1$, and an ordinal $\alpha_1$ of cardinal $\pi_1$. Under the excluded-chain assumption, $M_{\alpha_1}$ has cardinal at least $\pi_1$. The stage $\alpha_1+1$ supplies an injection into $M$. This is the contradiction to the size bound on the monoid. See S05 for the particular signs he writes.

**W08. The Karoubi envelope (552, p. 463).** Corollary 4.15.11 gives $\mathcal M=\mathrm{Ind}_\pi C=\mathcal M_\pi$ and calls it an « enveloppe de Karoubi de $C$ », for essentially small $C$ and $\pi>\operatorname{card}\mathrm{red}\,C$. The author reduces to $C$ reduced and invokes the previous corollary. He explicitly compares this strict bound with the earlier weak bound in 4.8.13. The sentence on its applications ends after the parenthetical reference to 4.15.3.

**W09. An iterated Hom (553, p. 464).** Section 4.16 opens with the covariant set functor $E\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{Hom}(E,I),J)$. The displayed value is $J^{(I^E)}$. When $I$ and $J$ each have at least two elements the author says the functor is nonaccessible. With $I=J=\{0,1\}$ it gives the iterated power set. He presents this as a counterexample to an earlier expectation concerning “reasonable” functors.

**W10. The inverse image of the nonempty sets (553–555, pp. 464–466).** In 4.16.1 the author considers $\mathcal M_z$, the objects $x$ admitting a morphism to $z$, as the inverse image of the nonempty sets under a representable contrafunctor. For commutative $k$-algebras, $k$ an infinite field and $z=k$ the initial object, he reports failure of $L_\pi$-stability for every $\pi$ in the example. The argument used the smallest universe containing the natural numbers. He distinguishes constructing the pair from proving its failure of accessibility under assumptions on the ambient universe.

**W11. A closed complement and small witnesses (555–557, pp. 466–468).** The complementary subcategory $\mathcal M'_z$ consists of objects with empty $\mathrm{Hom}(x,z)$. For the algebra example the proposed test asks whether $\mathrm{Hom}(A,k)=\varnothing$ is already witnessed by a sub-$k$-algebra $A'$ of bounded cardinal independently of $A$. The author also considers dropping the field assumption and replacing the target by an arbitrary object. The margin on 556 refers the reader to the objection on manuscript p. 466.

**W12. A general inverse-image question (557, p. 468).** In 4.16.2 an accessible functor has target $\mathcal N^\circ$. An accessible subcategory $T$ of $\mathcal N$ has, in the opposite category, a specified $L_\pi$-stability property, with $L_\pi$ also imposed on $\mathcal N^\circ$. The question asks for accessibility of $f^{-1}(T)$ in $\mathcal M$. The marginal « Faux » specializes to $\mathcal N=\mathrm{Ens}$, a representable functor and $T=\{\varnothing\}$. The last sentence begins a different choice of $\mathcal N$ and runs past the supplied leaves.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Q01 (550–552).** « Et je conjecture qu'elles sont toujours satisfaites » concerns the equivalent conditions for the weak hypothesis $\pi\geq\operatorname{card}C$. The following corollary establishes the strict case. At the boundary $\pi=\operatorname{card}C$, the statement still has the status of a conjecture in this packet.
- **Q02 (553–554).** « J'avais crû que tout foncteur “raisonnable” … devait être accessible » and « Deuxième désillusion » introduce two abandoned assertions. The second concerns strictly full subcategories stable under direct factors and bearing the proposed logical accessibility property. « Il n'en est rien » precedes the representable example.
- **Q03 (554–555).** « J'ignore si pour … autre univers, on peut trouver un corps $k$ » asks for suitable cardinality so that the relevant $\pi$-ultrafilters on sets in the universe are trivial. « Pour être vraiment probant, il faudrait un exemple de nature plus élémentaire » qualifies the example as evidence about logical accessibility.
- **Q04 (555).** « Il reste la possibilité qu'il y ait un th. métamathématique » asks whether a logically accessible pair with an accessible ambient category and a strictly full, eventually $L_\pi$-stable subcategory gives relative accessibility. The slanted note begins « C'est faux »; several middle digits of its forward reference are doubtful.
- **Q05 (556–557).** « Il se pourrait … qu'une telle catégorie soit … accessible » and « j'ai été incapable de le prouver » concern the closed representable preimage. He returns to the small-subalgebra question with « tendance à croire que ça doit être vrai ». The nearby marginal objection says « C'est faux, cf annotation marginale p. 466 ».
- **Q06 (557).** « Alors … $f^{-1}(T)$ est-elle accessible dans $\mathcal M$ ? » The question is qualified by both inverse-limit conditions. The author's marginal negative answer points to pp. 466 and 467.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01. Model remark, high confidence (548–549).** The marginal « mieux » compares the current stage with the colimit over all earlier stages. The live argument on the next leaf selects an element outside the union of all earlier stages. These two passages explain the role of the strengthened condition. The proposed word « non » in the canceled qualification on 549 remains low confidence.
- **R02. Model remark, high confidence (551–552).** The strict-cardinal argument depends only on $\pi_1\leq\operatorname{card}M_{\alpha_1}\leq\operatorname{card}M\leq\pi_0$ and on $\pi_1>\pi_0$. This explains why neither outside equality in S05 is needed for the contradiction.
- **R03. Model remark, low confidence as a discrepancy (546, 550–551).** The lemma says « un iso. » and the theorem's b) writes eventual equality to $1$. An identification of objects or a choice of reindexing might bear on their comparison. The five-condition summary does not explain that passage. Both of the baseline ones are clear.
- **R04. Model remark, high confidence about the references (555–557).** The closing « Faux » specifies $T=\{\varnothing\}$. This is the closed complement tested on 555–556. The diagram on 554 instead concerns nonempty sets. The two manuscript page numbers in the marginal citation pick out precisely the intervening leaves. The mathematical validity of the negative answer is reported on the author's authority.
