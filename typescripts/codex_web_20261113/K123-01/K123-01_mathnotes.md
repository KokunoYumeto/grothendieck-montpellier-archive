The scan numbers are the PDF scan numbers. Quoted French follows the reading in the page files. The separate doubts table identifies alternatives and cancellations.

## What the pages do

**Scans 4–5 · Kostant, notation and the regular semisimple locus.** The title sheet precedes a list for an adjoint semisimple group over $S$. The list distinguishes the Lie algebra of the group, the Lie algebra of its torus, the Weyl group $W$, and the quotient $J$. It introduces schemes of Borels, maximal tori and Killing pairs. Over the Borel scheme the universal Borel has a Lie algebra with maps to the group Lie algebra and to the torus Lie algebra.

**Scans 5–6 · The map to the quotient.** On the regular semisimple open, the handwritten product with the scheme of Killing pairs carries a diagonal Weyl action. Passing to the quotient produces a map to $J$. Grothendieck discusses freeness on the torus open and draws the resulting square. To extend the map beyond the open he reduces to a split group over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, then invokes the generic fibre and Kostant. The boxed note at the left describes invariant functions and asks how restrictive the word « affine » is.

**Scans 6–7 · The primed space and the locus of isomorphism.** Base change to $J'$ gives a space denoted by a primed Lie algebra. The diagram distinguishes the projective map $p$ to that space, the map $q$ to the original Lie algebra and the smooth map to $J'$. The next sheet describes the open of isomorphism by finite fibres, an isolated element, and the number of Borels containing a given element. It then asks whether that open meets every fibre of $f$.

**Scan 7 · A list of problems.** The list marked « Questions » has a preliminary item 0, then a)–e), with f) and g) in the left margin. It moves from faithfulness, regularity and flatness to orbit closures, centralizers, a section on the regular open and the dimension of a variety of Borels. The hatched NB next to the list returns to the ring of invariants.

**Scans 8–9 · Steinberg, construction of the target.** The second title sheet introduces the group version. Here $D=\mathrm{Bor}(G)$. The universal Borel has a unipotent part and a torus quotient. The sheet descends this torus to $S$, introduces its character lattice $M$, and describes the Weyl action using Killing pairs and a normalizer. The quotient $I=T/W$ receives $f:G\to I$. The last line gives its restriction to a torus in a Borel.

**Scan 10 · The correspondence over $T$.** A cartesian square defines $G'$ from $G$ and $I'=T$. A pair $(g,B)$ gives both an element of $G$ and its class in the torus quotient of $B$. The relation $\pi\widetilde f=fh$ yields the map $p$ to $G'$. The sheet checks the relation on a dense open, records properness and smoothness, and sets out the three regular loci.

**Scan 11 · The theorem and the proof on the regular locus.** The four parts give projectivity and smoothness, the maximal open on which $p$ is an isomorphism, fibrewise codimension statements, and Picard comparisons. Two rows of Picard groups distinguish the absolute groups from their relative sheaves. The ample cone is identified with the cone of weights denoted $P^+$. For part (ii) the proof starts with the regular semisimple open, uses finiteness of the fibres and properness, and invokes the « Main Theorem » with a normal target.

**Scan 12 · The Picard calculation.** Part (iv) starts again with an altered hypothesis on $S$. A depth argument and a fibrewise comparison lead to a lemma about $\mathrm{Pic}(B)$, the torus $T=B/B_u$ and the character lattice. The sheet writes the cohomology sequence with $H^0$ and $H^1$ of the sheaf of units, followed by the product formula for $\mathrm{Pic}(T)$; « H. 90 » appears under the factor set equal to zero. Twisting may leave a nonzero term. A section gives injectivity, étale sheafification yields the displayed diagram, and an affine map with a section enters the ampleness argument.

**Scan 13, a–b · Twisting and the hypotheses on $S$.** The map from $\mathrm{Pic}(D)$ to the Picard group of the universal Borel need not be surjective for a twisted torus. With finitely many connected components the text asserts finiteness of the cokernel. A sequence with $H^1(\Gamma,M_{\bar\xi})$ relates it to generic fibres. The legend distinguishes the generic point of $D$ from that of $S$. Regularity of $S$ enters a vanishing criterion. Part b) retains injectivity and the characterization of ampleness without the normality hypothesis.

**Scans 13–14, c · The pullback from $S$.** Under normality and the added vanishing condition $H^1(\xi,M)=0$, the display asserts $\mathrm{Pic}(S)\simeq\mathrm{Pic}(G')$. The argument restricts a class to the unit section, compares it on the regular locus and on the universal Borel, and follows the section over $D$. The first line of scan 14 finishes the sentence by factoring $D\to G'$ through the section over $S$. The adjacent diagram and sequence record the remaining finite group when the torus hypothesis is dropped.

**Scan 14, d and conclusion · The unprimed group.** The next sequence maps $\mathrm{Pic}(G)$ to the Picard group of its generic fibre. A note states when the latter vanishes. Restriction to $G^{\mathrm{reg}}$ leads to a finite-cokernel assertion, with vanishing in a specified case. The sheet ends by drawing a consequence for an analogous resolution of $f$ that would leave $G^{\mathrm{reg}}$ intact and satisfy the stated fibrewise codimension condition.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence.** The injectivity assertions on scans 12 and 13 have the same formal ingredient. For a structure map with a section, the composite pullback to the total space and back along the section acts as the identity on the base Picard group. This checks the claimed injectivity in those two places. The statements about the cokernel use additional input.

**R2 · Model remark, high confidence.** The two resolutions are easiest to keep apart by following the arrows labelled $p$. On scan 6 the target is a primed Lie algebra, while on scan 10 it is the primed group $G'$. The distinction matters when reading the final sentence of scan 14, which compares $f$ with $f'$.

**R3 · Model remark, low confidence.** The revised note beside the first Picard diagram on scan 11 appears to anticipate the cohomological criterion of scan 13. The groups $H^1(\Gamma,M)$ recur, but the earlier qualification is written across a cancelled line. I would reserve any identification of the precise hypotheses.

**Reading question · Scan 13, a, line before the sequence.** The frozen phrase reads « $S$ étant de dbl », with the last group marked. One could instead divide the joined strokes as part of « irréductible ». That reading spans a boundary of the original doubt mark and would change the hypothesis. The final transcription preserves the surrounding frozen words and gives the possible suffix at the marked place.
