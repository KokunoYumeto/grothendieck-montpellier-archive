# Hand and notation log

## Scan 3

Line references start with a tall looped l. The same outline occurs as the prime-number variable in item i, where the adjacent words distinguish its use. The large ovals at iii, iv and the foot single out entries or letters. They also occur on other leaves, but do not by themselves cancel a word. The pencil foliation is much lighter than the ink page number.

## Scan 4

The section numeral 2 is surrounded by two strokes. The loops round c and c′ single out individual characters. The compact M of A. M. is almost rectangular. The phi in the argument on p 14 has a descended stem, less distinct inside the final parentheses. A heavy diagonal cancellation recurs below pg 6 and at pg 7. ## Scan 5

The n in the first three lines sits before and below its capital. A bar belongs to each of the two right subscripts on K. Below the two Spec expressions the small suffix looks like upright et, with no definite accent. The word below the direct-limit arrow is fully written, including the final r and dot. The lower margin annotation is much lighter than the body.

## Scan 6

A second, paler, more upright writing occurs in the left margin. The circled replies and the capitals CANULAR are distinct from the long lines in ink. In the main text a tilde is written over m as a short form. The line across pg 53 runs from before the page reference through the sentence, stopping before the parenthesis at the right.

## Scan 7

Two voices are visible. The darker writing has level baselines and tall ascenders. The pencilled, oblique replies have compressed medial letters. On this leaf si beside A₀ is a response in the lighter hand. Numeral 8 stands by itself between rulings. A diagonal through the overwritten reference makes the preceding digit particularly uncertain.

## Scan 8

As in scan 6 a small tilde stands over a single m. The wedge follows S above it to the right. The left l-infinity indices on the last line sit below the two A's. The light que faire ? overlaps pg 103. The two belong to different hands. The bottom two thirds contain no continuation.

## Scan 28

A different hand writes large separated mathematical capitals, very small zeros, and nearly headless strokes. At the ends of both chains the left point is a filled dot, the right point an open oval. Two vertical strokes relate T and T′ to the D expressions below them. There is a dot-like enlargement at the end of some, but not all, horizontal shafts. The two isolated pen traces at the upper left have no discernible lettering.

## Scan 29

This hand flattens entire syllables into near-horizontal strokes. The open G often resembles C. Compare G, G′ and the clearer G of Gₘ. The rounded epsilon-like E denotes the repeated biextension on this leaf. Underlines beneath Ext and Hom are local to each occurrence. The quotient labels in the row diagrams are upright p, whereas the splitting labels look rounder. The cancellation covers several complete lines, within which there is also a shorter local strike.

## Scan 30

In the turned typescript, pen makes broad arrowheads, two-part ξ subscripts and sharp restriction strokes. They differ from the narrower keyed slanted φ. Typed overstrikes and later pen marks coexist on the same line. The underlining belongs to Tor and ends before its lowered 1.

## Scan 31

Low connecting strokes can reduce an entire word to three strokes. In the oblique margin, rotating each note separately restores the baseline. The ℓ in the final two lines loops below it. The handwritten quotient has separately framed Z shapes. The 1 on the falling arrows of the two small triangles is a label, distinct from the primes.

## Scan 32

At the top the pen distinguishes the narrow ξ below φ from ν above the typed crossed ell. The handwritten ν has a short entrance stroke and a longer rising terminal stroke. The pair in the margin models both forms. In the lower formulas the stylus explicitly supplies membership strokes and the number (2.3.3).

## Scan 33

In this hand n before a capital may be a low straight dash. The same writer puts a low n after the lattice letter in a quotient. The light arch between the two factors of the first induced pairing is separate from the short vertical identifications. Horizontal strokes through abandoned starts are much darker.

## Scan 34

The typescript arrow above the composite is supplied in pen and reaches the third group directly. The two loops in the margin are models for φ and ℓ. In (2.2.2) broad pen initials sit over cancelled typed objects. A long pen line below the square inserts the sentence about the vertical maps immediately before Notons.

## Scan 35

The upper-right title is underscored with one continuous stroke. Its d in Picard is compact, while in dualité the initial d rises. The separate lower-left 34 is in pencil.

## Scan 36

The landscape sheet is one ruled table. In the first two bands the recurrent coefficient has two bends, unlike the clearly double-stroked Z after a slash. Its open curl allows ζ or ξ. The other recurrent capital looks like U, with no closed lower loop. The last band has a separate dotted i-like coefficient. The lower-right F is to the right and below the closing parenthesis, while the middle F is on the left.

## Scan 37

The large upper brace has no lower label. A is written above a separate small arch on the next line. The vertical double stem has one upward head. In the list the final -eries is visible; the middle strokes of the coined word run together. At the foot both Zs have two oblique strokes.
