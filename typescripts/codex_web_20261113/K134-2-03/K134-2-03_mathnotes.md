# Mathematical notes on scans 106–115 and 128

## What the pages do

**Attaching cells and describing the same object by equations (106–107).** A finite closed covering by cells is ordered so that each new cell attaches along a specified subcell of a previous one. The dual description picks out tuples in a product by equating iterated sources and targets. The next sheet passes to the finite ordered set of incident cells and its distinguished family of standard pieces. On scan 107 the list for $\underline J_n$ has signed cells $S_j^\varepsilon$ and a final cell $D_n$.

**Adding an arrow with prescribed boundaries (107–108).** Condition (A) asks for an $h$ from $f$ to $g$ when their source and target data agree, with degree zero handled separately. Condition (B) builds the next stage on the same objects by adjoining arrows of this kind. The writer describes this as a universal problem. Two marginal qualifications limit the claims about amalgamated sums used in that description.

**Comparing choices of structure (108–109).** The writer imagines two mathematicians making different definitions and a third constructing comparison functors. The composites need only be related to what one started with by an $\infty$-equivalence. Further choices of comparison require comparisons of a higher order. The anecdote stops at seven mathematicians.

**Redundancy in adjoining operations (109–110).** Proposals range from adjoining an arrow for every eligible pair to choosing one per isomorphism class, adjoining one only when needed, or ruling out pairs factoring through another object. Binary and simultaneous compositions of a composable string illustrate the redundancy. At the next stages there are homotopies between operations and then between composites of homotopies.

**Allowable continuous maps (111).** A candidate $\operatorname{Hom}_\infty(K,L)$ is taken inside the continuous maps from $|L|$ to $|K|$. The writer describes closure under composition and an extension from a sphere to a cell. He considers piecewise linear or differentiable maps, then asks whether their relations agree with freely adjoining the required arrows. The functor to spaces is contravariant.

**Realization in a model category (112).** The writer asks for objects in $M$ and boundary maps, starting with a fibrant, trivial object and gluing copies along boundaries. The discussion leads to $K\mapsto |K|_M$, its extension through the stages, and a proposed connection with the usual homotopy category. The displayed chain with $M$, $\infty$-groupoids and (Ho) has spaces between the three terms. An interpretation in terms of sections over a topos is proposed.

**Naming stacks and coherators (113–114).** The existing use of “two-groupoids” and the dependence on choices both count against an unqualified name. The writer considers “stack”, “Gr” and a reference to the chosen category $C$ as specifications. He proposes “coherators” (“cohéreurs”) for the categories encoding a hierarchy of coherence homotopies and “$i$-homotopies” for elements of a component.

**What the category of a coherator determines (114–115).** The writer considers recovering the generators, their source and target arrows and a filtration using the category structure. He questions an argument involving duality involutions, then restricts attention to constructions that do not adjoin a new homotopy where an old one exists. The marginal note on 115 questions whether the initial subcategory should be included in the data.

**Closing and postscript (115).** The letter asks for comments before the proposed formalism has been worked out. The postscript corrects an earlier assertion about products by identifying the fiber products already present in the initial category and preserved in the later stages. It restates the corresponding condition on functors defining stacks. The final line runs beyond the lower edge. Scan 128 is a reused listing and adds no mathematical unit.

## Remarks of the model

- **M107-COUNT — high confidence, scan 107.** Independently counting the $n$ values of $j$ with two signs and one additional $D_n$ gives the written $2n+1$. For $n=0$, just $D_0$ remains in this count.
- **M115-SCOPE — high confidence, scans 107–108 and 115.** The quantifier changes in the PS. “these are fiber products also” picks out particular products from $C_o$. The earlier phrase “are stable under binary …” ranges over diagrams in the stage under discussion. This difference explains why the condition on the functor in the PS is stated on its restriction.
- **M114-DATA — medium confidence, scans 114–115.** There are two recovery questions in play. The first concerns automorphisms of all the generators and boundary arrows taken together. The other concerns recognition of the subcategory $C_o$ inside the coherator. The margin on 115 reopens the latter immediately after the identity assertion for the former.
- **M112-INK — low confidence, scan 112, line defining $S_n$.** Reading question: the frozen formula has a low $D_{n-1}$ between two pairs, with blank paper above it. A sum sign would make it resemble the preceding construction for $S_o$. No convincing strokes establish such a sign. The transcription retains the blank position.
