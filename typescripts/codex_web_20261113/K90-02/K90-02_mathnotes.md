# K90-02 · Mathematical notes

The scan numbers below follow archive order. Quotations preserve local abbreviations. The numbered labels identify statements referred to in the check-event record.

## What the pages do

**U14.1 · A cancelled representation statement, scan 14.** A proposition starts from a Dedekind ring, an affine group and an algebra satisfying the conditions of « Lemme 1 ». It puts the regular representation on an N from « Lemme 2 » and qualifies the map from G to the underlined Aut(N). A large cross covers the proposition, including that conclusion.

**U14.2 · Stable submodules, scans 14 and 16.** Three conditions on the Bᵢ appear below the cancelled proposition. The numbered assertions treat a smallest stable quasi-coherent submodule, its formation under flat base change, a direct limit of stable finite-type submodules and a surjection from a module of the form $F\otimes\check B_i$. Item 4 uses a hypothesis that finite-type quasi-coherent modules on X are quotients of locally free ones; the examples mention an ample invertible sheaf and regularity. The typescript leaf between the two scans interrupts a sentence.

**U15 · A reduction to fields, scan 15.** The surviving end of a proof seeks equality of two inverse images of Γ′. It tests the equality after base change to Spec κ(x) for x in X, reduces to the spectrum of a field and invokes separation in that case. The number of the corollary being proved does not appear on this leaf.

**U17 · Strictly local factors, scan 17.** The proof passes from a tensor product of fields to local factors C′ᵢ of C′. The hand-drawn diagram and the equations involving f, g, φᵢ, w and w′ compare one factor with the strict henselization of C. Compositions in both directions are identified with the identity. A remark weakens a finiteness assumption in the field argument. The following proposition begins with reducedness, normality and a residue-algebra assertion for a semi-local ring.

**U18 · A revised equivariant statement, scans 18 and 20.** The hypotheses on Bᵢ reappear for a flat affine group over S; the checked Bᵢ are described as sheaves of associative algebras with unit. G acts on X and on a relatively ample invertible sheaf. The author restates the smallest stable submodule condition, cancels a second item and tries two versions of a third. The later version mentions a quotient from a module of the form f*(H)(−n). Its H is legible; the following « où » trails off.

**U20 · Graded sections, scans 20 and 22.** The proof sets up the graded sections of E from the modules $f_*(E(n))$, and the graded algebra obtained by using the structure sheaf. It describes a left-exact functor into graded modules with G-action and an exact functor in the reverse direction. The next manuscript leaf states their adjunction and identifies the composite on E with the identity, then infers full faithfulness for the sections functor.

**U22 · The smallest stable module in degrees, scan 22.** The author takes a smallest G-stable module N containing the sections of F. The bracketed argument compares N in each degree with a minimal Mᶦ, and uses their sum and minimality to conclude that N is graded. A final sentence invokes functoriality for the submodule structure over the graded algebra.

**U24 · Returning to sheaves, scan 24.** The sheaf associated to N is compared with the associated sheaves of the sections of F and F′. The author claims compatibility with flat change of base. For finite type he reduces to affine S, chooses a twist generated by finitely many sections φᵢ and considers the G-invariant module they generate in degree n. A framed draft is crossed out. The last lines identify the resulting sheaf with F′ and conclude that it is of finite type.

**U19 · Corollaries and the unibranch criterion, scan 19.** Following a short topological argument, (18.6.10) compares the Cohen–Macaulay, (Sₙ), regular and (Rₙ) conditions for a noetherian local ring and its henselization. The next corollary compares associated primes by contraction. Proposition (18.6.12) relates unibranchness to irreducibility, with a reduced/integral variant. Its proof reduces to reduced rings and gives the implication b) ⇒ c) using an inductive limit. Only part of the proof is visible.

**U21 · Reduction, normality and the fibres, scans 23, 21 and 19.** The theorem headed (18.6.9) at the foot of 23 continues at the head of 21. It compares reducedness and normality under henselization and describes the relevant residue algebras. Its proof uses flatness and local inductive systems, then reduces the fibre claim to an artinian-ring lemma. A second lemma concerns a noetherian inverse limit of finite discrete spaces. The compactness argument at the top of 19 supplies the end of that reasoning.

**U23 · A universal property and integral base change, scan 23.** The opening draft extends a universal-property formulation to homomorphisms called semi-local. The whole paragraph is cancelled and the long margin note objects to the formulation. Proposition (18.6.8) then identifies $B\otimes_A({}^{\mathrm h}A)$ with the henselization of B for a semi-local B integral over A. Its proof locates maximal ideals and compares local homomorphisms into a henselian local ring C. The final displayed-in-line map actually uses the names Hom, without “.loc”.

**U25.1 · End of a proof about henselization, scan 25.** Part (i) obtains a section through the chosen point; (ii) uses local homomorphisms and a limit. Part (iv) compares quotients by ideal powers and their consecutive graded pieces, using flatness at the tensor-product display. Part (vi) appeals to the henselian case; a longer section argument is crossed out and a handwritten reference replaces an earlier one.

**U25.2 · The semi-local definition, scan 25.** Under (18.6.7) the henselization is the product of the henselizations of the localizations at the maximal ideals. The leaf describes its maximal ideals, the extended radical, the residue quotient and the morphism of completions. The claim that the henselization is faithfully flat as an A-module is inserted by hand.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence.** The direction in which the text continues differs between the two sets of leaves. The manuscript passes directly from « Considérons alors » on 22 to tilde-N on 24. The typewritten « l'an- » at the foot of 23 continues as « neau » at the top of 21. These joins can be checked without importing text from outside the packet.

**R2 · Model remark, high confidence.** The change of tensor base in the display on 25 is compatible with the ideal powers written there. The first tensor comparison uses the stated flatness of Aλ. The factor mⁿ/mⁿ⁺¹ is annihilated by m, which accounts for A/m under the second tensor sign. This check covers the displayed equality alone.

**R3 · Model remark, low confidence.** Scan 16 states a finite-type hypothesis for the ordinary modules in item 4, then writes « Alors tt …-module » without an explicit parallel restriction before its quotient assertion. A finite-type qualifier may have been implicit. The contracted module notation and the unfinished redrafting on 20 leave that intention open.
