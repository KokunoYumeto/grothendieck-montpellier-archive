# K57-04 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Plan and covers, 65–67.** The Picard plan moves from existence theorems to numerical equivalence, finiteness, separation and properness. Later entries concern Albanese, duality, relative curves and further material over a field. Several numbers are written over earlier numbers. A dependency sketch gives more information about the ordering than the list alone.

**An effective representative, 68–69.** On a nonsingular projective surface, the note combines a Riemann–Roch expression for \(\chi(D)\) with the cohomology expression involving \(\ell(D)\) and \(\ell(K-D)\). Its goal is a positive representative after adding a numerically trivial \(\Delta\) to a sufficiently large multiple of an ample \(H\). The argument has two boundary cases worth distinguishing below.

**The finiteness argument, 69.** The family \(M\) consists of positive divisors numerically equivalent to a chosen \(D\). Subtracting \(D\) from a member is meant to represent a numerically trivial class. The Hilbert-polynomial and Chow argument leads to quasi-compactness, then to statements about the quotient by \(\mathrm{Pic}^0\), Néron–Severi torsion and the intersection form on \(N=\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}/\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau\).

**Igusa's inequality, 70.** The note relates the alternating sum \(E(X)\) to \(c_2(X)\) and to the self-intersection of the diagonal. It introduces \(p=\dim\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}_X\) and the rank \(\rho\) of the Néron–Severi group. The first box computes \(b_2\) in the page's notation and the second states \(\rho\leq b_2\).

**Comments on the finiteness theorem, 71–72.** Grothendieck describes which parts of the argument he attributes to Kleimann and where the passage from projective to proper uses Chow. Restriction to a hyperplane section gives a criterion involving the inverse image of \(\mathrm{Pic}^\tau\). He then asks how much of the relative equivalence criterion follows. The distinction that matters on 72 is between the known monomorphism, the stated generic quasi-affineness and the finite-type assertion still in question.

**Divisors and the logarithmic differential, 80–81.** A title sheet introduces Serre extensions and line bundles with connection. Scan 81 constructs an extension with middle term \(\underline E_{X/S}\) using pairs \((D,\omega)\) related by \(d\log\). Applying \(f_*\) gives a connecting map. The page identifies its kernel term and introduces \(\underline P_{X/S}\) from meromorphic functions modulo units.

**The oblique arrow and the quotient, 83.** A function is sent to the pair \((\mathrm{div}\,f,df/f)\). To descend to principal divisors, the note examines the contribution of units. A marginal variant keeps their image in the denominator. Under the stated hypotheses the resulting \(\mathcal M_{X/S}\) lies in an extension of \(\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^w_{X/S}\) by \(\underline\omega_{X/S}\). “Cas favorable” records vector-bundle and representability assertions.

**Direct construction, 85.** The sheaf \(\mathcal M^\sharp_{X/S}\) comes from isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves on \(X\times_S T\) with a connection relative to \(T\). The presheaf sequence, its associated sheaf sequence and the kernel defining \(\mathrm{Pic}^w\) are displayed separately. The proposed comparison has vertical maps labelled can, α, can. The explanation stops after “sur un”.

**Correspondence fragments, 82 and 84.** The first has a date, addressee, organisation and salutation. The second consists of another salutation to Guetta. The surviving typed lines are cancelled. Neither fragment supplies a mathematical assertion.

**Scratch calculations, 86.** The sheet juxtaposes \(Z_\alpha\), base changes, products with an upper \(G\), equivariant Hom expressions and quotient spaces \(G/H\), \(G/K\). The clearest local condition is \(gKg^{-1}\supset H\). A tower at lower left and a fragment on automorphisms at lower right are spatially distinct. The sheet supplies no continuous proof connecting all the displays.

## Remarks of the model

- **M70, high confidence.** The arithmetic of the two displays agrees with the page's own \(b_1=b_3=p\). Substitution in the alternating sum gives the boxed \(b_2=E-2+2p\). That calculation leaves the meaning assigned to \(b_1\) and \(b_3\) on the page intact.
- **M83, medium confidence.** The quotient by the image of the units in the margin of 83 reappears as the left-hand term of the direct construction on 85. The handwritten labels on the comparison diagram make this connection visible even though the definition of α breaks off.
- **M85, high confidence as a reading of the definition.** The data specified beside \(Q\) are a line bundle and a relative connection. No curvature condition is written there.
- **M86, medium confidence as an interpretation.** For the usual left action of \(G\) on \(G/K\), the condition beside the diagram identifies the cosets fixed by \(H\). This accounts for the direction of \(\supset\). Whether the same interpretation accounts for the entire sheet depends on hypotheses the page does not spell out.
