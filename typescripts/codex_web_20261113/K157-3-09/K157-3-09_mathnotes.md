# Mathematical notes · cote 157-3, scans 130–145

## What the pages do

**M1 · A square and its dual (130–131, pp. 79–80).** In the pullback square the remark compares \(f\) with \(f'\). It argues from the fibres in one direction. For the reverse direction it points to surjectivity of \(\pi_0(S')\to\pi_0(S)\) and tests the empty-base case. It then distinguishes the two implications for \(i_0\) and \(i\) in a cocartesian square and comments on the dual derivator.

**M2 · Proposition 6 (131–134, pp. 80–83).** Section 2.2 considers a cocartesian square formed from an open immersion. The hypothesis places the inclusions of \(U,U'\) into their closures in \(\mathcal W\). Part (a) compares membership of \(i,i'\) under W(7 bis). Part (b) compares membership of \(p,q\) under W(7). In (a) the closed complements \(T,T'\) and their dotted subcategories are identified. In (b) these identifications supply two vertical isomorphisms for the application of the « lemme des cinq » W(8).

**M3 · Proposition 7 and its revisions (134–136, pp. 83–85).** The commentary asks what makes the inclusion \(U\to\overline U\) an equivalence in a way that persists under change of the data. Proposition 7 relates its asphericity to a condition tested after changing the complement by an arrow \(T_1\to T\). The local test in (a) uses \(U_{/t}\) at points of the boundary. A small replacement for b′ tests a one-point category with image in that boundary. The corollary that survives the cancellations on 136 treats a final complement. Its proof is deferred.

**M4 · From an open immersion to a presheaf (137–139, pp. 86–88).** The hoped-for stability under arbitrary change \(U\to U'\) motivates a stronger condition. With a one-point complement, the gluing datum becomes a presheaf \(H\) on \(U\). The sheet identifies \(U_{/t}\) with \(U_{/H}\). It writes the changed presheaf \(H'=p_!H\) and gives its value at \(u'\) in terms of the components of \({}_{u'}\backslash U_{/H}\).

**M5 · Definition 1 (139–140, pp. 88–89).** For a presheaf \(H\), total \(\mathcal W\)-asphericity asks for \(p_!H\) to be \(\mathcal W\)-aspherical for every map out of its base. For an object \(V\) of Cat, the corresponding calculation uses the terminal presheaf on \(V\). With a map \(f:V\to V'\), the page names the presheaf \(\pi_0(f)\) and its category over \(V'\), written with an underlined \(\pi\).

**M6 · A canonical comparison (140–142, pp. 89–91).** Starting from the adjunction arrow \(F\to f^*f_!F\), he obtains a map from the category over \(F\) to the category over \(f_!F\). It fits in the square of forgetful maps to \(V,V'\). Taking \(F=e_V\) gives the displayed factorization of \(f\). On the fibre at \(v'\), the map is presented as the inclusion into \({}_{v'}\backslash V\) followed by passage to components.

**M7 · A discrete fibration and its presheaf (142–144, pp. 91–93).** The proposed characterization requires the projection \(\pi\) to have discrete fibres and the comma categories for the first factor to be \(0\)-connected. For a projection \(W'\to W\) corresponding to \(F\), formula (*) of p. 92 gives \(\pi_!(e_{W'})=F\). The comparison on the following sheet uses the inclusion of the fibre over \(w\) in \({}_w\backslash W'\) and its effect on \(\pi_0\). Applying (*) and condition b) identifies the intermediate category. He leaves the identification of the first arrow with the earlier canonical arrow brief.

**M8 · Proposition 8 (144–145, pp. 93–94).** If \(U_{/H}\) is totally \(\mathcal W\)-aspherical, then so is \(H\). The proof sets \(V=U_{/H}\), uses the projection \(\alpha:V\to U\), and expresses \(p_!H\) as \(f_!(e_V)\) for \(f=p\alpha\). The question about the converse turns on which arrows out of \(V\) this argument tests.

## Statements left without proof

- **D1 · 130–131.** The remark offers « L'énoncé pertinent serait le suivant » and says of the dual \(\mathrm{HOT}^{\circ}\) that it « ne possède pas cette même propriété ». The empty-base observation is supplied. A formal treatment of the two corresponding derivator axioms is put off with « ce que je ferai sans doute sous peu ».
- **D2 · 132.** « Je m'en étais convaincu en 1983 » introduces the possibility of \(i\in\mathcal W\) without \(i'\in\mathcal W\), and likewise of \(p\in\mathcal W\) without \(q\in\mathcal W\). The recollection supplies no example in this batch.
- **D3 · 135–136.** After « Il reste à prouver », the point-complement reduction leads to a rewritten corollary. « Nous démontrerons ultérieurement ce corollaire » expressly defers it.
- **D4 · 137–138.** « Nous verrons que cette attente n'est pas fondée » announces the failure of the former stability expectation. The stronger condition is introduced with « il s'avérera » and the discussion turns to its reformulation.
- **D5 · 142–144.** « Il doit être facile de montrer » introduces the characterization up to isomorphism. After identifying the middle category, « il ne doit pas être bien dur de se convaincre » introduces the identification of the first map. Its detailed argument is left to the reader. « Il faut vérifier » on 143 is followed by an argument on 144 for the discrete-fibration formula.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Q1 · 130.** For the converse implication in the pullback square, « il faut supposer … que » precedes the condition on \(\pi_0(p)\). The empty \(S'\) explains the obstruction in the example.
- **Q2 · 132–134.** On 132 he says he does not seek a counterexample « s'il ne se présente de lui-même ». The question mark above the equivalence of \(\overline p\) and \(q\) on 133 belongs to a proof in progress. He closes that comparison on 134 with « qed. ».
- **Q3 · 134–138.** « Nous voudrions que la propriété … soit conservée » motivates the local conditions. The later « J'avais espéré » attributes the initial cobase-change hope to the suggestion of an « immersion de Dwyer ».
- **Q4 · 145.** « La réciproque de prop. 8 est-elle valable » asks whether total asphericity of \(H\) implies that of \(U_{/H}\). The hypothesis initially tests arrows that factor through \(U\). He begins to consider another condition, « que f s'insère dans un carré commutatif ». This is the last line supplied.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence (134–138).** There are two changes of data to keep distinct when reading Proposition 7. The modified pair in b) comes from a map into the complement \(T\). The hope discussed on 137 concerns a map out of the open part \(U\). The labelled domains, codomains and the later square on 138 distinguish them.
- **R2 · Model, high confidence (140–144).** The short underline beneath \(\pi\) matters. The ordinary \(\pi_0(f)\) appears as a presheaf. The underlined symbol is the category over \(V'\) that appears as an object in the factorization triangle. This distinction can be read directly across the two defining displays on 140.
- **R3 · Model, high confidence (144–145).** The quantifier in the converse question is the point at which the proof of Proposition 8 ceases to run backwards. A direct image of \(H\) tests composites \(p\alpha\). The category condition tests every functor with source \(U_{/H}\). This is the discrepancy the final paragraph states before it starts the square.
