# K80-04 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Orientation and the canonical functor, scan 55.** The vertices $a,b,c$, their « opposés » and three indexed edges close an argument about orientation. The following theorem calls the canonical functor from arrangements of pseudolines without bigons to one-dimensional arrangements without bigons fully faithful. The author then turns to its essential image and to the possible redundancy of the Euler–Poincaré relation.

**Drawings and counts, scans 57–59.** Scan 57 marks individual regions and gives a total of 31, split into 25 triangles, 5 quadrilaterals labelled « carrés » and 1 pentagon. A separate parenthesis gives $+9$ triangles and $+1$ carré. Scan 58 marks regions of a second drawing with 3, 4 and *id*, and has five isolated ink marks on the right. Scan 59 gives another 31-total count. Its legend associates $1_6$ with the icosahedron, $5_1$ with the hexagon, $5_4,5_2$ with « triangle bordé », and $5_5,10_3$ with « carré gammé ».

**Definition and first consequences, scans 61–62.** A system of pseudolines is a topological or combinatorial map in the real projective plane. Its vertex orders are even and at least four. Its edges fall into classes, the pseudolines, and each pair meets exactly once. Scan 61 describes the homotopy class of one pseudoline, realisation by straight lines in general position for $n\leq6$, and two hemispheres in the orientation cover. On scan 62 the vertices on a pseudoline give a polygonal structure. A margin note extends the combinatorial polygon description to the one- and two-vertex cases.

**Comparing orientations, scans 62–65.** At a vertex $s$, the orientations of the surface are compared with those of a pseudoline $D$ missing $s$. For two such lines the diagram poses a question about the resulting bijection. Auxiliary lines through $s$ give points and orders on $D,D'$. The argument treats the case with only one available auxiliary direction and then introduces other lines to make the comparison. Two-pencil configurations intervene. At the top of scan 65 the six lines through pairs of four marked points give another comparison, this time between the constructions at $r$ and $s$.

**Conditions on the bijections, scan 65.** A fresh formulation starts with $K^1$ decomposed into pseudolines. The comparison $\varphi_{s;D,D'}$ is to be simply transitive as $D,D'$ vary away from $s$. Condition b) compares two vertices on a third line by the arcs determined by its intersections with $D,D'$. A supralinear note allows $x=x'$. Condition c) begins by naming $t$, two lines through $s$, and their intersections. The sheet ends before stating the condition’s conclusion.

**Local orientations, scans 66–67.** Scan 66 resumes in mid-argument with an automorphism and discusses obstructions to uniqueness for a bigon, a pencil and a double pencil. With monogons and bigons excluded, it constructs $\Omega(s)$ by identifying the $\Omega(D)$ through the $\varphi$’s. For the two ends of a segment the triangle through $\Omega(D)$ compares the two fibres. Scan 67 asks for the remaining polygonal structure on the arcs leaving $s$ and for its two orientations to be identified with $\Omega(s)$.

**Cyclic permutations, scans 68–70.** An oriented line missing $s$ supplies a cyclic permutation $\rho_{\vec D,s}$ of the oriented lines through $s$. Conditions a) and b) describe inversion on reversing $\vec D$ and equality or opposition for a second choice of line. A cancelled c) is followed on 69 by a condition relating changes of vertex to arcs. Condition d) identifies a successor by the order of intersection points on $\vec D$. The figure and e) on 70 compare consecutive intersection points on two oriented lines. Condition f) relates the half-order iterate to the negative oriented line. The exponent in f) remains a reading doubt.

**Uniqueness, scan 71.** Assuming existence and conditions a)–f), the theorem states uniqueness of the permutations when the excluded small polygons do not occur. A second assertion concerns the local system with fibres $\{\rho_s,\rho_s^{-1}\}$. Its final qualification is interrupted by cancellation. The two slanted notes revisit which hypotheses suffice.

**Case table, scan 73.** The sheet compares projective and combinatorial data for one through five lines and for $n\geq6$ in general position. For three lines it distinguishes general position from concurrence. For four it separates general position, three concurrent lines, and four concurrent lines. The comparison uses the square, cube, hexagon, octagon and pentagon, group symbols with indices 3 and 4, and quotients by cyclic groups. Question marks and a change of mind in the five-line row belong to the table.
