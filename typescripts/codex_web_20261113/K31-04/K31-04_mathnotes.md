# Mathematical notes on cote 31, scans 002–017

Grothendieck, A. (n.d.). *SGA A [SGA 4] (résidus de rédaction)* [Manuscript and corrected typescripts, cote 31, scans 002–017]. Archives Grothendieck, Université de Montpellier. Scan references below are to the supplied facsimiles. The two occurrence orders given below identify continuations within the batch. The LaTeX keeps archive scan order throughout.

## What the pages do

**Cohomological dimension in characteristic \(p\) (002–003).** The first cover names \(k\), its characteristic, and the fppf topology. The manuscript introduces \(\mathrm{cd}_p(k)\) for \(p\)-torsion sheaves, then considers a dimension denoted by a prime in the body. A filtration \(G'\subset G''\subset G\), with \(G''=G^0\), leads to Lemma 1. It reduces vanishing in degrees \(i>n\) to the étale case and the two radicial cases, multiplicative and additive. The relation actually written between \(G''\) and \(G'\) is recorded under Slips.

**The multiplicative case (005, 007, 009).** A finite group of multiplicative type is put in an exact sequence with two tori. Lemma 2 tests vanishing of the \(p\)-primary part of torus cohomology above \(n\) and \(p\)-divisibility in degree \(n\). The sideways bracket on 005 gives the variant over finite separable extensions. The two rows on 007 address necessity. For sufficiency, the surviving argument uses change of field, the trace, reduction to groups split by a \(p\)-extension, and the sequence with \(\mu_p\) and two copies of \(G_m\) on 009. A separate argument about a cokernel has been crossed through.

**The additive case (009).** Lemma 3 gives 0 over a perfect field and 1 over an imperfect field. The lower word above \(\mathrm{cd}\) reads *add*; the upper word is uncertain. The explanation reaches a dévissage into groups \(\alpha_p\) and stops.

**Torsion subgroups and central homomorphisms (004, 006).** A rotated typescript first discusses reducing earlier density arguments to certain finite étale torsion subgroups. Section 5 then states that when two homomorphisms agree on a fibre and the first is central, they agree on an open neighbourhood under the stated finiteness assumptions. The proof passes first to a complete local base and its reductions, then uses finite-type approximation to treat the general case. The corollary specializes to the unit homomorphism.

**The fibrewise unit locus (008).** After a crossed version for two subgroup schemes, Corollary 5.3 adds separatedness to the assumptions of 5.2. It asserts that the locus where \(u_s\) is the unit homomorphism is open and closed. The proof turns to \(\ker u\), with an alternative argument using schematic density. A comparison with the proof of 5.1 follows. The original gaps for relation signs remain visible on these typescripts.

**The example and its induction hypotheses (010–011).** After a second cover, the manuscript fixes \(f\in A\) regular on \(A\), \(B=A/fA\), and \(g\) regular on \(B\), with \(B/gB\ne0\). It proposes an \(f\)-power-torsion module with successive annihilator quotients isomorphic to \(B\). The module is said to be \(fA\)-cofinite, whereas its quotient by \(f\) has countably many copies of \(B/gB\) and is said to fail that property. Three conditions describe the proposed finite stages \(E_n\).

**Constructing and checking a stage (015, 013).** Scan 015 constructs \(E_{n+1}\) as an extension of \(A/fA\) by \(E_n\). The first diagram gives the map from \(A\) determined by \(\xi_n\). The second relates the quotient modulo \(f\) to the last annihilator quotient. The choice \(\dot{\xi}_n=(\dot g,0)\) precedes the three verifications. Scan 013 takes up the second verification, defining \(y_n=f^{n-1}\xi_n\), and computes \(E_{n+1}/fE_{n+1}\) at the end. The manuscript’s order of argument here is 015 then 013.

**The limit (017).** The transition map induces a map on the quotients \(Q_n=E_n/fE_n\). The page says that it vanishes on \(gQ_n\) and describes the image of a basis after tensoring with \(B/gB\). The displayed colimit is a countable sum of copies of \(B/gB\). Taking \(M=\varinjlim E_n\) closes the example.

**A finiteness lemma and the splitting argument (016, 014).** On the typescript’s own pages 28–29, Lemma 5.12 says that a closed subscheme of a twisted constant prescheme, quasi-compact over the base, is finite over it. In the constant case the relevant subscheme lies among finitely many components. The text applies this to implications among earlier conditions, studies the isomorphism and homomorphism preschemes \(P,Q\), and obtains pieces \(P_i\) with open and closed images. The discussion continues on 014 with the finite étale splitting cover and a criterion expressed in terms of a sequence of open and closed finite pieces of \(R\). Long diagonal cancellations cross much of both pages.

**Propagation of fibre finiteness (014, 012).** Lemma 5.13 concerns an open and closed part \(Z\) of a quasi-isotrivial twisted constant prescheme. On a connected locally Noetherian base, finiteness of one fibre implies finiteness over the base. The argument first considers the finiteness locus on an arbitrary base, then passes étale locally to a constant prescheme. At the top of 012 the distinction is whether the indexing subset \(J\) is finite.

**Connected components and isotriviality (012).** Corollary 5.14 treats connected components over a normal locally Noetherian base, by way of their generic fibres. Remark 5.15 proposes the geometrically unibranch extension. Theorem 5.16 states that a finite-type group of multiplicative type over the normal base is isotrivial. The last lines point to an isomorphism prescheme and earlier numbered results. Several words in the supplementary handwritten argument are lost under cancellation.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Reading order (high).** The printed sequence on sheets marked 28, 29 and 30 is 016, 014, 012. « leurs » on 016 continues with « images dans \(S\) » on 014. The manuscript example instead announces three verifications on 015, resumes with « la deuxième » on 013 and forms the colimit on 017. This identifies continuations without moving a leaf.

**R02 · The annihilator step on 013 (high for the algebraic distinction and the conditional inference).** From \(y_n\ne0\) in a copy of \(B\), the inference \(hy_n=0\Rightarrow h\in fA\) requires information about the annihilator of this particular \(y_n\). For the bare inference, take \(A=\mathbf F_2[e,z,t]/(e^2)\), \(f=t\), \(g=z\) and \(h=y=e\). In \(B=A/(t)\) one obtains \(y=e\ne0\), \(hy=0\) and \(h\bmod f=e\ne0\). This tests what nonvanishing alone gives under the regular-element assumptions. The construction on 015 also specifies the image of \(\dot\xi_n\) in \(E_n/{}_{f^{n-1}}E_n\simeq B\) as the regular element \(g\), under the compatible decomposition chosen there. Multiplication by \(f^{n-1}\) is injective on that quotient by definition of its denominator. Thus \(y_n\) has the same annihilator over \(B\) as \(g\), namely zero. Subject to that compatibility, the construction supplies the missing reason. The optional regular local example on 011 is a stronger specialization.

**R03 · The exponent in the last calculation on 013 (high).** The last two expressions contain one copy of \(B/gB\) next to \(n-1\) copies, and then \(n\) copies next to \(B\). The count agrees with the change from stage \(n\) to stage \(n+1\). Only the multiplicities were checked.

**R04 · Numbering and the cover (low).** The 002 heading has an exclamation-shaped exponent, while the body on 003 has a prime. The distinct shapes do not by themselves establish a change of mathematical convention. The 010 cover still allows *proobjets*, *pro objets* and *produits*. Its association with the module example follows from the physical sequence.

**R05 · Reading questions raised in the notes (low for the alternative word divisions).** The reading originally indexed S02 is on 004. The deleted sentence before 4.7 and 4.8 is transcribed « au deux corollaires ». A terminal x in « aux » might coincide with the typewritten cancellation x. The crop leaves that possible plural ending open. On 011, before « que » in the sentence announcing \(M\), the frozen « t.q. que » may represent an abbreviated *tel* followed by *que* rather than an expansion that would repeat *que*. The two small signs sit close to faint reverse printing. On 015, after « sont », the final text retains « de de | ts de » followed by a 2-shaped sign; a different division of the strokes into the opening of « toutes de la forme » is possible from the sentence, but the contracted middle does not establish those letters.
