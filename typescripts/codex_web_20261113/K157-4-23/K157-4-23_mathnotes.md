# Mathematical notes on manuscript pages 72–86

## What the pages do

**348–350, proposition 5.1.** For an arrow $f$ in $\mathrm{Cat}$, the author uses the functorial factorization $X\to\overline X(f)\to Y$, with factors $i_f$ and $p_f$. He applies it along a filtered system. Once $i$ belongs to $W$, membership of $f$ in $W$ reduces to membership of $p$. The stated fibration hypothesis then allows him to test $p$ on its fibres. Proposition 5.1 expresses $\mathrm{Loc}(8)$, or its sequence version $8\mathrm{bis}$, in terms of stability of the $W$-aspherical objects under the corresponding limits.

**350–352, 5.2–5.3 and Loc 9.** The author distinguishes the set of arrows of $\mathrm{Cat}$ from its arrow category. Loc 9 says that $W$ is an accessible part of the former, viewed as the objects of the latter. Under Loc(8) he describes $W$ as the $L_1$-envelope of a small part. He chooses the arrows of $W$ in a cardinal-bounded part of the arrow category for that purpose. He sets the size of a category $X$ equal to the cardinality of its arrows.

**352–353, proposition 5.3.1.** A size bound on the generators gives the definition of accessibility at that cardinal. In the surviving b), the author tests an adapted presentation of $f$ by the cofinality of the indices for which $f_i$ belongs to $W$. In b′) he uses a chosen presentation. He spells out the three requirements for adaptation. They concern the indexing category, preservation of specified filtered limits by the presentation, and the size of the objects it uses. An earlier formulation of the proposition is cancelled on 352.

**353–355, canonical presentation and corollary 5.3.2.** For $f:X\to Y$ he takes pairs of small subcategories $X_i,Y_i$ for which $f(X_i)\subset Y_i$. Restricting $f$ gives a diagram of arrows whose inductive limit he identifies with $f$. The corollary expresses accessibility by enlargement of a given small pair until the induced arrow lies in $W$. The inclusions in the order formula on 354 require separate attention below.

**355–356, theorem 5.4 and lemma 5.5.** The theorem asserts accessibility at the first infinite cardinal for $\mathbb W_\infty$. The author limits the argument here to accessibility. Lemma 5.5 reduces the criterion to perfect arrows. With the additional fibration hypothesis, part b) reduces it further to the class of $W$-aspherical objects, including a version in the full subcategory of 0-connected objects. A partially written extra hypothesis stands just before that equivalence.

**357–359, lemma 5.6 and completion of 5.4.** Lemma 5.6 concerns the class $\mathbb W_0$ of arrows inducing an isomorphism under $\pi_0$, and then the 0-connected objects. A bracket records that he admits lemmas here. For a pointed $X$ presented by the $X_i$, he writes a limit formula for $\Omega^1$ and iterates it. For each $n$ he takes the indices where the corresponding $\Omega^n$ is 0-connected. He asserts their cofinality and closure under the specified filtered limits, cites a cofinality result for their intersection, and uses the path-object criterion to conclude the proof.

**359–361, theorem 6.1 and lemma 6.2.** The theorem gives two possibilities for a fundamental localizer satisfying the specified $D$ condition. If the connectedness axiom fails, the author refers elsewhere for $W=\mathrm{Fl}(\mathrm{Cat})$. In the other case he proves $W\subset\mathbb W_\infty$. The comparison lemma uses $f=pi$ to pass from inclusion of aspherical objects to inclusion of arrows. To finish the theorem, he takes the fibre at $x$ of the path-category arrow $\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}(X,x,-)\to X$, proves that $\Omega^1(X,x)$ is $W$-aspherical, and uses induction and Loc(5).

**361–362, corollary 6.3.** The author identifies $\mathbb W_\infty$ as the largest fundamental localizer satisfying the displayed connectedness and fibration conditions. He then suggests a stronger descent condition that might characterize it uniquely.

**362, 6.4 a)–b).** The author compares the fibration conditions with familiar homological, cohomological and rational equivalences. He suggests a connection with Whitehead’s simple homotopy types. He asks about fundamental localizers below $\mathbb W_\infty$. The page ends in the middle of b).

- **361–362.** « Je pense que nous trouverons plus bas une caractérisation “interne” » and « comme l'unique localiseur fond. satisfaisant » introduce his hope for an axiom of descent, Loc(10), added to the preceding conditions.

## Remarks of the model

**Model remark S354-PAIR · high confidence.** Take the identity functor of the discrete category on $a,b,c$. Set $X_i=\{a,b\}$, $Y_i=\{a,b,c\}$, $X_j=\{a\}$, and $Y_j=\{a,c\}$. Both inclusions on the right of the manuscript's implication hold strictly. The $i$ pair still fails to lie in the $j$ pair. A finite check of all 729 comparisons of valid pairs on these three objects agreed with the componentwise test $X_i\subseteq X_j$ and $Y_i\subseteq Y_j$. If strictness of the pair is required, at least one component must also differ.

**Model remark R358-POINT · high confidence about the direction, low confidence about the repair.** For the inclusion of the one-object category $[0]$ at the first endpoint of $[1]$, the second endpoint has no representative at the earlier stage. This finite check isolates the extra choice that the words « son image dans $X_i$ » would require. The supplied pages leave the handling of that choice implicit.
