# K91-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Matching lifts, scans 19–20.** The opening continues a diagram argument. To find one element with two specified images, the author reduces to the nonemptiness of $P(u_0,S)$. He writes its obstruction as an Ext class for the ideal of the graph of $u_0$. Localizing kills the class. The sequence involving multiplication by a power of $\pi$ produces a Hom class modulo an image and then a class denoted $\xi''$. Its vanishing controls the extension of $u_0$.

**Compatibility, scans 20–21.** A second lifting problem gives a double-primed class, denoted by a y-shaped letter. A short exact sequence with kernel $N$ gives a further class $\xi'''$. The chosen generic and global lifts have to satisfy the same composition relation. The author introduces a class for this compatibility, admits its identification with $\xi'''$, and concludes that $\xi'''$ vanishes.

**A proposed simplification, scan 22.** The problem at the head of the page asks whether one can dispense with properness of $X,Z$ and the existence of the modules $M_u,M_v$. The proposed replacement is injectivity of a map of Hom groups for every quasi-coherent $G$. The cancelled draft below the rule tries to formulate a generic criterion with a square-zero ideal and an obstruction on an enlargement of the base.

**Lifts as splittings, scan 23.** The lifts of $u_0$ and $v_0$ are described by splittings of two extensions. A map between the extensions gives a diagram with two rows. The second row carries a specified splitting. The desired solutions should be the splittings of the first row compatible with that one.

**Two conditions on a test map, scans 24–25.** The diagram labelled (D) has rows involving $L,M,N$ and $L',M',N'$. After choosing $\lambda:J\to G$, one seeks a map $\mu:M\to G$ satisfying two restrictions. The page gives conditions in terms of $R_1$ and $R_2$. The next sheet combines them as the vanishing of $\lambda$ on $R=R_1+R_2$, or factorization through $J/R$, and asserts that formation of the quotient commutes with flat base extension.

**Corollaire 1, scan 25.** The first corollary concerns $\prod_{X/k}P/X$ for proper $X$ over an algebraically closed field. An addition qualifies the assumption on the field. The text states representability and then essential quasi-projectivity under further assumptions. The footnote at the bottom makes a fibrewise condition at points in closures of associated points.

**Representability via closed subschemes, scan 26.** For proper flat $X/S$ of finite presentation, the theorem uses finitely many closed $Z_i$ flat and of finite presentation over $S$. Its hypotheses concern the section functors on the $Z_i$, normal flatness along them, and their intersections with the closures of associated points on geometric fibres. Its conclusions include representability of the section functor on $X$, quasi-affineness of restriction on a quasi-compact open, and a quasi-projectivity assertion under further hypotheses.

**Field and generic forms, scan 27.** Corollaire 2 uses points with radicial residue fields in the closures in question. Corollaire 3 asserts representability after a finite separable extension, with question marks in the margin. Corollaire 4 treats quasi-projective fibres. Corollaire 5 passes to a nonempty open in an integral base and then to a finite étale surjective cover. Its last sentence gives a condition under which one can take $U'=U$.

**Sections and topologies, scan 28.** The opening corollary uses geometrically integral fibres and a section along which $X/S$ is smooth. It gives representability, with essential quasi-projectivity under a condition along the section. Corollaire 7 gives local representability for the étale topology. The next paragraph proposes a different topology and weaker fibre conditions. Corollaire 8 states representability of the isomorphism and automorphism functors over a field.

**Relative automorphisms, scan 29.** For proper flat $X/S$ with a noetherian integral base, Corollaire 9 obtains representability of the automorphism functor after passage to a nonempty open and a finite étale surjection. The author links dispensing with that cover to effectiveness of descent data.

**Letter to Anantharaman, scans 31–32.** The author recasts an argument of Matsumura in terms of restricting maps $X\to Y$ along $Z\to X$. He singles out the open locus on which this restriction is unramified, invokes Murre’s talk, and seeks families whose open subfunctors cover the original Hom functor. Over a field, finite subschemes with radicial points serve as tests. The letter distinguishes representability after extending the ground field from what holds over the original field. It then discusses suitable flat subschemes over a general base, hypotheses on geometric fibres, and a quasi-projective target. It ends by offering the recipient a photocopy.

## Remarks of the model

**K91-02-M1 · High confidence.** In scans 24–25 the quotient by $R_1+R_2$ records the two conditions $\lambda(R_1)=0$ and $\lambda(R_2)=0$ together. This follows from additivity and the definition of the sum of two submodules of $J$. The observation applies to the two stated vanishing conditions even though the written formula defining $R_2$ is difficult to read.

**Editorial observation · High confidence.** The sentence ending “and we get” on scan 31 continues with “that” at the top of scan 32. This makes the letter a second account, in English, of several representability questions appearing in the French notes. The letter identifies its intended reader and gives the historical references that motivate the construction.
