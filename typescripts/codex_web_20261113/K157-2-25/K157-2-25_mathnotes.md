# Mathematical notes · 157-2, scans 383–397

## What the pages do

**383–384. Inductive systems of categories.** To a system $\underline t$, Grothendieck associates the categories $C_n(\underline t)$ and their inductive limit. Comparison through a common system yields an ind-object $H$ and a canonical map $H\to C_\infty$ in $\mathrm{Ind\,Hot}$. He describes its effect on the functor restricted to $\mathrm{Hot}_0$. His marginal NB stresses that $H$ is practically never essentially constant.

**385–388. Composition of paths.** The two factors use the same terms $\tau_0^n$, with transition maps acting on opposite sides. The target uses $\tau_0^{2n}$. The levelwise composition gives a candidate map in $\mathrm{Hot}$. Grothendieck compares choices of $\tau_0$ by a square and concludes that their maps agree. He then asks whether the two maps from a triple product agree as well.

**388–391. Kan complexes as a possible reduction.** Grothendieck considers composing paths in the semi-simplicial setting and restricting to Kan complexes. Composition changes the type of a path. He asks what comparison is available after embedding $X$ in a Thomason–Kan envelope. On 391 he abandons a proposed shortcut when the categories it would use turn out to be discrete.

**390–393. External Hom and the derivator.** Section 11 interprets the path object through maps from a finite category $Y$. Formula (*) on 392 relates maps into the external Hom to a Hom in the localization of $M(Y)$. The next two expressions involve $p_!$ and $p_*$ when they exist. To allow arbitrary $Y$ in the domain, he keeps the system $(\mathrm{Ch}_{n,\overline\Sigma}(a,b))_n$ as an ind-object.

**393–396. A test using degrees.** With $X$ of the homotopy type of a two-sphere, Grothendieck takes a four-object category $Y$ of circle type. He considers which degrees its maps into the loop object can attain. He expects larger representatives $Y_n$ of the same sphere type to attain further classes, and proposes a corresponding test when a higher homotopy group is infinite.

**396–397. The canonical map for a functor category.** Evaluation $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(Y,C)\times Y\to C$ supplies the comparison labelled (*) on 396. For Thomason–Kan $C$, Grothendieck compares maps into its two sides from $\mathrm{hot}(Z)$. The margin identifies an extra property needed in the argument involving $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(Y,C)$.
