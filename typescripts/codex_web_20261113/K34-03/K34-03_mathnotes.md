Section 9.7 then treats low cohomological degrees: degree zero is left to the reader, and degree one is reduced through normalization, a finite-descent sequence, restriction to an open, generic curves, and duality. **Scan 53, Exposé II, Theorem 1.1: the arithmetic fundamental-group statement.** The author sets up the geometric/arithmetic fundamental-group extension for a normal geometrically connected finite-type situation over a field of absolute finite type. After passage to the abelian pro-ℓ part of the geometric subgroup, he asserts finite generation over Z_ℓ and finiteness of the largest quotient on which the arithmetic quotient acts trivially. The written barred/unbarred I notation is left as it stands.

**Scans 54–56, Corollaries 1.2–1.4: representation consequences.** Corollary 1.2 gives a finite kernel for the induced map between abelian pro-ℓ quotients. Corollary 1.3 is revised to an essentially solvable geometric action and an essentially unipotent conclusion, with an essentially trivial conclusion under semisimplicity. Corollary 1.4 treats an essentially abelian action of the entire fundamental group and factors it through an arithmetic image after restriction. The proof continues into the diagram on scan 56; its interrupted diagonal strokes remain an editorial uncertainty.

**Scans 56–58, Theorem 1.5 and Lemma 1.5.1: abelian schemes over a separably closed field.** The rewritten theorem applies the representation results to a Tate module and concludes essential triviality and a radicial isogeny to a constant abelian scheme after a finite étale cover. The argument descends the data to a finitely generated field, uses the arithmetic statement, and then invokes a Mordell–Weil/Inventiones result through Lemma 1.5.1. The cancelled semisimplicity hypothesis and cancelled preliminary argument remain visible in the transcription.

**Scan 58, Corollary 1.6: change of ℓ.** The stated consequence is that the revised essentially solvable, equivalently essentially trivial, condition for one admissible ℓ holds for every admissible ℓ. No separate detailed proof is given beyond the preceding construction.

**Scans 58–59, Corollary 1.7 and Remark 1.8: a constant-family criterion.** The text adds characteristic zero and trivial n-torsion for n≥3, then argues by descent and the automorphisms of the constant fibre. A hypothesis word on scan 58 remains abélienne/unipotente, so the exact final statement is not certain. Remark 1.8 says descent removes the separable-closure assumption on the ground field.

**Scans 59–61, Theorem 1.9 and §1.10: an arithmetic form.** The theorem asserts that an abelian scheme over a field of absolute finite type, with an essentially solvable Tate-module action, comes by radicial isogeny from a finite extension of the prime field after a finite compositum extension. The proof spreads the scheme to a geometric base and applies the preceding result. In positive characteristic it obtains an abelian action and also discusses the converse. The characteristic-zero portion adds a good-reduction conclusion under a heavily revised extra hypothesis.

**Scans 60–62, §1.11 and Lemma 1.12: good reduction and inertia.** The text compares Galois groups after a field extension and invokes the Néron–Ogg–Safarevitch/Schafarevitch criterion (the last surname and citation are uncertain). The margin asks for expansion and points elsewhere for residue characteristic ℓ.

**Scans 62–63, Remark 1.13: CM examples and a converse hope.** CM abelian varieties are presented as the known examples of the abelian semisimple condition. The reduction conclusions are applied to them. The author then proposes that in characteristic zero an essentially abelian, or in the inserted strengthening essentially solvable, action should imply CM type.

**Scan 63, §1.14 and Theorem 1.15: higher cohomology and motives.** The author suggests extending the method beyond abelian schemes to motives of arbitrary weight. The rewritten theorem describes the algebraic group generated on a cohomology space and asserts that the radical of its identity component is unipotent, with consequences for solvable and semisimple representations.
