# What the pages do

**U01 · Coalgebras and chains (127, 129).** Items a) and b) put the differential, the immersion of the barred base, and divided copowers on the objects indexed by n. The differential in a) has degree −1. Item c) relates the starred object to the functor V with subscript k. In d), maps from a half-simplicial X are expressed first as an inverse limit over its simplices and then as a k-linear Hom out of a direct limit. The boxed formula on 129 names the resulting de Rham chain object. The parenthesis about X empty qualifies the map into it.

**U02 · Variance and degrees (129, 131).** The left margin of 129 says how the chain construction depends on X and mentions arbitrary direct limits. Item e) follows the dependence on the coefficient data and on k′ through Hom. In f) a grading on S and a prescribed degree for t give two degrees on the presentation with X variables and differentials. The notes distinguish exterior degree from total degree and say which one the differential preserves.

**U03 · Specialization to k[u] (131, 133).** Item g) takes S to be the polynomial object k[u], with barred part k. The displayed dual is a divided-power object in T over k′. On 133 the functional t takes u to 1 and the other displayed powers to 0. The following identifications introduce the abbreviated A notation and the DR pd complexes. The bigrading is carried into the chain and cochain descriptions.

**U04 · Integral coefficients (133, 135).** The last sentence of 133 continues on 135. The chain construction over k is obtained by tensoring the integral complex with k. The page then defines coefficients in an abelian group M by tensor product for chains and Hom for cochains. Its closing sentence connects the structures on the integral complex with multiplicative structures for the functor or functors in M.

**U05 · Cancelled work and Stokes (137).** The crossed upper half compares pullbacks and limits and sketches objects over X_i and X_j. A new underlined heading begins the dualization of Stokes maps. It gives the degree-zero identity, a square relating a differential to a semi-simplicial boundary, and two maps from singular chains to de Rham chains. The two marginal computations use different degrees and contain overwritten labels.

**U06 · Explicit duals (139).** The first quotient identifies T with the sum of the X_i. On the polynomial side the map sends Z_i to u+Y_i. The two pairings compare tuples of exponents using delta symbols. The exterior-algebra calculation describes a subspace by the vanishing of the sum of coordinates and the image as the kernel of contraction. The exact sequence near the foot supplies the stated exterior-power identification. Its overwritten degree deserves care.

**U07 · Simplex coefficients (141).** The top exterior factor has a displayed generator. The S-linear map evaluated on divided-power monomials gives a power of t times a coefficient γ. An ordinary monomial integral over the coordinate simplex is multiplied by a factorial ratio. The lower half starts again with Stokes, substitutes t minus a sum of X's, and collects coefficients in a recurrence. The last γ factor continues onto the bottom line.

**U08 · The finite-level extensions (136, 134).** Typescript p. 114 finishes the proof discussed there as 9.4.7. Full faithfulness and the extension criterion are reduced to earlier assertions and the strictly local case. Section 9.5 fixes P, R, Q and the two exact sequences with middle terms F and E. On p. 115 a generic extension panachée gives finite-level pairings c(X(n)_η); Cartier duality specifies Q*(n). Their passage to a projective system gives (9.5.5).

**U09 · Essential image and prolongation (134, 132, 130).** Proposition 9.5.6 starts on p. 115. Its functor to the generic fibre is fully faithful. The stated essential image is characterized by the vanishing of a pairing. In characteristic zero, the proof on pp. 116–117 uses Tate's theorem to extend a map uniquely and to identify its kernel; it then identifies the quotient needed for the extension panachée. A remark discusses dropping the restriction on K, and a second gives an alternative route to the good-reduction criterion.

**U10 · Inertia and the monodromy pairing (130, 128).** Section 9.5.8 passes, for ℓ different from p, to twisted constant ℓ-adic sheaves and representations. Its continuation on p. 118 identifies the homomorphism with the inertia action. Section 9.6 constructs the announced pairing without the restriction on ℓ. Formula (9.6.1) specifies P,Q,R,F,E. For E the text applies Cartier duality to an extension for the primed object.

**U11 · Inertia cohomology and torsion (140).** Typescript p. 135 identifies Φ with the torsion subgroup of H¹(I,U). It writes down the finite-level Hom identifications (11.3.9), the Cartier dual (11.3.10), and the pairing of complementary degrees (11.3.11). Two passages to the limit lead to (11.3.12) and (11.3.13). The last target is written D with subscript ℓ.

**U12 · The cokernel on the closed fibre (138).** Theorem 11.5 assumes S henselian and semistable reduction. It identifies the indicated fibre of the cokernel of the tilde-u map with the displayed Φ-group. The primed version uses the transpose and yields a perfect pairing. Remark a) includes the case ℓ=p. Remark b) begins a construction using 10.4 and an integral monodromy pairing.

# Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model, high confidence (135).** Substituting k for M in the lower cochain definition places k after the closing parenthesis of C(X). The photograph of the upper display places it before that parenthesis. This local comparison is the basis of S03.

**R02 · Model, high confidence for the three cases computed (141).** Using the simplex bounds on the page, the ordinary integrals for n=1 with index 0, n=1 with index 1, and n=2 with indices (0,0) are respectively 1, 1/2, and 1/2. The factorial multiplier in the display gives γ=1 in all three cases. The two zero-index examples agree with the questioned normalization Q02. These calculations cover only the indicated dimensions and indices.

**R03 · Model, low confidence (131, degree brace, second line).** The frozen reading is a parenthesized n or i on X_r and δ alone after the equals. If this is the nth divided power of an element of total degree δ, the candidate total degree is nδ. The raised letter is doubtful and the equality lacks an explicit factor n. This is a reading question.

**R04 · Model, low confidence (139, NB display).** The frozen first exterior exponent includes an overwritten group and a visible reading n−1, with n+1 also possible. For the kernel of the sum on the displayed n+1 generators, one expects the top exterior degree of the kernel one below that of the ambient span. That relation is a reason to inspect the two exponents together. It does not decide which older pen strokes were cancelled.

**R05 · Model, low confidence (127 d, first Hom line; 129, boxed line; 139, generators).** The frozen TeX uses ordinary X and A. The simplicial X has a sweeping, almost horizontal return stroke, different from the two diagonals on the coordinate variables X_i. A script or calligraphic X is another possible reading, and the looped A's raise the same alphabet question. The relative shapes are clearer than the choice of a LaTeX alphabet.

**R06 · Model, low confidence (137, square; 139, last line; 141, first map).** The frozen text has candidates k, π and κ on the square, and π on the two following sheets. In the last-line map on 139 and the top factor map on 141, an oblique arm makes κ another possible reading. In 141's lower expansion the exponent and index positions also alternate. The handwritten labels should be read locally before identifying the maps by name.

**R07 · Model, low confidence (137, Stokes square, upper edge).** The frozen diagram gives the horizontal line from S a right arrowhead. Its right end shows one small rise and a pool of ink, whereas the lower arrow has two distinct oblique strokes. A plain horizontal line with a thickened endpoint is a second possible reading.

**R08 · Model, low confidence (141, Stokes display, annotation below the right member).** The frozen reading of the differential under the brace is dY₀. The letter has a fork above one dark stroke. It could also be the writer’s narrow X, giving dX₀. The X in the factor immediately above and the X with subscript n−2 on the continuation supply local comparisons, but neither establishes the identity of this letter.
