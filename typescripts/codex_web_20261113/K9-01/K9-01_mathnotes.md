# Mathematical notes on cote 9, scans 2–15

## What the pages do

**Duality and the reduced module (2–3).** The cover pairs the subject of dual Barsotti–Tate groups with the reduction \(M\otimes_W k\). The next sheet sets \(G\) and its dual \(G^*\) side by side. One exact sequence has middle term \(H(G)\), the other the tangent space of \(\widetilde G\). The writer draws Frobenius-twisted rows beneath them, labels the maps \(F_0,V_0\), and uses their kernels and images to describe the reduced Dieudonné module. The corollary associates infinitesimality of \(G\), of \(G^*\), and of both with three different nilpotence conditions.

**A functional on \(A_n\) (5).** The writer sets \(\langle x,y\rangle=\varphi_n(xy)\) and boxes the values at \(1,F^i,V^i\) for \(i\geq1\). Conditions b) and c) move an \(F\) or \(V\) across the argument with a \(\pi\) on the resulting value. In the working below, a scalar \(a\) is separated from terms with powers of \(F\) and \(V\), and the line \(\varphi_n(z)=a\) survives. The scalar computations receive « OK » marks. The last \(Fz\) computation stops at an equals sign.

**Two duals (7).** With \(A_n=A/(F^{n+1},V^{n+1})\), the writer takes \(I_n=A_n\) as an \(A\)-bimodule, puts the \(I_n\) into a direct limit, and writes a Hom duality \(D\). He draws attention to the change of side for a module and invokes the involution exchanging \(F\) and \(V\). A second construction \(D'\) uses \(\operatorname{Hom}_{W_n}(M,W_n)\). An adjunction calculation and an identification of the bimodule with its dual occur in the crossed working below.

**Actions on the dual and their pairing (8).** The sheet writes the right \(F,V,\lambda\) actions on \(D'(M)\), then actions on \(D'(A_n)\) with subscripts distinguishing the side of multiplication. A warning concerns two \(W_n\)-module structures. The writer places a question mark over the proposed bimodule isomorphism and spells out scalar, \(F\)- and \(V\)-compatibilities for a pairing \(A_n\times A_n\to W_n\). At the foot a three-sided enclosure brings together the tensor product over \(A_n\) and two of the compatibility identities.

**A cancelled attempt at a connection (9).** The sheet asks for a direct construction of an integrable connection on \(\mathbb R^*f_*(\Omega^\cdot_{X/S})\). In a proposed lemma it starts with complexes on a topos, a sheaf mapping into degree \(-1\) Hom, and a torsor with a map \(\theta\). The displayed formula for \(\theta(g.p)\) has the sum of two differential terms under a brace marked \(\delta(i(g))\). The intended output is a map \(K^\cdot\to L^\cdot\) in \(D(X)\), accompanied by conditions about functoriality and replacement of the data. The entire sheet is cancelled, and the third condition breaks off.

**Monodromy and virtual good reduction (10–11).** After the title sheet the typescript starts with a discrete valuation ring that the writer allows himself to take complete. A handwritten addition specifies a perfect residue field. It names a condition on a Barsotti–Tate group over its fraction field \(K\). The group should have a composition series whose graded pieces acquire good reduction after a finite separable extension. A Galois choice of \(K'\) supplies actions on the good-reduction models. Map (1) takes the corresponding Grothendieck group to a group over \(S'\) with that action.

**The inertia character (12).** Map (2) restricts to the closed point. A covariant functor then takes the group to a free module over \(W(k')\), retaining the group action, and gives map (3). Composition yields (4). The next step restricts to inertia \(I\). A marginal note tracks the action on \(I\) and on \(W(k')\). In (5) the last map is the trace. Its value on the class of \(M\) is called the character. There are revisions in the exponents on this portion of the page.

**Comparison with the modular character (12–13).** The comparison starts under the hypothesis that \(M_{K'}\) is constant. On elements of inertia whose order is prime to \(p\), the writer identifies the character with the modular character of the representation on \(M(0)\). He asks whether the latter group determines the character in general, invokes Brauer’s virtual lifts, and gives \(\mathbf Z_p(1)\) with \(p\)-torsion \(\mu_p\) when the field lacks the \(p\)-th roots of 1 as the obstruction. He distinguishes constant and multiplicative reduction from an ind-unipotent case still to examine.

**The test with elliptic curves (13–14).** The writer proposes two elliptic curves with potentially good reduction and isomorphic \(p\)-torsion, over a field containing the \(p\)-th roots of 1. He asks whether their trace functions agree on the indicated elements of inertia. He singles out a curve reducing with zero Hasse invariant. A related question starts with an abelian scheme \(A_K\) whose \(p\)-torsion is constant and asks for the value \(n=\dim A_K\). In the elliptic case he discusses the smallest semistable extension when the residue characteristic is different from 2 and 3. A marginal « faux par Tate » accompanies the paragraph.

**Deformations and determinants (15).** A cancelled title about ordinary Barsotti–Tate groups gives way to a title about canonical stratifications on deformations. Below « L'isom. de nature globale » the box compares determinants of the étale parts of \(T_p(A)\) and \(T_p(A^*)\). It gives no construction on this sheet. Scans 4 and 6 carry Pisa stationery. The Italian vocabulary exercise and street plan are on scan 6.

## Questions and conjectures

- **3, item 4.** A gutter « ? » stands near « Le composé » and its connection with « la puissance \(p\)-ième des dérivations ». Its reach across items 3 and 4 is uncertain.
- **7–8, bimodule identification.** The proposal « isom de bimodules » carries a question mark above the arrow. The lower block on 7 had already been crossed.
- **9, whole cancelled sheet.** « Comment définir directement une connexion intégrable … ?? » introduces the problem. The third property is only « Si \(P\) admet une section, alors ».
- **13, general determination.** The question whether the character « est déterminé ainsi par la seule connaissance de \(M(\mathrm o)\) » receives the answer « Il n'en est rien ». Later the marginal « Cela impliquerait que la fonction caractère est à valeurs dans » \(\mathbb Z_p\subset W(k')\) is conditional. The index in its qualification is doubtful.
- **13, remaining reduction case.** « il conviendrait alors d'examiner encore le cas où il est de type ind-unipotent » precedes the elliptic « Cas test ». In that test « sont égales … ? » is qualified by the addition « sur les éléments premiers à \(p\) ».
- **14, constant torsion.** « est-il vrai que la trace » \(\phi_E\) « soit … égale à \(n=\dim A_K\) » on the specified elements of \(I\)? The next sentence calls for « un exemple où le critère de réduction semi-stable de Raynaud est en défaut pour \(\ell=p\) ».
- **14, special case.** « la question revient à celle de savoir si » the trace has constant value 2. A separate (?) points into the sentence mentioning the roots of unity. « faux par Tate » is a marginal objection, without an explicit identification of the precise assertion it rejects.

## Remarks of the model

- **R08, high confidence about the written distinction.** The upper formula on scan 8 reads \(u.V=\pi(u\circ F)\). In the lower action table it reads \(u.V=\pi(u\circ V_g)\). The rightmost capitals differ visibly, and the latter has a subscript. The formulas concern two presentations of the dual. The intended relationship would require further argument.
- **R12, high confidence about the endpoints.** Across scans 11–12 the target of (1) reappears at the start of (2), with \(S'\). The target of (2) reappears in (3), with the closed point \(s'\). This accounts for the source and target written in (4). The semicolon and comma usage varies between those occurrences.
- **R14, high confidence about the arithmetic.** The only prime divisors of the three printed numbers 2, 4 and 6 are 2 and 3. Under the stated residue-characteristic restriction, the numerical implication « donc est premier à \(p\) » follows if the asserted group orders apply. The classification of the group is a separate claim on the sheet.
- **R14b, low confidence about the intended subscript.** The second question on scans 13–14 starts with \(A_K\), yet the trace on scan 14 has a subscript \(E\), as in the preceding elliptic example. A carry-over of the letter is possible. The general question includes an elliptic example and the following paragraph returns to it, so identifying this as an error would go beyond the writing.
