# K29-07 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Transposition of group morphisms, scan 95.** Corollaire 3.2 takes a map of ordinary commutative groups over a nonempty base and compares it with the transposed homomorphism of diagonalizable groups. Its two parts pair injectivity with faithful flatness and surjectivity with a monomorphism that is also a closed immersion. The proof uses a module basis for one direction and a kernel described using the cokernel for the other.

**Exact sequences, scan 95.** Corollaire 3.3 starts with three groups and two labelled maps. In the transposed sequence the first map induces a faithfully flat, quasi-compact morphism onto the kernel of the second. The typescript identifies that kernel as diagonalizable.

**Kummer covering and the inertia term, scan 96.** A sum involving γ and the βᵢ precedes a Kummer covering construction. The exact-sequence drawing places a μ-group between a second tame homotopy group and the first tame group of the ambient space. A long return arrow and a bracket are separate marks. The following paragraph relates the extension to a degree-two class and calls it a self-intersection.

**Curve example, scans 96 and 98.** Exemple I splits into the case outside the boxed projective-line condition and the case in which it holds. The first calls the extension trivial. The second computes a finite μ-group using the part of the self-intersection degree prime to the characteristic. The hypotheses and the geometric base points should be read with their individual doubt marks.

**Kernels, quotients and factorization, scan 97.** Corollaire 3.4 treats a morphism of locally diagonalizable group preschemes with a finite-type hypothesis added above the line. Part (a) concerns its kernel. Part (b) gives the quotient by the kernel and effectiveness of descent. Part (c) factors the morphism through this quotient and describes the final map as a closed immersion. The proof passes locally to groups written as D applied to ordinary groups.

**Arithmetic example, scan 98.** Exemple II first takes the base to be Spec ℤ and works through the empty and nonempty divisor cases. A product over primes is inserted beside the remark about the abelian part and class fields. The writer asks about the full group and about π₂ after removing a nonempty set S.

**The n-kernel, scan 99.** Corollaire 3.5 identifies the kernel of multiplication by a nonzero integer on a diagonalizable group. In the presentation G = Dₛ(M), the proof writes the kernel as Dₛ(M/nM) and cites earlier numbered assertions for integrality and finiteness.

**Principal homogeneous bundles, scan 99.** Section 4 introduces principal homogeneous bundles under a diagonalizable group in the faithfully flat quasi-compact topology. The sheet recalls the local trivialization with the right translation action, deduces affineness by reference, and begins the description of an affine action by a graded quasi-coherent algebra. The last sentence continues past the sheet.

**Local and semi-global inertia, scan 100.** A cancelled finite-index subgroup argument and a square precede two headings naming Abhyankar. For a normal-crossings divisor, the semi-global version puts a Henselian condition on the pair (X,Z). Parts (a)–(c) address connectedness and surjectivity, the inertia groups and their quotient presentation, and the kernel generated by the commuting groups. The argument about residual characteristics and the local argument for commutation are unfinished here.

**Kummer equations and a characteristic class, scan 101.** Starting from effective Cartier divisors, integers and an extension by a product of roots of unity, the sheet describes a torsor and a local presentation with equations involving powers of the Tᵢ. The extension gives an element in degree two. A map labelled φ, a sideways diagram and a Chern-class annotation lead to the equality the writer wants to prove. A final numbered line proposes a formal-scheme variant.

**Restriction to the divisor, scan 103.** A corollary for a normal scheme and a Galois cover uses inertia to form a quotient with group Γ. The draft passes from the extension class to its restriction on the divisor and compares that restriction with the class of the normal bundle. Several formulations of the inertia group and a paragraph choosing a geometric point have been struck out.

**Conjugation and quasi-unipotence, scan 104.** This entire sheet is cancelled. Equation (6.4) is a relation between group elements. Lemme 6.5 recasts it for operators F and G on a finite-dimensional vector space. The proposed conclusion is quasi-unipotence of G. The proof tracks eigenvalues under λ ↦ λᵠ and breaks off after introducing two exponents.

**A functor for π₂, scan 105.** In the simply connected case the writer presents second cohomology with finite torsion coefficients as a functor and identifies it with continuous homomorphisms from an underlined π₂(X). For general X the page invokes the pointed universal cover. A pencil drawing has three labelled sheets. A separate group of broad marker symbols is oriented the opposite way.

**Unramified morphisms, scan 106.** The page finishes an argument about a section and its image. Proposition (17.4.10) tests whether a locally finitely presented morphism over S is unramified by the induced maps of fibres. The accompanying paragraph appeals to an earlier fibre criterion and compares the two inverse images of y.

**Smooth morphisms, scan 106.** Théorème (17.5.1) gives conditions at x involving smoothness, flatness and smoothness of the fibre, regularity, and formal smoothness of the local rings for the discrete topologies. The proof introduces a polynomial algebra and an ideal. Handwritten arrows and a replacement of the word « noethérien » qualify the argument.

**A local descent problem, scan 107.** The drawing places a dotted Z across two branches. Two primed local intersections are expressed using localizations of a completed ring, while the arrow diagram relates the corresponding fundamental groups. The notes below identify a descent theorem and certain canonical homomorphisms as work still to be done.

**Polynomial quotients and comaximal ideals, scan 108.** The first calculation starts with P = QQ′ and QR + Q′R′ = 1. The margin writes out an element of the intersection as a sum of two terms. A lemma lower down separates the product/intersection identity from solving two congruences. The last diagram compares surjectivity, bijectivity and conditions on the ideals.

**Memorandum on Bourbaki, scan 109.** The unidentified writer regrets Grothendieck’s departure from Bourbaki, criticizes the treatment of the functorial point of view, and suggests adopting his book on categories or issuing a fascicle of results from it. A divider introduces a list of questions whose contents are absent from this sheet.

**Decompositions and monic factors, scan 110.** Part (a) compares a product decomposition of a quotient with a pair of comaximal ideals. Part (b) specializes to a quotient by a monic polynomial and seeks monic factors. The sheet discusses uniqueness up to a unit, inserts a connectedness qualification, and begins a reduction to a local base for existence.

**Completion and drawings, scan 111.** Below a cancelled sentence the page pairs A, X and D with their hatted forms. The drawings associate marked points and sections of two boxes with hatted products. An H is linked to a product beside « composante connexe ». The lower diagram relates schemes and rings. The page gives no prose conclusion tying all these labels together.
