# Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 126, 124, 122 and 120, the English typescript.** In its own order, pages 74, 76, 77 and 78 treat a map to projective space defined by sections. Page 74 uses the surjection in (19) to contradict simultaneous vanishing on the indicated subvariety, then chooses a family of sections for the map. Page 76 compares values of coordinate quotients at Q and Q′. Page 77 writes a local function as a quotient of polynomials and obtains its pullback by replacing the coordinates with sections. Page 78 finishes the regularity calculation, cuts the image by a hyperplane and arrives at $\dim V^{(m)}=s$. The announced one-to-one assertion extends beyond the calculation visible here. The four page files follow the scan order.

**Scans 121 and 123, polarizations and cocharacters.** The opening question asks whether the automorphisms of the real data act transitively on polarizations. For motives over a finite field the author then singles out a conjugacy class of homomorphisms $i$, relates it to a normalized valuation and considers its conjugates. The note attached to “forme” questions integrality on a subgroup and gives numerical examples. On 123 the differences $i-i'$ are said to generate or define a subgroup of finite index. The author distinguishes the resulting choice of real fibre functors from what is known about the restrictions of the differences.

**Scans 123 and 125, conjugation.** Stability under complex conjugation supplies an involution written $u\mapsto u'$. The text gives its behavior on $\operatorname{Ker}j^*$ and $\operatorname{Im}j$. The short next sheet doubts that this involution lifts to the gerbe. Its explanation mentions a fundamental element and a divisibility condition on its order.

**Scan 128, totally real fields.** The author defines total reality and total positivity using every embedding in $\mathbb C$. He passes from elements and generating subfields to the maximal totally real subfield, then describes the corresponding closed normal subgroup in terms of the conjugates of complex conjugation. The page introduces its abelian quotient $H$.

**Scans 129–130, the extension above the maximal totally real field.** The quotient considered on 129 is generated by images of conjugates of an involution. Three conditions characterize the distinguished quadratic extension: agreement of the conjugations on it, a quadratic Galois condition over $\mathbb Q$, and pure imaginarity. The author gives an existence construction with an imaginary quadratic field. Scan 130 characterizes its subfields, gives the field diagram used in the necessity argument and describes centrality of the image of complex conjugation.

**Scans 131–132, a fixed value of the product with the conjugate.** The proposition puts the condition $\lambda_\alpha\bar\lambda_\alpha=Q$ at every conjugate alongside descriptions using a totally real field, the element $a=\lambda+\bar\lambda$, the polynomial $T^2-aT+Q$, and membership in the distinguished extension. Here $Q$ is the scalar and $\mathbb Q$ is the field. The proof uses the real conjugates of $a$, the discriminant and a relative norm. A separate proposed corollary is crossed out in full.

**Scans 132–133, construction in the remark.** Starting with a finite totally real $K$ and rational $Q>1$, the author chooses a generator $a$ and makes $4Q^i-a^2$ totally positive by choosing $i$ large. He takes a root of $\lambda^2-a\lambda+Q^i=0$. The continuation takes an $i$-th root, discusses its absolute value and the field it generates, and returns to integrality.

**Scan 135, reducing the group of a gerbe.** The subcategory generated by the Tate object supplies the map to the multiplicative group. Choosing a fibre functor there leads to a gerbe of fibre functors equipped with an isomorphism on that subcategory. The next paragraph defines a subcategory using finitely many simple objects in tensor constructions. Its group is the target of a quotient with neutral component as kernel. A choice of algebraic closure appears in the last lines.

**Scan 137, twists and their fibre functor.** A section on the primed subcategory gives a gerbe with band $G^\circ$. The author points out that the trivialization is a particular choice. He then writes objects as finite sums of twists $V_i(i)$, gives the tensor and dual formulas and evaluates the coefficients at $\bar k$. The gerbe and group diagrams below identify a product group and display a further kernel $U$.

**Scan 138, amendments to a topology text.** The sheet replaces “métrisable” with “séparé” in two places. It inserts a binary expansion map, removes a countable family of sequences and calls the restriction a continuous bijection. Another insertion treats countable sums and projective limits of Lusin spaces. A starred assertion concerns spaces of distributions with their strong topology. The last instructions change topology terminology and add a closure expression. The addition breaks off after “(pour”.

## Slips

- **121, the bracketed valuation identity (S121).** The two valuations are adjacent where the valuation of the product follows. An intervening plus sign would give the additive valuation identity. Confidence **high** that the visible juxtaposition lacks a plus. The margin supplies the normalization $v_{\mathfrak p}(q)=1$.
- **131, proof of (a) ⇒ (b″) (S131).** After deriving $4Q-a_\alpha^2\geq0$, the text concludes « donc $4Q-a^2$ est tot positif ». The proposition itself allows the value zero. “Nul ou totalement positif” would preserve that case. Confidence **high**. The example $Q=1$, $\lambda=1$ gives $a=2$ and $4Q-a^2=0$.
- **126, word choice in the English paragraph.** “Basis (not necessarily of linearly independent vectors)” apparently uses “basis” for a generating family. The parenthesis supplies the qualification. This is a terminology observation, with **medium** confidence about the intended English.
- **133, first line.** The product is followed by a small uncertain glyph after $Q$. The preceding construction uses $Q^i$. That suggests a possible missing or misplaced exponent, with **low** confidence. The position and state of the ink do not establish a correction.

## Remarks of the model

- **R120, high confidence.** The typescript numbering gives a way to read across the interleaving. In particular, the restriction sequence explicitly tagged (19) on page 74 is the one named on page 78. This identification uses the tag and the matching restriction map.
- **R121, high confidence.** In the normalization written on 121, take the rational element $\lambda=q$, fixed by conjugation. Both individual valuations are 1. Their product is 1 and the valuation of $q^2$ is 2. This tests the missing-sign observation S121.
- **R131, high confidence.** The equality case used in S131 already satisfies the first condition of the proposition. The qualifier “nul” in (b″) is therefore substantive. This observation checks the boundary case only.
- **R138, high confidence.** With the sheet's index starting at zero, the sequences $(1,0,0,\ldots)$ and $(0,1,1,\ldots)$ both map to 1. The displayed definition of D removes the first and keeps the second. This checks one instance of the asserted injectivity mechanism.
- **Reading question, 132, last line.** The final text reads « à l'étant ». The preceding small character could be the generator $a$, giving « $a$ l'étant ». Its accent and mathematical status were outside the frozen doubt mark. Confidence **low** about that character.
