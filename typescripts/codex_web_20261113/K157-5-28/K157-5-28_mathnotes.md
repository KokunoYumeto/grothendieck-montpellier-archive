# Mathematical notes on scans 437–452

Grothendieck, A. (n.d.). *Les Dérivateurs* [Handwritten manuscript, Montpellier, cote 157-5, autograph pp. 349–364]. Scan numbers below refer to this manuscript.

## What the pages do

**U28 · Proposition 4.13.28, scan 437.** Grothendieck states that the collection $\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\pi}^{\Phi}$ of $\Phi$-$\pi$-accessible subsets of $M$ is closed under finite unions when every $J\in\Phi$ is filtered. The strengthened version places $<\pi$ on the cardinal $\lambda$, assumes every $J\in\Phi$ is « grde devant $\lambda$ », and allows unions indexed by a set of cardinal at most $\lambda$.

**U29 · Corollaire 4.13.29, scan 437.** A small family of accessible subsets of $\mathrm{Ob}\,M$ has accessible union. In the first formulation the author adds the word “petites” in quotation marks.

**U30 · Lemme 4.13.30, scan 438.** A family of full subcategories $J_r$ of $I$ has index set of cardinal at most $\pi$. The final hypothesis is that the union of their objects is cofinal in $\mathrm{Ord}(I)$. The author states that some $J_r$ is cofinal in $I$. An earlier formulation required the union to contain every object.

**U31 · Théorème 4.13.31, scan 438.** For an accessible functor $f:M\to N$ between accessible categories, the inverse image of an accessible subset of $\mathrm{Ob}\,N$ is accessible in $M$. The text gives both the subset and full-subcategory formulations.

**U32 · Corollaire 4.13.32 and its proof, scans 438–441.** The author specifies two cardinals and four conditions on the target subcategory, $M$ and $f$. He checks preservation of the relevant limits and constructs a fully faithful functor from $\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi}\,\underline S(\pi)$ to $\underline S$. To show essential surjectivity, he represents an object by an adapted inductive system, applies $f$ and passes to a cofinal $I'$. The proof ends with « qed ». Two changes of ambient letter are recorded under *Slips*.

**U33 · Questions 4.13.33, scans 441–443.** The author turns from inverse images to essential images and asks for a factorization by accessible functors. He then asks about accessibility of the full subcategory generated by a small $A$ under specified classes of limits. A further version adds limits indexed by members of a small set $\Phi$ of small categories. The body and the two marginal responses give different stages of his assessment.

**U34 · Proposition 4.13.34 and its proof, scans 443–445.** In an accessible $M$ with small filtered colimits, he equates accessibility and stability under those colimits for $\underline S$ with generation from a small strictly full $A$ under the same colimits. The forward argument uses $\underline S(\pi)=\underline S\cap M_\pi$. For the reverse direction he chooses $\pi$, forms $B$ inside $M_\pi$, takes the essential image $\underline S'$ of $\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi}(B)$, and reduces equality with $\underline S$ to stability of $\underline S'$.

**U35 · Définition 4.13.35 and NB, scan 446.** The author calls a subset $S$ of $\mathrm{Ob}\,M$ $L$-accessible when the corresponding full subcategory is accessible in $M$ and stable under small filtered colimits. The right-hand note records closure operations and an inverse-image assertion. The inclined note glosses the relevant functor condition by commutation with small filtered colimits. The low mark beside $L$ varies in clarity.

**U36 · Proposition 4.13.36 and proof discussion, scans 446–447.** Under revised stability hypotheses, the author compares $\pi$-accessibility with cofinality in the slice over $x$, and with cofinality of the indices selected by $\underline S$ in an adapted representation of $x$. Variant c′ uses an ordered $I$. A bracketed c″ fixes one representation for each $x$. The proof discussion isolates the return from c′ to a and calls for a separate argument.

**U37 · Lemme 4.13.37, scans 447–449.** The aim is an $L$-$\pi$-adapted representation with ordered index category. Starting from $x$ represented over $J$, the author orders small filtered subcategories $J'$ of $J$ by inclusion and assigns to each the colimit over $J'$. To majorize a family of at most $\pi$ such subcategories, he forms the subcategory $K$ they generate and introduces a second lemma.

**U38 · Lemme 4.13.38 and construction, scans 449–452.** A subcategory $K$ of cardinal at most $\pi$ in $J$ « grde devant $\pi$ » is to lie in a filtered $K'$ of cardinal at most $\pi$. The qualification about obtaining a final object is repeatedly revised at the foot of 449 and continues on 450. The proof chooses maps $\alpha_k:k\to j$, equalizes pairs and distinguishes cases according to the position of the resulting object $e$. The remaining case supplies an endomorphism $p$ and maps $f_k$. On 452 the author begins checking closure of the enlarged collection of arrows under composition. The last diagram is followed by « que » at the edge of the supplied text.

## Statements left without proof

- **U28–U30, scans 437–438.** « Je n’explicite pas les démonstrations » follows the union results. Lemme 4.13.30 is stated immediately afterward. No separate proof of the lemma appears before Théorème 4.13.31.
- **U32, scan 439.** « L’existence d’un tel couple … résulte aussitôt de 4.13.16 » delegates the choice of cardinals to earlier material. A second reference is inserted. The subsequent proof concerns accessibility of the inverse image.
- **U35, scan 446.** The NB asserts stability « par petites intersections, et par réunions finies » and that the indicated inverse image « est $L$-accessible ». The note gives no proof of these closure assertions.
- **U36–U37, scans 447–449.** « Il suffit pour cela … d’invoquer le » introduces the ordered-index lemma. In its construction, « je dis que » introduces stability of $I$, commutation by the functor and membership of its values in $M_\pi$. The written argument develops the remaining common-upper-bound requirement.
- **U38, scan 452.** « Il faut voir que c’est stable par composition » is followed by a calculation. The end of that proof lies beyond the last supplied word.

## Questions and conjectures

- **U32, scan 440.** The inclined note asks for terminology in the form « Cette commutation devrait avoir un nom ». It proposes a condition written $L(\pi_0,\pi)$ on a functor.
- **U33, scans 441–442.** « Je conjecture » introduces the claim about « les images essentielles ». Beside it stands « Faux, = si on prend la » followed by the doubtful capital and « clôture de l’image essentielle ». On 442 the related question is « se factorise-t-il en un foncteur (accessible) ess. surjectif, et un foncteur accessible pl. fidèle ». The inclined qualification begins « Il faut se borner tout au moins » and ends « très optimiste ». The body retains the conditional « Si oui ».
- **U33, scans 442–443.** « Je conjecture que $\underline S$ est accessible dans $M$ » concerns the category generated from $A$ using the first class of limits. « Je conjecture encore que $\underline S$ est accessible » follows the addition of limits of types in $\Phi$. The ensuing proposition is called a « résultat très partiel ».

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence, scans 441–443.** The verdict « Faux » is placed alongside the essential-image conjecture. The later question beginning « Voici une question » introduces a new $M$ and $A$ and receives its own formulation on 443. The physical placement gives no basis for extending the marginal verdict to that second question.
- **Model remark, high confidence, scans 449–452.** The left NB on 449 states that ordered $J$ would suffice for the preceding lemma. The body nevertheless retains the longer discussion of $e$, including the endomorphism case on 451–452. This observation concerns the organization of the surviving text.
- **Model remark, low confidence, scan 452, lower calculation beginning $p(v_1u_2)=p$.** The frozen first exponent in the next expression allows *8*, *s* or a correction over *r*. Repeated $s-1$ and $s$ favour *s* in context. The closed loop and dense ink prevent a choice on the letterform alone. The separate low stroke after the parenthesized $p$ is also unresolved.
