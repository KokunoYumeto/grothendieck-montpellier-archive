## Legibility

The main French text, the sequence of statement numbers and most free mathematical letters are clear. The hardest readings are the upper-left note and heavy joins on 437, the revised parameter conditions on 439, the marks beside the two subscripts on 441, the global hypothesis and crossed a′ on 446, and the successive qualifications at the foot of 449. On 451, rotating the left edge resolves the end of the long note but leaves its middle uncertain. On 452 the words under the initial strike, the marked exponent and the small stroke after p in the last calculation remain the least secure local readings. Closed pen loops, thick horizontal joins and very short arrowheads make it especially difficult to distinguish writing over an old form from simple retracing. The last word, que, is visible at the foot of 452. The continuation is outside this set.
