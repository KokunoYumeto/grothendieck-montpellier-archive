## What the pages do

**Scan 32 · Two elementary subgroups.** The two inverse modules defined in (11) supply upper and lower triangular unipotent matrices. The page records multiplication and conjugation by the diagonal element $u(\lambda)$, the intersections with the diagonal subgroup $T$, and descriptions of the two triangular subgroups $B_0,B_1$.

**Scan 33 · Representatives of the other coset.** An element $\beta$ of $L_0^*$ gives an antidiagonal matrix. Its square is central, and it reverses the parameter of the diagonal subgroup under conjugation. The resulting bijection with $N^-$ is described by left multiplication for one parameter and by right multiplication for the other. The note beside (19) restricts the discussion to the free-module case.

**Scan 34 · Transporters.** The boxed relation (25) relates the two elementary maps, and the diagonal margin extends it to a scalar multiple of the parameter. The conjugate in (28) gives a condition on the top left entry of a matrix carrying $U_0$ into $U_1$. Equation (29) gives the two transporter conditions. The paragraph below explains why the inclusion obtained here is an isomorphism.

**Scan 35 · Products of three elementary elements.** Intersecting the transporters recovers $N^-$. When $\beta\gamma=-1$, the three-factor product gives the antidiagonal representative. Combining that representative with an elementary factor gives a matrix whose cube is 1. A general product, followed by a five-part equivalence, spells out how the scalar condition can be recognized. [M35.1, M35.2]

**Scans 36–37 · Recovering the pairing.** The page describes how to recover the pairing and the representative from the two subgroups and their module structures. The braid relation (38) describes a bijection on generators. The operation marked by a raised check reverses the scalar exponent in (40). Scan 37 contrasts this with the module-compatible map induced by a transporter element. Its last, cancelled paragraph begins a statement about $T\sqcup N^-$.

**Scan 39 · Three coordinate objects.** In an abelian category, the projections to pairs give isomorphisms. The cokernel $D$ of the map to the product identifies the three $V_i$ with a common object. A second exact sequence exhibits the corresponding $V$ as the kernel of the sum map $D^I\to D$. The passage ends with a categorical equivalence expressed using $\mathrm{Ens}_3$.

**Scans 39–40 · A proposed construction on sets.** The author asks for a noncommutative analogue. A choice of orientation orders the three groups and proposed bitorsors around a triangle. A central element of a contracted product would specify the coordinate triples making up $V$. The final paragraph discusses what changes if one group is commutative and an origin is chosen.

**Scan 41 · Automorphisms.** A remark discusses permutations of the three labels, compatible automorphisms of the groups and a coordinatewise action. The three explicit coordinate formulas specify the two group factors acting on each coordinate. Two margins revisit the common centre and its diagonal embedding. The heavily struck left margin does not support an unqualified formula for the full automorphism group.

**Scans 41–42 · Four labels and reconstruction.** An ordering of four indices gives an isomorphism between the first two coordinate objects. The next page writes an inverse relation and a composition relation. It seeks a subobject of the product by imposing a zero-sum condition on each complementary triple. It then asks when the proposed object's projection to two coordinates is bijective.

**Scans 43–44 · The four-index ratio.** Two maps with the same source and target give the ratio in (4). Swapping the last two labels gives the inverse. Swapping the first two gives a transported inverse, with conjugation explained in a marginal note. The page counts the indexed quantities and groups them by these symmetries. Scan 44 then introduces the exchange of the two middle labels and boxes the complement relation (7).

**Scans 44–45 · Criterion and commutative case.** The proposition formulates all 24 instances of (7) as a condition for the data to come from the proposed coordinate object. When the endomorphism rings are commutative, the ratios can be viewed in one ring $k$. On its elements for which both $\lambda$ and $1-\lambda$ are units, the page records inversion and complementation, and describes the map from orderings of $I$ to $U(k)$ as compatible with the permutation action.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence [M35.1, M35.2].** Direct matrix multiplication reproduces the general product on scan 35. Imposing $\gamma=-\beta^{-1}$ gives the antidiagonal matrix, and cubing the matrix $\sigma_0(\beta)e_0(\beta)$ gives the identity. This checks the calculation independently of the doubtful equation number.
- **Model remark, high confidence for the example [M41.1]; low for the author’s intention.** Scan 41 has “card J=3”. Scan 42 discusses a projection to a pair. These conditions differ. Over $\mathbb Q$, the four forms $x,y,z,x+y+z$ on $\mathbb Q^3$ give an isomorphism on every triple, while the projection to $(x,y)$ has a kernel. The example explains why the numeral matters; it does not determine a replacement for it.
- **Model remark, high confidence [M42.1].** On the 24 orderings of four distinct labels, exchanging the first two labels makes 12 pairs. This gives the numerical count beside (2) without using the uncertain composition formula (3).
- **Model remark, high confidence [M45.1].** On the stated unit domain the two scalar transformations are involutions, and their composite has cube one. The calculation checks the relations underlying the stated action. The geometric reconstruction and the sufficiency claim on scans 44–45 require a separate argument.
