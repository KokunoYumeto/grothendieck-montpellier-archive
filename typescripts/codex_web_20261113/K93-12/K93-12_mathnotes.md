# Mathematical notes on cote 93, scans 32–34 and 36–46

## What the pages do

**Scans 34 and 32, typed pp. 5 and 6.** In the typescript's own order, (iii) decomposes Y into the fixed-point space and the kernel of θ. The proof uses a weak-operator closure S and identifies a fixed point in an orbit with θ(y). Page 6 uses finite convex combinations to pass to strong convergence. It then turns to countably additive and purely finitely additive members of sba(A,X), referring forward for a compactness assertion. The fragment preserves the archive's order, in which p. 6 comes first.

**Scan 33, conditions 3°–6°.** Two injectivity conditions relate orientations along Y to its “rives”. The next conditions concern local existence and separation of the space of orientations over X. The last parenthesis moves from nearby points to an orientation known at a single point of a connected space. Marginal braces attach hypotheses and a principle of identity to parts of the list.

**Scan 36, exponential substitution.** Starting from g = exp f, the writer differentiates three times and combines the quotients g‴/g′ and g″/g′. He crosses the two 6f″ terms, compares expressions called G and C, and boxes an equation for C indexed by exp f. A trial with φ = exp t follows. Writing from the opposite edge discusses G(z,f), differential expressions and how the arguments transform. Several coefficients and the circled pen gloss remain doubtful.

**Scans 37–38, title and functor.** “V Programme de la Théorie (Cristaux)” introduces the construction of a Dieudonné crystal for a BT group over a base where p is locally nilpotent. The text passes to Sₒ and uses two arrows between M and M⁽ᵖ⁾ to define the F-V-crystal. It writes the two composites and compares the construction with the usual functor over a perfect field.

**Scans 39–40, Problems 1 and 2.** The ability to recover fibres over perfect fields leads to a question about recovering morphisms and characterising the essential image over a more general characteristic-p base. The second problem asks for a theory of finite locally free p-groups and, over a perfect base, an anti-equivalence for groups flat over Λₙ.

**Scans 41–42, filtration and deformation.** Section b) gives a filtration whose first part is the invariant differentials of the associated formal group. Its quotient is written as a tangent module of the dual BT group. The remark on 42 interprets this as a Hodge filtration. The deformation theorem identifies lifts to a divided-power thickening with lifts of the indicated locally direct-summand submodule. A pencilled proviso qualifies its closing sentence.

**Scans 42–44, admissibility.** The conditional remark about Problem 1 continues onto 43. Section c) then asks whether the filtration is recoverable from the crystal. A pair of maps of topoi, an F,V,F sequence, and a kernel/image identity give the proposed characterisation. The equation at the head of 44 identifies the pullback of Fil¹ with Ker F, also written Im V. The text defines admissibility by the existence of the indicated summand and says that crystals from BT groups satisfy it. Three margin entries extend the programme to limits, isogenies and duality.

**Scans 44–45, abelian schemes.** Section 2 defines a contravariant functor on abelian schemes using first crystalline cohomology on the nilpotent site. It describes its value by relative de Rham cohomology, then gives the Hodge exact sequence and its tangent-space interpretation (**). The deformation theorem is stated as an equivalence between lifts of the abelian scheme and pairs that include a lift of Fil¹.

**Scans 45–46, universal vector extension.** Part b) uses the universal vector extension E(A) to interpret the same functor and to construct a quasi-inverse. It describes the evaluation of the group crystal on a thickening using a lift A′, and calls for a transitive system of isomorphisms between evaluations made with different lifts. The characteristic-zero discussion refers to Tate. For the general case the writer mentions the exponential method and a further crystalline direct-image construction. The last sentence continues beyond the batch.

## Remarks of the model

- **M32, model remark, high confidence.** The final scalar estimate on 32 is strict because ε − Mε/(M+1) equals ε/(M+1), positive for the stated ε and nonnegative norm bound M. This checks the last inequality alone.
- **M36, model remark, high confidence in the computation.** For Q(h) = −2h‴/h′ + 3(h″/h′)², symbolic substitution gives Q(exp f) − Q(f) = (f′)² wherever the quotients are defined. The trial f(t) = t gives 1. The reading of the cancelled coefficient on the sheet remains low confidence.
- **M43, model remark, high confidence.** On a characteristic-p evaluation, multiplication by p is zero, so either written product equal to p gives a zero composite. The author announces exactness as a further assertion with “on prouvera mieux”.
- **Model reading question, low confidence, scan 36, circled gloss.** The final text places “paraboliques” first, with “projectives” and “hyperboliques” as alternatives. The tall stem and long descender carry more information than the beginning of the word. I have not used this gloss to interpret the calculation.
