# Mathematical notes on cote 51, scans 002–012

## What the pages do

**Proposition 1 and Corollary 1, scans 003–004.** Over a discrete valuation ring V with field of fractions K, the author starts with a quasi-coherent sheaf F on X and a quotient G′ of its restriction F′ to X′. He constructs G = F/J* by taking the largest submodule with the indicated restriction property. He describes G as the smallest quotient inducing G′, asserts that it is quasi-coherent and V-flat, and obtains a bijection by restriction to X′.

**Proof of Proposition 1, scan 004.** The proof takes the kernel of multiplication by a uniformizer π on G. Quotienting by that kernel still gives G′ on X′, so the minimality of G forces the kernel to vanish. In the converse direction the author compares a flat quotient with the smallest such lift and considers the kernel of their map. The final displayed line gives its zero generic fibre.

**Tensor products and schematic closure (C3), scan 005.** Corollary 2 asserts compatibility with tensor products. The accompanying argument uses flatness of the tensor product over V and compatibility with tensoring by K. Corollary 3 gives the geometric version. A V-flat closed subprescheme Y restricts to Y′, and the inverse construction is the schematic closure of Y′. The author also asserts compatibility with fibre products. The base letter after *plats sur* is uncertain in this line.

**Closed subgroups (C4), scan 006.** Corollary 4 applies closure to a closed subgroup H′ of the generic fibre. The author treats stability under inversion briefly. For multiplication he compares H × H with the inverse image of H in G × G, using the preceding product assertion to identify the closure of H′ × H′. The letter immediately after *Alors* carries a prime.

**The subgroup locus, scan 007.** Proposition 2 starts with the Hilbert functor for G over S and takes the subfunctor of closed subschemes that are proper and flat over S and are subgroup schemes. The author asserts representability by M and a closed immersion into the Hilbert scheme. Under the additional properness and finite-type hypotheses he describes proper pieces Mᵢ. A smaller note below identifies open loci for finite, separable and abelian subgroup schemes and says that the first are also closed.

**Two conditions on a family, scan 008.** The argument tests a proper flat closed family H after base change. It compares H with its inverse and compares H × H with the inverse image of H under multiplication. The two conditions produce closed loci T₁ and T₂. The sign joining them in the last equation is pointed. For properness the proof returns to Corollary 4 of Proposition 1.

**Extension of an isogeny, scans 009–010.** Proposition 3 starts with an isogeny from the generic fibre of an abelian scheme A. The author closes up its finite kernel, uses the projectivity of A to form a quotient, and compares that quotient on the generic and special fibres. The kernel is first written H′, then H for its closure. The later quotient denominator is angular and is registered as Γ or H. The N.B. discusses extension of morphisms and names Weil.

**Good reduction up to isogeny, scans 010–011.** Corollary 1 transfers good reduction between isogenous abelian schemes over K and says that their reductions are isogenous. The *Conséquence* therefore speaks of reduction for an isogeny class. The paragraph then passes to sums and simple components. Its sentence continues across the page break.

**The Shimura–Koizumi theorem, scans 011–012.** If A′ has good reduction, the theorem says that an abelian quotient also has good reduction. Treating the quotient as a direct factor, the author takes complementary endomorphisms on A′, writes their corresponding endomorphisms on A, and describes A as a product of their kernels. He uses properness of the factors and their fibres to conclude. The two rows of endomorphism identities differ at their last right-hand letter. The word *conclusion.* is at the top of 012.

**Remark on the image (C12), scan 012.** Starting with an abelian subscheme B′ of A′, the author takes an abelian model B and a morphism B → A. He identifies its schematic image H with the closure of B′ by comparing inverse images and generic fibres. The text subsequently tests when the map to A is a monomorphism by considering its kernel. The next sentence ends on this sheet at “il n’en est”.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark R1, high confidence, scans 005–006.** Corollary 3 supplies the uniqueness needed in the words “et c’est le seul”. Once H has been shown to be a subgroup scheme, any other flat closed subgroup with the same generic fibre has the same underlying closed subscheme by that corollary. This separates the uniqueness argument from the multiplication check.
- **Model remark R2, high confidence, scan 011.** The disagreement between the two displayed rows matters for the product argument. I tested the reading using the two diagonal coordinate projections on a rank-two free module. Their sum is the identity, and the square of the second is the second projection. This tests S11a as a proposed general projector identity.
- **Model remark R3, medium confidence, scan 012.** The move from the generic inclusion B′ → A′ to a morphism of models B → A leaves a separate question about its kernel. The distinction explains why the author returns to monomorphisms after identifying the image H. The unfinished sentence supplies no conclusion about that kernel.
