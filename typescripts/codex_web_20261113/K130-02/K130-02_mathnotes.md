# Mathematical notes on cote 130, scans 20–36

## What the pages do

**Scan 20 | From simple roots to a graph.** The typescript lists four possibilities for a pair of distinct simple roots. It records the two Cartan integers, an angle, the product of the associated reflections, and, in the last three cases, a relation between lengths. It defines a Dynkin diagram whose nodes carry squared root lengths and begins the proof that an isomorphism of such diagrams induces an isomorphism of the root systems. The proof stops mid-sentence on this sheet.

**Scan 22 | Global and local intersection formulas.** A sum over valuations relates the pairing of generic-fibre cycles to intersection numbers. Below a dividing line the writer considers a local base, imposes support and vertical-component conditions, and boxes the equality of a local pairing with the intersection number. D precedes C in the upper pairing, C precedes D in the lower box.

**Scan 23 | Shortening reflection words.** A conjugation calculation ends at the top. Lemma 5 shortens a product by one generator when a wall separates the initial and final chambers. Its proof follows the successive chambers to a pair on opposite sides of the wall. Lemma 6 uses this result and a minimal expression for an element carrying a chamber into contact with itself. Its proof continues beyond the sheet.

**Scan 24 | Local ring and model diagrams.** A local ring of dimension two, its depth, an element f and a regular quotient appear above a rule. Below are a generic-fibre product, closures of the two factors, arrows labelled α, β and N, several vertical chains, and a hatched drawing. The sheet gives no prose joining all these diagrams into one construction.

**Scans 25 and 27 | Chambers, walls and defining inequalities.** Scan 25 defines a chamber using a locally finite family of affine hyperplanes. It explains openness and convexity, defines a wall by local agreement with a half-space, and proves a half-space lemma. Lemma 2 continues on scan 27. Its three parts identify walls among the given hyperplanes, test whether omission of one wall changes the intersection, and express the chamber using its walls. The last argument begins with a segment meeting only finitely many hyperplanes.

**Scan 26 | A principal divisor.** The pairing with the divisor of a function is set beside an intersection and a valuation. The diagrams distinguish the original and primed spaces and mark the special fibre. The lower prose chooses an invertible sheaf and a rational section in connection with a rational point of the generic fibre. It considers its behaviour at the critical point and along vertical components.

**Scans 28 and 30 | Reduction through the Albanese map.** In the smooth case the manuscript introduces an Albanese, a dual object and a map α over S. After a cancelled attempt with generic-fibre divisors and their closures, it sets C′ equal to the pushforward of C and compares intersection numbers. Scan 30 treats a divisor differing from a pullback by a principal divisor. Two noncontainment conditions and a bracketed lemma reminder intervene. The last box isolates the principal-divisor formula, with a side condition written beside it.

**Scan 32 | The general case.** The writer takes a Néron model of the Albanese of the generic fibre. The triangle shows a map from an open part X′ to the model over S. A final line describes the removed point. The sheet breaks off there.

**Scans 29, 31 and 33 | Editorial context for the typescript.** Scan 29 addresses the use of Cartier’s earlier rédaction and hopes for printing. Scan 31 explains choices made in the seven sections, including the field hypothesis for the invariant algebra. Scan 33 gives contents and page references for invariants, Coxeter transformations and classification, down to the individual root-system types.

**Scan 35 | Functions of a point and an absolute value.** The notes start with absolute values on K and their prolongations to a closure. They identify the orbits of prolongations under Γ and consider a real-valued function invariant under the simultaneous action on a point and a valuation. For a fixed point the value depends only on the restricted valuation. The page also records the resulting functions over the perfect closure and over K.

**Scan 36 | Compatibility over finite extensions.** The manuscript attaches a function to each finite intermediate field L. It compares values after restriction of a valuation, describes a compatible family and gives the family a hatted name. It then claims independence of the choice of algebraic closure and ends with an underlined assertion of bijectivity for a natural base-change map.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 29.** The rédacteur « espère bien que c'est le dernier état » and that it « sera envoyé in toto, illico ». This is a hope about publication.
- **Scan 30.** The bracketed « Lemme » and the doubtful words following it leave a task at the step where a divisor representative has to meet both noncontainment conditions.
- **Scan 31, §§3–4.** « Bourbaki sera sûrement assez astucieux pour le court-circuiter » hopes for a shorter affine calculation. « On a le sentiment d'avoir notablement simplifié » assesses the treatment of closed sets of roots.
- **Scan 31, §5.** « par quel mystère insondable il n'est pas en Algèbre VIII » asks about the location of Maschke’s theorem. The writer describes the proposed extension of the invariant-algebra result to « un anneau convenable » as something the Congress notes « semblent dire ». He reports an unsuccessful attempt.

## Remarks of the model

- **R20.1, scan 20 — medium confidence about scope.** For two orthogonal reflections the product acts by minus the identity on the span of their normal vectors and fixes its orthogonal complement. This suggests reading the printed « −1 » on the two-dimensional span. The sheet does not state that restriction in the case list.
- **R20.2, scan 20 — low confidence, reading question.** The frozen archive-number comment reads 10. In a rotated enlargement the second character also permits 9: there is a small loop at the top and a longer arc returning down to the left. That would give 19, consistent with the pencil numbering on the other sheets. The typescript’s own page number is unambiguous.
- **R28.1, scan 28 — low confidence, reading question.** On the last line the first reading of the final connective is a minus sign, with equals as an alternative. The preceding chain favours equality. The second horizontal in this particular sign is little more than a point.
- **R32.1, scan 32 — low confidence, reading question.** The line defines X′ by removing a point and then appears to call that point « le pt singulier de X′ ». If both point names denote the removed point, an unprimed X in the final phrase would fit. The first point name is overwritten.
- **R36.1, scan 36 — low confidence about the scope of the final assertion.** For a one-point scheme, the restriction suggested by scan 35 is pullback of functions on places. If one place has two prolongations to the new ground field, target functions can distinguish them. This leaves open what additional restriction on the functions or on base change the last bijectivity assertion uses. The source field in that display is overwritten.
