# Mathematical notes on cote 85, scans 30–43

References in parentheses give the scan and a block in its page file. The block and line convention is described in the audit. Identifiers in square brackets also identify statements in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**Traces and orders (30, blocks 1–10, to 31, block 3).** The opening calculation applies Hamilton–Cayley to an automorphism of determinant one and pursues the case of residual characteristic two. The proposition introduces two monic polynomials by root values of the form $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$. One uses nonexceptional $n$th roots and the other primitive roots. The proof starts by putting the section in a maximal torus, where its parameter is $\lambda+\lambda^{-1}$. [U30]

**Small orders (31, blocks 5–9).** The three corollaries give scalar conditions for orders 3 and 6, then 4, then 5. Their values are $-1$ and $+1$, then $0$, then the equation $\alpha^2+\alpha-1=0$. Each corollary has a restriction on the residue characteristics. [U31]

**Finite order over fields and bases (31, blocks 10–12, and 32).** The NB lists possible orders of elements of a form of $\mathrm{Sl}(2)$ over a field. The next sheet separates characteristic zero from positive characteristic, then asks for exact order on every fibre of a scheme. In the theorem the generic case, $n=2p$, and $n=p$ receive different hypotheses on residue characteristics. The last two cases have a large caution in the margin. [U32]

**Finite subgroups up to conjugacy (33–34).** A cover introduces the next group of sheets. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, scan 34 lists cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral cases through their abstract group types. The margin relates the list to oriented regular spherical maps. A cancelled continuation computes centralizers and normalizers and sketches a functor between groupoids of maps and pairs. [U34]

**Cyclic subgroups and the Klein four group (35).** A cyclic subgroup of order $n$ is paired with a maximal torus and its $n$-torsion. The next case compares a Klein four subgroup with its normalizer. The corollaries recast these pairs using data over a base. The last one lists a torsor, a degree-four étale covering and a tetrahedron. The rank-two object in the first corollary is difficult to read. [U35]

**Dihedral and polyhedral normalizers (36).** The first display distinguishes even and odd $n$ for $\mathbb D_n$. The ensuing corollary discusses principal covers, polygons and a choice of a primitive root of unity. The lower half puts the cases of $\mathcal A_4$ and $\mathfrak S_4$ together. It associates forms of these groups and of $\mathbb D_2$ with the same covering and polyhedral data. [U36]

**Reference examples over the rationals (37).** The cyclic and dihedral cases use actions on the multiplicative group and its compactification. A side note tests when the group can be untwisted. A second note relates this to the half-cyclotomic equation. The proposed method for the next two groups takes the normalizer of an embedded $\mathbb D_2$. The author questions the resulting form. [U37]

**The icosahedral case (38).** After fixing a reference pair, the sheet describes other pairs using an inner form or a torsor. A split reference group leads to a degree-five cover with a trivialization of its orientation cover. Choosing a root of $\alpha^2+\alpha-1$ leads to a reference field $k_0$. Conditions on the field and the distinction between a split subgroup and a split ambient group enter separately. [U38]

**A group recovered from a torsor (39–40).** The subgroup $\Gamma^+$ is abelian of index two. The hypotheses prescribe inversion by the quotient, splitting, and generation by elements outside the 2-torsion. The complementary coset $\Gamma^-$ is a $\Gamma^+$-torsor. The notes list four pieces of a product on the union, then propose the converse construction from a group and a torsor. The proposition passes to affine lines and gives a formula for $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb D_n)$. [U39]

**Embeddings of $\mathcal A_5$ (41).** The invariant $\alpha(\varphi)$ evaluates $A$ at the image of a specified circular permutation. The equivalence with a discrete category of roots of a quadratic is rewritten as rigidity and representability by $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[T]/(T^2+T-1)$. The corollary says which root is obtained by precomposing with inner and fibrewise outer automorphisms. [U41]

**Numerical types of systems of Schwartz (42–43).** The second cover introduces regular hyperpolygons. A table records ordered triples for $\mathcal A_4$, $\mathfrak S_4$ and $\mathcal A_5$, distinguishing primed entries. The concluding question asks about simultaneous conjugacy when the three component classes are given. [U43]

## Remarks of the model

- **S36-R (36), model, high for the finite computation.** Write $\eta$ for a primitive eighth root. The maps $z\mapsto\eta^{2j} z^{\pm1}$ form the eight-element dihedral subgroup. Conjugation by $z\mapsto\eta z$ permutes these eight maps, and this rotation is outside the subgroup. This checks the even branch at one value of $n$.
- **S39a-R and S39b-R (39), model, high.** The cyclic generation test and the product of two distinct reflections can be checked independently. The second test uses only the three rotations and three reflections of $\mathbb D_3$.
- **R41 (38, 41), model, high.** The two handwritten expressions for $\alpha'$ agree on the quadratic locus. The remainder of $X(-1-X)+1$ modulo $X^2+X-1$ is zero.
- **R43 (43), model, high.** The printed multiplicities add to $3+1=4$, $6+3=9$, and $6+6+3+3+6+6+1+1=32$. These three numbers add to the written 45. This arithmetic checks the counts at the left edge of the table.

The pencil is faint at the junction of its upper and lower strokes. “(15 p)” remains an alternative for the cover count. The count had no doubt mark at the freeze and retains its frozen spelling.
