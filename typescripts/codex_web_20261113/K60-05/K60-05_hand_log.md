# Hand and typewriter log

Line numbers count body lines, starting immediately below the header. Pencilled lower-corner numbers belong to a separate foliation. An isolated addition in ink on these typescripts cannot be attributed to a named writer from its shape alone.

### Scan 48

The broad ∩ over the bound in line 10 is individually drawn. The keyed ∈, ≥, λ, μ and reference numerals have repeatable outlines. Compare the three identical descending right arms of λ in lines 16 and 18. The short underlying rule on p follows the letter into subscripts. A dot of multiplication can have a hollow centre on this machine (second case). The archive 47 is lighter and much larger than the type.

### Scan 49

The two horizontal strokes of the superscript join a rising left and descending right stroke. It occurs in the heading, prose and displays. I carry the same sign through the page. His D has a diagonal entry across the bowl; it does not have the parallel stems visible on R. The check on E stands quite apart from its upper bar. In the prose a word's interior may be only a thin broken line (especially commute, passage). The archival 48 has a different weight.

### Scan 50

The typewriter's underlined p and manually drawn cap recur. The small intersection before Ass also has two uneven uprights. The repeated n° and lemma numbers have the typeface's serifs. A curved tail descends from the rule below il in the lemma. It has no textual attachment.

### Scan 51

The x in a subscript can be little more than a notch. Both versions of E at the start of the pair with the left brace are distinct, as are their detached dual marks at the right. The rectangular rules surround full equations. The last box runs to the edge; the last summand visibly ends with a single closing parenthesis.

### Scan 52

The four chi-shaped signs have a descending diagonal and a crossing stroke with a curled foot. Compare the alpha signs in (i), whose closed bowls and typewriter pitch differ. The upper-left mark has one sloping stroke with no connecting words or leader. None of the capitals in the heading has a second stem.

### Scan 53

The weight is a rounded single loop with a descender (rho). The base p has an entry stroke before its stem; compare the two in the same last brace. The long descending chi can resemble a y. The lower delta has a closed bowl; several initials are corrections in place. The question over the equals is circled and the two question marks to its right have a left-pointing arrow.

### Scan 54

The theta has a loop returning above the oval and a short right bar; the small phi of varphi(M) is quite different. Both sizes of underlined p occur side by side, most clearly under the sum. ### Scan 55

The arrows meet two separate labels. The ones from chi (local and global) meet the geometric label; the ones from delta meet the arithmetic label. In the numerator and exponent the x can be almost a dot. The word before Ogg is especially reduced. The first argument E has a darker upright lying over earlier strokes. ### Scan 56

The large caps stand above a whole typed index. The printed capital P and p below them differ in both height and descender. There are two equals signs at the continuation of the same chain. The subscript on 1 in the lemma is B, and in the last formula is B with its own p.

### Scan 57

The first sum in the exponent has no subscript visible, unlike the sum on the inserted definition of chi. The two leaders of that definition go to the exponents in the neighbouring rows. The local and global epsilons have a three-like open form, distinct from the loop and descender of delta.

### Scan 59

### Comparison across the twelve leaves

On 49, 51, 53, 55, 57 and 59 the two outer turns of the raised angular sign recur, although the gap between the horizontal lines varies. The frozen first reading uses opposed harpoons, and keeps ordinary opposed arrows as the alternative. The weight in both cancelled twists on 49 and the two final lines on 51 has the downward looping stroke also visible in the separate weight hypothesis on 53. This distinguishes it from the base p. The R before the extraordinary extension on 49 has one upright, unlike the two stems in the nearby R's. On 56 and 58 the lowered brackets around A agree. The raised small o after n and the separate line under a prime ideal hold throughout the typescript. The classifier “unknown” for the drawn caps and chi's on those leaves records the limit of what those few glyphs can establish about their maker.

### Findings from the witness rereading

On each of the six manuscript leaves the angular superscript also admits a compressed musical natural. A thin pair of crossbars joins strokes that extend up on the left and down on the right. The final candidate set at the first such sign on each leaf is opposed harpoons, opposed arrows, and natural. On scan 55 the writer's p descends in both *coupé* and *découpage*. On scan 59 the centre of *modulo* rises in a d-loop and again in l. The word has no descending y. These differences can be checked without relying on the expected mathematical phrase.
