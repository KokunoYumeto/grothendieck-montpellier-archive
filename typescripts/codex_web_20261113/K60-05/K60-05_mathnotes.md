# Mathematical notes on cote 60, scans 48–59

## What the pages do

**Scans 49 and 51.** The handwritten calculation starts with the dual of a sum involving an extension from \(U\) and contributions at \(x\in Y\). The terms at \(x\) are collected into \(\mu_x(E)\). Scan 51 defines \(\alpha_x(E_\eta)\) as a difference of two local objects, then writes \(\mu_x(E)=\alpha_x(E_\eta)^\vee-\alpha_x(\check E_\eta)(1)\). The boxed result expresses the global dual in terms of the dual system and a sum over \(X^{(0)}\). A second box imposes \(\check E_\eta=E_\eta(\rho)\). The recurring raised angular sign has three readings in the edition.

**Scan 53.** The functional equation introduces \(\lambda_x=L_{\mu_x}\) and two global factors, \(\chi\) and \(\delta\), carrying the angular superscript. Substitution in \(t\), followed by a weight hypothesis, leads to \(q=p^{1+\rho}\) and the reciprocal argument \(1/(qt)\). The author puts question marks around a proposed relation between the global factors. The lower brace returns to the local terms and writes \(\chi_x=\chi(\alpha_x)\) and \(\delta_x=\delta(\alpha_x)\), with their arguments on the page.

**Scan 55.** The author writes \(L_{E_\eta}(1/(qt))=A(t)L_{E_\eta}(t)\). He circles the quotient of two local \(L\)-factors and connects it to \(A_x(t^{-1})^{-1}\). His N.B. records a cost of splitting \(\lambda_x\) into pieces, the loss of its additive character. The resulting list asks for constructions of the local objects and formulas for the two global invariants.

**Scans 57 and 59.** The author applies \(\delta\) to the virtual duality formula, computes \(\delta(\mu_x(E))\), and compares a square of the global factor with a product of local squares. Taking square roots introduces a sign. Two leaders attach \(\chi=\sum_x\chi_x(E_\eta)\) to powers of \(p\). The final line has \(\pm\) and \(p^{-\chi/2}\). Scan 59 reduces problem c) to b) up to a sign and refers to Artin representations.

**Scan 58, opening paragraph.** The typescript finishes a calculation of \(c_A\) after restriction of scalars. A free part contributes \(r_B(R)c_A(B)\). The torsion quotient contributes through the homomorphism \(N\). The paragraph points back to (19) and concludes (20).

**Scans 58 and 56.** Proposition 19 compares reflexivity over \(B\) with reflexivity of \(R_{[A]}\) over \(A\). Lemma 4 identifies the localization of \(R_{[A]}\) at \(\underline p\) with the intersection of the \(R_{\underline P}\) over \(\underline P\mid\underline p\). The proof passes through a semilocal principal ring. The first paragraph of scan 56 applies the intersection formula.

**Scans 56, 54 and 52.** Proposition 20 treats extension of scalars. It compares pseudo-nullity, gives (22) for the torsion characteristic, and gives (23) and (24) for \(c\) and rank. Lemma 5 uses localization in height at most one to control the kernel of an injection after tensoring. Scan 54 applies additivity, computes a coefficient by a ramification length, and uses a free submodule \(H\subset M\) with torsion quotient. Scan 52 finishes (23), discusses reflexivity under flat extension, and gives the two composition formulas (25) and (26).

**Scan 52, section 9.** Lemma 6 seeks a single \(a\in R\) whose images avoid prescribed subsets \(H_i\subset R/\underline p_i\). Its hypothesis concerns translates containing a nonzero ideal. Part (ii) gives a cardinality condition. The page begins the induction in (i) and breaks off while ordering the prime ideals.

**Scans 50 and 48.** The typescript relates independence in the residue spaces to the absence of torsion in \(M/L\). Lemma 8 seeks a nonzero \(x\) with \(M/Ax\) torsion-free. The construction takes an element \(y\), localizes away from a finite set of primes, chooses a second element \(z\), and sets \(x=z-by\). Three cases check its image in the residue spaces. Scan 48 then starts the induction in theorem 6 and ends mid-word.

## Remarks of the model

**R1. Model remark, high confidence.** The order of the typescript argument can be recovered within the supplied leaves. The sentence ending “est” on scan 56 resumes “lui aussi injectif” on 54. The sequence ending in \(Q\otimes_A B\) on 54 leads into “où \(I\) est pseudo-nul” on 52. Scans 50 and 48 join across “un anneau / principal semi-local”. The printed running numbers give the same order.

**R2. Model remark, medium confidence.** The self-duality assumption merits a separate mathematical question. Scan 51 introduces it with “Si”. On 53 the weight hypothesis leads directly to the duality relation. These leaves do not specify which additional structure on \(E\), if any, is being assumed.

**R3. Model remark, low confidence about authorial intent.** A literal algebraic check distinguishes the two normalizations in S57a and the two sign conventions in S57b. With one formal local factor, take \(p=3,\rho=1,\chi_x=2,\chi_{\rm global}=4,\delta_x=9,\delta_{\rm global}=-81\). The opening and local square relations hold. The two normalized squares are both 1. The preceding written right-hand side gives 531441. The global square root is \(-1\), while the unsigned split product is \(1\). These are scalar test values for the displayed identities. The pages supply no geometric realization for them.

**Reading question, scan 53, first line of the first brace (TeX line 11).** The frozen reading is \(L_{\check E_\eta}(1/(pt))\). A possible alternative is \(L_{E_\eta}(1/(pt))\) with a cancelled check. In 053_lhs.png the space above the upper bar contains a dark rounded mark, unlike the open check in several other occurrences. It is difficult to distinguish a retraced check from an obliteration. Low confidence.
