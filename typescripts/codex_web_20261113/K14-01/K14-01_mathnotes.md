# Mathematical notes on cote 14, scans 002–015

## What the pages do

**U1 · 002–003.** The cover names a “Structure d'effectivité”. Item 4° on the next sheet describes a subcategory of effective motives, its closure under sums, tensor products and subobjects, and the possibility of twisting an object into it. The author recasts the data in terms of a cone in a character ring. The margins propose conditions on irreducible classes, weights and the action of the identity component in weight zero.

**U2 · 003, 005, 007.** The sentence across 003 and 005 calls a link effective by testing its gradings on effective objects. For a given i, the notes form P(i) from objects with positive grading and distinguish the hypotheses ε(i) ≥ 0 and ε(i) > 0. They then consider intersections and conjugacy classes of links satisfying M⁺ ⊂ P(i).

**U3 · 007, 009.** A maximal torus T gives a lattice Γ and groups W and W′. The notes pass from a W-invariant cone to a “conique” part of Γ/W and describe the action of the fundamental group. On 009 a family of i gives intersections of P(i). A cone, the class of i, and a saturation condition enter the description. The single-i₁ case ends with a map from I(G) to Eff(G).

**U4 · 011.** A second map takes values in Pol(G) × Eff(G). Its source has an uncertain superscript. A gloss describes polarizing i modulo a real group action. The sheet asks about injectivity and image, then proposes tests using a torsor, a family with a rational point, and a commutative group.

**U5 · 013.** In a cancelled opening the author writes two fibre functors at a point, labelled Hodge and De Rham. For a torus over ℚ he introduces a lattice with π-action, ε and i in the respective invariant lattices, and the pairing ⟨i,ε⟩=2. He describes representations through nonnegative invariant elements of the integral group ring. He then considers a compact part U and a split part S of the real torus, asserts that S has dimension one, and begins a subsequently cancelled two-factor calculation.

**U6 · 015.** A lattice M with finite quotient action gives the ring of rational virtual characters \(K(T)=\mathbb{Z}(M)^\pi\). The notes describe a quotient of the affine group associated to M. They ask how to define motivic effectivity and propose testing a character along the displayed map from the multiplicative group to S. They end with the problem of finiteness in a fixed weight.

**U7 · 014, 012.** In typescript reading order, Theorem III.2 gives a stalkwise criterion for local separability together with finite presentation. Lemma III.3 treats the endomorphism algebra W of a finitely generated projective module over an ordinary commutative ring. Its proof runs from the evaluation map on 014 to the projectivity conclusion at the top of 012. Theorem III.4 on 012 applies the endomorphism construction to sheaves, and III.5 applies the stalk criterion to a tensor product.

**U8 · 010, 008.** Theorem III.6 handles extension of the base to a commutative algebra. The proof on 010 writes a finite local presentation, tensors the sequence, and invokes the result on stalks. The discussion of ring commutants begins at the bottom and continues at the top of 008. Theorem III.7 gives the commutants of the two factors in a sheaf tensor product. The proof starts by defining a map on presheaves and the induced map on sheaves.

**U9 · 006, 004.** Chapter IV defines an equivalence relation on locally separable algebras by tensoring with endomorphism algebras. Its classes form the Brauer group described on the sheet; the inverse representative carries a small oval superscript. The algebra argument favours the reading o for the opposite algebra. Theorem IV.1 begins on 006 and continues at the top of 004. The surviving proof constructs a map of presheaves, evaluates a family of local homomorphisms, and discusses finite presentation of the resulting modules.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 003, 015 · Medium confidence.** “Types de motifs effectifs” (003) and “caractères de motifs effectifs” (015) leave a consequential question of scope. Repeated direct sums at a fixed weight multiply a nonzero character by positive integers. Those multiples are distinct in the integral group ring on 015. If “types” means irreducible constituents, those sums introduce no new constituents. The last lines do not specify that restriction.

**R2 · Scans 007, 009, 011 · High confidence.** The construction of a cone from data i and the question about the image of I(G) run in opposite directions. 009 exhibits a map. On 011 the author separately asks whether structures arising from motives are in its image. The latter question remains open in these sheets.

**R3 · Scans 013, 015 · Low confidence.** Both sheets turn from ℚ to real points in a finiteness discussion. A precise implication between the two arguments would depend on the crossed last calculation of 013 and the compressed last sentence of 015. Neither passage establishes that implication here.

**R4 · Scans 004, 006, 008, 010, 012, 014 · High confidence.** The argument about W continues from the foot of III.4 to the top of III.5, and the commutant definition continues from III.6 to III.7. These joins give the English sheets a reading sequence opposite to their archive order. The page files retain the archive order.

**R5 · Reading question raised after freezing · Scan 004, second displayed formula · Low confidence.** The text ends with \(g_{\mathrm U}(\mathrm m_1)\). A very short vertical stroke lies high and well to the right of the closing parenthesis. An alternative would be a detached prime on the expression, \(g_{\mathrm U}(\mathrm m_1)'\). Its distance from the parenthesis is appreciably greater than the gap at the prime on α in the same line; a stray mark seems more likely. The transcription retains the hashed reading.
