# K14-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Hom sheaves (16).** The English typescript passes from a tensor-of-Hom map to its stalk at $x$. It uses finite presentation to identify the stalks and projectivity there to assert an isomorphism. It then makes the dual of a left module into a right module, describes its action on a family of local maps, and begins constructing $\psi$ from the tensor of the dual with the module to its endomorphism sheaf. The last line still belongs to the presheaf construction.

**An intersection number of both signs (19, 21).** The manuscript starts with an elliptic curve $A$, a curve $P$, and the difference $\Theta_0=\Delta-A\times(0)-(0)\times A$. It extends this divisor to $A\times Y$, takes the zero section into a projective closure, and pushes the divisor to a codimension-two cycle $z$. The calculation uses the normal class of the section to express the square through $\Theta^2c'$. The coefficients in the displayed square of $\Theta_0$ sum to $-2$ at $(0,0)$. The last lines give $-2$ and $2$ on replacing $c$ by $-c$. The first trace sign and the precise superscripts attached to $P$ need special care. [C21.1, C21.2]

**Affine reflections and chambers (22, 20).** Typescript page 14 defines affine pseudo-reflections using an affine hyperplane fixed pointwise. It relates them to reflections in two associated vector spaces, then states that the product of the orthogonal reflections in two parallel non-isotropic hyperplanes is a translation. Page 15 supplies the concluding formula $s'(s(x))=x+2t$ and identifies $\lVert t\rVert$ with their distance in the positive definite real case. Section 5 turns to real affine space. Its lemma identifies a connected component of the complement of a locally finite union of hyperplanes as the intersection of the open half-spaces containing it. The proof on this sheet stops after “il est clair”. [R22.1]

**Definition and ring (24–25).** Definition 1.1 calls a cycle Albanese-equivalent to zero if it is the defined value of a family on a degree-zero cycle of a smooth connected parameter space, subject to the displayed vanishing of $S$. Projectivity of the parameter is bracketed. Proposition 1.2 asserts that these cycles give a graded subgroup whose image is an ideal in the Chow ring, yielding the quotient $A'{}^*(X)$. It states pullback, proper pushforward and the implication from Albanese to algebraic equivalence.

**Product parameters and pullback (25–28).** To combine two families the manuscript passes to $T\times T'$, using $\mathfrak a\times a'$ and $a\times\mathfrak a'$. The chosen points must allow the evaluations to be defined. The paired specializations return the given cycles and the mixed ones vanish; the lower display gives $z+z'$. For pullback the manuscript changes representatives within their linear equivalence classes and explicitly leaves their existence to be checked. It also suggests reducing to a statement about Cartesian products. [R26.1]

**Functorial consequences (28).** Scholie 1.3 claims for the quotient rings the general functorial properties of the Chow rings, including the projection formula. For the point, projective spaces and flag varieties it relates Albanese equivalence to the equality of linear and algebraic equivalence stated there.

**Zero-cycles and divisors (29–30).** Theorem 1.4 gives a degree and Albanese-sum test on zero-cycles on a smooth connected projective $X$. It identifies Albanese and linear equivalence to zero for divisors. In (i) the diagonal and $T=X$ give one direction. In (ii) the divisor family gives a homomorphism $A_T\to\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}_X^0$; the formula is applied to a parameter cycle of zero sum. The small sign before $Z(\mathfrak a)$ is doubtful. Remark 1.5 asks about equality with linear equivalence more generally.

**Vanishing product (30).** Theorem 1.6 states $zz'=0$ in the quotient for two algebraically trivial classes. The qualification on $X$ has been revised. The proof uses the external product of differences of points on two parameter spaces. The four terms have coefficients $+1,-1,-1,+1$. A marginal note asks whether the resulting cycle might be linearly trivial, even for curves. [C30.1]

## Remarks of the model

- **R22.1 · High.** Typescript pages 14 and 15 join across scans 22 and 20. The former ends with $s'(x)-t=s(x)+t$; adding $t$ and using the stated involution gives the two equalities opening the latter.
- **C21.1 and C21.2 · High for the arithmetic.** The three nonzero point coefficients give $-2-2+2=-2$. Substituting $1$ and $-1$ for the scalar in $-2c(0,0)$ gives the final pair $-2,2$. This checks the displayed integers only.
- **R26.1 · Medium, conditional on the specializations as stated.** Distributing the sum of the two families across the sum of the two parameter cycles produces two original terms and two mixed terms. The page’s vanishing of the latter accounts for the plus sign at the foot.
- **C30.1 · High for the four coefficients.** Direct expansion of the two differences gives the four ordered pairs and their signs in the order written. The coefficients cancel separately for each first coordinate and each second coordinate. This calculation leaves the author’s question on linear equivalence open.
