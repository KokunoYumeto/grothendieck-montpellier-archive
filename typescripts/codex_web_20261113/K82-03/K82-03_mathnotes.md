# Mathematical notes · cote 82, scans 032–049

## What the pages do

**032 · Faithfulness.** In a basis with $q=xy+z^2$ and $\omega=e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3$, the action of $xX+hH+yY$ is written on three basis vectors with tildes. The conclusion is $x=y=h=0$ when the action vanishes. The author then returns to an isomorphism out of $E$ and to the construction of $\widetilde G$ over $G$.

**034 · Roots and a recurrence.** Four examples $F_3$ through $F_6$ precede the definition by the minimal polynomial of $1+\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$. The successive differences of labelled points on a circle give $e_1,e_2,e_3$. The last line expresses $u(e_2)$ in terms of $e_1,e_2$. The four polynomial examples agree with the displayed definition (F34.3–F34.6).

**035 · Subgroups and a torsor.** A chain of projective identifications ends in $\operatorname{Div}_2^+(X)$. Below it, $U=X-Y$ occurs as a torsor under the group written $J$ in the transcription. The lower work relates the inverse image of the inversion section in $N(J)/J$ to $X-Y$. Two arrows finish the sheet; the lower one is labelled $2\,\mathrm{id}_J$. Much of the intervening argument is crossed.

**037–038 · A rank-two centralizer.** For an endomorphism $u$ which is nonscalar on every fibre, the proposed commutant is the span of the identity and $u$. Its invertible elements are those for which the determinant in (2) is invertible (D37). Projectivizing gives a line with a quadratic divisor removed. The discriminant is $\tau^2-4\delta$. Scan 038 passes to the line modulo scalars in the Lie algebra of the projective group and asks what centralizes that line.

**039–041 · Conjugation and characteristic two.** Scan 039 compares conjugation with fixing $u$ or sending it to $u^{-1}$, then studies the rank of the commutator map on matrices. Scan 040 uses a universal section $\alpha+\beta u_0$ to test centralization. Scan 041 turns to $gug^{-1}=u+\lambda$. The trace gives $2\lambda=0$. The eigenvalue-pair example and the final Lie brackets locate the difficulty in characteristic two.

**042–043 · Normalizing an odd-order subgroup.** For a finite étale subgroup $\mu$ of odd prime rank in a form of $\operatorname{GP}(1)$, the sheets discuss a centralizer $H$, a normalizer $N$ and an inversion action. Scan 042 treats reciprocal eigenvalues and the separate case $u=\mathrm{id}_V+v$, $v^2=0$, $v\ne0$. Scan 043 formulates the assertions about $H$, $N^0$ and $N/H$, and begins a proof by lifting to $\operatorname{SL}(V)$.

**044–045 · Determination by the Lie line.** The author asks when a one-dimensional smooth subgroup can be recovered from its Lie algebra. He connects automatic commutativity with a deformation question. A unipotent subgroup supplies his counterexample to a proposed dimension for the normalizer. The one-line continuation proposes adding a discrete structure.

**047 · A quaternion algebra and the conic.** A list associates to $\mathcal M$ groups, Lie algebras, a special quadratic module and a conic $X$. In the split case $\mathcal M=V^\vee\otimes V$, a line $L$ gives $L^\perp\otimes L$ in $\mathcal M$. Formula (2) directs the map from $\mathbb P(V)$ to $X$; (3) describes it on $L$.

**048–049 · Rank-one operators.** The determinant quadric $Q$ is identified with a product of two projective lines. A rank-one operator gives its kernel and image, in that order. The oblique note gives the inverse using $L^\perp\otimes M$. Equations (8)–(9) encode the two lines by trace, determinant and composition. Equations (10)–(12) replace the operator line $J$ by a rank-two algebra with a homomorphism $\rho$ whose kernel is $J$.

## Questions and conjectures

- **038 and 040.** « Est-il vrai que $H$ est le centralisateur de $\mathfrak h$ » and « Est-il le centralisateur de $\mathfrak h$ ? » distinguish this question from the claim that $H$ centralizes itself.
- **040.** « Mais alors est-il possible d’avoir […] pour $g$ fixé » asks whether projective conjugation can multiply an entire family of sections by scalars. The universal-section calculation follows.
- **041.** « ? Mais le centralisateur […] semble être […] $H$. » The insertion « connexe » qualifies the tentative assertion.
- **044.** « est-il déterminé par $\operatorname{Lie}H$ ? » and « On se demande si la condition de commutativité est automatiquement satisfaite ? » The answer written for the first question assumes commutativity and residual characteristics different from two.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence (D37).** For a general $2\times2$ matrix, expanding $\det(\alpha I+\beta u)$ gives exactly the trace and determinant polynomial on 037. This checks the formula used to cut out the excluded divisor.
- **Model remark, medium confidence (R39).** Scan 042 ends with « de plus $N/N^0\subset(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times_S$ est »; 039 opens « alors un […] étale ». The repeated quotient, open subgroup and fibre comparison suggest a continuation. The sheets give no explicit cross-reference between the two passages.
- **Model remark, high confidence (N44).** For the split unipotent subgroup over a field, the normalizing matrices are upper triangular. Modulo scalars they form a Borel of dimension two, as required for the counterexample on 044.
- **Model remark, high confidence (A49).** In the split rank-one model, $u^2=(\operatorname{Tr}u)u$. Thus the operator line is an ideal in $\mathcal O I+J$, consistent with the proposed kernel of $\rho$ in (11). This checks the local multiplication behind the description.
- **Reading question, 048, (4)–(5), low confidence.** The frozen first glyph in $\operatorname{Var}(\Delta)$ also admits $\phi$. It has a vertical stroke through a triangular head, while the $\Delta(u)$ in (5) is an open-sided triangle with no descender. The identification with the determinant favours one quadratic form across both lines. It does not identify an accent or a pen correction in (4).
- **Reading question, 043, penultimate paragraph, low confidence.** The frozen sentence has « On a [word crossed] prouver que ». A possible alternative for the last verb is « prouvé ». Its final horizontal stroke could stand for a reduced r or a cursive vowel without a distinct accent. This affects whether the writer says a proof is due or already in hand.
