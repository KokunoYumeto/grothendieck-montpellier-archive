# K18-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Distributions and tangent maps (18).** The typescript transports the coalgebra structure on \(S(M)\) to the filtered module of distributions \(U(\underline V)\). It defines the tangent module as \(U_+(\underline V)\cap U_1(\underline V)\) and restricts \(f_*\) to obtain \(T(f)\). For a target given by a free module \(M\), projection to \(S^1(M)\) identifies certain formal maps with linear maps that vanish on \(U_0\). The next construction breaks off after “une application formelle”.

**Cycle classes and a choice of complex isomorphism (19).** For a smooth scheme over a field isomorphic to \(\mathbb C\), the manuscript begins with an algebraic class map. A choice \(\sigma\) produces a second map through topological cohomology and an integral sublattice. The comparison involves \(\sigma(2i\pi)\) and a power \(p\). The worked example has \(p=1\) and \(X=\mathbb P^1\). The NB distinguishes the effect of conjugation on a map and on its image lattice.

**Editorial changes to root-system nos. 5–8 (20).** The typescript asks for descriptions of \(P(R)\) and \(Q(R)\) using a projection and an intersection. It specifies how to introduce a group acting on the ambient space, when to divide by a factorial and how to describe the action on \(P/Q\). Later directions change rank hypotheses, ask for a 4 in the canonical form, and specify the positive roots in no. 8.

**Analytic and topological direct images (21).** Three diagrams compare constant coefficients with complexes of forms. The first displays two rows of direct-image comparisons. The small square locates the comparison at the level of \(i^!\). The framed square relates cycle classes in integral cohomology to classes with complex coefficients. The NB calls attention to the factor needed to preserve integral classes.

**Formal charts, kernels and morphisms (22).** Definitions 3–5 specify a formal chart of a \(K\)-algebra, compatibility between charts, a formal kernel equipped with a class of charts and a morphism of such kernels. The last test expresses a homomorphism of completed algebras in charts as \(g^*\) for a formal map \(g\). The paragraph after the definition says that one pair of charts suffices.

**Proper maps (23).** The square extends the preceding comparison to a proper morphism of holomorphic varieties of relative codimension \(p\). The top arrow is \(f_*^{\mathrm{top}}\). The bottom arrow carries \(f_*^{\mathrm{an}}\) and the coefficient \(1/(2i\pi)^p\). All four degrees have been worked over. The page suggests the algebraic version over \(\mathbb C\).

**Positivity and the choice of a basis (24).** The first paragraph finishes a contradiction by taking more partial sums than there are elements of \(P\). Step 4 passes to the hyperplane perpendicular to \(z\), uses induction on a root system in \(V'\), then extends the resulting basis to make the elements of \(P\) lexicographically positive. The following two numbered items defer discussion of the highest root and prescribe names for lattices \(Q(R)\) and \(P(R)\).

**Correspondences and the comparison isomorphism (25).** The manuscript expresses the action of a correspondence on \(X\times Y\) by pulling back along the first projection, multiplying by a class and taking direct image along the second projection. It gives the relation between the topological and algebraic classes and compares the induced actions. The central comparison passes through extension of scalars to \(\mathbb C\). In the lower square the scalar factors reappear on the arrows. The slanted note asks how far this compatibility can already be stated for the analytic and topological comparison.

**Classifying root systems (26).** The typescript describes identification of the two vertices of an edge of order 3, including what happens to the other incident edges and the variables \(\xi_i,\xi_j\). It gives an alternative about branching and chains, criticizes the statement of two lemmas, and distinguishes three group examples from the root-system cases.

**A topological field isomorphic to \(\mathbb C\) (27).** Without choosing an isomorphism, the manuscript considers a functor on motives with values in integral modules and gives comparisons after tensoring with \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) and \(K\). In its example involving \(\mathbb Z(-1)\), choosing a generator corresponds to orienting the projective line and choosing a square root of \(-1\) in \(K\).

**Closed sets of roots (28).** After directions concerning Serre’s propositions, the typescript starts Borel’s argument for proposition 15. Step 1 extracts a sum in \(P\) from a negative scalar product. Step 2 seeks to exclude a vanishing sum of elements of \(P\) by induction on its length. Step 3 seeks a nonzero \(z\) with \((z,x)\geq0\) on \(P\). Its sentence continues on the typescript page reproduced in scan 24.

**The two base fields and the integral submodule (29).** The opening writes \(\alpha_\infty(u)\) as \(v/(2\sqrt{-1}\pi)\) after the preceding choice. For a field isomorphic to \(\mathbb R\), the manuscript describes an integral functor with an action of \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), identified with a Galois group. It places \(\xi(M)\) in a product of local factors and says that the submodule is stable under that action.

**Coefficients, words and conjugacy (30).** A coefficient argument for a quotient precedes §8. The editorial directions describe a graph of words and a conjugacy argument through the successive words \(bAa,baA,aAb,Aba\). They also prescribe which assertions to retain about the exponents and where irreducibility is needed.

**Cover and beginning of the Betti–De Rham argument (31–32).** The cover names the Hodge and De Rham functors for base fields topologically isomorphic to \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\). The first page of the argument constructs the Betti realization, compares it with \(\mathrm{DR}(M)\), and uses a real structure and its involution together with the filtration to describe a bigrading. The choice of an isomorphism to \(\mathbb C\) is taken up in the last sentence, which continues beyond this packet.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High confidence.** Scan 28 stops with “alors, vu (1), on”. Scan 24 begins “peut pour tout entier” and finishes the partial-sum argument immediately before 4). Both the syntax and the printed sheet numbers (5) and (6) identify the join. The archive scan order is preserved.
- **M2 · Medium confidence.** On 22 the definition of a chart makes \(\varphi_i^*\) land in \(A\). With the usual right-to-left convention for composition, the equality printed with \(\psi_1^*\circ\varphi_1^*\) would type-check in the reversed order \(\varphi_1^*\circ\psi_1^*\). The analogous issue occurs in the second equality. The notation of definition 5 supports that convention. This is a conditional typing observation.
- **M3 · Low confidence.** On 19 the NB says conjugation changes the sign, just below the example \(p=1\). Applied to the scalar raised to a general \(p\), conjugating \(i\) would give \((-1)^p\). The example explains the minus sign for \(p=1\). The denominator has overwritten ink, and the scope of the NB is uncertain.
- **M4 · Low confidence.** On 21 the framed square labels the cohomological degrees \(2i\), while its scalar has exponent \(p\). The neighboring cycle-class notation suggests a common codimension. The page gives no equation identifying the two letters. Scan 23's overwritten degrees offer no independent basis for such a correction.
- **M5 · Reading question, low confidence.** On 29, line 4, the first raised numeral in the frozen \(H^1(\mathbb P^1,\Omega^1)\) could be mistaken for 2 because of its broad head and foot. The enlarged upright resembles the two raised ones beside it. The same paragraph's later word “possibles” could be compared with “primitifs”, but the end of the ink looks more like the former. Neither question changes the text.
- **M6 · Reading question, low confidence.** On 32, at the insertion before \(\mathrm{DR}(M)\), the frozen “(le K-vecteur)” has a faint beginning and a compressed ending. “(le K-module)” would also make sense, though the visible initial after the hyphen favours v. The mathematical construction does not determine the wording.
