## What the pages do

**002–003 · Papers and bibliography.** The pencil inscription connects the papers with Saavedra. The English proposal page lists publications 7–13, followed by research announcements 14–16. Its subjects include best approximation, metric projections, Fourier projection, Banach spaces and conditional expectations.

**004–005 · Descriptions of motives.** The cover names motives and crystals over a local field. The first list on 005 considers motives over a number field via Betti–de Rham and Betti–Hodge lattices, Hodge structures, Gauss–Manin, Tate and filtered crystalline data. The second list asks for analogues for \(p\)-divisible groups up to isogeny and mentions pseudo-motives. Its Hodge–Tate entry adds a module and a bigrading after extension of scalars. Its last entry describes a filtered crystal with a semilinear operation.

**006 · Comparing splittings.** A group extension carries two splittings. The author modifies one by the character followed by \(j_2\) and the other by \(u\), then sets the modified splittings equal. He writes their discrepancy as a product and considers what can be recovered on the kernels of the two maps. He ends with the quotient \(\Pi_1=\pi/\operatorname{Ker}u\).

**007 · Specialization and the cycle class.** The first square uses rational coefficients. The writer asks what information is lost on discarding torsion and discusses specialization in the Chow ring. For a smooth proper map, the lower square uses the group of cycles itself, “modulo rien du tout”, with integral adic coefficients. The definition of specialization breaks off in the last line.

**009 · Tate twists.** The note defines a twist of a mixed Hodge structure by retaining the underlying module and shifting the weight and Hodge filtrations. A separate formula concerns successive weight quotients. The last display gives the shifts for an integer \(n\).

**010–011 · Comparison points, filtrations and fields.** Three spaces of isomorphisms compare Betti, de Rham and Hodge realizations. The chosen points \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) satisfy three composition identities. The leaf describes a map from bigradings to filtrations, its unipotent group, and related quotient schemes. The field diagram puts the fields of the comparison points and their images in a single lattice with bounds on degrees. A boxed transcendence conjecture is followed by a list of proposed degree equalities.

**012–013 · Elliptic curves and transcendence.** The cancelled leaf 012 relates a conjecture about \(g_0\) to generation of an algebraic group. It treats periods and quasiperiods of an elliptic curve and singles out a determinant relation. Scan 013 asks three questions about the Betti–de Rham comparison over the algebraic numbers, separating the behavior of a splitting parameter in the complex multiplication case.

**014–015 and 017 · Fundamental classes.** A cover concerning the Hodge functor precedes a comparison of the analytic and topological fundamental classes. The first diagram on 015 places the residue construction beside comparison in cohomology with support. The author reduces to a transverse slice, then to the pointed disk. On 017 he integrates \(dz/z\), boxes the comparison in codimension one, and writes a diagram extending it to arbitrary classes. The reused typescript on 016 interrupts this argument in scan order.

**016 · Formal kernels.** Proposition 8 characterizes the linear maps induced by formal maps through a condition on \(U_0\) and compatibility with the diagonals. The proof chooses bases in the free case, writes the coefficients \(a_{\alpha,\beta}\), records their constant-term condition in (31), and gives their convolution identity in (32).

- **M09 · scan 009 · model, high confidence in the substitution.** Setting \(n=-1\) in the two final indices \(i+2n\) and \(i+n\) gives \(i-2\) and \(i-1\). The live indices above have these values. The ink under the first minus is crossed, so the calculation provides no reading for that earlier sign.
- **M11 · scan 011 · model, high confidence.** The equality \(g-b+u=g-l\) in the last line uses precisely the relation \(u+l=b\) in the small dimension list above. Substitution makes the difference zero. This check addresses the equality of the two right-hand expressions only.
- **M16 · scan 016 · model, high confidence under the nonnegative multi-index convention.** Setting \(\alpha,\beta_1,\beta_2\) equal to zero in (32) leaves \(a_{0,0}=a_{0,0}^2\), since the zero multi-index has only its zero decomposition. Equation (31) says this idempotent is nonzero.
- **M17 · scans 015 and 017 · model, high confidence for a positively oriented circle.** For \(z=r e^{it}\) with \(0\leq t\leq 2\pi\), the integral of \(dz/z\) is \(2\pi i\). Its reciprocal is the factor in the other boxed formula. The precise qualifying word beneath the two integral signs on 017 remains uncertain.
