# K12-08 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U1 · Weights over a field, scan 115.** Corollary 5.4 gives a range of weights for $R^if_*(F)$ when $X$ is smooth and $F$ is strictly special of weight $j$. It puts a conditional version for effectively admissible coefficients in parentheses. A second range ends at $2(i+j)$ when $i\leq m$, with successively smaller bounds on the Hodge levels. The writer offers two routes back to the constant sheaf and sketches a compactification with a normal-crossing boundary.

**U2 · Direct images for an immersion, scans 115–116.** Section 6 asks for effective admissibility and weights for higher direct images. In the constant-coefficient case the writer replaces an immersion by an open immersion and draws a triangle through a regular $Y'$. The boundary divisors $Z_i$ and their intersections enter the calculation of $R^qg_*(\mathbb Q_\ell)$, with twist $(-q)$ and the description “pur de poids $2q$”. The terms of the next spectral sequence are assigned bounds in $p$ and $q$.

**U3 · Weight and level tables, scans 116, 118, 120.** The crosses and tables organize the terms with total degree $i$. The sheet treats $i\leq n-1$ and $i\geq n$ separately. In the first boxed table on 118 the levels rise to $i$ and then fall to zero. In the second range the pair $q=2n-i$, $p=2(i-n)$ gives the displayed endpoint $2n$. Scan 120 collects the ranges in a table of $i$, level and weight and then considers how the bounds might change with coefficient weight.

**U4 · A relative bound at a point, scan 120.** The diagram places $y$ on the base of $f$. A quantity $d_y(f)$ is defined by a supremum involving a transcendence degree and the dimension of a closure at the base. The end of the expression is hard to read. A nearby box uses $n$ in both bounds, and an attached question asks when one can use $n-1$.

**U5 · Strictly special coefficients, scan 122.** The starting representative is written $F=R^dp_*(\mathbb Q_{\ell X'})$. A square gives two ways to calculate the direct image of the composite. Degeneration in the first makes $R^if_*(F)$ a “morceau” in degree $i+j$. The second sequence estimates the contribution of $R^q f'_*$ using $n'\leq n+j$. The final weight estimate stops after $\leq$.

**U6 · Admissibility by dévissage, scan 125.** A local finite filtration of $F|U$ is required, with graded pieces obtained as quotients of subobjects of a sheaf $R^ng_*(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. The construction places projectivity, smoothness, connected fibres and a dimension bound on $g$. The qualification beginning “ess. constant” is partly uncertain. Lemma 1 concerns a thick abelian subcategory and treats subobjects, quotient objects and extensions. Lemma 2 records inverse images.

**U7 · Direct images with proper supports, scans 127–129.** Lemma 3 puts the bound $d+d'$ on the type of the direct images for a compactifiable map of relative dimension at most $d'$. It reduces to a field, separates a dense smooth open from its closed complement and applies the filtration. The two sequences on 129 introduce $G,H,I$ to handle a subobject and a quotient. Complete reducibility is invoked to reach the iterated direct images with $p,q\geq0$. A proposed descending induction is cancelled.

**U8 · Reduction to dimension one, scans 131–133.** Artin factorization after shrinking $U$ reduces the argument to dimension one. A vanishing in degree zero then gives the displayed short exact sequence with outer degrees $2$ and $1$. The writer uses a smooth projective compactification $\widehat V$ and $Z=\widehat V-V$. On 133 he deals with the two extremes by definition and by induction.

**U9 · An open immersion into an excellent space, scans 133–135.** Proposition 4 starts with $i:U\to X$, an excellent $X$ of dimension at most $d$, and a $d'$-admissible $F$. The correction to the final numerical bound is unclear. The proof combines induction on $d+d'$ with induction on closed subsets of $X$. For a regular dense $V\subset U$, a triangle compares $F$, $\mathbb Rj_*F$ and $Q^*$. The support of the third object lies in $Z\cap U$, for $Z=\overline{U-V}$ taken in $X$.

**U10 · Normal crossings and the remaining coefficient case, scan 135.** The term on the smaller support is accepted by induction. A resolution factors the open immersion through a regular $X'$ with normal-crossing complement. For constant coefficients the text invokes purity and explicit calculations. For general coefficients it introduces a projective compactification over $X$, uses a direct factor and reduces to $d'=1$. The spectral sequence for $if$ is announced at the bottom.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 118 and 120 · high confidence.** The endpoint sits on the expected total-degree diagonal. With the two indices as written, $p+q=i$ and $p+2q=2n$. Expanding both differences gives zero.

**M2 · Scan 118 · high confidence.** In the first table the last level under $2i$ reads $2i$. In the later boxed table for the same range it reads $0$. The entries were checked separately against the two distinct cells. The page gives no instruction to erase the earlier one.

**M3 · Scan 133 · low confidence.** If the overwritten last part of the bound really is $-1$, substituting $d=d'=0$ produces $-1$, while the induction calls this case “trivial”. This makes the overwritten sign worth revisiting. The photograph and the sentence do not establish a corrected theorem.

**Reading question · Scan 115, Cor. 5.4, upper weight and the condition below it.** The frozen reading of the upper bound is $2m+2j$. A possible reading is $2n+2j$. The letter between the coefficient and the plus sign is a low continuous wave. It resembles the flattened $n$ in later tables, while this corollary also uses the reading $m$ below. The photograph does not cleanly separate one hump from two.
