## What the pages do

**Configurations and stabilizers (68).** Two tables place successive configurations in $C$ alongside products, cardinalities and automorphism groups. The first starts with $\mathrm{Pl}(L_1,C)=C$ and 27. The second goes from $B_1(C)$ to bases and bibases, with 72 and 36 in its last two rows. The accompanying “Étude cas par cas” starts with one vertex, then uses a pair of distinct unlinked vertices and a base opposite to the chosen base.

**Pairs, triples and roots (69–70).** The unordered pair is encoded by a bibase $B=\{b,b'\}$ and a marked element in a quotient of $B$. A three-free subset is then described using a bibase and three indices. The text gives 720 possibilities and associates sets of sizes 2, 3 and 3 to the construction. Interchanging the two three-element sets gives the involution on scan 70. The corollary relates a three-free subset to the two bases containing it, and the last paragraph relates the same data to pairs of roots at $60^\circ$.

**Hexagons and larger free subsets (71–72).** For a three-free subset $J$, the vertices outside $J$ unlinked to its elements form the hexagon in the left column. The drawing labels its six vertices. The following paragraph models the hexagon on a product of sets of sizes two and three. On the right, the four-free case has two orbits, giving extensions of degrees two and one. Five-free subsets are split according to whether a base contains them. The other case is related to the intersection $E(x)\cap E(y)$ and to pairs of unlinked points. The prefix attached to “étoiles” is pale and varies in shape.

**Bases and bibases (72).** The paragraph headed “Bases et bibases” describes $W=\mathrm{Aut}(C)$ acting on ordered bases, gives the counts of bases and bibases, and relates the marked objects to sets of prescribed cardinalities. The two category expressions in the left margin are relevant to the possible repeated subscript recorded under S3.

**Bitriangles and triades (74).** A bitriangle is described by a product of two three-element sets. The fibers of the two projections give six triangles in two families. The opposite graph defines the “bloc de triades”. The proposition states that the two graphs are isomorphic. Its argument uses the six graphs of bijections between the two factors, grouped in two classes using the parity of a permutation.

**The isomorphism and the proposed duality (75–76).** Each class gives a partition of the nine points. Scan 75 uses the two components of a point, a circular permutation and a transposition in the argument for bijectivity. After comparing adjacency and edge counts, the conclusion interprets complementation as an involutive autoequivalence. It then asks for a symmetric presentation of two sets partitioned in type $(3,3)$. On scan 76 the earlier word “relation” is struck and replaced by “bijection”; the proposed condition stops mid-sentence.

**Polyhedra, polygons and a pairing (78).** A list gives products beside prisms, double prisms, an antiprism, a pseudocube, cubes and a “dodécaèdre gauche”. A second list gives counts for squares, pentagons and hexagons. The associated figures include a numbered pentagon and a larger solid with indexed vertices. In the opposite orientation, a canceled block puts a three-case pairing on generators beside $\mathbb Z[C]$, a hatted $E(C)$ and $\lambda^\perp$. A separate count on this part of the sheet ends in 72.

**Binary coordinates and diagrams (79).** The sheet starts with a space over $\mathbb F_2$ indexed by a five-element set and distinguishes a two-element set $I$. Sums of the $e_i$ occur beside a four-entry table and a cube. Farther down are expressions with $\widetilde I$ and $J$, diagrams of fans, and lists of linked and unlinked vertices. The last two rows carry Greek labels with corrections and raised strokes.

**Prism data and a questioned implication (80).** A product at the top ends in 360; the next line places automorphisms beside $36\cdot4$ and 144. A hexagon, fans and two small solids accompany a description in terms of three-bases, a bibase and a partition of type $(3,3)$. The canceled lower block imposes conditions on $U,V,W$ at $P_u,P_v,P_w$ and at three named vertices. The implication following these conditions is explicitly questioned.

**Two sheets of graph working (81–82).** Scan 81 is canceled across the sheet. Its lemma begins with adjacent vertices of a triangle, introduces primed neighbors, and puts a barred implication after two triangle conditions. The remaining sketches and sideways lists compare possible incidences and labels. Scan 82 is a separate drawing of pentagons around a central pentagon. Vertex indices and edge numerals have different positions; three edges carrying the numeral 3 are drawn heavily.

**Differences and sums (83).** The writing distinguishes linked and unlinked vertices when forming their differences and their sums. Differences are counted in groups of six in one case, and sums in groups of five in another. The text associates a sum with $\lambda-\delta$, then separately counts 216 other sums and writes $27+216=243$. “sommes de 3 sommets” is the last heading.

**Course announcement (84).** “Polyèdres réguliers” is a 1976 first-year course outline. One part concerns polygons, convex polyhedra, faces and duality. The other concerns the five regular solids, their descriptions and symmetry groups. The letters of the three symmetric groups are written by hand into the type.
