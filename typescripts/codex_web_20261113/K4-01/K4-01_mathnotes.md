# Mathematical notes on scans 2–12

## What the pages do

**Scan 2, attached sheet 8.4.4.** The author distinguishes a subscript $ss$ for $F$ from a superscript $ss$ for $V$. Part a) states nilpotence above degree $n$. Part b) has $i<n$ for the full group and the twice-underlined $i\leq n$ for the torsion terms. The sequences $(*)$ and $(**)$ relate the free and torsion parts to $H^n(X,\underline O_X)$ and $H^0(X,\underline\Omega^n_X)$ respectively. He wonders if the two sequences are transposes.

**Scan 3, 8.1.** For a smooth proper $X$ over a perfect field $k$, the proposed derived reduction of crystalline cohomology over the Witt ring $W$ gives (8.1.2). The group on the left is $H^i(X)\otimes_W k$ and the one on the right is $\mathrm{Tor}_1^W(H^{i+1}(X),k)$. An interlinear addition takes semisimple parts. Its continuation up the margin distinguishes the actions of $F_X$ and $V_X$. Some of the exponents in that continuation are uncertain.

**Scan 3, (8.1.3)–(8.1.4).** Restriction to a hyperplane section $Y$ gives three maps $u_0,u_1,u_2$. The snake sequence passes through their kernels, $E(Y)\otimes_W k$, $E_{\rm DR}(Y)$ and the cokernel of $u_2$. The two versions of $E$ are defined by cokernels in degree $n-1$. The last restriction map is between Tor groups formed from degree $n$.

**Scans 3–5, 8.2–8.3.** Cancelled §8.2 transposes the map on torsion by duality and a Gysin map for $Y\to X$. Section 8.3 appeals to weak Lefschetz for torsion-freeness of $E(Y)$ and the isomorphism (8.3.1) on degree-$n-1$ torsion. With the crystalline analogue admitted, (8.3.2) expresses the relevant map on $X$ by cup product with the polarization. This removes the dependence of its kernel on the particular $Y$. The page turn gives $\mathrm{Ker}\,u_0=0$ in (8.3.3).

**Scans 5–6, 8.4–8.4.3.** For a finite-dimensional space with a $p$-linear endomorphism, $V_{ss}$ is the largest stable subspace on which the endomorphism is surjective. Applying this exact functor yields (8.4.1). The author interprets the resulting objects as étale $\underline{\mathrm F}_p$-modules or Galois representations. Lemma 8.4.3 identifies the semisimple parts of de Rham and structure-sheaf cohomology. Its argument uses the zero action of Frobenius in positive form degree on the first Hodge page and universal cycles in the surviving column.

**Scans 6–8, “Théorème” 8.5.** For smooth projective connected $X$ of dimension $n$ and a smooth hypersurface section $Y$ of dimension $n-1$, (8.5.1) maps $(E(Y)\otimes_W k)_{ss}$ onto $E(Y,\underline O_Y)_{ss}$. To their left are $\mathrm{Ker}\,v$ and $({}_p\mathrm{Tors}^{n}(X))_{ss}$, in that order. Here $v$ is coherent restriction. The earlier definition of $w$ by cup product with $d\xi$ is struck. Over a finite field the author reads the number of $p$-adic unit eigenvalues from the rank of the crystalline term. Equations (8.5.5) and (8.5.6) give the difference of the two ranks and a lower bound.

**Scans 8–9, 8.5.7–8.5.8.** The author singles out two cases for injectivity of $v$, namely $n=2$ and degree $d$ large enough for $H^{n-1}(X,\underline O_X(-d))=0$. He takes sections on the open set where the coherent semisimple rank is maximal and discusses constancy of the unit-eigenvalue count. The live large-degree display asks for generic positivity. An earlier arrow towards $+\infty$ in that display is cancelled.

**Scans 9–10, 8.5.9.** The parameter space $U_d$ selects smooth sections of the right dimension and maximal coherent semisimple rank. The sequences are described in families as étale local systems or representations of $\pi_1(U_d)$. In the corrected sentence it is the kernel of $\varphi$ that comes from the base field. After a finite extension its Frobenius eigenvalues may all be taken to be 1. The variation is then described using the coherent representation, Hasse-Witt matrices and Frobenius elements at points of $U_d$.

**Scans 10–11, 8.5.10 and Serre's example.** The revised question concerns when $\varphi$ is injective and hence bijective. The author relates this to surjectivity of $w$ and to crystalline torsion, and suggests comparing the torsion of a polarized lift. On scan 11 he takes a fixed-point-free cyclic quotient of a complete intersection, cuts to a surface and identifies a nonzero $\mathrm{Ker}\,\varphi$. The group comparison at the right edge of the Serre paragraph is clipped. A higher-dimensional product construction using $X'=X\times Z$ is cancelled.

**Scan 12, 8.6.** For the representation on $V$ coming from coherent vanishing cohomology, the questions are irreducibility and the size of the image in $\mathrm{Aut}(V)$. Assuming full image and $\dim V\ge2$, the author passes from available traces to the proposed realization of any prescribed residue of $\mathrm{card}\,Y(k')$ modulo $p$, over a finite extension $k'/k$ and in any given nonempty open set. He closes with the case of high-degree plane curves.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 7–8 · High confidence, conditional on B1.** The excess of the crystalline semisimple dimension over the coherent one equals $\dim\ker\varphi$. In (8.5.1) this is $\dim({}_p\mathrm{Tors}^n(X))_{ss}-\dim\ker v$. Exactness at the middle two terms gives the equality and the nonnegativity in (8.5.5)–(8.5.6). This checks the rank calculation only.

**R2 · Scan 12 · High confidence for the two enumerations.** As a finite check on B2, I enumerated the invertible $2\times2$ matrices over $\mathbf F_2$ and $\mathbf F_3$. In trace order $0,1$ there are $4,2$ matrices over $\mathbf F_2$. In trace order $0,1,2$ there are $18,15,15$ over $\mathbf F_3$. Thus each trace occurs in those full linear groups. The geometric monodromy image on the sheet is a separate question.

**R3 · Scans 7 and 10 · High confidence, conditional on B1.** Exactness makes $w$ surjective precisely when $\varphi$ is injective. If $\ker v=0$ as well, surjectivity of $w$ forces $({}_p\mathrm{Tors}^{n}(X))_{ss}=0$. This distinguishes the two implications that stand close together in 8.5.10.
