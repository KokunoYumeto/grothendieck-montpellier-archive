## What the pages do

**379, end of the preceding discussion.** The opening concerns arrows $f\times\mathrm{id}_X$ for a map of finite ordered sets. Grothendieck also considers more general ordered sets, with an Artinian condition on the opposite of $S$ and a Noetherian condition on its space. He says the last hypothesis can be dropped under $\mathrm{Loc}(8)$. The passage begins mid-sentence.

**379–380, §7.5.** He gives a case in which every $f_*$ exists. The displayed list reads $\mathrm{Loc}(6a,8,9)$ after a cancellation. A marginal NB gives $6'a$ in place of $6a$. The conclusion is that $\mathrm{HOT}_W$ is a derivator on all of $\mathrm{Cat}$. He replaces $\mathrm{Cat}$ by $A^\wedge$ for a suitable test category, invokes a closed Quillen triple, and recovers $f_!$, $f_*$ and exactness of the indicated squares.

**381–383, strength and limits of the argument.** He asks how much right homotopy theory one gets without a $W$-fibration axiom. A functorial factorisation is known in the semisimplicial setting, but he has no concrete description for the calculation he wants. Quillen’s strong saturation result gives a further localizer axiom. He then observes that the conditions under which he can prove existence on the right already give exact $W$-cartesian squares and exact cosuspension sequences.

**383–384, §7.6.** After taking stock of questions 1, 2 and 4 from §7.1, he sorts sufficient hypotheses by derivator axiom. The braced table distinguishes the starred axioms, the unstarred axioms on $\mathrm{Cat}$ and the unstarred axioms on finite ordered diagrams. The small lines above it concern cofibration and fibration structures. He sets aside $\mathrm{Der}\,1,2,3$ for individual treatment.

**385–386, decomposition and localisation.** He says decomposition holds for the prederivator of a model category without a condition on $W$. For localisation, he invokes a fibration or cofibration structure on the diagram models and the result attributed to K. S. Brown. He singles out two cases, a finite ordered indexing set and a factorisation chosen functorially in the arrow. He then applies this discussion to $\mathrm{Cat}$.

**387–389, the third axiom.** Under $\mathrm{Loc}(3\,\mathrm{bis})$, the passage relates full faithfulness on constant objects to $\mathrm{hot}_W(f)$ being an isomorphism. The homology comparison is expressed using $X\times S$ and $X\times T$. He takes $X=e$ in one direction and uses preservation of finite products in the other. He applies the criterion to $S\to e$ and collects the sufficient conditions for $\mathrm{Der}(1)$, $\mathrm{Der}(2)$ and $\mathrm{Der}(3)$ in a second table.

**389–392, §7.7.** He introduces $W_D$, the $D$-equivalences in $\mathrm{Cat}$, and reduces the asphericity assertion to a category $S$ with an initial object. Writing $p:S\to e$, he tests full faithfulness through the counit $p_!p^*\xi\to\xi$. A cofibrant replacement $y'\to y$ gives the displayed composite. Evaluation at the initial object identifies one arrow with the identity and the other with the component of the replacement. He concludes in $D(e)$ and mentions a finite-ordered variant for a category with just fibrations or cofibrations.

**393, Proposition 7.7.1.** For a special closed Quillen triple he states $\mathrm{Der}(1,2,3,4^*,5^*,6^*)$. For a very special triple he adds $\mathrm{Der}(4,5,6)$, giving a derivator. Already in the special case, $W_D$ is a fundamental localizer satisfying $\mathrm{Loc}(1\,\mathrm{ter},3\,\mathrm{bis})$. The following NB begins a qualification whose conclusion lies outside these scans.
