# Mathematical notes on scans 413–427

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 413.** The sheet finishes an argument about the arrow in (2), then turns to the source and target maps from the path category with subscript ∞. To show their fibres aspherical, Grothendieck fixes the source and expresses the resulting category as an inductive limit of categories of paths of a given type. He invokes a deformation of the indexing interval onto its origin.

**U2 · Scans 413–415.** A square built from $f:X'\to X$ gives a barred category of pairs $(x',c)$ with $\sigma(c)=f(x')$. The barred functor takes the other endpoint $\beta(c)$. The notes describe its fibre over $x_0$, organize the paths by their two indices, and distinguish stages according to the direction of the final part of the path. The question becomes whether transport along an arrow of $X$ gives a Hot-equivalence.

**U3 · Scans 415–417.** A square compares slices with ordinary fibres. At finite stages Grothendieck writes forward and reverse functors and considers their passage to a limit. He distinguishes strict compatibility from compatibility up to homotopy and asks for identities of composites and a commutative square. The single and double bars over $X'$ distinguish several steps of the calculation.

**U4 · Scans 418–420.** Section 13 compares $\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}_\infty(X)$ with $\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}(X)$, including their fibres with endpoints $a,b$. Two proposed results concern the constant-path inclusion and the source–target functor. A category over $\mathbb N^o$ supplies two parallel assertions, numbered $1'$ and $2'$. The diagram on 419 places this category between the two path categories. On 420 the comparison is made in Hot by inverting the second projection.

**U5 · Scans 421–422.** For the constant-path assertion, the notes factor $\alpha_1$ as $ji$. The fibrewise assertion about $j$ is formulated as a lemma for each path type $\tau$. For $i$ they use a base category with a final object. They then reduce properness to smoothness by passing to the opposite category and start the smoothness argument with an arrow $(a',b')\to(a,b)$ and a path $c$ over $(a,b)$.

**U6 · Scans 422–426.** Grothendieck forms a category of lifts $(c',w)$. He extends $c$ at the two ends to obtain $\widetilde c$ and compares the lift category to a slice over $\widetilde c$. The functor $\varphi$ adds constant endpoint pieces, including on morphisms. In the other direction $\psi$ uses the retraction $\rho_c:\widetilde c\to c$. Folios 163 and 164, scans 425 and 424 respectively, supply adjoining parts of these definitions. The slice has a final object. For the other composite the notes give a comparison morphism to each original object and its triangle.

**U7 · Scan 427.** “On a gagné !” closes the comparison of the two composites. The page then treats $1'$ by a diagram over $\mathbb N^o$, using the earlier fibre lemma and the final object $0$. It ends by stating $2'$, that the map from the intermediate category to $X\times X$ is proper and smooth.
