# Mathematical notes · cote 93, scans 165–178

## What the pages do

**165, displays (12) and (12′).** The de Rham cohomology of \(X/T\) is the abutment of the Leray spectral sequence for \(X/S/T\). The differential in the base direction comes from the Gauss–Manin connection. Flatness of \(\Omega^1_{S/T}\) gives the simpler expression for the second page in terms of de Rham cohomology on \(S/T\).

**165–166, the application (13)–(20).** A class whose edge image vanishes gives a class in filtration degree one. Under the flatness assumption this has a torsor description. The notes specify the translation law for \(d_P\) and its closedness condition. In characteristic zero a bracketed aside states the corresponding description using infinite principal parts and a stratification.

**167, variations with coefficients and with a varying base.** A quasi-coherent coefficient module \(E\) with integrable connection can be tensored into the filtered complex. Local splitting accounts for the additive filtration calculation. The writer qualifies the projection-formula step by a flatness hypothesis and writes the spectral sequence (22). He also proposes localising the construction on \(T\) and formulating its compatibility with connections over a further base.

**168–169, relative differentials and a \(p\)-basis.** The title sheet opens a new text in characteristic \(p\). With \(\Lambda=A[B^p]\), the notes identify differentials over \(A\) with those over \(\Lambda\). For one basis element the two rows give representatives \(1\) and \(x^{p-1}dx\) for cohomology (U1). The tensor-product calculation for a family then uses Künneth.

**170, construction of the map.** The proposed \(\psi\) takes degree-zero elements to their \(p\)th powers and \(dx\) to \(x^{p-1}dx\). The page verifies a product identity, draws the relative Frobenius diagram, and passes to the cohomology-valued \(\varphi\). The target of the initial assertion is explicitly cycles. See P3 and R2 below.

**171, the isomorphism and its inverse.** Theorem 1 gives the comparison after tensoring with \(\Lambda\) when a \(p\)-basis is present. The argument decomposes the comparison into one-generator maps and matches the indicated bases. Corollary 1 displays cycles as a direct sum with boundaries. The inverse on cohomology is named \(\widetilde C\); composing with passage from cycles to classes gives the cycle form of the operation.

**172–173, characterisation and logarithmic forms.** Corollary 2 specifies the operation by its action on exact forms, its algebra rules and its value on the one-generator representative. Corollary 3 applies this to \(dx/x\) for a unit. The special case with base mapping into \(B^p\) leads to the untwisted expressions \(C(x^{p-1}dx)=dx\) and \(C(dx/x)=dx/x\).

**173–174, schemes and functoriality.** The relative Frobenius and its twist organise the construction on sheaves. The image sheaf \(\underline\Lambda\) intervenes in the tensor product, and local \(p\)-bases provide an inverse. The two diagrams on 174 describe what happens under a commutative square of schemes.

**174–175, connections on an invertible module.** After a reference connection \(\Delta_0\) is fixed, another connection differs from it by a one-form \(\omega\). The proposition relates flatness to \(d\omega=0\) and compatibility with \(p\)th powers to an equality between the Cartier image and the twisted form. The margins on 175 propose expressing the obstruction directly as a tensor, also for modules of higher rank.

**175–176, computation in a basis.** In an affine neighbourhood with \(p\)-basis \((x_i)\), the \(D_i\) and multiplication operators satisfy the listed commutator and power relations. Writing \(\omega=\sum c_i\,dx_i\), the Jacobson formula produces the coefficient \(c_i^p+D_i^{p-1}c_i\). Closure gives the mixed derivative relation (**). The difference between the two forms has these obstruction coefficients.

**176–177, descent and the logarithmic-derivative theorem.** The notes use descent for a flat connection compatible with \(p\)th powers. Applied to \(L=\underline O_X\), a local horizontal generator gives a logarithmic differential. The theorem describes the image of \(f\mapsto df/f\) by closure and the Cartier condition; the NB identifies the kernel as \(\Lambda^*\) and gives its form over a perfect base.

**177–178, trace and residues.** For \(X\) smooth over \(S\), relative Frobenius is finite locally free. In relative dimension \(n\) the top differential sheaves are identified with relative dualizing modules. Theorem 3 identifies top-degree Cartier with the trace. Two systems of denominators, the twisted sections and the \(p\)th powers, are used to set up residues. The consequence beginning at the foot of 178 lies beyond the supplied text.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · model · high confidence for this example (169).** In the one-generator example \(B=\mathbf F_3[x]/(x^3)\), the columns of \(d\) on \(1,x,x^2\), in the basis \(dx,x\,dx,x^2dx\), are \((0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,2,0)\). The surviving cohomology representatives are \(1\) and \(x^2dx\), as in the two handwritten rows. This checks a single case.

**R2 · model · high confidence (170).** The cycle-valued ring homomorphism asserted in P3 fails additivity already for \(B=\mathbf F_2[x,y]\) over \(\mathbf F_2\). On \(d(x+y)\), the discrepancy between \((x+y)d(x+y)\) and \(x\,dx+y\,dy\) is \(y\,dx+x\,dy=d(xy)\). The two coefficients are nonzero in the free differential module. This discrepancy vanishes on taking its cohomology class. The argument uses the visible target and condition c, outside the torn portion.

**R3 · model · high confidence for this example (170).** Comparing the assignments in R2 after passage to \(H^1\) removes this particular obstruction. It explains why distinguishing the target \(Z^1\) from \(H^1\) matters when reading the change of target later on the folio. The computation concerns this pair of elements only.
