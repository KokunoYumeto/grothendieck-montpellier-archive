# Mathematical notes on K14-06

The seven handwritten leaves concern algebraic cycles and abelian varieties. Six reused typescripts interrupt them in scan order. References below use the scan number, with the typescript's own page number where it helps to reconnect a proof.

## What the pages do

**74 · Albanese and Picard comparisons.** Four lines set the fundamental groups and first cohomology of the Albanese and Picard varieties alongside the first and next-to-top cohomology of a smooth projective $X$. The comparison signs carry $t$ or $(t)$, and Tate twists occur in different positions. The questions about surjectivity and injectivity stand beside particular lines. The brace below associates the Albanese with $H^1(X)$ and the Picard variety with $H^{2n-1}(X)(n-1)$.

**75 · Cogroup structures on a polynomial ring.** The English typescript writes $m(t)$ as a sum of tensors with coefficients $\alpha_{ij}$. The counit conditions single out the two linear terms. The associativity calculation gives (3.1). The author chooses the largest first index $p$, then the largest second index $q$ in that row, and compares a term with coefficient $\alpha_{pq}\alpha_{pq}^{p}$. The analogous bound for the second index follows in one sentence. The sheet ends at “Consequently,”.

**76 · Duality and a graded cycle functor.** In the category VA the duality sends $A$ to $A^*$. A second functor gives graded commutative rings of cycles modulo algebraic equivalence. The notes distinguish a top-degree identification with $\mathbb Z$, described as canonical with restricted functoriality, from the functorial identification in degree zero. The projection formula is linked to the covariant cycle law.

**76 · Divisor classes and homomorphisms.** The “Application fondamentale” draws $C^1(A)$ mapping to $\operatorname{Hom}(A,A^*)^{\mathrm{alt}}$, with a dotted route through $C^1(A\times A)$. The lower diagram uses two varieties, divisors on their product, and $\operatorname{Hom}(A,B^*)$. The boxed marginal note asks whether this could serve as a definition of $A^*$.

**77 and 86 · Forgetting the module structure.** The end of the preceding argument uses a spectral sequence for composed functors and vanishing for $q\ne0$. Corollary 2.6 then compares the cohomology of an $A$-module with that of its underlying abelian group. Typescript p. −20− introduces the inclusion functor. Page −21− distinguishes its module and abelian-group versions with an underline, and compares positive-degree vanishings in the two presheaf categories. The closing “c.q.f.d.” precedes the new heading on acyclicity criteria.

**78 · Tate modules, duality and cycle classes.** A covariant Tate-module assignment and a contravariant $H^1$ assignment lead to a square with the dual-module functor twisted by $-1$. The page writes $H^*(A)$ as an exterior algebra on $H^1(A)$. Below the square, a class map from $C^i(A)$ lands in twisted cohomology and is described as compatible with the ring structure. The two top exterior powers for $A$ and $A^*$ are placed above two lower coefficient twists. The sign check is written beside those last identifications.

**79 · Epimorphic families and acyclicity.** The reused p. −25− begins with condition ii), requiring the higher cohomology of each epimorphic family to vanish. For the converse the proof picks a full subcategory with a family of generators, representable fibre products, and the chosen object $S$. It equips that subcategory with the induced topology and obtains the vanishing at $S$. Definition 3.5 names the resulting acyclic modules and sheaves.

**80 · Degree and transposition.** The first diagram is in equal dimensions. It identifies the two top cohomology groups with a coefficient twist and describes the map as multiplication by $d=\deg u$, including when $d$ is zero. A warning recalls the equal-dimension hypothesis. After the dimensions become arbitrary, the transposed map has target $H^{i+2(n'-n)}(A')(n'-n)$. The data named for it are the map in degree one and the two top-degree identifications.

**81 · The image of the unit.** The notes relate the unit to the cycle $u(A)$, separate the dimension-drop case, and name the sheaf-theoretic image $B$. They write a multiple $d$ of the class attached to $B$. A second description uses the transpose $u_1$ on rational Tate modules. The page distinguishes failure of injectivity from the injective case, in which it introduces a multivector with degree $\dim\operatorname{Coker}u_1$. Its description as an alternating form continues on 83.

**82 · Preservation of acyclicity by direct image.** The reused p. −23− begins with dependence on the underlying abelian sheaf and cautions about restriction along a continuous functor of sites. Proposition 3.3 requires a covering family refining a covering sieve. The iterated fibre products must exist on both sides, and the canonical comparison maps must be isomorphisms. Under that condition the direct image of a $C'$-acyclic sheaf is $C$-acyclic.

**83 · Determinants, lattices and the general transpose.** A sequence involving $K$, $H^1(A')$ and $H^1(A)$ precedes a top exterior-power comparison. A characteristic class multiplied by the class being sought is compared with $d$ times another characteristic class. The note on $d$ circles the two integral Tate-module subscripts. A cohomology pairing then identifies $H^i(A)$ with a twisted exterior power of the rational Tate module. The last two rows express the pushforward through a map of exterior powers.

**84 · Five forms of the acyclicity criterion.** On reused p. −22−, Proposition 3.1 sets out i), ii), ii) bis, iii) and iii) bis. They distinguish tests over covering sieves, Čech groups over objects, and vanishing of presheaves in positive degrees. The proof connects them by the numbered formulas, a spectral sequence and (2.5). Definition 3.2 calls the sheaves satisfying them $C$-acyclic.

**85 · Recovering the image from the class.** The writer takes the image of the fundamental class under a top exterior-power map with a Tate twist. The compressed addition at the right modifies the opening sentence. The last lines say that knowing $u$ applied to the unit determines the image of $u_1$ when the homomorphism is injective.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence for this calculation (75).** With only the $(1,1)$ mixed term left, the displayed polynomial law has the form $F(x,y)=x+y+cxy$. Expanding $F(F(x,y),z)-F(x,F(y,z))$ gives zero. This checks associativity for that family. The largest-index necessity argument has a different scope and was not independently verified.
- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence for the degree comparison (80–81).** Setting $i=0$ in the shift on 80 gives $2(n'-n)$, the exponent written on the lower $\gamma$ on 81. The tiny first character of the upper H-exponent on 81 remains a reading question. Its frozen candidates are 2 and 1, and the hook lacks the clear base visible on the lower 2.
- **R3 · Model remark, low confidence about the intended image (83, 85).** The integral subscripts are circled on 83, while 85 moves from rational Tate modules to $T_\ell$ in the final assertion. A distinction between a rational image and an integral sublattice would affect its interpretation. For example, the two integral matrices $\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(1,2)$ have the same determinant and different images modulo 2. This elementary check leaves open what data the writer includes in the class on 85.
