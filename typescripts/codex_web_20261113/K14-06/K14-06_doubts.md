### Notes on the notation table

The small o/0 on VA can also denote a circle sign (76, line 3 and 78, line 2). The final straight Tate-module connectors on 85, lines 3 and 8, both have blunt ends. Compare the headed arrow on line 4. The 2n versus ±2n possibilities belong only to the enclosed twist at the lower left of 78. Context can identify the mathematical role of a symbol more securely than its alphabet or its exact shape.

## Legibility

The typescripts usually distinguish letters and indices cleanly even when a type impression is faint. A pen often supplies just the tip of a typed arrow, an accent over a printed H, a letter ending, or a tail on a full stop. The weakest typed readings are the worn acronym on scan 82 and the C/G distinction on 84. On the handwritten sheets, ell, i and 1 can often be distinguished by height, a dot or the adjacent stroke. Determining an alphabet from a single looped Q is harder. The crossed labels on 76 and 78, the two overwritten superscripts on 83, and the cramped interline on 85 deserve particular scrutiny. A proposed reading inside a deletion describes what may still be visible underneath it. The inventory attributes the handwritten notes to Grothendieck. The pen and pencil additions on the reused typescripts have no signature, so their hand is recorded as unknown.
