# Mathematical notes on cote 19, scans 19–29 and 31–34

## What the pages do

**M01 · Relative exactness and injectivity (19–20).** A short sequence is called \(S\)-exact when its image under \(S\) is exact and splits. An object \(M\) is \(S\)-injective when applying the indicated contravariant Hom functor with target \(M\) gives an exact sequence. The first example is \(M=T(X)\). The bracketed argument on 19 revisits how many such targets are needed to test exactness.

**M02 · The sequence \(\Sigma(A)\) (19–20).** The manuscript puts \(A\) into \(TS(A)\), names a cokernel, and applies \(S\) to the resulting sequence. A one-sided inverse supplies the splitting. Taking \(M=A\) in the Hom test leads to the characterization of relatively injective objects as direct factors of objects of the form \(T(X)\). The comparison with Cartan and Eilenberg occurs at the end of that argument.

**M03 · From split images to derived functors (20–22).** A remark compares exactness with exactness after applying \(S\). The live calculation on 21 applies \(S\) to a quotient of a kernel by an image. Below the cancelled diagram, the text introduces a relative theory of derived functors and iterates \(TS=C^0\) to obtain a canonical resolution. Scan 22 asks when the resulting functors agree with the usual ones. The numbered conditions concern injectivity of the initial map, exactness of the auxiliary functors, and exactness of the sequence obtained with \(\Gamma C^0\). The final condition concerns left exactness of \(\Gamma\).

**M04 · Another draft of the relative theory (23–24).** The functor now appears as a tilde. The examples repeat the Hom test and the direct-factor description. The boxed canonical sequence starts at \(X\), passes through \(T(X)\), and ends in a cokernel denoted by an upper mark. Scan 24 defines an \(S\)-resolution together with homotopy data on the transformed augmented complex. The cycle sequences give a degreewise description.

**M05 · Effacing morphisms and right complexes (24–25).** A cancelled draft describes an effacing morphism using the image of a map between two Hom groups. Diagrams compare this description with a factorization through \(Y\). The lower part of 25 gives the structure maps of a right \(t\)-complex. One triangle is the augmentation triangle; the other expresses the differential in degree \(i\).

**M06 · Extension by induction (25–26).** Starting from \(u:A\to B\), the author constructs a map between the augmented complexes one degree at a time. The formula for \(\bar u^{n+1}\) uses \(s'^{n+1}\), \(T(\bar u^n)\), and \(t^n\). A calculation with \(d^n\) is struck through. The next paragraph gives a recursive formula for \(C^*(A)\), sets its initial degree, and describes its two resolution structures and its functoriality.

**M07 · Restriction and coinduction (27).** For \(V\) a subring of \(U\), the example takes the restriction-of-scalars functor. Two formulas spell out the Hom identification and the map \(e_B(f)=f(1)\). The map \(i_B\) is identified using \(B\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_U(U,B)\). The relatively exact sequences split over \(V\); the relatively injective \(U\)-modules are characterized by direct factors of \(\mathrm{Hom}_V(U,B)\).

**M08 · Tensor and sheaf examples (28).** The tensor–Hom identification leads to the dual statement about relatively projective modules. The two maps on the page go from \(A\) to \(U\otimes_V A\) and back, with their linearities written at the right. The third example uses sheaves of modules on \(X\), a product of categories indexed by its points, and the functor of Godement.

**M09 · Constructible sheaves (29, 31).** The cover names SGA 4 IX. Scan 31 sets out an outline about torsion, locally constant and constructible sheaves. It proposes equivalent tests involving pullback, a partition into locally closed pieces, presentations, filtrations and a dévissage. The lower part lists stability properties. Different hypotheses are written into individual clauses; in the crowded left-hand clause even their exact attachment is sometimes uncertain.

**M10 · Purity (32).** A crossed draft proposes equivalent purity statements on regular subschemes. The first requires local cohomology with support to vanish outside degree \(2d\); an additional statement in degree \(2d\) is crossed separately. The proof sketches implications through the codimension-one case and through a local calculation. A marginal qualification concerns étale extensions.

**M11 · Outline and diagrams (33).** The propositions labelled e, f and g concern limits, criteria for constructibility, and locally constant sheaves. Later entries name complexes of sheaves, torsion, Kummer and Artin Schreier. The page contains two versions of a numbered dependency diagram. The left version is heavily cancelled. “Leitfaden” can be read inside it.

**M12 · A fibrewise vanishing criterion (34).** A crossed argument relates membership in the image of \(H^n(Y,F)\) to the vanishing of a class on geometric fibres. A corollary connects a global acyclicity condition with fibrewise acyclicity. The proof invokes vanishing of higher direct images in a specified range and an exact sequence from the spectral sequence. The reduction to a section vanishing on fibres ends mid-sentence.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01 · Model, high confidence, scan 26.** The domains and codomains written beside the factors agree in the displayed inductive composite: it starts in \(X^{n+1}\) and ends in \(Y^{n+1}\). The differential identity is a separate claim left open by the crossed calculation.
- **R02 · Model, medium confidence, scan 27.** With the displayed natural identification of \(B\) with \(\mathrm{Hom}_U(U,B)\), the underlying \(V\)-linear composite \(e_B i_B\) evaluates the multiplication map at \(1\) and returns its original element. This uses the evaluation formula and explains the order of the arrows.
- **R03 · Model, high confidence, scan 33.** Following the strokes downward in the right-hand drawing gives two paths from 1 to 7: \(1,2,6,7\) and \(1,2,3,4,7\). The branch to 5 ends there. This is a reading of the numbered drawing; the strokes themselves have no arrowheads.
- **R04 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 25, upper diagram.** The returned diagonal begins in the upper-left cell. Its tail sits between the first and second rows of writing, so a start at \(\mathrm{Hom}(A,T(X))\) is also possible. The diagram label is read as \(\rho_A\); the adjacent prose independently has \(\beta_A\). Neither discrepancy establishes a change of notation.
- **R05 · Model reading question, low confidence, scan 31, heading 1.** The returned “Suite des faisceaux de torsion” may begin with “Sorite”. Between the initial S and the tall t the hand makes only a few shallow turns. This would affect the character of the heading without changing its subject.
