# Mathematical notes on scans 243–257

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 243.** Loc 4 concerns a category covered by two open subcategories. In the cocartesian square formed with their intersection, each inclusion out of the intersection belongs to W exactly when the opposite inclusion does.

**U02 · Scan 243.** Grothendieck lists variants of the W-fibration axiom. The strongest takes a condition tested with maps between contractile categories over Y and concludes that base change along f carries arbitrary W-maps over Y to W-maps over X. He next writes closure under filtered inductive limits and a stronger version involving accessibility and a small generating family.

**U03 · Scan 244.** Loc 8 requires a W-map to induce a bijection on connected components. He distinguishes axioms that enlarge W by closure from conditions that constrain its size. He puts connexity last in his order of practical importance and recalls a theorem relating failure of this axiom to W containing every arrow of Cat.

**U04 · Scan 245.** Three lists identify the axioms used in 8.4 and 8.5. The first lists 1 bis, 2, 3 for 8.4, parts 1° and 3°, and 8.5 b). The next adds 4 for 8.5 a). The last adds 7 for 8.4, part 2°, and points ahead to strong saturation. An isolated slanting stroke and dot follow the first list.

**U05 · Scans 245–247.** Proposition 9.1 asserts that a finite coproduct of arrows in W belongs to W under Loc (1,2,3,4). The filtered-limit axiom removes the finiteness restriction. Corollary 9.2 tests a map by taking its coproduct with the identity of a fixed Z. A cocartesian square gives that test. Two stacked squares then express the binary coproduct as a composite. A discarded argument crosses the break onto 247.

**U06 · Scans 247–248.** In the reverse direction Grothendieck asks whether each component of a W-map given as a sum must belong to W. He reduces the question to restriction along a direct summand and invokes a fibration condition to obtain it. Corollary 9.3 records the implication from the family sum to its members and the W-fibration property of a summand inclusion. The marginal note discusses the opposite implication under another list of hypotheses.

**U07 · Scans 248–249.** Proposition 9.4 sets out versions of decomposition under Loc (1). The two-part formulation concerns adjoining a fixed summand and restricting to a direct summand. Finite component sets or the limits axiom allow a test on individual connected components. A crossed corollary becomes clause c), with an implication from a finite coproduct to its factors. The last parenthesis returns to an arbitrary family.

**U08 · Scan 250.** The author proposes an axiom of decomposition in weak and strong forms. The first tests a finite family by its coproduct. The second allows an arbitrary indexing set. The small central version separates preservation of binary sums from reflection on the two summands. The lateral version relates reflection to the inclusion of a direct summand being a W-fibration.

**U09 · Scans 250–251.** Corollary 9.5 gives componentwise tests for a map to Y and for a map over Y, using the decomposition of Y into its connected components. Corollary 9.6 says that under the stated decomposition axiom, connexity amounts to requiring X to be 0-connected whenever a W-map X→Y has 0-connected target. The ensuing NB contrasts their common subject with the author’s conjecture that the two axioms are independent.

**U10 · Scan 252.** Proposition 9.7 supposes weak decomposition and failure of connexity. Its conclusion makes the full subcategory of nonempty objects in Hot_W equivalent to the point. The entire category is equivalent to the point or to Δ¹ according as the empty-to-terminal map is or is not invertible there. The proof begins with a missed component Y₀, multiplies by Y₀, and uses the fold from two copies of e to e to obtain failure of injectivity on components. Some of the map names are overwritten.

**U11 · Scan 253.** A map of intervals sends endpoints in different components of I to points of the 0-connected J. Two maps from X to Y can be joined using I. The construction separates I into two summands and uses the second projection followed by the prescribed map on each summand.

**U12 · Scans 254–255.** The solid arrows compare the two cylinders. A dotted extension h′ would give a homotopy using J. After localization the horizontal comparison becomes invertible, which supplies h′ there. The endpoint maps into J are homotopic, and the author uses their products with the identity of X to identify the two original arrows in the localized Hom set.

**U13 · Scan 255.** He extends the equality assertion to arrows between X and Y in Hot_W. For nonempty objects a morphism exists, leading to equivalence of their full subcategory with the point. Adding the initial empty object leaves the two possibilities distinguished by whether ∅→e is invertible.

**U14 · Scan 256.** The commentary gives four nontriviality tests for connexity. They concern exclusion of the point and Δ¹, infinitely many isomorphism classes, a preassigned map that changes components, and a pair of nonempty objects with different numbers of components. The added qualification X₀≠∅ belongs to the third test.

**U15 · Scans 256–257.** Proposition 9.9 revises 9.7 to use the fundamental localizer axioms plus closure of W under binary sums. The author says the localization then preserves binary coproducts. For a map missing a nonempty summand Y₀ and with nonempty source, he splits off ∅→Y₀. Applying Hom into Z gives a product projection followed by the map induced by f₁. The sheet ends immediately after the deduction that the projection is injective.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scans 246–247.** The crossed “je ne suis pas capable de prouver” concerns the forward implication. The live “Mais je ne vois aucun moyen de prouver la réciproque” concerns the reverse one. The question ending “en est-il de = de … ?” asks about the map over the first summand.
- **Scans 248–250.** “Mais il est assez scandaleux d’avoir à utiliser un axiome aussi fort” motivates a weaker hypothesis. The phrase “un marteau-pilon pour une mouche !” and “On aurait envie … plutôt d’ajouter … un axiome” introduce the independent decomposition formulations he tries on 250.
- **Scan 251.** “Néanmoins, je pense qu’ils sont indépendants” concerns connexity and decomposition. “Je présume qu’on n’aura jamais à travailler vraiment avec des W qui ne satisfont à ces deux axiomes !” is a separate expectation about examples. He adds “Mais apparemment il n’y en a pas besoin” so far, while connexity “pourrait jouer un rôle” in the comparison of structures near the foot.
- **Scan 254.** “S’il existait dans Cat une flèche h′ (pointillée)” asks for the extension in Cat. He obtains an extension in Hot_W and uses that weaker conclusion.
- **Scan 256.** “Je m’aperçois finalement que dans la prop. 9.7 l’hypothèse Loc (6) est inutile” introduces the revised proposition. Its new binary-sum clause is an insertion in the statement itself.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scan 257, last sentence. High confidence.** The injectivity of the first map follows from injectivity of the composite. Any pair it identified would already be identified by the composite. This checks the short inference that is actually on the sheet.
- **R2 · Scan 257, after “ce qui implique”. High confidence as a possible next inference.** Since Z(Y₁) has just been declared nonempty, an injective second projection allows Z(Y₀) at most one element. The preceding sentence also declares Z(Y₀) nonempty. A chosen object of Z gives a constant map into Z from each summand. The manuscript stops before stating what follows.
- **R3 · Scans 248–249, clause c) of 9.4. Low confidence about the intended equivalence.** The display after the cancelled corollary heading points from membership of the sum to membership of its factors. On these sheets I cannot identify a separate assertion in c) that supplies closure of W under binary sums. This is a reading question about the direction of the frozen implication and the scope of the edited clause.
- **R4 · Scan 253, definition of h and the next display. Low confidence that Grothendieck intended a correction.** The written codomain of the endpoint maps is I×X, while the written domain of h is X×I. Interchanging the factors makes the composition type-correct. The displayed order and the possible implicit interchange account for the uncertainty.
