# Hand log

The thirteen sheets contain French mathematical notes in the hand attributed to Alexander Grothendieck in the supplied archive packet. The editable text is AI-transcribed from the 13 whole-sheet images and their 39 overlapping thirds. The printed-paper and correspondent-hand distinctions do not arise on this sequence. Archive pencil numerals have their own comment on each sheet. [Montpellier archive, cote 64](https://grothendieck.umontpellier.fr/64.pdf)

## Recurring distinctions

| Habit and where it recurs | Consistency on these sheets | Readings it helps locate |
|---|---|---|
| Script D, plain D and double-struck D. Scans 15–22 | Clear in larger forms. In crossed or cramped writing, a single loop is insufficient. | The group at the opening of 15, the two-element D below, the two D’s in the first display of 16, and the subgroup labels in the triangle on 20. |
| Underlined small a and underlined omega. Scans 15–19, 23–25, 27 | Underlines are usually separate horizontal strokes. A bar can merge with the bottom of a heavily inked letter, as on 24. | The excluded involution on 15–16, the antipode on 17, the orientation sets on 19 and 24, and the field exponent on 27. |
| Large indexed initial of the permutation group. Scans 20–24, 27 | Its lower return resembles G. The small G which is sometimes added beside it is distinct. The lower curve varies. | The indexed four-letter group at 20–24 and the automorphisms of the three-element set on 27. The open alphabet question is tabulated by page. |
| Star, vee, wave and prime. Scans 15–21, 24, 27 | Their height and the presence of crossing strokes are fairly reliable outside cancellations. | Checked W on 15, the prime on x on 17, the wave above V on 21, and the vee dual and orientation star on 27. |
| Capital I and J, lowercase i and j. Scans 17–27 | The capital I commonly has two serifs. J hooks left. Lowercase i has a dot. At the foot of 27, the j descender remains below a retouched letter. | The index sets for the cube and for the product on 24–26, the projections and the two basis indices on 27. |
| Arrowheads, hooks, bare shafts. Scans 15–20, 23–27 | The end of a horizontal can be a small ink bead. A hook at its left determines only that endpoint. | Outside arrows on 15, map from S to D on 19, diagrams (2) and (8) on 26, final homomorphism on 27. |
| Numbers and narrow lower indices. Scans 17–27 | A 9 can have a very small or open top loop. The feet of 2 and 3 usually turn in different directions, with exceptions at the bottom of the triangle on 20. | The folio 9 on 19, the slanted 3 and diagonal label on 20, the two different 9s in the count on 22. |
| Terminal horizontals and abbreviations. Scans 15–27 | A continuous stroke can represent more than one letter. Dotted and undotted abbreviations coexist. Neither the full word nor the plural follows automatically. | The adverb in the first two lines on 15, classe(s) in e) on 21, isomorphisme(s) on 22, and comb. after cube on 23. |
| Line-end cancellations and insertion leaders. Scans 16–18, 21, 23–27 | A line beneath an insertion may be its leader. Cancellation sometimes continues onto the next baseline. | On 17 the right-hand end transl- and the barred continuation below sans belong together. On 23 the hook from comb. returns to cube. |
| Connected Greek letters. Scans 21–27 | Tau and its prime separate in products, rho has a descending stem, and sigma often has an upper tick. The two shapes in (7) on 26 still differ. | The paired transpositions on 21 and 23, the sum of a tuple on 26 and the reflections and two order-three maps on 27. |

## Scan 15

The left loop in the dihedral-group D is visible at the head, in Aut and under the strike at the foot. The upright of ordinary D in the pair C, D has a different entry stroke. A small squiggle can represent a whole ending in autom. Some occurrences end in a detached point. Each termination has to be inspected separately. Marginal rg is particularly compressed. The crossed t in tant, sont, respectent meets the body of the word without cancelling it.

## Scan 16

The curved leaders before and after F₂² run to the words le quotient and de 𝔻₄. Follow them to the baseline. The abbreviated lui-même here uses two parallel lines. In the lower paragraph several rules cover small words, while the rule under Remarque stops before the next sentence. The short groups of dots in cet ordre and at the foot are intentional.

## Scan 17

The sheet number 8 is in the ink of the text. The other numeral is thin and low in the left corner. In the large oblique NB the words ascend toward the right. Rotating it separates its lines from both the presentation and the diagonal edge of the sheet. Long joins form the middle of antipodisme and groupoïdes. A rightward stroke beside a is distinct from the line immediately under the letter.

## Scan 18

The left oblique paragraph crosses the height of two displays. Turning it brings the subscript and prime after its second A into view. 'de rg' and 'sur' are short waves in both occurrences at the top. In the last display the descender belongs to P. A thin entry stroke immediately before 'a quatre éléments' belongs to its initial A, close to the slanted note.

## Scan 19

His 9 at the head is open at the top with a long descending finish. The capital D at the right of the map from S has a different stroke from the d under the product. The small trailing strokes of sections and torseurs can flatten to a single line. The curved mark just above the word torseurs carries the inserted F₂-.

## Scan 20

The diagram spreads sentences about the three correspondences on both sides of the oblique arrows. The ends of the two diagonals have small points, partly lost against the oblique strokes. The central letter V has a separate high star. The script P in the line above has a long downstroke and two low comma-separated indices. The upper line is the top of the frame around the identities.

## Scan 21

The long t-bars in transpositions and contenus join their middles. The margin has two columns, type at far left and cardinal at inner left. The small addition before ss-groupe is a 2 with a hyphen. In e), the final curved stroke after classe could be an s or the exit from e. The change from six to trois has its own underline under the cancelled word.

## Scan 22

The joined strokes after h's dihedral group are cut by a horizontal stroke. The last note shares its first line with the end of the main block and is read down the lower right. Numbering g, h, i continues across the sheet.

## Scan 23

The elaborate lower paragraph has separate corrections over sommets and arêtes. Beneath the heading the first J can look like a backwards bracket. In the diagonal bottom-left sentence the successive short waves are distinguished by their separate baselines.

## Scan 24

In the top corrections, the tiny numerals above the line have their own insertion strokes. In the pen glosses beneath the displays the curved horizontal brackets bind explanations to particular factors. The square name Q has a curving exit crossing its bowl. The dark translation subscript has a separate dot.

## Scan 25

The wedge formula in the top line repeats the lower formula of scan 24. A slanting note low on the left has seven short lines, the final one crossed out entirely. The shortened words comb. and dist. recur in it. The small isolated pen group below the prose has an initial with an upper curl.

## Scan 26

The letter i in formula indices is normally a short upright with a free dot, obscured only where the parenthesis in (7) extends down to it. Exact-sequence strokes in (2) and (8) sometimes end in a short upturn. Only the clearly formed heads are typeset as arrows. The dotted arrows of (2) have actual full heads. Bottom-right P has the subscript M.

## Scan 27

The large initial of the permutation-group symbol has an internal return, also visible with a superscript plus beneath (14). The tightly set slanted (11) collides with the label (10) but the numbered lines have independent directions. The top-right number 13 is encircled. The lower-right note rises diagonally to the inverse formula.
