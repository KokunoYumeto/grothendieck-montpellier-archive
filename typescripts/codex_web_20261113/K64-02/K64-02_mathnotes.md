# Mathematical notes on cote 64, scans 15–27

## What the pages do

**The abstract group of a square, scan 15.** Grothendieck describes the dihedral group $\mathcal D$ as an extension of its abelianization $W$ by $\mathbb F_2$. One nonzero class is distinguished by its lifts of order four. The other two can be exchanged. The sequence for $\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal D$ and its two captions describe this action and its kernel. Remembering the square distinguishes the two classes associated with $C$ and $D$.

**Recovering a square, scans 15–17.** The loss of the central involution under conjugation explains the qualification “modulo antipodisme”. Scan 16 compares the torsor describing the group with the torsor describing a square. Scan 17 starts in the other direction. The four noncentral involutions form a square whose antipodal pairing uses the unique nonidentity central element. The author gives a presentation in terms of its vertices and a commutator. The slanted NB identifies the vertices of the square obtained on starting with $\operatorname{Aut}Q$.

**Vertices, edges and two-element fibres, scans 17–19.** The vertices form a torsor $S$ under a two-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-space. Choosing the antipodal translation singles out one nonzero vector. The other two form a basis indexed by a two-element set $C$. The fibres of $A\to C$ then supply a description of the vertices as sections. The dual construction on scan 19 exchanges the roles of edges and vertices. The embeddings into sets of two-element subsets describe incidence.

**Five identities for a square, scans 19–20.** The “Formulaire” puts $C$, $D$ and the orientation set side by side. It collects the two antipodal quotients, the two products of fibres and the corresponding wedge products. The line below (5) derives one of the latter relations using (2).

**Subgroups of the four-letter permutation group, scans 20–22.** The triangular diagram relates the three nonzero elements of the normal Klein four-group, the cyclic order-four subgroups, and the Sylow 2-subgroups. Items a–f list the subgroups of orders 2, 4 and 8. Items g–i treat orders 3, 6 and 12. The margin gives the indexing sets and orbit sizes. The argument then passes from conjugacy classes of subgroups to isomorphism classes of transitive $G$-sets.

**Two double covers, scan 23.** Let $J$ be the three partitions of the four-element set $I$ into two pairs. Squaring takes a four-cycle to its double transposition. Another map sends a two-element subset of $I$ to the partition consisting of it and its complement. The two six-element $G$-sets have different stabilizers. The first cover defines a cube with the four-cycles as faces, partial sections as edges and sections as vertices. Extension of sections describes incidence.

**Orientation and homogeneous spaces, scans 24–25.** The author asks for the explicit correspondences between vertices and order-three elements, pairs of opposite edges and reflections, and faces and order-four elements. He writes the orientation in terms of the two orientations of each square indexed by $J$. The inclusion of $I$ in a product of two-element sets gives a reduction of the group acting on that product. Scan 25 interprets the ten classes of transitive $G$-sets other than the one-point set using cells of the cube, antipodal pairs, frames and related configurations.

**Modules over $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$, scan 26.** For a free module $M$ of rank two, $M_0=M/2M$ is two-dimensional over $\mathbb F_2$. Its three nonzero elements index the fibres $M_i$. Their product is a torsor under $M_0^J$. Addition on $M$ singles out a torsor $P_M$ under the kernel $V_J(M_0)$ of the sum map. The manuscript labels run from (4) to (6).

**The endomorphisms of $M_0$, scan 27.** The author returns from $P_M$ to the individual fibres by projections. Evaluation on the three nonzero vectors identifies endomorphisms with triples of sum zero. The two subsequent diagrams use the three reflections and the two order-three automorphisms to describe two subspaces of $\operatorname{End}(M_0)$. The last lines give an identity for the inverse pair and a four-element basis.

## Statements left without proof

- **Scans 15–16.** “on a en fait un iso. can.” introduces the isomorphism $\check W\simeq W$ in the margin. The remark “Le groupe $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb D_4)$ est isom. à $\mathbb D_4$” gives the abstract isomorphism, while the surrounding text distinguishes the inner automorphisms. See D16 below.
- **Scan 17.** “est une équivalence de groupoïdes, car on reconstitue” introduces the reconstruction formula. The page gives generators, the displayed commutator relation and the construction on objects. Its treatment of morphisms is implicit.
- **Scans 18–19.** “qui donne en même temps un plongement” and “Dualement” introduce the two incidence embeddings. The section formulas describe the construction. The incidence equivalences receive no separate proof.
- **Scan 20.** “il a un unique él. d'ordre 2” and the surrounding triangle give the correspondences for the cyclic and Sylow subgroups. The centralizer clause requires the qualification S20 below.

- **Scan 26.** “le torseur $M_J$ sous $M_0^J$ provient canoniquement d'un torseur sous $V_J(M_0)$” follows the sum map and exact sequence. The construction is indicated through the fibre product and addition. See M26 for the finite model.

## Slips

- **S20, scan 20, horizontal arrow in the triangle.** The caption gives $\mathcal D^+$ as “le centralisateur” immediately after the unique involution of its cyclic subgroup. If the involution is the object of the centralizer, the centralizer is $\mathcal D$ (S20r). The centralizer of the square of a four-cycle has eight elements. The cyclic $\mathcal D^+$ has four and centralizes its generator. The left-hand caption on the same sheet also names $\mathcal D$ as the centralizer of $x$. Confidence is high for this conditional correction.
- **S21, scan 21, item d.** In “il y a d'abord $V^*$” the intended subgroup is $V$ (S21r). The preceding sheet identifies $V^*$ with the three partitions of type $(2,2)$. Item d requires a group of order four, and the nearby addition in e explicitly names $V$. The star is visible on the crop. Confidence is high.
- **C24, scan 24, caption below the lone wedge.** The caption reads “torseur sous $\mathbb F_2^J$”. This group acts on the product of the three two-point fibres at the foot. Their contracted product has the two parity classes and is a torsor under $\mathbb F_2$. If the caption qualifies the wedge immediately above it, the corresponding structural group should be $\mathbb F_2$. Confidence is low about the attribution of a slip because the caption starts with a cancellation and lies above the product diagram.
- **M27, scan 27, (9).** The subscript of the wedge is $M_0^J$, while the line above gives $P_M$ the action of $V_J(M_0)$. For the contracted product of that torsor with $M_0$, one would restrict $\operatorname{pr}_i$ to $V_J(M_0)$ and use the latter as subscript. Alternatively one could first extend the structural group of $P_M$. Confidence is medium because an extension of structure group could be implicit.
- **Scans 15–16, wording.** The right-hand gloss on scan 15 repeats “les les deux”. The ordinary phrase would have one article. On scan 16 there is a raised acute mark in “réconstruire”, conventionally “reconstruire”. Both forms are visible in the handwriting.

## Remarks of the model

- **D16 · High confidence.** Enumeration of the eight symmetries of a square gives eight automorphisms of their multiplication table, four inner. The automorphism group contains an order-four generator and an involution inverting it that together generate all eight. This checks the distinction used in the remark on scan 16.
- **S22 · High confidence in the count.** Enumeration in $S_4$ gives $9,7,3$ subgroups of orders 2, 4, 8, and $4,4,1$ of orders 3, 6, 12. Thus there are 19 and 9 in the two portions of the list, 28 proper nontrivial subgroups and 30 in all. The six and three conjugacy classes give eleven after adjoining both extremes. **Reading question, scan 22, line beginning “Il y a donc”.** The independently frozen numeral is “19”. Its second digit has a very small, almost open loop, which can also look like “17”. The next 9 on the sheet has a clearer loop. Agreement with the count leaves the graphic question separate.
- **C23 · High confidence.** The six four-cycles have three squares with two preimages each. A four-cycle has cyclic centralizer of order four. The permutations preserving one two-element subset form a four-element group with three involutions. These stabilizers distinguish the two $G$-sets. For the three two-point fibres of the first cover, there are eight full sections and twelve sections over a two-element subset.
- **M26 · High confidence in the example.** In $M=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2$, each of the three fibres has four elements. Among the $4^3$ triples of lifts, exactly sixteen have sum zero. This gives the size of the reduced torsor in the example.
- **E27 · High confidence.** Among the six invertible binary $2\times2$ matrices, the three reflections sum to zero. The two elements of order three sum to the identity. Every pair of distinct reflections together with both order-three elements spans all sixteen matrices. This checks the independence assertion including all three choices of a reflection pair.
- **Reading question, scan 19, after the first product · Low confidence.** The frozen text has “la donnée de cette famille équivalent à la donnée”. A finite verb “équivaut” fits the singular subject. The low joined strokes before the t are insufficient to attribute a grammatical slip to the author.
- **Reading question, scan 20, bottom vertex · Low confidence.** The frozen order is $3\mid2$. The numeral turns to the left at the foot. The correspondence drawn above would use the 2-Sylow subgroups. The ink and the mathematical interpretation point in different directions.
- **Reading question, scans 24–25 · Low confidence.** Scan 24 uses $\operatorname{Aut}^+(\mathrm{Cube})$, while the main paragraph of scan 25 has $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{Cube})$. The latter could refer to the cube with its orientation understood. The two spellings alone do not settle whether a change of convention was intended.
- **Reading question, scan 26, (7) · Low confidence.** The first coordinate has a pronounced zigzag with an overwritten lower stroke, favouring $\xi_i$. The summand has a single descending curve, favouring $\zeta_i$. The zero-sum formula would normally repeat one variable.
- **Reading question, scan 27, closing line · Low confidence.** The frozen first reading $x_i,x_j$ and the alternative $\sigma_i,\sigma_j$ concern a partly overwritten pair. The statement about a basis of $M_0$ favours the x’s. The finite calculation above concerns the four endomorphisms in the preceding clause and cannot decide how this pair was written.
