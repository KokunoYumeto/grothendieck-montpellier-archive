# Mathematical notes · Serre, scans 003–016

## What the pages do

**The question of the paper · 003–004.** Serre starts with the Tate module $T$ of a $p$-divisible group over the valuation ring of a local field. Tate’s decomposition of $T\otimes C$ gives information about the Galois image. Serre announces an algebraic subgroup that the envelope of this image must contain. In the one-parameter formal-group case he announces conditions under which the image is open.

**Compact linear groups and semisimplicity · 004–005, proposition 1.** A compact subgroup $G$ of $\operatorname{Aut}(V)$ has a $p$-adic Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$. Proposition 1 relates semisimplicity for $G$ and for $\mathfrak g$ to reductivity of $\mathfrak g$ and semisimplicity of its centre on $V$. The proof passes to a small open normal subgroup $G_1$ and then uses the finite quotient $G/G_1$.

**The algebraic envelope · 005–006, definition and proposition 2.** $G_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is the smallest algebraic subgroup of $\mathbf{GL}_V$ whose points contain $G$. Its defining ideal consists of the functions that vanish on $G$. Under proposition 1’s hypotheses, its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak g_{\mathrm{alg}}$, the group is reductive, and $G$ meets every connected component. In the proof, finitely many translates of the connected group $H^0$ account for the components.

**The open-subgroup corollary · 006.** When the centre $\mathfrak c$ is algebraic, Serre obtains $\operatorname{Lie}(G_{\mathrm{alg}})=\mathfrak g$ and concludes that $G$ is open in $G_{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathbf Q_p)$.

**Invariant tensors · 006–007, proposition 3 and lemma 1.** The tensor spaces $T_{r,s}(V)$ use $r$ copies of $V$ and $s$ of its dual. Proposition 3 recognizes points of the envelope by their action on all the fixed tensors. Lemma 1 gives the field-valued version for a reductive group $H$. In the sketch, a polynomial on $\operatorname{End}(V)$ separates $H$ from $Hg$, with values 0 and 1 respectively.

**The cyclotomic character and the weight spaces · 007–008, § 2.** The action on $p$-power roots of unity defines $\chi$. For a semilinear action on a $C$-space $X$, the vectors on which $s$ acts by $\chi(s)^i$ form the $K$-space $X^i$. Extending scalars yields $X(i)$ and a map $\varepsilon_i:X(i)\to X$.

**Injectivity and Hodge–Tate type · 008, proposition 4 and remark.** The sum of the $\varepsilon_i$ is injective. Serre recalls Tate’s argument with a shortest linear relation, normalizing one coefficient to 1 and comparing its Galois transforms. He calls $X$ of Hodge–Tate type when the sum fills $X$. Duality reverses the weight, and tensor products preserve the type.

**The group attached to the weights · 009, § 3 and theorems 1–2.** Assume that $V$ is semisimple under $G$ and $V_C$ has Hodge–Tate type. The map $\varphi$ acts by $\lambda^i$ on $V_C(i)$. Theorem 1 places its image $\Phi$ inside $G_{\mathrm{alg}}(C)$ by testing invariant tensors, which have weight zero. The smallest algebraic subgroup defined over $\mathbf Q_p$ whose $C$-points contain $\Phi$ is $M$. Theorem 2 states $M\subset G_{\mathrm{alg}}$.

**Connectedness, the differential and inertia · 010, remarks 1–3.** Serre places $M$ in the neutral component, gives the Lie-algebra consequence for $d\varphi$, and applies theorem 2 over the completion of the maximal unramified extension. He obtains $M\subset I_{\mathrm{alg}}$. An unsigned handwritten note alongside gives $M=I_{\mathrm{alg}}$ and names Sen.

**A two-weight case · 010, § 4, theorem 3 and its corollaries.** Suppose $V$ is absolutely simple for $\mathfrak g$, the weights are 0 and 1, and both multiplicities are positive and coprime. Then $G_{\mathrm{alg}}=\mathbf{GL}_V$. The equivalent Lie-algebra and topological conclusions are $\mathfrak g=\operatorname{End}(V)$ and openness of $G$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(V)$.

**Reduction to a connected algebraic group · 010–011, proposition 5.** Set $E=V_C$, $E_0=V_C(0)$, $E_1=V_C(1)$. Serre isolates three properties of the connected group $H$ on $E$: reductivity with scalar commutant, containment of the indicated homotheties on $E_1$ fixing $E_0$, and coprime dimensions. Proposition 5 concludes $H=\mathbf{GL}_E$.

**The centre and a one-dimensional torus · 011, proof steps 1–2.** Writing $H=T\cdot S$, Serre obtains the full scalar torus $T$. He constructs a one-dimensional group $\Psi$ inside the derived group $S$. **B1.** Its displayed weights are $n_1$ on the $n_0$-dimensional space and $-n_0$ on the $n_1$-dimensional space.

**Simplicity and the centre of $S$ · 011–012, steps 3–4.** A hypothetical product decomposition gives a factorization of a binomial character. Positivity of the coefficients and coprimality force a one-dimensional factor, contradicting the faithful action described in the proof. For $h=n_0+n_1$, the group of $h$th roots of unity is then seen among the scalar elements of $S$.

**The classification argument · 012–013, step 5 and lemma 2.** Lemma 2 identifies the image of a nontrivial $h$-dimensional representation of a simple group whose centre has order divisible by $h$. Serre treats type $\mathbf A_n$ first. The bounds for the other centres reduce the remaining cases to small dimensions; $\mathbf E_6$ and $\mathbf D_n$ are treated on scan 013. This completes proposition 5 and theorem 3.

**The two dimensions of a $p$-divisible group · 013, § 5 and proposition 6.** For $F$ of height $h$, its Tate module gives $V=T\otimes\mathbf Q_p$ of dimension $h$. Tate’s decomposition has weights 0 and 1. Their multiplicities are described in terms of the neutral components of $F$ and its dual $F'$: weight 1 belongs to $F$, weight 0 to the dual.

**Endomorphisms and commutants · 013–014, proposition 7.** $\operatorname{End}(F)$ is the union of the endomorphism rings over finite extensions. The integral commutant on $T$ uses $\operatorname{End}_A(F)$; the commutant of $G$ on $V$ tensors this ring with $\mathbf Q_p$. For $\mathfrak g$ on $V$, the corresponding formula tensors the larger ring $\operatorname{End}(F)$.

**The openness criterion for $F$ · 014, theorem 4 and remark.** Serre applies theorem 3 assuming semisimplicity, $\operatorname{End}(F)=\mathbf Z_p$, and positive coprime dimensions for $F$ and its dual. He obtains the full envelope and Lie algebra and an open Galois image. He also deduces connectedness for $F$ and its dual by considering $V(F^0)$.

**A one-dimensional formal group and its torsion · 014–015, proposition 8 and lemma 3.** Proposition 8 asserts $\mathfrak g\cdot z=V$ for every nonzero $z\in V$, hence simplicity. Serre writes the power series for multiplication by $p$, describes the kernels of its iterates and singles out $T'_n$, the elements of exact order $p^n$. Lemma 3 bounds the ramification index of $K(x)/K$ below by $c\,p^{nh}$ for $x\in T'_n$.

**From ramification to an open orbit · 015, proof of proposition 8.** Using lemma 3 provisionally, Serre obtains a lower bound on the sizes of the finite images of $G\cdot z$. Its normalized Haar measure is positive. Its analytic orbit is open, so its tangent space gives all of $V$.

**The valuation estimate · 015–016, proof of lemma 3.** Serre compares the valuation of $f(x)$ with an increasing function $\varphi$, then iterates its inverse $\psi$. This bounds the valuations of points of exact order $p^n$. **B2.** The two final bounds are $v(x)\leq c_3/p^{nh}$ and $v(x)\geq c_4/e$, where $e$ is the ramification index. Their comparison gives the asserted estimate.

**The final theorem and two remarks · 016.** **B3.** In dimension one the multiplicities are $n_1=1$ and $n_0=h-1$. Theorem 5 concludes that the image is open when $\operatorname{End}(F)=\mathbf Z_p$. Remark 1 describes $\mathfrak g$ as the commutant of $\operatorname{End}(F)$ more generally. A handwritten reference to Sen adjoins it. Remark 2 applies the results to inertia after base extension. Its final sentence continues beyond the supplied scans.
