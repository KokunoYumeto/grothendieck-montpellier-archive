# Cote 27 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Proposition 1.3 and its proof, scans 3–4.** With the inequality (2) and the geometric irreducibility assumptions in place, the proposition relates conditions on the stabilizer letters and on the map from X to V. It distinguishes a property at the marked point from a generic property. Quasi-finiteness, being unramified, being étale, and smoothness occur in different conditions. The displayed chain and the two qualifications below it give the implications. The proof uses the earlier dimension estimate and a normality hypothesis for a further implication.

**Corollaire 1.4, scans 4–5.** Under the displayed equality of stabilizer letters and normality of V at a, the corollary asserts smoothness for the two maps at their respective points and for the stabilizer at e. The proof compares a rank and several dimensions through a chain of inequalities. It then uses the identification of the fibre at a with the stabilizer and invokes openness and smoothness results. The author says the corollary will not be needed in the sequel.

**Corollaire 1.5, scans 5–6.** The conditions are now stated independently of an initial choice of a in W(k). Some assert existence of such a point. The main implication chain singles out what follows over k and the final clause makes the conditions equivalent when k is algebraically closed. The proof passes to the algebraic closure and chooses a point where the map is unramified and its image regular.

**Corollaire 1.6, scans 6–7.** A criterion for birationality adds uniqueness of the conjugate of W through suitable points on an open subset. For a fixed point the author compares uniqueness, finite nonempty incidence, and the existence of an isolated point in the scheme of conjugates. Under a reducedness hypothesis he adds membership in the isomorphism locus as condition (iv). The proof invokes the Main Theorem twice and uses the map from X to G/N in discussing reducedness.

**Remarque 1.7, scan 7.** The author introduces a W-regular point and gives an inverse-image criterion beyond the algebraically closed case. He then treats a generically étale map, its degree of projection d, and the open locus on which it is finite and étale. The final parenthesis breaks off after “rationnel”.

**Plan of §6 and cancelled 6.4, scan 9.** The section title brings together the maximal central torus and a torus quotient. The list proposes a definition, existence questions and a case for algebraic groups. A cancelled statement over a perfect field introduces a finite subgroup of the maximal central torus R and a product decomposition involving a further group H. It breaks off at “H est tel que”.

**Th. 6.4, scans 9–10.** A separate statement with the same number concerns a pointed map from the product of two geometrically integral spaces to the multiplicative group. Its displayed factorization separates the two variables. Scan 10 puts this in terms of two restrictions being identically 1 and records reductions to the affine case and to dimension one. The name “Rosenlicht” stands in pencil beside the theorem.

**Corollary drafts on maps and characters, scan 10.** The notes contemplate replacing the multiplicative group in the preceding statement. One corollary says that a morphism from a smooth connected group that takes the identity to the identity is a group homomorphism. The lower draft asserts finite generation for the group of characters over k. Several versions of the heading and conclusion have been cancelled.

**Proposition on a quotient of multiplicative type, scan 11.** Part a gives a map from G to H through which maps to groups of multiplicative type factor uniquely. Part b concerns extension of the base when k is perfect. Part c identifies H as a torus when G is connected and smooth. The adjacent diagrams distinguish the groups, their reduced components and their quotients. The note beginning “Si k est imparfait” ends before it specifies the final object of comparison.

**Extensions by a torus, scan 11.** A separate proposition concerns an extension by R which splits as a principal homogeneous bundle. The last line asserts that it splits. A crossed cohomology expression, a sequence involving the two C-terms and a Hom-term, and a short argument in the right margin accompany it. The margin traces the extension back to one of H by R.

**Corollaire 5.8, scan 12.** On a normal, noetherian, irreducible base, the author considers an open subset with complement of codimension at least two. He asserts that a subgroup of multiplicative type over the open has a unique extension of the same type.

**Corollaires 5.9 and 5.10, scan 12.** The first gives a criterion for extending a subgroup of the generic fibre by inspecting a reduced component of its closure over points whose local ring has dimension one. “rédiger mieux” stands beside this condition. The cancelled 5.10 returns to the torus case and the residue characteristics. The words “On utilise un / Lemme …” remain below.

**Local corollary and the prime-set variant, scan 13.** A family H(n) of subgroups of multiplicative type and its behaviour at the closed point are intended to determine a unique subgroup H. The low indices matter to the stated compatibility. The remark considers restricting n by its prime divisors and invokes a density theorem. The bracketed reference ends with an ellipsis.

**Picard groups over an algebraically closed field, scan 14.** The first proposition states finiteness, then vanishing when the reduced group is solvable. For smooth connected G it writes Pic(G) as a quotient of Pic(G/B) by the image of the characteristic map from D(T). The diagrams put this beside the quotients by R, examples involving GP(n) and Gl(n), and a square of Picard groups and character groups. The semisimple case relates vanishing to simple connectedness.

**An annihilator over a field, scan 14.** A second proposition gives a positive integer annihilating Pic(G) and links it to the degree of a suitable field extension. The further characteristic-power assertion is inside a cancellation. “On … les précisions suivantes” points forward at the foot of the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scan 5, Cor. 1.4 · high.** The inference from equality of the two extreme dimension terms to equality of every intermediate term is justified by the displayed order inequalities. This particular inference was checked separately.
- **R2 · Scans 3–6, reading question · low.** The frozen transcription uses N and M for the two handwritten multi-peak letters. Lowercase fraktur n and m are another possible reading. In (i) the angular two-peak sign sits on the same line as a plainly typed capital N in “N est lisse”; the pen form has the shape of joined minims. Similarly (vi) has typed M with a subscript. The choice of alphabet could change an equality of groups into one of associated objects. The order reversal on scan 5 is visible in either alphabet.
- **R3 · Scan 3, line after (3), reading question · low.** The frozen insertion after “normal” is “on a”. Its first two pen strokes could instead read “en a”. The latter would locate the normality condition at the point, as on scan 4. The cancelled type below the insertion is unreadable.
- **R4 · Scan 12, rewritten generic subgroup · low.** A bar spans several overlapping vertical strokes. A single barred H and a T written over H are different readings of the same area. The disputed layer affects which subgroup is referred to in the criterion.
