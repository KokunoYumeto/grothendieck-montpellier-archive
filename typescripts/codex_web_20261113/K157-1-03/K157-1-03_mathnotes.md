# Mathematical notes on scans 34–49

The scan numbers are those of the archive PDF. Line references in the slip and reading entries are to the frozen page fragments. The stages of a cancelled argument are reported with their cancellation status.

## What the pages do

**34. Base change and full faithfulness.** Dér 4 asks for an isomorphism of base change for direct images. Its dual uses the square labelled $(1')$ with $j_0\backslash I$ above $e$. Dér 5 relates the contractibility of the slices at $j_0$ to full faithfulness of $f^*$. The two adjunction maps are written in opposite directions. The concluding NB calls the corresponding direct-image functors localization functors.

**35. A cancelled test in Hot.** The first argument passes from a comparison of $\operatorname{hom}(e,F)$ and $\operatorname{hom}(X,F)$ to one of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hot}}$ by taking $\pi_0$. It ends with “$X$ contractile, OK.” The diagonals also cross the following reservation about the general case.

**35. Restarting the definitions.** After recording the removal of manuscript pages 33–40, Grothendieck defines a prédérivateur as a 2-functor from $\mathrm{Diag}^{\circ}$ to $(\mathrm{Cat})$. He specifies finite ordered sets, finite limits and sums, and closure under the complementary full subcategory in the open/closed case. The sloping addition distinguishes indexing categories from coefficients.

**36–37. Inducing a prederivator.** A category with a class of arrows gives the construction written $\mathbb D_{\mathcal M,\Sigma}$. The notes place it in a 2-functor from PREMOD to PREDER. The induced prederivator is given by $\mathbb D_I(J)=\mathbb D(I\times J)$. For prederivators supplied by a pair, the notes identify the new pair using $\mathcal M(I)$ and $\Sigma(I)$, the latter read fibre by fibre.

**37. Duality and localization axioms.** The dual uses both an opposite index category and an opposite value category. Der 1 sends a binary sum of indexing categories to a product up to equivalence and assigns a final category to the empty category. A corollary gives the finite-family version. Der 2 asks that the functor to diagrams in the fundamental category $\mathbb D(e)$ be conservative.

**38. Relative cohomology and homology.** Der 3(g) requires $f^*$ to have a right adjoint $f_*$. Its value on a coefficient is called relative cohomology. A corollary concerns finite products and their preservation. Der 3(d) introduces the left adjoint $f_!$ and the corresponding relative homology. The foot explains the labels (g) and (d).

**39. Complementary immersions.** In the diagram $U\to X\leftarrow Y$, the closed inclusion is labelled $i$ and the open inclusion $j$. The first statement identifies a counit, full faithfulness of $i_*$ and the essential image, detected by a final-object condition on the open part. The dual formulation uses $j_!$ and an initial-object condition on the closed part. The author questions the need to make this a separate axiom.

**40. Computing a direct image at an object.** The square over $y\in Y$ compares the value of $f_*F$ at $y$ with sections over $X_{/y}$. The lower half is a cancelled draft about a constant coefficient on a contractile category. It extends into a crossed corollary about $F\to f_*f^*F$.

**41. Cofinality and sections.** Dér 5(g) gives a restriction map on $\Gamma_{\mathbb D}$ for a map $\alpha:X'\to X$ satisfying a comma-category condition. A marginal NB identifies sections with direct image along the map to $e$. A further extension of the cofinality statement is struck and marked “Faux !”.

**41. A proposed stronger condition.** The Remarque turns to $\alpha_!(\varepsilon_{X'})\to\varepsilon_X$ in HOT. It calls the resulting condition homotopical triviality over $X$ and gives a dual condition for cohomotopical triviality. Grothendieck asks how these conditions relate under duality.

**41–42. Constant coefficients and an attempted corollary.** Cor. 1 treats the unit for a constant coefficient on a contractile category. Cor. 2, in a more general form, is crossed. Scan 42 tries to evaluate its comparison pointwise, using the triangle through $\Gamma(Y_{/y_0},F)$, the final-object map and an identification of a further slice with $X_{/y_1}$. The marginal “qui est fausse” is attached to the second part invoked at the end.

**43. A pointwise base-change criterion.** Following a cancelled triangle over $S$, the live square has a two-cell relating $fq$ and $pf'$. The proposed criterion is expressed through the functors $X'_{/y'}\to X_{/p(y')}$. The notes describe a category of triples by an explicit commutative square. An annotation rejects the strict version and another reverses an arrow for the costrict case.

**44. A fully faithful functor.** Cor. 5(g) concerns the counit $f^*f_*F\to F$. The argument tests on fibres at $x$, with $y=f(x)$, and compares $X_{/y}$ with $X_{/x}$. A cancelled line follows that identification.

**44–45. A final object and evaluation.** Cor. 3 concerns $f:Z\to e$ and the map $p:e\to Z$ selecting a final object. The comparison is $f_*F\to p^*F$. A crossed alternative hypothesis involved the action of a limit on epimorphisms. The surviving formulation is placed in a triangle and then in a base-change square continued on scan 45.

**45–46. Recovering the description of the essential image.** The open immersion supplies a base-change square with empty upper-left corner. This identifies the restriction of an object extended from the closed part with a final object. In the converse direction the unit $F\to i_*i^*F$ is tested after applying $i^*$ and $j^*$.

**46. A cancelled comparison axiom and question.** The crossed Dér 6 gives a restriction comparison for cohomology along a costrict equivalence. The subsequent question asks which $f$ give this comparison for all coefficients and all left derivators under consideration, or even for the named examples. The closing lines object to a possible characterization by costrictness.

**47–48. The fibre criterion for proper and smooth functors.** Starting with the fibre inclusion $X_{y_0}\to X_{/y_0}$, Grothendieck compares a coinduced category with the fibre at $u_0$ of the functor between categories of specializations. This gives a criterion for the fibre inclusions to be costrict equivalences. Scan 48 calls the resulting property homotopical properness and gives a dual presentation for smoothness using categories of generalizations.

**48. Closure assertions and examples.** A proposition asserts stability under change of base and composition. A longer cancelled clause attempts to deduce properness of one factor from that of the composite and the other factor. Interlinear remarks discuss closed immersions and images, and the NB treats the map to $e$ and projections using categories with an initial or final object.

**49. Change of base and iterated localization.** A cartesian square induces a second square of localized categories. Its fibres are compared with those before changing base. The lower argument identifies an iterated localization with a localization at the source of an arrow. After a correction from *propre* to *lisse*, Grothendieck states a proposition relating contractible fibres and hot-equivalence.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scans 34–35.** The side-note beside Dér 5 begins “Totalement canulé”. The next sheet has “Mais le cas général … reste douteux …” inside the cancellation. The intervening quoted noun on scan 35 is uncertain.
- **Scan 37.** “il faudra vérifier qu'ils sont stables” and “comment ils se comportent par dualité” set a test for the candidate axioms.
- **Scan 39.** “c'est prématuré ici” postpones the interpretation of the two earlier squares.
- **Scan 41.** “On devrait pouvoir renforcer considérablement cet axiome” proposes the terminal-object condition. “Cette condition n'est sans doute pas autoduale” records his reservation.
- **Scans 41–43.** The annotations “Faux !”, “qui est fausse” and “Faux pour … stricte” concern different lines. The first two bear on the proposed cofinality extension and its use. The third qualifies a base-change criterion.
- **Scan 44.** “plus généralement …” introduces the crossed alternative involving preservation of epimorphisms. The line “Va résulter de Der 6 (g)” points to a different way of treating the surviving statement.
- **Scan 46.** “On peut se demander de caractériser les” introduces the question about $f$. The parenthesis names $\mathrm{Der}(\mathrm{Ab})$ and $\mathrm{HOT}$. “Il n'est pas vrai” rejects the proposed implication to costrictness within the cancelled discussion.
- **Scan 48.** “Attention, une équiv. de cat. n'est pas propre !” is a correction beside the crossed closure argument. The added statements about images and inclusions have no further justification on this sheet.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1. Model remark, high confidence.** The destination problem in S4 can be checked without deciding the strict/costrict question. In the upper square on scan 43, $f$ and $p$ both end in $Y$. At the four corners of $(*)$ the outermost functor is respectively $f,p,f,p$.
- **M2. Model remark, high confidence.** The substitution written at the foot of scan 44 reproduces the stated target of the final-object comparison. With $Z'=S=e$ and $f'=\mathrm{id}_e$, the expression $f'_*(p^*F)$ reduces to $p^*F$. This checks the specialization of the formula. The isomorphism assertion is the manuscript's.
- **M3. Model remark, high confidence about the annotation.** When using the corollaries on scans 41–42, the qualification about constant coefficients belongs to crossed Cor. 2. The live Cor. 1 has $M\in\mathbb D(e)$ in its own hypotheses. Their visually similar unit maps have different stated coefficient data.
- **M4. Model remark, low confidence about scope.** The parenthesis “on dirait lisse” on scan 49 could extend the last proposition to smooth functors. Its placement supports that interpretation. The immediately preceding correction from *propre* to *lisse* belongs to a different sentence.
