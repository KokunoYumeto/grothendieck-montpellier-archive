# Mathematical notes · K161-6-01

## What the pages do

**A moving frame on the sphere (scan 4).** A unit vector u, a unit tangent vector v and the chosen orientation define w=u∧v. The notes express a point at angular distance α along the great circle and a second tangent direction turned through θ in this frame.

**The triangle (scans 4–5, E05).** Two chord-length calculations give the condition for an equilateral triangle. The page gives the half-angle equation, a formula for cos α in terms of cos θ and, in the margin, the condition on θ for a solution to exist. It then fixes θ=2π/5.

**The twelve vertices (scan 5).** The five rotated tangent directions give the points xᵢ. The author adjoins u, their antipodes x′ᵢ and u′, and defines an icosahedral triangle and a six-point cap. The two left-hand notes count distinct points and name the resulting icosahedron.

**A cap and its stabiliser (scans 5–6).** The consecutive peripheral distances are compared with the distances to the apex. The cap carries a cyclic order. Its stabiliser has five rotations and five orientation-reversing elements, described using planes through the axis and the peripheral vertices.

**Moving the cap (scans 6–7, E07).** The lemma selects another six-point configuration. After proposing a check of three lengths, the author composes rotations about u and w, lists six required images and computes the matrix in the moving frame. The explicit computation breaks off during γx₁.

**Edges, faces and group orders (scans 8–9, E08).** Two tables give 30 edges and 20 faces in families indexed modulo five. The theorem acts first on vertices, then on oriented edges and ordered face triples. Counting gives 60 rotations and 120 orthogonal symmetries. Two marginal notes describe edges by distance and faces by mutual adjacency.

**Recovering the group from incidence (scans 9–11).** The argument propagates fixed vertices from an initial face. It then treats the face–edge graph through adjacent faces and their intersections, identifies it with the dodecahedral graph, and proposes coherent cyclic orders on caps as additional data that distinguish the rotation subgroup.

**The two fifth-root sums (scan 13, E13).** The top finishes a product calculation. The boxed quadratic, its two roots, the two cosine values and the square-minus-two relation occupy separate parts of the page. The arrays below compare exponents with their negatives and pair the four sums into two values.

**Inversion and coefficients (scan 15, E15).** Powers of a primitive fifth root describe the automorphisms. The map T↦T⁴ is evaluated on powers of T and compared coefficient by coefficient on a polynomial of degree at most three. The written fixed-coefficient conditions are c₁=0 and c₂=c₃.

**Quotients and trace (scan 15).** Several diagrams put the primitive-root locus, inversion quotient and λ↦λ+λ⁻¹ side by side. Small annotations give ranks, group labels and a totient product. The trace of γ is computed, and the sheet stops during the corresponding norm product; its terms can be compared with the opening of scan 13.

**The proposed sum in (OM) (scans 16 and 14).** Starting with I and J mapping to K, the pencil argument treats injectivity and surjectivity of a map of Hom sets. It defines the extension by a supremum, checks its restrictions and considers finite infima. The inverted sheet continues the computation with arbitrary suprema. The diagonal strokes crossing this work are part of its manuscript state.

**Restricted products (scan 14 and the inverted foot of scan 13).** A family (Iλ) is followed by the product of tuples equal to 1 in almost every coordinate. Coordinate maps α⁰μ and a map γ into K enter conditions a)–c). The two reverse-direction lines on scan 13 are headed d) and e); e) stops at an implication arrow.

**The sketch leaf (scan 17).** Loops with named flower parts, a shaded component, polygonal figures, neighbourhoods and a numbered chain coexist with notation for a group action. The most legible algebra at right involves X≃G/H, equivariant maps and Eᴴ. The faint writing does not establish one argument connecting all the drawings.

## Remarks of the model

**R04 · Model remark, medium confidence in the interpretation.** In the frame of scan 4, the stabiliser of (u,v) in O(E) has two elements. This fits a reading of “doublement transitive” as counting two isometries between frames. In the modern two-transitive sense the action does not have this property: the scalar product of the two base vectors is invariant. The count is unambiguous, but the page does not define the phrase.

**R07 · Model remark, high confidence.** Substituting the angle of scan 5 into the matrix of scan 7 sends the six displayed vectors to their six listed images. This checks the rotation even though its handwritten verification ends after a bare equals sign. E07 records the exact coordinate check.

**R08 · Model reading question, low confidence in identifying a slip.** In the second lower face triple (scan 8, source L31) the frozen subscript reads i−1 or i−2. The antipodal image of the adjoining triple calls for i−2. The pen itself leaves a narrow numeral, and the alternative remains in the transcription.

An enumeration of the vertex permutations gives 120 graph automorphisms. All preserve the vertex Gram matrix, and 60 preserve orientation. On scan 11 the cyclic orders supply the orientation information needed to distinguish the two groups.
