# Mathematical notes on scans 15–27

## What the pages do

**Local extension and effectivity, scans 15, 17 and 19.** Scan 15 seeks an abelian scheme from data already available on local bases. Uniqueness gives comparisons between the local abelian schemes under generization. The missing existence statement is that this family belongs to the essential image. Scan 17 compares a putative global scheme with its restriction over the open set and compares its Tate object with the given one level by level. The proposed lemma that a local system of abelian schemes comes from an abelian scheme bears the marginal verdict “Douteux, sans doute faux”. Scan 19 formulates a more local extension problem for an isomorphism, with successively smaller neighbourhoods of a point. Its reduction stops at “Alors”.

**A group action with trivial stabilizers, scan 16.** Example 0.4 gives a semi-simple group over the complex numbers acting on a nonsingular quasi-affine scheme. The three assertions concern trivial stabilizers, existence of a geometric quotient of finite type and failure of properness. The construction uses SL(2) on pairs consisting of a nonzero linear form and a quartic that is the square of a homogeneous quadratic of discriminant 1. The sheet ends after the word “by”, where the writer has just introduced a map to the affine line.

**EGA proof corrections and their cover, scans 18 and 20.** The most substantial correction on scan 18 proposes using a regular sequence to obtain a basis of the conormal module and then splitting a local basis of the middle term into two packets. Another entry asks for a reference about filtered limits of finitely presented modules. Other entries concern punctuation, numbering, letters and references. Scan 20 is a dated printer’s proof cover with three circled references and a fourth in parentheses.

**Algebraicity of a map of Tate objects, scan 21.** Given abelian schemes A and B over S, the manuscript asks when a map from Tₚ(A) to Tₚ(B) is induced by a homomorphism from A to B. A crossed version discusses the fibrewise locus. Part a) proposes a largest closed subscheme T of algebraicity and compatibility with base change. Part b) gives a neighbourhood statement at points of T of residue characteristic p. The final corollary imposes a specialization condition involving T, the characteristic-p locus and a set Z of points singled out by maximality or association. The definition of Z has several rewrites.

**Categorical and geometric quotients, scan 22.** The typescript gives a field case in which a geometric quotient of a normal algebraic scheme is algebraic. Part (3) applies the universal categorical quotient assumption first to open subsets and then to points. It states a criterion for a universal geometric quotient using universal submersiveness and the image in the fibre product. Part (4) lists conditions for universal openness of the group projection. Two “loc” insertions and a pencilled note by “type” affect the hypotheses. A separate, crossed manuscript strip contains primed base objects and a comparison of two Tate objects.

**Descent of morphisms, scans 23 and 25.** Lemme 1 on scan 23 asserts exactness of a Hom sequence for a proper epimorphism and a formally unramified target, with a bracketed Noetherian qualification. The proof passes through a continuous factorization and a kernel of two direct-image maps. Corollaire 1 reduces the question to an integral radicial case under additional finiteness hypotheses. Scan 25 develops the local-ring argument through several cancelled versions. Corollaire 2 applies the lemma to the scheme of group homomorphisms of abelian schemes. Corollaire 3 proposes the parallel descent assertion for p-divisible groups and identifies the point still requiring proof.

**An orbit and its stabilizer, scan 24.** A dimension formula at the top is used in an argument by specialization of generic points of orbits. The lower part starts “lemma 0.3”. It characterizes properness of the orbit map at x by closedness of its orbit and properness of its stabilizer. The page gives the forward implication and ends during the converse, immediately after “replace G”.

**Prefatory acknowledgments, scan 26.** The typescript mentions the Italian school’s problem about the completeness of the characteristic linear system on a surface in characteristic zero. It acknowledges sources of encouragement and attributes individual results, chapters and ideas to mathematicians by name. The dated closing gives Harvard University.

**Effective descent, scan 27.** Under an additional effective-epimorphism hypothesis, the writer asks whether a descent datum on an abelian scheme is effective. Parts a) and b) reduce the base first to a local ring and then to a complete local ring with algebraically closed residue field. The four-level diagram records the descent assertions needed at different stages. The text explains why the earlier corollary cannot be applied directly to S₂ and turns to its kernel argument. The crossed lower block treats a zero-dimensional case, stable affine open sets and the construction of a formal abelian scheme over Spf R.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scans 15 and 17.** Does the compatible local family come from a global abelian scheme? The text asks about the essential image on scan 15. The marginal “Douteux, sans doute faux” on scan 17 concerns the local-system lemma. “[ ] inutile?” separately asks about the bracketed hypothesis.
- **Scan 16.** “the example shattered over-optimistic conjectures that the author had entertained!” Their precise statements are absent.
- **Scan 18.** “Est-il encore possible de changer le texte en conséquence ?” asks about changing a proof. The question on page 224 asks for a reference for a claim the writer regards as true.
- **Scan 21.** “quand cela provient-il d’un hom” asks for an algebraicity criterion for the given map.
- **Scan 22.** “I only know one fact in this direction” qualifies a discussion of whether the quotient is noetherian or locally noetherian. The beginning of the question is on an earlier sheet.
- **Scan 23.** “[ ] inutile si X loc de t.f.?” asks about weakening the hypotheses of Lemme 1.
- **Scan 27.** “à prouver que cette donnée est effective” is the main question. “car S₂ n’est plus loc. noeth !” singles out the obstruction to using Cor. 2 at the next level.

## Remarks of the model

- **M17. Model remark, high confidence.** Full faithfulness can supply uniqueness of the comparison maps used on scans 15 and 17. Obtaining a global object additionally requires the assertion about the essential image. The manuscript itself makes that latter step conditional. This explains the force of the margin beside the lemma without deciding its truth.
- **M21. Model remark, high confidence for the parenthetical restatement.** For prime p, the definition $S_p=V(p1_S)$ on scan 21 identifies the points of residue characteristic p. Thus the final existence condition for s is exactly the nonempty intersection in the inserted parenthesis. This check concerns that restatement alone.
- **M24a and M24b. Model remark, high confidence.** In lemma 0.3 the two necessary conditions follow from separate properties of a proper morphism. Its image is closed, and its fibre over x is proper over the residue field by base change. The sheet identifies that fibre with S(x). The converse is a separate step.
- **M25–27. Model remark, high confidence.** The Hom construction in scan 25 addresses descent of homomorphisms. Scan 27 begins with an abelian scheme equipped with a descent datum and asks for its effectiveness. The shift in the object being sought explains why the earlier corollary is invoked inside a further argument.
