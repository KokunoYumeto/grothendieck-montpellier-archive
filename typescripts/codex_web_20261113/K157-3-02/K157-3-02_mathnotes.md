# Mathematical notes on scans 018–033

## What the pages do

**018–019 · Paths and composition.** An element of \(\mathrm{Ch}_\infty(a,c)\) is drawn as a zigzag. A proposed redefinition and its equality are cancelled. The live passage gives arrows in both directions between the signed composite and \(e_{a,c}\). A functor together with a path between chosen points is followed through a second such pair. The curved arrow on 019 defines the composite path \(c''\).

**019 · Pointed pairings.** The discussion turns to pairings in pointed Hot, their associativity and the value of Hom cat. Two marginal notes qualify the assertions about products. The first writes a product of pointed objects followed by a construction in which their wedge is contracted to the point. The last lines single out the image of the null map \(a\to0\to b\).

**020 · Localization and additivity.** The author asks what would make zero a neutral object after localization. He wants conditions on the localization map for finite sums and products. When \(M\) is additive and \(k\)-linear, he considers direct sums of arrows in \(\Sigma\) and a stronger condition of the kind suggested by the five lemma.

**021–023 · Linear pairings.** The author considers an adjunction between \(\Sigma\) and \(\Omega\), then places the hoped-for path object in a derived category of \(k\)-modules. One convention uses upper cohomological bounds, the other lower homological bounds. A tensor pairing is to give a pairing in pointed Hot. The comparison map on 022 and the triangle beneath it indicate how to pass from the stronger data to the weaker pairing.

**023–024 · Representing the first group.** A group attributed to Quillen is compared with the first group of the path object. Holding \(a\) fixed and varying \(b\) leads to the possible representing object \(\Sigma a\). The second representation uses \(\Omega b\). The text now uses \(W\) for weak equivalences to distinguish that class of arrows from the suspension symbol.

**024–025 · Tests for a homotopy theory.** Circled a and b distinguish conditions involving the existence of the functors and the behaviour of path categories. A third test, circled c, is stated in terms of pointed-Hot-valued Hom objects. Two formulas labelled (**) distinguish the cases in which loops or suspension exist. The accompanying discussion treats \(\Omega b\) as a group object and \(\Sigma a\) as a cogroup object.

**026–027 · Constructing a loop object.** Under the heading « Essai de construction du foncteur Ω », the diagonal of \(S\) is factored through a path object \(\mathrm{Ch}(S)\). A square then takes the fibre over the map \(e\to S\times S\). The remaining problem is to identify maps from \(T\) into this candidate with the first group of the path object for \(T,S\).

**028 · Letter.** The closing page of a letter asks for comments on a dossier intended to help people organize with the means at hand. The writer describes a metre-high pile of documents and hopes continued collaboration will make them useful. The typed text, handwritten initials and date lie within the cancellation strokes.

**029–030 · Chapter cover and printed reverse.** A handwritten cover announces a comparison of Cat with a triangular symbol carrying a raised angle and has a separate X. The neighbouring landscape sheet carries the PREDONTA printer banners, six job-status lines and a computing-centre bulletin.

**031–032 · Asphericity and fibrations.** A numbered recollection of definitions begins with a \(\mathbb D\)-aspherical category and a \(\mathbb D\)-equivalence. It proceeds to aspherical and coaspherical functors, smooth and proper functors, and fibrations. Base change, fibres and the two slice orientations are written explicitly. The next leaf separates left/cohomological and right/homological variants.

**032–033 · From a derivator to a localizer.** The class \(W_{\mathbb D}\) collects the \(\mathbb D\)-equivalences in Cat. The author recalls having used a class \(W\) as initial data in PS and asks how much of the present theory can be developed that way. The final sentence announces a return to the axioms and continues beyond the last supplied leaf.

## Questions and conjectures

- **018–019.** A question mark sits over the equals sign in the cancelled formula. In the live discussion, « Il est difficile de croire qu'ils pourraient ne pas être associatifs ! » concerns the pairings. « Si tout ceci marche » introduces the proposed Hom cat theory.
- **020.** « Il faudrait montrer que 0 est aussi un objet neutre » in the localization. « à faire » stands beside the conditions for finite sums and products. At the foot, « un lemme des cinq ? » asks what stronger additive requirement one could state.
- **021–022.** « Mais peut-être ici faut-il un axiome plus subtil » introduces the possible isomorphism condition. « l'attente naturelle ici » concerns the derived-category value of the path object. « on s'attend bien à avoir une théorie forte des accouplements » returns to the derived tensor pairing.
- **023.** « Si ce que j'avance sans preuves dans 1°) est vrai, il doit s'ensuivre qu'on a un isom. can. » precedes the comparison with the first group attributed to Quillen. « Si … est représentable » introduces suspension conditionally.
- **024–025.** « sous réserve d'existence » qualifies both constructions. « à peu de choses près ? » questions the restatement of a condition. The doubt expressed at the foot of 024 continues with « ces … conditions puissent être satisfaites pour M finie » on 025, where two trivial cases are singled out. The third test adds « mais peut-être ça résulte du reste ? ».
- **025.** « Il faut les voir séparément » accompanies the two versions of (**). The final sentence expresses doubt about their chances of holding without the circled condition b. The outline of the pronoun in « Je … suis convaincu » is uncertain.
- **026–027.** « Je vais essayer … de dégager des conditions générales » proposes going beyond those of Quillen. The projection is to be a fibration « dans un sens convenable, à dégager ». The next leaf asks whether the auxiliary map to \(e\) should also be a fibration and states « Il faut s'assurer » of the bijection natural in \(T\).
- **033.** The words « Il se pourrait que » introduce the possibility that earlier statements remain « valables plus généralement en termes d'un localisateur W ». The author sets aside the specifically cohomological and homological statements, then asks whether conditions « à expliciter sur W » could permit recovery of a derivator and of \(W\) as \(W_{\mathbb D}\). He proposes to « examiner s'ils sont tous vérifiés pour » before the paper runs out.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · My remark, high confidence about endpoints (022).** In the triangle, the arrow labelled can. ends at the same \(\gamma(P*Q)\) from which \(\gamma(c)\) starts. Their composite therefore has the source and target of the bottom arrow \(c_\bullet\). This checks the endpoints of the comparison used in S22. The bottom vertex uses a cartesian cross, while the preceding line labelled (*′) has a star-shaped mark.

**R2 · My remark, medium confidence (023–024).** With the preferred group-letter reading, the first and third displays on 024 identify \(\mathrm{Hom}(\Sigma a,b)\) and \(\mathrm{Hom}(a,\Omega b)\) with the same written \(\Pi_1(a,b)\). Substituting this common expression gives the two Hom terms of the adjunction line in the same order. The author explicitly conditions this step on the existence of both representing objects. The Π/π letterform and the separate thick index on 023 limit a broader identification with the preceding leaf.
