# K82-04 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**The involution (50).** The interchange of the two factors of $X\times X$ induces an involution on $Q$. To give the corresponding antiautomorphism of $M$, the author chooses a local generator of $\det\check V$. It supplies the map $\alpha$ in (13). Formula (14) sends $u$ to $\alpha^{-1}({}^{t}u)\alpha$. He records independence of the choice, reversal of a product, the trace and determinant identities (16)–(17), and the induced operation on a pair $(A,\rho)$ in (18).

**The projection of $Q$ (51).** Formula (19) maps $Q$ to the projective parameter space associated with the special subalgebras. With $A_\Sigma$ the universal subalgebra, (20) identifies $Q$ with its spectrum. The next question asks which pairs of lines give a section lying in a fixed $A$. A heavily cancelled proposition sets down a first set of conditions, including trace-zero or orthogonality conditions.

**The line criterion (52–53).** The new proposition compares an orthogonality condition on $\underline L$, preservation of the line $L$ by $A$, an invertible subbundle $J$ annihilating $L$, and the existence of $M$ with $L^\perp\otimes M\subset A$. The text derives the character $A\to\mathcal O_S$ induced on the line. The proof uses local generators, the trace pairing, and the kernel and image of a generator $u$ of $J$. It also gives uniqueness of $M$.

**Special subalgebras and planes (53).** Below a cancelled heading, the author describes giving $A$ by its perpendicular $H$ of rank two. Formula (21) passes between the space of special subalgebras and two presentations of a projective plane. Its right-hand brace gives a description in terms of lines.

**Intersection with the conic (54).** The map $D\mapsto D\cap X=Y$ identifies the space of lines in $P$ with effective divisors of degree two on $X$. Formula (23) identifies this intersection with $\operatorname{Spec}A$. In the projective-line description these are the $L$ preserved by $A$. For an invertible $u$ as in the text, (24) writes the same scheme as $X^u$.

**The stabilizer (54–55).** Functoriality of (23) compares the action on $Y$ with conjugation on $A$. The proposition distinguishes preserving $Y$ from acting trivially on it. The first condition is $uAu^{-1}=A$. For the second, the page gives the condition $u\in\Gamma(S,A)$.

**Subgroups from special algebras (55–56).** Formula (25) associates to $A$ the image of its invertible elements modulo scalars. The subgroup is described as commutative, smooth, connected and of relative dimension one. The argument for the converse compares a group with the subgroup constructed from its Lie algebra through a centralizer. The characteristic qualification and the handwritten request for verification belong to this argument.

**The dictionary and its scope (57–59).** A cover introduces the dictionary associated with $\mathrm{GP}(1)\simeq\mathrm{SO}(3)$. The table lists torsors, forms of groups and Lie algebras, quaternion or Azumaya algebras, projective-line and conic bundles, and oriented quadratic data of rank three. Two entire rows have been cancelled. Asterisks refer to a restriction concerning 2 on the base. The next leaf counts the pairwise equivalences and the isomorphisms required for their transitivity.

**The construction using gerbes (59–60).** For each category the author chooses a typical object over $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ and lets $G_0$ act on it. Identifying its automorphism scheme with $G_0$ would exhibit the corresponding gerbe as the category of right $G_0$-torsors. He restricts the base in the starred cases and says that the individual functors still need to be described.

**Typical objects and their actions (60–61).** The list gives the trivial torsor, trivial group and Lie-algebra forms, the matrix algebra, the standard rank-three module with quadratic form and volume element, the projective line, and the projective plane with its conic. Section II starts with left translations. It then describes inner automorphisms, their action on the Lie algebras, and the action on $M_0$ that factors through $\mathrm{Gl}(2)/G_m$.

**The trace-zero model (62–63).** The coordinates of a trace-zero matrix are $(x,y,z)$ as displayed in (3). Its negative determinant is $xy+z^2$. Conjugation preserves the determinant, giving the action on $(E_0,q_0)$ via (6). The next leaf invokes the vanishing of the character group in the displayed Hom expression to obtain preservation of the volume element as well.

**Projective actions and the isomorphism assertion (63).** The scalar action on the projective line is trivial, so the linear action descends to $\mathrm{GP}(1)$. The action on the rank-three quadratic module acts on its projective plane and conic. The author then admits that all the resulting homomorphisms (7) are isomorphisms, with the earlier qualification on the base.

**Twisting and the Borel (64).** Formula (8) attaches to a torsor $R$ the twist of a typical object. For the projective-line category the functor becomes $R\mapsto R/B_{0S}$. The two displays labelled (10) identify a Borel subgroup of $G_0$ and give its upper triangular lift with diagonal entries $\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$. The last line identifies the line it stabilizes and breaks off at « s'identifie ».
