## What the pages do

**Lemma 1 and its reductions — scans 4–5.** The notes seek surjectivity of H¹(k,G) → H¹(k,G′) for an exact sequence with G and G′ “simples/k”. The stated hypotheses are H²-vanishing for tori, finite étale commutative groups, abelian varieties and connected unipotent groups killed by p. A maximal torus and its normalizer reduce the kernel to several cases. The finite étale case is related to Galois cohomology and a named argument whose first surname is doubtful. The term “simple” is retained as the manuscript’s terminology.

**Finite-kernel corollary — scans 5–7.** A corollary assumes H²(k,K)=0 for every finite K and aims to recover the hypotheses of Lemma 1. The notes use torsion in cohomology and finite multiplication kernels for tori and abelian varieties. For the remaining unipotent case, they pass to purely inseparable extensions and then to the Frobenius argument that follows.

**Frobenius lemma and corollary — scans 6–7.** Lemma 2 compares the kernel of restriction to k raised to p to the minus n with the kernel of the map induced by the iterated Frobenius. A triangular cohomology diagram and a smaller diagram over Spec(k) accompany it. The next corollary identifies the kernel with an image from the cohomology of the Frobenius kernel and uses its radicial and finite character to finish the preceding corollary.

**Reduction of the H¹ problem — scans 7–8.** Lemma 3 reduces the general epimorphism problem to the cases where G and its kernel are “simples/k”, and where the kernel is radicial. A suitable invariant radicial subgroup I gives a quotient diagram. The proof continues with a diagram of H¹-sets and a twisting/lifting argument. The visible primes on ξ and ξ′ are preserved even where their roles appear to shift.

**The radicial case — scans 8–9.** Lemma 4 assumes H²-vanishing for finite radicial commutative groups. It reduces the kernel to height 1, passes to its Lie algebra, chooses a Cartan subalgebra and uses a normalizer. The argument then reduces through nilpotence and commutators to a commutative case. The lower bracketed note questions, or appears to question, a p-closedness assertion; both the wording and question mark are doubtful.

**Brauer hypothesis and the H¹ theorem — scan 10.** Lemma 5 starts from vanishing of Br(K′) for finite separable extensions and states higher cohomology vanishing for finite commutative groups. Its dévissage separates multiplicative type from α_p; the first case is placed between tori and treated using the named Tate–Nakayama input. The page concludes with the theorem that cd(k)≤1 gives an epimorphism on H¹ for every epimorphism of algebraic groups over k. Several local letter and degree issues are retained below as possible slips.

**Kernels locally isomorphic to a group — scan 11.** A theorem treats a kernel K′ locally isomorphic to an algebraic group K. It states that H²(k,K′) and its primed part are the same one-point set. The proof lifts an H¹-class from underlined Aut Ext(K) to underlined Aut(K), obtains a representing group, and then invokes the action of the centre’s H². The inserted field hypothesis points back to “du/de Thm.” without a numbered reference.

**Subgroup classes and the first reduction — scans 12–13.** The notes ask whether a kernel on a site X comes from a sheaf of groups, expressing the question by membership of c(K) in a primed H². They associate a sheaf of subgroup classes, discuss a normalizer operation, and construct a canonical class through a local category of groups with a chosen subgroup. A first reduction principle relates membership in the primed H² for that normalizer to membership for c(K). Its bracketed name is doubtful.

**A second reduction principle — scan 13.** Starting with a “surjectif” morphism K → K′ and an identification of K′ with the kernel attached to a sheaf F′, the notes form a local category of lifts F. This produces a kernel K″ and an exact sequence involving the I-like symbols attached to K and K′. A class c′ is then used to reduce the original question to a primed-H² question for K″. The lower paragraph alternates k-like and X-like base symbols; those have not been normalized.

**General algebraic kernels — scans 14–15.** The stated target is H²(k,K) ≃ H²(k,K)′ ≃ one element. The proof is organized by dimension. In dimension zero it further reduces by degree to étale and radicial cases, using a lifting assertion in the first and infinitesimal Cartan subgroups in the second. In positive dimension, commutators, Frobenius and maximal tori reduce the problem to finite étale and connected unipotent cases, then to the commutative case. The cancellations show several revisions to this sequence of reductions.

**A matrix example — scan 17.** The page defines G by a block matrix whose two 2×2 blocks have equal nonzero determinant. Projection of the second block modulo its centre is said to give a homomorphism onto GP(2). The displayed kernel Z is identified with both the centre and radical of G. The author then says that G is defined over k but Z is not when the characteristic is 2 and a is not in k², and asserts analogous examples in every positive characteristic. The precise sense of the statement about Z is not explained on the sheet.

Scans 2, 3 and 16 are title sheets. Scan 16 introduces questions concerning radicals and the “variété des drapeaux”; it contains no developed mathematical argument.
