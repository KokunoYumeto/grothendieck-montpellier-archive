# What the pages do

**U1 · The universal coefficients (66).** For a finite set $I$, Grothendieck forms a generic matrix and a ring whose generators are indexed by singletons and unordered pairs in $I$. His map $\varphi$ identifies off-diagonal entries in symmetric positions and doubles the diagonal entries. He calls the image of the generic determinant $\Delta$.

**U2 · From permutations to graphs (66–68).** The determinant expansion separates fixed points, transpositions and longer cycles. After forgetting orientation, a fixed point gives a single vertex, a transposition gives a doubled edge factor, and a longer cycle gives the product around a polygon. Reversing any longer cycle leaves its edge monomial unchanged. The last display on 68 groups the resulting terms by graph.

**U3 · The square and the sign (69–70, 72).** Modulo 2 only graphs consisting entirely of paired vertices contribute. In odd cardinality the determinant vanishes modulo 2. For $|I|=2m$, Grothendieck calls the sum of the matching monomials $B$ and seeks a sign with $B^2\equiv\varepsilon\Delta\pmod4$. The off-diagonal terms in $B^2$ involve two different matchings. Where they disagree, the edges compose even polygons, and the two matchings can be interchanged independently on each polygon. The final line on 72 combines two sums with coefficients $2(1+(-1)^{\varepsilon(\gamma)})$.

**U4 · The square of a lift (74).** With $A_0=A/2A$, the question becomes when $\Delta=b^2-4c$ in $A$. The identity for $b'=b+2x$ shows that the square modulo $4A$ depends only on $b_0$, the reduction of $b$. The lemma calls that class $\gamma(b_0)$. Condition b) under the lemma raises the issue recorded in S2.

**U5 · Descending a square (74, 76).** The next calculation starts with a relation involving a squared multiplier and passes to $A_0$. A quotient involving $b'_0$ is used to obtain a square root $b_0$ of $\Delta_0$. The boxes mark where regularity and integral closedness are needed. The calculation then returns to $A/4A$ to cancel the multiplier. Several earlier powers and factors have been crossed out.

**U6 · Proposition and marginal qualification (78).** The proposition gathers conditions on $2$, $A_0$ and $\Delta_0$. It compares a discriminant presentation after introducing a squared multiplier with the existence of $b,c\in A$ satisfying $b^2-4c=\Delta$. The exponent and part of the wording in the first condition are too uncertain for a precise general statement. The slanting NB considers a weaker condition on the multiplier.

**U7 · A localized quadratic algebra (78–80).** After inverting $2\Delta$, the algebra obtained by adjoining a square root of $\Delta$ is written $A_{2\Delta}[T]/(T^2-\Delta)$. The author supposes it extends to a finite étale algebra $B_\Delta$ and imposes $\operatorname{Pic} A_\Delta=0$. Scan 80 continues the sentence with a single quadratic generator and identifies the ideal generated by its discriminant.

**U8 · The unit factor (80).** Comparing the two quadratic presentations gives $b^2-4c=u^2\Delta$ after localization. A proposed decomposition of all local units into powers of $2$, powers of $\Delta$ and an old unit is struck through. The subsequent argument concerns the integral closure of $A_\Delta$ in $A_{2\Delta}$. The page turns to the converse and ends in a canceled equation.

The interleaves on 71, 73, 75, 77, 79 and 81 are a sales circular, a film announcement and four thesis notices. Their titles and dates are in the accompanying page notes.

# Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence within the finite check (70, 72).** On eight labeled vertices there are 105 perfect matchings. Among the 10,920 ordered pairs of distinct matchings, 4,830 edge multisets occurred twice and 315 occurred four times. In every case the multiplicity was $2^r$, where $r$ counts the alternating cycles of length at least four. This tests the independent swaps described on 72, including the first case with two such cycles.

**R2 · Model remark, high confidence within the checked sizes (66, 69, 72).** For generic symmetric matrices with doubled diagonals of sizes 2 and 4, every coefficient of $B^2-(-1)^{|I|/2}\Delta$ was divisible by 4. There were respectively 1 and 14 nonzero coefficients in the differences. For size 3 the five coefficients of $\Delta$ were all even. These exact polynomial computations give small-dimensional checks of the manuscript argument.

**R3 · Model remark, high confidence (80).** The boxed integral-closure hypothesis has a direct use at P5. Both $u$ and $u^{-1}$ satisfy monic quadratic equations over $A_\Delta$, since $u^2$ is a unit there. This accounts for membership in $A_\Delta^*$. The subsequent plain $A^*$ is recorded separately in S3.

**Reading question · Scan 72, upper paragraph, after “sur” and in the symmetry insertion.** Frozen $J(\sigma,\sigma')$. Another possible reading is a double-stemmed $\mathrm{II}(\sigma,\sigma')$. A dark top and foot join two distinct verticals in both places, while the first $J$ on the sheet has a hook. Low confidence in an identification of the double-stemmed character.

**Reading question · Scan 78, upper paragraph, just after the uncertain odd-number expression.** Frozen “comme nous notons supra.” The middle and last groups might instead read “entier naturel impair”. In the enlargement the pen rises to a peak in the third group and descends below the baseline in the last, consistent with *naturel* and *impair*. The first group and the sign in the expression remain unresolved. Low confidence in the whole alternative phrase.

**Reading question · Scan 76, just below the line beginning with the lift $b\equiv b_0\ (2)$.** The frozen text records one canceled factor before $bv$ in the expression for $b'$. A small crossed group sits directly below it. It could be a second attempt at the same factor rather than part of the upper cancellation. The strokes do not identify a further complete expression. Low confidence.
