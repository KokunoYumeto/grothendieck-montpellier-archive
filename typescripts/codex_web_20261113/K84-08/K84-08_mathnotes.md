# Mathematical notes · cote 84, scans 113–129

## What the pages do

**113 · Norm and base change.** A square compares the norm on a tensor product with \(N_2\) on \(A\). The author first evaluates on \(x_1\otimes x_2\), then compares the associated bilinear forms using conjugation, trace and the inserted definitions of \(x'\) and \(y'\). The sheet ends «O.K.».

**114–115 · Three involutions.** From \(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3=1\), the author writes the three cyclic identities for products of two involutions. The involution identity is INV-114. He obtains a common trace and a formula for \(2x\), with an injectivity qualification in the margin. On 115 the crossed opening anticipates multiplicative identities. The live calculation pairs four symbols \(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3\) and obtains their full product in each composed norm. It retains the hypothesis that \(k\to A\) is injective. See NORM-115 below for the product calculation alone.

**116–117 · The group acting on the tensor product.** The three factor involutions give an action on \(A_1\otimes A_2\otimes A_3\). The author explains why a single-factor involution need not descend to \(A\). He then singles out the kernel of the coordinate-sum map to \(\mathbb F_2\), lists its three nonidentity elements, and specifies how these act on \(A\). The generator observation is GEN-116, and the parity list is EVEN-116.

**117–119 · A section and scalar invariants.** For three quadratic covers, the product maps to \(X_1\wedge X_2\wedge X_3\). Its algebra maps to the invariants of the tensor product and then to \(A\). On 118 he asks whether the resulting map of schemes factors through a section. The inclusion of the image in the invariants is IMG-118. On 119 the fixed-point equations give \(4x=T_{A/k}(x)\). When 2 is invertible this places \(x\) in the scalar image. The arithmetic is TRACE-119 and the unit condition is UNIT-119.

**119–121 · The étale case.** The proposition assumes quadratic étale \(A_i\), injectivity of \(k\to A\), generation of \(A\) by the images of the \(A_i\), and scalar invariants. It identifies \(\operatorname{Spec} A\) with each of the three pairwise products and describes the system as a twist. In the split description a chosen \(\varepsilon\) picks out the triples whose wedge is \(\varepsilon\). The author gives the converse, then reduces the direct implication to a lemma about a stable subscheme of \(X_J\) when the group acts transitively on \(J\). The displayed hypothesis and the ideal calculation use different amounts of information. See LEM-120.

**121–122 · The question without étaleness.** The author takes the inverse image of a section and asks for a biquadratic structure. He writes down the universal base \(X_1\wedge X_2\wedge X_3\), with three maps from the triple product to the pulled-back covers. A second diagram records the algebras \(B,B_0,B_1\), proposed ranks, trace and a questioned norm. Finiteness, local freeness and flatness are distinguished explicitly.

**124 · Maps of split extensions.** Two rows \(L,E,M\) and \(L',E',M'\) are linked by \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). The author compares compatibility with the backward maps to \(L\) and from \(M\). He tests the comparison on tensors and components leading to \(E'_1\wedge E'_2\). The page ends with a lone \(E_1\wedge\).

**126 and 128 · Changing a splitting.** A square with translations \(T_U,T_{-U},T_{-\gamma b},T_{-\gamma b'}\) accompanies the identification of \(E_1\vee E_2\) with \(E_1\wedge E_2\) for \(\chi\)-split extensions. On 128 the author switches to tilded data, compares the signs of \(b_i,U_i\) and the translation, then asks for a translation carrying the splitting defined by \(b\) to that defined by \(-b\). The box gives \(v=-\gamma b\) under \(\gamma\chi=2\). The square’s scalar calculation is SQUARE-126 and the boxed choice is SHIFT-128.

**125 and 127 · Typescript pages 465 and 466.** On 465 the typescript moves from the space and arms races to the author’s own attitudes toward the qualities he calls «yang» and «yin». On 466 he relates this distinction to his childhood, his parents, and the discovery of meditation. Its footnote names two other sections.

**129 · Typescript page 467.** In the entry dated «3 octobre», the author recalls his expectations about the Weil conjectures and distinguishes them from his assessment of the standard conjectures on algebraic cycles. He says that the value of a conjecture depends on the question it brings to light. He pairs the standard-conjecture question with resolution of singularities. These are the author’s recollections and judgments on this page.

## Remarks of the model

- **NORM-115 · model observation, high confidence.** Counting factors in the live product \((x_0x_1)(x_0x_2)(x_0x_3)\) gives multiplicities \(3,1,1,1\). This agrees with \(x_0^2(x_0x_1x_2x_3)\) on the next line. This check uses the displayed monomials alone.
- **EVEN-116 · model observation, high confidence.** Enumerating the eight binary triples gives precisely \(000,011,101,110\) of even weight. The last three agree with the live list below the kernel display. The crossed \(001\) has odd weight.
- **TRACE-119 · model observation, high confidence.** With \(T_i(x)=2x\) for the three fixed-point conditions, the coefficient left after subtracting \(2x\) from their sum is \(3\cdot2-2=4\). This checks the passage to the boxed coefficient.
- **LEM-120 · model observation, high confidence about the literal statement.** Take \(J\) to be a singleton with trivial group, \(k=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), \(X_J=\operatorname{Spec}k\) and \(\mathscr X=\operatorname{Spec}(k/(2))\). The subscheme is proper and stable, while the displayed map to the sections of \(X_J\) is the identity. The example therefore satisfies the written injectivity condition and contradicts the written conclusion.
- **LEM-120R · model reading of the intended hypothesis, low confidence.** In the same example the restriction \(k\to\Gamma(\mathscr X,\mathcal O_{\mathscr X})\) has kernel \(\{0,2\}\), the ideal of the subscheme. Requiring this map to be injective excludes the example and fits the ideal-intersection sentence on 121. That single test does not establish the general corrected lemma. The sheaf character itself carries a doubt mark.
- **SHIFT-128 · model observation, high confidence.** For the explicitly proposed \(v=-\gamma b\), the coefficient in \(b+\chi v\) is \(1-\chi\gamma=-1\) under the stated scalar condition. This verifies the choice without requiring \(\chi\) to have an inverse.
- **Reading questions from the notes.** On 113, lines 1–2, the frozen sentence ends «similaire au précédent». Its sense suggests «commute», a word in the crop-first observation, but the strip shows no separate word after «précédent» before the diagram. On 128, lines 5–6, the final margin reading is \(-T_{-U}\) with a separate stroke before the second \(T\). A continuation dash instead of a unary minus remains possible. The neighbouring inverse explains why the distinction matters. Neither reading question changes the transcription.
