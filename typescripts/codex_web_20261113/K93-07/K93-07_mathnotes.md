# What the pages do

**Crystals and vector extensions (47).** The opening identifies $\mathbb D^*(A)$ with the Lie algebra of the extension of $A^*$. A lift of the indicated filtration is associated with a smooth vector subgroup $L$. Taking the quotient by $L$ and then the dual is said to give the required abelian scheme $A'$. The author relates the two choices of Dieudonné functor by a perfect pairing compatible with the filtrations.

**Two constructions for a BT group (47).** The remark proposes a cohomological construction and one through the universal vector extension. It also distinguishes two routes to the extension itself. The author singles out the exponential route for the deformation theorem.

**Comparison for an abelian scheme and its BT group (48).** With $p$ locally nilpotent on $S$, the author returns to Berthelot's crystalline site. He announces a base-change-compatible comparison of $\underline D^*(A(\infty))$ with first crystalline cohomology, with compatible filtrations on evaluation at $S$. The pencil addition describes the use of the proposed comparison to arrive heuristically at a definition for general $G$.

**Infinitesimal lifts and extensions (48).** The comparison is said to identify the infinitesimal lifting theories of $A$ and $A(\infty)$ over the divided-power thickenings specified here. The text points to dévissage and the Serre–Tate theorem. A bracketed passage first compares the two $E$'s and then compares the two underlined $\underline E$'s as crystals in groups. Two cancelled lines propose a passage to the limit and a problem on filtered F-crystals.

**Canonical filtration (49 a).** On a perfect ring of characteristic $p$, the text gives the condition $\mathrm{Fil}^{1\,(p)}=\mathrm{Ker}\,F_M=\mathrm{Im}\,V_M$. It says that this determines the submodule uniquely and adds that every Dieudonné crystal is then “admissible”.

**Isogroups and restriction (49 b).** Rationalizing the morphisms defines isogroups over a general base. The author makes the analogous construction for Dieudonné isocrystals and displays the two categories. He records a difficulty with the provisional definition as a stack. A proposition asserts an equivalence under restriction through the indicated nilpotent immersion. A marginal insertion asks for the discrete valuation case.

**Essential image (49, 51).** Problem 4 asks for the image of the preceding functor, a valuative test for membership, and a possible Zariski-open description in the Grassmannian for a complete discrete valuation base of mixed characteristic. The question crosses from the foot of 49 to the head of 51. The next paragraph records a failure of essential surjectivity already for $A=W(k)$ with $k$ algebraically closed.

**Filtered modules and representations (51).** Problem 5 asks how to construct the functor from suitable two-step filtered Dieudonné modules to finite-dimensional $p$-adic Galois representations, and whether rank conditions might suffice. Problem 6 asks for a construction over the more general $A$ of Problem 4 with values in twisted constant $p$-adic sheaves on $\mathrm{Spec}(A_p)$. The cancelled Conjecture 7 begins a related formulation and ends mid-sentence.

**Resolvents (52, exercise 21).** The reused typescript starts with the resolvent identity (1) for an algebra-valued function on an open set. The five parts ask for its local series, its derivatives, a decomposition near infinity, an invertibility criterion for being a resolvent, and the identity for a prescribed series. The sheet supplies a coefficient-comparison hint for the decomposition.

**Approximation and factorization (52, exercise 22, continuing on 50).** Part a) gives an estimate involving the inverse of $(1-\gamma)e+\gamma u$. Part b) asks for $z=xy$ with $y$ in the closure of $Az$ and close to $z$, under a bounded approximate-unit hypothesis on finite sets. On scan 50 the hint controls successive $t_nz$, then passes to limits in $x_n$ and $t_nz$. The text's printed pages 35 and 36 establish this order.

**Entire functions and nilpotence (50, exercise 23).** A trigonometric-polynomial bound on an entire Banach-space-valued function is used to bound its degree. An integral $J$ extracts a Taylor coefficient. A decay condition gives a strict degree bound in b), which c) asks the reader to apply to the resolvent of a quasi-nilpotent element.

**Two Laurent expansions (50, exercise 24).** A function entire in $1/(\zeta-1)$ is expanded inside and outside the unit circle. Bounds on the two coefficient sequences are to imply a degree bound in $1/(\zeta-1)$. The hint changes to the variable $\omega$ and compares radial estimates. This scan ends at “et montrer que”.

**Slopes (53, pencil group).** A brace joins an inequality between partial sums of primed and unprimed $\lambda_j$ and an equality of sums. A lower expression involves $i$ and $h'_i$. A slanted leader runs from the words concerning Hodge to the primed sequence. Some bounds and the relation between the second and third lines remain doubtful.

**Tate's theorem and its image (53).** For a noetherian normal integral $S$ with function field of characteristic zero, the stated theorem makes the restriction of BT groups to the generic point fully faithful. For a complete mixed-characteristic discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field the author asks which Galois modules occur. He attributes a necessary Hodge–Tate condition to Tate and asks for an explicit route back to filtered F-crystals.

**Equal characteristic (53–54).** The author conjectures that the restriction on the characteristic of the function field can be removed. In characteristic $p$ he asks about full faithfulness of restriction for locally free crystals or, at least, F-crystals. He indicates reduction to $k[[t]]$ for algebraically closed $k$, where the question involves formal connections. The possible deduction of the equal-characteristic Tate theorem has two stated prerequisites.

**The two Euler characteristics (55–56).** The cover names $\chi$ and $\chi_!$. Given a field $k$, a two-row table introduces spaces satisfying the respective cohomological finiteness conditions. Rules a)–c) compare the two theories for compact spaces, give compact-support additivity for a closed subspace and its complement, and give the ordinary inclusion–exclusion formula for two closed subsets covering $X$. The page cites the corresponding exact sequences and adds a bracketed finiteness assertion.

**Homotopy and normalization (56 d–e).** A pair of maps illustrates homotopy domination and its finiteness consequence. The next line discusses homotopy-equivalent spaces and their Euler characteristics. The final item gives the point value 1 and the empty-set value 0.

**Disjoint sums and basic spaces (58).** The boxed note treats disjoint unions and additivity. The main calculation gives ordinary and compact-support values for Euclidean space, its indicated compactification, the ball and the sphere. The two hemispheres give a recurrence with initial value $\chi(S^0)=2$. Removing a point from the sphere supplies the last Euclidean-space calculation.

**Cell counts (58).** A finite filtration starts at $X_{-1}=\varnothing$. The $i$th difference is a finite union of $f_i$ copies of $\mathbb R^i$. The stated compact-support characteristic is the alternating sum of the $f_i$, with a further conclusion when $X$ is compact.

**Products (60 f).** The two product rules assert finiteness and multiplicativity. The explanations invoke Künneth for compact supports in one case and Künneth for homology with field duality in the other.

**Fibrations and coefficients (60 g).** For a locally trivial fibration, the author proposes a base-times-fibre formula, flags a reservation and writes the spectral sequence with $R^q f_!(k_X)$ as local coefficients. The rank-$b$ local-system lemma would give the required factor in the alternating sum. The page works out a case with a finite filtration into cells, on each of which the local system is constant.

**Reused policy text (59, 57).** Printed pages 9 and 10 concern institutional research policy, allocation of funds and the proposed assessment of institutions. They bear no mathematical argument in this sequence.

# Remarks of the model

- **M52, model remark, low confidence as a proposed correction.** Take the scalar algebra with $u=1$, $w=0$, $C=2$, $\gamma=0$ and $\varepsilon=1/2$ in scan 52, exercise 22 a). The approximation error in the hypothesis is zero for every positive $\eta$, and $y=1$. The left side printed in the conclusion is 1. This calculation refutes that wording at the specified values. It leaves the candidate replacement S52 unproved.
- **M58, model remark, high confidence.** Starting from 2 and applying the stated hemisphere recurrence gives $2,0,2,0,2,0,2,0,2$ for dimensions 0 through 8, in agreement with the displayed sphere formula over this finite range.
- **M60, model remark, medium confidence.** The rank factor in the last cell calculation is the same one requested in the local-system lemma. The annotation below the restricted coefficient system says “ct. sur” the particular cell. This identifies exactly where the page uses constancy in its finite-filtration argument.
- **Reading question, scan 49, lines 26–27.** The frozen wording is “d'Idéal $J$ annulé par une puissance de $J$”. Both visible letters are J. The alternative “une puissance de $p$” would make a different hypothesis in the surrounding $p$-local discussion. The preceding immersion has already been called nilpotent, so the repeated J could also be deliberate. Low confidence in any proposed emendation.
- **Reading question, scan 56, line 37.** The frozen line has a doubly directed sign as first candidate and a rightward implication as second. A dark stroke at the left head could be a correction. The preceding domination statement and the direction needed to deduce finiteness favour the rightward reading. Any claim of a logical slip here has low confidence.
