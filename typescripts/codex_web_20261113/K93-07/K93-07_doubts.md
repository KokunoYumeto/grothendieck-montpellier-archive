## Legibility

On the comparison typescript the live letters are usually clear. A row of typewritten x’s often leaves enough of a descender or a serif to recover part of the earlier word, but the order of superposed keystrokes is much harder to establish. The small rings typed below the line on scans 49–52 do not reliably distinguish o from 0. The light pencil and the two strokes between the sums on scan 53 carry more uncertainty than the surrounding type.

On the handwritten Euler-characteristic sheets, comparison across scans 55, 56, 58 and 60 helps distinguish χ from X and a subscript exclamation from punctuation. The differences between script forms of E, the crossed words at the starts of items, and the compressed circled note on scan 60 are less secure. The two completely obscured groups are on scans 51 and 56. The administrative sheets read more easily after turning them upside down; the mark inside “le…montant” on scan 57 remains ambiguous.
