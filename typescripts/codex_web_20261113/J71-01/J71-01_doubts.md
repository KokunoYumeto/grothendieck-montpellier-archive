## Legibility

The notice on scan 4 and the bank letter and form on scan 9 can be read closely, down to punctuation and registration numbers. The autograph on scan 4 and the very small printer’s imprint on scan 9 remain uncertain. On scan 3 several low digits and the signs drawn over the capitals are hard to distinguish. On scans 6 and 7 the most difficult places are the small interlinear formulas, the sloping pencil note and the nested indices and links in the large diagram. Two long pen lines across scan 7 are reproduced at their positions; their extent as a cancellation remains open. Scan 8 has a short upside-down address under a crossing stroke and its bottom edge actually cuts off the bases of the final words. On scan 10 two versions of parts of the corollary occupy the same space. In the doubts table, a dash in the alternatives column means that no second reading could be made out. Where the evidence column describes the ink alone, the surrounding words supplied no additional preference.
