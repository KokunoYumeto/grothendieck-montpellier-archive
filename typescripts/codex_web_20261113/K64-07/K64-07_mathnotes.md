# Mathematical notes · Cote 64, scans 78–90

## What the pages do

**Scans 78–79, (16)–(20).** Grothendieck fixes a square $Q_0$ with an identification relating its two orientations to the square roots of $-1$. A chosen side has endpoints $s_i,s_{i'}$. Its drawing places $s_{i'}$ above $s_i$. The relations $s_{i'}=i\,s_i$ and $s_i=i's_{i'}$ use $i'=i^{-1}=-i$. The same pair of bases describes $T_0$ after tensoring by the indicated line. Equations (19) and (20) pass from the diagonals of the fixed square to the orientation data of $J$.

**Scans 79–80, (21).** Over a field $k$, the data of a Legendre pinning are expressed as a three-element set $J$, a point $t$ of the punctured projective line $\Sigma_J^*$, and an identification $\alpha$ of two two-element sets. A level-two Jacobi pinning labels $J$ by $0,1,\infty$. This picks out $\omega^+$ and leaves a choice of square root $i$ of $-1$. The pair $\{t,i\}$ parametrizes the resulting rigid groupoid.

**Scan 80, (22)–(24).** On the absolute base $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[1/2]$, the manuscript uses the torsor of vertices $Q_{0S}$ to twist a module $M$, with superscript $\natural$. The marginal note describes this as tensoring by $\underline O_S^\natural$. A second twist with superscript $\flat$ uses the quadratic cover $\mu_4^*=\mu_4\setminus\mu_2$. The tensor-square assertion is written in (23).

**Scans 81–82, (25)–(30).** An étale cubic cover $\underline J$ gives a projective line $\Sigma_J$ from the trace-zero module of functions on the cover. The short diagram alongside (25) labels the trace. The author twists $\underline O_{\Sigma_J}(1)$ and describes its square using the orientation cover $\underline\omega_J$. For an elliptic curve $E/S$, (28) takes the three nonzero points of order two for $\underline J$. The quotient by the involution gives $\Sigma_{\underline J}$. The image $t$ of the zero section relates the tangent lines in (30).

**Scans 82–83, (31)–(34).** A relative Legendre pinning is an isomorphism from $\Delta_{E/S}$, identified underneath with $T_{E/S,0}$, to the twisted line at $t$. Its tensor square gives (32) through a section $\alpha$ of the contracted product of two quadratic torsors. For fixed $\alpha$, the compatible isomorphisms form the sections of a $\mu_2$-torsor. The classification is restated in terms of $(\underline J,t,\alpha)$. A marginal note asks for an explicit inverse construction.

**Scans 84–85.** An automorphism of the triple must respect $\alpha$ and fix $t$. The decomposition of the even-permutation group separates its identity section from the two orientations of $\underline J$. A nowhere-identity section of that group gives a cyclic action of order three. The condition $t=\rho(t)$ puts $t$ in the fixed-point locus drawn at the foot of scan 84. The next leaf identifies the corresponding elliptic curve as exceptional and hexagonal.

**Scans 85–86, (35)–(39).** A second pinning supplies the labelling $u$ of $\underline J$. The text draws both isomorphisms in (35) and (36) towards the left. It identifies the rigidified moduli problem with sections of $U_{0,3}$ and $\mu_4^*$, and gives their product as the representing scheme. Over this base the square $Q_{0S}$ is trivial. The universal line in (38) is canonically identified with the indicated $\underline O(1)$ in (39).

**Scans 87–88, (40)–(44).** The $\mu_4$-torsor $\mathcal L_{E/S}$ of Legendre pinnings is compared with a second torsor $Q_{E/S}$. The sections of the latter are isomorphisms to the untwisted line whose squares come from a section of $\underline\omega_{\underline J}$. Its vertices modulo $\mu_2$ give the diagonal cover in (42). Contracting $Q_{E/S}$ with the fixed square torsor gives $\mathcal L_{E/S}$ in (43). Formula (44) records the resulting quadratic torsor and the text remarks on the choice of convention for later comparison with level-four Jacobi pinnings.

**Scans 89–90, (45)–(50).** The two pinnings give an action of the product of the permutation group on three letters and $\mu_4$. Equation (47) gives its action on each factor of the modular scheme. The action on $\mu_4^*$ has mixed character $\operatorname{sg}(g)\lambda^2$. Its kernel is the fibre product of (48). After choosing $i=\sqrt{-1}$, (49) gives a constant group and (50) describes it as an extension of $D_3$ by $\{\pm1\}$. The final sentence starts a further distinction and continues beyond scan 90.

## Slips

- **Scan 83, opening sentence.** The surviving words give « celle d'une […] couple ». The crossed noun is « paire » and its replacement is « couple ». An article consistent with the replacement would be « d'un ». The isolated final e of « une » is visible on the enlarged line. **Low confidence** in assigning a slip because the author was revising the noun.

## Remarks of the model

**M23 · Scan 80, (22)–(23). Model remark, low confidence in a proposed correction.** Read the marginal note and the contracted product in (23) as scalar twists. Over a field splitting both torsors these twists preserve rank. For a free module of rank two the left side of (23) then has four basis tensors and the displayed right side has two basis vectors. A possible intended right side is $(M^{\otimes2})^\flat$. The glyph for the operation in (22) is reworked, and the pages give no general explanation of a missing power.

**M49 · Scan 90, (48)–(50). Model remark, high confidence for the constant group of (49).** With $\lambda=i^r$, the condition on a pair says that the parity of $r$ equals the parity of the permutation. Enumerating all six permutations and the four possible exponents gives twelve compatible pairs. Each permutation has two lifts. The two above the identity have $r=0,2$, agreeing with the displayed kernel $\{\pm1\}$.

**Reading questions.** On scan 81 beside (25), the outgoing shaft at the outer edge has no readable terminal object. On scan 82, the last visible sign in (31) is $\simeq$, with no further term on that line. On 89 the inserted direction is read « à g. » with « à d. » as the alternative. Its one small loop and dot leave the handedness of the action open. None of the interpretations above requires choosing a direction there.
