## Legibility

The prose on 86–91 is clearest at the beginnings of words. Endings, dots and short words often reduce to a single stroke. On 92–94 the hardest distinctions are a prime on a generator versus a prime on its index, a retraced exponent versus a cancellation, and a comma versus a low digit. The title and count on 95 are legible in two different weights of writing. The faint strokes on 97 and the intersections, corrections and cropped lower edges of 96 and 98 are the least secure parts of the drawings. The TikZ paths record my reading of their geometry. A faint segment that passes across other lines can have more than one plausible endpoint.

The looped indexed group capital and the E-like signs have deliberate first-occurrence alternatives. The witness often renders the former as Gothic S. Several vertical strokes interpreted in the frozen text as bars around E could instead form the name consisting of a double-struck E followed by l. Where only a part of that name was marked, the witness comparison was confined to that part. The later mathematical interpretation does not determine the alphabet of the handwriting.
