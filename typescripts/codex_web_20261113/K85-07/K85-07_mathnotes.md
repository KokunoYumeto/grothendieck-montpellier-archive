# K85-07 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 86–87.** Starting from a free product, the writer packages the conjugates of a homomorphism from γ into a single map from a free product of copies of γ indexed by the acting group. His half-marked multiplication represents the resulting semidirect product. In the case with subscript 3 he imposes commutation relations, writes a free product of three copies of γ² and maps the result to automorphism groups. He draws both profinite and discrete versions.

**Scans 88–89.** The discrete signs sit inside the profinite units. Reduction through the units modulo 6 and modulo 3 gives a map back to the signs. The writer compares the induced maps on the larger groups, then forms Π(6) by imposing sixth-power relations. He gives a factorization for the resulting action and applies it to a finite group whose elements have order dividing 6.

**Scans 90–91.** Six operations indexed by permutations are conjugates of one involution. The relations include commuting pairs. The tetrahedral example has 96 systems, eight signed classes, and four classes after passage to the larger automorphism action [C90]. The following sheet replaces 6 by an exponent bound N and lists examples of the resulting unit groups [C91].

**Scans 92–94.** The writer follows individual generators through transformations of triples, then works in additive coordinates. The tetrahedron drawings on 93–94 label faces, vertices and edges beside signed triples of types (3,3,2) and (3,3,3). Some third coordinates and a case distinction are open. Scan 94 returns to conjugation and commutation between indexed operations.

**Scans 95–97.** A new title sheet introduces subgroup classes. The next sheet sorts subgroups using triangles, squares, points and pentagons of a five-element set. It combines multiplicities and redraws inclusions several times [C96]. Scan 97 has geometric sketches with retraced edges and no legible group labels.

**Scan 98.** A further subgroup diagram stands beside counts of conjugacy classes for the ambient group and six smaller groups [C98]. A separate calculation identifies a centre, centralizers and normalizers and singles out a normal subgroup of index 6 [N98].
