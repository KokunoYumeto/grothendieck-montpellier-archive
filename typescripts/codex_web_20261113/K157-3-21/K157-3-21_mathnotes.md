# Mathematical notes on scans 312–326

## What the pages do

**U01 · Proposition 20, scans 312–314.** For $\mathcal M=\mathrm{Cat}$ and a fundamental localizer $W$ satisfying $W(7\ \mathrm{bis})$, Grothendieck gives an “intrinsèque” condition on a commutative square $Q$. The map $\varphi_Q$ from the construction drawn over the integral sign to $X_3$ must belong to $W$. The wording of the property tested by this condition changes between the statement and the end of the proof; see L01 below.

**U02 · Reduction to Dwyer immersions, scans 313–314.** He factors $S\to X_1$ through $\widetilde X_1$ by an open Dwyer immersion followed by a map in $W$. A comparison of squares gives a triangular diagram and an appeal to an equivalence on each fibre. Exchanging the two indices gives the corresponding reduction on the other leg. For the resulting square $Q'$, he writes $\varphi_Q=\pi\varphi_{Q'}$ with $\varphi_{Q'}\in W$ by a cited corollary. This compares the intrinsic map with the map from $X_1\amalg_S X_2$ to $X_3$.

**U03 · The cubical proposition, scan 315.** The appendix takes a map of squares with $W$-cocartesian upper and lower faces. Condition $a$ says $f_0\in W$, $b$ says $f_1,f_2\in W$, and $c$ says $f_3\in W$. The proposition states $a\mathbin{\&}b\Rightarrow c$ and $b\mathbin{\&}c\Rightarrow a$. It adds $a\mathbin{\&}c\Rightarrow b$ when $W$ is stable under direct factors.

**U04 · Passage to cofibres, scans 316–317.** After a functorial factorisation he considers a map $g:S\to T$ in $\mathrm{Cof}_W$, its mapping cone $Z(g)$, and the map $\alpha(g):T\to Z(g)$. Doing this to the four vertical arrows produces a third square with vertices $Z_i=Z(f_i)$. Membership of $f_i$ in $W$ becomes $W$-asphericity of $Z_i$. The primed conditions $a',b',c'$ express the same three implications using these four objects.

**U05 · The cofibres form a square of the required kind, scans 318–319.** The lemma says that the square $Z$ of cofibres in the stated cube is itself $W$-cocartesian. A portion of an infinite “déploiement” displays three successive comparison objects $U,U',U''$. The first two face conditions are expressed by asphericity of $U$ and $U'$. Their connecting map is $\mu$, and the author identifies its homotopy cofibre with $U''$ to conclude that the third face has the required property.

**U06 · Direct implication, converse and retraction, scans 319–322.** With the lower square reduced to the constant square on the final object $e$, he proves the first implication using the direct part of $W(7\ \mathrm{bis})$ and the second using its inverse part. For the third, he factors $i_1,i_2$ and changes base along $Z_0\to e$. In the case $Z_0=e$, the retraction of $Z_1$ onto $Z_0$ gives one of $Z_3$ onto $Z_2$. The direct-factor hypothesis applies to $Z_2$ and, by symmetry, to $Z_1$.

**U07 · The general theorem, scans 322–324.** He restates the implications for a category of models $(\mathcal M,W)$ with a theory of homotopy cofibres and a map between $W$-cocartesian squares. The first implication is stated generally. With a final object, the second is equivalent to the axiom he calls conservative. For the third he assumes that a direct factor of a $W$-aspherical object is $W$-aspherical and adds the final-object assumption above the line.

**U08 · Cons. 1 and Cons. 2, scan 323.** The named conservative axiom reads $j_1\in W\Rightarrow i_1\in W$ in the indicated square. The paragraph gives the equivalences for both pairs of opposite edges. Cons. 1 tests a cofibration $f$ by asphericity of the fourth object $U$ of a square whose third vertex is $e$. Cons. 2 says that, with $i_1,i_2\in\mathrm{Cof}_W$, asphericity of $Z_1,Z_2,Z_3$ implies asphericity of $Z_0$. The source calls the square in Cons. 2 “cartésien”.

**U09 · The corollaries, scans 324–325.** Corollary 1 formulates the third implication as a condition on a single square: asphericity of $Z_0,Z_3$ should imply that of the other two vertices. It gives the form with $Z_0=e$ when there is a final object. Corollary 2 combines conservativity, the direct-factor condition for aspherical objects, and the existence of a final object. Under these hypotheses any two of the three conditions on p. 272 imply the third.

**U10 · Contents sheet, scan 326.** Item I announces counterexamples concerning open $W_\infty$-aspherical immersions and $W_\infty$-cofibrations. Item II proposes describing the recollement of an open and a closed part as a $W_\infty$-amalgamated sum. The question mark belongs to this second entry.

## Statements left without proof

- **B01 · Scans 314–315.** “on sait que $\varphi_{Q'}\in W$ (cor. 3 p. 149)” and “On a vu (prop. 6 p. 78) que … $W(7\ \mathrm{bis})$ implique $W(7)$”. Each is used as an earlier result.
- **B02 · Scan 318.** “Ce lemme résulte de la théorie du ‘déploiement des diagrammes cubiques’ … qu'il me faudra bien écrire !” Scan 319 develops the portion needed for the lemma. The construction of the general theory is deferred.
- **B03 · Scans 323–324.** “Cet axiome est équivalent à la conjonction des deux axiomes” introduces Cons. 1 and Cons. 2. Corollary 1 likewise says “équivaut à chacun [or chacune] des deux … axiomes”. The pages give the formulations and a marginal pointer back to p. 78. They do not give separate demonstrations of all these equivalences.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 316.** “C'est un peu vif, car il n'est pas clair que … soit $\in W$, sans utiliser déjà …” The question is whether the reduction by factorisation already uses part of $W(7)$. The two amalgamated sums and the implication $f_0,f_1,f_2\in W\Rightarrow f_3\in W$ are clearer than the rest of this passage.
- **Scans 318 and 322.** “qu'il me faudra bien écrire !” records the projected theory. In the asterisked note the author qualifies his ability to verify its existence for $(\mathrm{Cat},W)$ by the weak form of $W(7)$. In the other margin he writes “à vérifier que la factorisation suffit, $=$ si tous les objets ne sont pas fibrants sur $e$”.
- **Scan 326.** “Recollement d'un ouvert et d'un fermé … (?)”. This sheet poses the question as a heading and supplies no examples or argument for it.

## Slips

- **L01 · Scan 312, lines 13–16.** The proposition says “$W$-cartésien”. Its opening context on 312 and the concluding phrase on 314 say “$W$-cocartésien”. The latter is the apparent intended form. Confidence in that interpretation is medium.
- **L02 · Scans 315–316, upper face of the cubes.** The front vertex reads most like $X_2$, where $X_1$ would match the vertical $f_1$ and lower vertex $Y_1$. The index itself is doubtful, so a slip is only a low-confidence possibility.

## Remarks of the model

- **M01 · High confidence, scans 315 and 325.** For the three propositions $a,b,c$, the three displayed implications together exclude exactly the truth assignments with two true propositions. I checked all eight assignments. This accounts for the wording “deux parmi les conditions … entraîne la troisième” independently of the categorical steps that establish the individual implications.
- **M02 · High confidence, scan 314.** The map through $X_1\amalg_S X_2$ and the single diagonal in the diagram have the same endpoints. This can be checked from the figure even with the alphabet of the source letter left open. The order $\pi\varphi_{Q'}$ in the adjacent equality agrees with that path.
- **M03 · High confidence, scans 321–322.** The retraction argument uses the actual cocartesian square that the author specified at the top of 321. In that square, the retraction onto $Z_0=e$ induces the indicated retraction onto $Z_2$ by the pushout property.
- **R01 · Reading question, low confidence, scan 313, lines 7–11.** The frozen reading gives $\widetilde Q\to Q$ above the triangle, but its upper horizontal arrow runs from the object indexed by $Q$ to that indexed by $\widetilde Q$. Reversing the latter arrow or interchanging its subscripts would align the two orders. The text here does not establish that either change was meant.
- **R02 · Reading question, low confidence, scan 323, lines 17–19.** The frozen word in Cons. 2 is “cartésien”. “cocartésien” would echo the preceding named axiom and the first condition. The crop supports the shorter word, so the longer one remains a question about intention.
