# Mathematical notes on scans 227–242

References to an author's page below are to his handwritten numbering. The two checked comparisons and the conditional square calculation are identified by M1–M3. Their premises are B1–B3 in `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv`.

## What the pages do

**227–228. The localisers.** The résumé starts with « les conditions W(1,2,3) sans plus ». Its first fork places the right and left classes above the class written simply as $W_S$. On the smooth and proper families the two displayed chains exchange the roles of $d$ and $g$. On the $W$-fibrés the fine, right, left and universal forms coincide. The separate comparison with $W_S$ is where the additional axiom enters. The ruled clauses distinguish $W(4\mathrm b)$ on the fibrés and $W(4\mathrm b')$ on the parfaits.

**228–230. The categories over $S$.** In section 2 the author changes which families to use where the older diagram referred to $W_\infty$. The diagram on 229 puts the fibrations on the left, the cofibrations on the right and the comparison through Parf in the middle. The caption calls the squares cartesian and the hooked functors « pl. fid. ». On 230 he names the five comparison functors in the order Φ, Ψ, Φ₀, Ψ₀, C. The NB on 229 explains that *faibles* is his newer term for the fibrations in question.

**230–232. Four localisations.** On 230 he defines the categories with superscripts *lc* and *lc!* by fine and coarse localisation of the $W$-fibrés. The inclusion of the fine class into the coarse one gives λ. On 231 localisation of categories over $S$ by the right and left classes gives the two lateral categories. The author revises the condition on a full subcategory several times. In particular, the possible exception for a functor with source Parf matters for fine localisation. Diagram (*) on 232 puts $i,j$ on the two upper edges and ρ, σ on the lower ones, with λ between the upper and lower vertices. See M1 below.

**233–234. What the comparison functors supply.** Under circled (a) the natural links to Φ and Ψ are assigned the right and left classes respectively. Under (b), $C(X)$ takes values in the perfect family and the link $i_X$ is assigned to $W_S$. The stronger fine assertion is crossed out. The square at the foot of 233 compares the restrictions of $C(f)$ and $f$. The next sheet warns against asserting a fine conclusion for $C(f)$ without a further axiom. This is the reason given for the uncertainty about the fine localisations of intermediate full subcategories.

**235–237. The stronger axiom and adjunction.** Assuming $W(4\mathrm b)$, the author makes the fine assertion about $i_X$ for a $W$-fibré $X$. He calls λ « le foncteur identique » and writes the equality of the categories with superscripts *lc* and *lc!* (B1). He states the two adjunctions $(\rho,i)$, $(\sigma,j)$ and the full faithfulness of $i,j$. The boxed comparison on 236 has $CY$ in the two lateral Hom sets and $Y$ in the coarse one. The corollary on 237 specialises to $Y$ a $W$-fibré, using $i_Y$ in the universal class (B2). The short note at upper right explains what a Hom with subscript Σ means.

**237–239. Weaker variants.** Section 5 asks what survives if equality of the fine and coarse classes is known only on the perfect family. The author cannot establish the equivalence of λ there. He considers a test using the derivator of modules, then sets it aside. He next proposes a condition on a cartesian functor between categories fibrées over the same base: if its total functor is in $W$, its fibre functors should be in $W$, with the stated $W$-base-change assumption. He discusses the cocartesian version, changing to the opposite base, and a possible conclusion for the saturation of $W$. The later marginal comparison on 237 adds the axiom of limits to its assertion.

**240–241. Perfect and bifibred categories.** The *Question* asks whether the inclusions from Parf into the smooth and proper families give equivalences after fine localisation. A further question concerns Bifib, where an object is both Cat-fibrée and Cat-cofibrée. Even essential surjectivity would say that all the relative locally constant homotopy types in question can be obtained this way. The author asks for a functor playing the two roles of Φ and Ψ simultaneously. Across the page turn he considers what Φ does to proper objects.

**241–242. An attempted counterexample.** For base Δ¹ the author first considers a closed subcategory and its complementary inclusion. He strikes a block that claims to produce a counterexample. The live calculation alongside it identifies the two slice expressions with $X$ and $X_1$. On 242 he reformulates the question using a local coisomorphism and inverse image. A proposed example with Δ¹ inside Δ² is also cancelled. The surviving final-object square has $j$ at the top, two upward $W$ arrows and a lower arrow marked with a tilde (B3). It yields « d'où j ∈ W ». The last line begins a new search.

## Questions and conjectures

- **227 and 230–232.** The opening asks « quel est l'axiome mettant en relation » a derivator and a fundamental localiser. The fine-localisation comparison excepts « peut-être » the arrows out of Parf. The four-category diagram only « semble épuiser » the natural comparison functors.
- **234.** « je ne peux rien dire sur les propriétés de fidélité » of the functor from the fine localisation of Parf to that of Fib. The possible range of other fine localisations depends on this issue.
- **237–238.** « Que reste-t-il de toutes ces belles choses » under the weak axiom? Of λ he writes « je doute que … ce soit le cas ». The proposed test names Der(k-Mod), with the integers and, in an insertion, ℤ/pℤ or ℚ. « Je ne vais pas le faire maintenant. »
- **238–239.** The « tout ou rien » expectation concerns four canonical categories or only three. The next sheet tries a weaker criterion and says « Sauf erreur ». Its expected strong consequence is explicitly allowed to involve $\overline{W}$, the saturation, in place of $W$ itself. The old and new versions of the primed axioms are distinguished in that discussion.
- **240.** « induisent-elles … des équivalences pour les localisés fins … ? » covers both arms of the fork out of Parf. « Même question » introduces a variant of the Parf subscript and then the inclusion from Bifib. « Je ne me rends pas compte » introduces the question of essential surjectivity.
- **241–242.** « Je n'ai pas même vérifié » refers to Φ and properness. « Si c'était pourtant vrai » starts a conditional about the essential image. The cancelled text asks « est-il encore propre ? ». In the live text the question becomes whether specified maps between slices of a cofibrée always lie in $W$. The answer for the final-object square restricts the kind of counterexample still sought.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High confidence.** In (*) on 232, the routes through $i$ then ρ and through $j$ then σ start at *lc* and end at *lc!*, just as λ does. The text describes the diagram as commutative. On 235, after B1 identifies these vertices, the corresponding two pairs of arrows return to the starting vertex. This checks the placement of the arrows and the role assigned to λ under the stated hypothesis.
- **M2 · High confidence.** In the boxed formula on 236 only the lateral Hom sets have $CY$ as second argument. The explicit $g$-calculation on 237 inserts $i_Y$ between the expressions with $Y$ and $CY$, then uses adjunction. The words « et itou » give the parallel $d$-case. This accounts for every change of argument between the box and the corollary under B2.
- **M3 · High confidence, conditional on the lower arrow belonging to $W$.** In the final square on 242 the two verticals have explicit $W$ labels and the lower arrow has a tilde. If that arrow is in $W$ and $W$ satisfies two-out-of-three, those labels and commutativity force the top arrow $j$ into $W$. A finite membership check gives the same result. The check concerns precisely this square. It supplies no further counterexample.
