# Mathematical notes · cote 63, scans 36–40 and 66–72

## What the pages do

**36 · Invariants and changes of coordinates.** Starting from the five coefficients of a plane cubic, the writer defines four β’s, two γ’s and Δ. A change of variables fixing the point at infinity gives transformation laws for the coefficients and weights for γ₄, γ₆ and Δ. The right-hand ovals connect these quantities to j. The double frame at the foot gives a curve depending on j.

**37 · Smoothness, the differential and the short equation.** The two partial derivatives locate the possible affine singularities. The writer gives two expressions for ω and works through its change of coordinates in the left margin. For characteristic different from 2 and 3, the equation has only a₄ and a₆. Three cases distinguish the generic invariant, 1728 and 0. They give square-, fourth- and sixth-power conditions, field extensions and automorphisms.

**38–39 · Small characteristics.** In characteristic 3 the split is between a₂ nonzero and zero. In the latter case translation of x remains available, and the parameter r satisfies a cubic condition. In characteristic 2 the split is between a₁ nonzero and zero. The former case involves the difference s²−s. The latter ends with three simultaneous equations for automorphisms of y²−y=x³.

**40 · Comparison over a field.** The writer counts forms with a fixed generic invariant by means of separable quadratic extensions, including one additional form. The second paragraph draws conclusions about extension degrees and automorphism groups for all invariants.

**66 · Weights, forms and periods.** The page changes from the weighted symbols x₂, y₃, g₄, g₆ to coordinates depending on ω. Differentiation gives formulas for dx and dy. The writer pairs ω with η=ωx, writes the periods using a lattice and gives series for x and y. The two formulas begun for γ₄ and γ₆ at the foot have no right-hand sides.

**67 · Two splittings.** The writer compares ω, η with ω, ω̄. The equation η+αω=βω̄ determines a two-period quotient for α. A marginal note identifies its denominator. The next line associates a 2-form π=αω² with the comparison and leads to the two questions below.

**68 · Integration of a representation.** This typescript leaf treats U(λ) as an integral of operators. Formula (9) tests the integral on a vector and a dual vector. Formula (10) relates convolution of measures to operator multiplication. The proof invokes continuity, a topology of compact convergence and Lebesgue–Fubini. The last sentence introduces a calculation on a following leaf.

**69, 71 · Evaluation of the η-periods.** Scan 69 evaluates an antiderivative between u and a translate of u, separates one summand and lets u tend to zero. Scan 71 writes the two sums in integer coordinates, extracts the exceptional pairs and expresses the period difference in terms of τ. The last line returns to α from scan 67. Two arrows on 71 carry previously written summands into empty places in subsequent lines.

**72, 70 · Inverses in a complete normed algebra.** In the typescript’s own order, page 6 on scan 72 gives a geometric series for an inverse and a unit ball contained in the invertible group. Proposition 5 concerns a convergent sequence of invertibles under two boundedness hypotheses, the second with commutativity. Page 7 on scan 70 treats the two cases through the product of an inverse sequence term with its limit.
