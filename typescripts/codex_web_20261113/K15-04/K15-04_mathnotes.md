# K15-04 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**103 · Maps of ringed spaces.** The typescript names the two maps associated with inverse and direct image of sheaves of rings, then uses $\theta$ to make a direct image into a module over the ring downstairs. It gives the maps $h_*$ for internal Hom and $t_*$ for tensor product. Extension of scalars defines $\Psi^*$ on modules. The sheet ends during the construction of the maps from the Hom adjunction.

**104 · A character group modulo roots of unity.** The manuscript identifies a rationalized $M^0$ with $P/U$, with $U$ the group of roots of unity. In the cancelled calculation a candidate map takes an $n$th root and passes to its class modulo $U$. The next paragraphs ask whether suitable higher powers reach the required groups.

**105 · Writing a function in local generators.** On the English page numbered 94, the argument passes to $\bar V$, expresses $\bar\alpha\bar y$ in the sections $\bar y_\nu$, and carries the equation back. Since $y$ is nonzero at $P$, (24) writes $\alpha$ as a sum of the ratios $y_\nu/y$ with local coefficients.

**106 · A proposed motivic group and its eigenvalues.** A conjectural description gives the compact part through $M_0\otimes\mathbb Q$ and displays a product with $\mathbb G_m$. It identifies $j$ and the square character. In the eigenvalue formulation, an algebraic integer is written $\lambda=p^{i/2}u$. The conditions concern a power of $u$, its field and its paired valuations. The desired conclusion concerns $\lambda^m$ on a semisimple motive of weight $i$.

**107 · Transition functions.** Sections $\psi_\mu$ without a common zero define a cover. The sheet compares the hyperplane bundle with the multiple $[nD]$ on the refined intersections $U^{(i)}\cap U_\mu$. The two handwritten systems of transition functions retain distinct left subscripts 1 and 2.

**108 · Totally real fields and imaginary quadratic extensions.** Part A tests a quadratic extension at the real embeddings by the sign of its discriminant. Part B passes between a generator $x$, the real quantities formed from $x,\bar x$, and membership in $\mathbb Q'(i)-\mathbb Q'$. For the reverse implication the writer takes $K=\mathbb Q(a,b)$. A small insertion expresses $x\bar x$ using two squares.

**108 · A weaker list of conditions.** Part C places the trace and the square of the difference beside conditions on membership in a field and total positivity of norm products. The return arrow out of the last condition carries a question mark.

**109 · The centralizer of an element.** Statement 2.8 gives generators of $G_x$ in terms of $T$, roots satisfying $\alpha(x)=1$ and Weyl-group representatives fixing $x$. The proof extracts the groups $G'$ and $T'$. Corollary 2.9 puts $T'$ in every maximal torus containing $x$. Remark 2.10 gives a diagonal element of $\mathrm{PSL}(2)$ whose centralizer need not be connected.

**110 · A valuation and its field of definition.** The manuscript draws the fields $K,L,K_0,L_0$ and constructs a group from elements with prescribed $\lambda\bar\lambda$ and integrality away from $p$. A prime over $p$ defines a valuation on $M$ and hence a homomorphism from a multiplicative group. Its decomposition group gives the field $L_0$, and $K_0=L_0\cap K$. The final paragraph describes the resulting local field data.

**111 · Restriction of sections.** The English page numbered 86 uses (14), (21) and (22) to obtain the dimension equality (23). An exact sequence identifies the restriction of $f_{m'}$ to the component $\mathscr E'_\nu$. For a higher multiple, the problem becomes finding a section that vanishes at $P_1$ and does not vanish at $P_2$. The sheet ends mid-argument.

**112 · The norm-one construction.** From $L_0/K_0$ the writer forms $U$ using a norm kernel, then takes a product down to $\mathbb Q$ to obtain $G'$. Its rational points are described by the field norm. A homomorphism $\varphi:G'\to G$ and a commutative square express the factorization of $v$. A final condition concerns lifting the specified $f_0$ to a rational point of $G'$.

**113 · A one-to-one map and an inductive cut.** Distinct values of a regular function give disjoint loci. Infinitely many preimages lead to a contradiction, then to equality of local rings and uniqueness of a preimage. The next paragraph chooses a hypersurface through $W$ and applies the induction assumption to the component cut out on $V$.

**114 · A Picard-group example.** The note reduces the desired example to the action of $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ on the Picard group of the ring of integers of $L$. It says the action is “par symétrie” and asks for a Picard group that is not a 2-group.

**115 · Two cohomological bounds.** The first exact sequence introduces a coherent sheaf $G$ supported on $\mathscr E'_\nu$. Its eventual $H^1$ vanishing gives (21). The second sequence passes through $O_V$ and gives the inequality labelled (22). These are the two labels used on the sheet numbered 86.

**117 · Two criteria for a real fibre functor.** With $U$ the kernel of $\epsilon$, Proposition 1 relates $U$ being a torus, primitivity of $\epsilon$, and existence of a normalized real fibre functor. Proposition 2 relates the factor $\mu_2$, the square condition on $\epsilon$, and nonexistence of such a functor. The marginal note qualifies the normalization on effective motives of weight zero.

**117 · The argument and the even subcategory.** The writer uses $\epsilon j(\lambda)=\lambda^2$, an alternating form on objects of odd degree, and a cohomology group of a compact real torus. The exponent in the vanishing statement remains doubtful. In even degrees the page writes $j=2j'$ and $\epsilon j'=\mathrm{id}$. The corollary gives a real fibre functor on the even subcategory.

**118 · Corrections to a separate group-prescheme text.** The cancelled French draft proposes relaxing a separation hypothesis when the fibres of $G$ are connected. Paragraph b) invokes examples of Raynaud for the general case. Paragraph c) states an immersion result for a monomorphism of finitely presented group preschemes when $G$ has multiplicative type. Two shorter instructions change $S$ to $S'$ and remove “lisse sur $k$”.

**119 · The case of Proposition 2.** The corollary describes $G^0$ as a product, identifies $\epsilon$ as a projection and describes the real tensor category using a characteristic cohomology class. It singles out an object of degree 1 and rank 2 whose generated subcategory is already real-twisted and on which $U^0$ acts trivially.

**119 · Polarizing structures in the favourable case.** The sheet describes the isomorphism classes of objects and the polarizing structures as torsors. The action is expressed through $\mathord{}_2U(\mathbb R)$. The map from $H^0(\mathscr G_{\mathbb R})$ to $\operatorname{Pol}(\mathscr G_{\mathbb R})$ is said to respect that action and hence to be bijective. The next sentence begins at the foot of the sheet.
