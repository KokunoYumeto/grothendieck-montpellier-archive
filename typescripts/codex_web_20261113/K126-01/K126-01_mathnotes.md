# Mathematical notes on cote 126, scans 002–016 and 018

## What the pages do

**Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, scan 002.** For a flat covering morphism with quasi-compactness, quasi-separatedness and affineness qualifications revised above the line, the first display identifies degree zero with sections and gives vanishing in other degrees. Corollary 1 restates the comparison for cohomology sheaves without the affineness hypothesis on \(X\). The argument uses compatibility with flat base change and passes to affine covers over which the morphism has sections.

**Corollary 2, scan 002, sideways in the margin.** A degree-zero comparison leads to a statement about sections after varying the base \(S'\). The text calls the resulting functor an fpqc sheaf and reduces the verification to Zariski coverings and faithfully flat affine coverings. The earlier wording of the functor is crossed out.

**Corollary 3, scans 002–003.** For a topology between Zariski and fpqc, the notes compare the cohomology of a quasi-coherent sheaf in the two topologies. Leray reduces the question to affine schemes. The Cartan spectral sequence reduces the positive-degree calculation to a covering family. Since the sheaf takes sums to products, the text passes from a finite family to a single faithfully flat morphism and invokes Theorem 1.

**Corollary 4 and its remarks, scans 003–004.** A spectral sequence relates the cohomology of \(S\) to Čech cohomology of \(S'/S\) with coefficients in the functor \(T\mapsto H^q(T,F\otimes_S T)\). The manuscript writes the low-degree sequence and allows a covering family in place of \(S'\). For affine \(S\) it then proposes vanishing for every \(p\) when \(q\ne0\). A question mark stands beside that assertion. The next display spells out the \(p=0\) kernel.

**An equivalence relation across a nilpotent thickening, scans 004–006.** A stable closed sub-prescheme \(X_0\subset X\) inherits a relation \(R_0\) from \(R\). The proposition asks for effectivity of \(R\) given effectivity of \(R_0\) and flat covering conditions on its projections. The proof passes to a square-zero ideal. With \(Y_0=X_0/R_0\), the identification \(R_0\simeq X_0\times_{Y_0}X_0\) provides gluing data for the ideal on \(X_0\). The descended quasi-coherent ideal on \(Y_0\) is named at the head of scan 006.

**The equalizer ring and lifting a function, scans 006–007.** Taking \(Y_0\) affine with ring \(A_0\), the text defines \(A\) as the equalizer of the two maps from functions on \(X\) to functions on \(R\). It identifies the kernel of \(A\to A_0\) and sets out to prove surjectivity. A function on \(Y_0\) gives \(f_0\) on \(X_0\). Its obstruction to lifting lies in a first cohomology group. The two projections have the same obstruction, so the author places the class in a kernel he says vanishes.

**Correcting the lift, scans 007–008.** A lift \(f\) can still have unequal images on \(R\). Their difference gives a 1-cocycle \(y\). Changing \(f\) by a section \(\lambda\) of the ideal leads to \(d(\lambda)=y\). The Čech \(H^1\) vanishing at the top of scan 008 supplies the correction and finishes the argument for surjectivity.

**Construction of the quotient, scan 008.** The author takes \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}A\). The diagram places \(Y_0,X_0,R_0\) beneath \(Y,X,R\). The ideal calculation identifies the closed base change. The last paragraph asserts flatness over \(Y\), sets \(P=X\times_Y X\) and concludes that \(R\to P\) is an isomorphism from its reduction over \(Y_0\).

**Title sheet, scan 009.** Its three lines group the fpqc topology, the coefficient groups of the spectral sequence and the application to equivalence relations.

**Proposition 1 and Corollary 1, scans 010–011.** Here \(C\) has a pretopology \(T\), and a full generating subcategory \(C'\) has a pretopology \(T'\). Conditions (a)–(c) concern covers by objects of \(C'\), fibre products and an alternating covering construction. For the topology they generate, the proposition characterizes a covering \(X_i\to X\) by a \(T\)-cover \(U_\alpha\to X\) and \(T'\)-covers \(U_{\alpha\beta}\to U_\alpha\) whose terms factor through some \(X_i\). The corollary gives the shorter test when \(X\) belongs to \(C'\).

**Verification of the covering axioms, scans 011, 014 and the opening of 012.** The proof begins with transitivity and saturation in one direction, then considers the axioms for the proposed families. On scan 014 the upper diagram treats base change and the lower diagram treats refinement of a composite covering. Condition (c) extracts families \(V_{\alpha\lambda}\to U_\alpha\). The opening of scan 012 supplies maps from their terms to \(X_{ij}\). The numbering attached to some of the axioms is doubtful.

**Corollary 2, scan 012.** A presheaf on \(C\) is a sheaf for the generated topology if it is a sheaf for \(T\) and restricts to a \(T'\)-sheaf on \(C'\). The slanted marginal proof considers the families for which the presheaf satisfies descent and invokes their closure as a topology.

**Proposition 2 and the sheaf test, scans 012–013 and 015.** Taking the Zariski pretopology and finite, jointly surjective flat families of affines, the author describes the resulting covering families. The sheaf test combines the Zariski sheaf condition with the equalizer for each faithfully flat map between affines. The text also lists faithfully flat quasi-compact maps and jointly covering families of flat open maps. Scan 015 returns to sums and products to reduce the test to a single morphism. The margin of 012 lists further classes of morphisms, ending with “étale”.

**Title sheet, scan 016.** “Descente fidèlement plate” is the whole foreground text apart from the archive folio.

**Factorization, scan 018.** Heading A announces an application to holomorphic étale coverings. Under B, a proposition treats a finite universally open morphism over a locally noetherian base. It counts geometric points of the fibre by \(d(y)\). With a qualification read as “loc. cste.” or “constant”, the author states a factorization through a finite surjective radicial map followed by a finite étale map. Every map over \(Y\) from \(X\) to an étale \(Y\)-scheme is said to factor uniquely through the intermediate object. The argument begins by restricting to a suitable open-and-closed part of \(Y\) on which \(d(y)\) is constant. The supplied selection ends there.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1. Model remark, medium confidence.** The obstruction group used on scan 007 is the \(p=0,q=1\) instance of the kernel written on scan 004, after replacing \(S'\to S\) by \(X_0\to Y_0\) and \(F\) by the descended ideal. This identifies which part of the questioned remark the lifting argument appears to use. The pages give stronger, all-\(p\) wording on scan 004.

The difference of the two pullbacks of \(f\) is cancelled by the opposite difference for \(\lambda\). The conditional sign check leaves the two possible ink readings intact.
- **R3. Model remark, high confidence about the argument order.** The \(V_{\alpha\lambda}\) chosen at the end of scan 014 are the ones mapped to \(X_{ij}\) at the top of 012. Separately, the interrupted “stables par” at the end of 013 joins “changement de base” at the beginning of 015 inside a cancellation. These two joins explain why following either proof requires moving between nonadjacent scans.
