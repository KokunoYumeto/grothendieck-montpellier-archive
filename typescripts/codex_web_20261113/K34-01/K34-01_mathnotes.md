# What the pages do

**Scans 18–19, integral representations.** Section 4 equips the automorphism group of a finite-type Z_ell-module with its profinite topology. It proves that the kernel of reduction modulo ell is pro-ell, then deduces finiteness and injectivity modulo ell for closed subgroups that are inverse limits of finite groups of order prime to ell.

**Scans 19–20, reduction of inertia.** The next corollaries restrict an inertia representation to an open subgroup so that it factors through an open subgroup of Z_ell(1), or through a commutative tame quotient. The bottom of 20 reformulates the reduction using a finite separable extension of K. The repeated corollary number and the cut lower-edge notation remain as written.

**Scan 21, rational representations.** Paragraph 4.10 invokes an invariant integral lattice in a continuous finite-dimensional Q_ell-representation of a compact group. This transfers the preceding finite-image consequences to rational representations.

**Scans 21–23, unipotence and algebraic closure.** Definition 5.1 distinguishes unipotence from unipotence after restriction to an open subgroup. Paragraphs 5.2–5.3 pass to the algebraic group generated by the image and relate quasi-unipotence to its identity component. The argument concludes on 23 by identifying a finite-index algebraic subgroup of the connected component with that component.

**Scans 23–24, invariant spaces and filtrations.** The long cancelled drafts examine independence of E^U from a suitable open subgroup U and construct a filtration from successive fixed spaces. The live text identifies the unipotent elements with the identity component in characteristic zero and the largest subgroup of pi acting unipotently. The cancelled argument and its revisions are part of the transcription, not silently discarded proof material.

**Scans 24–25, the finite quotient and splitting.** The notes use the vector-group structure of commutative unipotent groups and a commutator filtration. They invoke cohomology vanishing for the finite component group Gamma and assert a unique semidirect-product splitting and a natural Gamma action on E. The degree range and the uniqueness claim need the qualifications listed below.

**Scan 26, the quasi-abelian case.** A cancelled restatement returns to the largest unipotently acting subgroup and its fixed vectors. The live paragraph assumes Q_ell coefficients and commutative G°, identifies the compact image with a Z_ell-lattice, and relates its rank to the dimension of G°.

**Scan 27, recovery from quotient data.** The kernel N and the open subgroup U give a diagram of extensions inside the groups of points of G and G°. The notes describe scalar extension of U/N to recover the algebraic extension, then announce a universal extension when the ell-primary abelianization module of U is of finite type.

**Scans 28–29, the local case and a category description.** Paragraph 5.7 describes the abstract algebraic group through Gamma and its action on an ell-primary vector space. In the stated strictly local case the relevant character is declared trivial, leading to a product with either a zero or a one-dimensional vector group. The right margin of 29 formulates a projective-system and tensor-category version. A separate cancelled top paragraph on 29 records P-invariants.

**Scan 29, the finite residue-field draft.** The cancelled beginning of section 6 identifies the residue-field Galois group through Frobenius and records its q-power action on roots of unity. The proposed representation argument breaks off. The slanted margin asks to generalize the residue-field hypothesis to an infinite extension obtained by adjoining ell-power roots of unity.

**Scan 30, the first general criterion.** Proposition 6.1 treats an extension with solvable inertia-type subgroup and a finite-index commutator condition. Its proof passes to connected algebraic image groups and uses a torus/unipotent decomposition. Extensive revisions replace commutativity by solvability and reductive-part notation by a torus; the margin points to a new version at the end of the section.

**Scan 31, the arithmetic application.** Proposition 6.2 applies the criterion when adjoining ell-power roots of unity to the residue field gives an infinite extension. The argument reduces to the Z_ell(1) quotient. Corollary 6.3 claims essential triviality on inertia for an essentially abelian representation and points to II 1.12.

**Scans 32–33, the revised criterion.** The new Proposition 6.1 uses finite coinvariants of ell-primary abelianizations for every open subgroup. It studies the radical of the connected algebraic inertia image, reduces to its abelian quotient, and excludes a torus quotient. Corollary 6.2 concludes essential unipotence from essential solvability. Proposition 6.3 is only an unfinished heading about the monodromy group.
