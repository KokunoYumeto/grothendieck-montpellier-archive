# Mathematical notes · K136-14

## What the pages do

**The simplicial differential algebra (204, 206).** The handwritten notes build a component from divided-power variables and their differentials, subject to the sum of the variables being the base element and the sum of their differentials being zero. A homomorphism of base rings together with a scalar acts on the construction. A filtration counts the coefficient degree together with the number of differentials. Regularity of the target base element determines the scalar from the ring map.

**Endomorphisms over the divided-power polynomial ring (206, 208).** Specializing to S = k{T}, the notes introduce Gₖ, Hₖ and Ωₖ. They describe the intersection through (λ{T}−1)T = 0 and the coefficient conditions λ₀ = 1 and (i+1)λᵢ = 0. For torsion-free k this leaves the identity. Two diagrams compare the monoids and the augmented-algebra endomorphisms. Several object names were overwritten and the annotation of the first upward arrow is uncertain.

**The first two components of an endomorphism (210, 212).** The images under φ₀ provide coefficients τᵢ subject to a multiplication rule. The next component φ₁ is semilinear over φ₀. The notes study a map between two augmented differential algebras by writing ξᵢ = Aᵢ + Bᵢ dX₀. They record the multiplicative and differential conditions, then the additional condition imposed by the second augmentation β. The last displayed sum has positive indices on both variables and no written coefficients.

**Divided powers on a graded algebra (215).** Given a ring with a divided-power ideal and a module graded modulo two, the theorem forms the tensor product of the divided-power algebra on the even part and the exterior algebra on the odd part. It specifies a functorial structure on the augmented ideal and gives a universal property by restriction to the generators.

**Extension from the even ideal (217).** A list of data for A⁰+A¹ includes an alternating multiplication on A¹ and two containments involving J¹. The author writes down the usual divided-power identities on J⁰ with explicit coefficients cᵢⱼ and dᵢⱼ. The proposed extension to x+u, with u odd, is x⁽ⁱ⁾+x⁽ⁱ⁻¹⁾u. The subsequent tests distinguish strict commutation from the vanishing of twice a product of two odd parts.

**Two differentials (219).** The de Rham and Koszul constructions use opposite maps between the first symmetric and exterior powers of M. Both operators square to zero. The first lowers the symmetric degree and raises the exterior degree. The second reverses those changes. The two case displays identify the degree-zero term with A. The first discussion separates characteristic zero and positive characteristic; the second mentions free, then flat M.

**The finite-order lemmas in chapter IX (213, 211).** In the internal order of chapter IX, lemma 4.7.1 bounds the order of an automorphism acting trivially on n-torsion. Lemma 4.7.2 identifies a finite group of exponent two. The proof of 4.7.1 passes to an endomorphism ring and to cyclotomic fields to use the divisibility of u−1. Section 4.7.4 proves commutativity in the second lemma by reversing the product xy. The following remark treats a divisible commutative group in a topos.

**Fixed and toric parts (209, 207).** Section 5.1 passes from the finite thickenings Sₙ to a formal subtorus of the completed group. The next internal page identifies finite schemes with their formal completions in the situation under discussion. It defines the toric part inside the fixed part and describes the finite-level Tate systems. The labels (5.1.1)–(5.1.9) run across the two leaves.

**The good-reduction criterion (205).** An argument about extending a projection by the Néron property ends at the top. Corollary 5.10 characterizes good reduction of an abelian scheme over the function field of a trait through its pro-ℓ Barsotti–Tate group. The proof passes through semistable reduction and a Galois action. The last sentence continues beyond the supplied leaf.

**Stoia on Gorenstein loci (216, 218).** The typescript recalls a local Ext criterion, defines the fibrewise Gorenstein locus of a flat morphism and begins the proof that it is open in X by factoring the morphism using an immersion and a projection. Folio 4 resumes a complex and Tor argument for flatness. Its corollary gives an open locus in Y for proper flat morphisms.
