# K158-03 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Conditions on a monoid, scans 36–38.** Folio 15 bis, physically scan 37, lists four numbered conditions using φ and ψ. It introduces the relation given by uψ=vψ and the action on M₀. Its last line, with r=s+h, continues at the top of folio 16 on scan 36. That page argues from invertibility of a minimal element and a cancellation law to invertibility of all elements, then begins Proposition 3. Scan 38 separates a collision of powers from the case in which their exponents must agree. In the latter case the author forms a quotient and writes a map to ℤ using δ(f)=q−p.

**Corollary and theorem, scans 40 and 42.** Scan 40 first states a cofilteredness criterion for a monoid with a minimal object. It then considers a category with a least object e, the monoid $\operatorname{End}_X(e)$, and an action generated by φ. Scan 42 follows the alternatives. In the first, the quotient has a fundamental group mapping onto ℤ. In the second, the image in a monoid of epimorphisms is a group Γ. The author relates Γ to the fundamental group of the open subcategory of least objects and returns to cofilteredness.

**The administrative circular, scans 39, 41 and 43.** Jacques Roux describes how aid for school projects will be allocated. The Inspection Académique examines the files in December. The rector allocates a global sum in January, and a commission is to distribute it. These three copies interrupt the mathematical foliation.

**Finite monoids, scans 45–47.** Proposition 1 assumes a finite pseudo-cofiltered monoid with trivial enveloping group. The author seeks p with up=p for every u. Finiteness first gives an element that factors through every u. In a finite transformation representation, the image E₀ of that element is least. Restriction gives a homomorphism into Aut(E₀). Its triviality supplies the fixed-point identity, and the author concludes that the monoid is cofiltered. The earlier claim of uniqueness is crossed out.

**Attempted extension, scans 47–49.** A cancelled Proposition 2 asks for a result about a 1-connected finite pseudo-cofiltered category with a greatest object a. Its continuation tries to choose maps p_b from the same source e and relate them to the choice over a. The triangles and the diamond on scan 49 keep track of competing maps and composites. This draft has no surviving conclusion here.

**A corollary with a marginal objection, scan 50.** The author takes the open subcategory U of least objects and compares it with the full subcategory U₀ on e∈U. Proposition 1 is then applied to $\operatorname{End}_X(e)$. A star beside the claimed equivalence U₀→U and the nearby marginal correction qualify this argument. The concluding NB suggests weakening finiteness to the existence of a least object and finiteness of its endomorphism monoid.

**Least orbits and a local system, scans 51–52.** A new Proposition 1 sets F(x)=Hom(e,x). The author describes a least principal orbit Hₓ, its image Hᵧ=fHₓ, and the resulting cardinal inequality. Under the further hypotheses the induced maps between the Hₓ are bijective. With X also 1-connected, he describes the local system as constant and ends with fu=gu for u∈Hₑ and parallel maps f,g from e to x.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High confidence.** In the displayed calculation on scan 46, u(E₀) contains E₀ and is the image of the finite set E₀. It therefore equals E₀, and the restricted self-map is bijective. This checks the specific inference beside the dark marginal mark. The identification of that mark as a question remains uncertain.
- **R2 · High confidence.** In scans 45–47, the single identity up=p for every u supplies a common right equalizer for every pair u,v, since up=vp=p. This checks the passage from the identity to the cofilteredness of the one-object category.
- **R3 · Low confidence as an interpretation of the cancelled proof, scan 45.** A three-element example explains why uniqueness could fail. Take an identity 1 and two elements p,q, with xy=y whenever y is p or q. Both satisfy the fixed-point condition, and all three elements are idempotent, hence trivial in any group image. The computation checked all products and associativity. It supplies one possible reason for the author’s cancellation.
- **R4 · High confidence about the inclusion, scan 50.** Take the category of pointed sets with one object of size one and one of size two, and all basepoint-preserving maps between them. There are maps both ways, so both are least in the associated preorder. The singleton is initial and supplies cones. The two objects have different sizes and cannot be isomorphic. Inclusion of the full subcategory on the singleton therefore fails to be an equivalence. The example identifies a specific difficulty at the star. The category itself is cofiltered.

- **R4a · High confidence.** The inclusion used on scan 50 is fully faithful because the subcategory is full. It would be an equivalence if and only if every object of U were isomorphic to e. This is the condition missing from that step.
