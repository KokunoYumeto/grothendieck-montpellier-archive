# What the pages do

**Scans 100–101 — closing monodromy consequences.** The continuation drops a quasi-projectivity assumption in favour of finite type and separatedness, then gives Corollary 3.7 under conditions (i) and (ii) of 3.5: finite generation of the indicated cohomology groups and quasi-unipotent inertia action, with a compact-support variant. Remark 3.8 discusses characteristic zero, resolution of singularities, and the remaining hypothesis when n=1. Scan 101 isolates a purity criterion for the invariant part, treats curves, and states Corollary 3.9 with echelon 3 in general and echelon 2 for normal X.

**Scans 102–103 — a purity condition and its stated scope.** Section 4 defines cohomological purity for regular schemes through codimension-one local cohomology, gives an equivalent higher-direct-image vanishing formulation and a bounded-dimension version, and discusses strict localisations. The manuscript states the general purity conjecture, lists cases it regards as known, and adds stability under étale localisation and regular subschemes. These are the claims at the time of the notes; no present-day status is assessed here.

**Scans 103–104 — arbitrary codimension.** Proposition 4.2 gives the local-cohomology concentration in degree 2d and a canonical identification involving a negative tensor twist. The proof proceeds from the codimension-one case through a regular divisor and the displayed support-transitivity spectral sequence. It then refers elsewhere for the canonical isomorphism. The displayed subscripts and reference numbers are preserved, including the irregularities listed below.

**Scans 105–106 — relative cohomology and a class.** Corollary 4.3 introduces the notation G(d), states the long exact sequence for a regular closed subscheme, and relates the composite map to a canonical class γ(Y). In codimension one it identifies the class with the first Chern class of the displayed line bundle through the Kummer sequence. The argument uses the localisation spectral sequence and the section 1; compatibility statements are referred to SGA 5 IV. Remark 4.3.3 comments on the purity hypothesis used.

**Scan 107 — a normal-crossings complement.** With U the complement of a normal-crossings divisor, Proposition 4.5 describes R¹f_* as a coproduct of component contributions, identifies the higher direct images with exterior powers under purity, and writes a component-intersection formula. Several tensor expressions are visibly revised into parenthesised twists; the transcription preserves both states.

**Scans 108–109 — the degree-one map and a cancelled proof attempt.** The factorisation through each U_i constructs a map from the component contributions. A proposed Leray argument is cancelled across the lower part of 108 and upper part of 109. The live replacement restates the degree-one assertion using μ-coefficients and reduces it and the exterior-power assertion to the strictly local case. The uncertain coefficient and its cancelled superscript are not resolved by these notes.

**Scans 110–111 — induction and a rewritten exact sequence.** The degree-one assertion is referred to the tame fundamental group. For the higher-degree assertion the manuscript introduces U′ and D′_r and a relative exact sequence. The long lower calculation on 110 is cancelled; 111 writes the sequence again with Λ(-1) and describes the induction identifications of its terms.

**Scan 112 — vanishing of α_i.** The manuscript asserts that α_i is zero, uses the induction computation and the cup-product description, and relates the relevant divisor class to a class on the strictly local X. It then gives the short exact sequences (4.5.6). Preliminary and final abandoned calculations remain cancelled in the transcription.

**Scan 113 — comparison with exterior powers.** A degree-one decomposition yields the exterior-power exact sequence (4.5.7). Cup products give maps to (4.5.6). The text explicitly admits compatibility of the two exact sequences, says the second square seems to require verification, and finishes by the outer and middle vertical maps.

**Scans 114–115 — adjoining roots and base change.** Section 4.6 constructs X′ by adjoining m-th roots of local equations, introduces the divisors Δ_i, and records p*(D_i)=mΔ_i and U′=p⁻¹(U). It writes the base-change map and two diagrams whose bottom maps are m.id and m^p.id. The last sentence ends at a colon; its conclusion is outside these supplied pages.
