# Mathematical notes · cote 161-2, scans 66–79

## What the pages do

**Pointwise limits (066).** For a 2-representable theory $T$, the opening paragraph gives conditions on a diagram shape $\delta$ and a category $C$ under which limits in $T(C)$ are calculated “argument par argument”. It relates this calculation to the evaluation maps $r_C$ and to change of theory or of $C$.

**Functor categories and the square (066–067).** Exchanging the two arguments of an iterated Hom gives a formula for $T_R$ on a functor category. Specializing to sets and passing to an opposite indexing category gives the presheaf example. A square with two horizontal inclusions expresses a 2-cartesian assertion under a generation condition. The next sheet singles out $T'=\tau^I$ and goes no further.

**The case $d''=\varnothing$ (068).** For structures specified by projective limits, the map from $C$ to its presheaves induces a fully faithful map from $T(C)$. Its image is described by testing whether certain composites with the construction functors are representable. The bottom square expresses the version using a generating family of constructions.

**Recovering the representing category (069).** Write $S=T(\mathrm{Ens})$. The construction functors and the occurrence of $R^\circ$ in $S$ lead to a strictly full subcategory $\Sigma$. The test for membership in $\Sigma$ uses $\xi\in T(C)$ and a representable composite out of $C^\circ$. The conclusion identifies $\Sigma^\circ$ with $R$. A cancelled fourth item starts a new representability argument for a smaller class of categories.

**Three examples (070).** For empty shape sets, the examples use categories, groupoids and ordered categories. The groupoid example uses the fundamental groupoid of a category. The ordered example uses an associated preorder. The two marginal graphs distinguish making one arrow invertible from equating two parallel arrows. Each example describes the generating functors $I\to R$.

**Envelope and fractions for all small diagrams (071).** Pb 1 identifies the envelope by a chain of four Hom categories and interprets the objects of $I$ as a cogenerating family. Pb 2 starts from the ordinary category of fractions, imposes closure on $\Sigma$, then considers the infinite products needed for the localization to preserve limits. Separate paragraphs examine filtered systems and adjoining arrows.

**Cogenerators and presentations (072–073).** A theorem characterizes the representing categories by small strictly cogenerating families and gives a condition for a map from $I$ to be basic. The argument introduces a fully faithful category on the chosen objects. The remarks describe the successive operations of adding arrows, equating composites and inverting arrows. They give conditions on the sets of arrows defining quotients, then identify the limit-preserving set-valued functors with representables.

**Evaluation at sets (073).** The corollary describes a 2-equivalence by $T\mapsto T(\mathrm{Ens})$. To describe $T(C)$ in terms of $S$, the text interprets the functors on $S^\circ\times C^\circ$ that are representable in each argument separately. It also uses $S\simeq R^\circ$ to compare the limits and colimits available in $R$ and $S$.

**Adjoints and subtheories (073–074).** The author considers a functor $\varphi:R\to R'$ and a left adjoint $\psi$. He relates passage to fractions to full faithfulness of the adjoint, then translates the description of subtheories to a condition on a full subcategory $S'\subset S$. The inclusion $i$ has a left adjoint $j$, interpreted using a free structure. A bracket singles out the case where $C$ is a topos.

**Arbitrary $d'$ and its envelope (075–076).** Inside the envelope for all small limits, one first forms limits of the listed shapes from objects of $I$. If that step does not produce a closed subcategory, it is iterated. The auxiliary envelope $C\to\rho(C)$ is identified through set-valued functors. This supports the restriction and extension of functors used in Pb 1.

**Fractions in the prescribed class (076).** Pb 2 forms fractions in the larger envelope, then takes a strictly full subcategory containing the image of $C$ and closed under the required limits. The arrows $\alpha,\beta$ compare the functors out of the result with those that invert $\Sigma$. A marginal diagram tests whether the ordinary fractions already suffice.

**The tentative presentation theorem (076–077).** A small $d'$-cogenerating subcategory is described by a transfinite construction inside $R$. The next sheet tests a shorter presentation via a category of fractions of $\widetilde I'$. The diagram separates fullness, essential surjectivity and cogeneration. The proposed three steps are stated under conditions on the allowed diagrams.

**Finite limits and ind-objects (078).** For finite diagrams the four objects under comparison are $T$, $R$, $S$ and $\Sigma$. The lemma identifies $\mathrm{Ind}(\Sigma)$ with $S$ through the inclusion of representables. The corollary identifies objects of $\Sigma$ by preservation of filtered colimits by $\mathrm{Hom}(X,-)$. The sufficiency argument factors the identity of $X$ through a member of a filtered presentation and obtains a section.

**Finite presentation, restriction and a question (079).** The first displays reconstruct $\Sigma$, $R$ and $T$ from the objects of finite presentation. Two corollaries concern a theory on $\Delta_1$ coming from finite limits and the action of restriction on morphisms of theories. The last question changes the allowed shapes to finite discrete diagrams. It asks which full subcategories can give the same $S$ and reduces the search to subcategories of $\Sigma_0$ stable under finite sums.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Model · medium confidence.** The change from 068 to 078–079 can be read as a change in how to recognize the same distinguished kind of object. The early test composes with each construction and asks for representability. In the finite-limit case the corollary uses the single functor $\mathrm{Hom}(X,-)$ and filtered colimits. The two tests are stated in different hypotheses, so the comparison concerns their role in recovering $\Sigma$.
- **M2 · Model · high confidence.** The location of the question mark on 077 matters. The page first asserts cogeneration in each of two stages, then puts “(?)” after their composition. This echoes the warning on 071, “le fait d'être strictement cogénérateur n'est pas transitif”. The sheets give no general resolution at that point.
- **M3 · Model · medium confidence.** The last question singles out sums because the diagrams on 079 have just been restricted to *discrete* finite ones. The immediately preceding case used all finite diagrams. The text asks for every suitable $\Sigma$ after fixing $S$, whereas the previous corollary singled out the full category of finitely presented objects.
- **M4 · Model · low confidence, reading question.** At 075 L16 the frozen mark is <code>\unsure{I|i}</code> in “l'image essentielle de …”. An upright with a loop may also be an overwritten R or C. The explanatory phrase “de C par i” immediately above makes it difficult to decide whether the later position names a category or the embedding itself.

- **M5 · Model · low confidence, reading question.** At 076 L10 and L19 and 077 L15 and L17 the frozen reading is <code>m\textsuperscript{me}</code>. Another diplomatic rendering is the bare sign $=$. The short horizontal pairs look like the sign retained before $S$ in the final question on 079. The contexts favour the sense *même*, but the lines do not consistently show separate letters.
- **M6 · Model · low confidence, reading question.** At 075 L16 the frozen reading puts the short quoted group as “s.a” or “s.a. à”. A compressed “sont” is also possible when the line is read from the source functors to the following identification. The surviving strokes are too few to fix the middle letters.
