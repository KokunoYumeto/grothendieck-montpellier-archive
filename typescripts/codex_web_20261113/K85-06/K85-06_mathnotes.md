## What the pages do

**Scan 72.** A cancelled working sheet tests the triple $(5,5,3)$. A pentagon and chords accompany a half-angle calculation. The author writes a quadratic in a long-tailed letter, computes with $\alpha$ and its conjugate, takes the norms of $2-\alpha$, $1-\alpha$ and $3+2\alpha$, and returns to the radical in the quadratic formula. Several intermediate values have been overwritten.

**Scans 73–74.** For $(5,3,5)$ and $(3,5,5)$, the two angles give a half-difference of $2\pi/15$. An inequality is reduced to a comparison involving $\alpha\leq2/3$. Replacing the last 5 by $5'$ leads to a second test and the verdict “pas vrai !”. The next leaf records equality for $(5,5,5)$ and leaves the primed cases open. The precise trigonometric discrepancy is recorded below. (S73.angles, S73.alpha, S74.equality.)

**Scan 76.** For $n=2$, $G_0$ acts faithfully on $X$ and may have a kernel on $C$. The sheet expresses $\rho,\widetilde\rho,\sigma,\sigma'$ in terms of the two parameters and writes smoothness conditions for the relative curve $Q_C=Y$ and surface $Q_X$. The orders of $\rho_f=\tau_0\tau_1$ and $\rho_s=\tau_1\tau_2$ are then linked to $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$. (S76.def.)

**Scan 76.** The proposed description of an épinglé regular 2-polyhedron over $S$ uses a conic $Y$ and a map $G_0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(Y)$. Condition a) concerns the squares of the two rotations on every fibre. Condition b) concerns the non-collinearity of three sections. Under additional hypotheses the sheet gives a fixed-line condition b′), then a stable-divisor condition b″) on a smooth fibre. The left margin qualifies the hypotheses.

**Scan 77.** The reciprocal roots of $\zeta_i^2-\alpha_i\zeta_i+1=0$ parameterize the order calculation. The proposition separates $\alpha_i\ne\pm2$, $\alpha_i=-2$ and $\alpha_i=2\ne-2$, with a convention for $p$ in characteristic zero. The prime on the order symbol matters and is uncertain where it is first defined.

**Scans 77–78.** In characteristic zero the list has six entries, I, II, III, II′, III′ and IV. They correspond to the tetrahedron, octahedron, icosahedron, cube, dodecahedron and pentagram. In IV both orders are 5 and the two $\alpha_i$ are distinct roots of the same quadratic. The remark on fields of definition counts nine isomorphism types over a field containing a root. (R78.count.)

**Scans 78–79.** The proof outline passes to the real and complex situations and uses a positive invariant quadratic form to obtain a smooth conic. For the quotient of the projective line by the finite image of $G_0$, a stable divisor of degree two excludes the two-branch-point possibility and the dihedral case. The remaining ramification triples are $(2,3,3),(2,3,4),(2,3,5)$. The text relates these to the tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral groups, then invokes duality and a pentagram construction.

**Scans 80–81.** A crossed argument uses cellular maps on the sphere. The live continuation invokes the fundamental group of the sphere minus three points and obtains the bound $3\leq\nu_0,\nu_1\leq5$. A real symmetric matrix gives positivity restrictions. After duality the list of pairs is short. The text rules out $(4,4)$ and $(4,5)$ and, for $(5,5)$, keeps the roots whose sum is $-1$. (S80.matrix, R80.minors, R81.roots.)

**Scan 83.** Three underlined generators with product one define $\Pi_0$. The sheet introduces its profinite completion and interprets homomorphisms into $H$ by product-one triples, with $H$ finite in the profinite case. Surjectivity distinguishes the Schwartz systems. A right action, two rows involving inner and outer automorphisms, and passage to $H$-conjugacy classes prepare the enumeration. The first link of the profinite row carries a question mark. (S83.rel.)

**Scan 84.** A permutation sends a generator to the permuted generator for an even permutation and to its inverse for an odd one. Two triangles express the resulting maps. The sheet then lists three power operations. In each row two generator images are prescribed first. The remaining image is written as a product. Indexing the chosen pair by a two-element subset $I\subset\{0,1,2\}$ gives the intrinsic notation $\rho^I$. (R84.product.)

**Scan 85.** Conjugating a power operation by a permutation transports both $I$ and its exponent vector. The author uses this relation to compare the subgroups generated using one or all three choices of $I$. He describes an invariant subgroup, whose operations the margin calls “élémentaires”, and writes four semidirect-product formulas. A new construction takes the free product of two groups, maps it to the first factor, identifies the kernel as a normally generated subgroup, and inserts a canonical splitting. The page ends at “d'où”.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model, high confidence (R74.values).** The two labels on scan 74 agree algebraically because $1+(1+\alpha)/2=(3+\alpha)/2$. With the arguments actually written, the left side exceeds the right by $\sqrt5/2$. This explains how the line “On a égalité” can coexist with the displayed inequality.
- **Model, high confidence in the calculation (R78.count).** The nine types on scan 78 come from three single contributions and three double contributions. The arithmetic checks the count conditional on the stated existence and uniqueness.
- **Model, low confidence in any error attribution (S80.matrix, R80.minors).** The live entries of the matrix on scan 80 give leading minors $2$, $\beta_0(4-\beta_0)$ and $2\beta_0^2\beta_1(4-\beta_0-\beta_1)$. The factor $\beta_1$ helps explain why the lower bound on the second parameter matters in the following brace. Four earlier coefficients in the matrix are struck.
- **Model, high confidence in the algebra (R81.roots).** The explicit roots $(-1\pm\sqrt5)/2$ have sum $-1$, as in the final parenthesis on scan 81. One earlier radicand on that sheet still carries a doubt.
- **Model, high confidence in the reading (Q81.ink).** On scan 81, L2, the frozen transcription has $\nu_0,\nu_1>3$. An enlargement of the original ink shows a separate lower bar. Read $\nu_0,\nu_1\geq3$. The next display also includes 3. This was an unmarked transcription error at the freeze. The audited LaTeX preserves it under the rule limiting witness edits to marked places.
