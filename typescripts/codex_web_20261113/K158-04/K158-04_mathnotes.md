# K158-04 · Mathematical notes

The scan numbers below are those of cote 158. Quotations preserve abbreviations. Where a hypothesis is doubtful, its status is given with the statement.

## What the pages do

**053–054 · Cofilteredness.** Proposition 2 starts with a finite pseudo-cofiltering category $X$. It singles out the open subcategory $X_0$ of smallest objects and assumes that it is 1-connected. The conclusion is that $X$ is cofiltering. A corollary starts instead with the 0-connected objects of $X^\wedge$ and assumes they are 1-connected. Scan 054 gives its short argument by reference to Proposition 2 and an earlier observation.

**056–057 · Surjective endomorphisms.** With $E$ infinite, the writer puts $M=\operatorname{Ep}(E)$. He gives right cancellation and introduces minimal $w$ by the factorization $w=uw_u$ for every $u$. He describes them by their fibre cardinalities, relates them by permutations of $E$, and introduces $M_0^*$ through the equation $f\sigma=f$. The first paragraph on 057 uses these ingredients to reach the assertion that the enveloping group is trivial.

**057 · Injective endomorphisms.** Section II replaces the surjections by $M=\operatorname{Mon}(E)$. Its factorization is $w=w_u u$. Here the cardinal condition on a maximal element concerns the complement $E-w(E)$.

**058 · Intersections and components.** For a locally irreducible $X$, the relation $R(a,b)$ asks whether $X_{//a}\cap X_{//b}$ is nonempty. The page identifies its equivalence classes using $\operatorname{Ob}X\longrightarrow\pi_0(X)$. A marginal extension concerns finite intersections inside one connected component. The exact inequality on their number is doubtful. The last sentence gives a sufficient condition for 1-connectedness of $X$.

**059 · Cancelled surjection draft.** The page tests the order in which maps can be cancelled, tries a factorization through a minimal map and asks about the associated group. Its diagonal discussion of two minimal maps and a permutation is heavily struck. The direction of cancellation in the first line matters because the later line uses $uf=vf$.

**061 · Groupoids and diagrams.** The opening diagrams set families indexed by $s,s'$ beside arrows of groupoids. In the central phrase a new word, « champs », is written above a deletion. The lower sketches introduce two arrows $u_1,u_2$, an object $a$ mapping toward $b,c$, and a comparison between $\operatorname{Hom}(e,x)$ and components of slices. The sheet gives several constructions on the same form without a prose account identifying every arrow between them.

**062 · A proposed obstruction.** Four objects $B,C,\beta,\gamma$ enter a lemma about 1-connectedness. Four Hom sets are specified as nonempty. The next conditions are glossed as absence of a common source or a common target. A marginal « faux » calls for another hypothesis and the small diagram at the foot puts an extra point between the pairs.

**063 · A component computation.** A box sets $Z=e\backslash X$ and writes it as an amalgamation over $T$. Another box expresses $\pi_0(S_{/x})$ using $I(x)$, $J(x)$ and a two-element set labelled $\beta,\gamma$. The image $I(x)$ comes from maps out of $b$ and $c$. The line defining $J(x)$ repeats $J(x)$ on the right. Below it the page follows a class of a pair $(u,\alpha)$ into $Z$. A final diamond is accompanied by $X'\sim S^2$ and $X'\sim\Sigma^1Z$.

**064–066 · Pension circular.** A letter dated March 1991 announces an annual point value of 2,179 F and describes the CSG deduction. The continuation sets out exemptions, notification to the fund, and four payment dates for the 1991 quarters. The letter ends with the director’s title and signature.

**067 · Two suspensions.** The stacked diamonds label several squares cocartesian relative to $W$. They identify $T$ by a union and denote $e\backslash X$ by $\widetilde X$. The equations at the foot put $\widetilde X\sim\Sigma^1T$, $X'\sim\Sigma^1\widetilde X$ and $X'\sim\Sigma^2T$ side by side.

**068 · Geometry of the suspension.** A double cone has a hatched section and marked points. Alongside it are a pair $(X,e)$, a four-object diamond and products with $I$. Long pen strokes run through the sheet. The sketches carry no accompanying proof.

**069 · A sphere or a cohomological test.** Beginning with a 0-connected $X$ and an uncertain filtering hypothesis, the writer seeks a cofinal map $X\longrightarrow X'$ with $X'$ equivalent to $\mathbb S^2$ relative to $\mathbb W_\infty$, or at least with its second cohomology. A condition on $f^*$ with a given abelian group $k$ leads to a failure of total $W$-asphericity. Separately oriented personal notes occupy the bottom of the paper.

**070 · Diagrams over a base.** A square sends $X,Y$ to barred objects over $S$. A neighbouring triangle contains uncertain labels. The lower square has a base arrow $S'\longrightarrow S$ marked $w$. Beside it, a cohomology arrow runs from an expression at $S'$ to one at $S$, with a doubtful upper character on $H$. The last arrow is $S'\times T\longrightarrow S\times T$.

## Remarks of the model

- **R57 · My remark · high confidence.** The qualification that $w_u$ can be chosen minimal is used in the last group calculation on 057. Both factors called minimal there have trivial image. I rechecked that substitution under the stated assumptions. It does not establish the earlier existence or generation assertions.
- **R59 · My remark · low confidence in the scope of the crossed question.** A four-element example reproduces the failure of cancellation for a surjection to a two-element set. Swapping the two elements in one fibre leaves the map unchanged. The computation tests a case of the displayed question on 059. The long deletion makes its intended relationship to the endomorphism monoid uncertain.
- **R63 · My remark · medium confidence in the proposed reading of the argument.** On a two-element set, none of the four subsets equals its own complement. This small check isolates the problem with the last boxed line on 063. It gives no separate evidence about which letter the author intended.
- **R67 · My remark · medium confidence.** The order of the three equations at the bottom of 067 makes $T$ the common starting point for the two suspension comparisons. The intervening $\widetilde X$ is named explicitly in each of the two single-suspension lines.

**Reading question from the notes, 062, lemma at the foot (page-file line 72).** The frozen expression has $\beta\amalg\gamma$. One could also read the first strokes of the sign as a small triangle, $\Delta$. At full size the baseline joins a sloping stroke on the left and a short stem on the right. The oblique note uses a separately written product. **Low confidence** in an alternative reading of the sign in the lemma.
