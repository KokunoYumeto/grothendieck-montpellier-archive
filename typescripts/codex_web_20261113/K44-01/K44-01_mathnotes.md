## What the pages do

**Kernel and dimension bound (002, Th. 1–2).** A cycle gives a map from $\operatorname{Alb}(S)$ to a quotient of cycle groups on $X$. The programme identifies its set-theoretic kernel with $N(\zeta)$, where the parameter could also read $\xi$. It bounds the dimension of $\operatorname{Alb}(S)/N(\zeta)=P_\zeta$ by a number depending only on $X$ and adds the proposed bound $\tfrac12 b^{2i-1}(X)$ in brackets.

**The cycle quotient and its duality (002, Th. 3–4).** The programme gives the quotient of the groups with subscripts “alg” and “alb” a natural abelian-variety structure, denoted $P^i(X)$. It pairs two such varieties. The complementary exponent is overwritten. In dimension $2m-1$ it singles out $P^m(X)$ and proposes a polarisation from its canonical self-duality. A circled note qualifies Th. 4 as written.

**Graded completion (003, cancelled draft).** The sheet starts with a nonnegatively graded ring, its completed product, and a flatness assertion under hypotheses on the filtration and the ring. It compares $M\otimes_A\hat A$ with a product of graded pieces. Under the condition written “TF” it separates a finite-type part $M''$ and a part $M'$ bounded above in degree. It gives the completed sequence, two product identities and a description by sums, and ends with a claim of exactness and faithfulness on graded modules. The large cancelling strokes cross this whole argument.

**A pairing on a hyperplane section (004, Th. 5).** For two cycles on $X$ the statement takes their sections $z$ and $z'$ on $Y$ and assumes algebraic equivalence to zero there. Several insertions specify the pencil, fibres and codimensions. The pairing attributed to Néron–Tate on the resulting elements is set equal to $(-1)^m Z.Z'$. One marginal note treats vanishing outside $2i=2j=n$; the other points to fibrations and a local variant.

**Restriction and the fixed factor (004, Th. 6).** The programme describes the relation from $P^i(X)$ to $P^i(Y)$ as an isogeny if $2i\le n-1$. If $2i=n$, it identifies the fixed factor on $Y$ with the image from $X$. A final bracket adds surjectivity for $2i\ge n+1$. Between these clauses are cancelled attempts involving an equality, parity and a finite kernel.

**Two descriptions using roots and reflections (005, cancelled draft).** The first starts with a free abelian group $P$ and a finite generating set $\Delta$. It imposes conditions on signs and multiples and asks for reflections preserving the set. A form given by a sum over $\Delta$ is used to characterize the reflections. The next formulas introduce a dual vector and an integrality condition. The second description uses two groups, two sets and a bijection. At its foot two reflection formulas act on the respective sides, with the normalization $\langle\alpha',\alpha\rangle=2$ in the margin.

**Equivalences of cycles (006, Th. 7).** A three-line statement identifies numerical equivalence and $\tau$-equivalence for algebraic cycles on a nonsingular projective $X$. A name, read as Matsusaka, is the sole marginal annotation.

**Editorial terminology (007, “Commentaires”).** The typist records a rearrangement of preliminary graph lemmas, discusses “sommet pendant” and “point d'arrêt”, then explains the difficulty that a “copoids radiciel” need not be a “copoids” under the adopted definitions. Three proposed remedies are to introduce different names, interchange two meanings, or dispense with the special terminology. The “dactylo” aside carefully distinguishes “covocables” from “convocables”.

**Intermediate Jacobians in the letter (008).** Writing to Serre, the author associates $J^i(X)$ to algebraically trivial cycles. He names the endpoint varieties Pic and Alb, describes complementary $J$’s as dual, gives a dimension bound, and places a module of $J^i$ within the cohomology of $X$ as a quotient of a submodule. He describes a stronger statement he hopes to prove and gives a characteristic-zero Hodge version. A handwritten addition marks a related inclusion as conjectural.

**Positivity as a question about a correspondence (008–009).** In odd dimension the letter asks whether the divisor correspondence on $J^m$ gives a positive class. It describes a symmetric correspondence on the product, with a qualification about the factor 2. A marginal note invokes primitive decomposition. With parameter space $T$, a marked point $a$, and cycles $z$ on $T$ times $X$ satisfying $z(a)=0$, it forms $D=r_*(p^*(z)\mathbin{.}q^*(z))$. It passes to $A=\operatorname{Alb}^{o}(T)$ and divides by the kernel $N$ to obtain $J=A/N$. Positivity on this quotient is the open point. As a possible restriction of the question, Serre is offered $T=\operatorname{Pic}^{o}(Y)$ for $Y$ of codimension $m-1$ in $X$. The letter closes by thanking him for a copy of a letter to Ogg.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence.** In the notation of the letter, substituting $i=m$ and $n=2m-1$ into its legible $n+1-i$ gives $m$. This explains why the letter switches from complementary duality to self-duality in its odd-dimensional case. The recorded symbolic calculation checks only that substitution. It leaves the overwritten exponent on 002 open.
- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence.** The passage from a quotient of a submodule to a submodule is a genuine change in the strength of the claim the author wishes to make. The letter states the first form and puts the second in the future tense. The two are therefore distinguished in the summary above.
- **R3 · Model remark, medium confidence.** The repeated “tel quel” makes it likely that the margin on 008 explains the qualification attached to Th. 4 on 002. Both notes sit by the odd-dimensional positivity assertion. Only 008 explicitly supplies the words “décomposition primitive”.
