# Mathematical notes on scans 34–49

## What the pages do

**U1 · Conditions L1–L5 (34–35).** For $W_{\mathbb D}$, a functor detects membership by isomorphisms (L1). L2 concerns products with the interval. L3 starts from a final object and records the stronger requirement for aspherical categories. L4 tests slices at $y$. L5 tests a cartesian morphism over $S$ on its fibres.

**U2 · Additional conditions on $W$ (35).** La) requires $\pi_0(f)$ to be bijective and fails for the null derivator. Lb) c) concern finite sums and products under localization. A marginal note mentions Quillen. Ld) asks for passage to opposites.

**U3 · Chapter heading and outline (36–39).** Chapter XI names Hot-fibrations and proper and smooth functors. The outline on 39 adds aspherical and coaspherical functors. Its test for “fibrations parfaites” pulls back to an interval and examines both ends. Scans 37 and 38 contain printer output and a veterinary article.

**U4 · Théorème 1 (40).** The theorem compares preservation of Hot-equivalences in cartesian pullbacks, tests with a final object or slices, and a condition on locally constant coefficients and direct images after base change. Dual tests are crossed out. The diagram leaves $c\Rightarrow a$ to prove.

**U5 · Degree zero (40–41).** In a cartesian square with $p$ a Hot-equivalence, the author compares sections on $X$ and $X'$. Base change follows because the direct images are computed “fibre par fibre”. The written base of the coefficient is discussed under S1.

**U6 · Torsors and the gerbe of lifts (41–43).** The degree-zero argument gives full faithfulness on torsors. A cancelled local argument continues across the page turn. The live proof compares two exact sequences of pointed sets. After considering a neutral-obstruction test, Grothendieck obtains the required lift using the section categories of a locally constant gerbe.

**U7 · Abelian coefficients (43–44).** For the two Leray spectral sequences, he first compares $R^q f_*$ under base change and then compares cohomology on the bases. The isomorphism of initial terms gives the comparison on abutments.

**U8 · Definition and propositions (44).** Définition 1 names the Hot-fibrations. Proposition 1 records stability under base change and composition. Its final sentence repeats $f$. The inserted Proposition 2 makes $s\mapsto\mathrm{hot}(X_s)$ a local system in Hot. Its sideways continuation qualifies the canonicity of fibre isomorphisms.

**U9 · Théorème 2, sufficiency (44–46).** For a triangle of functors over $S$ whose legs are Hot-fibrations, $S_0$ meets every component. The proof tests locally constant sheaves on these fibres, then treats torsors locally on a base with a final object. Leray handles the abelian case.

**U10 · Théorème 2, necessity (46–48).** A lemma supplies a Hot-fibration $\bar S\to S$ with Hot-aspherical source when $S$ is 0-connected. The square on 47 passes the equivalence to $\bar f_{\bar s}$, which identifies with $f_s$. Surjectivity reaches every $s$. The proof at this point is conditional on the lemma.

**U11 · Path-category construction (48–49).** Infinite paths starting at $s_0$ form a fibre of the source functor. Grothendieck identifies this category in the factorization from VII and evaluates each path at its target. He questions whether the earlier result about the target map depends on HOT, and calls for a treatment of proper and smooth functors. Isomorphisms and product projections are his immediate examples.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, high confidence (46–47).** The square on 47 supplies a local check on the direction of the converse. Both fibre inclusions point from the bottom row to the top one. Given the three Hot-equivalences named in the prose and two-out-of-three for these equivalences, commutativity gives the claimed equivalence of the bottom arrow. This check concerns that square only.

**R2 · Model, high confidence (48–49).** The lemma uses the two ends of a path for different purposes. Fixing the source selects the category that should be Hot-aspherical. Evaluation at the target supplies its map to $S$. This distinction locates the author’s reservation on 49 precisely at the step invoking the earlier work on the target functor.

**R3 · Model, low confidence (49).** The lower-left addition may broaden the author's stock of examples beyond the product projections in the main text. “J'exagère” and “Kan” support that reading. The crossed words do not establish the exact class of functors he meant.
