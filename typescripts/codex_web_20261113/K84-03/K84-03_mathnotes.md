# Mathematical notes on scans 35–50

## What the pages do

**Scans 35 and 39 · Uniqueness in a quadratic algebra (U35a).** In a presentation \(A=O[u]/(u^2+bu+c)\), the proposed image of \(u\) is \(\bar u+\alpha\). Preservation of trace and norm gives \(2\alpha=0\) and \(\alpha(\alpha-b)=0\). The proof separates the complement of \(X_0=V(2)\) from a point \(s\) of \(X_0\) where the algebra is étale. The argument broken off at the bottom of scan 35 resumes at the top of scan 39 and ends with \(\alpha_s=0\).

**Scans 36–38 · The symmetry in a line (U37).** A totally nonisotropic line \(D\) defines a symmetry \(\sigma_D\). After choosing a generator \(e\) and normalizing \(Q(e)=1\), the map \(u\mapsto ue\) transports the canonical involution of the quadratic algebra to the quadratic module. The resulting formula is \(\sigma_e(x)=\varphi_Q(e,x)e-x\). The proposition on scan 36 characterizes the symmetry by its actions on \(D\) and \(E/D\), with an additional local condition whose wording is heavily revised.

**Scan 39 · The non-étale example (U39).** For \(A=\underline O[u]/(u^2)\) the note writes \(u\mapsto\lambda u+\alpha\), with \(\lambda\) a unit, \(2\alpha=0\) and \(\alpha^2=0\). In characteristic two the displayed restriction reduces to \(\alpha^2=0\). The two oblique notes return to whether the symmetry can be the identity.

**Scans 38, 40 and 41 · The normalizer and the torsor (U41).** Conjugation by \(\sigma_D\) acts on \(A\) by \(u\mapsto\bar u\). The calculation on scan 40 reduces this to the action of the canonical involution on multiplication operators. The author then defines \(M\) by \(\Theta u=\bar u\Theta\) and describes \(\Pi\) by a determinant or norm condition. He treats \(M\) as an invertible \(A\)-module and \(\Pi\) as a torsor under the norm-one group.

**Scans 41–42 · Equivariance (R42b).** Removing \(X'=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) from the projective bundle gives \(P^*\). The map \(f\) sends \(D\) to \(\sigma_D\); the group homomorphism governing this action sends \(u\) to \(u\bar u^{-1}\). The last line specializes it to \(u\mapsto u^2\) on the norm-one group.

**Scans 43–45 · A new set of notes (U44).** The musical-paper title on scan 43 names splittings, contracted products and generalized invariants. Scan 44 begins “Revêtements quadratiques”. For a quadratic algebra \(A\) it writes the dual module \(E\), a line \(L\), the trace \(T\), the norm \(N\), and the fibres \(P_\lambda\) of evaluation at 1. The split calculation gives \(T=x'+x''\), \(N=x'x''\) and \(\delta=(x'-x'')^2\). The warning about the sign accompanies the relation \(T^2-4N=\delta\). Scan 45 is blank.

**Scan 46 · The polynomial on the torsor (U46).** On \(P_1\) the polynomial is \((x-x')(x-x'')\). Its homogeneous extension to \(E\) is written \(Q(x)=x^2-\psi(x)Tx+\psi(x)^2N\) and restricts on \(L\) to \(x\mapsto x^2\). The proposed description of a quadratic cover uses a line \(L\), an \(L\)-torsor \(P_1\) and a quadratic polynomial whose degree-two part is squaring. Its zero scheme recovers the cover.

**Scan 47 · The polar map and étaleness (S47).** The polar form of \(N\) defines \(\varphi_N:A\to E\). The first box relates \(Q\circ\varphi_N\) to \(N\) and \(\delta\). Its sign is obscured. The author lists étaleness of \(X'/X\), invertibility of \(\varphi_N\), and nonvanishing of \(\delta\) as equivalent conditions. He also considers the case \(\delta=0\).

**Scans 47–48 · From \(x\) to \(z\) (U47b).** The map \(x\mapsto z=2x-T\) is invertible where 2 is a unit. The calculation \(Q(z+T)=z^2-\delta\) sends a zero of \(Q\) to a square root of \(\delta\). The involution sends \(x\) to \(T-x\) on \(P_1\) and to \(\psi(x)T-x\) on \(E\).

**Scan 48 · The congruence modulo 4 (U48b).** On \(X_0=V(2)\) the trace gives \(T_0\in\Gamma L_0\). The notation \(T_0^2\equiv\delta\ (4)\) refers to squaring a lift on \(X_1=V(4)\). Changing that lift by \(2\alpha\) leaves the square unchanged modulo 4. A section of the cover gives \(z^2=\delta\) and \(z_0=T_0\).

**Scan 49 · The functor of lifts (U49a, U49b).** The author introduces the functor of sections of \(L\) whose square is \(\delta\) and whose restriction is \(T_0\). It has an action by the square roots of unity that restrict to 1 on \(X_0\). He relates this action to the involution on \(X'\). With 2 regular on the base, he describes quadratic covers by \(L,\delta,T_0\) satisfying the congruence modulo 4.

**Scan 50 · Recovering a section (U50a).** Under the regularity hypothesis a compatible \(z\) determines \(x=(T+z)/2\). The equation \(z_0=T_0\) supplies divisibility by 2, and the author checks \(\psi(x)=1\) and \(Q(x)=0\).

**Scan 50 · Two systems of coordinates (U50b).** A local lift \(\tau\) of \(T_0\) gives a description of \(E\) by pairs. The first uses a coset of \(L\) in \(\tfrac12L\); doubling its second coordinate produces \(\zeta=2x-\psi(x)T\) with \(\zeta_0=\lambda_0T_0\). In the second system the projection extracts \(\lambda\) and \(T\) corresponds to \((2,0)\).

- **Scan 46 (U46).** “Equiv. de cat. fibrées” heads the equivalence between covers and triples. The zero-scheme construction is named, but a check of the equivalence on morphisms is absent. For the inclusion of the values of \(Q\) in \(L^{\otimes2}\), the reason is abbreviated to “argument ‘universel’”.
- **Scan 47 (S47).** The three items under “Conditions équivalentes” are listed together. “Vérification à partir du cas décomposé” is written beside the preceding box; the verification is not carried out there.
- **Scan 49 (U49a).** The margin calls the two functors “des faisceaux fpqc” and appears to say “pas repr.” under a local condition on 2. No argument follows. The qualification is partly crossed by the main writing.
- **Scan 49 (U49b).** “Supposons \(2\) … régulier. Alors la donnée des rev. quadratiques … équivaut à celle des” introduces the data \(L,\delta,T_0\). The next scan works out sections and coordinates. It does not construct and check an equivalence on arbitrary morphisms.

## Questions and conjectures

**Scan 50.** “Quelle condition à mettre sur \(z\) ?” The author answers by comparing \(T+z\equiv0\ (2)\) with \(z_0=T_0\), then uses \(z^2=\delta\) to check the quadratic equation.

**Scans 35–36.** “qu'il faut regarder à part, en un point \(s\in X_0\)” sets aside a local condition. The argument resumes on scan 39. The later NB “Cette dernière condition” is too heavily reworked to tell whether the author calls the condition automatic or questions its necessity. No distinct conjecture is legible.

## Remarks of the model

**Model remark R42 · Low confidence about the parenthetical reading.** Scan 42, first formula, has been transcribed as \(f(u.D)=u\bar u^{-1}f(D)\), with the addition \((=f(D)\bar u^2)\). The high 2 is distinct; beneath it the ink also admits a pair of short horizontal strokes with no identifiable \(u\). If the latter factor is literally \(\bar u^2\), the parenthesis needs a qualification. For the scalar \(u=2\) over \(\mathbb Q\), the first multiplier is 1 and that putative right multiplier is 4. This discrepancy belongs to the reading of the parenthesis.

**Model remark R42b · High confidence.** The last line of scan 42 is consistent with the stated group homomorphism. A norm-one unit satisfies \(\bar u=u^{-1}\), so \(u\bar u^{-1}=u^2\).

**Model reading question R48 · Low confidence.** Scan 48, lines 8–10, reads \(\psi(T)=T,\ \psi(N)=N\). The first sign could instead denote the involution acting on the trace and the norm. A return arrow joins these lines to the preservation of \(Q\) below them. Also, the evaluation map \(\psi:E\to O\) on scan 44 has \(\psi(T)=2\). This makes the identity of the handwritten operator worth leaving open in the commentary.

**Model remark U50a · High confidence for sections on the stated base.** Scan 50 uses regularity a second time after dividing \(T+z\) by 2. It allows \(4Q(x)=0\) to imply \(Q(x)=0\) in the invertible module \(L^{\otimes2}\). The note’s argument takes place over the base with the stated regularity hypothesis.
