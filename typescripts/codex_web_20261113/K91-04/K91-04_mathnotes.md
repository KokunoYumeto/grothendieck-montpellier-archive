# Mathematical notes on cote 91, scans 47–58

## What the pages do

**47–48, the open locus of freeness.** To make $F_1$ stable under multiplication by $B$, the author sets the coefficients $\varphi_{i\lambda\mu}$ equal to zero. For $M=F/F_1$, a tuple $\xi$ gives a map $u_\xi:B^k\to M$. Invertibility of its determinant tests whether the tuple is a basis. The union of the corresponding determinant opens is “l'ouvert cherché”. The rank written after “projectif de rang” is uncertain.

**48–49, preservation of finite type.** Under the stated reservations, the author claims that $f_*(g)$ is of finite type if $g$ is. He first takes $Y=T$. A multisection in a fibre is assumed to lie in an affine open. He works in $X^\nu$ and chooses finitely many affine $U_i$ for which the union of $U_i^\nu$ covers the same tuples as the union over all $U$. This reduces the discussion to an affine morphism. He ends with a Spec formula involving a locally free sheaf $F$. The marks around the $F$ in both Spec expressions remain doubtful.

**49, a dimension bound that fails.** The “Remarque” cautions against bounding the fibre dimension of $f_*(X/T)$ by the number of components times the fibre dimension of $X/T$ in general. For $S=\operatorname{Spec}(k)$, $T$ purely radicial over $S$, and $X=T\times_S T$, he identifies the functor with $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(T,T)$ and points to a positive-dimensional automorphism group. The particular algebra written and crossed out is uncertain.

**50–51, lifting and étaleness.** The proposed theorem has separate clauses for a “simple” morphism and an étale morphism, with revised hypotheses on $T/S$. A lift in diagram (A) is expressed as a lift in (B) after passing from $S'$ to $T'$. For the étale clause the remaining problem is finiteness of the geometric fibres. The author counts lifts of a given section on the finitely many connected components of $T'$. On a connected component, the lift is determined by the image of one point.

**51–52, Corollaire 1.** With $\nu$ the maximum number of absolute connected components of $T_s$, and $n$ the analogous maximum for $X/Y$, the bound written at the top of 52 is $n^\nu$. The exponent sits above $n$. Its two possible readings, $\nu$ and $v$, are recorded in the transcription. A cancelled corollary below it tries to formulate a different bound using suprema of dimensions.

**52–53, Corollaire 2.** For $T/S$ finite of degree $n$, $D(s)$ and $D'(s)$ collect dimensions of fibre components before and after applying $f_*$. The equality and the following pure-dimension claim are examined below as **S52**. The argument passes to an algebraically closed field, separates the components of $T$, and uses a factorization into an étale map and a polynomial projection. The last model is $f_*(T[t_1,\ldots,t_d]/T)$. **P53** is the displayed formula turning the coproduct of the $f_i$ into a product of their direct images.

**54, degree and four equivalent conditions.** Corollaire 1 bis assigns $\lambda^{\nu_s}$ to the fibre of the image of a proper étale map of degree $\lambda$. The four conditions in Corollaire 3 concern local constancy of $\nu(s)$, preservation of finite étale morphisms, a test on finite étale $T'$ over $T$, and a test using $f_*(T\amalg T)$. The arrows in the margin close the chain of implications.

**55–56, homogeneity, surjectivity and principal bundles.** The author calls a flat $T/S$ homogeneous when the component count is locally constant. He asserts stability under composition and base extension in the finite case. Corollaire 4 concerns preservation of surjectivity. A proposition and its finite-group example discuss direct images of principal bundles. The qualifier attached to “principaux” and parts of the final group hypotheses on 55 are difficult to read.

**56–57, trial construction for finite sets.** The case of a finite group leads the author to $\varphi(I)=f_*(T\times I/T)$ for a finite set $I$. In a cancelled argument he chooses a geometric point $a$, introduces a finite set $A_a(T)$ with $\pi_1(S,a)$-action, and proposes an étale object $\overline T$ and a map $T\to\overline T$. He restarts from $S'\mapsto\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(T,S')$ and records left exactness.

**57–58, the representing object.** The author fixes $a$ and describes a left-exact functor of finite $\pi_1(S,a)$-sets by an internal Hom from $A=A(T)$. He denotes the associated étale object by $f_*^{\mathrm{ét}}(T)$. Formula (*) compares two internal Hom objects. Taking sections gives an isomorphism of ordinary Hom sets, **H58**. The last line substitutes $S'=f_*^{\mathrm{ét}}(T)$ and continues beyond this selection.

## Slips

**S52 — which dimension is multiplied.** Scan 52, live Corollaire 2, reads $D(s)=nD'(s)$, with the prime clearly on the right. The definition on the preceding lines assigns $D'(s)$ to the direct image. The particular case ends in $nd$, and the last line of the calculation on 53 gives $nd$ again. In the pure-dimension case, the particular claim gives $nd$ for each component of a direct-image fibre, hence $D'(s)=\{nd\}$ when such a component exists. For instance, with $n=2$ and source dimension $1$, the direct-image dimension given by the particular case is $2$. The written equality would then identify $1$ with $4$. The full sets require more care, as **R52** records.

The overwritten exponent and comparison in the abandoned draft on 52 remain reading questions. They supply no separate error claim.

## Remarks of the model

- **R52 — model, high confidence for the split example.** The product formula **P53** tests a possible correction to **S52**. Take $T$ to be two points over an algebraically closed field, $Y=T$, and over each point take the disjoint union of an affine line and an affine plane. Component dimensions in the product are the sums $1+1,1+2,2+1,2+2$, giving $\{2,3,4\}$. Doubling $\{1,2\}$ gives $\{2,4\}$. If $nD$ means the set of scalar multiples $nx$ for $x\in D$, interchanging the primes still misses dimension $3$. If $nD$ means the repeated sum of the set with itself, this example gives exactly that sum. The manuscript gives no separate definition of $nD$ here.
- **R58 — model, high confidence for the direction of the map.** In **H58**, take $S'=f_*^{\mathrm{ét}}(T)$. The identity on the left then corresponds to an $S$-map $T\to f_*^{\mathrm{ét}}(T)$ on the right. The source and target follow from the two ordinary Hom terms. At the foot of 58 the transcription reads “on trouve” with the alternative “un hom”. A tall stroke at the start of the second group and a low return at its end also allow the abbreviated noun.

- **Reading question, scan 51, lower diagram beside “à prouver”.** The frozen reading has a thick downward arrow from $f_*(X)$ to $f_*(Y)$. The bottom head has two distinct arms. At the upper end a small point projects on both sides of the shaft. It could be a second, upward head or the end of a retraced stroke. Confidence in a second head is low.
