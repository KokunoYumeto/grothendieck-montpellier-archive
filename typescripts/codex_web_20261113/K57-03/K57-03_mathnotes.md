Line numbers refer to the individual scan files. French quotations retain the wording of the transcription. The word *simple* is left visible where the author applies it to a scheme or a morphism.

## What the pages do

**Corollary 4, scan 50.** For commutative finite-type group schemes $H,G$ over $S$, with $H$ rigid, the change-of-base map from $\operatorname{Hom}_{S\text{-gr}}(H,G)$ to $\operatorname{Hom}_{S'\text{-gr}}(H',G')$ is asserted to be bijective. The alternatives in the hypothesis concern a faithfully flat finite-type morphism and spectra of fields with a primary extension. The final sentence expresses the Hom bijection as full faithfulness.

**A stronger rigidity condition, scan 51.** The proposed condition over a field asks for the same density property on suitable closed connected subgroups. The marginal name is « très rigide ». A composition series over an algebraically closed field is mentioned, followed by permanence assertions. The notation for the first composition factor is uncertain.

**The failed openness assertion, scans 51–52.** An entire proposed proposition on the kernel of $G\to H$, and the beginning of its argument over a valuation ring, are crossed out. The live remark uses two finite étale subgroups of degree $p$ in an abelian scheme. On the next face, sections $(\xi,e)$ and $(\xi,\eta)$ in a product of elliptic curves give two subgroups with the same special fibre and different generic fibres. The map to the product of the two quotients exhibits the difficulty with openness of the kernel.

**Descent of the underlying closed subset, scans 53–54.** Starting with $H'\subset G\otimes_k k'$ over a primary extension, the proposition assumes rigidity of its neutral connected component. After a finite radicial extension of $k$, it compares the underlying sets of two base changes. The corollaries distinguish a closed subset of $G$, a stronger conclusion over a perfect field, and a relative statement about inverse images from $G$ to $G'$. In the latter, topological submersiveness is invoked to obtain a closed subset downstairs. The relative statements and their marginal qualifications have several layers of revision.

**Reduction to the finite case, scans 54–55.** Rigidity is applied to $H'_0$. Passing to the quotient removes that component, and the finite group $H'$ is then killed by an integer $n$. The left subscript in $\,{}_nG'$ matters here. In the finite case the text takes an inverse image on underlying points, passes to a suitable radicial extension to obtain separable residue extensions, and gives that inverse image its induced reduced structure. The two tensor bases in the last sentence differ.

**Families of subgroups, scans 56–57.** Over a perfect field and under an irreducibility hypothesis on the parameter scheme, a family $H'$ with rigid generic neutral component is compared with a fixed subgroup $H$ of $G$. Under the quotient map $u\colon G\to G/H$, the family is recovered as the inverse image of a subgroup $H'_1$. This quotient subgroup is finite and radicial over the parameter scheme. The text relates its flatness to that of $H'$. The lower marginal corollary states what happens if a flat family has a simple fibre.

**The descent lemma, scan 58.** For a surjective group morphism $u\colon G\to G_1$ with kernel $N$, the lemma matches $N$-invariant closed subschemes of $G$ with closed subschemes of $G_1$ by inverse image. It treats flatness, the subgroup condition, and a radicial-surjectivity condition involving the underlying sets of $H$ and $N$. The last line appeals to descent theory.

**Torsors with a morphism from $X$, scans 59–60.** The title sheet starts a new subject. The following sheet compares a principal homogeneous space $P$ with a map $X\to P$, descent data on $G_X$, and a map on $X\times_S X$ satisfying a cocycle and an effectivity condition. A further formulation singles out the diagonal condition under stronger hypotheses on $X/S$. The square and the parallel arrows in the margin show the different spaces on which the data live.

**The prorepresentability argument, scans 60–61.** The author writes $T_X(P)$ for the functor of maps under consideration and passes to minimal pairs. He interprets such a pair by its map on $X\times_S X$. Closed subgroups through which the map factors form a descending filtered family. A minimal member supplies the smaller pair in the argument.

**The abelian target, scan 61.** With $A$ abelian and after a change of base to $S'=X$, the displayed chain passes through pointed maps and divisor correspondences to a Hom into the relative Picard scheme. The box has $\widehat A$ as the source of this last Hom. The author calls this a result of the type of Cartier duality. Several letters designating $A$, $B$ or the functor at the left of the displays have been written over.

**The Albanese group and its torsor, scan 62.** Using abelian duality, the sheet defines $\underline A^0_{X/S}$ from the dual of $\underline B_{X/S}$. A torsor under an abelian target $A$ with a map from $X$ corresponds to a homomorphism $\underline A^0_{X/S}\to A$. A canonical torsor $\underline A^1_{X/S}$ carries $\psi_{X/S}$, and the displayed product with $(A,u)$ constructs $P$. The two Albanese schemes are called homogeneous and inhomogeneous.

**Base change and a possible intrinsic definition, scan 63.** The canonical data are said to commute with arbitrary base change. The bracketed note asks whether representation of the torsor functor, together with this compatibility, could define the Albanese scheme axiomatically. It discusses a largest abelian subscheme where the whole neutral Picard scheme fails to be proper. The final diagram links the two Picard schemes of the Albanese objects with the Picard scheme of $X$ and with $\underline B$.

**The criterion along the identity, scan 64.** The author compares simplicity along $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0_{X/S}$ with behaviour along the identity and discusses the openness of the neutral part. After a properness assumption he lists conditions on the Picard fibres and cohomological flatness. He asks about a converse, then considers the identity conormal sheaf $\underline\omega_{G/S}$ over bases of residue characteristic zero. He closes by distinguishing a reduced base from an arbitrary one.

## Questions and conjectures

- **51, L4–8.** « Il semble plus prudent » introduces the proposed stronger density requirement in the definition over a field.
- **53, L14.** « ? N.B. Si on veut … » raises the question of bounding $H'$ by a subgroup defined after field extension. The middle of this marginal question is uncertain.
- **63, L7–21.** « Il y a peut-être lieu de définir » introduces the axiomatic formulation. « Il suffit mais il n'est pas nécessaire sans doute » qualifies the abelian-subscheme hypothesis. « Il est plausible » introduces existence over a field even in the nonproper case. The particular obligation is to « prouver évidemment une propriété universelle de ce dernier, pour tt changement de base ».
- **64, L20–28.** « Est-il vrai réciproquement que (a) et (b) impliquent » simplicity along « la section unité ??? » The next proposal begins « je pense que (b) implique en effet (a), dans la forme plus générale » and invokes local freeness of the conormal sheaf.
- **64, L30–35.** « il y a lieu de se demander si » the conditions hold automatically for projective simple $X/S$ in characteristic zero. For an arbitrary base he writes « je ne sais rien ». The wording of the final underlined exclamation is doubtful.
