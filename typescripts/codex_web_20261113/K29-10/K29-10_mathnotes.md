## What the pages do

**157. Proposition 3.1.** For an étale \(X\) over \(Y\) with a finite group \(G\) acting by \(Y\)-automorphisms, the proposition singles out an open and closed locus, then asserts the existence of \(X/G\) and étaleness of the indicated maps. The proof considers the inertia condition \(s\in G_i(x)\), unions of components and the action on the set \(I\) of connected components. The faint insertion in (i) matters to the formulation of the locus. The last sentence stops after the decomposition of the quotient.

**158. Axioms for a Galois category.** A category and a functor to finite sets are followed by axioms about sums, products, decomposition into connected objects, sections, isomorphisms and quotients of Galois objects. In Gal 3 a section is constructed from the inverse of an isomorphism on a component, followed by that component's inclusion. A side note asks how much can be expressed through the functor. The abandoned first Gal 3 and the order of the surviving labels show the axioms under revision.

**159. A second list of categorical data.** The sheet starts with a category, a full subcategory of points and a class of morphisms called “non ramifiés”. It formulates component, fibre and quotient conditions in terms of maps from \(\xi\). At the foot it introduces the based \(\pi_1(X,x)\). A square with \(x\) and \(x'\) on its horizontal sides illustrates covariance.

**160–161. Descent and principal bundles.** The class \((D)\) is required to have stability and descent properties. The author distinguishes descending an arrow in \((D)\) from descending every morphism. He adds a boxed projection condition, gives classes of morphisms of preschemes as examples and starts a theory of principal bundles. The next sheet records admissible operator spaces, homomorphisms of structural sheaves and inclusions.

**162–163. Proper morphisms and fundamental groups.** For a proper dominant map to a connected normal \(Y\), with a locally noetherian qualification added in the margin, the sheet claims finite cokernel for the fundamental-group map. It reduces to a generic point and a connected component after a field extension. The contrasting example uses a rational curve with an ordinary double point and its normalisation. The author then considers a relative Hom with target \(Y\amalg Y\) and tentatively proposes a direct image \(f_*(X_1)\) under a universal openness assumption. Scan 163 draws \(X\to T\to S\) with the second arrow pointing left on the sheet.

**164–165. A pointed-bundle functor.** A title sheet introduces the fundamental progroup for infinite principal coverings. For a connected prescheme with geometric point \(\xi\), the author considers classes of principal homogeneous bundles with structural group \(\pi\), pointed above \(\xi\). He claims left exactness in \(\pi\). Finite products are addressed briefly. The discussion of monomorphisms compares associated bundles and the unique isomorphism carrying one chosen point to the other.

**166. The equaliser step (S166).** A section through the specified point of a quotient is unique over the connected base. The author then passes from a pair of group maps to the corresponding parallel pair for the functor. In the bundle diagram, \(i_1\) and \(i_2\) have the same source. The coincidence of \(u i_1\) and \(i_2\) determines the locus used for a reduction of the structure group. The last paragraph uses openness, closedness, descent and geometric fibres.

**167–168 and 170. Reduction of the structure group (S168).** The new heading distinguishes the locally noetherian and artinian hypotheses. Two reductions lead to one for the intersection of their subgroups. The resulting family is filtering and decreasing. The artinian case uses stationarity. In the other case a crossed inverse-limit argument gives way to the component \(P^0\) of the marked point. The live argument seeks surjectivity over the base, reduces to a complete discrete valuation ring and on 170 invokes triviality over that base. It then returns to the intersection of reductions.

**169. Application and example.** The sheet describes a group and fibre on an étale object through an action on the fibre and a principal subbundle of a relative Isom. It relates these data to an action of the fundamental progroup. The example passes to twisted constant commutative group schemes and admissible modules whose underlying \(\mathbb Z\)-module is of finite type. The transition into the map to \(\pi\) has a reading question below.

**170. Prorepresentability.** The consequence allows \(\pi\) to vary in a category of artinian groups stable under finite products and subgroups, or allows the corresponding stability conditions with the extra hypothesis on \(S\). It asserts strict prorepresentability. The turned note names the representing progroup and comments on interpreting it as a topological group.

**171–172. Comparison of covering classes (S171).** The author defines \(H^1_{\mathcal C}(S,G)\) as a limit over covers \(T/S\) and relates the individual terms to \(K_{T/S}\) and \(\pi_0(K_{T/S})\). He lists five classes, starting with faithfully flat quasi-compact preschemes and ending with coverings finite étale by pieces. The comparison maps are injective and are said to be bijective for finite \(G\). Further comparisons concern a Dedekind ring, a discrete valuation ring with algebraically closed residue field, and a complete local ring. In the last display the pointer from \(k\) explicitly identifies the residue field.
