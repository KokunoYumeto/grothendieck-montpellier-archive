# Mathematical notes · cote 72, scans 98–113

## What the pages do

**Scans 98–100.** A proof already in progress follows the successive vectors $s_i$ under the pseudo-reflections $\tau_i$. It relates the coefficient of $a_i$ modulo $E_i$ to a product called $\lambda_i$. The corollary rewrites incidence conditions for the lines $h_i$ and hyperplanes $H_j$ as vanishing pairings and a unit condition. In the induction step the author compares the three conditions in A″ with the four in B″.

**Scans 101–102.** The third part of the proof takes up formulas (15) and (16). Corollaire 2 collects identifications of kernels and images, incidences, the intersection $E_i\cap H_i$, and noncommutation even up to a unit scalar. The proof of (24) introduces a scalar $\rho$, evaluates operators on a vector, and restricts a map with two coordinate forms to obtain a triangular matrix.

**Scans 103–106.** In rank $n+2$, freeness is strengthened to a basis condition. A proposition gives conditions on $a_i,a'_i$ for the local existence of $s_0$. A triangular determinant gives the dual-basis argument. Conversely, the conditions on $s_0$ select a rank-two direct summand $F$. The last two restrictions require a section to avoid two lines, $D$ and $\Delta$, in every fibre. A crossed-out attempt with an auxiliary linear form precedes the proposition.

**Scans 107–110.** The title sheet introduces the Stiefel–Whitney classes and the Brauer class of the invariant form. The author projects $s_0$ onto $V$, computes its quadratic value, and uses an orthogonal decomposition recursively. Scan 109 supplies the two-dimensional terminal calculation. Scan 110 collects the factors for $f_V$ and brings in a further line factor for $f_E$.

**Scan 111.** For $n=2$ the first computation uses an extra invertibility assumption. The author then takes the orthogonal complement of the span of $a_0,a_2$, with generator $u=\beta_0a_0+2a_1+a_2$. The second list assigns the symbols $\eta_0,\eta_1,\eta_2$ anew. It is this second list that accompanies the formulas for $w_1,w_2$ at the foot of the sheet.

**Scans 112–113.** The class $c(f_V)=w_2(f_V)+w_1(f_V)^2$ and the class obtained by subtracting its value on $X^2+yz$ are associated with two different geometric vanishing criteria for a bundle of projective lines. The author gives a base for the two-parameter calculation, asks about ten products in degree two, then poses a problem for the quadratic form with indeterminate coefficients. Its final note distinguishes three degree-one classes.

## Remarks of the model

- **R109 · Model, high confidence.** Using the three values marked under the terms of $f(u)$ gives $4\beta-\beta^2=\beta(4-\beta)$. With $\beta=2+\alpha$ this is $(2+\alpha)(2-\alpha)$. Both identities were checked by polynomial expansion. **B109.** The three input values on 109 were taken from the page.
- **R111 · Model, high confidence.** With the second set of degree-one classes on 111, the linear term of the product is $\eta_1+\eta_2$. Its quadratic term is $\eta_0^2+\eta_0\eta_1+\eta_1\eta_2+\eta_2\eta_0$. This is an algebraic check of the two homogeneous parts in characteristic two. **R111BRIDGE.** Reading the two sets of definitions separately, the degree-one term of each product is the sum of the classes of $-(\alpha_0+\alpha_1)$ and $\beta_1$.
- **R112 · Model, high confidence.** Substituting those two homogeneous parts into the definitions of $c$ and $\operatorname{Br}$ reproduces both right members on 112 when the comparison term is $\varepsilon^2$. The geometric vanishing criteria were not tested by this computation.
- **R2UNIT · Model, low confidence as a proposed omission.** The displayed base on 112 inverts 2. In its rational function field, the four proposed unit representatives $-1,\beta_0,\beta_1,4-\beta_0-\beta_1$ have coefficient-prime valuation zero at 2, while 2 has valuation one. Thus their products and squares cannot represent 2. If the boundary has the unit-square-class interpretation used on 113, $\partial(2)$ also needs accounting for on 112. This calculation concerns degree one; the ten degree-two products were not checked.
- **Reading question · scan 98, corollary below the bracket.** The frozen wording is «les sous-variétés associés». «les sous-Modules associés» is another reading: the letter after the hyphen has a tall irregular upright like the M in «sous-Module» on 104, while the middle of the word is a shallow wave. The first reading was unmarked at freeze. The enlarged letterforms favour «sous-Modules».
- **Reading question · scan 98, the sideways note beside (18) and (19).** The frozen line has a cancelled candidate $a_i$ followed by a doubtful $0$ or $a$. A further clockwise rotation places the isolated oval at the *beginning* of the note. An indexed $a$, possibly $a'_i$, then a nearly vertical relation and an $E$ with an apparent subscript $i-1$ can be followed across the crop. A possible reading is $a'_i\mid E_{i-1}$. A pen stroke runs across the relation and part of the subscript. This reading also accounts for more of the surviving ink than the two isolated glyphs in the frozen line.
