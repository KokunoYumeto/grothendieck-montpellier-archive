# Cote 29 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**The cover and the two rows (002, 018).** “Tame ramification” is the title on 002. On 018 a primed and unprimed G occupy the upper row, with M′ and M below. The upper connecting stroke has no visible arrowhead. The sheet gives no word explaining the relation between the rows.

**Change of base (027).** The typescript gives ramification data over S and over T, a functor on the categories C, and a morphism u between two composite functors. A condition on each u(M) leads to a functor between categories of coverings and a comparison with the categories written Rev(S) and Rev(T). The last paragraph gives a condition on the quotient by each subgroup H under which the comparison is an isomorphism.

**Exactness and pointed fibres (028).** After naming cases where change of base behaves as required, the author sets transitivity aside. He asserts that the functor is exact. Given the hypotheses on a geometric point, its image and the geometric fibres, he invokes “les sorites de Exp V” for a canonical homomorphism. The typescript leaves blank some of the places where one would look for a point or a map symbol.

**Two special base changes (028).** For an étale base change the author refers back to a known square. With T finite over S, S and T connected, and the hypotheses of example 3, he identifies the image of the monomorphism by the pointed cover T. The second case starts with an integral, radicial and surjective morphism. This sheet ends during its hypotheses.

**Descent and passage to the limit (030).** For the data in example 3, the typescript proposes effective descent for a faithfully flat, quasi-compact morphism, then applies it to Kummer covers. A projective system with affine transitions is to give a limit property. The author describes a pointwise criterion for a cover to be Kummer and mentions a local ring, its henselization, its strict henselization and, conditionally, its completion. The sentence continues beyond the selected page.

**Common domination (034).** Condition e) requires two given objects to admit a common dominating object after passage to the members of a covering family. The next paragraph says that this presentation of Kummer ramification data dispenses with introducing certain isomorphisms u. The typescript shows only part of the final formula.

**Finiteness over a field (040–041).** Under the cover “Finitudes” or “Finitude”, scan 041 lists finite generation for π₁ of a proper X, finite presentation for the primed group, and finite generation for the group with superscript t. A margin note explains the prime by “partie première à la caractéristique”. Another relates t to tame ramification at a divisor. Descent, reduction to a curve, specialization and a transcendental method appear in the discussion. The heading's field qualification and the status word in b) are both marked in the transcription.

**Completion in two variables (042).** Beneath the pencil marks lie hypotheses on A, x and y, two fork diagrams, a coordinate sketch and three coefficient sums. The left and right series are identified on the page with \(k[y][[x]]\) and \(k[x][[y]]\). Near the centre is \(k[[x,y]]\). The three conditions bound α, β and α+β respectively and concern how many terms or coefficients can be nonzero. Their positions on the sheet distinguish the three conditions.

**Tame and local finiteness (043).** The upper revision returns to c), adds “Prouvé”, and invokes a conjecture about tame ramification and birational invariance. Section 2 sets out hypotheses on a local scheme X and an open U. It calls for finite generation of the tame group and finite presentation of the primed group. The proposed route mentions resolution, an analogy with section 1, descent to normal X, reduction to dimension two and Abhyankar. A margin note calls for a necessity check.

**Coherent modules on a formal pair (044).** A cancelled draft describes coherent modules modulo a closed subscheme using a class of arrows with conditions on kernel and cokernel. It invokes a category of fractions. For X proper over Spec A, the example compares coherent modules on X and on its formal completion. The lower part of the square concerns objects supported on \(X_0\). The final written line compares the formal-pair category with \(\mathrm{Coh}(X-X_0)\).

**Finiteness across fibres (045).** Section 3 seeks uniform bounds on numbers of generators and, in b), relations for the groups attached to geometric fibres. A stronger proposal would make the structure of the primed group a constructible function of s. The note “c'est fait” stands beside a), whose line includes “si X/S propre O.K.” A final sentence relates the hoped-for methods to those of an earlier numbered section.

**Finite formal schemes and fractions (046).** The page defines R of a formal scheme and a category for a formal pair. It describes the inverted arrows through the corresponding homomorphisms of algebras and a condition on modules modulo \(X_0\). The example invokes comparison to relate the formal pair to \(R(X/X_0)\) and then to \(R(X-X_0)\). A diagonal crosses the argument. The lower bracket has an underlined “à vérifier” beside it.

**Relative finiteness and a generic-fibre map (047–048).** The title on 047 precedes a lemma on two étale covers and a map between their geometric generic fibres. The heavily revised statement uses a smallest field of definition, its normalization over Y, and a unique homomorphism or isomorphism after base change. The proof starts with underlined Hom and Isom, reduces to \(X'=X\) and to connected \(Z=X''\), and describes a fibre using a connected étale extension of y. The page ends with a field inclusion.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark · high confidence · 027–028.** The blanks around the corners of the typescript squares and in the formula for the canonical homomorphism matter when reusing these pages. In the same rows the subscripts and functor names remain distinctly inked. A reader reconstructing an arrow or a base point would be supplying an extra sign.
- **Model remark · high confidence · 041 and 045.** Scan 041 distinguishes finite generation from finite presentation in its wording. Scan 045 makes the distinction concrete by asking for bounds on generators and relations separately. The prime and the raised t remain distinct in both lists.
- **Model remark · low confidence · 042.** The positions of the three bounds help associate each explanation with its sum. They give little help with the shape of the first superscript on x because that individual letter is unusually small. No inference about a different exponent convention follows from it.
- **Model remark · low confidence · 044 and 046.** The phrase “Reprenons l'exemple précédent” and the repeated formal pair suggest that the coherent-module example is the intended point of comparison for the example concerning finite formal schemes. The large cancellations leave the status of the comparison uncertain.
- **Model remark · low confidence · 048.** The struck existence statement and the surviving “Plus précisément” distinguish two stages of the lemma. Quoting the opening as a clean list of hypotheses would conceal the competing top-right corrections. The words following *fid. plat* are among the least secure on the sheet.
