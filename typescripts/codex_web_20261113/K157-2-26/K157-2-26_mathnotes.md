# Mathematical notes on scans 398–412

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scans 398–399.** Section 12 gives two reasons to construct categories of paths. One is to obtain external Hom objects with values in $\mathrm{Hot}$ for a prederivator given by a pair $(M,\Sigma)$. The other is a functorial version in $\mathrm{Cat}$ of the Cartan–Serre construction. For the latter Grothendieck takes all arrows of $X$ and fixes a projective system of types whose useful lengths grow without bound.

**U02 · Scans 399–400.** The two endpoints give a projection $p$ to $X\times X$. The constant path supplies a section for each endpoint separately. For $f:X'\to X$ the cartesian square defines $\overline{X'}=C(f)$. The second endpoint, composed with $g$, gives $\bar f$, and the dotted section $\alpha$ gives $f=\bar f\alpha$.

**U03 · Scans 400–402.** Reversing the interval exchanges source and target. A system fixed by reversal gives an involution on its path category. Grothendieck then asks for transport between the fibres of $\bar f$ along $c:a\to b$, depending on its homotopy class. He writes the proposed functor from $\Pi(X)$ to $\mathrm{Hot}$, then two functors to $\mathbb D(e)$ for a general derivator.

**U04 · Scan 403.** Item 5° compares two constructions over the nerve of $X\times X$. One takes the nerve of the categorical path projection. The other starts with paths on the nerve of $X$. A second formulation asks only for canonical isomorphisms of homotopy types at fixed endpoints.

**U05 · Scans 404–405.** Changing the sign of every transition exchanges the two generating intervals. A square relates paths on $X^\circ$ to the opposite of a path category on $X$. To examine smoothness of $p$, Grothendieck fixes $w=(u,v)$ in the base and $c'$ over its target. The source path $c$ and the lift $c\to c'$ vary together.

**U06 · Scans 406–407.** The choice $\tau_0=\Delta_+\Delta_-$, $t_n=\tau_0^{2n}$, gives a bi-infinite zigzag whose arrows are identities sufficiently far out at both ends. The two-index stage is empty for $n>m$. For $n\leq m$, its defining display confines possible nonidentity arrows to $[n,m-1]$. At $n=m$ he identifies the constant paths with $X$.

**U07 · Scans 407–409.** The alternating word is described by its length and initial sign. Translation through $p$ positions preserves each of the two sign components when $p$ is even and exchanges them when $p$ is odd. On their sum the manuscript adds reflection $S$, writes its relations with translation $T$, and describes the resulting group action.

**U08 · Scans 410–411.** The question is whether shifting twice induces the identity in $\mathrm{Hot}$. Grothendieck moves from the chosen projective system to its pro-object and then to an ind-object. He claims an identity in $\mathrm{Hot}_0^{\wedge}$. To draw the conclusion in $\mathrm{Hot}$, the text appeals to a faithfulness property as a hope.

**U09 · Scans 411–412.** Returning to item 1°, he restricts the limit to stages with both endpoints even. He uses their fibred structure to argue for smoothness, and duality for properness. The next square compares the two slice categories with the two actual fibres. Its dotted bottom arrow is to be compared with transport obtained from $u^\circ$ and $v$. The last sentence continues beyond scan 412.

## Statements left without proof

- **U02–U03 · Scans 399–402.** The first diagram records $\sigma\tau=\beta\tau=\mathrm{id}_X$ and $p\tau=\mathrm{diag}_X$. Opposite paths give «un isom fonctoriel en $X$» that «échange source et but». On 402 the extension to every HOT-fibration is expressed as «ça … doit marcher chaque fois». The proposed inverse-transport formula is qualified by «en admettant que les chemins triviaux … donnent l’identité».
- **U05 · Scan 404.** «il suffit à chaque fois de prouver l’un des deux». The square supplies the stated reason for exchanging smoothness and properness. The corresponding argument for the general $\bar f$ is left implicit here.
- **U06–U07 · Scans 406–409.** The transition is given by $\delta_p * \mathrm{id}_{\tau_0^{2n}} * \delta_p$. On 408 an isomorphism «dans $\mathrm{Pro}$» of the sign-changed systems is invoked for endpoint-preserving isomorphisms. On 409 the two displayed group relations lead to the assertion «qui opère sur» the sum of the path categories.
- **U08 · Scan 410.** «Il en résulte déjà que $T^2$ induit l’identité sur l’ind-objet». He also asserts the consequences for the corresponding object of $\mathrm{Hot}_0^{\wedge}$ and for fixed endpoints.
- **U09 · Scans 411–412.** For the even stages he writes «Cat-fibrée sur $X\times X$» and «lisse (i.e. HOT lisse)», then passes to the limit. The margin specifies why the inclusions do not preserve cartesian or cocartesian arrows. Properness is invoked for «les inclusions des fibres». On 412 «la théorie de variance générale (du IX)» supplies an isomorphism between the lower terms.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scans 399–400.** «à vérifier ensuite (si faire se peut)» introduces the programme. Item 1° asks for $p$ smooth, proper and a HOT-fibration. Item 2° asks for trivial endpoint fibrations with aspherical fibres. Item 3° asks for smoothness, properness and a HOT-fibration for $\bar f$, with triviality «ssi $f$ est une Hot-équivalence». The proposed Thomason factorization is qualified by «est à voir !».
- **Scans 401–402.** «on voudrait en conclure une … dans Hot» asks for transport compatible with composition. Two marginal reminders ask for its effect on $\pi_0$, the first ending «décrire quand même l’effet sur les $\pi_0$ !». The second has a doubtful stroke over $\pi$.
- **Scan 403.** «il faudrait … prouver un énoncé de comparaison». He even asks whether the classical arrow is «définie pour tout objet $X$» of the indicated presheaf category.
- **Scans 405, 410–411.** The early reduction leaves «deux faits à vérifier», smoothness of $p$ and of $\bar f$. The later digression asks how to connect $c$ with $T^2c$: «Je ne vois aucun moyen». The hoped-for functor is «tout au moins fidèle». The dated marginal comment on a fibration on 411 has an uncertain main verb.
- **Scan 412.** «on a envie de dire que c’est $\varphi$». Identifying the variance isomorphism with the dotted map would settle that step. The proposed calculation with pairings on ind-objects ends with «Cela … ne prouverait pas pour autant la commutativité de (*) dans Hot, mais».

## Remarks of the model

**M01 · High confidence, scans 408–409.** My check concerns the integer indices of the group relations. Translation uses $j-1$; reflection uses $-j$ for vertices and $1-j$ for edges. Exact composition gives $j$ for $S^2$ and $j+1$ for $STS$ on both sorts of indices. The categorical action itself is reported in U07.

**M02 · Medium confidence, scans 400 and 411–412.** I read two distinct tasks in the final square. Properness supplies the vertical comparisons and hence a candidate bottom map in $\mathrm{Hot}$. Variance supplies an isomorphism with the same endpoints. Grothendieck’s request for commutativity records precisely what the agreement of endpoints leaves open.

**M03 · Low confidence, scans 406–407.** In the long zigzag on 406 the edge between $a_0$ and $a_1$ is labelled $u_1$. In the useful-portion sketch on 407 the edge between $a_n$ and $a_{n+1}$ is labelled $u_n$. This makes it unsafe to transfer an edge subscript from one drawing to the other without also specifying which adjacent vertex supplies it.

**M04 · Reading question, low confidence, scan 402, source line 10.** The frozen wording is «classe d’homotopie de $\bar c$». The path in the preceding definition is $c$, so «de $c$» is a possible intended expression. A short horizontal mark actually sits above the letter. I would keep the bar in a diplomatic reading.
