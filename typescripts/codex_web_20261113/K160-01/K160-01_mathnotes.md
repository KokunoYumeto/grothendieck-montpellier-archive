# K160-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scan 2, formal sections.** Given a map of noetherian schemes, a closed subset and a formal section along it, the lemma relates two schematic density conditions to density in the source. It then asserts étaleness near the section. The proof divides the last density condition into two parts. The sheet ends at “i.e.”.

**Scan 3, kernels over a field.** A coefficient tuple defines a monic additive polynomial $f_a$ and hence a point of the scheme $X_n$ of rank-$p^n$ subgroups of $G_a$. Over an algebraically closed field, the argument separates the connected subgroup from the constant subgroup, introduces a power of Frobenius, and compares ranks.

**Scans 3–4, uniqueness.** Two polynomials with the same kernel induce isomorphisms on the quotient. An automorphism $u$ of the target relates them by $f_b=u f_a$. The next sheet expands $u\circ f_a$, removes successively the highest coefficients of $u$, and uses monicity to obtain $c_0=1$.

**Scans 4–5, properness.** The valuative argument starts with a subgroup over a trait and kernel coefficients over its fraction field. A power of the uniformizer clears the denominators. If a denominator were needed, the resulting special-fibre polynomial would have smaller degree, contradicting the stipulated rank of the kernel.

**Scans 5–6, the étale open.** The proposed description of $X_n^{\mathrm{ét}}$ uses the open set of coefficient tuples on which $a_0$ is invertible. The next sheet asks how far smoothness extends outside it, first for $X_n$, then at the origin of $X_1$.

**Scans 6–7, the tangent space.** A flat lift of $H_0$ gives quotient groups and an extension. The notes relate lifting the quotient map to a splitting and then to a homomorphism out of $H_0$. A long calculation in the case $H_0=\alpha_p$ is struck. A separate, live line identifies the tangent space with a Hom group. Its subscript and the notation of its target remain doubtful.

**Scan 7, the subgroup theorem.** The theorem states that $\varphi_n$ is an isomorphism. In family terms, every finite locally free subgroup of the indicated rank is the kernel of a unique polynomial of the displayed form. The last line characterizes étaleness by the unit condition on $a_0$.

**Scans 8–9, determinant and quotient.** The action of $\Gamma_n$ on affine space gives a quotient and an open of linearly independent tuples. The determinant $\Delta_n$ is compared with the product of normalized linear forms. Divisibility and degree leave a constant factor. The next sheet identifies the vanishing locus with the dependent tuples over a field.

**Scans 9–10, finding the coefficients.** Requiring $f_a(\xi_i)=0$ gives a linear system. Where its determinant is invertible, the displayed quotients give the solution, subject to a marginal sign warning. With indeterminates for the $\xi_i$, the author writes $\Delta_i=A_i\Delta_n$. The sideways corollary records invariance, symmetry and the degree $p^n-p^i$.

**Scans 10–11, the open quotient.** The coefficient map $A_*$ factors through $Q_n$. On the open where $a_0$ is invertible, the manuscript gives an inverse using the finite étale subgroup attached to $f_a$. The lower line of the diagram sends $a$ to $f_a$ from right to left.

**Scans 11–12, the invariant ring.** The proposed isomorphism $Q_n\to\mathbb E^n$ identifies the invariants with the polynomial ring in $A_0,\ldots,A_{n-1}$. Starting from birationality, the argument seeks surjectivity and finite fibres. For surjectivity it removes initial zero coefficients and appends zero coordinates. A specialization lemma supplies the step. The finite fibres are identified with group orbits.

**Scan 12, special-linear invariants.** The final NB turns to the special linear group and singles out $\Delta_n$ as an invariant. Its explanation breaks off after “en fait”.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model, high confidence for the two computed cases.** For $n=2$ the written row order gives $XY^p-X^pY$. Direct multiplication of $X\prod_{a\in\mathbb F_p}(Y+aX)$ gives the same polynomial for $p=2$ and $p=3$. Thus the undetermined $c$ on scan 8 is 1 in both examples.

**R02 · Model, high confidence.** For $n\geq1$ the formal derivative of the displayed $f_a$ is $a_0$ because $p$ divides each higher exponent. This gives a direct connection between the kernel presentation and the unit condition on scans 5–7.

**R03 / R03r · Model, low confidence as a comment on the wording of scan 8.** In “plus grand ouvert … opère librement”, *plus* is a doubtful reading. For $n=1$, $p=2$, the group $\mathrm{Gl}(1,\mathbb F_2)$ is trivial and acts freely on the whole affine line. The independent one-tuples omit zero. In this case the independent open is strictly smaller than the free locus.
