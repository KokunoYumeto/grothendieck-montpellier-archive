# Mathematical notes on cote 94, scans 14–24

## What the pages do

**Report on local fields, scan 14.** A typescript compares the treatment of number fields and function fields. Its five editorial comments concern extending an absolute value to a complexification, unramified extensions, multiplicative representatives and the exponential, decomposition groups, and cocycle calculations. The rédacteur says where the finiteness of an index is used and suggests two ways of establishing an inequality, whose comparison sign is blank.

**Corollaire 11, scans 15–16.** After a cancelled first draft, the statement gives two tests for a projective system of modules prorepresenting a functor \(F\). One is finite generation of \(F(\Omega)\). The other tests the canonical map on quotienting by parameters in a local ring. The conclusion is stationarity. The proof compares the direct limit of Hom groups with a particular term in a square, then applies a local criterion to the transition maps.

**Théorème 12, scans 16–17.** Over a Noetherian ring that is a quotient of a regular ring, the theorem asks when a functor from modules to sets is represented by a module of finite type. Its tests concern left exactness, filtered limits, two completion maps, finite generation of values, constructibility of the rank function, and a generic comparison after reduction by parameters on quotient rings. For sufficiency the author first seeks representation after inverting a non-nilpotent element. Prorepresentability and Corollaire 11 then turn the last condition into stationarity of a localized system.

**Remarque 13, scan 17.** The alternative hypothesis requires that every integral quotient \(B\) have a regular localization \(B_f\) for some nonzero \(f\). The author says this removes the earlier regular-quotient hypothesis and makes (vi) imply (v).

**Théorème 14, scan 18.** For a proper morphism of finite presentation and finitely presented modules \(F,G\), with \(G\) flat over the base, the author represents the functor in the coefficient module \(M\) by a finitely presented \(P\) on the base. The first formula is about underlined Hom sheaves. Taking sections gives ordinary Hom and the uniqueness of \(P\) up to unique isomorphism. The statement includes arbitrary base change. The oblique NB considers a quasi-coherent variant and passage to a limit. The main proof passes to an affine base of finite type over the integers and isolates condition (vi). The draft using a fibrewise Hom dimension is cancelled.

**The larger family of functors, scan 19.** The next paragraph keeps the tests other than left exactness and proposes applying them to higher direct images of sheaf Ext and to a derived Hom expression. The two bracketed alternatives distinguish the sheaf calculation from its affine cohomological formulation.

**Lemme 15, scans 19–20.** Up to a fixed degree \(N\), a local comparison for Ext and reduction by a regular sequence is asserted on a dense open. The affine argument chooses a free resolution, labels the coefficient module \(G'\) and the resulting complex \(K^\bullet\), and rewrites the question as commutation of cohomology with tensor product. Truncation leaves finitely many terms and cohomology modules to test for higher Tor vanishing.

**Lemme 16, scan 20.** The lemma supplies the local Tor vanishing on a dense open. The author passes to objects graded by the nilradical and invokes generic flatness. A short insertion adds the graded base ring as a second flatness requirement.

**Lemme 17, scan 21.** For a proper morphism over a Noetherian base and a coherent module, the displayed map compares a stalk of higher direct image, reduced by a regular sequence, with the corresponding higher direct image on the localized scheme. The proof applies Lemma 16 to compare ordinary and derived tensors, represents the direct image by a bounded complex, and uses the projection isomorphism. Shrinking the open set again gives the Tor vanishing on the cohomology terms.

**Remarque 18, scan 22.** The author proposes calling a functor satisfying the conditions of Théorème 12 apart from left exactness “constructible.” He suggests extending the definition to functors between categories of quasi-coherent modules and lists composition, sheaf Ext, Tor and higher direct image as examples to consider.

**Théorème 19, scans 22–24.** Under “Applications”, \(f\) is proper, flat and of finite presentation. The theorem asserts affine representability over \(S\) of the functor written \(\prod_{X/S} Z/X\) for \(Z\) affine over \(X\), and gives finite-presentation and finite-type conclusions. The proof passes to an inverse system over an affine base. For \(Z=V(F)\), the representing module \(P\) gives \(V(P)\). In the general case a closed immersion into a space of that form leads to the question of where a section factors through a closed subscheme.

**Corollaire 20, scans 23–24.** A closed immersion \(Z\to Z'\) of affine \(X\)-schemes gives a closed immersion on applying \(f_*\). Its proof is intertwined with that of Théorème 19. The diagram on scan 24 supplies the section of \(Z'\) over \(X\). Its inverse image of \(Z\) is called \(Y\), and the factorization condition becomes the functor written \(f_*(Y)\). A reference to Murre supplies the asserted closed subscheme of \(S\).

**Remarque 21, scan 24.** The vector-bundle identity is extended to a quasi-coherent \(F\). The author obtains a quasi-coherent \(P\) by passage to a limit from finitely presented modules.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 18 and 23 · High confidence.** Scan 18 writes an underlined Hom identity before taking sections. The first case on scan 23 again uses underlined Hom on both sides and then identifies \(f_*(V(F))\) with \(V(P)\). The shared underlining and the occurrence of the same representing module make the link visible in the formulas themselves.

**M2 · Scans 20–21 · High confidence in this special case.** In the proof of Lemma 17, shrinking the open set matters even for the comparison of ordinary and derived tensor. Take the identity morphism, \(A=\mathbf F_2[t]\), \(E=A/(t)\), and the regular element \(t\) at the origin. The two-term resolution of \(E\), tensored with \(A/(t)\), has zero differential and degree-one homology \(\mathbf F_2\). The underived tensor has only degree zero. This calculation checks the tensor step at one point outside a permissible smaller open.

**Reading question, scan 19, upper affine pair.** The final fragment ends the bracketed Ext expression with a closing square bracket. Just beyond it, a tiny cluster has the shape of an asterisk in one orientation, although it could be stray ink. Its relation to the formulas is unclear. The fragment has no separate glyph there.
