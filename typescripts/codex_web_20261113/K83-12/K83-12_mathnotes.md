# Cote 83 · Mathematical notes on scans 178–181 and 183–193

## What the pages do

**Construction of a product, 178–179.** The first sheet begins in mid-sentence. Flatness of the \(F_i\) enters an injectivity assertion. Grothendieck seeks a canonical \(\lambda\)-splitting on the product and writes the pairing obtained by tensoring the \(u_i\). For two factors he chooses a multilinear map \(g\) on the quotients, expands \(f(x_1+\alpha_1,x_2+\alpha_2)\), and tests the change \(\alpha_1\mapsto\alpha_1+\alpha'_1\). The marginal note on 178 considers how much injectivity is needed in the input data.

**The unit problem, 180–181.** The product gives an internal operation on \(\lambda\)-split extensions, with a qualification about flat quotients, and on torsors. Grothendieck considers a split extension of \(k\) by \(k\), parametrizes its trivialisation by a scalar, and examines the functor it induces. He lists three conditions on a general object \(M\). They concern equivalence of the product functor, invertibility of the two line modules together with surjectivity of \(u\), and invertibility of \(M\) for the product.

**Recovering multiplication, 183–184.** For an Azumaya algebra of rank four, the determinant and the unit give the trace pairing. Its nondegeneracy reduces recovery of \(xy\) to knowledge of \(\operatorname{Tr}(xyz)\) for every \(z\). Reversal leaves two cyclic orders, with traces \(\tau,\tau'\). The symmetric product \(xy+yx\) gives their sum \(\Sigma\). A crossed calculation tries to subtract a linear combination of \(1,x,y\) from \(z\) to make the remainder orthogonal.

**The oriented difference, 185–186.** One sheet relates the difference of the traces to \(1\wedge x\wedge y\wedge z\) measured against the orientation \(e\). The other uses the alternating commutator trace, invariance under automorphisms and a choice of two matrix units with their commutator. The coefficient and sign convention are under revision. The two scans retain their archival order.

**Computing the product, 187–189.** A weight and permutation argument proposes a polynomial for \(\Delta^2\) in the \(b\)'s and \(t\)'s. A test triple gives a contradiction. The determinant invariants \(c,c',c''\) enter through the Gram matrix on \(1,x,y,z\) for the polar form of the determinant. Scan 189 writes out a polynomial \(\Pi\) and collects the sum, product and oriented difference of the two traces. The boxed product and the Gram formula give different results in a simple case, recorded below.

**Three-variable invariants, 190–192.** Under “À vérifier”, the author proposes that the invariant algebra be free of rank two over the polynomial algebra in nine trace and determinant functions, generated by \(\tau\) with a quadratic relation. He describes the involution and the action of permutations on the two traces. When \(2\) is invertible he uses \(\tau-\tau'\) as generator. In characteristic two the same difference is the sum and belongs to the base algebra.

**Four variables, 192–193.** A new section starts with the polynomial functions in four algebra elements. The four traces, four determinants and six pair traces already give fourteen functions. Grothendieck compares this with dimension thirteen and turns to the traces of triples with distinct indices. Cyclic order cuts the 24 ordered triples down to eight classes, which he groups in pairs by reversing the order.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model, high confidence, scans 188–189.** A symbolic expansion in the nine letters of the matrix on 188 gives \((\Sigma^2-\det(\mathrm{Gram}))/4=\Pi_{\mathrm{box}}-4cc'c''\). Together with the identity-matrix check, this locates the discrepancy in S2. It does not require choosing the orientation sign.
- **R2 · Model, high confidence, scans 192–193.** Enumerating ordered triples of distinct indices from \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) gives 24. Quotienting by cyclic rotation gives eight, and interchanging the first two entries pairs those eight into four pairs. This checks the count of index classes. The claim that the corresponding functions are pairwise distinct over a given base is a separate statement.
- **R3 · Model, high confidence, scans 185 and 187.** The words “les \(b\) étant de” at the foot of 185 continue as “poids \(1\) et les \(t\) de poids \(2\)” at the head of 187. This identifies a textual continuation across the intervening scan.
- **R4 · Model, reading question, low confidence, scan 193, last two lines.** The frozen list has \(t_i,t_j,t_k\); the earlier pair traces are \(t_{ij}\). The one-index symbols might abbreviate the pair traces associated with the complementary index in a triple. The visible list itself uses one lower index at each of these places.
