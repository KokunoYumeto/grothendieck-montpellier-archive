# Mathematical notes on cote 8, scans 35–49

Fonds Alexandre Grothendieck. (n.d.). *Cristaux (1970). Gribouillis et calculs divers : notes manuscrites (s.d.)* (Cote 8, scans 35–49) [Facsimiles]. Montpellier archive.

Scan numbers below refer to the supplied images. The seven handwritten leaves form a sequence in increasing scan order. The eight typescript leaves have their own page numbers. Reading those in increasing printed order brings their definitions before the propositions that use them. The transcription preserves archive order.

## What the pages do

**Scans 36 and 38.** The handwritten argument splits $A\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p} W$ into factors indexed by $\Sigma$, identified with $\mathbb Z/f\mathbb Z$. Frobenius cyclically permutes the factors. Giving $F_M$ thus amounts to giving maps between the $M_\nu$. In the rank situation on scan 36 the components become invertible modules. Identifying them puts the successive maps in the form of powers of $\pi$ times the identity. The last map has an additional unit $u$.

**Scan 38.** The calculation of $F_M^f$ records the sum of the component exponents. The quotient $M/F_M M$ gives a length, hence a dimension. The boxed conclusion characterises dimension one by a uniformizer and, equivalently, by a single exponent equal to one with all others zero. The overwritten expressions involving $v(u)$ are important because they distinguish the successive versions of this calculation.

**Scan 40.** The proposition states the dimension-one classification for $G_0$ with $A$-action and height $h=ef$. It presents $M$ and its Frobenius using an invertible $A$-module, a component index, a uniformizer and a projection. The next paragraph describes $A$-linear homomorphisms in terms of a map $\Omega'\to\Omega$. The inequalities in the last line measure the differences of partial sums of the two sets of exponents.

**Scan 42.** The first two cases give the Hom module for dimension-one objects according to the ordering of $\nu$ and $\nu'$. The second case has the extra factor $\pi$. The note then changes the problem to classification up to isogeny. The $M_\nu$ become vector spaces and the last map supplies an endomorphism $\varpi$. Effectivity is expressed using lattices preserved by $\varpi$ and $p^f\varpi^{-1}$.

**Scan 44.** The opening identifies the cotangent space with a quotient of a Frobenius component and distinguishes two actions of the residue field. For the component $\nu=0$ the text says that the actions coincide. It gives the module and Frobenius of $G_0(A,\pi)$ and writes the general object using $\Omega$. It then asks for a lift over $A$ with a specified action on the tangent module.

**Scan 46.** Under the divided-power assumption on $\pi A$, lifting $G_0$ is expressed as lifting a filtration. Compatibility with $A$ makes the submodule a module over $A\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p}A$. The two sequences on the sheet relate invariant differentials and tangent spaces by duality. The action on the lift is compared with restriction of scalars along the multiplication map $\rho_0$.

**Scan 48.** A rank comparison and a surjection identify $t_G$ by base change. The displayed result is $t_G\simeq\Omega^\vee$, equivalently $\omega_G\simeq\Omega$. The author compares the result with Lubin–Tate and expressly qualifies the uniqueness statement. A second pair of sequences, after passing to $K$, records what survives up to isogeny when the last integral hypothesis is dropped.

**Scan 49, typed p. 76.** Two identities express compatibility of scalar multiplication with exterior multiplication and contraction. Section 6.8.10 defines symmetric powers of vector bundles by their fibres. Sections 6.9.1–2 weaken the regularity or restrict the field of an analytic structure and state compatibility with pullback and an earlier construction.

**Scans 47 and 45, typed pp. 77–78.** Section 7 sets the convention $r-k=r$ for $r=\infty,\omega$. Contact in charts gives an equivalence relation on germs and defines a jet, with its source and target. For functions one obtains an algebra of principal parts with projection $P_x^k$. Composition defines invertible jets and frames. Truncation gives surjective maps between orders. The coordinate description begins at the foot of p. 78.

**Scan 43, typed p. 79.** The coordinate description continues in terms of the values of $\Delta^\alpha f^i$. The text identifies a jet space with a product having an origin-to-origin fibre $L^k_{p,q}$, gives polynomial representatives and describes the invertible-jet groups and their action. It specifies kernels of truncation maps. Section 7.1.6 begins its chart construction here. Printed p. 80 is absent from the supplied images.

**Scan 41, typed p. 81.** Function jets form a vector bundle in algebras. First jets are related to tangent vectors. A morphism supplies a section of a jet fibration. Sections 7.1.10–11 describe iterated jets, the characteristic-zero embedding, and the action of changes of source and target.

**Scan 39, typed p. 82.** Section 7.1.12 describes the change of regularity of a jet bundle. Section 7.2 defines a section jet by the identity-jet condition after projection. It gives the corresponding subvarieties, fibrations and module structures, followed by truncation and its kernel.

**Scan 37, typed p. 83.** The kernel at consecutive orders is identified with $S^k(T(X)^*)\otimes E$. The iterated-jet construction restricts to section jets. A map over the base induces a map on section jets. The text states composition, exactness and tensor-product identities. Section 7.2.6 begins the pullback construction across a change of base.

**Scan 35, typed p. 84.** Section 7.2.6 concludes. Section 7.2.7 introduces relative principal parts for a submersion by killing jets coming from sections vanishing on the base. It identifies the fibre with jets along the fibre and gives a base-change formula. Section 7.2.8 concerns weakening the regularity of the resulting space.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 38.** The small raised mark at the equality added to the dimension box may be a question mark. The frozen reading starts $\operatorname{long} A(\varpi A)$ and also admits $A/(\varpi A)$. The mark could qualify the intermediate equality. Confidence in that interpretation is low.
- **Scan 40.** “On cherche les hom compatibles avec l'action de $A$ de $G_0$ dans $G'_0$”. The note proceeds to a criterion. Scan 42 opens a further problem with “Classifions à isogénie près ces $G_0$”, retaining the visible deletion next to “ces” in the transcription.
- **Scan 44.** “Nous nous proposons maintenant de relever $G_0$” introduces a lifting problem. Scan 48 expressly narrows the uniqueness conclusion with “sous une forme moins forte d'ailleurs en ce qui concerne l'unicité”.
- No explicit conjecture is labelled as such on these leaves.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1, high confidence.** The order of the typed pages is recoverable from two independent features on these sheets. The folios decrease with increasing scan number, and the opening of typed p. 84 completes the sentence at the foot of typed p. 83. Printed p. 80 remains a gap.
- **R2, medium confidence.** The handwritten classification uses both $\pi$ and $\varpi$. Scan 38 explicitly allows changing $\pi$ in the dimension-one case, and scan 40 uses $\varpi=\varpi'$ for the nonzero-Hom criterion. Treating the symbols as interchangeable throughout would erase a distinction made by the pages.
- **R3, low confidence.** The strengthened uniqueness wording on scan 46 depends on the tiny insertion above “près”. The first reading at the witness stage is “(non unique”, and “canonique” and “unique” remain alternatives. Scan 48 makes an independent qualification about uniqueness. It cannot determine the letters in the earlier insertion.
- **R4, low confidence.** Scan 44 reads $\Omega/p\Omega$ and gives $k=A/\pi A$ in the next line. If $pA$ is strictly contained in $\pi A$, these quotients of an invertible $A$-module have different lengths. This raises the question whether $\pi\Omega$ was intended in the dimension-one case. The written denominator has a descending stem. The exponent in the preceding quotient is itself doubtful.
