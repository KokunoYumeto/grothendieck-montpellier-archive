# Hand and type log

Four leaves have typewritten text with individually added ink symbols (20, 22, 24 and 26). The remaining eleven are manuscript leaves, including the cover at 28. The attributions below distinguish individually inked characters from the typewriter impressions. Fine corner numerals belong to the archive.

## Scan 20

The index (K) added beneath the second V′ in line 9 has a freehand K without serifs and thick, uneven brackets. The three other parenthesized base-field indices have type feet. This distinction holds locally. The small pencil 19 is at lower left. Repeated λ has a short hooked head; μ has a left descender.
## Scan 21

The numerator-like slant after the parenthesized spectrum is a prime, also written beside the spectrum in the diagonal note. The free-standing bar below the ring O differs from the hairline connecting the inserted hypothesis. The hand uses large X for the space and small x for a point, but their strokes differ principally in height. The U/X changes over one another in the proof remain visible. The circle cut by a rising slash has been set as the empty-set sign. The upper-right marks are from the paper or its other face.
## Scan 22

The section sign before 1 has a tapering tail extending far below the line. The π has a loop at the left of the crossbar, with the same shape in three positions. Pale baseline marks under p/P recur even outside underlined prose. Lower-left 21 has a thin ascending second digit.
## Scan 23

The numeral 2 in the heading has a tall curved upper stroke and a flat foot, resembling a question mark without a dot. The faint isolated pencil mark to the left of (a) has a separate stop. Reading them by their individual strokes distinguishes 2 and ?. Long descenders connect the two drafts of the generic-point clause. The bar under connexe is horizontal and separated from the word, unlike the through-strokes at the beginning of (b).
## Scan 24

The section signs vary in the width of the loop and length of tail. Both large unions have freehand uprights of unequal height; the large intersection is open below and unindexed. The crossed product circles and division strokes vary across the sheet. A typed i can lose the white interval below its dot on this copy. Comparison with the i + 1 immediately following the slash identifies the index in the tensor products.
## Scan 25

The long lower brace after en y₀ attaches ni f propre to the statement. A second, angular balloon links + théor. de dim to lemme 1 in the proof. In the slanting note, each bar over x is a separate stroke. The hand abbreviates parts of words to a long horizontal with small returns, most conspicuously in the cancelled quotation. The distinct lower dot of the question mark after facile survives at enlargement.
## Scan 26

The heading’s E, C and IV show ordinary single key impressions. Compare the open arrows in the two short exact sequences: their right tips are separated from the shafts, and the second shaft is shorter. The union is squared off at the base, and its entire subscript lies below it. The small hollow bar belongs to p even in that subscript. The upper-left marks are staple holes.
## Scan 27

K has a vertical first stroke and long oblique legs. The k’s in k+1 and = k are both shorter. The abbreviation formellt has a tall interior double shaft. The two notes beside Cor. 3 run into its last two lines at a different angle; some pen strokes belong to both visual fields. The page’s faint upright backing letters are a watermark.
## Scan 28

The earlier word is darkened along its baseline. The horizontal below the replacement is higher than the old baseline. Its last stroke before de is small enough to leave the number of Couple(s) uncertain. The comma between the two letters descends as a short vertical stroke. The first crossed letter is shorter than Y and its case is open. Pencil 27 sits well in from the lower-left corner.
## Scan 30

The pale main hand changes to densely inked interlinear words and then to a broad wavy cancellation. The writing to its left is much smaller. In the two erased derived-image symbols the first upright has a short raised i, while a separate angled mark is visible above the second f. The pale correction at the foot attaches (cf N°2) to the paragraph numbers; (N°1) stands at another level, below the finiteness reference.
## Scan 31

The tall letter before the cohomology degree has two uprights and a crossbar. Both dots lie above the main line. The roof over X and F is distinct from the separate superscript i at the right of H. The long bar at the foot crosses several abandoned clauses. The isolated loop in the bottom left is a callout to the sideways note; its mate is at the end of the text.
## Scan 32

The capital-like two-legged pi recurs in items 6 and 8, with indices at the lower right. Two long arrow shafts start at the same tilted margin note. The digits at the left of the first pair of items 3 and 4 are inside the cancelled rectangle. A separate heavy 1 lies across its lower edge.
## Scan 33

Indices /Y sit below the bottom of X and F. The 0 on H in the second display is a closed oval, higher than H. The two letters that look like script L in the last display have loops above and below. Underlines below the category annotation and the examples note do not cancel either.
## Scan 34

The marked L in the direct sum has a notch above it. The examples distinguish the X subscript on the first structure sheaf from the base integer on the second m. The heavy angular sign before conditions on the display is a divider, with the two conditions written on its right. The lower left N.B. is an uncancelled note next to a separate, cancelled N.B.
## Scan 35

Five vertical shafts descend between the two rows. The two marks alongside α and α′ resemble the same small sinuous upright. The words bijective and surjective share a long tail, but only the first is crossed after φ est. The first glyph in the final degree condition has two rising branches and no clear high dot.
