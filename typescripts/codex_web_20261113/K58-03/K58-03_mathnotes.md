# Mathematical notes on cote 58, scans 20–28 and 30–35

All scan references are to the supplied facsimiles of Montpellier, fonds Alexandre Grothendieck, cote 58. The archival order interleaves manuscript leaves with two separate typescripts.

## What the pages do

**Scan 20, extension of scalars.** The second leaf of typescript nᵒ358 finishes a proof about right vector spaces. The map \(\lambda\colon V'_{(K)}\to V\) is tested by extending \(K'\)-linear maps, by the images of bases, and by linear independence together with generation. The proof gives \(a)\Leftrightarrow b)\), \(a)\Leftrightarrow c)\Leftrightarrow d)\), \(c)\Rightarrow e)\), and \(e)\Rightarrow c)\). The initial statement of the proposition precedes this leaf.

**Scan 20, the two definitions.** A \(K'\)-structure on \(V\) is a \(K'\)-vector subspace \(V'\) satisfying the equivalent conditions of Proposition 1. The elements of \(V'\) are the rational vectors. Once \(V'\) is fixed, a \(K\)-subspace \(W\) is rational over \(K'\) when \(W\cap V'\) generates \(W\) over \(K\).

**Scan 21, Lemme 4.** For a connected noetherian prescheme \(X\), the inserted hypothesis concerns irreducible components of dimension at least \(k\). The lemma passes from connectedness in dimension at least \(k\) of the punctured local spectra at closed points to the same conclusion for \(X\). The proof supposes that removing a closed \(Z\) of dimension less than \(k\) disconnects \(X\), takes the closures of the two pieces, and localizes at a closed point of their intersection. The marginal variant uses points with \(\dim\overline{x}<k\).

**Scans 26, 24 and 22, Proposition 1 of typescript nᵒ355.** In the typescript’s own order the setup is a ring homomorphism \(\pi\colon A\to B\) and a \(B\)-module \(F\) flat over \(A\). Proposition 1 on scan 26 says that an associated prime \(P\) of \(F/pF\) contracts to \(p\). Its argument uses multiplication by an element of \(A-p\). The editorial preface attributes the generalization from a ring to a module to a suggestion of Grothendieck.

**Scans 26, 24 and 22, Proposition 2.** For an \(A\)-module \(E\), the typescript gives \(\operatorname{Ass}_B(E\otimes_A F)\supset\bigcup_{p\in\operatorname{Ass}_A(E)}\operatorname{Ass}_B(F/pF)\), with equality when \(A\) is noetherian. An embedding of \(A/p\) in \(E\) gives the displayed inclusion. The reverse inclusion is treated first for finite \(E\) with one associated prime, next for finite \(E\) using a reduced primary decomposition, and finally for arbitrary \(E\) by passage to its finite-type submodules.

**Scan 22, corollary and remarks.** Under the noetherian hypothesis on \(A\), an associated prime of \(E\otimes_A F\) contracts to an associated prime of \(E\). When \(B\) also is noetherian, the criterion for \(P\) separates the condition on \(p=\pi^{-1}(P)\) from the condition on the prime \(P'\) of the fibre ring \(k(p)\otimes_A B\). The second condition is that \(P'\) belong to \(\operatorname{Ass}_{k(p)\otimes_A B}(k(p)\otimes_A F)\). The last remark takes \(F=B\) with \(B\) flat over \(A\).

**Scan 23, Théorème 2.** Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be proper, with \(Y\) integral, \(y_1\) its generic point and \(y_0\) the point of specialization. The text requires unibranchness at \(y_0\), with a bracketed geometric qualification, and dominance over \(Y\) of every irreducible component of \(X\). If the components of \(X_1\) have dimension at least \(k+1\) and \(X_1\) is connected in dimension at least \(k\), the two bounds carry to \(X_0\). The author assigns the component bound to the dimension theorem and ordinary connectedness of \(X_0\) to the connectedness theorem.

**Scans 25 and 27, Lemme 5 and the local calculation.** The local lemma drops properness and the unibranch hypothesis. It considers the local spectrum of \(X_0\) at a closed \(x\). Its marginal reformulation explicitly primes the spectrum and uses points with \(\dim\overline{x}\leq k\). In the reduction to an integral case the dimension formula identifies \(\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}\) with \(\dim\mathcal O_{Y,y_0}+\dim X_1\), with summands labelled \(n\) and \(\geq k+1\). Scan 27 then quotients by an ideal defined by \(n\) elements and invokes a general theorem with \(K+1=(n+k)+1\) and \(K-n=k\).

**Scan 27, three corollaries.** Cor. 1 proposes the geometric version, pairing geometric unibranchness with geometric fibres. Cor. 2 gives a version with \(f\) proper and universally open. Cor. 3 considers a projective \(X\) and \(Y=X\cap H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_\ell\). It announces analogous dimension and connectedness conditions. The short group before “lieu de \(k\)” does not yield a secure formula for the replacement bound.

**Scans 28 and 30, title and introduction.** The cover names Lefschetz and Grauert and displays a pair. The introduction recalls comparison of higher direct images with completion and an existence theorem over a complete noetherian \(I\)-adic base. It describes an equivalence involving coherent modules and coherent pro-modules on the completion, compatible with cohomology. The proposed extension includes a morphism no longer assumed proper, with an open immersion as example. The author aims to express the requisite finiteness hypotheses in local properties of the sheaves.

**Scan 31, depth and applications.** The previous sentence ends “l'aide de la notion de profondeur”. The author proposes comparing a projective variety with a hyperplane section through its formal completion, subject to depth hypotheses. In (i) he states bijectivity of \(H^i(X,F)\to H^i(\widehat X,\widehat F)\) for locally free \(F\), with \(i\leq n\) and the interlinear bound \(n\leq\operatorname{prof}F-2\). Clause (ii) concerns a locally free formal sheaf, the condition \(\operatorname{prof}(\mathcal O_{\widehat X})\geq2\), and generation by sections. The twist, the bracket following “par ses sections”, and the qualification about a finite set are uncertain. These words do not support a precise restatement of the exceptional locus.

**Scan 32, outline.** Nine numbered items organize formal finiteness, comparison, existence, local finiteness, the local and projective cases, Lefschetz–Grauert closed subsets, and applications to \(\pi_0,\pi_1\) and Pic. The last items concern permanence, purity, a Lefschetz theorem for \(\pi_1\), and quasi-factorial rings and Pic. The earlier items 3 and 4 are cancelled. Two arrows connect the marginal reference on étale morphisms to the circled \(\pi_1\) and item 8.

**Scan 33, comparison and algebraization.** Property \((LG)_n\) asks for bijectivity of the comparison on cohomology of every locally free \(F\) through degree \(n\); injectivity in degree \(n+1\) is an addition. The weak condition concerns degree zero and is glossed as full faithfulness on locally free modules. The next formulations quantify over neighbourhoods of \(Y\) and ask whether a locally free formal module is induced from one on some open neighbourhood. The final display calls the functor from the “Pseudolim” of these neighbourhood categories to the formal category an equivalence. The revised adjectives attached to the different formulations remain uncertain.

**Scan 34, Remarque 1.** The author identifies the weak property with \((LG)_0\). His explanation for injectivity in the following degree uses extensions of \(\mathcal O_X\) by \(F\) that split formally. He then asks the analogous question for arbitrary \(n\). His answer gives the short exact sequence \(0\to F\to I\to G\to0\) and invokes completion and the five lemma. The word qualifying the construction of \(I\) is indistinct.

**Scans 34–35, Remarque 2 and the induction.** Scan 34 proposes testing \((LG)_n\) on a family of locally free \(L_\alpha\). The quotient condition uses sums of \(\check L_\alpha\), and its reformulation contains the inserted “loc. facteur direct”. The examples use \(\mathcal O_X\) for quasi-affine \(X\) and \(\mathcal O(m)\), \(m\geq m_0\), in the quasi-projective case. Scan 35 chooses a sequence with \(L\) a sum of test objects and \(G\) locally free. Its two-row diagram compares a five-term segment of cohomology with its completed counterpart. Case (a) seeks surjectivity of the middle map in degree \(n\); case (b) seeks injectivity in degree \(n+1\). The supplied leaf ends while listing the maps used in case (b).

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 23.** A pencil “?” is placed beside (a) and a grouping rule. Its precise scope is open.
- **Scan 25.** “une réduction facile (?)” questions the reduction. “expliciter” asks for detail in the argument involving formal equidimensionality.
- **Scan 27.** “Généraliser au cas d'un morphisme” is followed by “Peut-on généraliser de façon analogue le th. local ?” The first note draws \(X\to P^r_k\). The words following the question are compressed.
- **Scans 30–31.** “ces résultats peuvent se généraliser à certaines situations où … n'est plus supposé propre” expresses the announced extension. The margin on 31 gives the purpose as “préciser et de … généraliser les résultats de Grauert”.
- **Scan 33.** “Question … implique-t-elle … \(\operatorname{prof}\mathcal O_{X,x}\geq2\) ???” asks about points \(x\) with \(\bar x\cap Y=\varnothing\). The frozen abbreviation reads “Lef” or “LGf”, with “LG” subsequently admitted. The page gives no answer. The marginal “inutile” also queries the role of the added degree-\(n+1\) clause.
- **Scan 34.** “est-il vrai que … est un mono pour … \(i=n+1\) … ?” asks whether isomorphism for \(i\leq n\), for all locally free \(F\), entails the next injectivity. The author answers “Oui, prendre …” and refers to the five lemma.

## Slips

- **Scan 20, second line.** The writing has \(\bar f':\lambda^{-1}\). The two dots are vertically separated. The surrounding sentence describes the extension \(V\to W\), for which \(\bar f'\circ\lambda^{-1}\) would supply the composite. Confidence is high in the colon and medium in the explanation of its intended use.
- **Scan 25, diagonal reformulation.** The transcription reads \(\operatorname{Spec}\underline{\mathcal O}_{X_0,x})'\). A matched opening parenthesis would produce \((\operatorname{Spec}\underline{\mathcal O}_{X_0,x})'\), or the opener could stand immediately after Spec. On renewed inspection a curved trace to the left of the ring letter might itself be an opener. Confidence in an authorial omission is low. The main statement on this leaf has a closing parenthesis without the marginal prime.
- **Scan 35, case (b).** The degree letter in the frozen text reads \(v\) or \(i\), followed by \(=n\). The two degree cases above it favour \(i\). The initial stroke lacks a separate dot. Confidence in an authorial substitution of letters is low.

## Remarks of the model

**M1. Scan 27. High confidence for the arithmetic.** With \(K=n+k\), subtracting the indicated \(n\) generators from the index gives \(K-n=k\). This matches the two occurrences in the local argument. The calculation provides no value for the obscured replacement bound in Cor. 3, where \(\ell\) counts hyperplanes.

**M2. Scans 34–35. High confidence for the degrees.** The central object is in degree \(i+1\). Substitution of \(i=n-1\) and \(i=n\) places the two middle-map claims exactly at the boundary degrees \(n\) and \(n+1\) of the criterion on 34. This observation uses the legible exponents. It leaves the identity of the last hatted letter in the diagram open.
