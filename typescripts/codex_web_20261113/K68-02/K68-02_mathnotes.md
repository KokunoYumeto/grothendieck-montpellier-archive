# Mathematical notes on cote 68, scans 017–022 and 024–032

## What the pages do

**Scan 017, interval products.** The sheet writes down two indexed families of intervals, $E_i$ and $F_j$, and expresses three products in these families. Beside the first two product expressions stand inequalities on their coefficients. The coefficient's upper index and the index on the resulting term do not always look alike.

**Scan 017, the turned half-sheet.** A second run of writing specifies a group over $G$, collections of finite subsets attached to its elements, and a cone of nonnegative sums. The lettered conditions concern total order, reversal followed by translation, products in a cone, and translation by an element of the kernel. The final bracketed union breaks off. Scan 018 has no mathematical writing on the supplied face.

**Scan 019, rearrangement.** A permutation changes a finitely supported function on a set $G$ into an equivalent one. For positive functions the sheet defines $|f,g|$ by maximizing the pairing over rearrangements. It describes equality via inclusion among the level sets and gives equivalent tests for $f'\prec f$ using other functions, finite subsets, and partial sums of decreasing values.

**Scan 020, “Couples prétassés”.** The preorder is related to the equivalence $f'\sim f$. From here $G$ is a group and the operation is convolution. The proposition compares its largest value under rearrangement with a pairing maximum and with an inclusion condition on translated level sets. A corollary specializes the condition to $(f,f)$.

**Scans 021–022, “Couples tassés”.** A stronger condition compares the entire convolution in the preorder. The other formulations introduce a third function or a finite subset. For a collection $S$ stable under convolution, the corollary compares pairs, products of several factors and tests on finite sets. “Condition K” names a hypothesis about the cardinalities represented in $S$.

**Scan 022, expansions.** For $S_0=S\cap\mathcal P_f(G)$ the writer considers positive sums over totally ordered families of sets. He expands $f*g$ in terms of the $A_i*B_j$, then considers ordering the union of their level sets, or requiring a nonempty intersection. A separate four-object group diagram stands at the foot.

**Scan 024, “Jeux de position”.** A relation $R$ specifies the permitted successors of a position. $C_0$ contains the positions without successors. The map $\alpha$ assigns the initiative at every other position, and $G_0(j)$ specifies the terminal wins of player $j$. The sheet defines a play, prolongation, a finished play and winning for $j$. A margin introduces composition of plays.

**Scans 024–025, strategies and winning positions.** A strategy selects a nonempty set of moves where $j$ has the initiative and allows every legal move elsewhere. Winning from $x_0$ requires every compatible maximal play to end in $G_0(j)$. The sheet names the set of such origins $G(j)$. A lemma about a play compatible with several players' strategies is invoked in the proposition on intersections.

**Scans 026–027, following a winning strategy.** The opening proposition carries the winning property along a play following the strategy. The theorem then gives separate descriptions of $G(j)$ on $C(j)$ and on its complement among nonterminal positions. The first uses a successor in $G(j)$, the second requires all successors to be in $G(j)$. For the converse in the first case, the proof modifies a strategy at $x_0$ and considers a first return there.

**Scans 027–029, assembling the second strategy.** The proof chooses strategies indexed by successors of $x_0$ and puts a well-order on those successors. For a reachable $x$, $E(x)$ records the successors from which the corresponding strategy can reach $x$. Its least element, $\varepsilon(x)$, selects the strategy used at $x$. A lemma compares these choices at successive positions. On a hypothetical infinite play the choices stabilize, reducing the tail to one of the winning strategies.

**Scans 029–030, strategies against a player.** An admissible strategy against $j$ keeps a maximal play from being won by $j$. Scan 030 defines a particular $\Sigma'$ in two cases. It identifies the complement of $G(j)$ with the positions admissible against $j$, and with those admissible for that particular strategy. For two players with disjoint terminal winning sets, the corollary partitions $C$ into their two winning sets and $N$.

**Scan 031, the player with the initiative.** The sets $G^*,P^*,N^*$ classify nonterminal positions relative to the player who has the initiative. Three identities recover the separate pieces of $G(j)$. At the foot the writer asks for an extension of the initiative map to all positions that changes player at every move.

**Scans 031–032, alternation.** For two players, the text calls such a game “alterné”. It gives conditions on nonterminal moves and on the predecessors $T(y)$ of terminal positions. The extension is determined at a terminal position with a predecessor. Its value is arbitrary at an isolated position. Definitions of $G$ and $P$ follow. The last surviving words, “et on … aura”, leave the sentence open.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 024–026 · High confidence.** At a terminal position the only maximal play starting there consists of that position. The definition of winning therefore gives $G(j)\cap C_0=G_0(j)$. This also explains why the doubtful capital in scan 026 matters.

**M2 · Scan 025 · High confidence for the finite example.** Take one terminal position $t$, two players $j,k$, and $J'=\{j\}$. Give $t$ to $j$ alone. Then $t\in G(j)$, while the two terminal winning sets have empty intersection. With $J'$ on both sides the example has $\{t\}$ on both sides. This isolates the effect of the unprimed range in S3b.

**M3 · Scans 027–029 · High confidence.** The well-order on $R(x_0)$ gives the phrase “donc stationnaire” its force. The set of labels visited along the putative infinite play has a least element. Once it is reached, the nonincreasing labels cannot subsequently change.

**M4 · Scan 020, main proposition heading · Low confidence, reading question.** The frozen line reads “Prop”. The initial carries extra dark strokes, also visible at the opening of the lower “Déf”. They could be retraced letters or an earlier word under the final one. The image does not clearly establish a separate cancelled heading.
