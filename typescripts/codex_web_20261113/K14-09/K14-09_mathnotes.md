# Mathematical notes · Cote 14, scans 117–132

## What the pages do

**117 · Notation for cycles.** For a projective nonsingular $X$, the sheet pairs cohomology $H^*(X)$ with its primitive part $P^*(X)$. It names the Chow ring $A^*=A_r^*$, the group $N^*=A_a^*$ using algebraic equivalence, and $J^*=A_r^*(X)^a$. A sequence puts $J^*$, $A^*$ and $N^*$ between zeros. The sheet assigns primitive parts $M^*$ and $I^*$ to $N^*$ and $J^*$, then asks what a primitive part could mean for the group in the final line.

**118 · Finiteness and products.** Condition 1) concerns describing classes of numerically trivial cycles, modulo rational equivalence, by finitely many algebraic families. Its annotation relates numerical and $\tau$-equivalence and mentions finite type. Condition 2) is the alternating four-point expression $(x,y)-(x,b)-(a,y)+(a,b)\sim_a0$. The sheet associates these conditions with abelian varieties, vanishing products, an Albanese bijection and a bijection involving the product $X\times Y$.

**120 · Bounded subsets of $K(X)$.** For $X$ projective and $M\subset K(X)$, three proposed characterizations use differences of classes of locally free sheaves, quotients of a fixed twist with a finite set of Hilbert polynomials, and a translate by $\gamma(A)$ described by sheaves that are quotients of $B$. The proof for (ii) to (iii) writes $G=O(-n)^N/R$ and adds the class of the twist. The last line breaks off after “sont quotients d’un”.

**121 and 123 · Bounded families of cycle classes.** With $X$ also nonsingular, scan 121 lists five conditions on $M'$. They concern bounded algebraic families, bounded numerical classes together with a translate represented by positive cycles, classes related by $(i-1)!\,z=c^i(\xi)$ to a bounded family in $K(X)$, an image from $K^{(i)}(X)$, and numerical boundedness alone. The chain on 123 puts (ii), (i), (iv), (iii) and (v) in that order. Only the link between (i) and (iv) goes both ways.

**124 · Construction of a Cartesian square.** This cancelled typescript page, numbered 2, describes $Z(S')$ by pairs $(g,v)$. The identification with $G\times_S W$ sends $(g,w)$ to $(g,g.w)$. The square (xx) gives $q(g,w)=(g^\bullet,g.w)$. The second coordinate distinguishes the two written actions, $(g,v).n=(gn,v)$ and $(g,w).n=(gn,n^{-1}.w)$. Diagram (D) exhibits the maps to $G/N$ and $V$, including the projections drawn with broken shafts. The final lines describe the subsheaf over a section $v$.

**122 · Descent and tangent spaces.** Page 3 of the same cancelled typescript continues the formula for $M_v(S')$ and gives hypotheses for the representability of $X$ by descent across the square (xx). Over a field the argument compares the dimension of $N$ with the rank of its Lie algebra. It introduces the tangent space of $M_a$ at the neutral element and gives the inclusions and inequalities grouped under (1). Only the beginning of Lemme 1.1, with condition a)(i), appears here.

**126 · Six conditions and their bis variants.** The upper rows assert vanishing of internal and external products, first with grades $i,j$, then for zero cycles. The succeeding lines give the alternating four-point expression and the equality for $(x+y)-(x)-(y)+(0)$ on an abelian variety. The lower rows compare $A$ and a group of zero cycles on $A$, assert an Albanese isomorphism and state that the product map in 6° is bijective. The left-hand arrows and brace organize these assertions. The equivalence sign in 3° is a plain tilde. In 4° an $a$ sits below it.

**128 · Working sheet for the implications.** The first computation juxtaposes cycles of the form $z\times b$ and $a\times z'$ in $Z\times Z$, with $Z=X\times Y$. Farther down, maps between $A$ and $A_0(A)^\tau$ accompany a diagram grouping the numbers from 126. The long return arrow has a question mark. Calculations below the diagram compare differences of $\varphi$-values and repeat the four-point expression. Separate pencil jottings, turned half a turn, distinguish two quotient expressions on $S'$ and list four inverse identities ending in $b$.

**129 · Regular sections and a Cartan subgroup.** The cancelled typescript page numbered 24 bis finishes a fibrewise argument for an isomorphism $\psi^{-1}(U)\to U$. It invokes conjugacy, then characterizes $U$ as the largest open with the stated property. Corollaire 3.2 says that a regular section of $G$ lies in a unique Cartan subgroup in the sense specified there. The proof identifies such a subgroup with a lift to $X$. The last paragraph begins to describe the regular points in the fibres and stops at “On”.

**127 · The generic Cartan subgroup.** The cancelled page numbered 25 continues with the fibration of the regular open over the scheme parametrizing Cartan subgroups. Corollaire 3.3 compares $k(G)$ with the function field of a smooth connected affine nilpotent group over the function field of the torus parameter space. The correction imposes the affine, zero-unipotent-rank condition for the unirationality conclusion. A following paragraph explains what “sous-groupe de Cartan générique” means here.

**127 · The torus lemma.** Lemme 3.4 states that a torus has function field contained in a purely transcendental extension of $k$. Its proof starts with a finite separable splitting extension $k'$, open subsets of affine space and the large product indexed by $\operatorname{Spec}(k')/\operatorname{Spec}(k)$. The text stops at “eucli-”. The authorial instruction “Conserver le texte ci-contre, barré par erreur” applies to this visibly cancelled block.

**131 · An incidence cycle and an image formula.** The page begins with $Z\subset X\times S$. A smaller diagram labels $X'$ as the blow-up and places $P(N)$ near the codimension annotation. A larger one relates $C\times S$, $P(N)$, $C_\eta$, $X$, $X'$, $Y(\eta)$ and $S$. The calculation uses $f_*(y')=x$, a correction term $\alpha_*(u)$, and the assertion $u_\eta=u$. It ends with a boxed expression of $A(Y(\eta))$ as a sum of images from $X$ and $C$, followed by a graded expression with $i-j$ on $A(C)$.

**132 · Ring jottings.** Beside the pen trials are a difference of powers, a crossed beginning, a bracketed polynomial variable with two listed powers, and $k[x,y]/xy$. Some characters are much fainter than the printed letterhead on the reverse orientation. No statement relates the sketches to the rings on this sheet.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 120 · High confidence, conditional on additivity.** The input on scan 120 is $G=O(-n)^N/R$ (P120). Write $H=O(-n)^N$. If $[G]=[H]-[R]$, the expression $[F]-[G]+[H]$ has coefficients $1,0,1$ on $[F],[H],[R]$. This checks the signs in the handwritten passage to $\gamma(F+R)$ under its additive interpretation. It leaves the claimed boundedness of the quotients open.

**M2 · Scan 123 · High confidence.** Following the heads in the five-item diagram gives a path from (ii) to (v). Starting at (v), no displayed arrow leads back to (ii). This is a check of the implication directions as drawn.

**M3a–M3d · Scan 128 · High confidence.** In the order of the rotated pencil lines, $a^{-1}(ab)$, $a(a^{-1}b)$, $(ba)a^{-1}$ and $(ba^{-1})a$ each reduce to $b$ by adjacent inverse cancellation. The sheet gives no link between these side calculations and the cycle diagram.

**M4 · Scan 131 · High confidence about the drawn endpoints, low confidence about a slip.** The arrow $f$ goes from $X'$ to $X$. The arrow $i'_\eta$ ends at $X'$, and the longer $i_\eta$ ends at $X$. The input $f_*(y')=x$ (P131) therefore assigns $y'$ and $x$ to different spaces. This accounts for the priming question under **Slips** without changing the reading.

**Reading question arising after the witness check · Scan 128, middle-right sum.** The final text has $n_{x,y}$. The first stroke of the coefficient could also belong to $u_{x,y}$. It descends into an open trough before rising again. The top is faint where type shows through from the reverse. The transcription retains $n_{x,y}$.

**R1 · Late reading correction · Scan 122, “Avec les … précédentes” in Lemme 1.1 (page file L14).** The frozen text reads “notations”. A larger view isolates a detached acute above the a, leaning in the same direction as the acute on the neighbouring é of “précédentes”. The word on the sheet reads “notátions”. This spelling discrepancy was identified after the witness check and is recorded here under the freeze rule.
