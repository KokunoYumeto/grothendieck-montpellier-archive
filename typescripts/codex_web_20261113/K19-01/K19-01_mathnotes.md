# Mathematical notes · cote 19, scans 2, 4–12 and 14–18

## What the pages do

**U04 · Scan 4.** From an adjunction \(f:A\to B,\ g:B\to A\), the author forms \(\varphi=fg\), the counit \(\alpha\), and \(\lambda=f*\beta*g\) from the unit \(\beta\). He draws the two Godement conditions, gives the two equations on \(u:X\to\varphi(X)\) defining an object of \(K(\varphi,\alpha,\lambda)\), and sends \(Y\) to the object with underlying \(f(Y)\) and structure map \(f*\beta\).

**U05 · Scan 5, first half.** The theorem headed “Cartier” gives conditions for the comparison \(h\) to be essentially surjective and an equivalence. Its hypotheses concern the existence of equalizers of pairs of arrows and their preservation by \(f\). The equivalence criterion adds conservativity. A sloping note restricts which pairs have to be examined.

**U06 · Scans 5–6.** Starting with the data on \(B\), the author takes \(A=K(\varphi,\alpha,\lambda)\), lets \(f\) forget the structure map, and writes down \(g(X)=(\varphi(X),\lambda(X))\). He constructs maps in the two directions between the Hom sets. The square on scan 6 supplies the component of the unit at \((X,u)\). He refers back to the numbered conditions to identify the composites.

**U07a · Scan 7, upper half.** The conclusion compares the data of the adjoint pair with the data of \(\varphi,\alpha,\lambda\). The corollary compares full faithfulness of \(f\) with invertibility of maps involving \(\alpha*\varphi\) and \(\lambda\). One connective in this line is heavily struck.

**U07b · Scan 7, a)–c) and theorem.** The author treats inverse limits and direct limits separately, then asks which commutations of limits pass from \(B\) to \(A\). In the topos case he relates the realization of \(f\) as an inverse-image functor to left exactness and accessibility of \(\varphi\). The last clause relates essentialness to preservation of arbitrary products.

**U08 · Scan 8.** For \(B=\prod_iB_i\) the components are \(f_i:A\to B_i\), with right adjoints \(g_i\). The formula for \(g\) is a product. Under the stated product-preservation hypothesis the \(j\)-component of \(\varphi\) becomes a product of \(\varphi_{ji}(X_i)\), where \(\varphi_{ji}=f_jg_i\). The author resolves the two structural maps into component maps.

**U09 · Scan 9.** The map \(\lambda_{kji}\) goes from \(\varphi_{ki}\) to \(\varphi_{kj}\varphi_{ji}\). The small upward arrow in the box inserts the unit into the middle \(g_jf_j\). The text distinguishes all-equal indices, consecutive equal indices, three different indices, and \(i=k\ne j\). The last case gives a map out of an identity functor.

**U10 · Scan 10.** For two factors the remaining data comprise \(\varphi':B''\to B'\), \(\varphi'':B'\to B''\) and the two maps \(\lambda',\lambda''\) of (3.14). In the long diagram the author asks about the equality of the parallel maps. He writes two compatibility relations and gives the component maps \(u',u''\) of an object.

**U11 · Scan 11.** Two triangles in (3.16) express proposed conditions on the components. The example makes the composites constant with terminal values, in which case the conditions are said to be empty. Formulas (3.17) give the projections and their right adjoints. In (3.18) the essential image of \(g'\) is described by invertibility of \(u''\), and that of \(g''\) by invertibility of \(u'\).

**U12 · Scan 12.** The author lays out the individual components of \(\beta':X\to g'f'(X)\) and asks when it is an epimorphism. He describes the intersection of the two essential images through the simultaneous invertibility of \(u'\) and \(u''\). The conditions numbered (3.21) ask for the resulting category to be equivalent to a terminal category and express this on either factor.

**U14a · Scan 14, upper part.** A second adjunction \(F:C\to C',\ G:C'\to C\) gives the powers of \(E=GF\). Degree \(n\) contains \(E^{n+1}\). The author inserts “co” above “simplicial” and writes the coaugmentation \(X\to\widehat E(X)\). He then gives the primed, simplicial construction for \(FG\), with the degree written below.

**U14b · Scan 14, (i), (ii) and Application.** Two displayed comparisons concern \(G(X')\) and \(F(X)\). The next formula asks for a transformation \(h:T\circ E\to T\) splitting \(T*\varphi\), then compares \(T\widehat E(X)\) with \(0\). The application asserts that \(\widehat E(X)\) resolves \(X\) when \(F\) is exact and faithful in the stated additive setting. Part b) begins and breaks off.

**U15 · Scan 15.** The functor \(h_B\) is specified by \(h_B(A)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)\). The inverse to the map in (*) evaluates a transformation at the identity of \(B\). The text passes to the additive case and the embedding into additive functors. It then assumes a generator \(U\) and AB 3, 4, 5, and announces a converse.

**U16 · Scan 16.** The proposed converse evaluates \(h\) at \(U\), giving a right module \(M(h)\) over \(R=\mathrm{Hom}_C(U,U)\). The author describes an inverse on Hom sets and draws the proposed functors. He proposes \(T''(M)=M\otimes_RU\) and reduces an equivalence claim to \(\mathrm{Hom}_C(U,M\otimes_RU)\simeq M\). The last line returns to representability by \(h_B\).

**U17 · Scan 17.** An additive \(S:C\to C'\) with the indicated representability property leads to \(T:C'\to C\). Applying the Hom comparison to identities yields \(\varphi:\mathrm{id}_C\to TS\) and \(\psi:ST\to\mathrm{id}\). The sideways note spells out how the components of \(\psi\) specify the Hom comparison. Several objects have been renamed in ink.

**U18 · Scan 18.** The two triangle diagrams exhibit \(S(A)\) and \(T(X)\) as retracts. The author then characterizes the unit being injective on every object by injectivity of the maps that \(S\) induces on all Hom sets. In the last triangle the isomorphism points from the rightmost Hom set towards the middle.

## Remarks of the model

**R04 · Scans 4–6 · High confidence.** Whiskering \(\mathrm{id}_A\to gf\) with \(f\) and \(g\) gives exactly the source and target \(fg\to fgfg\) of (1.4). This checks the order of the four letters.

**R09 · Scans 9–10 · High confidence.** With two index values there are eight ordered triples. Two have all three entries equal, four have just one adjacent equality, and the remaining two have \(i=k\ne j\). Only the latter two fall in (3.11b). This count is independent of the numerals in the canceled paragraph.

**R14 · Scan 14 · Reading question, high confidence about the strokes.** In (i) and (ii) the relation has three visibly wavy tiers. The final text stacks three tildes at the marked occurrence in (i) and retains its approximate typeset sign at the unmarked occurrence in (ii). The ink alone does not state what kind of equivalence the author assigns to the sign.

**R16a · Scans 15–16 · High confidence.** The unmarked formula in P16b fails for an arbitrary generator under the written hypotheses. Take \(C=\mathrm{Ab}\), \(U=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\), and let \(e\in R=\operatorname{End}(U)\) project onto the first summand. Endomorphisms are lower triangular. Their lower-right entries make \(M=\mathbb Z/2\) a nonzero right \(R\)-module with \(Me=0\). The first column identifies the left module \(Re\) with \(U\), so \(M\otimes_RU=0\). Thus the displayed Hom group is zero for this \(M\). This concerns the asserted identification for all right modules.

**R16b · Scan 16 · High confidence.** A narrower instance of P16b holds for \(C=\mathrm{Ab}\) with \(U=\mathbb Z\). Here \(R=\mathbb Z\), tensoring with \(U\) gives \(M\), and evaluation at \(1\) identifies \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,M)\) with \(M\).

**R18 · Scans 17–18 · High confidence.** Under the Hom comparison, the map on Hom sets induced by \(S\) becomes postcomposition with \(\varphi(B)\). Injectivity for every test object \(A\) is the definition of \(\varphi(B)\) being monic. This accounts for both directions of the last criterion, including the leftward comparison arrow.
