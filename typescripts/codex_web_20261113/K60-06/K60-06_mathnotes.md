# Mathematical notes on cote 60, scans 60–70

The alternating leaves carry two kinds of mathematics. The handwriting on scan 61 concerns cohomology at infinity. The handwriting on scans 63, 65, 67 and 69 develops a functional equation for a rational function. The typescript leaves carry portions of document n° 392. References below follow scan order. In quotations, the ideals are identified by their letters. Their original underlining is retained in the transcription.

## What the pages do

**60, opening and (i).** A proof already in progress compares vanishing of two ideals under an integral extension. After reduction to a torsion module, contraction of a height-one prime and existence of a prime over it give the two directions of the comparison.

**60 (ii).** The page applies the invariant $\chi_A$ to a torsion $B$-module regarded as an $A$-module. This gives a homomorphism $D(B)\to D(A)$. Its value on $B/P$ is computed at $p=P\cap A$ from a residue-field degree. The homomorphism is identified with $N$.

**60 (iii).** Torsion is removed before choosing a basis over $L$ and a basis of $L$ over $K$. Their products give the basis used for (21). The next sentence breaks off after “un sous-$B$-module”.

**61, first triangle and boxed row.** For a compactifiable map, the page compares extension by zero and direct image with the restriction to the boundary $Y$. Applying the compactified direct image yields the boxed sequence, followed by a definition of the groups with lower index $\infty$.

**61, second triangle.** A relative-cohomology construction uses $Y=\bar X-X$. An earlier row is crossed out below the triangle. The last sentence describes a shift of one unit. In the earlier boxed row the written sign between $i$ and 1 remains doubtful.

**62, opening d).** The exercise asks for a discrete valuation ring with a separable extension whose integral closure has no single algebra generator. The suggestion starts with a cyclotomic extension.

**62, 8a.** Under separability of the residue-field extensions for the integral closure of a Dedekind ring, the problem asks for a single differential $dx$ generating $\Omega_A(B)$.

**62, 8b.** A second problem asks for a complete discrete valuation ring with a separable extension and a radicial, nonmonogenic residue-field extension. It then asks for failure of single generation for the differential module.

**63, change of function.** The starting relation compares $L(1/(qt))$ with $A(t)L(t)$. For $L'=\lambda L$, the multiplier changes by the ratio $\lambda(1/(qt))/\lambda(t)$. The desired multiplier is a constant $c$.

**63, comparison of corrections.** Two choices of $\lambda$ differ by a rational factor $\alpha$. The page writes an equation for $\alpha$ under the same substitution and circles a restriction to $\pm1$. At the foot it introduces the action of $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ on nonzero rational functions.

**64, 9.** A linear form on $k[X,Y]^2$ sends the two basis vectors to $X$ and $Y$. The exercise contrasts its free rank-one kernel with the quotient, which is identified with an ideal and asserted to be nonreflexive.

**64, 10, definition and a) (T64.10a).** The ideals $c_k(R)$ are constructed from pairings with the dual after taking exterior powers of a submodule of a finite free module. A presentation $M=L/R$ is used to define the determinantal ideals $d_k(M)$. Part a) asks for independence of presentation and states the resulting chain of inclusions.

**64, 10b–d.** Part b) relates the successive $c_k$ to invariant factors over a principal ideal ring. Part c) compares the first index at which $d_h(M)=A$ with the minimum number of generators. Part d) bounds powers of the annihilator by determinantal ideals.

**64, 10e–f.** Part e) gives a sum-of-products formula for a direct sum. Part f) gives a comparison for a quotient and asks for a further inclusion involving products of ideals of $N$ and $M/N$. The orientation of the first inclusion matters in the check below.

**65, normalization.** The incomplete boxed equation is followed by the solution $\lambda=1/L$, giving $L'=1$. The written aim is a correction of lower “niveau”.

**65, cancelled proposal and margin.** A condition involving $L(0)$, absolute values of roots and poles, and an interval of weights is crossed out. Inside this cancellation are earlier horizontal deletions. The sideways note specifies a power of $p^{1/2}$ multiplied by an indexed algebraic integer. The overwritten exponent and the word qualifying the roots remain difficult.

**65, product.** A new line factors $L(t)$ into ordinary and primed $L$-factors with successively shifted arguments. The following lines discuss inverse eigenvalues and give the absolute value for $L'_{\rho+n-i}(p^it)$.

**66, 6.** The ring $A=k[X^2,XY,Y^2]$ sits in $B=k[X,Y]$. Part a) asks for normality and finiteness. Part b) uses the height-one ideal $p=AX^2+AXY$ to distinguish $p\otimes_A B$ from the extended ideal $pB$. It asks for conclusions about torsion, reflexivity, divisoriality, injectivity and flatness.

**66, 7.** For a finite torsion-free module $E$ over a Noetherian integrally closed ring, the canonical map from $E^*\otimes_AE$ to $\operatorname{End}_A(E)$ is compared up to pseudo-isomorphism. Reflexivity of its source is proposed as the test for projectivity.

**66, 8a–b.** The high-degree Tor and Ext modules are asserted to be pseudo-null. Degree one is also considered when $M_1$ is torsion-free.

**66, 8c–d.** For a torsion module $M_1$, three identities use $\chi$, the rank of $M_2$, and tensor, Hom, Tor and Ext. The two Hom identities interchange the modules and have opposite signs on the right. Part d) gives two formulas with $c$ and ranks for general finite modules.

**67, allowed corrections.** The manuscript restricts the form of the $\lambda$-product and distinguishes the weight interval from an upper bound on the level. The adjacent factors with argument $pt$ have different indices. A sentence beginning “Si le” was abandoned.

**67, functional equations of the factors.** Assuming a functional equation for each $L_i$ and $L'_i$, the writer first expands the reciprocal argument and then collects the $\delta$-factors and powers of $t$. A long curve on the left joins the two parts across the intervening sentence.

**68, § 4, 1.** A family of reflexive lattices in one vector space contains a common lattice. The exercise asks about their intersection and points to their associated dual lattices.

**68, 2.** The proposed reflexivity test for $F$ uses $\operatorname{Hom}_A(E,F)$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^1_A(E,F)$. Its hint passes to the quotient of the bidual of $F$ by its image and displays an exact sequence.

**68, 3.** A reflexive lattice contained in a free lattice is to be represented as the intersection of two free lattices. The hint isolates finitely many height-one primes and localizes away from them.

**68, 4a–b.** An explicit quotient of submodules of $k[X,Y]^2$ is offered as a nonsplit example with torsion. Part b) proposes $A/AXY$ as an obstruction to a direct sum of cyclic modules with prime-power annihilators.

**68, 5.** The numerator is $(aM)\cap(bM)$ and the denominator is $(a\cap b)M$. The problem asks for pseudo-nullity of the quotient and an example where it is nonzero.

**69, product for the multiplier.** The ratio $L(1/(qt))/L(t)$ is written as a scalar and a power of $t$ times two bracketed products. The following $A(t)$ display rewrites them using a product index $i$. A possible prime on the last denominator affects how the two products compare.

**69, $n=1$.** Three factors are written, and shallow brackets pick out the first and third. Their product is written on the following line.

**70, 26.** For polynomials over a factorial ring the exercise gives a criterion for generating the unit ideal. It uses a Bézout relation over the fraction field, a common denominator, a gcd of coefficients and reduction modulo each extremal element dividing that denominator.

## Questions and conjectures

**Scan 63.** “On veut simplifier l'équation fonctionnelle” leads to “on aimerait obtenir” the equation with constant multiplier. “Si on trouve un tel $\lambda$” leaves existence conditional. The last line asks about finding $\lambda$ for a specified $c$.

**Scan 65.** After giving the trivial solution, the writer wants to “imposer que $\lambda$ … soit de ‘niveau’ … inf à $L$”. The bounds in the diagonally struck passage belong to an abandoned formulation of that requirement.

**Scan 67.** “Se permet de ‘corriger’” introduces a restricted product. The writer proceeds under hypotheses on $A(t)$ and the factors. No explicit conjecture is labelled on the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

**M63.sign — high confidence.** Applying $t\mapsto1/(qt)$ twice to the displayed equation for a nonzero rational $\alpha$ gives $a^2=1$. This accounts for the circled restriction on scan 63.

**M64.reverse — low confidence about the intended correction.** With the interpretation of 10a given above, the relation module for a quotient contains the relation module for $M$ inside the same free module. The monotonicity stated just before 10a then gives $d_k(M)\subset d_k(M/N)$. For $A=\mathbb F_2$, $M=N=A$, and $k=0$, the defining pairings give $d_0(M)=0$ and $d_0(M/N)=A$. This tests the first inclusion of 10f alone.

**M65.weight — high confidence.** In the last line of scan 65, the exponents on the left add to $(\rho+n+i)/2$. Multiplication by $p^i$ accounts for the change from $-i$ to $+i$ in the numerator of the exponent.

**M67.index — low confidence.** In the second row of the long product, the exponent attached to the $\rho+n$ factor is $\chi_\rho$. The neighbouring group pairs the same subscript on $\delta$, $\chi$ and $L$. This suggests a possible missing $+n$. The line contains a cancellation immediately before the factor.

**M68.zero — high confidence.** In exercise 2 take $A=\mathbb Z$, $E=0$ and $F=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. Both groups occurring in the assumptions vanish. The dual and bidual of $F$ vanish as well, so $F$ fails the stated reflexivity conclusion. Requiring $E\ne0$ would exclude this example.

**M69.prime — low confidence, reading question.** Scan 69, the denominator of the second product, has a faint separate stroke above the dotted $i$. The final reading retains a possible prime and an unprimed alternative. That choice matters if the ratios are compared factor by factor.
