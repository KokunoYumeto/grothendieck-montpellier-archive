# Mathematical notes on cote 57, scans 14–19 and 24–33

Scan numbers are those of the archive PDF. L-numbers refer to the individual LaTeX files. The identifiers link these notes to the check register. Quotations preserve the manuscript’s language. A question mark in the English commentary indicates an editorial doubt.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Multiplication pullback (14–15).** The cover reads “Carré, cube, rigidité…”. The first box on 15 expresses \(n^*(L)\) using \(L^{\otimes n}\) and \(\delta L\). Below it the author introduces a primed delta, writes \(\delta'L_x=L_{2x}L_x^{-4}\), and rewrites the formula. The power of \(L\) in the second box is blotted.

**U2 · Picard functors of a product (15).** A boxed formula splits the \(\tau\)-part into factors. The longer sequence below has a product of the individual Picard functors on the left and a product of \(\mathcal C_S(X_i,X_j)\) on the right. The note under its product sign specifies two-element subsets of the index set. Sections of the \(X_i/S\) enter the splitting statement.

**U2a · Marked invertible sheaves (16).** The definition of \(\mathrm{Pic}_{\mathrm{sp}}(X/Y)\) specifies an invertible sheaf with marked section and vanishing \(H^i_f(\underline L)\) for \(i>0\). It gives a rank for \(\underline H^0_f(\underline L)\). The upside-down diagram elsewhere on the sheet relates \(\mathcal D,\mathcal A,P,Y\) and their fibre products. The last sentence breaks off at the sheet edge.

**U3 · Pair factors and the cube (17, 19).** Scan 17 distinguishes disjoint index pairs from pairs meeting in \(\alpha\), then assembles terms \(L_{x_i+x_j}\), \(L_{x_i}\) and \(L_\varnothing\). Scan 19 starts from the three-variable cube formula, works out the four- and five-variable products, and groups the arguments for the induction step. The five-variable box contains ten separately written pair factors.

**U3a · The exponent of \(L_\varnothing\) (17–18).** A linear trial is crossed out. The author uses \(\alpha_{n+1}=2\alpha_n-\alpha_{n-1}+1\) and numerical starting values to obtain the boxed \(\alpha_n=(n-2)(n-1)/2\). A second box returns this exponent to the product formula.

**U4 · A product decomposition (24–26).** After the “Rigidité” cover, a theorem expresses a morphism \(f:X_1\times_S X_2\to G\) through maps on the factors. The normalization uses a section \(\varepsilon_1\). With a section on the other factor, the corollary writes a third term coming from \(S\). The proof fixes \(f_2\) by restriction, subtracts its contribution, and extends the region on which the remainder factors through \(X_1\).

**U5 · Factorization near a fibre (27–28).** Lemma 1 starts with a proper \(f:X\to Y\) and a finite image \(g(X_y)\) in \(Z_y\). Its proof introduces \(X'=g(X)\), the algebra \(g_*\underline O_X\), and \(Z'\). It constructs a section \(\sigma\) over a neighbourhood. The corollary continues across the page turn with the unique section on the open locus.

**U6 · Closedness of the finite-image locus (28).** Lemma 2 treats a universally open morphism of finite type. The proof reduces to a discrete valuation ring and compares the generic and special fibres. A heavily revised remark returns to the product corollary with a proper structural map \(p\) and a condition on \(p_*\underline O\).

**U7 · Maps from an abelian scheme (29).** Corollary 2 gives \(f=u+c\), with \(u\) a group homomorphism and \(c\) determined by the value on the unit. Corollary 3 asserts commutativity and the uniqueness of a composition law with the given two-sided unit. Its argument applies the earlier product corollary to \(A\times A\).

**U8 · A pointed functor (30).** “Reformuler le th.” introduces three forms of triviality for a pointed map into a group-valued functor. The sheet passes from a subscheme with the same underlying set to a neighbourhood of a fibre, then to a connected component. It ends with an unramifiedness formulation for the pointed Hom functor.

**U9 · The factorization locus and its corollaries (31–32).** Scan 31 defines \(U\) by asking whether a fibre element comes from the residue field. It claims openness and a unique section over \(U\), and leaves a hypothesis for closedness unfinished. Subsequent corollaries treat connected bases, marked sections and products. On 32, Corollary 4 tests triviality on the two section loci; Corollary 5 gives the decomposition by the two projections and a base term.

**Q1 · A question about correspondences (32).** A marked invertible module is trivialized along both section loci, compatibly at their intersection. The question asks whether triviality over a dense open extends, and restates the problem as separatedness of \(\underline{\underline{\mathrm{Corr}}}_S(X,Y)\). The answer written below is conditional on a Picard functor.

**U10 · Deformation and a cancelled second theorem (33).** The first theorem asserts existence and uniqueness of an abelian-scheme structure with prescribed unit under the written hypotheses on \(X,S,e\) and one fibre. The corollary retains determination by the unit. In the cancelled draft the author returns to maps out of a product; the English corollary ends with a square relating \(X\times X\), \(X\), \(G\times G\) and \(G\).

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Model remark, high confidence (18 L6–11, L43–61).** The boxed polynomial gives \(0,1,3,6,10,15\) at \(n=2,\ldots,7\), including the \(0\) written next to \(\alpha_2\). Its second difference is exactly one. These two arithmetic checks use the live recurrence and boxed polynomial.
- **M2 · Model remark, high confidence (25 L35; 32 L3, L15).** Restriction along the section of one factor defines a component of a map on 25. On 32 the two section loci first test the triviality of a map, then carry trivializations of a module. This parallel concerns the loci explicitly named on the sheets.
- **M3 · Model remark, low confidence (33 L17–41).** The English corollary and square can be read as an application the author considered for the decomposition above. Their placement in the same cancelled draft supports the connection. The adjacent question mark has no visible leader.
- **M4 · Reading question, low confidence (18 L44).** The final transcription has \(\tfrac12\,9\) in the substitution for \(\alpha_3\). The denominator under 1 lacks the clear bottom stroke of the 2 in the line above; the narrow double curve could also be a 3. The quadratic coefficient and the substituted values favour \(\tfrac12\). The reading remains as hashed.
