# Mathematical notes · cote 136, scans 220–234

The handwritten leaves and the interleaved typescript belong to different arguments. References below use scan numbers. The typescript runs in its own order from scan 234 (p. 189) through 232 and 230 to 228 (p. 192). Quotations are from the images. A reading question here leaves the frozen transcription intact.

## What the pages do

**The two differentials, scans 220–222.** On the product of the divided and exterior power algebras of a module, scan 220 specifies how a de Rham operator acts on divided powers and how a Koszul operator acts in the reverse direction on the degree-one factors. It records two vanishing calculations with A in degree zero, under hypotheses stated separately for the two complexes. Scan 221 compares the symmetric-power and divided-power versions. Scan 222 describes the Koszul differential associated with a linear form, then writes the mixed operator as \(\theta=d\delta+\delta d\). The boxed equality is \(\theta x=nx\) on elements of total degree \(n\). The page uses invertibility of \(n.1_A\) to infer vanishing in that degree.

**Degree zero and duality, scan 223.** The question is whether a homogeneous element of positive degree with \(df=0\) can be nonzero. The proposed argument composes the differential with the Koszul operator and identifies multiplication by the degree. The notation moves between several letters for the degree. The second half starts from a pairing of two modules into A. It pairs symmetric with divided powers and exterior with exterior powers, then describes the two differentials as transposes and multiplication as dual to comultiplication.

**A representing object for normalized chains, scans 225–226.** Proposition 6 on the perforated sheet describes the \(i\)th term of \(ND_\bullet(L_*)\) by a Hom from an exterior power of a fixed object. The terms for \(i\ge1\) and the term for \(i=0\) are identified separately. The corollary writes a tensor formula for the dual construction. The other leaf gives a Hom comparison involving \(DP\). The diagram-and-proof block above that comparison and the second version of Proposition 6 below it are both crossed out. The uncancelled paragraph between them retains the argument through \(DP\).

**A two-term complex and its quotient, scan 227.** The diagonal \(k_*\to\Phi_*\) gives the quotient \(\Psi_*\). The page writes the normalized terms of \(\Phi_*\) in degrees 1 and 0, with boundary the identity on \(k\). It deduces a homotopy statement for functors of \(\Phi_*\), computes the homotopy groups of the quotient and states the corresponding one-degree concentration for its exterior powers. Here \(\Phi\) denotes the recurring looped letter as transcribed. Its alphabet is uncertain on the associated leaves.

**Derived exterior powers and suspension, scan 229.** The first comparison is written for \(k[1]\). A proposed extension to complexes of degreewise projectives has a question in the margin. A filtration with three exterior-power terms and a middle term labelled “flasque” yields a shift of homotopy groups. The page distinguishes that conclusion from a probable identification of the whole normalized complex with \(k[i]\). It also names divided, symmetric and tensor operations and returns to the exterior power in Corollary 1.4.

**Total and exterior degree, scans 231–233.** Scan 231 uses Eilenberg–Zilber, Dold–Puppe and Künneth in the calculation of the homotopy of a tensor product. It next labels the two gradings of a divided-power de Rham complex and singles out the diagonal bidegree. Scan 233 describes a truncation of a flasque resolution. The piecewise definition keeps the old term below \(p\), takes cycles in degree \(p\) and gives zero above \(p\). The ensuing comparison with cohomology has the corresponding cutoff.

**A divided-power topology, scan 233.** After the double rule the page starts again with a ring \(S\), a divided-power ideal \(S^+\) and \(t\in S^+\). It defines \(S^{[i]}\) using powers of \(t\) indexed by \(j\ge i\), and describes the closed ideals after passage to a separated complete ring. The final superscript on \(t\) is one of the doubtful readings.

**Trivializations and the analytic exact sequence, scan 234.** The typescript pulls back a biextension along two displayed maps. It identifies its trivializations with lifts on either side and introduces the rigid-analytic exact sequence (14.1.7). It refers to [24] for this definition. The sentence about functorial dependence continues on scan 232.

**Formal completions and the map \(e\), scan 232.** After descent to semi-stable reduction, the typescript passes from an isomorphism of formal completions to one on generic fibres. Two open immersions and these identifications give the commutative square (14.1.11) used to characterize \(e\).

**Existence, algebraicity and the dual sequence, scan 230.** Remark 14.1.12 distinguishes the uniqueness of \(e\), obtained from connectedness, from its existence, referred to Raynaud. It discusses the status of \(i^{\mathrm{an}}\), announces an arithmetic characterization, and identifies the lift on the dual side, expressly questioning its sign.

**Finite-level and Tate-module sequences, scan 228.** Section 14.2 passes from (14.1.7) to an exact sequence of finite group schemes and then to a sequence of \(\ell\)-adic modules. The text compares the resulting arrows with those obtained algebraically in (14.2.3). The last sentence starts the justification for the first arrow and breaks off at the foot of the extract.

Scan 224 carries a computer job listing and an administrative notice. It contains no mathematical argument.

## Remarks of the model

**R227 · Model remark, high confidence.** The normalized complex printed in the argument on scan 227 has \(k\) in degrees 1 and 0 and the identity as the only nonzero differential. Its kernel in degree 1 and cokernel in degree 0 are both zero. This checks the homology calculation once those terms and that differential are granted. The simplicial assertion on the sheet also uses the passage from normalized chains back to simplicial objects.

**R229 · Model remark, high confidence.** The question in the margin of scan 229 belongs beside the comparison for a general \(L_\bullet\). The later “Il est probable” concerns a different assertion, the identification of the normalized exterior-power complex with \(k[i]\). Their positions and different subjects are visible on the sheet.

**R233 · Model remark, medium confidence.** The case \(i=p\) in the construction on scan 233 explains why the displayed object uses cycles at its last retained degree. This reading follows from the separately written \(Z^p\) and the cutoff in the cohomology formula. No identification of the ambiguous script capital is needed.
