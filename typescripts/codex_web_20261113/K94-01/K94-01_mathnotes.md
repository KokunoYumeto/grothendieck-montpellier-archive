# Mathematical notes on cote 94, scans 2–13

## What the pages do

**Cover, scan 2.** The two titles connect the construction of coherent modules from a functor with prorepresentability along a section. The last line gives an Ω with a raised 1 and a further, uncertain symbol. There is no accompanying definition of that invariant on the cover.

**Change of rings and Lemme 1, scan 3.** A functor from \(A\)-modules to sets induces one on \(B\)-modules by restriction of scalars. The triangle records the composition. The lemma gives an equivalence for faithfully flat \(B\) involving left exactness, representability, and the finite-type or finite-presentation versions of representability.

**Lemme 2, scans 3–4.** Over a noetherian ring the author tests representability by a finite module using a localization at \(f\), the quotients by powers of \(f\), a completion comparison, and filtered direct limits. For sufficiency he takes \(B=\widehat A\times A_f\). The modules representing the quotient functors « se recollent ». Their inverse limit gives the module used over the completion.

**Proposition 3, scans 4–5.** The criterion asks for representability after some localization on every nonzero quotient. The upward arrow moves the completion condition ahead of this quotient condition. The proof uses noetherian induction on quotients, chooses a non-nilpotent element, and invokes Lemme 2.

**Proposition 4, scans 5–6.** For a local noetherian ring the author replaces the localization condition by a comparison on completed finite modules and a finiteness condition on their values. He passes to the complete case, tests using the indecomposable injective of each quotient \(A_n\), and takes the inverse limit of the representing modules.

**Lemme 5, scans 6–7.** Under five conditions the claimed representing object is a strict projective system of finite modules, stationary after localization at each prime. For an element \(\alpha\in F(M)\) the proof seeks its smallest module of definition. It constructs local image submodules, tries to extend their agreement from a largest open set, and uses a reduction modulo the prime and Nakayama at the end.

**Remarque 5 bis, scan 8.** The proposed replacement for the generic representability condition is constructibility of the residue-field rank of \(F(k(p))\). An interlinear qualification concerns the normal locus of every integral quotient. For the converse the author works on an open of constant rank, compares local modules with a free module, and shrinks the open using codimension-one information.

**Lemme 6, scan 9.** For a coherent sheaf and the nilradical \(J\), the author chooses a dense open on which two associated graded objects are locally free over the reduction. Part b) asserts positive-degree Tor vanishing for reduction modulo a regular sequence. The proof uses the Koszul complex and induction on the sequence length.

**Corollaire 7, scans 9–10.** On a dense open the stalk of the sheaf Hom commutes with passage to the quotient by a regular sequence. The proof presents the source by two free modules. It writes a two-term complex, a degree-zero comparison and two Tor vanishings, then invokes Lemme 6(b). The coefficient symbols in the statement, prose and complex differ.

**Lemme 8 and Corollaire 9, scan 11.** The lemma passes a fundamental sheaf on a Cohen–Macaulay prescheme through a regular sequence to the closed scheme that it defines. At a point, a system of parameters yields the indecomposable injective for the quotient local ring.

**Corollaire 10, scans 11 and 13.** On a dense open the local rings are Cohen–Macaulay and a coherent module yields an indecomposable injective after quotient by parameters. Part b) stops at « est » and resumes with « l'injectif indécomposable » on scan 13. Part c) uses the resulting Hom tests, for every parameter system, to detect when a surjection of finite modules is an isomorphism.

**Crossed Corollaire 11, scan 13.** The draft returns to a surjection \(R\to R'\). It introduces two Hom functors and states a comparison with regular-sequence quotients and an isomorphism on Ω. Its conclusion says that the original map is an isomorphism. Diagonals cross the heading, hypotheses, both tests and conclusion.

**Valuations typescript, scan 12.** The definition of an absolute value is followed by the exponential associated with an archimedean valuation. Proposition 1 characterizes a valuative absolute value by the maximum inequality or by boundedness on the images of natural integers. Its converse uses a binomial estimate. The corollary applies the criterion in positive characteristic.

**Equivalence and Proposition 2 of the typescript, scan 12.** Equivalence is expressed by raising one absolute value to a real power. The proposition lists the \(p\)-adic and ordinary absolute values on \(Q\). The nonvaluative case begins by expanding a power of one integer in the base given by another. The sheet stops at « On en déduit ».

## Remarks of the model

- **R4.1 · model · high confidence, scans 4–5.** The arrow gives a practical reading order for the proof. The reference to (iv) on scan 5 is used precisely to choose the non-nilpotent localization element in the quotient condition.
- **R9.1 · model · medium confidence, scans 9–10.** The two arguments are linked by the positive-degree Tor vanishing. On scan 9 the Koszul calculation concerns a quotient by a regular sequence. On scan 10 the same kind of quotient becomes the tensor factor in the Hom comparison, and the author explicitly cites « lemme 6, b) ».
- **R10.1 · reading question · low confidence, scan 10, first display.** The final subscript on the right is transcribed with \(s\) first and \(n\) second. Its tight loop looks more like \(s\) than the wider \(n\) on the left. The repeated parameter sequence favours \(n\) mathematically. This is separate from S10.1.
- **R13.1 · model · low confidence, scan 13.** The cancelled functor definitions may show an attempt to put a tensor factor inside Hom before replacing it by \(M\). Both the coefficient and the operation lie under local strikes. Their identity cannot support a claim about a change of convention.
