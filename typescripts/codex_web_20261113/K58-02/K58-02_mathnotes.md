## What the pages do

**Scans 11 and 13, Corollaire 1.** Under conditions whose labels appear to be $a_k)$ and $b_k)$, the intersection of $X'$ with $V(f_1),\ldots,V(f_k)$ is asserted to be connected for $f_1,\ldots,f_k\in rA$. The argument reduces to complete $A$, takes $k=1$ from lemma 2, and supposes the result known for smaller $k$. Lemma 3 supplies a finite set of points to avoid. The choice of $f_2$ allows the induction hypothesis to be applied to $X'\cap V(f_2)$ with the other equations. The contradiction is « connexe, ce qui est absurde ». The text begins in the middle of an earlier sentence.

**Scans 13, 15 and 17, converse.** The next corollary assumes a bound read as $\pi_0(X'\cap V(f_1)\cap\cdots\cap V(f_k))\leq1$ for every such family. It concludes that the components of $X'$ have dimension at least $k+1$ and that $X'$ is connected in dimension at least $k$. A marginal qualification excludes an irreducible $X$ of dimension 1. The finite intersection on the smallest component is the turning point of the revised argument. Scan 17 treats the irreducible case and passes through a $Y'$ of dimension 1 associated with a $Y$ of dimension 2. The account of the final cut and the last two lines are particularly uncertain. The cancelled attempts have their own predicates and bounds.

**Scan 16, graded groups.** Typescript n°354 fixes a commutative monoid $\Delta$ with neutral element 0. A grading of an additive group $G$ is a direct-sum family $(G_\lambda)$. The definition distinguishes homogeneous elements, their degrees, and the homogeneous components of an arbitrary element. Zero belongs to every degree. Example 1 puts the whole group in neutral degree.

**Scans 16 and 14, changing degrees.** Example 2 regroups the summands by a homomorphism $\rho\colon\Delta\to\Delta'$ with $\rho(0)=0$. The word « gra- » at the foot of 16 continues as « duation » at the top of 14. The latter leaf gives a weighted finite-support example. Example 3 applies the two projections and addition on a product monoid to get two partial gradings and a total grading. It also describes when two gradings recover a bigrading from intersections of their summands.

**Scans 14 and 12, graded rings and modules.** Formula (1) gives the multiplicative compatibility of a ring grading. Proposition 1 on scan 12 places the unit in the neutral component when all degrees are regular. Its proof decomposes 1 into homogeneous parts and compares components after multiplying by a homogeneous $x$. Definition 3 gives the left and right module conditions in (2). Example 4 identifies the gradings compatible with a trivially graded ring by requiring the summands to be submodules.

**Scan 18, rational subspaces.** The n°358 fragment starts mid-proof. Linear forms in $H$ vanish on $W'$ and on the space it generates. Their kernels define $W_1$. The equality $W_1\cap V'=W'=W\cap V'$ and proposition 2 identify $W_1$ with $W$.

**Scan 18, remarks and a new section.** Remark a describes $K'(v)$ using the values $h(v)$ of rational linear forms and states $W_L=V_L\cap W$. Remark b treats the finite-dimensional dual and the condition $f(V_L)\subset L$, then points out a distinction for infinite dimension. The new section « Critères de rationalité » introduces the left-space notation $ {}_L K$. Its sentence about endomorphisms continues off the leaf.

**Scan 19, corollary and consequence.** The manuscript cuts $X'$ by $H_1,\ldots,H_k$ with $k\leq n-1$. It asserts connectedness of $Y'$ and surjectivity of the indicated map. The proof asks that a connected étale cover of $X'$ remain connected over $Y'$ and invokes irreducibility and dimension. The target subscript is doubtful. The following paragraph proposes reducing a finite-generation question for $\pi_1(X')$ to normal local rings of dimension 2.
