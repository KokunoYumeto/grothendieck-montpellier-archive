# K15-08 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**195, (i) and (ii) · Fibres at finite places.** For an $X$ dominating $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, the notes attach an $\ell$-adic object to a geometric point and describe its dependence on that choice. At a point in characteristic $p$ they add two $p$-objects. A revised passage compares points of characteristics $p$ and zero using $\mathrm W(k)$, with a separate comment about finite $k$.

**195 and 197, (iii) · The characteristic-zero fibre.** Hodge–de Rham supplies a fibre with two filtrations. The sideways note places the graded pieces of one beside the canonical filtration of motives. The continuation asks how much of the fibre depends on the valued point, describes a semilinear action as descent data, then considers a rational trivialization and its possible $\ell$-adic comparison.

**199, (iv) · The integral lattice.** A complex-valued point supplies an integral fibre. The brace gives two scalar extensions of this lattice, to $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and to $\mathbb C$. A separate displayed isomorphism relates the fibres at $x$ and its complex conjugate. The end of the sheet divides the discussion according to the closure of the residue field in $\mathbb C$.

**199 and 201 · The real completion.** In the real case the notes attach an involution to the integral data. They distinguish it from a semilinear operation on the infinite fibre. Two unpointed lines in the diagram link the real and integral objects to a complex object; the following display spells out both comparisons.

**203 · Two involutions.** The decomposition by types and the real form give rise to the operations labelled $\tau'$ and $\tau''$. The writer identifies their product with $\tau$, considers the invariant spaces, then boxes an action on the $\ell$-adic fibre. Both the product and the boxed conclusion have a nearby « à vérifier ».

**205 · A proposed description by level.** The N.B. relates the arithmetic filtration on rational cohomology to the Hodge filtration and $f_\infty$. Its two-line formula puts a rational part, a filtered complex part and its $f_\infty$-transform in one intersection. The following parenthesis associates the resulting subspace with a submotive of $F$.

**206 · Programme for the Lie-algebra text.** The typescript proposes a new title and lists material for the next redaction, including infinite modules, invariant differential operators, classical split algebras and triality. It assigns an arithmetic contribution to Cartier. The appendix instructions concern Zariski topologies and dominant polynomial maps.

**204 and 202, typed pages (3)–(4) · Weight spaces, descent and rank.** Page (3) gives additive and multiplicative rules for the image of a bilinear map on weight spaces. It revises the use of descent and the adjoint action. Its last sentence continues on (4) with the multiplicity of the eigenvalue 1. Page (4) also treats rank, regular elements, Cartan subalgebras and restrictions in positive characteristic.

**200, typed page (5) · Cartan subalgebras and scalar fields.** The instructions formulate transitivity on Cartan subalgebras and give a criterion for an intermediate subalgebra of the same rank. They also address restriction of scalars. The sheet then begins §2 on representations of $\mathrm{sl}_2$ with edits to the definition of weights.

**198, typed page (6) · Simple modules.** The editor prescribes conventions at the ends of a weight string and specifies a basis for $W(m)$. The instructions for decomposition of modules request finite-dimensional hypotheses, a reference for complete reducibility and a Casimir calculation. The last paragraph revises the treatment of holomorphic representations.

**196, typed page (7) · Roots.** The text asks for definitions of splitting, a description of a primary subspace, a dimension statement and an explicit isomorphism $\varphi_\alpha$. It ends with instructions for choosing one root from each opposite pair.

## Questions and conjectures

- **195.** « provenant sans doute de » qualifies the proposed origin of the $\ell$-adic object. « Ils ne sont en général pas isomorphes, sans doute » qualifies the comparison at $p$. The word introducing the « relation » has several possible readings, one of them « Conjecture ».
- **195–197.** « Il n’y a pas lieu de penser que … les deux foncteurs obtenus, soient isom ». In the rational case the writer says « il n’y a pas d’isom canonique prévisible » and questions the existence of any isomorphism.
- **198.** « Il semble qu’on n’a pas besoin du (ii) et du (iii) de la prop. 6 ; si oui, ça passe en exer. » The proposed rearrangement of n° 3 is conditional. The probable difficulty of a replacement calculation is conveyed by « pas si “facile” ».
- **202.** « Vérifier la formule du bas de la p. 9. » Its statement is absent from the packet.
- **203.** « à vérifier » appears twice, first beside the factorization and then beside the boxed action at the real place.
- **205.** « La conjecture de Hodge interprète la filtration induite … », followed by « Elle s’exprime en disant que ». The intersection assertion and its claim to be motivic belong to this conjectural description.

## Remarks of the model

**Model remark, high confidence, S202-2.** I checked the characteristic-two parenthesis on scan 202 for the ground field $\mathbb F_2$. Computing all matrix commutators in the eight-element trace-zero algebra gave a lower central series of sizes 8, 2 and 1, so the algebra over this field has nilpotency class two. The calculation covers this field case.

**Model remark, medium confidence, scans 201 and 203.** Reading the diagram on 201 beside the two marginal checks on 203 helps locate the remaining question. The first sheet distinguishes operations on different fibres; the later sheet proposes a factorization relating operations and names Frobenius below the box. The margin positions support this connection, but do not determine exactly how much of each adjoining assertion the writer wanted checked.

**Reading question, scan 203, second display.** The frozen line reads $\xi_{a,\infty}{}_{\mathbb R}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ on the right. A possible interpretation places the first $\mathbb R$ with the preceding fibre as a restriction of scalars. The low placement is visible; its attachment is less clear. The transcription retains its position.
