# Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 044 and 046.** The topological Teichmüller groupoids have compact oriented surfaces with boundary as objects and isotopy classes of homeomorphisms as arrows. The bioriented version allows homeomorphisms that reverse orientation. A signature map to the one-object groupoid \([\pm1]\) has the oriented version as its fibre. A slanted note qualifies the construction by referring to connected surfaces.

**Scans 044–046.** The special dévissé version adds a collection of circles and a finite set of rigidifying points. The notation distinguishes the internal circles \(\Sigma\), the boundary, and their union \(\widetilde{\Sigma}\). Scan 046 lists three full subgroupoids specified by restrictions on the circles or points, with oriented and bioriented names in each row. Scan 045 preserves a cancelled start on a weighted rigid graph.

**Scan 047.** A graph is specified by vertices \(S\), oriented edges \(\vec A\), an involution \(\sigma\), and an origin map \(o\). Fixed elements of the involution are free edges. The drawing uses isolated vertices, loops, multiple edges and free edges. The page attaches genus to vertices and a rigidification index to edges. It also presents the underlying graph as an ordinary graph plus a set of free edges with their origin map.

**Scan 048.** The functor marked \((*)\) takes the cut surface to a doubly weighted graph. The vertices index the components of the cut surface. An oriented edge records a circle and a side, with the boundary giving one available side. Exchanging sides defines the involution. Incidence, genus, and the number of rigidifying points on the circle give the remaining data. The page also states compatibility with direct sum.

**Scan 049.** Connected components agree on the surface and graph sides. A margin formula expresses total genus as the sum of vertex genera and a graph term read as the rank of \(H_1\). In the case with no internal cutting circles, the displayed data concern free edges and the genera of vertices. A connected surface in the version with trivial edge weights has numerical data \((g,\nu)\), genus and number of holes.

**Scans 050–052.** The proposed refinement replaces a numerical edge weight by the actual set of repères on that circle with its circular permutations. The two orientations, when both are allowed, give inverse permutations. The pages describe the same data in terms of torsors and then use combinatorial polygons and an action of \(D_\infty\) to keep track of the marking and the orientation on free edges. Scan 052 collects the data into the graph with weights and repères and calls for reconstructing the canonical functor to these graphs.

**Scans 052–054.** Erasure of repères replaces \(R\) by \(R\setminus R'\). The marked subset \(R'\) appears as extra input to the erasure functor. Scan 053 sketches a diagram with one branch that erases those repères and one that forgets their designation. The target at the bottom is glossed as graphs still carrying one weight, with the \(r\)-weight forgotten. Scan 054 describes erasure in the oriented set \(\vec R\) using a subset stable under both dihedral generators.

**Scan 054.** Erasing cutting circles removes the chosen circles and contracts their corresponding ordinary edges, also erasing the repères above them. The page calls the resulting graph map a morphism of generalization and leaves the update of the weights to be explained.

**Scan 055.** Filling selected boundary circles with cones erases the associated free edges and their repères. The draft also combines this with the previous erasures. The mixed operation includes every repère lying over a circle in the chosen clopen part and carries the weights forward.

**Scans 056–057.** Surmarquage adds a prescribed number of repères in each open interval of an effectively marked circle. The result carries a marked subset of newly added points, so that erasure returns the original object. Scan 057 proposes restricting attention to uniform operations on each edge, using a divisor for partial erasure and a multiplier for uniform surmarquage.

**Scan 058.** Cutting along an internal circle replaces the corresponding ordinary edge by two free edges. The marking follows the cut. The text separates effectively marked circles from unmarked ones and discusses a finite ambiguity in recovering the original from the cut in the marked case. The supplied scan ends during that discussion.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence, scans 047, 054–055 and 058.** Free edges serve both as a record of pre-existing boundary circles and as the output of cutting an ordinary edge. This explains why erasing boundary data and cutting an ordinary edge act differently on the same graph data.
- **Model remark, medium confidence, scans 044 and 048.** The connectedness proviso matters to a single signature on a surface. Independent components can carry independent local signs, which would require a choice to combine them if the codomain still had only two values.
- **Model remark, medium confidence, scans 049 and 058.** A loop can contribute to the graph term in the genus formula. After a cut it produces two free edges, so the graph loses that cycle contribution and retains the two new boundary circles.
- **Model remark, medium confidence, scans 050–051 and 056.** The pages discuss the polygon even for one or two repères, when the corresponding cyclic permutation gives few distinctions. The orientation data and dihedral description offer a way to keep those small cases visible in the combinatorics.

## Reading questions after the freeze

- **046, upper object list.** The frozen line has \(R\subset\Sigma\), with crossed ink following it. \(R\subset\widetilde{\Sigma}\) would allow repères on boundary circles, as considered in the later graph-weight construction. The written sigma in that position lacks a clear tilde.
- **049, second diagram.** The frozen first reading is \(A=A_0\), with \(A=A_\ell\) as the alternative. The low character is rounded, while the preceding prose favours the free-edge symbol.
- **056, middle condition.** The frozen line has \(\Sigma^{+}\setminus R\cap\Sigma^{+}\), with the first sigma’s form marked. The circled decomposition discussed here may intend \(\Sigma^{+}\setminus(R\cap\Sigma^{+})\). No grouping parentheses were visible between the set operations.
