# Cote 145 · Mathematical notes on scans 020–034

## What the pages do

**020, relations (4)–(8).** Grothendieck writes the three loops $l_\infty,l_1,l_0$ as words in $\rho$ and the involutions $\sigma_i$. In the left margin he adds the cyclic conjugation relation and reduces the generators to $\sigma_0,\rho$. Setting all three $\sigma_i$ equal to $1$ leaves $\rho^3=1$. He uses this quotient to show that $\rho$ cannot be a word in the $\sigma_i$, then turns to square roots $\varepsilon_i^2=l_i$.

**021–022, lifts and square roots.** Scan 021 gives the relations of the lifted permutations $\widetilde\sigma_i$, their action on the loops and three order-two lifts $\tau_i=\sigma_i\widetilde\sigma_i$, in (13)–(16). Scan 022 gives relations for the $\varepsilon_i$ in (10). It explains the choice of lifted permutations by embedding the permutation group in $\Gamma$ using the sign character. Its elements fix $P$, so the trivial loop at $P$ gives the lift in (11)–(12).

**023, the cartographic group and its completion.** The inverse image $\Sigma_\tau$ of the subgroup generated by $\tau$ has three involutions as generators. Formula (17) expresses each $l_i$ as a product of two of them. Sending each $\tau_i$ to $-1$ gives a signature homomorphism with kernel $\pi$. Below the dividing line he distinguishes the profinite completion $\widehat\Sigma$ from the doubly hatted group of loop-preserving automorphisms of $\widehat\pi$. Sequence (18) identifies the quotient of the latter by $\widehat\pi$ with the loop-preserving outer automorphism group of $\widehat\pi$.

**024–026, the multiplier and the conjugating elements.** After describing the commutant of the permutation group, he specifies the images of the loops using a multiplier $p=\chi(u)$ and three elements $g_i$. The three images must have product $1$ and generate $\widehat\pi$ topologically. Keeping the choices of $g_i$ gives an extension. He computes composition in (23) and reads the functions $\gamma_i$ extracting the $g_i$ as a cocycle with values in the opposite group in (24). Scan 026 gives the coordinates associated to $(g,(\alpha_i))$ and records how $\rho$ permutes the functions.

**027–029, fixed points and partial splittings.** He takes $\pi$ to be the fundamental group of the projective line punctured at $0,1,\infty$ and studies lifts from $\Gamma$ to $\Sigma$. The fixed points $P,\bar P$ of $\rho$ give order-six stabilizers. For the fixed point $2$ of $\sigma_0$, a quarter of a great circle supplies a path from $P$ to $2$ and lets him transport the stabilizer action between universal covers. He treats $-1$ and $1/2$ in the same way, naming the three points $0',1',\infty'$. On 029 the fixed set of $\tau\dot\sigma_1$ is the unit circle with $1$ removed. It contains $P$ and $\bar P$, so this case yields a lift already encountered.

**029–031, a presentation of the whole extension.** A cancelled attempt using $\rho'$ runs from the foot of 029 into 030. A new list on 030 gives generators above the permutations and $\tau$. On 031 he keeps just $\rho,\sigma_0,\tau_0$. The last relation becomes $[\rho,\sigma_0][\rho^{-1},\tau_0]=1$, giving a quotient of the free product $(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)*\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z$. He observes the symmetry interchanging $\sigma_0$ with $\tau_0$ and $\rho$ with $\rho^{-1}$, and says it cannot respect the extension because the induced map on the quotient would have to preserve the centre.

**032, the discrete extension.** An explicit description of the extension $S\Gamma$ of $\Gamma$ by $\mathbb Z^{\{0,1,\infty\}}$ would give the requested group by the fibre product $S\Sigma=S\Gamma\times_\Gamma\Sigma$.

**033–034, multiplication and conjugation in $S\widehat{\widehat\Sigma}$.** In the section marked with a circled 2 he represents an element by $(p,\sigma,g_0,g_1,g_\infty)$. Relations (2)–(3) specify the product condition and the images of the loop generators. He gives the map to the underlying automorphism and the map $i$ from $\widehat\pi\times\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\{0,1,\infty\}}$. Formula (8) multiplies two tuples, and (9)–(9′) specialize it when the first comes from $i$. Conjugation in (10) sends the group coordinate $g$ to $u(g)$ and the three exponent coordinates to $p\alpha_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}$.

## Questions and conjectures

- **020–022.** “On voudrait maintenant expliciter les $\varepsilon_i$” leads to the displayed square roots. “Il faut expliciter les opérations” on 021 leads to (14). The margin headed “Critique” questions the notation for lifts. On 022 he asks “si $\rho$ s'exprime en fonction des $\varepsilon_i$” and answers “on trouve comme tantôt que non”.
- **024.** The marginal “j'ignore si cette inclusion est une égalité” concerns the displayed inclusion involving the commutant. He restates the same question as equality between the commutant and the subgroup marked by an exclamation point. These sheets leave it open.
- **026.** “Il faudrait expliciter les opérations” under conjugation, “et en particulier l'action de $\rho$”. The marginal “Il y a lieu d'introduire un groupe” proposes keeping track of lifts in a further extension.
- **029–031.** “Il faudrait quand-= donner aussi une description de $\Sigma$ tt entier par gén. et relations” introduces the presentation. The crossed-out “Question” on 030 asks about expressing $\rho$ using the other generators “et substituer dans les autres”. The presentation actually written on 031 retains $\rho$.
- **032.** “Il faudrait encore expliciter l'extension” refers to $S\Gamma$. The sheet gives the resulting fibre-product formula.
- **033–034.** “Cette formule n'est pas, du point de vue du calcul, entièrement explicite et satisfaisante” concerns (8). Computing $u(g'_i)$ still requires expressions for the $g'_i$ as words in the loops or limits of such words. He raises the same difficulty for $u(g)$ after (10).

## Remarks of the model

- **My remark, high confidence, 027–029.** The labels $0',1',\infty'$ give a way to follow the list across the page break. Each of the three real arcs contains exactly one primed point on 028. This accounts for the three lifts named in the opening lines of 029 and helps locate its missing middle index.
- **My reading question, medium confidence, 024 (21)–(22) and 025 (23).** The frozen reading treats several inverse exponents on the conjugators as cancelled, with a live inverse as the alternative at the least clear sites. Short oblique strokes cross the numerals. The positive conjugators in 033 (3) favour cancellation, though a later convention cannot settle the earlier marks.
- **My reading question, medium confidence, 033, left of (6)–(7).** The frozen margin reads “que (4) est surjectif” with $p$ as the alternative to $4$, and gives $i(\widehat\pi)$ as kernel. The middle mark is overwritten. If it refers to the map $p$ in (4), then (7) gives $pi(g;0,0,0)=\mathrm{int}\,g$. This makes the stated kernel difficult to reconcile with that map.
- **My remark, high confidence, 033–034.** Equation (3) specifies $u$ on the three loop generators. Equation (8) asks for its value on another tuple's conjugators, which are arbitrary elements of $\widehat\pi$. That difference explains why a formula defining multiplication can still leave a computation to perform.
