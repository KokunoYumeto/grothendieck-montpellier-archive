# Mathematical notes · Cote 148, scans 44–58

## What the pages do

**44. Topological Teichmüller groupoids.** The objects are compact oriented surfaces with boundary. Its arrows are isotopy classes of orientation-preserving isomorphisms. A variant admits both signs of isomorphism, and a signature morphism identifies its oriented fibre. A marginal note qualifies the discussion for disconnected surfaces.

**45–46. Decorations on a surface.** Scan 45 contains a cancelled two-line beginning about weighted graphs. Scan 46 gives triples consisting of a surface, a one-dimensional submanifold and finitely many marked points. The writing and its revisions distinguish boundary or joining circles, cutting circles and rigidification points. A table picks out full subgroupoids by setting parts of the data empty.

**47. A graph with two weights.** Finite sets of vertices and arcs carry an involution on the arcs and a map giving their origins. A fixed arc represents a free edge. One map assigns genus to a vertex, another assigns a rigidification index to an edge. The degree diagram distinguishes the ordinary two-arc fibres from the one-arc fibres over free edges. The drawing includes isolated vertices, parallel edges and loops.

**48. From a cut surface to its graph.** The vertices come from components of the cut surface. An arc uses a circle and a choice of side or orientation. On a boundary circle the side in the surface is prescribed. The side determines the incident component. The two weights record its genus and the number of marked points on the circle. The page states how the functor acts on $\pi_0$, on $\pi_1$, and on direct sums.

**49. Components, genus and special cases.** Surface and graph have corresponding components. The left margin gives total genus as $\sum_{s\in S}g_s+c(\Gamma)$ and relates the second term to the rank of $H_1(\Gamma)$. In the case with no interior cutting circles, only free edges remain. In the connected special case at the foot, the pair $(g,\nu)$ records genus and number of holes.

**50–51. Keeping track of the marked points.** Finite sets $R_a$ carry circular permutations indexed by orientations of an edge. Reversing the orientation inverts the permutation. A torsor formulation retains the order $r(a)$ and the orientation twist. Scan 51 describes a combinatorial polygon, then a contour of frames on which the two involutions $\sigma_0,\sigma_1$ act. A chosen part supplies the orientation over free edges.

**52–53. Graphs with frames and erasure of a chosen marking.** The diagram on 52 collects the frame, arc, vertex and genus data. The intended combinatorial description must recover the associated functor. Given $R'\subset R$, erasing the chosen points sends the triple to $(X,\Sigma,R\setminus R')$. Scan 53 distinguishes this operation from forgetting which subset was chosen. Its commutative diagram compares the results after retaining the vertex weighting. The marginal NB separates removing all the points on a component from removing only some.

**54. Contracting cutting edges.** Erasing frames corresponds to removing a subset stable under the two involutions. In the next operation, removing selected cutting circles contracts the corresponding graph edges to points. The author calls the resulting map a morphism of *générisation*. The frames on those edges disappear, and the rule for the weights is left to be described.

**55. Filling holes and combining erasures.** Capping selected boundary circles removes the associated free edges and their marks, with the vertex weights kept. The mixed construction selects both marks and circles. Its inclusion condition requires the selected marks to contain those lying over the selected circles or edges.

**56. Adding marks.** On circles already carrying marks, the gaps between them are open intervals. A function $r'$ on these intervals specifies counts of new rigidification points. The resulting marking contains a distinguished subset of new points. Erasing this subset recovers the initial triple.

**57. Uniform operations.** The author considers restricting to operations expressed on each torsor. The order $r'(a)$ for uniform partial erasure divides $r(a)$. For uniform overmarking, $r''(a)$ is a multiple of $r(a)$ with multiplier $d(a)$. The page contrasts the additional structure carried by partial erasure with the data needed for uniform overmarking.

**58. Cutting along selected circles.** The graph operation cuts an ordinary edge at its middle and replaces it by two free edges. The text distinguishes a marked circle from an unmarked one when considering how to reverse the operation. The supplied discussion breaks off at “la façon dont nous allons travailler”.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence.** Erasing the points of $R'$ and forgetting their designation have different outputs. Scan 53 draws separate arrows out of the same marked groupoid. The definition on 52 explicitly removes $R'$ for the first arrow.
- **Model remark, medium confidence.** The warning in the margin of 47 seems to motivate the extra data on 50–52. It asks for a polygon in place of an edge weight. The later pages successively introduce finite sets with circular orders, polygons, and frames. The pages do not explicitly identify the marginal proposal with the final construction.
- **Model reading question, scan 48, line 15, high confidence in the written form.** The frozen reading is $A=\pi_0(\widetilde\Sigma)$. Comparison with the orbit set on 47 can suggest $\widetilde A$ in this position. The second reading found a plain $A$ here and a distinct wavy stroke over $\Sigma$. The difference matters when comparing the edge sets on the two sheets.
