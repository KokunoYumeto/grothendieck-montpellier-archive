# Hand log

The entries follow the leaves in reading order. Each observation applies to the cited examples. An entry does not establish the form of every occurrence.

## Scan 17

The p has a long low loop in the products and in the subscript of t. The two strokes and low return in the coefficient of H read as μ, with a separate 2. The overwritten material before M has less definition than the following G. Three long fine diagonals cross baselines without following a letter. Short horizontal cancellations and the leader from “un torseur sous” are distinct. The right-hand square bracket ends the note on cardinality.

## Scan 18

The low rounded ω recurs in the entries and subscripts of all four diagrams. A long looping T recurs at the three vertices and in the fidelity question. Its top stroke can resemble τ. The 0 over each complex differential is a small ring. The two vertical equality strokes below each middle object in the β diagram differ from the descending arrows above. Sharp and flat-like superscripts are much less distinct than F and V. The local qualifications beside the pair of maps have leaders.

## Scan 19

Reused typescript of a Tribu paper. Typed raised o in “n°”, flat accent bar and separate star over μ. The sloping loop of the final C differs from a typewriter capital and is recorded as a supplied script letter. Ink across the r of “dira” leaves the endpoints readable. The type has a round dot on the i of “emboités”.

## Scan 20

Indices can be on either side of M. The superscripts over the M sequence are small wavy strokes; the leftmost F sits on the following M. Tildes over D reach into adjacent letters. The sequence under Δ and the two shorter sequences distinguish D with and without the tilde. In the rings, double strokes are vertical identifications; dashed return arrows curve against the solid downward arrows. The short enclosing hooks at J and I are inclusions. Two left arrows, then three left arrows connect adjacent columns at lower right. The far right column reaches the paper edge.

## Scan 21

The mathematical type f recurs with the same foot and crosspiece. Inserted a, x, y and z have wider, swept strokes, unlike the narrow keyed z in the Schwarz statement. Φ is a circle on a stem with small horizontal terminals. In both headings PROPOSITION the capitals are ordinary keyed capitals. C beside A has an extra stroke. The nearly colourless impression makes fine vertical bars especially difficult: there is a distinct upright between z and the raised −k in the proof.

## Scan 22

The tall single loop at the two left vertices is ℓ. A separate prime at upper right and a small v above the loop can occur together. The marks above Δ in the cohomology diagram are distinct from the cancellations beside it. Θ has a bar reaching across both sides of its oval. The first G-sequence has an n-like letter heavily retraced; later n's in the same column are much lighter. Some short connecting shafts end in blunt dots.

## Scan 23

The typescript reads after a half turn. On the upright sheet the archive numeral is inverted at the top right. The narrow open C before a singleton and the rounded, reinforced C denoting the plane differ substantially. The character between paths near the end has two meeting upstrokes, ∨. A bent margin stroke and a second upright cross four lines without giving any letters. The small path indices are 1 and 2.

## Scan 25

S with a low 0 distinguishes the base from its lift S. The upright in Spec is larger than the linked strokes of the abbreviated noun “schém.”. Q loops through itself and P has an extended base and a prime. Small Greek σ is separate from W. The double vertical stem of the large functor D is especially clear. The correction above the count of entries has a descending p-like stroke. Horizontal ink deleting “filtration” continues across its formula, while the insertion lies above it.

## Scan 26

The matrix entries and coordinate formulas are copied in their written order. An arrow with a wave above it indicates the splitting, and the top-row u has a prime only on the lower identification. Capital Φ has a horizontal base projecting beyond its stem. The margin slopes upwards towards the right. Its “nuls” is underlined; the words after it turn back towards the page edge.

## Scan 27

The first and second boxes have distinct primes on Φ and ω. The subscript of the second ω′ is ruled through. The hat on Ω, its superscript 1 and its low S/W are three separate features. A short double stroke after “de” has the author's shorthand value “même”. The boxes enclose the equations only.

## Scan 29

The capital phi has a closed oval and an extended baseline. In the compatibility gloss F is drawn directly over an earlier phi. A second pair of triangles in opposite directions makes the over-written connection symbol look like a star. On this leaf short connecting strokes can lack a head. The i in the computation and the i after « et » have a detached dot.

## Scan 30

The small cursive r is an angled two-stroke letter. A heavier downstroke distinguishes the side of V inside U in the patch labels. R has a single principal upright here. The terminal flourish of the plural s returns below the line in the list of families.

## Scan 31

Lowercase varphi has a real descending stem, quite unlike the wide capital with a baseline. The short sigma above u and omega can resemble a bar. The small exclamation above the middle group has a dot. In the final gloss « dans » has a very long continuous middle.

## Scan 32

The corrected reference has a long handwritten 4 on a typewritten digit, unlike the adjoining typewritten 6. Lowercase ell in the type line is a narrow upright crossed diagonally; the added one in the last line has a loop. The manuscript η under X has a descending right arm. The machine's x-strikes can leave the tops of letters, accents and descenders readable.

## Scan 34

The three joining strokes of the upper-left square and the crossbar above the lower square are plain. The two inclusion arrows between the rings have distinct hooks. A short, winding lower-case sigma sits low next to A. The crossed characters immediately before the two live operators have different residual stems. A horizontal extended letter can occupy several letters' space in « endom » and « donné ».
