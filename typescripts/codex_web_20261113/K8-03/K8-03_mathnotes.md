# Mathematical notes on cote 8, scans 17–34

## What the pages do

**17, cancelled items 3–4.** The first item describes the freedom in data attached to a fixed pair by a category of torsors under $\mu_2$. It assigns the isomorphism classes to an $H^1$ torsor and the automorphisms to $H^0$. The second item asks for the equivalences between two such pairs. Its relative group uses a trivialization over $A$, and its last sentence gives uniqueness up to unique isomorphism.

**18, construction and functoriality.** A triple $(\omega,F_\omega,V_\omega)$ gives pullbacks, a triangle and two comparison maps, $\alpha_\omega$ and $\beta_\omega$. The two lower diagrams constrain a morphism given by $u$ and $v$. The writer asks whether the enlarged data recover all morphisms between triples.

**19, « Tribu n° 66 ».** This reused editorial typescript records decisions on drafts 446 and 447. It changes notation and terminology for integration and measures, and assigns a separate chapter to measures on separated spaces. Its collective voice and named editorial assignments belong to the Tribu document.

**20, lifting and descent.** The triangles and the divided-power ring diagrams organize a descent problem for a complex and its two pieces. The writer first lists descent of the pieces and then of the whole triangle. At the foot, choices, automorphisms and an obstruction are assigned to relative Ext groups in degrees 1, 0 and 2. The refinement in degree 1 is expressly tentative.

**21, Proposition 2.** The typescript gives a Schwarz estimate for a holomorphic function with a zero of order $k$. It uses the holomorphy of $z^{-k}f(z)$ and the boundary maximum on $|z|=1$.

**21, Proposition 3.** A uniformly bounded family on each compact subset of a domain in $\mathbb C^p$, with values in a Banach space, is locally controlled on a smaller closed polycylinder. The text uses derivative bounds and integrates along a segment to obtain the common factor $2pM/\rho$ and hence equicontinuity.

**22, filtered complexes.** A triangle is displayed together with filtrations on $\Delta$ and $\Theta$, maps between Hom groups, and cohomology sequences. The example $\Theta=\underline O_X[1]$ leads to a vanishing assertion for one $H^1$ and a nonzero kernel for the map to crystalline cohomology.

**23, variation of amplitude.** The reused typescript expresses the change of amplitude through the integral of $dz/z$, first on a branch of the logarithm and then around a circuit. It extends the definition to arbitrary paths avoiding a point by deformation to a « chemin carrossable ». Reversal changes the sign, and juxtaposition adds the variations.

**25, ordinary Barsotti–Tate groups.** Over a smooth $S_0$ with a formal lift $S$ and a Frobenius lift $F_S$, a category $C$ is specified by sheaves $P',Q'$, their extension $M$, a flat connection, a Frobenius map and a splitting after reduction. The source lists the two induced Frobenius maps separately, with a factor $p$ on the $P'$ map. A contravariant Dieudonné functor lands in $C$.

**26–27, change of splitting.** A splitting expresses the Frobenius and connection through $\Phi$ and $\omega$. Replacing the splitting by one with parameter $L$ changes these entries by $\Phi'=\Phi+pL-F_S^*(L)$ and $\omega'=\omega-dL$. The leaf gives $d\omega=0$, $F_S^*(\omega)-p\omega=d\Phi$ and $\Phi_0=0$ as the three conditions on the entries. The slanted margin on 26 links compatibility with the specified splitting on $S_0$ to vanishing there.

**29, arithmetic and connection matrices.** A short calculation ends in the underlined $2p+i$. Separately, the action of Frobenius is encoded by $\Phi$, invertible after inverting $p$. The boxed formulas express compatibility with the connection, vanishing curvature, and the existence of a $\Psi$ with $\Psi\Phi=p^i\mathrm{id}$.

**30, families of charts.** The drawings pair coordinate neighborhoods $U_i$ with subsets $V_i$. The four named families have different differentiability thresholds. For two members the sheet gives intersection conditions, the two restrictions of charts and two identities involving $U_i\cap V_j$. It gives no proof or attribution for this list.

**31, morphisms and invertible modules.** Compatibility with a connection gives $du=u\circ A-A'\circ u$, with a separate Frobenius square. The invertible-module example specializes to scalar data. An invertible $u$ changes the form by $-du/u$ and the Frobenius entry by the ratio $u/u^\sigma$. The final row expresses classification in terms of a failure of exactness.

**32, reduction and inertia.** The proof distinguishes $\ell\ne p$ from $\ell=p$. The latter case passes to an auxiliary prime and a finite Galois extension giving semi-stable reduction. It tests triviality on three quotients through the trace of their sum. The argument invokes independence of the auxiliary prime and a calculation with $T_p(A_K)$. Remark 5.13.1 then distinguishes virtual good reduction from the additional level condition called « essentielle d'échelon $n$ ».

**34, Lubin–Tate groups.** For a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_p$ with residue field $k$ and uniformizer $\pi$, the page sets $W=W_\infty(k)$ and $A_W=A\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p}W$. It translates the initial group problem into module data with $A$-action, $F_M^f=\pi_M$ and $F_M V_M=V_M F_M=p$. It identifies the scalar automorphism for the resulting semilinearity. The leaf ends with this semilinear reformulation.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1. My observation, high confidence.** On 29, the two parenthesized products in the live arithmetic line have difference $i+2p$, agreeing with the underlined result. The expanded polynomial establishes the equality without needing to identify the overwritten intermediate letter.
- **M2. My observation, high confidence.** On 26–27 the two change-of-splitting laws preserve the compatibility equation $F_S^*(\omega)-p\omega=d\Phi$. Substitution cancels the $p\,dL$ terms and the pullbacks of $dL$, using commutation of pullback with the differential. This checks the signs in the three live formulas.
- **M3. My observation, low confidence.** On 25, « sextuples » would agree with the six visible entries. The old count and the replacement overlap. This observation supplies no additional letter and establishes no error.
- **M4. My observation, medium confidence.** In 26 the upper-right matrix entry $L$ and the formula $u(x,y)=(x,y+Lx)$ can both be read directly. A row-vector convention would reconcile those two particular displays. The leaf does not declare a convention, so the displays alone do not establish that the writer used it throughout.
