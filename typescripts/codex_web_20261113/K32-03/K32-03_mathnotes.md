## What the pages do

**Base change · scan 37.** A Cartesian square with compactifiable $f$ gives a base-change isomorphism involving $g^*Rf_!$. Its transpose is (3.1.6.3). The four arrows of (3.1.6.4) are arranged so the prose can discuss the two diagonals, the hypotheses on the square and the two compatibility statements for composites.

**Abandoned openings · scans 38, 41 and 46.** Scan 38 cancels a heading for a construction of $Rf^!$. Scan 41 cancels “Prenons” and a fresh start with “Soit”. Scan 46 abandons Proposition 3.1.12 in the middle of a word. None goes on to state the proposed result.

**The induction morphism · scan 39.** Starting with a complex-level tensor morphism, 3.1.7 transposes it to internal Hom and then derives it. The six-object diagram compares the numbered arrows through adjunction. The text invokes the projection arrow for ②, deduces the assertion about ①, and restates the result as the “formule d’induction” in Proposition 3.1.8. Two object conditions at the foot remain open.

**Restriction of scalars · scan 40.** For a homomorphism $u:\mathcal B\to\mathcal A$, the special choice of the bimodule complex $L$ identifies an internal Hom with restriction from $\mathcal A$ to $\mathcal B$. A square on $X$ and $S$ expresses its compatibility with $Rf^!$. The writer calls the rôle of $\mathcal A$ in this definition “bidon”.

**The stalk description · scan 42, upper slip.** One line describes $K^!$ on a sheaf using a presheaf of Hom groups. The next gives the stalk of $\underline H^n(RK^!L)$ as a direct limit of Ext groups over neighbourhoods. The small index of the limit is damaged at the edge of the slip.

**The compactification and the flat model · scans 42–43.** The larger sheet uses $f=\bar f j$, a bound on fibre dimensions and a truncated complex to specify $K(U)$. Under $n\mathcal A=0$ it relates the calculation to the constant coefficient $\mathbb Z/n$. It tests flatness for $n=\ell^k$ using the successive powers of $\ell$ and gives a quasi-isomorphism after tensoring with $\mathcal A$. Scan 43 then describes a canonical flat resolution, starting from a module freely generated by the underlying sheaf of sets and iterating on the kernel. It applies this construction to define $K_0(U)$ and states a comparison for an acyclic complex.

**A comparison deferred · scan 44.** The complete mathematical content of the slip is the writer’s sentence that he has not checked agreement of the construction with 3.1.9.

**A family indexed by W · scan 45.** Given a compactification triangle, 3.1.15 specifies a complex $K(W)$ for a triple $W$. The first display uses $\bar X_V=\bar X\times_S V$. The next relates $j_!K(W)$ to a complex on $\bar X$. The marginal diagram and questions trace the maps and ask how to read the coefficient and direct image.

**Adjunction and its localization · scan 47.** Formula (3.1.16.1) is an adjunction between ordinary Hom groups. The text writes it at the level of complexes and restricts along $j:V\to S$. The resulting Hom calculation yields (3.1.16.2), a morphism between derived internal Hom expressions that the text identifies as an isomorphism by localization.

**The closed-point example · scans 47–48.** For $X$ over an algebraically closed field, a closed point $x$ and invertible $n$, 3.1.17 expresses a stalk of $\underline H^{-i}(Rf^!\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)$ as a direct limit of Hom groups involving $H_c^i(U,\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)$. The proposed biduality step identifies a pro-object with one represented by $H_x^i(X,\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)$. The duality displayed next is introduced conditionally.

**Finitude and injective dimension · scan 48.** The introductory line appeals to the construction of the component functors and their preservation of injectives. Proposition 3.1.18 gives a vanishing bound and, after a discarded version, an inequality for injective dimension. The two written arguments of the cohomology groups in (i) are both $L$.

**Quasi-finite maps and immersions · scan 48.** Section 3.1.19 takes $d=0$ in the construction for a quasi-finite map, describes an underived right adjoint and identifies its derived functor. For an immersion the text names “sections à support dans $X$” and writes (3.1.19.1). A pencil line asks for an addition about an étale map.

**Plan and diagonal calculation · scan 48, foot.** A proposed order for §3.1 groups formal properties for fixed $f$, properties for varying $f$ and the local construction. An arrow points back to the boxed base-change addition. The small diagram uses the two projections and the diagonal. The calculation below it annotates successive isomorphisms with base change and a question about relative purity.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · model · high confidence · scan 39.** Under the commutativity stated on the sheet, the four comparison isomorphisms and the isomorphism ② determine ① as a composite of isomorphisms. This check uses the directions and the labels of the diagram. It supplies no independent verification of the projection formula.

**R2 · model · high confidence · scan 48.** To test the literal implication in L48b, take $k=d=1$ and a complex concentrated in degree zero with nonzero degree-zero cohomology. The hypothesis allows it. The conclusion would require its degree-zero cohomology to vanish, since $0\geq 1-2$. The two bounds coincide when $d=0$. This tests the written implication only and does not determine the intended formula for $Rf^!$.

**R3 · model · low confidence · scans 42 and 45.** The pencil question with $2d$ on 42 may belong with the later truncation in (3.1.15.1). Both refer to the degree at which the complex is cut off, but the marks on 42 leave the requested revision open.

**R4 · model · low confidence · scan 48.** The arrow from “à ne pas oublier !” ends at the boxed base-change item of the plan. The lower calculation also labels an isomorphism by base change. The page does not say whether that calculation was intended as the argument for the planned item.
