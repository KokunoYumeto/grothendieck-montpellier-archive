## What the pages do

**Scans 2–3. Programme.** The two title sheets put the relative product and the Hom functor alongside Greenberg, flattening and passage to the quotient. The second also names the automorphism functor. Neither gives a statement about Greenberg.

**Scans 5–7. A representability criterion.** For a flat finite-type morphism \(X\to S\), with \(S\) locally noetherian, the writer considers \(\prod_{X/S}Y/X\). He relates representability to commutation with adic projective limits of Artinian rings. The hypothesis on \(Y\to X\) was changed. A marginal note names a monomorphism, and an insertion treats immersions and closed immersions. Scan 6 is a cancelled start on the same subject.

**Scans 5 and 7. The numbered tests.** The writer invokes fpqc descent and commutation with direct limits. Conditions 4°–6° are reduced to comparing the values at an injection \(A\to A'\) of Artinian rings. For 7° he draws a square involving \(T,T_0,U,U_0\) and uses schematic closure. For 8° he seeks a dense open \(U\) and uses “ess. libre”. The first group-theoretic corollary at the foot of 7 is cancelled.

**Scans 8–9. The local test and condition R.** Condition (L) asks when containment of a neighbourhood of the special fibre forces \(X_T=Y_T\). The proper case is marked “O.K.” For a group and a subgroup the writer passes from the unit section to an open subgroup and gives a discrete valuation test. He lists connected fibres and a condition involving finite subschemes \(G_i\) and \(G_{\bar s}^0\). Scan 9 names condition R and applies it to representation by a closed subscheme of \(S\).

**Scans 9–10. Centralizers, transporters and normalizers.** A cancelled example on 9 leads to the corollary on 10 for \(u:H\to G\). The hypotheses on \(H\) include flatness, finite type and condition R. The text distinguishes representability of the centralizer from the additional assertion about being closed. It introduces a subgroup \(K\), treats the transporter, and ends with the normalizer of \(H\). Several assumptions in the transporter paragraph were revised.

**Scans 11–12. Flattening.** The theorem introduces a coherent \(E\) on \(X\). A two-case formula assigns either the empty set or its singleton to \(S'\), using flatness and a faithfully flat variant. The letters used in the test change on the page. The proof again takes up conditions 1°–8°. Scan 12 spells out (L) for a local base and singles out the proper case and the group case with \(F=\mathcal O_X\).

**Scans 12–13. Factorization and the quotient.** Corollary 3 compares two factorizations of \(f\) with flatness of the projection \(X\times_S X\to X\). The short qualifier on \(f'\) is uncertain. The proof introduces \(R=X\times_S X\), the sheaf \(X/R\), and an \(S'\) representing it. It uses a base change to discuss the covering \(X\to S'\). A second empty-set/singleton formula describes the quotient subfunctor. The text then returns to (L) over an affine noetherian base.

**Scan 14. Two existence statements.** For \(G\) acting on \(P\), the first corollary selects a base \(S'\) through which \(P\to S\) factors and over which \(P\) is principal homogeneous. It specifies faithful flatness over \(S'\). The second corollary considers a homomorphism \(u:G\to H\) of finite presentation with flat kernel \(N\) and concludes that \(G/N\) exists.

**Scans 16–17. Restriction of Hom functors.** Diagrams over \(S\) and \(S'\) introduce restriction along \(\varphi:Z\to X\). A box proposes expressing it in terms of relative products. The pointwise test for the unramified locus is an injection between two Hom groups involving \(\Omega^1\). Coherent modules encode this test. The map labelled (*) points towards \(M_u\). Its surjectivity after tensoring with \(k(s)\) is used with Nakayama to obtain a neighbourhood. Scan 17 names the resulting subfunctor \(F_\varphi\) and states relative representability by unramified morphisms.

**Scans 17–18. Prolongation.** The proof fixes \(v\) and asks for \(u'\) satisfying the composition and module conditions. In the remaining one-dimensional case the text introduces \(J\), \(S_0=V(J)\), an initial map and a compatible generic prolongation. The first square compares four sets \(P\) of prolongations. The second places corresponding Hom groups in the same four positions. Stars mark the upper-right and lower-left corners of the first square. The supplied text ends mid-sentence.
