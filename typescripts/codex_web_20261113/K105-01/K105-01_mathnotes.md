# Mathematical notes on cote 105, scans 2–8 and 10–18

## What the pages do

**Lifting tests in categories, scan 2.** The French lemma tests a map to $e$ against $\phi\to e$ and a family of product maps involving $\partial$, $I$ and $S^n(N)$. It concludes that $X$ is $W_\infty$-aspheric. The second clause uses left or right cones and speaks of $W_\infty$-properness or smoothness. A corollary gives further lifting tests and ends with $W_\infty$-perfection. Several words specifying the tests in the corollary are hard to recover.

**The arrow-category projection, scan 3.** The cancelled draft draws $\underline{\mathrm{Ar}}(C)\xrightarrow{p} C\times C$. It distinguishes categorical fibrations from the proposed $W_\infty$-fibrations, considers properness and smoothness, and ends by asking for a Quillen structure on $(Cat)$.

**Two pairs of lifting classes, scans 5 and 7.** In a topos $A$ the writer begins with $W\subset\mathrm{Fl}(A)$ and three clauses of weak saturation. Generating classes $(TC)_0\subset C_0$ give $F,TF$ by right lifting and $TC,C$ by left lifting. A square pairs $TC$ with $F$ and $C$ with $TF$. On the next leaf he defines $\widetilde W$ as maps $f=pi$ with $p\in TF$ and $i\in TC$ and writes $TC=C\cap\widetilde W$ and $TF=F\cap\widetilde W$. He emphasizes that $W$ itself has not yet entered those conclusions.

**Products and internal Hom, scans 7 and 10.** For $i:A\to B$ and $f:X\to Y$, the manuscript writes a comparison from $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(B,X)$ to a fiber product, labelled $\underline{\alpha}(i,f)$. It formulates closure under cartesian products and under internal Hom. The proof on scan 10 moves the lifting problem across the product–Hom relation in each direction.

**The boxed product, scans 10 and 12.** A second lifting problem introduces a primed test map. The writer displays an amalgamated sum mapping to $B'\otimes B$ and relates the nested $\alpha$ to an $\alpha$ for a boxed product. Five rows on scan 12 collect closure conditions. The order of the two classes in the last row and the final letter $F$ are significant features of that display.

**Monomorphisms and intervals, scans 12 and 14.** In the topos example $C$ consists of monomorphisms. A generating map uses a monomorphism $i_0:A_0\to B_0$, an interval $I$ and its section $e$. The written source is $(B_0\times e)\cup(A_0\times I)$. Two squares expand what happens on taking a boxed product with $i'$. The French sentence on scan 14 expresses the result as $h(i'\boxtimes i_0,I,e)$ and immediately calls for care.

**Factorisation and the choice of intervals, scans 14 and 16.** M1 and M5 remain outstanding. The writer provisionally assumes the two factorizations, then turns to $\widetilde W=W$. Taking all $W$-aspheric intervals would give a family that he calls not “small”. He proposes taking some such family and determining what it must satisfy.

**Inclusions in $W$, scans 16 and 18.** The first inclusion singled out is $TF\subset W$. The handwritten condition numbered 1 uses $L_A\to e$ and $UW$; the preceding box explains the notation using products with every $X$. The next reduction is to $(TC)_0\subset W$. A pushout square on scan 16 leads into the conditions on intervals, monomorphisms, limits and direct factors on scan 18. There the factorisation argument produces a section and a retract. The leaf ends with the implication from the five numbered conditions to $\widetilde W\subset W$.

Scan 4 is a marked cover. Scans 6, 8, 11, 13, 15 and 17 contain administrative computer output or service notices.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 3, cancelled.** “Il semblerait que $p$ soit une $W_\infty$-fibration. … C’est une chose à vérifier.” The earlier “Peut-être au moins” asks about the two composites. The final question asks whether the fibrations and corresponding cofibrations “font-ils une … structure de Quillen sur $(Cat)$ ?”
- **Scans 7 and 10.** “under suitable assumptions” qualifies the displayed implications. The separate “(is in $\Phi_{*}$ ?)” and “check language more carefully” leave the formulation for the general lifting classes open. The capital in that notation carries a doubt mark.
- **Scans 10–12.** “Thus we have to check” leads to closure under $i\boxtimes i'$. Scan 12 adds “(resp. in $C$?)” twice and considers requiring just one of the two maps to belong to $TC$.
- **Scans 14–16.** “We want even prove $\widetilde W=W$”; “But this may make a difficulty for factorisation”; “see what we need on it”. The choice of the interval family and the equality are goals. Scan 16 separately writes a question mark above the inclusion into $W$.
- **Scan 18.** “not a consequence of saturation?” qualifies closure of $W$ under direct factors. The manuscript concludes the inclusion under the listed conditions and leaves this question open.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence, scans 5–18.** The manuscript numbers link seven English leaves across the intervening printer sheets. “provided” at the head of scan 12 continues the last sentence on scan 10. The opening “because this bunch is not ‘small’” on scan 16 answers the difficulty at the foot of scan 14.
- **Model remark, high confidence, scans 14, 16 and 18.** The desired equality and the conclusion on the last leaf have different logical strength. The former is explicitly an aim on scan 14. The final sentence on scan 18 concludes $\widetilde W\subset W$.
- **Model remark, low confidence, scan 18.** A separate mark following “Thus we need” could be another circled 4. It shares the upright and crossbar of a numeral, but its enclosure merges with those strokes. The unambiguous circled 4 above “filtering direct” is formed differently. No change in numbering convention follows from the uncertain mark.
