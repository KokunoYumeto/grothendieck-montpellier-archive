# Hand log · cote 105, scans 2–18

Positions run down each face in reading orientation.

### Scan 2

The heavy closed hook before a product is read as ∂. There are two short parallel strokes on the terminal relation underneath it. The short terminal flick on an arrow often merges into its shaft. The paired abbreviation at the right reads “resp.”. The counters in the lowest lines are filled with heavy ink, so their outlines alone do not consistently distinguish letters.

### Scan 3

Two long diagonal pen strokes cancel the whole discussion, including the last question. The individual shorter strikes underneath are separate cancellations. The two signs joined by small round o's are underlined s and b in the first pair. At the second pair the second s has its own underline and b stands at the right. The horizontal pair used for “même” is explicit in “De =” and “lui-=”.

### Scan 4

The lighter pencil spells Ahmed. Two darker oblique lines run through the name, one beginning before the capital. This hand cannot be identified from this word. The lower curved double line has two dark ends and a detached fleck at the upper end. Its status as an intentional mark is uncertain.

### Scan 5

Small paired u-like and v-like strokes in the two-out-of-three condition need the product on the line above. Short underlines under NB are distinct from the strokes through the abandoned inclusions. The last diagram has a central vertical stroke that passes through both horizontal double-ended arrows. The right margin is a separate slanting English glossary.

### Scan 6

The face is a job-accounting printout. The isolated thin 5 in the lower left has the same position and pencil as the other archive numbers. The black rows of asterisks belong to the printer's table. At the cut right edge the next paging-field letter shows its left upright and two horizontal strokes.

### Scan 7

A tilde stands clear of W at the top. The upright parallel bars under it express a vertical equality. The i in the factorization has a dot and no descender, while the f at the start has a loop below the baseline. A compact rounded membership sign closes in the last line. The two vertical implication signs at the foot share a slanted marginal qualification.

### Scan 8

The system print uses a short tail to distinguish Q from zero in the bottom priority field. The time starts at the left cut edge. The light crossed 7 below the listing is the running archive foliation. There is no manuscript continuation on the printed face.

### Scan 10

The looped capital for a class has an oval and a flat foot like Φ. Its upper and lower stars often occupy both places during revision. Several lower stars are inked over. The easily confused i′ and j′ in the argument of external Hom show a substantial descender in the first occurrence. The crossed circle and crossed square of the tensor constructions are distinct. Lower-left notes slope upwards to the right.

### Scan 11

Read the job sheet a quarter-turn counterclockwise. In the printer font capital I has full upper and lower bars. Zero and O have nearly identical contours in the JES output identifier, where the following line of print helps but does not supply an extra stroke. The archive numeral stays upright in the unrotated image.

### Scan 12

The central list has several layers. Round and square frames enclose similar crosses; the frames decide the operator. The top line uses long leaders to insert two parenthetical clauses. Several letters in the last square were laid over others, so the ordering of the underlying letters remains uncertain. The two 1s at the foot are distinct from the pen number 4 above.

### Scan 13

The fixed-pitch job card is sideways. Capital O and zero have a similar outline; the right-hand sequence digits repeat in a fixed eight-column field. The pencilled 12 belongs to the archive, with the same orientation as the manuscript rather than the print.

### Scan 14

Separate braces in the square point to names of vertical morphisms. The foot of the upright I is retraced on both lower vertices. The page number 5 has a detached top and little hook. The margin at the left becomes two legible underlined lines when rotated clockwise. The word after constructing is reduced to an initial and undulations, so its ending is uncertain.

### Scan 15

The group after each time is J0365CNU, with upright capital J and a zero in the next position. The time separators are points. This is fixed-pitch print; the pencilled archive number at lower left in portrait orientation is 14.

### Scan 16

The wave over W and question above inclusion have independent positions. Labels beside the diagram at the foot attach to vertical inclusions; the doubled horizontal sign next to W is a separate implication. The left note has to be rotated about sixty-four degrees. Several words run together there, especially the qualification of the topos and the sentence following the brace.

### Scan 17

No manuscript corrections to the notices. French capitals lack accent marks even in NUMEROTATION, RESEAU and the other printed headings. Points and slashes distinguish the two forms of the terminal code. The pencil numeral 16 is upright with the portrait facsimile.

### Scan 18

His circled numbers here read 2, 4, 3, 5 in spatial order. A small hook is often much clearer than a nearly level arrowhead. The f-arrow along the base of the little triangle at left points back to A. In the lower square a broad upright bowl has very little indication of a middle bar; B and D remain alternatives.

### Cross-page comparison

Scans 5 and 7 use Fl with no separately visible final stop. In scans 7, 10 and 12 the line underneath Hom or α is part of the notation. A line through the same sign on scan 10 marks a rejected start. Scans 10 and 14 repeat the oval-and-foot class sign with stars above or below; both first occurrences retain the Phi/script-L alternative. On scans 12 and 14 an e and a zero beside B make a product B₀×e; the small zero is below the line. The upright I in scans 12, 14, 16 and 18 has an upper and lower bar, often much thicker after revision. The wave over W in scans 7, 14, 16 and 18 is distinct from the level bar in the last paragraph of scan 18. The handwritten 1–7 and the corner numerals belong to different series.
