## What the pages do

**Scans 205–206, (34)–(45) · LIN205, STAR205.** A determinant generator $e$ specifies an edge set by $x\wedge y=e$. Its alternating form defines a map $\Phi_e$ on the module and a map $\varphi_e:S\to S^\vee$ on sign classes. The star of a vertex is read through the pairing with the second set. The following lines give a strictness hypothesis and single out an exceptional case.

**Scans 206–207, (46)–(55) · PARAM207.** Units modulo sign act on both sets. Replacing $e$ by $\lambda e$ multiplies $\Phi_e$ by $\lambda^{-1}$. The author then indexes the gauges by forms on the determinant instead. The inverse scalar in (53) and the direct scalar in (55) refer to these two choices.

**Scans 208–209, (56)–(63) · PRE208.** A pregraph consists of two sets and an incidence relation. Separation means that the sets of incident elements distinguish points. Exchanging the two sets transposes the relation. Under separation on both sides, each component of a relation-preserving pair determines the other. Diagram (63) gives the two projections of the automorphism group.

**Scans 209–211, (64)–(65) · AD210, GAUGE211.** A homothety is recorded with the inverse on one component. For the graph of a bijection this gives the antidiagonal of a product of permutation groups. The author considers an alternative notation using automorphisms, then follows what it does to the pairing and to compatibility with a chosen gauge $\varphi_0$.

**Scans 211–212, (65)–(67) · COMM212, FREE212.** The extra condition is $\lambda^\vee\varphi_0=\varphi_0\lambda^{-1}$. After examining composition, he fixes a commutative group of homotheties. Its action takes compatible gauges to compatible gauges and acts freely on their set $\Pi_Z$. A marginal note explains where the initial gauge comes from.

**Scans 212–214, (68)–(70) · TOR213, SUB213, FREE212.** The wish for transitivity leads to (68). The author circles “torseur” and writes “faux”. Below it he gives a bijection from a particular subset $\mathcal G(\varphi_0)$ to the gauges, and says that subset need not be a group. The “Scholie” specifies the two gauge conditions. Its marginal correction again retains freeness of the $Z$-action.

**Scans 215–216, (71)–(73) · INV215, LOOP216.** With the diagonal as incidence relation, a gauge is an involution which reverses each element of the chosen commutative group. On a torsor with an origin, the examples have the form $z\mapsto\lambda-z$. A loop is a solution of $2z=\lambda$. Varying $\lambda$ can therefore change whether the graph has loops.

**Scans 216–217, (74)–(77) · EVAL217.** Starting with a single $Z$-torsor of gauges $\Pi$, the author evaluates $(\varphi,s)$ at $\varphi(s)$. This passes to $\Pi\wedge_Z(S!)$ and identifies it with $S^\vee$. Here $S!$ uses the inverse action. The last line asks about equivariance for a larger group.

**Scan 218, (78), (79) and (79 bis) · EQ218, LIN218.** For an automorphism centralizing $Z$, (78) tests ordinary commutation with a gauge. One gauge in $\Pi$ suffices. Formula (79) defines the largest subgroup passing this test. The diagonal example identifies it with the commutant of an involution. A slanting linear example reaches $\det u\in\pm1$ and adds a qualification after (79 bis).

**Scan 219, (81)–(86) · DUAL219.** The starting tuple is restated, then the two sets are exchanged, the incidence relation and the operator pairs are transposed, and the gauge set is inverted. Equations (84)–(85) compare the actions under $\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{-1}$. The last formula reads $S\simeq\Pi\wedge_Z(S^\vee!)$ and ends with a comma.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, high confidence · AD210.** In the three-point diagonal example, the transpositions $(12)$ and $(23)$ give a quick test of the antidiagonal. Their product is a three-cycle. The second component of the product of the two antidiagonal pairs is that same cycle, whereas its inverse is the opposite cycle.
- **Model remark, high confidence · TOR213, FREE212.** Take the three-point diagonal relation and $Z=1$, using the author's examples on 215 and 218. There are four gauges (the identity and the three transpositions), and the centralizer has six elements. A four-element set cannot be a torsor for this six-element group. The trivial $Z$ acts freely. These counts distinguish the claim marked “faux” from the assertion retained at the foot of 213.
- **Model remark, high confidence · LOOP216, EQ218.** In the author's order-two example, the parameters $0$ and $1$ give two and zero fixed points respectively. In the separate three-point example with gauge $(12)$, precisely two of the six permutations commute with the gauge. The latter gives a concrete instance where (77) fails for some automorphisms.
- **Model remark, low confidence · LIN218.** The words “du moins” after (79 bis) suggest that the determinant condition describes the part of $\mathcal G_0$ visible in the linear group. Several words in that marginal qualification carry doubt marks, so its precise scope is open.
- **Model reading question, low confidence · READ219.** Scan 219, slanted left note beside (82)(1)–(2), final TeX line 29. The frozen word before “exiger que $R$ soit séparante” is “moins”. The first strokes, when viewed in the direction of the note, also permit “sans”. The latter gives an intelligible qualification, but the initial letter is crowded against the previous line.
