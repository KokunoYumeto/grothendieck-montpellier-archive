# Mathematical notes · K10-03

## What the pages do

**89 · Admissible filtration.** A fibre functor $F$ takes values in locally free sheaves on $S$. Conditions (0)–(iv) ask for locally direct factors, additivity, compatibility with tensor products and duals, and a filtration which is locally exhaustive and separated. The author then passes to $\operatorname{gr}F$, discusses its tensor unit, and considers exactness when the source is semisimple.

**90–91 · Splittings and their torsor.** Put $G=\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}_S(F)$. The isomorphisms from $F$ to its associated graded form $P$. Those that respect the filtration and act identically on the graded pieces form $Q$. The text assigns $Q$ a left action by $H'$ and a right action by $H$. The inserted “quasi-” and the local-section question qualify both torsor descriptions.

**91 · Infinitesimal description.** A generator $M$ tests how an automorphism acts on the filtration of $F'(M)$. The boxed formula gives the Lie algebra of $H'$ as the sum of the strictly positive weight spaces. The bracketed note gives the nonnegative sum for the subgroup preserving the filtration. It identifies a centralizer and then names a Levi subgroup.

**93 · Descent.** In characteristic zero with semisimple source, the summary gives graded fibre data, a cocharacter $i_1$ and a right torsor $Q'$ under the indicated subgroup. Twisting $F'$ by $Q'$ recovers the fibre functor with its descended filtration. Triviality of the torsor is related to splitting of the filtration.

**93, 95 · Complementary cocharacters and Tate degree.** A central $j$ determines $i_2$ by $i_1(\lambda)i_2(\lambda)=j(\lambda)$. The corresponding parabolics are called opposite. With the Tate structure, the character $\varepsilon i_1$ gives $\lambda^n$. The specified compatibility asks for $n=1$.

**95–96 · De Rham, Hodge and Betti.** For motifs over a connected prescheme over $\mathbb Q$, the example takes the filtered de Rham functor and identifies its associated graded with the Hodge functor. When the base is a point the page asserts a splitting. An embedding $K\to\mathbb C$ gives the Betti fibre functor. The complex comparisons and the real involution $\sigma$ lead to the written expression $F^{(p)}_{\mathbb C}\cdot\sigma(F^{(q)}_{\mathbb C})$ for $F^{p,q}_{\mathbb C}$. The last prime in the comparison display is uncertain.

**97–98 · Stabilizer, kernel and weights.** A cocharacter $j$ grades the forgetful functor on representations of $G$. The filtration on $V$ is the sum of the pieces of weights $i\ge p$. The group $P$ preserves this filtration and $U$ acts identically on its graded pieces. The weight calculation for their Lie algebras uses a faithful $V$ and the smallest nonzero weight of an operator. A subsequent cancelled construction intersects $G$ with stabilizers in an ambient automorphism group.

**99 · Parabolicity and splitting.** The proposition identifies $P$ as a parabolic subgroup and $U$ as its unipotent radical for connected reductive $G$ of finite type. The text distinguishes characteristic zero from positive characteristic. The corollary applies a vanishing of $H^1(k,U)$ to a splitting assertion over a perfect field.

**99 · Generation and fixed vectors.** For $T=\operatorname{Im}j$ and a dense open $U'\subset U$, the remark asserts that the conjugates $uTu^{-1}$ generate $T.U$ over an infinite field. It sketches a Lie-algebra argument in characteristic zero and writes the common fixed vectors as an intersection. A further argument involving $V^P$, $V^U$ and rank one is crossed out.

**111 · Finite-type representations.** An abandoned opening over a field expresses a representation as a direct limit of finite-dimensional stable subspaces. The revised paragraph distinguishes quasi-coherent $G$-modules from its finite-type subcategory $C_f(G)$ and displays $C(G)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ind}C_f(G)$ as an equivalence. The hypotheses show several layers of correction.

**111–112 · Affine base change.** For affine $f:T\to S$, put $\mathscr B=f_*\mathcal O_T$. A $G_T$-action on $\underline F$ is described as a $G$-action on $\underline E=f_*\underline F$ commuting with $\mathscr B$. The two product and automorphism displays explain the assertion. The text then describes how the pair consisting of the category and its forgetful functor determines the corresponding data after base change, including the finite-type objects.

**113 · Reconstruction.** The author constructs the map from $G(A)$ to the tensor automorphisms of the forgetful functor. The diagram retains a cancelled second target. The affine algebra of $G$ is proposed as the place to test injectivity and surjectivity. The page ends by asking which tensor categories with a functor to modules arise this way.

Scans 92 and 94 contain printed letterheads. Scan 100 is the cover « Notes Saavedra ».

## Questions and conjectures

- **90–91.** « Il faut s'assurer que loc. … $Q$ admet une section » and « s'il est vrai que les deux foncteurs fibre sont isomorphes » identify the missing local step. « Il semble que ça marche du moins » introduces the characteristic-zero, semisimple case.
- **91.** The slanted, struck « A examiner » question asks whether smoothness passes from $G'$ to $H'$. Its verb groups carry doubt marks. The concluding « Pb : étudier les relations » concerns one-parameter subgroups and parabolics with Levi decompositions.
- **95.** « Cela doit être $1$ » is the expected Tate degree in the case under consideration.
- **99.** « Il faut sans doute travailler un peu » explicitly reserves the positive-characteristic argument. The marginal qualification about connectedness has three possible opening words. The bracket « ou sur un anneau quelc. » suggests an extension of the field corollary.
- **111.** « Nous voudrions … définir » introduces the problem of describing equivariant modules after affine base change. Scan 112 supplies the description.
- **113.** « Dans quelle mesure $G$ se récupère-t-il », « On présume que c'est injectif » and « On présume sans doute aussi que c'est surjectif » remain questions on the sheet. The second assertion has the annotation « à détailler ». « À quelle condition » asks for a characterization of categories arising from an affine group scheme. « Il faudrait supposer » introduces the Ab-topos and direct-limit hypotheses.

## Remarks of the model

- **R91-weights · Model remark, high confidence.** Scan 91 places degree zero in the Lie algebra of the filtration stabilizer and excludes it from the boxed Lie algebra. The two final sums on 98 repeat exactly that distinction. The lower bounds on both pages are clear.
- **R95-splitting · Model remark, high confidence.** Scan 93 relates the triviality of $Q$ to the existence of a splitting. On 95 « le splitting … n'est pas canonique » concerns the choice of one. The two passages therefore make compatible claims.
- **S98-example · Model remark, high confidence.** In a two-dimensional graded space with basis of weights zero and one, take the operator sending the first basis vector to the second and annihilating the second. It preserves the written filtration and has smallest nonzero operator weight $+1$. This tests the standalone comparison with zero without assigning a reading to the previous doubtful letter.
- **R99-fixed · Model remark, high confidence as a conditional.** If the conjugates in the generation remark generate $T.U$, the vectors fixed by all of them are exactly the vectors fixed by $T.U$. This accounts for the live intersection formula independently of the subsequent cancelled equalities involving $G$ and $P$.
- **R113-scope · Model remark, low confidence.** The repeated cancellations of $f$ may indicate that the author was reconsidering how much finite-type data to retain. The visible pattern supports a change of formulation. It does not identify a final finiteness hypothesis.

- **Reading question · 93, start of the descent paragraph.** The frozen text has « A ces données ». A closer look while reading the descent construction shows a detached down-right stroke directly above the capital. « À ces données » better accounts for that stroke. **High confidence** in the accent.
- **Reading questions · 112, affine-algebra paragraph and last line.** Before *défini*, the frozen candidates are plain $B$, script $B$, $T$ and calligraphic $B$. The sign also looks like a single-bow $D$ with an interior downstroke. **Low confidence** in this further candidate. At the end of the page the frozen reading is plain $B$ in « sur $B$ ». The long diagonal foot there allows $\mathscr B$, like the sheaf of algebras higher on the page. **Medium confidence** in the script reading.
