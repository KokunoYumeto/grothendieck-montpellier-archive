# Mathematical notes on cote 57

## What the pages do

**Local Picard groups in dimension one and in characteristic \(p\) (193).** The first remark starts with the kernel of a map from \(\mathrm{Pic}(\widehat E)\) to \(\mathrm{Pic}(X)\). It relates connected components of local Picard groups to \(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z\), with \(d\) the projective degree of \(X\). It then suggests a version in higher dimension in positive characteristic, allowing \(p\)-groups in the comparison. The obstruction groups written down are \(H^2(X,\underline O_X(n))\).

**Passage through the henselization (194).** The second remark inserts the henselization between \(S\) and its completion in a sequence of Picard groups of punctured schemes. The author asks how generally the last map is an isomorphism. He invokes the blow-up in the case where \(X\) has dimension one and points back to an example with plane curves.

**Title leaf (195).** The heading pairs \(G^\tau\) and \(G^0\) with the local structure of the relative Picard object. “Locale” is an addition. The cancelled subtitle is too heavily crossed to use as a description of the contents.

**Variation of the finite quotient (196).** For a projective group scheme over a local noetherian base the manuscript writes \(T_t(G)=G_t/G_t^0\) and assigns the quotient a rank \(\sigma(t)\). Four conditions compare existence of an abelian subscheme, flatness of a quotient and equality of these ranks. The writer distinguishes what follows when \(G^0\) is universally open and what follows over a reduced base.

**Semicontinuity and the valuative argument (196).** The lemma concerns upper semicontinuity of \(\sigma\). Over a discrete valuation ring the argument compares the rank at the closed point with the ranks of the two fibres of a finite quotient \(Q\), then considers equality in each comparison. A continuation written sideways at the left uses a parameter scheme for \(A\) and appeals to a valuative criterion.

**Dimension and chains in the typescript (197).** Items (i)–(iv) recall the number of parameters in a local noetherian ring, the chain condition for a regular local ring, dimension and transcendence degree for an integral algebra of finite type over a field, and a Hilbert–Samuel formulation. Proposition 7 and its corollaries restate dimensions geometrically for algebraic preschemes and their irreducible components.

**Torsion ranks and cohomology (198).** A corollary compares ranks of the kernels of multiplication by \(n\) in a closed fibre and at generizations. A large question mark accompanies the assertion. Beneath it a three-column calculation sets lengths of three \(H^1\) groups above expressions involving \(\pi_1\), the \(p^n\)-torsion of Pic, and a kernel of \(F^n\). Bars, coefficients and a crowded additive-part label matter to interpreting this table.

**Two cautions about dimension (199).** The reused typescript distinguishes failure of the chain condition from failure of strict equidimensionality. Its second example uses a discrete valuation ring \(V\), its fraction field \(K\), residue field \(k\) and an indeterminate \(T\). It compares maximal ideals of ranks two and one and writes down \((aT-1)\) in the rank-one case.

**Ring and local-ring formulations (199).** “2. Dimension des preschemas.” defines dimension and rank by passage to the spectrum. Proposition 6 identifies a codimension with a local-ring dimension; the typed subscript is \(X/Y\). The corollaries express the chain condition and the supremum defining dimension in terms of local rings and, in the noetherian case, closed points.

**Components of a group over a field (201).** A four-part statement treats finite type and irreducibility of the connected components, invariance of the identity component, translation by rational points and existence of the discrete quotient by \(G_0\). The first formulation is cancelled. The new numbering and altered positions of the zero on \(G\) are visible.

**The algebraically closed case (201–202).** The proof uses a product of connected schemes and a neighbourhood \(U\) of the identity to obtain \(U^2=G_0\). It passes to rational points, brings in intersection of nonempty opens, and appeals to translation when discussing irreducible components. At the end of 202 the author returns to an arbitrary field.

**Descent and open subgroups (203–204).** The argument follows a closed point after extension to \(\bar k\), taking a finite union of its geometric components. The corollary concerns an open subgroup \(H\), closedness, the faithfully flat map onto \(G/H\), and its group structure when \(H\) is distinguished. The proof first takes \(H=G_0\). The opening line of 204 dispatches the general case.

**Torsion in the group of components (204–205).** For commutative \(G\), the notes introduce \(\Gamma\), the multiplication maps, their kernels indexed by \(n\), and the open subgroup \(\Gamma^\tau\). They give a pointwise torsion criterion. For quasi-compact \(S\) over \(k\) the corollary identifies \(\Gamma^\tau(S)\) with the torsion of \(\Gamma(S)\).

**Definition of \(G^\tau\) and a reformulation (205–206).** The inverse image of \(\Gamma^\tau\) in \(G\) is denoted \(G^\tau\). The text compares least subgroups with specified quotients and unions of finite subgroups. Scan 206 recasts the pointwise tests using \(\pi_n\), a finite subgroup and powers of the point. The writer flags a separate issue for the radicial case. Some of the qualifications at the foot of 205 are overwritten.

**A finite-type criterion (208).** The proposition starts with a locally finite-type separated open morphism and proper fibres, over locally noetherian preschemes. Its proof shrinks the base, uses a quasi-compact open containing the generic fibre, and compares it with a proper model. Openness and properness are then used to rule out a nonempty complement. A marginal question connects this criterion with the relative Picard object.

**The locally constructible subset (209).** The corollary replaces the source by a locally constructible subset \(Z\). A pointwise lifting condition for generizations replaces an earlier openness clause. After shrinking, a proper piece \(Z'\) is separated from its complement. The proof ends with the case of empty generic fibre and the assertion that \(Z\) is empty.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 199. Model remark, high confidence.** The choice of a generator \(a\) of the maximal ideal explains the example \((aT-1)\). The quotient of \(V[T]\) by that ideal is \(V[1/a]=K\). Localizing at \(a\) identifies its prime chain with the one below the linear polynomial \(T-a^{-1}\) in \(K[T]\). This checks the rank-one example appearing on the sheet.

**M2 · Scans 204–205. Model remark, medium confidence.** The quasi-compact qualification in the statement about \(\Gamma^\tau(S)\) has a concrete use. Once local sections are individually annihilated on a finite cover, a common multiple of their orders annihilates the section. This checks the finite-bound step; the existence of the open kernels is part of the manuscript's argument.

**M3 · Scans 208–209. Model remark, high confidence about the structure of the argument.** In 208 openness lets a nonempty complement reach the generic point after the reduction to an integral base. The last sentence on 209 uses the lifting condition in (iii) at exactly the corresponding point: a point of \(Z\) would have a generization above the generic point. This explains the particular replacement made in the crossed condition. Its exact superseded wording remains uncertain.

**Reading question · Scan 202, L6.** The final reading is \((U\cap xU)(k)\). A possible alternative is \((U\cap xU^{-1})(k)\): the upper strokes at the join with \((k)\) are crowded. The first form is what the independent audit could distinguish. The transcription remains fixed.
