# Mathematical notes on cote 67, scans 53–68

## What the pages do

**M01 · Circles, gauges and duality (53).** A table relates toric circles, real Euclidean vector planes and unitary complex lines. Its oriented row leads to torsors under the circle group. The calculations below relate a pair of gauges and a point of a contracted product to a duality between two complex lines and a gauge on one of them. The crossed alternatives in the table and the subscripts of the two \(J\)-terms are especially hard to read.

**M02 · Recovering the boundary charts (54–56).** The local model is a closed half-plane. The author distinguishes the germs extendible across the real line from continuous functions holomorphic in the interior. Théorème 1 says that the interior conformal structure determines the boundary charts. Lemme 1 applies reflection to an open embedding. To obtain an actual chart at the boundary, the argument then examines the first nonzero power \(a_nz^n\) and concludes \(n=1\).

**M03 · A second equivalence (56).** A bracketed Théorème 2 identifies compact bordered conformal surfaces with smooth proper algebraic curves over \(\mathbb R\). It occupies a separate paragraph between the power-series argument and the new section on cutting.

**M04 · Cutting (56–58).** Théorème 3 gives the cut surface \(\widetilde X\) a sheaf of continuous functions conformal in its interior. Lemme 2 splits the local problem into a point inside a segment and an endpoint. At an inside point one chooses a bank. At an endpoint the two half-intervals on the boundary map injectively onto the same part of the segment. The two sketches on 57 correspond to these two cases.

**M05 · Comparing the banks (58).** The scholium gives each bank a real analytic structure and asks how regular the identity between them is. This separates the analytic structure seen from one bank from the regularity of the curve as embedded.

**M06 · Sewing (58–61).** Théorème 4 proposes an equivalence between surfaces equipped with a complex \(K\) and vertices \(S\), and cut surfaces equipped with marked boundary points and an involution. The reverse construction uses the topological quotient and a sheaf of continuous functions. In the rewritten Lemme 3, two copies of a half-plane are sewn along an interval in case a). In case b), one half-plane is sewn to itself at the two intervals meeting at zero. The dated c) is a separate oblique addition.

**M07 · Capping a circle (61–63).** With \(S\) empty, the author restricts the boundary involution. He relates a projective structure on a chosen compact boundary circle to capping it with a disc. Choosing a centre in the disc gives a reduction to a compact subgroup and a toric structure. The proposition places a bordered surface with a toric boundary circle on one side and a surface with a marked pointed disc on the other.

**M08 · The shadow of a disc (62–64).** The pointed disc determines a Euclidean disc in the conformal tangent line at its centre. Scan 64 calls the image \(o(D)\), the “ombre de \(D\)”. Concentric shrinking scales the shadow. The author then considers a finite family of disjoint discs about marked points and asks for hypotheses under which their shadows recover them.

**M09 · An admissibility condition (65–66).** Removing the interiors of the discs gives \(X^*\). Each boundary circle can inherit a toric structure from its disc and another from the metric on the complement. Equality of the two defines admissibility. The following question asks how one could recognize it from the geometry of \(X\), especially when \(X\) has a Bergman metric.

**M10 · Capping in Teichmüller space (66–68).** The diagram sends \(TB_{g,\nu}\) to \(T_{g,\nu}\). The coordinate model at the source contains \(\nu\) real length parameters in addition to the complex coordinates. The author proposes that capping forgets those parameters and examines a sphere of type \((0,3)\). He asks whether each removed disc depends only on its own length.

**M11 · The last conjecture (68).** Assuming the preceding picture or a variant, the author expects a space of disc systems homeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^\nu\). He starts a conjecture for a punctured conformal surface, at least in the hyperbolic case. The supplied leaf ends immediately after \((D_i)\longmapsto(o(D_i))\). A predicate for that map would require the next leaf.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model remark, high confidence in the arithmetic (67).** Counting each complex coordinate as two real ones gives \(2(3g-3+\nu)+\nu=6g-6+3\nu\). With coefficient 2 instead of 3 the difference is \(\nu\). This tests the agreement of the two expressions. The coefficient on the sheet is overwritten and the transcription preserves both readings. Confidence in attributing an authorial error here is low.

**R02 · Model remark, high confidence in the counterexample, low confidence in the intended repair (55, 61).** The continuous function \(f(z)=\bar z\) is real on the real line and remains continuous off any rectifiable segment. Its difference quotients at zero along the real and imaginary axes are \(1\) and \(-1\). Thus the literal continuity hypotheses in the isolated reflection sentence on 55 and in the marginal c) on 61 cannot imply the stated holomorphy. The shorter reflection sentence on 55 (R02-55) has “holo” struck. Its contextual reading (R02-55a) retains the explicit interior holomorphy of the full Lemme 1. On 61, S03a is a possible repair to the second occurrence of “continue”. Whether S03a suffices under all intended curve hypotheses is open in these notes.

**R03 · Model remark, high confidence in the distinction (63–68).** Scaling the shadow of an individual concentric disc is the explicit operation on 64. The independence of the disc at one puncture from all the *other* lengths is the additional condition singled out on 68. The latter is precisely where the author writes “Il faudrait …”.

**R04 · Model remark, high confidence in the limit of the text (68).** The last display gives the domain elements and their images. The manuscript provided here ends before the author states a property of that assignment. A restatement as a bijection or a homeomorphism would need evidence beyond this leaf.
