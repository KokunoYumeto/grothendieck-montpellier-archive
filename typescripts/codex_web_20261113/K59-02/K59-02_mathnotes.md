# Mathematical notes · cote 59, scans 15–21 and 23–28

## What the pages do

**A perfect complex in the determinant construction (scan 15).** A complex $F^\bullet$ on $X$, perfect or relatively perfect over $S$, is put into the determinant of the derived direct image defining $M_g$. The change from a section $g$ to $g'$ produces a power involving $\chi_F$. Grothendieck writes an additive map on Néron–Severi classes, a map of torsors and a boxed section $L_F(\delta)$ of a contracted product. He ends by reducing the calculation to a field.

**Two Riemann–Roch calculations (scans 16–17).** For a relative curve the sheet reads off a constant term and a term of degree one. The boxes give $\chi_F(\delta)=1-g+d+c$ and the canonical polarization of $B$. Comparing sections leads to the invertible module $\det^*F$. The abelian example begins with $\operatorname{Todd}(X)=1$ and uses the top component $a_n$ for the Euler term and $a_{n-1}$ in the polarization formula.

**A construction with the Weil sheaf (scans 17–18).** Section f) represents $\lambda$ by an invertible module trivialized along $g$. A determinant on $X\times_S B$ gives $l(g,\lambda)$ on $B$. Scan 18 compares two trivializations. The formula (*) expresses their difference using $\psi_X(g')-\psi_X(g)$ and $-\chi(\lambda)$. Its boxed conclusion restricts the map to the component indexed by $\delta$.

**Translation by a point of the Picard group (scans 19–20).** Formula (**) varies $\lambda$ by $\beta$. A cancelled argument uses generators of $B(k)$ expressed as differences of points. The live argument reduces to $X=A$ with its zero section and uses a transform of $L\otimes L_\beta$. Taking its determinant yields a class translated by the homomorphism associated to $\varphi(\delta)$ evaluated at $-\beta$. The sign in the blotted (**) remains open.

**An abelian scheme mapping into the Picard scheme (scans 20–21).** A chosen homomorphism $i:B\to\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}$ supplies a torsor $Q$ under the dual of $B$ and a map $X\to Q$. For a $B$-torsor $P$ mapping compatibly to a Picard component, successive displays give a map from $X$, its factorization through a power of $Q$, and a section of a contracted product. The pen replaces an earlier Picard factor by $P$ in all three displays.

**The Albanese torsor and a sign choice (scan 23).** A new sequence starts with $B=\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0_{X/S}$ and its dual $A$. The map $\psi$ into the Albanese torsor is described by the difference $\delta(x,y)=\psi(y)-\psi(x)$, represented by $N_yN_x^{-1}$. Inverting the difference leads to an inverse torsor and a second description of the universal solution. He specifies which sign he will use to identify the translation group with the dual of $B$.

**The diagonal of a curve (scans 24–25).** The diagonal defines the map from the curve to its degree-one Picard torsor. Comparing this with the Albanese construction gives an isomorphism $c:B\to A$. The author asks which polarization it realizes and begins testing it on a divisor difference. Scan 25 stops after « classe de » and a cancelled expression.

**The theta divisor on a torsor (scans 26–27).** The new heading concerns principally polarized abelian schemes. Starting from $P^\delta$, the notes pass from a divisor $D_\delta$ on $A\times P^\delta$ to a divisor $\Theta_\delta$ on the quotient. The proposition asserts existence and uniqueness of a torsor with a relative positive divisor inducing the given polarization. Part b) gives a converse, c) constructs a map to the Picard component from the action on the torsor, and d) compares the curve case with the degree $g-1$ Picard and Albanese torsors.

**The inverse torsor and its square (scan 28).** The first line finishes d) by matching $\Theta$ with $W_{g-1}$. A separately cancelled draft begins a construction from a symmetric divisorial correspondence. The live bis paragraph compares $P$ with $P^{-1}$ and interprets the resulting identification $K$ as a section of the contracted square. In the curve case it identifies that section, now of degree $2g-2$, with the class coming from $\underline{\Omega}^1_{X/S}$.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · scan 16, lines 8–13. Model remark.** High confidence in the conditional arithmetic, low confidence in diagnosing an authorial error. Truncating the product of the three written degree-zero and degree-one factors gives coefficient $c+rd+r(1-g)$ in degree one. At $r=1$ this is $1-g+d+c$. This calculation uses the local degree labels and does not resolve the rank glyph or establish which hypotheses were intended.

**M2 · scans 19–20 and 27. Model remark.** High confidence in the placement of the cautions. The first two sign notes stand beside the translation calculation, while the third stands beside the identification with a degree $g-1$ torsor. No common sign correction follows merely from the recurrence of the warning.

**M3 · scan 18, line 7. Reading question recorded after the freeze.** Frozen reading $L(\lambda,g')$ in the right-hand projection. Alternative $L(\lambda,g)$. In the first crop the tiny high stroke is close to the following parenthesis. The displayed change of trivialization is sensitive to that one mark. Confidence in an emendation is low.

**M4 · scan 28, lines 17–22. Model remark.** Medium confidence. The author first places the identification in a contracted square, then changes the notation to $P^{(2)}$ and identifies it with the component of degree $2g-2$. Doubling $g-1$ accounts for that degree. This arithmetic alone does not identify the section with the canonical sheaf.
