# K138-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**002–003 · Interlocking rods.** The bulletin announces Alan Holden’s constructions of rigid polyhedral forms by linking polygonal rings of rods. It invites readers to construct further examples. The first photograph shows several physical models.

**002 · Mixed polyhedra.** Emmerich and Aubry study the spaces left between concentric arrangements of regular polyhedra. The circular calls these spaces “polyèdres mixtes” and distinguishes planar or rounded faces and planar or skew boundary polygons.

**002 · Rotors.** The notice on Michael Goldberg gives a concrete definition. A noncircular oval moves inside a regular polygon while remaining in contact with every side.

**002 · Zonohedra and exceptional motions.** The circular lists Hanegraaf’s twenty combinatorial problems. A separate paragraph announces Roth’s questions about finite or infinitesimal motions in special positions of otherwise rigid structures and their associated energy functions.

**002 · Networks and assemblies.** Haider’s contribution concerns polyhedral linkages and architectural applications. Tarnai studies lattice cylinders and networks with vertices on a sphere. Miyazaki and Takada assemble regular dodecahedra face to face. The second photograph shows an assembly of faceted cells.

**003 · Poly-kit.** March’s testimonial concerns teaching with Baracs’s kit. The notice says it builds the regular and semi-regular polyhedra and some of their duals. The remaining material concerns the journal and orders. Scan 004 is a cover.

**005–006 · Descent of one scheme, (1)–(10).** Starting with a field, a separable closure and a connected scheme over that closure, the notes distinguish automorphisms over the two fields. They associate an intermediate field with a closed subgroup of Γ and express a descent datum as a splitting, or a partial splitting over the subgroup. A split product is written with a dot and a small 1/2 beneath it. Effectivity enters when the datum is used to give a model over the smaller field.

**006–007 · Automorphisms of covers, (11)–(16).** The automorphisms of the universal profinite covering map form an extension with kernel π₁. For a normal closed subgroup N of π₁, the notes take its normalizer in that extension, divide by N, and identify a group of automorphisms of the resulting cover. A marginal note specifies the continuity understood in the outer action on π₁.

**007–008 · Which automorphisms lift, (16)–(19).** The notes relate surjectivity for the map between the tall-G groups to invariance of N. They express the geometric group as an inverse image and as a kernel, and give separate conditions on the map of geometric groups and on the image in Γ. The lower note on 008 describes open images under finiteness hypotheses. The base letter in its first hypothesis is uncertain.

**009 · Descent of a covering map, (20)–(23).** A datum for the arrow between the two schemes is a splitting of the map to Γ. Over an intermediate field the splitting is partial. The large display defines restricted automorphism groups, including an intersection and two inverse images. The corresponding product appears at the foot of the page.

**010–011 · Passage to a finite quotient, (24)–(26).** A bracketed diagram records three successive factors. The text distinguishes the geometric extension from that carrying the arithmetic data. In the case of finite geometric groups and an infinite Γ, it describes a finite quotient of Γ, a finite extension over it, and a pullback description. A partial splitting with normal image is compared with one whose image commutes with the geometric group.

**011–013 · Models and the centralizer, (26)–(31).** The partial splitting is interpreted as reduction of the cover to a smaller field. The page requires a finite étale group scheme in the resulting sequence to be constant. Several models over a fixed field can occur. The centralizer and its intersection with the geometric group yield a central extension over the image Γ₀. Its partial sections describe the solutions in question.

**013–014 · Obstruction and indeterminacy.** With trivial centre, the notes give uniqueness and a condition for existence in terms of Γ₀. For a general centre z and a fixed Γᵢ, the page puts the obstruction in degree two and the indeterminacy in degree one. It specifies the trivial action and writes the latter group as Hom(Γᵢ,z).

**015–016 · Zero, one and two punctures.** The example removes a finite set from a projective line and considers its maximal abelian cover, with “modéré” in brackets. For zero or one point the cover is identified with the original curve. With two points the curve is a torsor under a form of the multiplicative group. Choosing a puncture and then an origin supplies coordinates. The maps given by raising t to its n-th power lead to finite-level automorphism groups and a projective limit.

**016 · Three or more punctures.** With three points the notes give an isomorphism of groups, name the Fermat tower and write the group πₙ as a quotient of powers of μₙ. With at least four points they choose a triple and send its members to 0, 1 and ∞. The remaining points acquire coordinates. The extract ends at the field symbol with a small subscript.

## Questions and conjectures

- **002, the circular.** Holden “défie les lecteurs d’en trouver d’autres”. Hanegraaf invites readers to examine “vingt problèmes combinatoires”. Roth “formule une série de questions mathématiques” about exceptional positions and energy functions. The individual problems are not printed on these sheets.
- **005, after (7).** “sous réserve … d’effectivité” is a qualification on the passage to an actual model.
- **009, beside (23).** The margin begins “oubli à réparer” in the first reading. The verb is doubtful. The note raises an editorial task without specifying a new mathematical conjecture.
- **010, square bracket below (24).** “On a envie de plus … de faire varier” the covers so that the universal cover is their projective limit. The criterion written at the end of the bracket is that the intersection of the kernels be 1.

- **M16.1 · Model remark, high confidence in the finite computation.** If the denominator in S16.2 is embedded diagonally, the three-coordinate quotient has four elements for n=2 and nine for n=3. I counted the orbits of simultaneous addition modulo n on the eight and twenty-seven triples respectively. This tests two instances of that interpretation.
- **M16.2 · Model reading question, low confidence.** In scan 016, C, the frozen transcription uses the broad G in both subscripted names after the isomorphism. A possible other reading is a Gothic S in the subscripted names. Their pen outlines are nearly the same as that of the first letter. The triple of marked points makes a permutation-group reading plausible. The assignment of these particular letterforms cannot be settled by that interpretation.
