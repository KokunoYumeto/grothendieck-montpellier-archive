# Mathematical notes · cote 83, K83-07

## What the pages do

**145–147.** The opening fragment specializes two quadratic algebras to $H=k\oplus L$ and $H'=k\oplus L'$ with square-zero summands. The scheme map takes two square-zero sections to their tensor. Scan 146 inserts a discussion of the three quadratic subalgebras of $P=H\otimes H'$ and of generation by pairs. The displayed map at the top of 147 sends $u\otimes v$ to $-2uv$. The marginal generalization uses a line and a section of its tensor square. The example distinguishes the contracted product from the familiar product of quadratic étale torsors.

**148.** Grothendieck asks for associativity and a construction with any finite number of factors. As a test with three factors, he prescribes traces when one input of a trilinear form is the unit, then asks which quadratic condition should accompany them.

**150–151.** The dated digression starts with a module with unit and a linear form taking the value 2 on the unit. Dually, an augmented module $E$ carries the involution $x\mapsto\varepsilon(x)t-x$ for $\varepsilon(t)=2$. On $A=\varepsilon^{-1}(1)$, the discussion separates a fixed origin when 2 is invertible, translation by $t$ when 2 is zero, and the intermediate example $k=\mathbb Z$.

**151–153.** A pairing consists of an affine-space map $f$ and a bilinear map $f^0$ on the translation modules. Two rules prescribe the change in $f$ under translation of either input. Choosing origins gives a formula with $c=f(x_1,x_2)$ and $\tau_i=\sigma_i x_i-x_i$. The bracketed example on 153 explains why the data include $f^0$ even when $f$ is constant.

**153–154.** The universal affine product $A_1\ast A_2$ has translation module $A_1^0\otimes A_2^0$. A formula in $E_1\otimes E_2$ realizes the pairing. The two involutions induced from the two input factors agree on the product. The last display imposes compatibility with the involution on the target.

**155.** The proposed unit is the affine line with involution $x\mapsto1-x$. Its affine envelope is $k\times k$ with the sum augmentation and the coordinate swap. The distinguished point $e_0$ gives the displayed map $x\mapsto e_0\ast x$.

**156–157.** Coordinatewise translation rules define an $n$-argument pairing problem. Grothendieck writes a sum with successive coefficients $1,-2,4,\ldots$, extends the pairing to the enveloping modules, and rewrites the product and its involute as sums indexed by parity. The distinction between elements given by the formula and the affine space they generate matters in characteristic 2.

**157–158.** When 2 is invertible, $t=t_1\otimes\cdots\otimes t_n$ gives a complementary line. The augmentation of the proposed envelope takes $t$ to 2, while the tensor of the individual augmentations takes it to $2^n$. He then considers the character spaces of the group of independent involutions.

**159–160.** The data become involutive modules with submodules $E_i^0$, on which the involutions act by minus the identity, and quotients on which they act by the identity. A second multilinear map on the submodules occurs in the pairing. A proposed exact sequence and a diagram compare the universal object with the ordinary tensor product. Marginal qualifications accompany the sequence.

**161.** With 2 invertible, he describes the product separately on the negative and positive parts. He then returns to an affine space with invertible translation module $L$. A quadratic divisor gives a quadratic form on its envelope with values in $L^{\otimes2}$ and prescribed restriction $u\mapsto u^{\otimes2}$ on $L$.

## Questions and conjectures

- **145.** « Je voudrais expliciter tout dans le cas où $H$ et $H'$ sont unipotentes ». This is the aim of the square-zero calculation.
- **148.** « Il faudrait au moins vérifier … l’associativité ». He says it is clear if, at points of characteristic 2, all but at most one factor are étale. He calls this « une condition assez idiote, et sûrement inutile ».
- **148.** « On … aimerait une description directe d’un produit contracté multiple ». The three-factor trial ends « mais laquelle ?? » about the quadratic condition.
- **151.** « Je voudrais définir une opération de produit tensoriel d’espaces affines à involution. » This introduces the pairing problem.
- **156–157.** « Sauf erreur » qualifies the tensor-space realization. The marginal correction on 156 says « non, engendré, pas “formé” ». On 157 the deleted generator is followed by « (?) », next to « vérifier … en car 2 ! ».

## Remarks of the model

- **R150 · Model remark · high confidence.** The value $\varepsilon(t)=2$ in the unmarked formula on 150 makes $\varepsilon(\sigma x)=\varepsilon(x)$. Substitution then gives $\sigma^2x=x$. This also accounts for $\sigma(t)=t$, used when reading the complementary line on 157.
- **R155 · Model remark · low confidence for the manuscript reading.** On 155 the coordinate swap sends $(0,1)$ to $(1,0)$, so the difference is $(1,-1)$, the image of 1 under the displayed linear embedding. This checks the later assertion for the live point as transcribed. The first coordinate is overwritten, so the calculation cannot decide what was underneath.
- **R156 · Model remark · high confidence for $n\geq2$.** The marginal example with all $t_i=0$ in characteristic 2 can be read directly from the displayed expansion. All terms with a $t_i$ vanish; the final coefficient is a multiple of 2. This explains why the author's correction to « formé » matters.
- **R157 · Model remark · low confidence for the exponent.** The alternatives $i+1$ and $i-1$ in the exponent of $-1$ differ by 2 and give the same sign. That agreement cannot decide which sign he drew in the blot.
- **R158 · Model remark · high confidence for the example, low for the editorial diagnosis S158.** Take two copies of the rational coordinate-swap module from 155, with $t_i=(1,1)$. Swapping the first factor fixes $t_1\otimes t_2=(1,1,1,1)$. The all-ones character requires its negative. Thus this displayed generator of the complementary line fails the sign-character test in an explicit example.
- **R159 · Model remark · high confidence.** In the last formula on 159, each of the other $n-1$ inputs is in $E_i^0$, where $\sigma_i-\mathrm{id}$ acts as $-2$. Multilinearity accounts for the exponent $n-1$ in $f(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)=(-2)^{n-1}f^0(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$.
