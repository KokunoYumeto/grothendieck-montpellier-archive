# Mathematical notes on cote 130, K130-01

Scan numbers refer to the supplied archive images. Quoted phrases follow the sheets. An ellipsis inside a quotation denotes intervening words.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 002, 004–005.** The title introduces Néron’s formulae. On scan 004 a rational-valued function of a divisor and an underlined cycle is specified for pairs with disjoint support. The numbered properties give bilinearity, translation invariance, an intersection formula for a principal divisor and a boundedness condition. The refinement (iv bis) says that a value obtained by varying a rational point depends only on its class modulo the indicated subgroup. Scan 005 gives integrality statements and uses the degree of the component group to write a pairing with values in ℤ/mℤ. Formula (vii) adds an intersection term to v to obtain the local symbol.

**U2 · Scan 006.** The corollary relates values with translated arguments. For a family of discrete valuations satisfying the product formula, the sheet adds the local symbols and passes to classes. It writes a real-valued pairing, then gives its values in (1/m)ℤ and names it the Néron–Tate form. The exponent on the second A of the displayed pairing is crossed out.

**U3 · Scan 007.** The relative Picard scheme of a proper morphism is assumed to exist. The closure of the identity component of the generic Picard scheme enters a proposed Néron-model criterion. The argument passes to a smooth base change, observes regularity there, and extends a Picard class from the generic fibre. A side note flags the case with no étale-local section.

**U4 · Scan 008.** The reused contents sheet lists elementary lemmas, Coxeter groups and their representations, reflection groups, and root systems. Its pencilled list links the names Coxeter, Cartan and Dynkin to some of these topics. The sheet supplies no argument about heights.

**U5 · Scans 010–011.** A pencil of hyperplane sections cuts a base locus Z on X. Blowing up Z produces a map to the projective line. The triangles compare Picard groups and their subgroups of classes having degree zero on the geometric generic fibre. A drawing on 011 shows a curve meeting fibres over three positions of the base. The accompanying question asks for the kernels and images of two maps. The text identifies vertical divisor classes in the kernel and, for integral fibres, reduces the kernel under discussion to multiples of a fibre class.

**U6 · Scans 012–014.** The opening expression on 012 relates the transformed fibre class to a pullback and a sum over Z. The nonempty base locus is used in the injectivity argument. The notes identify the image of the connected Picard group with the fixed part of the Jacobian and form its quotient J′, called the “partie mobile”. After passage to Néron–Severi groups, 013 gives a rank-one kernel under the fibre hypothesis and argues for surjectivity modulo a finite group. Scan 014 invokes the vanishing of a Brauer group and boxes a relation between Picard numbers and the rank of the rational-point group of J′. The last lines distinguish irreducibility from integrality when multiple sections of the pencil are allowed.

**U7 · Scan 015.** A canonical polarisation of J′ is said to produce a positive form Q. The author sets this beside the negative intersection form on the indicated subgroup of NS(X) and asks for equality after taking the negative. The proposed duality argument in the upper half is cancelled. The equality itself remains a question.

**U8 · Scan 017.** Over a discrete valuation base, the author considers horizontal cycles C and D with c+d=n+1. Their generic restrictions are assumed algebraically equivalent to zero, and an intersection is required to be defined. The aim is to express its number using a local symbol on the generic fibres alone. The last insertions make the independence sought stronger and qualify the expected rational values.

**U9 · Scan 018.** The reused typescript ends a preceding proof, then states Theorem 2 on Dynkin diagrams for reduced irreducible root systems, up to homothety of the coefficients. Its list gives the A, B, C, D, E, F and G families with their node weights. The proof refers to the corresponding Coxeter diagrams, excludes certain angles, compares root lengths and displays the two orientations of the four weights in the F case.

**U10 · Scan 019.** The question about local symbols is recast for several cycles whose codimensions add to n+1. Two of the generic terms are singled out as algebraically equivalent to zero. The desiderata include symmetry, invariance, a transposition formula for two terms, normalisation for a divisor of a function, continuity and extension of the base field. The final evaluation reaches the edge of the page.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 004–006.** In (vii) the symbol with subscript v is the sum of the auxiliary function and an intersection term. The following global sum uses that subscripted symbol. Keeping the two expressions distinct preserves the transition from the local formula to the global one.
- **R2 · Model remark, low confidence, scans 010–014.** The middle and left members of the boxed equality, taken literally, imply ρ(X̃)=ρ(X)+z−1. The numerals in the two boxes have been retraced and the definition of z speaks of singular sections. The preceding blow-up discussion is about the base points Z. These leaves do not identify those counts. There is no secure basis here for replacing a digit or changing the definition of z.
- **R3 · Model remark, medium confidence, scan 015.** The prose calls Q a “forme quadratique”, while the boxed expression gives it two arguments. The associated pairing is a possible interpretation of the displayed question. The sheet specifies no convention for passing from one to the other.
- **R4 · Model remark, high confidence, scan 018.** Reversing the four vertices of the lower path identifies the weights 1,1,2,2 with 2,2,1,1 and preserves the three links. This checks the particular isomorphism invoked in the last paragraph.
- **R5 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 017, 019.** Substituting two cycles in the codimension condition of 019 gives exactly c+d=n+1 as on 017. This short comparison concerns the hypotheses alone. The desired existence and independence of the local symbol are still open in these leaves.
