## What the pages do

**Scans 145–146.** A choice of oriented lattice basis gives a period ratio in the upper half-plane. Formulas (7) and the two displays numbered (8) give the family by translation, its complex-torus fibre, and the multiplicative description with $k=\exp(2i\pi\tau)$. The next page changes the basis by a determinant-one integer matrix and writes the new ratio $(d\tau+b)/(c\tau+a)$.

**Scans 147–148.** The square on 147 compares two choices of coordinate in the tangent line. Its labels track the two periods separately. Formula (14) gives a lift to the family. On 148 the inverse matrix and conjugation by $\tau_\infty=\operatorname{diag}(-1,+1)$ turn the right action into the convention $g\tau=(a\tau+b)/(c\tau+d)$. The author explicitly says that he will use (13) thereafter.

**Scans 149–150.** Formula (15) combines the matrix and lattice translations. The theorem identifies the family with strict Torelli rigidification as a universal covering of $M_{1,1}$ and specifies its automorphisms. Three corollaries pass from the group of the covering to isotopy classes of a pointed oriented surface of genus one, and then to the group described using $\pi$, its abelianisation and its ends.

**Scans 151–152.** Choosing a basis of $t_E$ gives the differential rigidification. Together with the strict Torelli choice it produces ordered periods $(z_1,z_2)$ with $z_2/z_1$ in the upper half-plane. The family and the two commuting actions appear in (20)–(22). The text distinguishes their individual freeness from the stabilisers for the product action. The revision at the foot of 152 breaks off while discussing fixed points and successive quotients.

**Scan 153.** A title sheet introduces the normality of quadratic extensions.

**Scan 154, above the rule.** A canceled calculation puts a finite-field kernel under a group sequence, then writes the same element in the orders $ux$ and $yu$. Parallel equalities expand $g'g$. The whole group calculation is struck. No passage here connects it explicitly to the quadratic extensions below the rule.

**Scan 154, below the rule.** A quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q(t)$ is compared with a ring over $\mathbb Z[T]$. The author treats localisations at $pA$, singles out $p=2$, and distinguishes being a square modulo 2 from being a square modulo 4. He then discusses normality in codimension one and the remaining finite locus. The relation is first written with $P$. The later tests use $Q$.

**Scan 155, upper part.** Over an “anneau jauge” $A$, the notes study $A[S]/(S^2-a)$. The cases use the membership of $a$ in $\mathfrak m$, and, for a unit in residue characteristic 2, whether its residue is a square. In the square-residue case a lift $\alpha$ gives $b=\alpha^2-a$. The substitution $T=S-\alpha$ puts $b$ in the constant term. The class of $b$ in $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ supplies the last test.

**Scan 155, lower part.** The application concerns quadratic covers of the projective or affine line over $\mathbb Z$, generically étale on the fibres. For the generic extension the text lists positive integer primes and primitive irreducible polynomials with positive leading coefficient. Formula (1) groups distinct factors into $P=\pm qQ$ and records the squarefreeness and content conditions. Some exponents in the preceding square-class notation remain doubtful.

**Scan 156.** For $p\ne2$ the equation of the fibre gives a generic étaleness test. The restriction on $q$ leads back to the fibre at 2 and a square-modulo-4 condition with a choice of sign. The example $Q(T)=T(T-\lambda)$ is followed by two affine presentations for punctured opens and the change $T'=1/T$.

**Scan 157.** On a reused university notice the pen returns to $T^2+2\alpha T+b$. Beside $\mathbb F_2[T,\pi]$ are a homogeneous quadratic and $\alpha^2-4\beta=\alpha^2$. A second calculation in $x,y$ and a partly written factor stand to the left. These are separate jottings, without a statement of how they are to be combined.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Scans 146–148 · high confidence.** Inserting the displayed basis change into $z'_2/z'_1$ gives (10). With determinant one, the inverse and the indicated diagonal conjugation give the matrices used for (11) and (13). This checks the labels in the two orders in which the action is written.
- **M2 · Scan 152 · high confidence for this example.** For $(z_1,z_2)=(1,i)$, the two operations of $(-1,-I)$ leave the period pair fixed and send the fibre coordinate to $-z$. Modulo the square lattice, $0,\frac12,\frac i2,\frac{1+i}{2}$ are all fixed. The manuscript's question has an uncertain antecedent, so it cannot securely be identified with this computation.
- **M3 · Scan 154 · low confidence as a proposed slip.** $P(t)$ in the ring relation and $Q(t)$ in the square tests could reflect a distinction intended by the later notation $P=\pm qQ$. Replacing one letter throughout would suppress that possibility. In particular, the two linear examples are accompanied by different signs $\varepsilon$.
- **M4 · Scan 155 · high confidence for the calculation.** With $2\in\mathfrak m$, changing a lift by $h\in\mathfrak m$ changes $\alpha^2-a$ by $2\alpha h+h^2$, which lies in $\mathfrak m^2$. This checks the substance of the second NB independently of its overwritten membership sign.
- **M5 · Scan 156 · low confidence as a reading question.** In the example line the frozen text has a raised 2, a crossing, then “(2)”. Another possibility at the raised digit is 3. For $\lambda=4$, direct substitution gives $Q(T)-T^2=-4T$, so a congruence modulo 4 would also hold. The calculation gives no reason to replace the written “(2)” or assert that the author intended an equivalence. The separate claim that $-1$ is not a square modulo 4 agrees with the residues of the four possible squares.
- **M6 · Scan 156 · low confidence as an interpretation of the last presentation.** Under $T'=1/T$, the polynomial $Q(T)/T^2$ becomes $1-\lambda T'$. The last relation contains $\lambda T'-1$. A choice of $\varepsilon$ could account for this. The line gives no secure basis for changing a sign or naming the new squared variable.
