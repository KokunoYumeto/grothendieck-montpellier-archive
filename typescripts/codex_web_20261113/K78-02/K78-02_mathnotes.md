# Mathematical notes · cote 78, scans 17–31

The numbered equations and French quotations refer to the manuscript. The labels in square brackets identify statements in the check register.

## What the pages do

**[U1] A pinned polygon as data, scan 17.** Proposition 2 encodes a regular pinned \(n\)-gon in the affine plane \(E\) by an affine action \(\varphi\) of \(D_n\) and a point \(s_0\). The reflection fixes \(s_0\), and the three successive points \(s_0,us_0,u^2s_0\) are noncollinear. The sheet separately records faithfulness of the action and unimodularity of \(u\). The group presentation and renumbered conditions occupy the top of the sheet.

**[U2] The possible initial points, scans 17–18.** The point lies on the first mirror. A second involution \(\sigma'=u\sigma\) gives the exclusion of the intersection of the mirrors. The drawing locates the centre \(c\), the two axes and neighbouring vertices. The author announces that the eligible points form a torsor under the affine transformations commuting with the action.

**[U3] The commutant, scans 19–20.** Proposition 3 separates intersecting mirrors from parallel ones. In the first case their intersection is the fixed origin and the commutant consists of nonzero homotheties. In the second case it consists of translations in their common direction. The proof exhibits a subgroup transitive on the eligible points and invokes freeness of the commutant there to identify the two groups.

**[U4] Isomorphisms and pinning, scans 21–22.** The first corollary passes from isomorphic admissible representations to isomorphic regular pinned polygons. The second fixes one polygon and transports it by affine intertwiners into the realizations subordinate to the target representation. The “bijection” on 22 strengthens the preceding surjectivity. The point chosen on the punctured axis is described as an “épinglage” that eliminates automorphisms without introducing new isomorphism classes.

**[U5] Dropping faithfulness, scans 23–24.** Section IV keeps the vertex conditions while reconsidering (12). Proposition 4 tests a rotation on \(s_0\): since it commutes with \(u\), fixing this point also fixes its orbit, which affinely spans \(E\). The next parts relate the kernel to the rotation subgroup and introduce a divisor and a quotient polygon. The changing roles of the two divisors matter in reading the conclusion.

**[U6] Quotient polygon and covering, scan 25.** The first corollary uses reversal of the two circular orders to exclude an orientation-reversing element acting as the identity. The second retains the pinned point for the induced \(D_{n'}\)-action. The scholium interprets the original realization as “un revêtement à \(d\) feuillets” of the smaller one.

**[U7] Why postpone the faithfulness test, scans 25–27.** Computing realizations for a fixed \(n\) can expose nonfaithful actions. Over an integer or cyclotomic coefficient ring the author envisages fibres in exceptional characteristics with fewer sides. He wants to keep these polygons in the calculation. No particular ring, fibre and polygon are worked out here.

**[U8] The infinite dihedral formulation, scans 27–29.** In (14) the two affine generators square to the identity, and their ordered product \(u_E=\sigma'_E\sigma_E\) has an additional \(n\)-th power relation. Scan 28 discusses a divisor test using the example \(n=9\). Omitting the last relation gives a representation of \(D_\infty\). Finite polygons then appear through actions that factor through a finite dihedral quotient.

**[U9] Realizing an abstract polygon, scans 29–31.** Write \(\Pi=(S,\omega)\). Equation (17) and its commuting square express compatibility between vertex symmetries and affine automorphisms. The data in (18) separate the group action from the equivariant vertex map. Condition (19) requires the image to span the plane. A base vertex and its stabilizing reflection yield (20)–(21). With the specified shift, (22) imposes noncollinearity. A choice of identification with the standard polygon then leads to Proposition 5 for \(\Pi_0(\infty)\), formulated in terms of two affine involutions and one point.

## Remarks of the model

**[R1] Model remark, high confidence.** The two uses of the torsor property on 22 can be checked separately. Freeness kills the stabilizer of a chosen pin. Transitivity gives an isomorphism between choices. This accounts for both clauses of “épinglage” without requiring a preferred \(s_0\).

**[R2] Model computation, high confidence.** Over \(\mathbb Q^2\) take \(u=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(\sigma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(\sigma'=u\sigma\), and \(s_0=(1,0)\). Exact integer multiplication gives \(\sigma^2={\sigma'}^2=I\), \(\sigma'\sigma=u\), \(\det u=1\) and \(\sigma s_0=s_0\). The first three vertices are \((1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)\), with affine determinant \(3\). The order of \(u\) is \(3\), so the \(n=6\) relation holds while its rotation of exponent \(3\) is already in the kernel. A period test that includes every proper divisor \(m\geq3\) includes this case. This example was constructed for this comparison.

**[R3] Model computation, high confidence.** Enumerating the divisors in the stated interval for \(n=3,\ldots,9\) gives an empty list for 3 through 8, then just 3 for 9. Enumerating *all* proper divisors that could be orbit periods of at least 3 instead gives 3 for 6, 4 for 8 and 3 for 9. These two lists account for S8 and the gap in the printed restriction.

**[R4] Reading question, low confidence.** Scan 31, Proposition 5, second entry of the triple. The frozen mark after the subscript \(E\) was “\(*\)”. An upright resembling 1 is another possible description of its surviving ink. The spot abuts what looks like an overwritten subscript. The mathematical notes draw no change of convention from it.


**[R5] Reading question, low confidence.** Scan 17, right of (11), last group presentation. The frozen reading places an equality between the two squared generators. At this small scale the mark after the first square could be a comma. The two-generator presentation in the same margin does not settle how this particular line is punctuated.
