# Mathematical notes on cote 91, scans 87–98

## What the pages do

**A1 · Atomic preschemes (87).** The first two diagrams send sums of spectra of fields to functors on fields, first without a specified base and then over a field $k$. The same assignment is described contravariantly on punctual schemes. To $X$ the text assigns $|X|=\coprod_{x\in X}\operatorname{Spec}k(x)$. It asserts faithfulness on reduced preschemes.

**A2 · Constructible maps and schematic sets (87, 89).** A map of the atomic objects is called a pseudo-morphism or an “application constructible” if, on affine opens of the source, it is induced piece by piece by morphisms on reduced locally closed constructible pieces. A cancelled note considers testing the condition on a cover. A quasi-separated, quasi-compact prescheme considered with these maps is called an “ensemble schématique”. A diagram relates the categories, retaining cancelled arcs and labels.

**A3 · Refinement (89).** A lemma proposes refining a constructible cover by a partition into affine locally closed constructible pieces. The next proposition compares constructibility with the existence of a finite partition on which the map is “morphique”. A third condition, using differences of successive quasi-compact opens, is struck out.

**A4 · Inverse images (91).** The first proposition says that inverse images of constructible sets under a constructible map are constructible. The argument restricts to the pieces $X_i$ on which a morphism is available, invokes a theorem of Chevalley, and reduces to a quasi-compact, quasi-separated morphism of preschemes. A slanted marginal objection sits beside this step. The corollary concerns invariance of the notion of constructible subset.

**A5 · Isomorphisms on pieces (91, 93).** The second proposition characterizes an isomorphism by finite partitions of source and target and isomorphisms between corresponding pieces. Starting with inverse maps $f,g$, the proof refines partitions using $X_\alpha\cap f^{-1}(Y_\beta)$ and $Y_\beta\cap g^{-1}(X_\alpha)$. After a cancelled argument it factors the restrictions through the pieces on the other side. The two resulting morphisms are stated to be inverse.

**A6 · Corollaries (93).** In the setting of the isomorphism just studied, Cor. 0 carries the noetherian property from $X$ to $Y$. Cor. 1 gives an affine representative $|X|$ for a schematic set. Cor. 2 gives an affine-morphism representative for a morphism of schematic sets. A marginal note discusses the meaning of “noethérien”. Cor. 3, about finite type, is cancelled.

**A7 · Finite type and products (95).** Finite type is tested on a partition into affine locally closed constructible subsets of the source. The text returns to representation by a morphism of affine schemes, marks transitivity, and places schematic sets over $\mathbb Z$. It associates a schematic set and an immersion with a constructible part $E$, and writes $X=E\amalg(X-E)$. Fibre products and finite limits follow. The final line identifies the product of chosen parts $X',Y'$ with the intersection of their inverse images under the two projections.

**A8 · Passage to a projective limit (97).** For a filtered inverse system $(X_i)$, with the stated conditions on the preschemes and the eventual affineness condition on transitions, $X=\varprojlim X_i$ is asserted to exist. The page asks whether its constructible object is the inverse limit of those for the $X_i$. Maps from a test prescheme $T$ are expressed using finite partitions. The two displayed calculations put the inverse limit over $i$ and the direct limit over partitions in opposite orders. A second system $(Y_j)$ leads to a question about maps $X\to Y$.

**B1 · Tensors and derivations (92, continued on 90).** Exercises 1 and 2 concern stability of symmetric and antisymmetric tensors and the criterion for a bilinear multiplication to be invariant. Exercise 3 uses the action of $\mathrm{gl}(V)$ on tensor powers involving $V$ and $V^*$. It asks for the behaviour of the semisimple and nilpotent parts of an endomorphism on those tensors, then applies it to derivations. The sentence broken off at the bottom of typed page 137 resumes at the top of typed page 138.

**B2 · Irreducible modules and duality (90).** Exercise 4a starts from an invariant tensor $\sum e_i\otimes f_i$ with the $f_i$ a basis of an irreducible finite-dimensional module. It identifies the span of the $e_i$ as stable and its representation as dual. Parts b and c ask about inclusions of irreducible modules after tensoring with a dual pair, and common simple submodules after field extension. Both point back to a).

**B3 · Separating elements of an enveloping algebra (90, 88).** Exercise 5 places the filtration $U_n$ and homogeneous symmetric terms $U^n$ alongside the action on the $s$-fold tensor power $W_s$. On typed page 139 the proposed congruence computes the image of $z$ modulo terms with an identity factor. Part b asks for a finite-dimensional representation detecting $z$. The universal quantifier as printed includes zero.

**B4 · Invariant forms and tensors (88).** Exercise 6 expands a linear form in a tensor basis and its dual, then uses an invariant nondegenerate bilinear form to write an invariant tensor. Its last part characterizes invariant functionals on the enveloping algebra by $f(uv)=f(vu)$.

**C1 · Bounded convergence (94).** The fragment uses bounded projections and homothetic copies of the unit balls to identify the bounded-convergence topology on a space of multilinear maps with a norm topology. The supremum is written with $\lVert x_1\rVert<1$ and $\lVert x_n\rVert\leq1$. A final sentence discusses where to put a fuller account.

**D1 · Homogeneous spaces (96).** The report proposes revisions to numbers 4 and 8. In the visible part of number 4 it defines continuous action, observes that each group element induces a homeomorphism, and specializes to a transitive action. It factors the orbit map as $G\to G/H_o\to E$ and recalls continuity of the induced bijection for the quotient topology. The choice of a name for $H_o$ is left as an editorial question. The final definition breaks off at the lower edge.

**D2 · Inverse limits of quotient groups (98).** Proposition 25 takes a convergent filter base of closed normal subgroups of a Hausdorff topological group. The transition maps of the quotients give a projective system. The canonical map identifies $G$ with a dense subgroup of the limit. The argument checks compatibility, injectivity, density and the neighbourhood condition needed for the topology on the image. The final line leaves possible failure of surjectivity as an exercise.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · 88, 90, 92 · high.** The physical scan order reverses the order of the three exercise pages. The printed page numbers are 139, 138, 137. Exercise 3c resumes at the top of 138, and the $W_s,M_s,\pi_s$ of exercise 5 on 138 recur at the top of 139. This identifies the continuations without supplying missing text.
- **R2 · 91, 93 · low, reading question.** In the inverse-image proof the final transcription has “constructible dans $\alpha$”. The alternative in the frozen reading was $X$. The low two-tailed loop supports α, while the comparison with the piece $X_i$ makes $X$ easier to interpret. On 93, both subscript orders occur on $Y$ and the first occurrence is overwritten. An editor cannot infer a systematic indexing error from those strokes.
- **R3 · 94 · high for the signs.** The first displayed bound is strict and the last is weak. The preceding sentence expressly mentions the non-discrete case. The fragment supplies no statement that one may close or open all the balls without affecting the claimed norm.
- **R4 · 97 · high for the displayed order.** The first expanded Hom has a direct limit over partitions outside an inverse limit over $i$. In the comparison Hom the inverse limit over $i$ is outside. Any argument identifying these two displayed expressions has to address this change of order. The page itself leaves the comparison open.
- **R5 · 93, 95 · medium.** The statement about finite type at the foot of 93 is crossed out, and 95 opens with a live definition of finite type. Their proximity helps locate the topic; no implication from the cancelled claim is needed for the definition.
- **R6 · 98 · high.** The last exercise distinguishes density of $g(G)$ in $G'$ from equality with $G'$. This is consistent with the actual conclusion of proposition 25, which specifies a dense subgroup.
