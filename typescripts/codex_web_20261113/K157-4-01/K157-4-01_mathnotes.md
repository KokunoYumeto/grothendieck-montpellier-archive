# Mathematical notes on cote 157-4, scans 002–017

## What the pages do

**Chapter opening, scan 002.** The cover announces chapter XIII on the construction of model categories and derivators. Its two short index entries refer to later pages.

**The lifting operations, scans 003–005.** For a class \(\Phi\) of arrows, \(\Phi_*\) consists of arrows with the right lifting property against \(\Phi\). For \(\Psi\), the class \(\Psi^*\) uses the left lifting property. Proposition 1 describes the two order-reversing operations, their composite closures and how they exchange closed classes. It gives formulas for intersections. Part d) writes the two equivalent class inclusions expressing the lifting relation between \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\).

**Stability of a closed class, scan 006.** Proposition 2 lists properties of a left-closed class. It contains isomorphisms and is stable under composition, cobase change and direct factors. The ordinal assertion specifies the transition maps in a well-ordered system, a colimit condition at limit elements and the canonical map into the final colimit. The last paragraph gives a dual statement on the right.

**Brown’s axioms, scans 007 and 009.** The author starts with \(C\), puts \(TC=C\cap W\), and writes \(F=(TC)_*\) and \(TF=C_*\). He distinguishes factorisation of a general arrow from factorisation of \(X\to e\). He lists what his comparison would additionally require. The next manuscript sheet contrasts passage to \(S\backslash M\) with passage to \(M/S\) and discusses exact sequences and \(W\)-homotopy fibres and cofibres.

**Reused typescript, scans 008 and 010.** Typescript pages 426 and 427 form one continuous passage. The author describes physical work, intellectual work and his effort to balance them. He discusses the writing of *Récoltes et Semailles* in that connection.

**Quillen pairs, scan 011.** Définition 1 recalls lifting and the two forms of closure. It defines the factorisation property by \(f=pi\) with \(i\in\Phi\) and \(p\in\Psi\). A Quillen pair has the associated lifting classes and this factorisation property. The page expressly calls the lifting relation “non symétrique”.

**A theorem with a small set of generators, scan 012.** Théorème 1 assumes an accessible \(M\) closed under small inductive limits. It takes a small \(\Phi_0\), puts \(\Psi=(\Phi_0)_*\), and assumes \(\Phi_0\subset\Phi\subset\Psi^*\). Part a) applies the three conditions from Proposition 2 to characterize left closure and obtain a Quillen pair. Part b) retains factorisation when the direct-factor condition is omitted.

**Accessible parts, scans 012–013.** The remark singles out the small set of generators. The author asks for a notion of accessible parts of \(\mathrm{Fl}\,M\) that would supply such small sets and interact with the two lifting operations. He describes what would follow for an associated pair of accessible closed classes.

**Weak Quillen data, scans 013–014.** Section 2 starts with \(W\subset\mathrm{Fl}(M)\), containing the isomorphisms and satisfying the condition on \(f,g,gf\). It gives four further classes \(C,F,TC,TF\). M1 and M2 concern lifting and factorisation. The bracketed M3 and M4 list stability properties. M′ places \(TC\) and \(TF\) in intersections with \(W\). M″ places \(C\) and \(F\) inside the \(W\)-cofibrations and \(W\)-fibrations. The comparison with Quillen uses the manuscript’s word “propriety”.

**Closure of the four classes, scans 014–015.** Proposition 3 closes the two classes on each side under isomorphisms, composition and the appropriate change of base. Its proof treats the lifting axiom, then reduces the remaining M′ inclusions to stability of intersections with \(W\). The lemma states such a result for a subclass of \(\mathrm{Cof}_W\) and gives its dual. The boxed equality and its gloss connect one of the intersections to arrows remaining in \(W\) after cobase change.

**Universality under change of base, scans 016–017.** Corollaire 1 identifies the intersections of \(W\) with \(\mathrm{Cof}_W\) and \(\mathrm{Fib}_W\). The explanation of the latter uses all maps \(Y'\to Y\). Proposition 4 concerns \(W\)-arrows between objects fibred over \(S\), assuming a factorisation with first factor in \(W\) and second in \(\mathrm{Fib}_W\). The proof factors the change of base, compares the three rows of objects in the diagram and appeals to weak saturation. The statement also gives the dual over a cobase.

**Direct factors, scan 017.** The closing commentary returns to Proposition 3. It asks for closure under direct factors in M3 and M4 as well. The author adds direct-factor stability of \(W\) to weak saturation and discusses the resulting stability for the \(W\)-cofibrations, the \(W\)-fibrations and the two universal classes.
