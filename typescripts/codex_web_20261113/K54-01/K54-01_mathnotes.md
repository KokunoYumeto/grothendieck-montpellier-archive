# Mathematical notes · K54-01

## What the pages do

**The cover and the first extension (scans 2–3).** The cover names duality in the cohomology of abelian varieties. The first working page calls $A$ the variety and $B$ its dual. It writes $H^*(A)$ in terms of the de Rham complex, identifies $t_B$ with $H^1(A,O_A)$, and relates $t_A$ to the dual of the space of one-forms. Its first row places $H^1(A)$ between $t_A^\vee$ and $t_B$. The accompanying assertion of canonicity carries a qualification about characteristic zero.

**The universal extension (scan 3).** A rewritten classification of vector extensions leads to a universal extension $E$ of $A$. Passing to tangent spaces gives a row with $t_B^\vee,t_E,t_A$. The writer identifies this extension with $S_B$, writes $t_E=H^1(B)$, and says it is transposed to $S_A$. He boxes the resulting duality between $H^1(A)$ and $H^1(B)$.

**Other degrees (scans 3 and 5).** The sentence at the foot of scan 3 continues at the top of scan 5. It passes to $H^*(A)$ and $H^*(B)$, then exchanges $p$ and $q$ in their individual Hodge groups. The exterior-algebra expression marked $(1)$ is invoked for a reduction to dimension one. Two coefficient groups in the following line have been rewritten. The argument ends by referring back to a duality to be defined in degree one.

**Generic freeness and its proof (scan 4, crossed typescript).** An opening corollary starts with a ring with irreducible spectrum and minimal prime $\underline p$, an algebra and a module of finite presentation. It seeks an $f$ outside $\underline p$ such that $M_f$ is free over $A_f$. The proof introduces a finitely generated ideal $J$ testing base changes for which the module becomes free or flat. It uses $J_{\underline p}=0$ to choose $f$ with $J_f=0$.

**Flatness modulo powers of an ideal (scan 4).** In Proposition 4.1 the writer cancels the nilpotence hypothesis on $I$. A handwritten change moves the conclusion to $A/I^{n+1}$ for every $n$. The family of algebras with jointly zero kernel and the flat or free hypotheses survive in the type. The separate handwritten corollary at the foot tries out cases involving a nilpotent ideal or a noetherian ring and a separation condition. It ends with an equivalence involving $M_0$ and the $M_i$. Several of its assumptions are cancelled.

**Cartier duality and the degree count (scan 5, D5).** In characteristic $p>0$ the writer pairs the groups denoted ${}_pA$ and ${}_pB$. He writes ${}_F A=\operatorname{Gr}(t_A)\subset {}_p A$ and gives respective degrees $p^d$ and $p^{2d}$, with $d=\dim A$. A right bracket groups the inclusions for $A$ and $B$. He asserts that the two $F$-subgroups are orthogonal.

**The flattening subscheme (scan 6).** “Proposition A” replaces a corollary heading. After restriction to a suitable nonempty open of $Y$, it specifies a closed subscheme $Z$ through which precisely the base changes making $F'$ flat over $Y'$ factor. Extra affine hypotheses give a freeness assertion for sections on a finite affine cover. The proof passes through a noetherian ring, invokes generic flatness, and adds the ideals $J_i$ attached to the affine opens. It takes $Z=V(J)=\operatorname{Spec}(A/J)$ and reduces the verification to affine test schemes.

**The transposed rows and the last question (scans 5 and 7).** The two horizontal rows in scan 7 carry $F$ and $V$ in opposite orders. The next sequence places ${}_p A$ between $\operatorname{Gr}(\underline t_A)$ and $D(\operatorname{Gr}(\underline t_B))$. The writer calls this an extension of $D({}_F B)$ by ${}_F A$. He returns to $S_A$ as an extension of abelian $p$-Lie algebras. An enclosed addition attributes zero $p$-th power to $t_A^\vee$. The page closes by asking about recovery of the extension from ${}_p A$.

**An intersection of regions (scan 8, opening proof fragment).** The locally finite hyperplane family meets the closed segment from $y$ to $z$ in only finitely many relevant hyperplanes. The author uses induction on their indices in the intersection of the $D_H(C)$ and concludes $C'=C$. The scan begins in the middle of this proof.

**Two chamber corollaries (scan 8).** Corollary 2 applies Proposition 10 to the family with one hyperplane removed and concludes that this hyperplane is a common wall of two chambers. Corollary 3 describes the relative interior of $\bar C\cap H_o$ in the wall. Its points lie on no other hyperplane of the family and are adjacent to $C$ and one other chamber, the same chamber for every such point. The proof identifies this interior with $\Gamma$ and begins the converse for a point of $\bar\Gamma$ outside $\Gamma$. The last sentence continues beyond the scan.

## Remarks of the model

If the corresponding subgroup on the B side has the same degree, the product of their degrees equals the larger degree written here. The calculation concerns the displayed exponents alone. The manuscript's assertion about the pairing is recorded above as its own claim.
