# Mathematical notes on cote 79, scans 17–32

## What the pages do

**U1 · Components and units (17–18).** The image \(\Lambda_0=\operatorname{Im}(\mathbb Z\to\Lambda)\) controls the connected components of the contour at a vertex. Formula (40) gives their set as \(L_x/\Lambda_0\). The condition \(\Lambda_0=\Lambda\) leads to \(\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with \(n\geq3\) under the standing restriction \(2\,1_\Lambda\ne0\). The foot of 17 asks what happens for \(P_M\). On 18 the additional condition on units leaves \(n=3,4,6\) in the finite case.

**U2 · Orientation (18–19).** A face bijection \(\alpha_f\) distinguishes the orientation mapping to \(e_M\). Compatibility along adjacent faces gives an orientation \(\mu\) on \(SP_M\). Changing the gauge \(e_M\) to its negative reverses this choice. Formula (45) describes the group action on \(\mu\) by the determinant.

**U3 · Paths between vertices (20–22).** The required chain has consecutive wedges in \(\{\pm e_M\}\). For a pair forming a basis, the author inserts an intermediate vector. In the semilocal case he first obtains two consecutive bases, rescales an intermediate vector, then inserts \(z'\) to obtain the three wedges in (47). He states a distance bound of three and identifies the icosahedron as a case of equality. The extension to \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\) is explicitly left open at this point.

**U4 · From linear actions to automorphisms (22–24).** A linear map acting trivially on the three vertices of a face must be scalar by sign. The argument chooses a triple with sum zero and compares its coefficients in a basis. Diagram (50) puts the induced injections into a map of exact sequences. The identification of oriented frames in (51) gives a simply transitive action. A lower lemma on scan 23 was cancelled. Scan 24 states the automorphism theorem and regularity for the rings under consideration.

**U5 · Counting and genus (24–27).** The prime-power decomposition of \(n\) gives a product for the group order \(N\). The primed group has order \(N'=N/2\). Oriented edges, unoriented edges and faces are counted by the frame and triple quotients. In (62) the vertex, edge and face counts are \(N'/n,\ N'/2,\ N'/3\). The author obtains \(g=1+\frac{N'}2(\frac16-\frac1n)\). After several crossed versions, (66) gives genus zero for \(3\le n\le5\), genus one for \(n=6\) and genus at least two for \(n\ge7\).

**U6 · Cartographic generators (27–28).** Section II names rotations around a vertex, an edge centre and a face. An isomorphism sends them to integral matrices modulo sign in (68)–(69). The next presentation includes three involutions; (71)–(72) give matrices for the larger group. The slanted NB expresses each rotation as an ordered product of two involutions. The flag drawing labels which adjacent flag each involution selects.

**U7 · Frames and change of rings (29–31).** A basis up to simultaneous sign is a point of \(\operatorname{Rep}(M)'\). The map from the integers to \(\Lambda\) makes this set a right group-set for the integral group. The substitution \(g\mapsto g^{-1}\) gives the left action. A gauge singles out the symplectic frames in (75). The author expects their oriented polyhedron to recover the earlier \(SP_M\) and discusses the components when \(\Lambda_0\) is smaller than \(\Lambda\).

**U8 · Choosing a flag (32).** For \(\Lambda=\Lambda_0\), a triple of distinct vertices \((s,t,u)\) determines \(r=(s,a,f)\), with \(a=\{s,t\}\) and \(f=\{s,t,u\}\). Equation (78) writes the vertices in terms of representatives \(x,y,z\) of sum zero. Equation (80) is the positive choice \(x\wedge y=e_M\). The closing parenthesis identifies the choice of a direct frame with a symplectic basis modulo sign, in preparation for writing down the flag automorphisms.
