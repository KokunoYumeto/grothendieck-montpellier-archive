# Mathematical notes on scans 164–179

Line references are to the individual page files. The statements marked C170, C174 and C176 have entries in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**Lemma 4.4.1, scans 164–165.** Given the localizer $W$, Grothendieck chooses $C_0$ with $TF=C_{0*}\subset W$, sets $C=\widetilde C_0$, $TC=C\cap W$ and $F=(TC)_*$, and identifies the two factorizations needed for a Quillen structure. He singles out the second pair $(TC,F)$. Stability of $TC$ under change of cobase leads him to impose $C_0\subset\mathrm{Cof}_W$.

**Proposition 4.4.2 and Corollaries 4.4.3–4.4.4, scans 166–167.** The proposition gives stability properties of $\mathrm{Cof}_W$, including stability under filtered inductive limits. Intersecting with $W$ gives the class $W^{\mathrm{univ}}$ of co-universal $W$-equivalences. With finite amalgamated sums and a small part $C_0$ of accessible arrows, the construction gives $C\subset\mathrm{Cof}_W$ and $TC=C\cap W^{\mathrm{univ}}$. The argument invokes the description of $\widetilde C_0$ as the smallest left Q-stable class containing $C_0$.

**Corollary 4.4.5, scan 167.** Accessibility of the objects supplies the first Quillen pair. A sufficient condition for the second is a small part $TC_0\subset TC$ with $\widetilde{TC_0}=TC$.

**The next proposition and its proof, scans 167–168.** The proposition compares four conditions on $TC_0$. These identify its generated class with $TC$, identify $F_0$ with $F$, express $TF$ as $F_0\cap W$, and express $W$ as $TF\circ\widetilde{TC_0}$. Marginal boxes distinguish the two previously known equalities from the assertions involving $TC_0$. The proof refers back to Proposition 4.3 and a factorization theorem. A slanted addition calls attention to the smallness of $TC_0$, words he had struck from the statement.

**The change of question, scan 169.** He records his unsuccessful attempt to obtain a small generating $TC_0$ from a cardinal filtration and accessibility of $W$. Section 5 turns to accessible parts of the objects of a category and stability under filtered inductive limits.

**Hypotheses a)–c), Lemma 5.1 and Corollary 5.2, scans 170–171.** From the hypotheses on the category and filtration he extracts a small generating family and invokes an existence result for projective limits. The partially ordered set $\mathrm{Ss}(X)$ of subobjects has arbitrary infima and suprema. Its formula for a supremum in terms of upper bounds is C170. Intersecting the subobjects through which a map factors gives its image. The map to that image is epic, with a further lemma phrased using equalizers and strict subobjects.

**The extra hypotheses, scans 171–172.** Condition d) bounds the size of an indexing category by its arrows. The slanted note gives this closure condition the later name $d_0$. The pages then discuss staying in $\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}$ on taking an image, distinguishing subobjects ($d_1$) and quotients with full image ($d_2$). A separate marginal note names an exactness condition e) on filtered limits of monomorphisms.

**Proposition 5.3, scans 172–174.** The subobjects of $X$ belonging to $\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}$ form $\mathrm{Ss}_{\pi}(X)$. The author obtains upper bounds for families of size at most $\pi$ by taking the image of a coproduct. He then presents $X$ as a filtered inductive limit of small subobjects, under either the stated condition on monomorphisms or the accessibility hypothesis. In the proof $i\mapsto X'_i$ sends a presentation index to an image in $X$. The next sheet’s diagram has identity composite. Its argument about a monomorphism with a section is C174.

**Corollary 5.4 and Scholie 5.5, scans 174–175.** The corollary gives cofinality in $\mathrm{Ss}_{\pi}(X)$ for a sufficiently large filtered part presenting $X$, under the cardinal hypothesis. After cancelling an earlier corollary, Grothendieck takes $\mathrm{Ss}_{\pi}(X)$ itself as the indexing set. With the stated exactness condition, a filtered $J$ of cardinal at most $\pi$ has a supremum $i_J$ with $X_{i_J}=\varinjlim_{i\in J}X_i$. He calls this presentation « (parfaitement) π-adapté ».

**Conditions (5.1) and (5.2), scans 176–177.** For an isomorphism-stable part $M$ of the objects, (5.1) tests membership of $X$ by cofinality of the small subobjects belonging to $M$. On $\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}$, $X$ is itself the greatest subobject, the observation C176. Starting with $M^\pi$ therefore recovers $M^\pi=M\cap\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}$ in (5.2). The text next draws a restriction on $M^\pi$ from closure under the relevant inductive limits and direct factors.

**Condition (5.3), scan 177.** Membership is now tested inside every cofinal $J\subset\mathrm{Ss}_{\pi}(X)$ closed under the specified filtered suprema of size at most $\pi$. The marginal note also considers replacing cofinality by the condition that the limit over $J$ maps isomorphically to $X$. It relates the two formulations when the objects of $\mathrm{Filt}^{\pi}$ are $\pi$-accessible.

**Proposition 5.6 and Lemma 5.7, scan 178.** The proposition relates (5.3) to accessibility of $M$. The author qualifies it in the margin by requiring stability of $M^\pi$ under filtered inductive limits of cardinal at most $\pi$. The lemma singles out the cofinal part $I'$ of a $\pi$-adapted presentation where $X_i\to X$ is monic. It also states closure of $I'$ under the indicated suprema.

**The two implications, scans 178–179.** Assuming $X\in M$, he uses the image of $I'$ in the small subobjects and then applies the argument to tails of $I'$, obtaining cofinality of $I_M$. For the converse he restricts the presentation to $I_M$. Its failure to remain visibly $\pi$-adapted is the difficulty in the last paragraph. The supplied text breaks off after « il faut ».

## Remarks of the model

- **C170 · Model remark · High confidence.** On scan 170, lines 27–31, the identification $\sup J=\inf J'$ passes an order-theoretic check when $J'$ is the set of upper bounds. The reason is the defining greatest-lower-bound property, which covers the empty-family case as well.
- **C174 · Model remark · High confidence.** The sentence « mono. ayant une section » on scan 174, lines 10–13, suffices for the isomorphism step. The check uses cancellation by a monomorphism and the stated identity composite.
- **C176 · Model remark · High confidence.** On scan 176, lines 23–35, the cofinality test does recover the chosen $M^\pi$ on the small objects. In that case the subobject poset has $X$ as its greatest element, so containing that element characterizes cofinality.
