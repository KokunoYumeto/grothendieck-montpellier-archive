# Hand log, cote 68, scans 33–44

## Scan 33

The game letter G can have only a short downstroke at the right foot. Compare the more squared G in the paired equations below the fold. The player j has a tall upright and a leftward foot. A horizontal stroke above it distinguishes the other player. The set-difference stroke falls to the right. The stars on the first two right sides differ in the number of visible arms. The small membership and inclusion signs nearly close up in heavy ink, locally only. The low trailing horizontal in the opening Une need not cancel the word.

## Scan 34

The angular powerset P has an additional upper left stroke compared with the ordinary P. Tildes are long waves, distinct from the two slopes of the hat on α. The bar in condition 4 lies above that hat. The player's j has a long left foot even without a prominent dot. A high short tick to its right in the last union is a prime, not the bar used elsewhere. The overwritten quantifier in (2) has two long surviving oblique uprights. The highly abbreviated phrase il f et s. uses a long descending f.

## Scan 36

The algebra symbol changes by overwriting a pale U with a dark A. There are two stems of different heights and often a grey curve outside the dark triangle. Neither colour nor thickness by itself signifies blackboard bold. In contrast Q has an internal extra stroke, and the natural-number N has a double rising stroke. N naming nilpotents has an ordinary curved opening. The ideal has a near-rectangular three-sided outline open to the left, resembling the player-set J on 34; I remains a possible naming on this leaf. The tail of a slanted pencil note crosses into the main column. Several corrections to a whole line are placed above it; the long curved leader is separate from the mathematical delimiters.

## Scan 37

An inner stroke in the G and the leading U of the units functor is visible even in the lighter writing. A plain U names the algebra, whereas the units functor is double U followed by an ordinary n. The flat bar over the double G is independent of its subscript. In the paired diagram the intervening vertical inclusion has the appearance of a cap. In the formula after “i.e.” one loop has a descender that the surrounding barred e's lack. The upper prime on K is distinct from the comma after K in Hom gr.

## Scan 38

i has a dot above a short horizontal ending; j has a descending leftward foot. In the coefficient expansion the letters are quite flat but the feet are visible. The opposed vertical pairs of little strokes under the expansions are upright equals. The group letter W has a doubled first upright. In this hand a nearly straight low dash makes an n in an exponent, as on y over n!. The lower cancelled equation has both a horizontal strike and separate oblique cancellations.

## Scan 39

A second upright distinguishes the initial A of the automorphism functor and the E of the endomorphism functor from ordinary Aut and End. This varies even within a sentence, notably in the points over K′ and the coefficients uₙ. The near-horizontal strokes in the second evaluation repeat the line above. A head at the left of the map in (**) is visibly different from the map in (*). The two abbreviated t's share a crossing in tt.

## Scan 40

A short high slash with a point ends the abbreviation beside the first sum. Its second occurrence below the fold has only a stop. The two bars above the lower e's survive a darker correction to the letter itself. The lower v and u have different numbers of turns, including beside f in item 3. The parallel rules on G indicate its formal version only where drawn.

## Scan 41

A dotted i has a short shaft; j returns left a long way below its attachment, even at the height of an exponent. The two letters in the mixed powers have been darkened and a curl from the earlier letter remains. Beside the first sum dfn runs up to a bracket. The operator later read as rho has a long descender, unlike the short horizontal cross in theta.

## Scan 42

The single rising N on rho contrasts with the two initial strokes of the N following the membership sign. The short horizontal line above m is an abbreviation mark, as on the preceding pages. Horizontal continuations in the fast word for commutative and in the word after d' in the last clause do not determine their full endings. The t cross in nilpotent is broad.

## Scan 43

The doubled stem of W is distinct at the fold. The script M has an entry curl and low outward exit, unlike the upright M in the earlier Hom arguments. The change from one to two pen strokes after K in the vertical diagram is a prime then a double prime. The final membership sign below exp is turned toward its term. The small list letter resembles a zero. The last baseline word is bien.

## Scan 44

Open-ended E resembles epsilon in the sentence about an S-automorphism. Elsewhere the initials of the three labelled E's have two uprights and three horizontal strokes. Compare the round crossed D under X in Si X and the curled remnant under the first E in the last sum. The plus on the right and the comma following the first theta in the bracket remain visible. Under the structure sheaf letter he draws a separate baseline. The closing ornament has three strokes.


## Comparisons across the eleven leaves

On 33–34 the hat on the player and the separate bar on the opposing player do different work and have different ink. The low curl of j in those arguments also occurs in the indices on 38 and 41. On 36 the underwritten U has an open mouth; it must actually be visible before it can be recorded under A. On 37, 38 and 42 the same short bar over a low wave after de supports an abbreviated m, with an equals sign also possible on each leaf. From 37 through 44 the formal action generally carries a high bar; every individual G and e was read at its own location. The open epsilon-like capital on 44 differs from the squared doubled E of the final brace. The single shaft after u on 44 and the leftward shaft after u on 39 are visible exceptions to the more usual assignment arrow.

| 33–34, 38, 41–42 | Short dotted i and deep left-hooked j | On 33–34 this separates the player j from an index i.
