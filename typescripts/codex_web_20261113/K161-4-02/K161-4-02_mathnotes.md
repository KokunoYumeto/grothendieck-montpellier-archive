# Mathematical notes · K161-4-02

## What the pages do

**U18 · Comparing products of topoi, scan 18.** The page asks whether the comparison in (2), from $\widehat{C\times D}$ to $\widehat C\times\widehat D$, is an equivalence. It treats the case of categories closed under finite limits, then introduces $i:C\to C'$ and $j:E\to\widehat D$. A square involving $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\gamma'$ and a factorization $\alpha=\alpha_1\alpha_0$ give an embedding. The last paragraph describes a full subcategory by conditions on $p_*(F)$ and $q_*(F)$. Identifying it with the essential image is the remaining step.

**U20 · Finite sums and finite constant spaces, scans 20–21.** The three numbered properties describe the summand inclusions, the empty pairwise intersections and the decomposition after a change of base. A finite set $I$ gives the sum of $I$ copies of $e_k$. The notes pass between this space, functions on $I$ and decompositions by idempotents. A composition law on $I$ produces a comultiplication on the algebra. The first line of scan 21 identifies the underlying spectrum.

**U21 · Spectra of immersions, scans 21–22.** The page compares two fibre-product squares, then asks for the closure of the image of $Z=V(J)$ and states a condition for dominance. The next sheet gives formulas for a prime, the inverse image of $V(J)$ and the inverse image of a principal open. Surjections and localizations furnish the closed and open examples.

**U22a · Two topological preliminaries, scan 22.** An opening remark describes noetherian spaces using closed subsets and irreducible components. A second line singles out connected components when irreducible components are finite or locally finite. The next heading introduces the basis of principal opens.

**U22b · Products and underlying points, scan 22.** After the formula for the spectrum of a product of algebras, a warning compares the points of a fibre product with pairs of points in the factors. The text identifies the fibre over $(x,y)$ by the spectrum of a tensor product of residue fields. A marginal prompt asks for an example. A final qualification distinguishes the statement about maximal points over an algebraically closed field from a statement about their topology.

**U23a · Course plan, scan 23.** The English paragraphs give separate prerequisites for the introduction to algebraic geometry and the course on algebraic groups. The first course will recall basic facts. The second assumes familiarity with algebraic geometry. The author mentions homework, time for proofs and the relation between the courses.

**U23b · Systems of equations, scan 23.** After the horizontal divider the notes return to French. The polynomial algebra indexed by $I$, evaluation at a tuple, and a system indexed by $J$ introduce two viewpoints. The geometric viewpoint varies the coefficient algebra $k'$ and keeps track of how the solution set changes. The bottom brace separates the embedded problem from study independent of the embedding.

**U24 · Representing the solution functor, scan 24.** Passing from equations $S$ to their generated ideal $J$ leaves the solution functor unchanged. The quotient $A_J=P_I/J$ represents it. The discussion relates a choice of generators to a map out of a polynomial algebra, distinguishes epimorphisms from surjections, and describes finite type by embeddings into finite-dimensional affine space. The last formula reverses inclusion between ideals and their functors of zeros.

**U25a · Restricting to fields, scan 25.** Circled 6 restricts the functor of zeros to fields. The displayed ideal of functions vanishing at the field-valued points is $\widetilde J$. The page relates this to intersections of kernels of homomorphisms to fields and to the nilpotent elements. The discussion at 6′ asks how far the class of test fields can be restricted. A side note invokes the Nullstellensatz.

**U25b · Functions on affine space, scan 25.** Circled 7 calls attention to nilpotents. Circled 8 identifies $A$ with maps from $V_A$ to the affine line. Evaluation and the arithmetic of functions appear in the left margin. For a quotient presentation, the value of $f$ can be described using a polynomial lift.

**U26 · Cancelled topos calculation, scan 26.** The diagram places an underlined Hom between slice expressions and $X'$. The pencil work below repeats Hom expressions with $I_{X'}$ and $E$. The last line names the topos associated to the compact space $I^E$. The whole draft is cancelled. The intervening pencil words do not determine a completed construction.

**U27 · Limits on the affine side, scan 27.** Products and fibre products of affine spaces are paired with tensor products of algebras. The notes draw the base-change construction, exhibit an equalizer using a diagonal, and give the corresponding quotient by differences $u(x)-v(x)$. Filtered projective limits, the kernel of a group homomorphism and the determinant example finish the sheet.

**U28a · Stabilizers, scan 28.** The orbit map sends $g$ to $g.x$. A square gives the stabilizer of one point. For a family $x_i$, the text describes an intersection of stabilizers and the stabilizer of the tuple for the action on the product.

**U28b · Colimits and test algebras, scans 28–29.** The affine quotient example uses a triangular subgroup of the general linear group. For a finite sum, the comparison of $k'$-points is expressed as a map from a disjoint union of Hom sets to a Hom set out of a product. The description by orthogonal idempotents explains the connected case at the top of scan 29. The empty sum is then described on zero and nonzero test algebras and called a strict initial object. Coequalizers lead to a question the course will revisit about finite equivalence relations.

**U29 · Underlying points, scans 29–30.** Closed affine subspaces are put in order-reversing correspondence with ideals. The underlying points of the spectrum are introduced as « lieux » and associated to prime ideals. The next page attaches a residue field to a point and describes it through field-valued points with the given locus.

**U30 · The topology on the spectrum, scan 30.** The closed set $V(S)$ depends on the radical of the generated ideal. The page gives intersection and finite-union formulas for closed sets and an elementwise argument involving a prime ideal. The margins describe the closure of a point and specialization. The basic opens satisfy $X_{fg}=X_f\cap X_g$.

**U31a · Quasi-compactness, irreducibility and sobriety, scan 31.** A finite-subfamily criterion for sums of ideals matches the covering condition. Closed subsets can be given the spectrum of a reduced quotient. For reduced $A$, irreducibility is compared with integrality. The corollary identifies the irreducible closed sets with the closures of points and concludes that the spectrum is sober.

**U31b · A sheaf on the principal opens, scans 31–32.** The page assigns $P(f)$ to an open and a restriction map to an inclusion. The relation written $f\prec g$ is expressed by $f^n=gh$. A covering family leads to a diagram with two maps into the product over pairs. Scan 32 constructs $M_f$ as a direct limit, identifies the restrictions $M_g\to M_f$, states the sheaf condition and begins a reduction to $f=1$. It ends with the structure sheaf, local rings and localization at a multiplicative set.
