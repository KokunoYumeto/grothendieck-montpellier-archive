# Mathematical notes on cote 89, scans 12–21

## What the pages do

**Scans 12–13, torsors and partitions.** Diagram (7) puts the category of torsors for a plane $V$ between two products of categories of torsors for lines $D_i$. A torsor for a line over $\mathbb F_3$ is described by a three-element set $E$ and an identification of its two cyclic orientations $\omega_E$ with $D^*$. Taking the disjoint union of two such sets gives a six-element set partitioned into two triples. Diagram (8) compares the descriptions indexed by $\tau$ and $\tau'$. Interchanging the two families gives a quasi-inverse.

**Scan 14, the square and its plane.** Adjoining zero to each two-element set $\omega_i$ reconstructs a line. The product of the two lines gives the plane with its distinguished pair of lines. Equivalence (9) puts these data in correspondence with a combinatorial square $Q$. In (10) the two ends of diagonal $i$ form $d_i$. The vertices form the union of the two distinguished punctured lines. The displayed product for the edges also describes the complementary part of $V^*$.

**Scan 15, duality.** The complementary pair of lines defines the dual square $DQ$. The map $\alpha_Q$ goes from $V(DQ)$ to $V(Q)$. Applying the construction twice gives a composite identified with minus the identity and with the map induced by the antipodism. The last formula reverses the roles of $Q$ and $DQ$.

**Scan 16, projective points over a ring.** For a free rank-two module over $\Lambda$, $V^*$ consists of elements which are everywhere nonzero, described here as members of a basis. The quotient by $\Lambda^*$ gives $P(V)^!$, the free lines among the rank-one direct summands. To a point $x$ the text assigns its line $D_x$ and a torsor $T_x$. It also defines $\omega_V=\det(V)^*$. Disjointness of two lines is expressed by a direct sum. The field and local-ring cases are distinguished.

**Scan 17, the wedge pairing.** For disjoint points the exterior product induces a pairing of the torsors with values in $\omega_V$. The contracted product is identified with this determinant torsor. Reversing the factors changes the sign. Equations (7)–(8) relate equality of three cyclic wedges to $u+v+w=0$ and to transversality of the third direction.

**Scans 18–19, reconstruction of addition.** The data are the set of points, the family of torsors over them, a common determinant torsor, disjointness, and the pairing for disjoint points. In a field, the punctured lines and a common zero give the underlying set of vectors. Addition is described in three cases. One summand may be zero, both summands may lie on one line, or they may lie on disjoint lines. In the last case the common wedge value selects $w$ and the sum is $-w$. Scan 19 begins a list of properties required of the data. Only item a) appears before the change of subject.

**Scan 20, combinatorial polygons.** The outline places topological realization beside the passage from topological to combinatorial polygons. It describes an incidence order on $S\amalg A$, with vertices minimal and edges maximal. Duality exchanges them and reverses the order. For components of order at least three it gives descriptions by two-element subsets of $S$ and, dually, of $A$. For a polygon of this size there are two inverse circular orders, and choosing one gives an orientation.

**Scan 21, orientations and the successor operation.** An oriented edge has an origin. Orientations on the edges fit around a contour when the two signs induced at each vertex are opposite. The theorem relates such choices to a map written $\rho_\omega$. Its conditions specify orientation preservation and an edge from $s$ to $\rho_\omega(s)$ with origin $s$. The last note describes the same choice from the two edges incident to a vertex. Its little drawing distinguishes the two local signs.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 16.** « On a envie d'expliciter comment on récupère … V à l'aide de l'ens $P(V)^!$ ». This is the stated aim of the reconstruction on scans 18–19.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Scans 14–15 · High confidence for the finite example.** In the coordinate plane over $\mathbb F_3$, each of the six choices of two distinct lines gives four vertices in their punctured union. Adding one nonzero vector from each line gives exactly the other four nonzero vectors. Direct enumeration checks the set identification in this example.
- **M2 · Scans 17–18 · High confidence for the finite example.** In the same plane I tested every ordered independent pair $u,v$ and every $w$, including zero. In all 432 triples the cyclic determinant equalities held exactly when $u+v+w=0$. There were 48 solutions, one per ordered independent pair. This gives a concrete check of the missing relation identified in S9.
- **M3 · Scan 21 · High confidence for the examples.** One four-edge cycle has two assignments of edge directions with one incoming edge at every vertex. Two disjoint such cycles have four. Enumerating all directions on their edges reproduces the distinction between the main count and the marginal count.
- **M4 · Scan 21 · Medium confidence.** Reading the theorem's count of two for one connected polygon makes it consistent with its marginal count for $r$ components. The margin explicitly names components, while the live theorem simply says « Tout polygone combinatoire ».
- **Reading question · Scan 14, immediately before (9) · Low confidence.** The final transcription has « équivalence ». « équivalences » is another possible reading of the last curl. That curl resembles both a final s and the outgoing pen stroke seen on other words of the line. The page and fragment retain their post-witness checksums.
