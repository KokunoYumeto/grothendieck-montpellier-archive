## What the pages do

**87 · A connection from a rational form.** Starting in mid-sentence on an open set U, the author passes from a splitting for principal parts to a first-order operator on the invertible sheaf L. He applies the operator to the canonical rational section associated with D and divides the result by that section. He gives the local test for the resulting form by subtracting df/f, then interprets the sheaf of pairs (divisor, connection) and an extension obtained by inverse image.

**88 · A working question.** Four SGA reminders precede a triangle from X to A over S. The draft pulls back an extension of A* and asks for its interpretation. It then tries formulas for a differential along a divisor, its residue and the difference from df/f. A sketch marks both branches meeting at a point with 1. The page is crossed diagonally.

**180 · Divisors with prescribed invertible sheaf.** A relative divisorial subscheme transverse to the fibres is described by a section that is a non-zero-divisor on each fibre. Fixing L, the author distinguishes isomorphism and different kinds of equivalence of L(Y). He describes which sections are to be identified by multiplication by a unit. The star on the chosen part of the space of sections belongs to this construction.

**181–182 · The fibre of the divisor functor.** A section θ of Div maps to the section λ of Pic associated with L. The quotient of sections in the previous calculation is interpreted as the sections of a relative divisor space. Base change in S leads to a representability condition. A fundamental invertible sheaf Λ on the product with Pic would recover the full divisor space from this construction.

**182–183 · Projective bundles and base change.** Under properness, flatness and a functorial Hom description using E, the candidate parameter space is P(E). The primary-fibre hypothesis and the abbreviation qualifying it have different degrees of legibility. The second proposition lists seven equivalent conditions involving flatness, smoothness («simple»), symmetric powers, local freeness of E, flatness of L in the sense of item (vi), and surjectivity of a fibre comparison. Several implications have arrows drawn beside them.

**184–186 · From a punctured cone to the vertex.** The cover introduces the Picard groups of cones and their local rings at the vertex. The next sheet expresses Pic(C−a) in terms of Pic(X) and the class ξ of O_X(1). The restriction to the punctured local scheme is related to open neighbourhoods of a. To show that restriction is an isomorphism for regular X, the author reduces the question to the class of a divisor whose support avoids a.

**186–188 · A divisor on a line bundle.** Passing to the projective cone produces a divisor on a vector bundle with support proper over X. A lemma, restricted by insertion to rank one, asserts triviality of the divisor class after the zero section is removed. For a regular irreducible support, a base-change square and a section q express the class by pullback and pushforward. The proof then removes a closed subset containing the singular locus. The conclusion identifies Pic(C−a), Pic(Cₐ−a) and Pic(X)/ℤξ; normality leads to a description by divisor classes.

**189–190 · Comparison with the completion.** The author asks whether completing the local ring preserves the Picard group of the punctured scheme. He blows up the origin and writes diagrams relating the punctured cone to the complement of the exceptional X. The two orders of hat and tilde distinguish the blow-up of the completion from a formal completion along X. The existence theorem is invoked to compare Picard groups; the remaining morphism concerns a line bundle E and its formal completion Ê along X.

**191–192 · Infinitesimal neighbourhoods and a curve criterion.** The Picard group of Ê is written as an inverse limit over the neighbourhoods Eₙ. The author identifies J/J² with O_X(1), writes a restriction with a left inverse, and turns to bijectivity. He identifies the successive kernels with H¹(X,O_X(n)). The proposition gives a vanishing criterion for all n at least 1 and states its necessity when X is a curve. The plane-curve corollary uses the degree and genus to exhibit failure of the condition.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scan 192 · High confidence.** The stated numerical cutoff follows from the genus formula on the same sheet. Substitution gives g−1−d = d(d−5)/2, so a positive integer degree satisfies d < g−1 exactly from 6 onward. This checks the arithmetic of the corollary only.

**R2 · Scans 191–192 · High confidence.** The curved arrow in the small diagram explains why the author switches from the map going into Pic(Ê) to the one leaving it. The composite is an isomorphism. Identifying the two outer groups therefore gives restriction a section. Injectivity of that restriction is then the remaining condition for the section to be bijective.

**R3 · Scans 186 and 188 · Low confidence as to scope.** The lemma acquires «de rang 1» by insertion, and a later margin mentions «rang quelconque». The latter supplies no replacement statement specifying how the open complement or the divisor class should be treated in higher rank.

**Reading question · 186, L8 to 187, L2.** The text runs «tel que suppt / soit propre sur X». One would expect «suppt D», referring to the divisor named earlier in the sentence. The final tall stroke on 186 looks like the t in the abbreviation, and the next sheet begins directly with «soit». I have used D in explaining the subject of the lemma; it remains implicit at this junction in the transcription.
