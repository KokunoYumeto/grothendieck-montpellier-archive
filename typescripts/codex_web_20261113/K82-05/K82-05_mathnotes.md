# Mathematical notes on scans 65–79

The formula numbers below are the manuscript’s. The scan number distinguishes repeated labels. “Checked” refers to the specific calculation or comparison named in the corresponding entry of CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 65–66, (11)–(18).** The stabilizer of a section of the projective line is written as a triangular subgroup. The orbit map identifies a quotient with that line. Twisting the identification by a torsor gives the quotient description for a form of the line. An isomorphism torsor and its automorphism group then give functors between categories of forms.

**U2 · Scans 66–69, (19)–(31).** One passes from a simply connected group to its quotient by the centre and, in the other direction, to the simply connected cover. The Lie functor produces two different Lie algebras. A point of the twisted line has a Borel subgroup for its stabilizer. For a quaternion algebra the simply connected group is obtained as the kernel of the reduced norm on its units.

**U3 · Scans 69–71, (32)–(38).** The quaternion algebra \(M\) gives both the adjoint group \(G\) and its cover \(\widetilde G\). The group of units is the common middle term of two exact sequences. Their Lie algebra counterparts describe \(\mathfrak g\) as \(M/\mathcal O_S1\) and \(\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\) as the kernel of the reduced trace. The maps from cover to quotient are written as an inclusion followed by a projection.

**U4 · Scans 71–72, (39)–(43) and the second (42).** The trace of a product gives a duality on \(M\). The orthogonal of the scalar line is the trace-zero submodule, yielding the pairing between \(\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\) and \(\mathfrak g\). The polar form of \(-\det\) differs from the trace pairing by the product of the two traces. Their restrictions agree if one argument is trace zero.

**U5 · Scans 73–75, (43)–(50).** Restriction of \(-\det\) defines the ternary quadratic form \(q\). A bracketed calculation states the factors relating its polar form and the Killing form. A basis \(\omega\) of the determinant line is obtained locally from a matrix algebra and discussed up to sign. Its tensor square enters the divided and ordinary discriminants. The author then sets \(E=\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\).

**U6 · Scans 75–76, (51)–(55).** Under the duality already described, \(f:\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\to\mathfrak g\) represents the trace pairing restricted to trace-zero elements. After an explicit choice of orientation for the bracket, the reverse map \(f'\) is given by \(\bar u\mapsto(\operatorname{Tr}u)1-2u\). The manuscript checks that this formula descends and that both composites are multiplication by \(-2\).

**U7 · Scans 76–78, (56)–(59).** The proposed projective model is the conic cut out by \(q\). The proposition describes inverse images of Borel subgroups and the associated corank-one Lie subbundles. Scan 78 characterizes a Borel subbundle by the vanishing of \(q\) on its orthogonal line. A marginal note describes the elements of that line as square-zero elements of \(M\).

**U8 · Scans 78–79, (58)–(61).** In characteristic two, the scalar line lies in \(\widetilde{\mathfrak g}\) and in the orthogonals used to define the map. The Borel-to-Lie-algebra map consequently factors through a projective line bundle. The author identifies this target with \(X^{(2)}\), and the map with relative Frobenius. He describes it as radicial, finite locally free of rank two, and failing to be a monomorphism.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 70–74. Model, high confidence for the split calculation.** I evaluated the formulas on two-by-two matrices. The polar form of \(-\det\) is \(\operatorname{Tr}(uv)-\operatorname{Tr}(u)\operatorname{Tr}(v)\). On the basis \(E_{12},E_{21},\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) of trace-zero matrices, the trace pairing has determinant \(-2\). The adjoint traces give the factors 4 and 8 in (45). These checks leave the sign convention for \(\omega\) on scan 75 open.

**R2 · Scan 76. Model, high confidence for the displayed formula.** In the split algebra, the expression \((\operatorname{Tr}u)1-2u\) is invariant under replacing \(u\) by \(u+\lambda1\), and its trace is zero. Direct substitution gives both composites in (54). This check does not establish the separate identification with \(\Lambda^2 f\).

**R3 · Scan 78. Model, high confidence for this counterexample in characteristic two.** Over \(\mathbf F_2\), let \(L\) be the plane spanned by the classes of \(E_{12}\) and \(E_{21}\) in \(\mathfrak{pgl}_2\). Their commutator is the class of the identity, hence zero. The orthogonal in \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) is the scalar line, and \(q(1)=1\). This answers Q2 in the negative if the question includes characteristic two. The manuscript does not specify that scope in the margin.

**R4 · Scan 79. Model, high confidence for the split chart.** On an affine coordinate the squaring map has zero derivative in characteristic two. The coordinate ring is free over the subring of squares of the coordinate with basis \(1,t\) over \(\mathbf F_2\). This gives the local rank two in the manuscript. I did not independently construct the canonical identification for every twisted conic.

**Reading questions retained after the witness check.** On scan 65, just above (12), the frozen line has a struck “définit” followed immediately by “section”. A later enlargement shows two groups of higher strokes that may be an insertion “et la” before “section”. The first group overlaps the cancelled ascender. On scan 77, in “image inverse de \(L_1\) dans \(M\)”, the frozen subscript could be a low comma after \(L\). On scan 73, the frozen space-or-plus reading in (44) remains an ink question even though the algebra selects addition. Neither observation changes the page files.
