# K23-04 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scans 67–68.** The “Structure Main Theorem” outline begins with a global argument for a locally noetherian base and a quasi-projective or compactifiable morphism. Part b) seeks the case of a separated quasi-finite morphism of finite presentation by locality on the base. The alternative route uses completion and induction on dimension.

**U02 · Scan 68.** Part c) asks how to remove finite presentation. One route starts with a quasi-projective morphism and passage to a limit. A second uses étale localization to descend a proposition. The author points out that the latter route already uses the Main Theorem locally upstairs.

**U03 · Scan 70.** Three conditions compare a projective limit, a factorization through an intermediate scheme, and an affine presentation using a filtered family of algebras. The displayed implication is (i) ⇔ (iii) ⇒ (ii). Under a locally noetherian hypothesis on the base, the text asserts equivalence of all three. It uses (i) to define “ess. de prés. finie”.

**U04 · Scan 70.** The next proposition lists examples and permanence properties, including base change, composition and a test involving the composite gf. A separate proposition over a universally Japanese base describes geometrically unibranch schemes by inverse limits and adds a parenthesis about normal schemes.

**U05 · Scan 71.** An essentially affine morphism is described by an affine first factor and a second factor of finite presentation. The writer compares the definition with a projective limit when the target is locally noetherian. The examples in (i) are affine morphisms and morphisms of finite presentation. The page stops after writing (ii).

**U06 · Scan 73.** The first list relates closed points, compact neighbourhoods, local rings of the reduction, the map from the constructible topology, and open or constructible parts. A separate list records implication directions. A note on totally disconnected locally compact spaces with a sheaf of rings whose stalks are fields runs down the right margin.

**U07 · Scan 73.** The corollary puts a reduced locally compact condition alongside flatness of modules, flatness tests for modules of finite presentation on affine opens, a quasi-coherent version under a separation hypothesis, and an isomorphism of ringed spaces.

**U08 · Scan 74.** The first paragraph finishes an earlier argument by base change and the proved part of 9.1. Theorem 9.4 gives effective descent for étale sheaves along a surjective morphism satisfying one of four conditions. The page lists an integral morphism, a proper morphism, a flat morphism locally of finite presentation, or a discrete target.

**U09 · Scan 74.** In 9.4.1 a sheaf with descent datum gives a functor G. Its value on Y is written as the kernel of the pair from F′(Y′) to F″(Y″). The author says G is a sheaf and exhibits an injection into the direct image. The last line ends with a comma.

**U10 · Scan 75.** Condition (h₀) asks for injectivity on open-and-closed subsets. The author phrases it through inverse images and étale coverings, and specializes it first to a nonempty connected base and then to a disjoint union of nonempty connected pieces. A marginal note proposes a fibrewise test.

**U11 · Scans 75–76.** A proposition carries (h₀) through a change of base over a field and states a converse with a nonempty hypothesis. The proof reduces to fields and to an algebraic closure. The rewritten opening of 76 uses the translates of U′, its saturation and the resulting open-and-closed subset.

**U12 · Scan 76.** The corollary presents a Cartesian square of sets of open-and-closed parts. Given V on T whose image on T′ comes from U′ on S′, it asks for a U on S. The last two lines invoke descent and injectivity after the double base change.

**U13 · Scan 77.** The diagrams place pairs A, B over different bases. The lower part asks when a morphism after pullback to T extends. Under two bracketed hypotheses the author writes that there is at most one extension, and tests existence after changing base by S′ to S.

**U14 · Scan 78.** The cancelled upper group contains a square, a second diagram over S, and differentials with superscript 1. Below the rule a separate list compares maps on connected components, open-and-closed parts, sections, and faithfulness or full faithfulness for étale covers. Marginal arrows cite graphs and Hom.

**U15 · Scan 79.** This typescript proves a local constancy and constructibility claim by noetherian induction. Near a maximal point it makes an étale replacement, restricts to a neighbourhood where the morphism is a sum of radicial morphisms, and concludes from a finite product of constant constructible sheaves.

**U16 · Scan 80.** Another list compares open-and-closed parts, connected components, a pullback functor and density. The lines following the implications restrict their scope, propose a submersiveness hypothesis, then return to finite base change. The little square bears the labels V and U?.

**U17 · Scan 81.** The handwritten side juxtaposes an inclusion, two equalities involving Λ, maps of squares and a base-change arrow. The star on h is raised and the star on p is lowered.
