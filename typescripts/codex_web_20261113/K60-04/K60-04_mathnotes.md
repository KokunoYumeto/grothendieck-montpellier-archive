# Mathematical notes on scans 36–47

## What the pages do

**36, opening.** A proof by contradiction continues from an earlier leaf. It asks whether the divisor of Z in B comes from D(A). The proposed ramification-index assumption leads to Δb/b = −XY and a calculation of Δ(−XY).

**36, exercises 24 and 25.** Exercise 24 characterizes logarithmic derivatives in characteristic 2 and asks for an invertible representative in a local factorial ring. Exercise 25 specifies a derivation of a two-variable formal series ring. The count of pairs defining N(i,j) gives the dimension of a space to be found inside C(A). Localization at a power of X leads to an example of a Dedekind ring with infinite ideal class group.

**37, proposed local factors.** The writer considers defining the factor on simple objects and extending by linearity over their composition factors. He objects that the resulting assignment at x fails to commute with the indicated étale extension. The added words place the residue-field condition at x.

**38, exercises 12–14.** The first two exercises ask for a proof and an extension of earlier results. The third identifies quotients of A[[T]] with algebras of cyclic groups. It then asks for the intersection of the principal ideals and for a projective-limit description of A[[T]].

**38, exercises 15–16.** Exercise 15 asks for a factorization of a polynomial over a complete valued field's valuation ring, with prescribed reductions and a specified coefficient. Exercise 16 starts a construction by restricting the residue in a discrete valuation ring to the prime subfield. Its list of units continues beyond the page.

**39 and 41, invariants.** A finite-index subgroup U acts unipotently. Scan 39 describes successive invariant spaces and a composition series stable under D. Scan 41 passes to virtual classes. In characteristic zero it uses the exact invariants functor and draws the compatibility triangle for V contained in U. The top note on 39 discusses an open subgroup invariant under D. Its hypotheses are among the less legible lines.

**41 and 43, the operation on classes.** The angular sign denotes the operation just constructed. Scan 43 describes the image of the representation group of D/U in that of D, discusses continuous representations and mentions extending the base field. A crossed-out paragraph explores the case D/I ≃ ℤ and a characteristic series. It is an abandoned continuation.

**40 and 42, exercise 8.** The numerical condition on i, j, k, the element v and the auxiliary series v′ are introduced at the foot of 42. The line at the top of 40 continues the coefficient recursion. Parts b–d ask for an obstruction to association in the larger series ring, a factorial ring whose series ring fails to be factorial, and a local example involving completion.

**40, exercises 9–11.** Exercise 9 relates local factoriality, projective divisorial ideals and formal series over a principal ring. Exercise 10 gives a Prüfer-ring assertion and a pseudo-bezoutien Krull-ring assertion. Exercise 11 takes the intersection of K[X,Y] with the subfield K(X²,Y/X).

**44 and 42, exercises 4–6.** Exercise 4 compares a rational conic ring with its scalar extension obtained by adjoining i. Exercise 5 gives a factoriality criterion involving a polynomial in powers of X₁, applies it to quadratic forms, and singles out the four-variable determinant-shaped example. Exercise 6 begins on 44 with graded prime ideals and a positive ternary form. Parts c–d on 42 compare factoriality for two inhomogeneous conics.

**42, exercise 7.** A root of Zᶜ−F is adjoined where F has weight q. The exercise gives two sets of hypotheses for factoriality, with separate suggestions for their proofs. It ends with the ring defined by Zᵃ−Xᵇ−Yᶜ.

**45 and the opening of 47, trace.** The writer computes the trace on the virtual class, first summing over composition factors and then using the projector for the finite group I/U. A boxed formula averages traces over lifts of φ. The preceding calculation retains a cancelled sum. The argument for independence of the choice of lift ends on 47 with a reference to the composition series.

**46, Dedekind modules.** A rank-induction proof ends with the inverse image of a free submodule and its direct-sum decomposition. A remark discusses reflexivity of the quotient. Section 10 identifies divisors and fractional ideals. Proposition 21 treats pseudo-null modules and the relation between pseudo-injective, pseudo-surjective and pseudo-bijective maps and their ordinary counterparts. Proposition 22 states the equivalence of torsion-freeness, reflexivity and projectivity for a finite-type module.

**47, geometric specialization.** The writer takes D and I to be decomposition and inertia groups at x. He identifies the quotient with πₓ and with the profinite integers, and gives E at x as a πₓ-module. The final display combines the term from a dense open U with a sum over x in Y. Two additions give Y = X−U and the map from Y. The last line specifies the closed-point map from Spec k(x).

## Slips

**S45-lifts — scan 45.** Immediately above the box the source gives g ∈ I/U mapping to φ. Inside the box the representatives belong to D/U. For a representative of an arbitrary element of D/I the source should be D/U. In the example recorded below, I/U maps only to the identity coset. The initial I and the D of the box are clear on their respective lines.

**Scan 39, inserted subject — low confidence.** The added phrase starts with E in “E opère […] sur les” factors. U is the apparent intended subject, since U acts on E earlier in the argument. The wording belongs to a revision and opère itself is doubtful.

**Scans 36, 38 and 46.** The blank after n° or exerc., and the additional blank after prop. on 36, are unfinished references. Their intended numbers cannot be obtained from these leaves.

## Remarks of the model

**M45-average — high confidence for the computed example.** I checked the boxed average with D cyclic of order four, I its subgroup of order two, U trivial, and a generator acting on two complex coordinates by −1 and i. For the nonidentity coset the traces on its two lifts are −1+i and −1−i. Their average is −1, the trace on the one-dimensional I-fixed space. For the identity coset the two traces are 2 and 0, with average 1. The computation used exact integer real and imaginary parts. The check concerns these two cosets only.

**Reading question — scan 43, cancelled lines. Low confidence.** The choice between donné and déterminé affects the phrasing of the relation between a class and its characteristic series. A full-height deletion crosses the relevant letters. I have not treated this crossed text as a theorem of the manuscript.

**Reading question — scan 36, exercise 25 and the hint in 25 a). Low confidence.** The opening names L as the fraction field of B. The hint then calls the series F with ΔF = F² “les éléments de L” and introduces L with a subscript q. The transcription has plain L at both places. The second L may denote a group from the preceding exercise. This leaf does not supply a distinct letterform or the earlier definition.
