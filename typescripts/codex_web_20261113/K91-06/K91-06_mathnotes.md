# Mathematical notes, cote 91, scans 73–85

## What the pages do

**Universal property and gluing, scans 73–74.** Given a map from \(W(T)\) to the decorated \(\mathfrak X\), the argument uses condition \((\beta)\) to cover \(T\) by \(T_i\). The resulting maps to the local \(X_i\) give maps to \(X\) which agree on the intersections. A common refinement of two covers gives uniqueness. The displayed Hom map on 73 is the map whose bijectivity is asserted.

**Lemme 3, scan 74.** A natural isomorphism between the underlying spaces denoted \(\lvert T\rvert\) and \(\lvert\underline W(T)\rvert\) gives \((\beta)\) for the open covers in the inserted phrase. The author specifies cases by Roman numerals.

**Lemme 4, scan 74.** The author singles out cases I′ and II* for condition \(\alpha\). The only geometric fact named in the brief justification for I′ is that a finite morphism is closed.

**Corollary and Théorème 1, scan 75.** The opening corollary concerns case I with \(S'\) proper over \(S\) and a local noetherian condition. Théorème 1 gathers the special Greenberg case and the case of a finite, locally free, radicial morphism. It states existence for the preschemes in question and calls the functor « de nature locale ».

**Théorème 1 bis (i), scan 75.** In case I′, existence for \(\mathfrak X\) gives existence for each open \(\mathcal U\), with an open immersion into the object for \(\mathfrak X\).

**Théorème 1 bis (ii), scans 75–76.** The cover of \(\mathfrak X\) must contain the specified finite parts of the fibres in individual members. Under this condition, the objects for the members, if they exist, cover the object for \(\mathfrak X\).

**Proof of the covering assertion, scan 76.** Starting with \(T'\) over \(T\), the author forms inverse images \(T'_i\) and uses the complement of the image of \(T'-T'_i\) to form \(T_i\). The fibre over \(t\) is used to see when \(t\) belongs to \(T_i\). A diagram records the base change. A separate attempt to decompose a fibre into components is cancelled.

**Corollaire 1, scans 76–77.** The finite-parts condition is imposed using affine opens. The resulting object is the union of the objects attached to these affines. A note at the left relates the numerical bound \(n(s)\) to the fibres of \(S'/S\), although several words in that definition are still uncertain.

**Corollaire 2 and its finite-family lemma, scan 77.** Under the finite-type and local noetherian hypotheses, the result has finite type over \(S\). The supporting lemma extracts a finite subfamily of opens while preserving containment of every subset of cardinal at most \(n\). The proof displays the covering of the \(n\)-fold fibre product by products of a single open with itself.

**Closed immersions, scan 78.** Section 4 opens with a proposition on the image of a closed immersion. The affine-open hypothesis in case I′ has been rewritten. The proof reduces to the affine case.

**Immersions, scans 78–79.** A corollary with quasi-projective target factors \(f\) into a closed immersion \(f'\) and an open immersion \(f''\). The sentence applying the preceding results to the two factors continues onto 79.

**Separatedness, scan 79.** Corollaire 2 treats a separated morphism by applying the proposition to the diagonal into the fibre product. The existence of the object for the target in case I′ appears as a bracketed proviso.

**Finite-type and affine morphisms, scans 79–80.** The proposition on 79 assumes a finite-parts condition for inverse images of affine opens, local noetherianity of the target, and finite type of \(f\). Its conclusion concerns finite type of the induced morphism. The one-line proposition on 80 refers back to this for affine morphisms.

**Constructibility, scans 81–82.** The cover « Schémas localement compacts » introduces a different topic. Scan 82 relates constructibility of \(Z\) to quasi-compactness and the existence of suitable neighbourhoods of its points. The sufficiency argument selects finitely many of the \(U_x\) and takes a finite union.

**Locally closed subsets and the example, scan 82.** A corollary specializes to locally closed \(Z\). It writes down \(U-Z\cap U\) and the condition on intersections of quasi-compact opens. The prescheme example adds a local description by finitely many equations on affine neighbourhoods.

**Series and endomorphisms, scan 83, through b).** The reused typewritten face describes the complete metrizable space \(V_{(L)}\) and the unique expansion in powers of \(T\), with coefficients zero sufficiently far to the left. Part b) gives an analogous expansion for endomorphisms.

**Exponentials, scan 83, c)–e).** In characteristic zero the series defining \(e^{Tu}\) is written explicitly to order two. A vector killed by \(u\) is fixed by this exponential. Part d) gives a tensor-product identity. The text of part e) breaks off after the hypothesis that \(V\) has an algebra structure.

**Irreducible closed subset, scan 84.** Beyond the drawing and boxed hypothesis, the sheet fixes a closed irreducible subset \(T\). It gives two equivalent conditions, one using a nonempty open part of \(T\) constructible in \(X\), the other a quasi-compact open \(U\) meeting \(T\) with \(U-U\cap T\) quasi-compact.

**Coefficients and polarisation, scan 85, upper part.** After a truncated sum, the typewritten exercise defines a two-index coefficient in \((xT_1+yT_2)^k\). The next line relates it, with factorials, to evaluation of \(f_k\) on a sequence of \(x\)'s and \(y\)'s.

**Adjoint identities, scan 85, b).** The text expands the \(n\)-th power of the adjoint operator in powers of \(L_x\) and \(R_x\). In characteristic \(p\) it asks for the displayed identities (1) and (2), then for identities involving \(f_{p-1}\) and \(g_{i,p-1-i}\). The prefatory definitions of the multilinear operations are outside the surviving fragment.

## Questions and conjectures

**Scan 74.** The parenthesis after « fini est fermé » may read « Les hyp. inutiles » or « Sans hyp. noeth. ». It concerns the hypotheses for the argument. Its wording is too faint to establish a conjecture.

**Scan 77.** The bottom note begins « Le foncteur … n'est pas local en tous les … . Cas où il n'existe pas … ». The last word may be « même ». It raises the issue of failure of existence, without supplying the example on this sheet.

**Scan 79.** « sous réserve que … existe dans le cas I′ » records an existence qualification. The slanted note about « Greenberg spécial » appears to concern how to present the cases, although its middle is particularly difficult to read.

## Remarks of the model

**R77 · model · high confidence.** The indices on 77 explain why the author uses one open for a whole finite subset. The product on the left repeats the same \(i\) at both ends of each term under the union. The underbrace identifies the number of factors.

**R82 · model · high confidence.** The typed material on 83 and 85 belongs to separate exercise fragments. The tears and reversed rows of type line up with the backs of 82 and 84. These features identify the paper reuse. The author of the typescript exercises is unnamed.

**R85 · model · high confidence for the stated finite check.** I checked (1) and (2) of scan 85 for every ordered pair of \(2\times2\) matrices over each of \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). Iterating \(y\mapsto xy-yx\) gave the printed right-hand sides for all 256 and 6,561 pairs, respectively. The check register records each identity and characteristic separately.


**R84 · model reading question · low confidence.** Scan 84, upper drawing. The frozen labels are \(U_1\) and \(U_2\), repeated under the intersection. On a further enlargement while writing these notes, the short stroke on the first main label rises and turns over like an \(f\). The second coincides with the oval boundary and could be a \(g\). The small copies below the ovals are less distinct. \(U_f,U_g\) is a possible reading of the pair.
