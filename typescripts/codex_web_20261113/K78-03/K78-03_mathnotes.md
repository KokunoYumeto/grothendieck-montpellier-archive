# Mathematical notes on cote 78, scans 32–46

## What the pages do

**Unimodularity and infinite polygons, scans 32–34.** The realization is called unimodular when its affine transformation \(u_E\) is unimodular. The Scholie puts finite-order \(u_E\) and pinned infinite polygons in the same discussion. For a fixed combinatorial polygon and parameter \(\alpha\), Grothendieck invokes rigidity of the category of regular realizations to speak of an intrinsic \(E(\Pi)\). The map \(S\to E(\Pi)\) and its compatibility with the group action remain to be described.

**Powers of a polygon structure, scans 34–35 (P6).** Formula (23) replaces each rotation \(u\in\omega\) by \(u^r\). Proposition 6 treats transitivity, composition of these operations and equality of two such structures. For finite \(n\), orientation identifies a generator, while forgetting orientation identifies its two signs. This yields the quotient \(\Psi_n=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^*/\{\pm1\}\) in the margin. The proof tests the map \(r\,\mathrm{id}\) on the cyclic group; it introduces \(\mathbb Z_\infty\) as well.

**Realized polygons, scans 35–37 (E26, A27).** With orientation, formula (25) acts directly on the chosen generator. Formula (26) changes the polygon structure on the same vertex set in the same affine space. The normalizer argument accompanying (27) identifies the two automorphism groups as subgroups of permutations of the vertices.

**Isomorphism after étoilage, scans 37–38 (Q37).** He asks whether \(P\) and \(\psi_r(P)\) can be isomorphic when \(r\ne1\) in \(\Psi_n\). In terms of the affine invariant the proposed test is \(\psi_r(\alpha)=\alpha\). He distinguishes the case \((n,p)=1\) from \(p\mid n\), and mentions the triangle and hexagon in characteristic 3 and the square in characteristic 2. Much of the exceptional-case wording is overwritten.

**Unrestricted exponents, scans 38–40 (D28).** The proposed extension to every integer \(r\) starts from an affine dihedral representation. Formula (28) lists relations on \(\sigma_E,u_E\). Replacing \(u_E\) by \(u_E^r\) leaves a question about the position of the vertices. The image can become collinear; the text asks for the geometric meaning of the resulting representation.

**Forgetting the generator, scans 40–42 (T41, N42).** Section VII defines a quasi-polygonal structure as an orbit under étoilage. What survives on the finite set \(S\) is a cyclic translation group \(Z\) for which \(S\) is a torsor. A polygon structure selects a generator up to sign; an orientation selects the generator itself. The notes describe the corresponding category using affine lines over \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\). The extension on scan 42 describes \(\mathrm{Aff}(S)\); choosing a vertex gives a splitting. The inverse image of \(\{\pm1\}\) gives \(\mathrm{Aff}^{\pm}(S)\).

**Regular systems of vertices, scans 42–44 (V43).** A pair \((S,Z)\) in the affine plane is regular when a compatible polygon structure gives a regular polygon. There is also an extension criterion for the permutations in \(\mathrm{Aff}^{\pm}(S,Z)\), and an orbit formulation. The paper then asks whether the underlying subset of the plane remembers \(Z\), and separates the dihedral subgroup from the full affine group of the finite torsor.

**Geometric classification, scans 45–46 (P7).** The data consist of a cyclic subgroup \(Z\subset\mathrm{Aff}(E)\) of order \(n\) and one of its orbits \(S\). The conditions concern noncollinearity and a normalizing affine transformation that inverts the rotations and fixes a point of the orbit. The wording at the foot of 45 is revised. Proposition 7 restates the correspondence and adds a possible unimodularity condition. The final remark distinguishes two automorphism groups; its question continues beyond the extract.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Q37, scans 37–38.** « Peut-on avoir isomorphie entre un n-polygône régulier P et ψᵣ(P) » for a nontrivial class of \(r\)? The text’s summary is « une nette tendance à changer la classe d’isomorphie ».
- **Q40, scans 38–40.** « sans restriction sur r » leads to « la question de la signification géom » and « à caractériser en termes de α, r ! ». The small addition asks « quand trois sommets … sont alignés ».
- **Q43, scan 43.** « On ne dit rien sur les autres automorphismes » and the slanted « Question à élucider » accompany the distinction between the two transformation groups.
- **Q44, scans 44–45.** « Z est-il déterminé de façon unique par la donnée de la seule partie S de E ? » The subsequent combinatorial alternatives ask what is determined by knowing \(\mathrm{Aff}^{\pm}(S)\) or by knowing \(\mathrm{Aff}(S)\). The full-group branch ends « Dans le cas de Aff(S)… ».
- **Q46, scan 46.** « On peut se poser la question, dans l’un ou l’autre cas, de » is the last live clause. Its object is not stated on the sheet.

## Slips

- **S34, scans 34–35, high confidence.** The necessity of « soit fini » in Prop 6(a) misses the infinite torsor with \(r=1\), and also \(r=-1\). The proof names \(\mathbb Z_\infty=\mathbb Z\). The corrected criterion **S34r** adds \(r=\pm1\) for that case.
- **E26, scan 36, high confidence.** The displayed left side is \(\psi_r(E)\). The preceding line calls the input \(P=(E,S,\omega)\), so the apparent intended argument is \(P\).
- **S38, scan 38, low confidence.** The first reading of the overwritten prime list is \(p=3,3\). The nearby « carré en car. 2 » favours \(p=2,3\). The first 3 is marked in the transcription.
- **D28, scan 39, high confidence.** The right side of the conjugation relation has \(u_E\). In the dihedral relation **D28r**, the right side is \(u_E^{-1}\). The superscript is visible on the preceding \(\sigma_E\) only.
- **S40, scan 40, low confidence.** In the example involving \(D_r\), the text gives \(u_r=\mathrm{id}\). The power substitution just discussed suggests \(u^r=\mathrm{id}\); the little r lies low in a heavily revised paragraph.
- **S43, scan 43, low confidence.** The phrase « structure quasi-régulière » occupies the place of the « structure quasi-polygonale » defined on 40–41. A change of adjective is visible. It is uncertain whether it was intentional.

## Remarks of the model

- **M27, high confidence, scans 34–37.** Formula (27) concerns permutations preserving the unordered pair of generators. Passing to a unit power gives the same subgroup of permutations. The full normalizer of the cyclic group belongs to the later construction on scans 41–42. The distinction follows from which data each permutation must preserve.
- **M43, high confidence, scan 43.** The list \(3,4,6\) matches exactly the integers \(n\ge3\) with \(\varphi(n)=2\). This checks the numerical list; the handwritten form of the phi remains doubtful.
- **M44, high confidence, scans 44–45.** For the purely combinatorial full-group question there is an eight-point counterexample. On \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\), the permutations \(x\mapsto x+1\) and \(x\mapsto5x+1\) generate distinct regular cyclic groups. Enumeration of all 40,320 permutations gives the same 32-element normalizer for both. Knowing that subgroup of permutations therefore does not determine the translations. The example addresses the combinatorial question; whether a particular planar realization imposes additional restrictions is a separate part of the manuscript’s question.
