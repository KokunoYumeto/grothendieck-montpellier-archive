# Mathematical notes · cote 11, scans 29–32 and 34–42

Line numbers refer to the comments in the transcription. The French manuscripts and the English typescripts follow two different arguments. The entries below keep the archive scan numbers. Quoted passages keep the language of the sheet.

## What the pages do

**U29a · 29 L1–12.** A functor takes values in “Algèbres bivariantes jaugées”. The page calls $k$ initial in the opposite category and identifies a coproduct there with the left tensor product of algebras. It describes the map on elementary tensors and chooses an augmentation on $A\otimes B$. A marginal formula spells out its sign.

**U29b · 29 L13–27.** The writer tries a formula for $(f\times g)_*$ on a tensor, then underlines “Mais c'est faux”. He records a discrepancy and recalculates the sign when the augmentation or the tensor of maps is taken in another convention. A boxed expression records his choice with the ordinary tensor of maps. A symbol inside that box and a cancelled word alongside it remain difficult to read.

**U30a · 30 L2–5, typed -2.17-.** A calculation of conjugation on the additive group ends a proof. Finite specialization gives the inclusions of that group and then of $B$ in $P(\lambda)$.

**U30b · 30 L6–19.** Proposition 2.7 asserts invariance of $\mu^L(x,\lambda)$ when the one-parameter subgroup is conjugated by an element of $P(\lambda)_k$. The proof introduces a limit $y$ and a second limit $\gamma'$, cites its centralizing property and rewrites the value of $\mu$. The page ends at the invocation of property (i).

**U31a · 31 L1–8.** The argument restricts to maps of even degree, writes the resulting formula for the ordinary tensor of pushforwards and considers symmetry on $A\otimes B$. The two sides of the comparison display exponents $xy$ and $(x+f)(y+g)$.

**U31b · 31 L9–17.** A projection square for $X\times S$ and $Y\times S$ comes with two exchange identities. A corresponding algebra square and the calculation beside it compare tensoring with $1$ and applying the functional of $R$. The second calculation has a question mark above the equality and ends “O.K.”

**U31c · 31 L18–27.** Two composable maps give a diamond of graphs and another of tensor products. The sheet writes an exchange formula for the two routes and mentions its symmetric counterpart. A marginal question asks what class of squares allows this calculation.

**U32a · 32 L2–12, typed -2.13-.** A factorization of $\phi$ by two $R$-valued points and a nontrivial one-parameter subgroup produces a $K$-valued point that has no $R$-valued extension in the source, although its image has one. The page applies this to the composite action morphism and concludes that the induced action is not proper.

**U32b · 32 L13–23.** Corollary 2.5 asserts properness of the action on the specified stable locus. After replacing $X$ by that locus, the proof fixes a one-parameter subgroup, introduces its properly stable open set and uses an invariant affine neighbourhood with zero-dimensional stabilizers. The last sentence continues beyond the sheet.

**U34a · 34 L1–13.** The objects are algebras over $k$ with a form. A map from $A$ to $B$ consists of the algebra map $f^*$ in the opposite direction and the additive map $f_*$ in the forward direction. The page specifies two projection formulas and $k_B f_*=k_A$. In the graded anticommutative case the text inserts the even-degree condition. The margin distinguishes invertibility of the pair from the further condition $f_*(1)=1$.

**U34b · 34 L14–27.** The form on a product yields a map $\varphi_A$ to the dual, with the argument order written in a separate insertion. Assuming injectivity for $\varphi_B$ and a condition on the transpose of $f^*$, the writer constructs $f_*$. The identity labelled $(***)$ characterizes it. An insertion sets $y=1$, and the last lines explain how $f^*$ determines $f_*$ under the stated hypotheses.

**U35a · 35 L2–7, typed -3.11-.** An invariant power of a section $D$ is expressed as the pullback of a section $E$ of a tensor product of the $M$’s. The text invokes “SGA 8, §1” for existence of $E$.

**U35b · 35 L8–21.** Section 2 seeks an open set of $Z$ on which the displayed tensor product is ample. Definition 3.7/Proposition 3.4 defines the stable configurations by a strict inequality for the fraction of points lying in each proper linear subspace. The proof begins to describe the complement as a finite union indexed by pairs consisting of an index set and an integer $n_0$. It stops in the middle of that description.

**U36a · 36 L1–12.** For forms concentrated in degrees $d=d(A)$ and $d'=d(B)$, the text compares the degrees in which their pairings can be nonzero. It splits $f_*(x)$ into graded pieces and obtains $i'=i+(d'-d)$. The corrected inequality in the component argument contains $d'-j$.

**U36b · 36 L13–24.** The proposed category imposes degree $d(B)-d(A)$ on $f_*$ and an upper-degree factorization on $k_A$. The author considers the full subcategory of even formal degree, and sometimes the interval $[0,d(A)]$. He gives anticipated universal constructions as a reason to work beyond Poincaré algebras.

**U37a · 37 L2–18, typed -3.9-.** After the end of one implication and a reference for another, the proof shows $2)\Rightarrow3)$. It chooses coordinate points and uses nonvanishing determinants to form the sets $S_k'$. A partition of the coordinates of the kind excluded in the text would put every point in the union of two linear subspaces. The argument ends by obtaining an $R$-partition.

**U37b · 37 L19–25.** The text identifies the regular locus with the pre-stable points and begins constructing a geometric quotient. The charts include coordinate permutations of the original charts. The page closes with invariance of the pairwise intersections.

**U38a · 38 L1–9.** The left graded tensor product acts on the category in question. The author explains why the bivariant category generally lacks the diagonal map needed for a categorical product, then asserts the product property when both algebras are Poincaré algebras.

**U38b · 38 L10–23.** A simplicial bivariant algebra is described as a contravariant functor on nonempty finite sets. The sheet calls it “spécial” when the maps for a disjoint union give the displayed tensor isomorphism. It then asserts an equivalence under a Poincaré condition on $A_0$. The small expression identifying the input for $A_0$ has competing readings.

**U39a · 39 L2–11, typed -3.8-.** Definition 3.6 takes the union of the chart opens and their images under factor permutations. Proposition 3.3 gives three conditions on a configuration: the stabilizer has dimension zero, the points cannot all be in the union of two disjoint proper linear subspaces, and the point belongs to the regular open set.

**U39b · 39 L12–20.** Over an algebraically closed field, the proof places the two subspaces in coordinate subspaces and exhibits a block-diagonal subgroup. The displayed matrix partitions coordinates into blocks of sizes $r+1$ and $n-r$. The corresponding sentence continues on scan 37.

**U40a · 40 L1–13.** A proposition about subspaces $A_n$ in the cohomology of powers of $X$ lists conditions involving evaluation, products, units, and graph classes. A second description uses subalgebras and inverse and direct images for projections and injections. The paragraph draws consequences for arbitrary simplicial maps. Several arrow shapes and one endpoint in clause (i)c are uncertain.

**U40b · 40 L14–18.** The third description views elements as maps between cohomology groups. It asks for closure under composition and a separately drawn operation, for identities, and for the direct and inverse images of simplicial maps. The operation is a densely inked square; the transcription records its possible readings.

**U41a · 41 L2–9, typed -3.13-.** Proposition 3.6 characterizes stable geometric points by the existence of nonvanishing monomials $P_i$ in the expressions $D$. For each $i$, the exponent on the $i$th factor is $N-N_0$ and the exponents on the other factors are $N$, where $N\geq N_0>0$.

**U41b · 41 L10–20.** The proof assigns a zero-one exponent vector to each $D$ and assigns the sum of the vectors to a monomial. It defines a convex cone from the vectors of determinants that do not vanish at the given configuration. The sheet ends at “Then it is easy to see that:”, before the next assertion.

**U42a · 42 L1–12.** The sheet recapitulates graded bivariant algebras, the gauged version, their tensor product, and the simplicial version. It gives $k$ in degree zero as a particular object. The special case includes both an isomorphism for a disjoint union and a compatibility condition for the lower-star maps. The order of two indices in that condition is overwritten.

**U42b · 42 L12–17.** A Poincaré hypothesis on $A_0$ leads to an asserted equivalence. The writer then describes a fully faithful functor into algebras equipped with $\Delta$ and leaves its axioms open. In the slanted note he sets $\Delta=\delta_*(1)$.

**U42c · 42 L18–25.** The example starts with a theory on nonsingular complete varieties and invokes Künneth and Poincaré. Applying the functor to $I\mapsto X^I$ yields a canonical gauged simplicial algebra. The wording immediately before $H^*(X)$ at the end is uncertain.

**U42d · 42 L26–27.** A footnote considers when the two parts of an isomorphism are inverse. Its example takes $f^*$ to be the identity, multiplies by a unit for $f_*$, and changes the form by the inverse multiplier. The letter naming the unit is uncertain.

## Remarks of the model

- **R29.31 · Model, high confidence about the connection.** The final margin of 29 singles out an even degree. The very first sentence of 31 restricts the discussion to even-degree maps and writes the ordinary tensor sign again. The uncertain symbol in the box on 29 is unnecessary for recognizing that change of hypothesis.
- **R34.42 · Model, high confidence about the connection.** The left margin of 34 singles out the normalization $f_*(1)=1$. The footnote on 42 repeats precisely that condition in its discussion of when the two components of an isomorphism are inverses. Its multiplier illustrates the distinction, even though the handwritten name of that multiplier remains uncertain.
- **R35 · Model, high confidence about the arithmetic.** In the inequality on 35, take $m=3$, $n=1$ and let the proper linear subspace be a point containing two of the four entries. The two fractions both equal $1/2$. This pair fails the strict condition and meets the weak inequality indexing the complement. This checks the equality boundary in one instance.
- **R39.37 · Model, high confidence about the join.** The typescript page -3.8- on scan 39 ends with “Then the subgroup of transformations:” and its matrix. Page -3.9- on scan 37 opens “leaves x fixed. Secondly,” before the next implication. The page numbers and the grammar together identify the continuation.
