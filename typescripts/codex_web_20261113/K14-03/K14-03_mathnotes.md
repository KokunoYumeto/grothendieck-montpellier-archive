# Mathematical notes on cote 14, scans 31–42 and 44

## What the pages do

**Theorem 2.1, scans 31–32.** From a cycle class $Z$ on $X\times Y$, the author obtains a map on zero-cycle classes and then a homomorphism $u_Z$ on the degree-zero part $A_Y(k)$. He asserts dependence on the class of $Z$ under the equivalence indicated in (i). Part (ii) gives an isogeny $\varphi:A'\to A_Y$ and a cycle $Z'$ whose associated homomorphism agrees with $u_Z\circ\varphi(k)$. A cancelled formulation above (i) already involves an isogeny and a new cycle.

**Construction and proof, scans 32–34.** Grothendieck first supposes that a correspondence $U$ gives $S_Y(U(a))=a$, and composes it with $Z$. He then replaces this normalization by $S_Y(U(a))=na$. The calculation on 33 uses divisibility and concludes “On prend comme isogénie la multiplication par $n$.” For (i), scan 34 invokes theorem 1.6 for algebraic equivalence, obtains $mu=0$ in the case of $\tau$-equivalence, and uses divisibility of $A_Y(k)$.

**Three corollaries, scan 34.** The first characterizes algebraic equivalence to zero by Albanese equivalence to a value $Z(a_0)$ in a family on $A\times X$ that gives a group homomorphism. Its number is overwritten. Corollary 2.3 states divisibility of $P^i(X)$ by every positive integer. Corollary 2.4 gives $ZZ'=0$ when one factor is algebraically equivalent to zero and the other is $\tau$-equivalent to zero.

**Definition 2.5 and Proposition 2.6, scan 35.** A map from $Y(k)$ to the group of cycle classes on $X$ is called algebraic under two conditions. The associated map on zero-cycles must factor through the indicated Albanese group. Some positive integer multiple of the induced homomorphism must come from an algebraic cycle. The left-margin diagram shows multiplication by $N$ between the components labelled $1$ and $N$. Proposition 2.6 applies theorem 2.1 to a map already defined by a cycle.

**Functoriality and algebraic homomorphisms, scans 35–36.** Proposition 2.7 lists the subgroup property, a condition on multiples, functoriality in $Y$, and functoriality in $X$ for an integral correspondence. After fixing $y_0$, the note on 36 separates a value in the cycle group from a homomorphism vanishing at the origin. It calls a homomorphism $P^j(X')\to P^i(X)$ algebraic when a positive multiple is defined by an algebraic cycle. It qualifies the use of rational coefficients and ends “Ces hom se composent”.

**Duality and transposition, scan 37.** For a perfectly paired $A,B$ and indices with $i+j=n+1$, the page relates a map $A\to P^i(X)$ to a map into $B$ obtained from the transpose of a cycle. The author explicitly asks for independence from the representing cycle. He then poses a more general transposition question for maps between two cycle groups.

**Theorem 3.1 and the reduction to a curve, scans 38–39.** The cycle defines $u_Z$ and a cohomology map $a({}^{t}Z)$ with target $H^1(A)$. The proposed implication relates their vanishing after passage to rational $\ell$-adic coefficients. The author asks about the converse. He chooses a “courbe ample”, restricts to $Z'$, and writes the three terms of the decomposition for $X\times C$. He says the two outer factors contribute zero maps and reduces the question to the middle factor, whose projection and restriction he discusses. The sheet ends at this reduction.

**Tate's argument, scans 40–42.** The title names decomposable cycles. The notes assume the degree-two Tate statement and give conditions on the action on the module $\mathbb T_K(A)$. These involve Frobenius eigenvalues, or a decomposition into $V_1\times V_2$ with distinct eigenvalues for almost all Frobenius operators. They discuss a power of an elliptic curve definable over a finite field and invoke Mumford for (a). The display on 42 equates the classes labelled decomposable, the algebraic classes, and an invariant part. The explanation uses two inclusions and equality of the extremes. The alphabet of the final exponent is uncertain.

**Letter to Serre, scan 44.** On 27 August 1965 the writer asks for a purely geometric proof. He defines $C^i(X)$ by algebraic equivalence and rational coefficients, and $\xi$ by the hyperplane section. Conjecture A says multiplication by $\xi^{n-2i}$ induces $C^i(X)\simeq C^{n-i}(X)$ for $2i\leq n$. He proposes a reduction, names the first open case, and discusses the relation with the cohomological Lefschetz statements. The page breaks in the sentence about $H^i(Y)\to H^{i+2}(X)$.

- **Scans 32–33.** “on ne sait pas si on peut trouver $U$ comme dessus” qualifies the proposed degree-one normalization.
- **Scan 37.** “Une question plus subtile est s'ils peuvent s'inverser” asks about inverses. “Question de transposition se pose, plus généralement” opens a separate question.
- **Scan 38.** “A-t-on une réciproque” is a marginal question. The large question mark above the double shaft reinforces the uncertainty over the direction.

This accounts for the appearance of $P^1(A)$ in that line. The dark stroke before the live exponent remains a low-confidence reading. The arithmetic does not identify it.

This affects the description of the surjectivity assertion. The page file retains its hashed reading.
