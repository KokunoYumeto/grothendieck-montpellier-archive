# K161-1-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 004–005, unit and counit.** For $u:E\rightleftarrows F:v$, the writer expresses the map on Hom sets in terms of $i(Y):Y\to vu(Y)$, and the map in the other direction in terms of $j(P):uv(P)\to P$. Conditions (1) and (2) concern full faithfulness on a full subcategory together with closure of its essential image. Part c pairs the subcategories by taking essential images. Part d names the largest ones $E_0$ and $F_0$.

**U2 · Scans 005–006, factorisations.** The argument asks when $v$ has image in $E_0$. The bracketed calculation expresses an adjoint of $F_0\subset F$ using $u_0$ and $v$ restricted to its image. The dual calculation for $E_0\subset E$ continues on 006. Section f places the image, unit/counit and factorisation conditions side by side, then reconstructs $u,v$ from two subcategories with adjoints and an equivalence between them.

**U3 · Scan 007, point categories.** With $E$ a point, an initial object of $F$ appears in the description of the adjoint. The second example reverses the roles and uses a final object of $E$.

**U4 · Scans 007–008, representability question.** The writer factors $u=\beta\alpha'$ through $E_1$ and asks if $\beta$ has a right adjoint. Adjoining a single object $a$ gives two functors and a bifunctorial map. Taking the outgoing Hom sets from $a$ empty leaves a question about representability of the other functor. The continuation relates the essential image of $\alpha$ to objects whose contravariant Hom takes arrows of $\Sigma$ to bijections.

**U5 · Scans 010–011, tensor products of finite families.** An object of $\Phi(A)$ is a finite indexed family. A morphism is given by a bijection and component isomorphisms. Disjoint union combines families. Sorting them by cardinality gives the pieces $\Phi_n(A)$. The restriction proposition, its corollary and the following definition introduce the tensor product of a family. An NB describes what happens when the family members are themselves indexed families.

**U6 · Scans 011 and 013, symmetry and inverses.** Two possible orders on a two-element set give $L_aL_b$ and $L_bL_a$. When the factors coincide the writer considers the automorphism exchanging them. For an invertible $L$, he records an element $\varepsilon(L)$ of $\operatorname{Aut}(1_C)$. He next defines the datum of an inverse $L'$, compares two maps $L'\otimes L\to1$ and draws the two contractions of $L\otimes L'\otimes L$.

**U7 · Scan 012, contraction.** An object of $\Phi^c(C)$ adds disjoint two-element parts of the index set, each equipped with a pairing to $1_C$. In brackets the author asks of a pairing that it be perfect. Forgetting the contracted indices gives one of two functors. The bottom arrow compares their tensor products by a contraction.

**U8 · Scan 014, theories and constructions.** Two squares compare Hom categories and 2-categories of theories, including variants with index $I$ and subscript zero. A construction for $T$ is described as a morphism into the theory $i$ of a base set without structure. The author then writes the category of constructions, its evaluation on $T$, and evaluation at an object $\xi\in T(C)$. A long diagonal stroke runs through several parts of this sheet.

**U9 · Scan 016, testing exactness.** The first question extends a right-exact functor by a Hom construction and asks whether it still preserves the specified finite operations. A lifting triangle translates the proposed condition into an injectivity requirement. A nonempty constant set furnishes the writer’s obstruction. The second question starts with a left-exact functor into $E$, extends it by colimits, and asks for left exactness. A topos and the role of a strictly generating functor enter here.

**U10 · Scan 017, a separate pencil calculation.** A field diagram, filtered crystals, a fibre functor and a representation of $\pi$ appear on a sheet written in the opposite orientation. A grading is expressed as $j_\Omega$ with a conjugation identity. Below it a graded isomorphism involves a term labelled DR, a Tate object and weight indices. Several letters and exponents in this last formula remain uncertain.

**U11 · Scans 018–019, extension and restriction.** For a full subcategory $C\subset E$, $\alpha(\varphi)(X)$ is defined by a colimit over $C/X$, while $\beta(\psi)$ is restriction to $C$. The author writes the composites, their comparison maps and the Hom adjunction. Condition (*) is necessary for the extension to preserve colimits. The separate dark-ink entries on 019 are a struck colimit and the diagram $X_i\to X\leftarrow Y$.

**U12 · Scan 020, the double diagram and the lemma.** The restriction functor on the primed Hom categories is asserted to be fully faithful. The question of essential surjectivity leads to iterated colimits over the partial systems indexed by $i$ and $j$. The author reduces the required comparison to a lemma about $Y_i$ in $C$ with limit $Y$ in $E$, and asks whether the map into $C/Y$ is cofinal.

## Remarks of the model

**R16 · Model, high confidence, scan 016.** The displayed obstruction using $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ens}}(x,\varnothing)$ works for the stipulated nonempty $x$. If the value of the target functor at the initial object is empty, that component alone rules out a natural transformation from the nonempty constant functor. This checks the specific obstruction on the sheet.

**R20 · Model, low confidence as a diagnosis of the lemma, scans 018 and 020.** The arrow assigned to $\beta$ on 018 restricts to objects of $C$. That codomain alone raises a problem for evaluation at an arbitrary colimit in $E$. This accounts for the proposed $\alpha$ in L20b without settling the doubtful letter in the lemma’s hypothesis.

**R13 · Model, low confidence, scan 013.** The exponent written sign raises a convention question. If it takes the ordinary sign values $+1,-1$, the two powers of an element of order two coincide. A parity exponent would distinguish them. The page leaves the meaning of sign unstated.
