# Mathematical notes · Montpellier, cote 57

Scan numbers refer to the fifteen supplied images. Quotation marks identify wording on the sheets. A cancellation is identified wherever it affects the status of a statement.

## What the pages do

**Scan 89.** A section of \(\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\omega_{X/S}\) gives a class on \(S\) with coefficients in the displayed quotient of \(\underline\omega_{X/S}\). For a section represented by an invertible sheaf \(\underline L\) on \(X\), the author describes its image through a kernel involving \(H^1(X,\Omega^1_{X/S})\) and a higher direct image. The short connecting stroke and the last differential-sheaf symbol are uncertain.

**Scans 95 and 90, in the English text's own order.** Manuscript page 56 considers sequences approaching a boundary point. The quotients indexed by \(i=1,2\) supply two limiting coordinates in a product of closed unit discs. The author concludes that the fibre of the monoidal transform has dimension one greater than the corresponding fibre of \(M(\Gamma)\to S(\Gamma)\) in the case under discussion. After a qualification about fixed points, the text turns to levels 1 and 2. Page 57 specializes to \(g=2\), gives the series expansion at \(t_o\), and describes even theta functions of order \(4k\). At order four the map embeds the quotient of \(T_1(t)\) by its involution as a plane conic. The discussion then returns to local monoidal transforms. Its last sentence breaks off after “different”.

**Scans 91–92.** A homomorphism \(u:B\to\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}_{X/S}\) from an abelian scheme is related to a \(B^*\)-torsor \(A\) and a morphism \(X\to A\). The three-row diagram compares the extensions for \(X\), \(A\) and \(B\). The author argues that translations act trivially and uses descent to express the extension as an invariant of \(B\). He starts computing its class by asking for a relative connection on an invertible sheaf on \(A\times_S B\). The adjective identifying the sheaf is doubtful.

**Scans 93–94.** Scan 93 is a cancelled salutation. On 94 the decomposition of \(\underline H^{11}(A\times_S B)\) has two factor terms and two mixed tensor terms. The author uses the two inclusions to account for vanishing on the first two factors. A separate, cancelled calculation asks whether two lifts of a line bundle with connection correspond. An isolated \(W\) with subscript and \(S_0\leftarrow S\) stand below the cancellation.

**Scans 96–97.** The cover introduces a table about restriction from a projective \(X\) to a nonsingular hyperplane section \(Y\). The rows distinguish \(\mathrm{Pic}\), \(\mathrm{Pic}^\tau\), \(\mathrm{Pic}^0\), \(TN\), \(LN\), \(N\) and restriction to \(X_m\) for large \(m\). The thresholds vary with the row and with the generality of \(Y\). A margin compares the ranks \(\rho(X)\) and \(\rho(Y)\). Its dimension-two clause is struck.

**Scan 99.** For normal projective \(X/k\) of dimension at least two, the theorem asserts that \(H^1(X,G)\to H^1(Y_{\overline K},G)\) is injective for the generic hyperplane section. The additional adjective on \(X\) and the precise expression following “sans” in the hypothesis on the affine algebraic group \(G\) remain uncertain. The sheet reduces the problem to additive, finite separable, \(\alpha_p\) and \(\mu_p\) cases, separates a cancelled Picard corollary from the live fundamental-group corollary, and brings in Frobenius.

**Scans 100–101.** The argument relates two Frobenius sequences to \(df\) and \(df/f\), then reduces to injectivity of restriction on global differential forms. The Picard corollary describes a finite unipotent \(p\)-group as kernel. A marginal annotation says “purement infinitésimal”. Normal hyperplane sections are used to pass to the properness question for a normal curve. A second argument for properness of \(\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^0_{X/k}\) considers maps from \(\operatorname{Spec}(k[t,t^{-1}])\). Its lemma asserts surjectivity of \(\mathrm{Pic}(X)\to\mathrm{Pic}\,X[t,t^{-1}]\). The proof takes the closure \(D_1=\overline D_0\) of a divisor and invokes flatness.

**Scans 102–103.** With \(U\) a retrocompact open and \(f:X'\to X\) faithfully flat and quasi-compact, the lemma compares restriction and pullback. Its boxed assumption identifies the structure sheaf with the pushforward from the open. The proof uses full faithfulness for injectivity and starts the surjectivity argument with \(\underline E=i_*\underline F\). It identifies the pullback with \(\underline E'\). The resulting squares of isomorphism classes of finite-rank locally free sheaves and of Picard groups are called “cartésien”. Scan 103 states a consequence for the two horizontal Picard maps.

**Scan 104.** A new cover names Seshadri and the corresponding problems. These two lines supply the title only.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High.** The English sheets join in the order 95, 90. The handwritten 56 and 57 agree with the exact sentence join “we shall / limit ourselves to the case \(g=2\).” This explains why the closing conclusion on 95 and the local analysis on 90 concern different stages of the argument.
- **M2 · High.** On 101 the heading uses the underlined Picard object with a superscript zero, while the lemma immediately below writes \(\mathrm{Pic}(X)\) and a Laurent-variable target. The change of notation accompanies the passage from a properness assertion to a statement about line bundles.
- **M3 · High for the conditional set-theoretic implication.** Given the cartesian Picard square on 102 (**K102-Pic-square**), the upper horizontal map inherits each of injectivity, surjectivity and bijectivity from the lower horizontal map. This gives a reason for the possible abbreviation “surj” on 103. Confidence in that reading remains low because its terminal stroke also permits other readings.
- **M4 · High for the literal difference, low for an emendation.** The insertion on scan 100, line 33, says that the underlined Picard object over \(k\) is proper and gives it no superscript. The continuation at the head of 101 specifies \(\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}^\tau_{X/k}\). Restoring a superscript to the insertion would go beyond its visible strokes.
- **R1 · Reading question, scan 89, LaTeX line 3.** Frozen reading \(\operatorname{Im}f_*\mathcal O_X^*))\); possible alternative \(\operatorname{Im}f_*(\mathcal O_X^*))\). On the enlarged denominator, the curved stroke just left of the small loop can belong to the script letter or to an opening delimiter. The two closing strokes are clearer. This question arose during the notes.
