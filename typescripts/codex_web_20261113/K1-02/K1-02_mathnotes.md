# Mathematical notes on scans 65–78

The scan numbers below refer to the images. Formula numbers belong to the manuscript and sometimes restart. Unit labels U01–U14 and identifiers beginning S or M refer to the check-event register. Each unit describes the manuscript. A separate event records an actual check, a use as background or a counterexample.

## What the pages do

**U01 · Composition and adjoints, scan 65.** Formula (10) composes two induced exterior-power maps. The two lines of (11) pass an adjoint through a mixed exterior product and through a pure exterior power. A crossed-out polarization route survives above the calculation. In the live calculation, the determinant of the pairings $(u a_i,b_j)$ is the determinant of $(a_i,u^*b_j)$.

**U02 · Positivity and positive powers, scans 65–66.** The author writes $h=k^2$ and takes the exterior power of both sides. He then treats positive integral and reciprocal-integral exponents, passes to rational exponents, and appeals to continuity in the exponent for (12). Proposition 1 collects boundedness of the mixed product and the multiplicative and adjoint properties of the pure power. The statement breaks off after “opérateurs positifs en”. Its verbal continuation is at the head of scan 77.

**U03 · Eigenvalues of an exterior power, scans 67–69.** Proposition 2 on scan 67 lists the products $\lambda_{i_1}\cdots\lambda_{i_n}$ for strictly increasing indices. In the finite-rank case the author splits off a space $E_1$ on which $u$ vanishes. In the exterior-power decomposition all terms containing a factor from $E_1$ vanish. The remaining term is computed on the finite-dimensional $E_0$ using a triangular matrix. Scan 69 appeals to approximation for a general compact operator. The struck sideways note on 67 also discusses the order and multiplicity of the eigenvalues.

**U04 · Two different products, scan 69.** In the side-written corollary, (21) expresses the leading eigenvalue of an exterior power as a product of eigenvalues, while (22) expresses its norm as a product of singular values. In the main text, (23) bounds the absolute value of the first product by the second. The lower diagonal lines continue a computation begun sideways beside (22).

**U05 · Composition of compact maps, scans 69–70.** The statement beginning at the foot of 69 becomes the product inequality (24) at the top of 70. The short live argument applies the operator norm bound for a product to the exterior powers. A much longer crossed calculation in terms of $(uv)(uv)^*$ remains legible in parts.

**U06 · Trace coefficients and determinant, scan 71.** The new outline begins with a factorial bound on the trace norm of a mixed exterior product. It identifies $\alpha_n$ with a trace and compares $|\alpha_n(u)|$, $\alpha_n(|u|)$ and the trace norm of the exterior power. Several numbered corollaries are cancelled and their numbers reused. The determinant statement under the live Corollaire 4 is discussed below (S71).

**U07 · Genus zero, scan 71.** Théorème 1 states summability of the eigenvalues and a product formula for $\det(1+zu)$. The following lines express the coefficients as elementary symmetric sums in the eigenvalues, give the trace as their sum, and give the corresponding positive expression in the singular values. A reduction to the positive Hermitian case is announced.

**U08 · Mixed products and determinants of a sum, scans 71–72.** Prop 2 estimates the product of a $p$th and a $q$th exterior power with factor $p!q!/(p+q)!$. Scan 72 expands the two operators as sums of rank-one tensors. Sorting the two families of indices produces the factorials. The next two corollaries compare the determinants for $u+v,u,v$ and then their individual coefficients. The expansion of $\Lambda^n(u+v)$ supplies the binomial coefficients.

**U09 · Partial products and a supremum, scans 72–73.** Théorème 2 gives a bounded-length analogue of the determinant inequality. The notation $\sigma_n^m$ in (17) sums products of $m$ of the first $n$ singular values. Scan 73 relates it to a supremum over contractions of rank at most $n$. A finite-rank lemma gives the upper bound. The author pairs the two singular-vector systems to attain it, then applies the coefficient inequality (14) to the compressed sum to reach (16).

**U10 · A determinant supremum, scan 74.** The author asks how strong the simultaneous partial-product inequalities are and suggests a direct route from (12) to (15). Proposition 4, labelled (18), sets the product of the factors $1+\rho_i(u)$ equal to a supremum involving $\det(1+v_nu)$. The same norm and rank restrictions on $v_n$ appear as on 73.

**U11 · Logarithms and decreasing weights, scan 75.** Taking logarithms of the partial products gives an additive inequality. The page introduces an integral transform $f_\varphi$ and a sum $S_\varphi^n$ over singular values. The faint parenthesis uses integration by parts to pass from a comparison of two functions to a comparison involving their derivatives against a decreasing weight. A step-shaped weight is drawn beside the converse observation.

**U12 · Power sums and the question on functions, scans 75–76.** Substituting $\varphi(r)=r^{-p}$ with $0<p<1$ gives a constant times $\rho^p$. Scan 76 boxes a subadditivity bound first for a finite initial sum and then for the untruncated sum. A separate “Remarque” compares another pair of sums and invokes Weyl. The “Question” asks which functions of one positive variable give the indicated subadditivity for every $n$.

**U13 · Mixed products of Fredholm maps, scan 77.** Proposition 3 bounds the trace norm of $u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_n$ by $1/n!$ times the product of the individual trace norms. For rank-one $u_i=\bar b_i\otimes a_i$, the mixed product is written as a single tensor of two wedge products divided by $n!$. The passage to general operators uses continuity.

**U14 · Trace and Hilbert–Schmidt norms, scans 77–78.** Corollaire 1 gives the mixed and pure trace identities (14). Scan 78 adds the verification note and derives (15). Corollaire 3 expresses the trace norm using $\alpha_n(|u|)$ and the square of the Hilbert–Schmidt norm using $\alpha_n(u^*u)$. The proof uses the adjoint-product identity and writes the positive square root as a power $1/2$.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 68.** After “matrice triangulaire” there is a small hook followed by a contracted word. Whether this is an authorial question is unresolved.
- **Scans 71–72.** A separate “?” stands next to the cancelled pair of norm identities on 71. The side note “notation ? à réviser” on 72 concerns the $\sigma_n^m$ notation.
- **Scan 74.** The inequalities “semblent les inégalités les plus fortes possibles” concerning the singular values of $u+v$ given those of $u$ and $v$. The formulation of optimality is left open.
- **Scan 76.** “Trouver la forme générale des fonctions $f$ d'une variable positive” for which the displayed inequality holds “quel que soit $n$”. At the end of the preceding remark, “on trouve rien de spécial” may also carry a question mark. The last line asks for a “Généralisation à des fonctions de $n$ arguments.”

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark M1, high confidence.** The sentence ending on scan 66 resumes at the first words of 77. “et opérateurs positifs en” is followed there by “/ op. hermitiens positifs, enfin satisfait à la formule (12).” This local join explains why the first line of 77 reads differently from the proposition immediately below it.
- **Model remark M2, medium confidence.** The two appearances of the factorial estimate, on 71 and 77, anchor two numbering sequences. The trace identities are (2)–(3) on 71 and (14) on 77. A cross-reference can sometimes identify a different stage of the same writing. The pages establish the correspondence of the formulas more firmly than the chronology.
- **Model remark M3, low confidence.** In the definition after “Question” on 76, the independent reading of the summand is $\rho_i(u)$ or $f_i(u)$. The final text also records $f(\rho_i(u))$. The question about varying $f$ favours the last expression. The ink has too few distinguishable strokes to establish it.
- **Model remark M4, low confidence.** The crossed definition $w=vu$ near the top of 70 and the surviving $uv$ in (24) run in different orders. Formula (10) on 65 makes the order of composition explicit, but the earlier definition on 70 is struck. These pages do not settle whether the author meant to reverse the maps in the statement or change the product notation.
