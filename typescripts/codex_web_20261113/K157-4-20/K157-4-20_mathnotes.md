# K157-4-20 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**M01 · Weak fibrations, 303.** A continuation questions the relation between loc 6 a and loc 6. Section 4 then recalls a test for a weak \(W\)-fibration \(X\to S\). For an arrow \(S''\to S'\) over \(S\) between contractible categories, the induced comparison of the two pullbacks is to belong to \(W\). A second test uses a homotopism as the base arrow. The manuscript names the resulting class of weak fibrations \(\mathrm{Fib}f_W\).

**M02 · Classes of arrows, 304–305.** An inclusion diagram puts perfect morphisms, \(W\)-fibrations, weak \(W\)-fibrations and two classes of Cat-fibrations on one sheet. The text describes \(\mathrm{Fib}_W\) using the base-change functor on slice categories. It then discusses factorization, base change, composition and direct factors separately. The margin on 305 qualifies the scope of the factorization observation.

**M03 · A reference localizer, 305–307.** Section 4.2 introduces \(W_0\subset\mathrm{Fl}(\mathrm{Cat})\) and assumes \(W\supset W_0\). Proposed choices include the least localizer discussed earlier, the ordinary weak equivalences (written with a doubled W), and \(W\) itself. Diagram (4.2.1.) displays the classes attached to both localizers. The following page compares different hypotheses of the form \(\mathcal F\subset\mathrm{Fib}_W\).

**M04 · Reformulating Loc(7), 308.** The former formulation named on this leaf uses \(\mathrm{Fib}f_W\subset\mathrm{Fib}_W\). Grothendieck proposes to replace the class on the left by its sub-class \(\mathcal F_W\), written using Catfib with superscript \(W\). He announces that this change gives the same result.

**M05 · Categories over a base, 309–310.** The construction of \(\mathrm{HOT}_W(S)\) passes through a category of fractions of \(\mathrm{Cat}(S)\). The manuscript identifies \(\mathrm{Cat}(S)\) with split fibred categories and splitting-preserving morphisms. It recalls a comparison with fibred categories and cartesian functors after localization, explaining the use of fibrewise equivalences on the latter category. The canonical functor before localization is described as faithful and less than fully faithful.

**M06 · Local constancy, 310–311.** Membership of \(f:X\to S\) in \(\mathcal F_W\) is expressed by two clauses. The arrow is fibrant, and the object it defines belongs to \(\mathrm{HOT}^{lc}_W(S)\). Under the saturation qualification on 311, the manuscript reads the second condition on the functor \(S^\circ\to\mathrm{Hot}_W\) sending \(s\) to \(\mathrm{hot}_W(X_s)\). The words « localement constant » have been revised in the manuscript.

**M07 · A cartesian-square test and a hierarchy, 311–313.** Stability under base change leads to square (4.2.2). With \(f\) in the selected class and the bottom map \(p\) in \(W\), the question is whether the top map \(q\) lies in \(W\). Section 4.3 displays \(a\Rightarrow b\Rightarrow c\) and a bracketed fourth consequence. A long addition on 312 revises the claimed hypothesis needed for \(f_!\). Scan 313 contrasts that discussion with an expectation about cofibration hypotheses and left exactness.

**M08 · The clauses written out, 314–315.** Under the heading « Loc 6 », clause a specializes the square test to \(\mathcal F_W\). Clause b concerns a triangle over \(S\) with structure maps in \(\mathcal F_W\) and \(f\in W\). Clause c reads \(\mathcal F_W\cap W\subset W^u\); its prose version describes the fibres of \(f\) as \(W\)-aspherical. The added F) asserts the existence of sufficiently many \(W\)-fibrations. The text gives \(Y=S,\ q=\mathrm{id}_S\) for \(b\Rightarrow c\) and points elsewhere for \(a\Rightarrow b\).

**M09 · Conditions on an arbitrary class, 316.** Section 4.4 names three conditions \(A(\mathcal F),B(\mathcal F),C(\mathcal F)\). The middle condition takes arrows in the full subcategory \(\mathcal F_S\) of the slice, intersects with the indicated weak equivalences and compares with \(W_S^u\). The slash on the first \(W\) in this formula is doubtful. The marginal note extends the language of the conditions to a model category \((M,W)\). A proposed requirement about isomorphisms and base change is crossed out.

**M10 · Comparing the conditions and beginning a lemma, 317.** Two triples of classes are listed. Formula (4.4.3) compares the \(A\)-conditions for the fibred class and the weak-fibration class at each level. The author says the same comparison applies to \(B,C\); small modifiers on the reference-level classes are indistinct. A long marginal calculation counts the conditions. A first lemma is cancelled. Its replacement assumes a factorization and starts to relate \(A(\mathcal F)\) to the existence of enough \(W\)-fibrations. The supplied page ends inside the sentence.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · 305–307, 316–317 · Model remark, high confidence.** There are two independent choices to track in (4.4.1): the class \(\mathcal F\) inserted into \(A,B,C\), and the ambient \(W\) used to test membership. Substituting a class carrying \(W_0\) does not by itself replace the \(W\) on the right of the condition. This reading follows the introductory containment \(W\supset W_0\), the fixed right-hand sides on 316 and the explicit specialization \(W_0=W\) on 317.

**R02 · 315–316 · Model remark, medium confidence.** The specialization \(Y=S,\ q=\mathrm{id}_S\) on 315 raises a question about the general assertion \(B(\mathcal F)\Rightarrow C(\mathcal F)\) on 316. To use that particular specialization in the full subcategory \(\mathcal F_S\), the identity of \(S\) must be one of its objects. The sentence about containing isomorphisms is visibly crossed out. The pages give enough evidence to isolate this point, but leave open how the author intended to handle it for an arbitrary class.

**R03 · 317 · Model remark, high confidence in the arithmetic.** The long margin starts with eighteen conditions and then counts four triples. Replacing two of those triples by one condition each leaves eight. This checks the numbers in the margin; the equivalences that justify the identifications are announced in the text.

**R04 · 309, 311 · Model remark, low confidence about any variance issue.** The displayed Hom on 309 has an undecorated \(S\); the functor on 311 starts at \(S^\circ\). They occur in different constructions. The difference in the ink is clear, but these leaves alone do not establish that a superscript was omitted on 309.
