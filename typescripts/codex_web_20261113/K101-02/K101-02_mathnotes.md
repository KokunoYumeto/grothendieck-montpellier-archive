## What the pages do

**16. Topologies and a question about Ext.** The opening continues a sentence. It compares a topology on \(H^0(Y,G)\), written as a product of local rings, with one on \(H^1(U;F)\). The bracket calls \(A\) complete, adds “donc fermé” and concludes that the quotient is separated. A second question asks about vanishing of \(\operatorname{Ext}^i(K,A)\) in all degrees.

**17–18. A title and an outline.** “Notes antiques” introduces a plan running from local cohomology functors and Cohen–Macaulay modules through local duality to the lower-index functors \(H_i\) and dualizing complexes. The cup product and the relation with cohomological codimension enter as additions to the plan.

**19. The fundamental module.** For a noetherian ring \(A\) of dimension \(n\), the definition asks that \(\Omega\) be finite and that \(D(\Omega)\simeq H^n(A)\). The writer discusses uniqueness up to isomorphism for a local or semilocal ring and gives choices in the complete local and the regular equidimensional cases. A marginal instruction proposes recasting the definition using \(M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(M,\Omega)\).

**19–21. The duality maps.** Théorème 4 starts with a map \(H^n(\Omega)\to I\) and pairings. Scan 20 preserves a largely cancelled attempt to describe their two extreme degrees, including what happens to the identity of \(\Omega\). The continuation on 21 gives \(H^i(A)=0\) for \(k\leq i<n\) as the condition for isomorphisms in degrees \(p\geq k\). Scan 20 also carries a separate interline assertion about the nonzero groups \(\operatorname{Ext}^q(k,M)\) and \(\operatorname{Ext}^i(k,M)\).

**21–22. Consequences in the Cohen–Macaulay case.** The corollaries compare the Ext groups of \(k\) against \(\Omega\) with the duals of \(H^{n-i}(k)\). They identify \(H^n(\Omega)\) as dualizing and give vanishing of higher self-Ext and \(\operatorname{Hom}(\Omega,\Omega)\simeq A\). Scan 22 says each endomorphism is given by a unique scalar, gives injective dimension \(n\), and adds a criterion for a finite module in the left margin.

**23. Making the maps explicit.** From \(H^n(A)\simeq D(\Omega)\) and the associated pairing, the writer forms a pair of maps for each finite \(M\). He specializes to \(M\simeq A\) and \(M=\Omega\). The transpose sends \(1_{\widehat A}\) to the identity of \(\widehat\Omega\), and a general scalar to its homothety. The symbol naming the pairing has several possible readings.

**23–24. The annihilator and two tests for the pairing.** Proposition 9 identifies \(\operatorname{Ann}(\Omega)\) with an intersection of primary ideals and passes to the quotient by this intersection. The margin on 23 compares two lengths. The first corollary on 24 relates surjectivity to faithfulness. The second relates injectivity to whether every endomorphism of \(\Omega\) is a homothety, and gives a dual expression for the kernel.

**25–26. When the endomorphism ring equals \(A\).** Nakayama is invoked for the identity as generator of a cyclic endomorphism module. Corollaries 3 and 4 collect equivalent tests for bijectivity and give Cohen–Macaulay and normal rings as sufficient cases. In the normal case the proof places a finite endomorphism module in the fraction field and then in the integral closure. Scan 26 asks whether the conditions always hold and offers a nonnormal example of dimension two.

**26–27. Quotient by a non-zero-divisor.** Proposition 10 imposes \(xH^{n-1}(A)=H^{n-1}(A)\). The displayed exact sequence identifies \(H^{n-1}(A/xA)\) with the annihilator of \(x\) in \(H^n(A)\). The manuscript then identifies this with \(D(\Omega/x\Omega)\) and gives a corollary for part of a system of parameters.

**27–29. Passage through an ambient ring.** The writer presents \(A\) as a quotient of a local Cohen–Macaulay ring \(A'\) equipped with a fundamental module. He applies duality over \(A'\), specializes to the top degree, and considers localization. Proposition 11 gives the localization assertion for a quotient of a regular local ring. A lemma and corollary express vanishing in Ext degrees over the ambient ring.

**29. Higher modules.** Displays (3) and (3 bis) introduce \(\Omega_A^{(i)}\) and pair their duals with local cohomology of \(A\). Degree zero recovers the fundamental module. The last equivalence characterizes the Cohen–Macaulay case by vanishing for every \(i\geq1\).
