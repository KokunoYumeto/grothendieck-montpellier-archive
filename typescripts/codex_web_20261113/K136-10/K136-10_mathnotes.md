# Mathematical notes on cote 136, scans 95–110

## What the pages do

**The branch-order pairing (97, 103).** The French typescript writes the boundary at a double point as the difference of the two branches. Ordering the branches defines a coordinate functional on the lattice M up to sign. Tensoring the functional with itself and summing gives the positive definite pairing (12.4.5). The later remark treats a single singular point and distinguishes ranks one and zero. Section 12.10 gives a weighted sum with coefficients \(d_\lambda\geq 1\) in the case where regularity of X is no longer assumed.

**The analytic exponential (103, 99, 107).** Section 13 introduces a commutative analytic group over S. Its Lie module gives a vector bundle E, and the exponential gives the exact row (13.1.3), with kernel R and image the union of the neutral components. Section 13.1.4 describes smooth groups with connected fibres by pairs \((E,R)\). The next paragraph describes the rank of the local system R when the group is “abéloide” and the complex structure on its associated real vector bundle.

**Completion and recovery of group maps (107, 95, 105, 101).** Section 13.2 introduces the infinitesimal neighbourhoods of s and the formal completion. Lemma 13.2.1 compares germs of group maps with maps of the completions under hypotheses (a)–(c). The proof passes to maps of the Lie bundles and asks that R map into R′. The generation hypothesis and Nakayama are used on 105. The first sentence of 101 completes the argument, and the corollary applies it to determination of the group near s.

**The fixed part (101).** Section 13.3 extends the group of germs of sections to a constant subgroup near s. The text identifies its image as the largest constant subgroup of R, names it \(R^f\), and forms \(E/R^f\). A handwritten natural sign labels this quotient in (13.3.3).

**A comparison algebra from an exact sequence (96, 98).** A marked element T gives a map \(k\to\Phi\) with quotient Ψ. The expression for \(\mathrm{DR}(\Phi,T)\) combines divided powers of Φ with exterior powers of Ψ, with separate degree labels. The comparison to the original algebra is drawn as a square. Scan 98 argues that a derived category is equivalent to the point and gives an additional hypothesis for the universally flasque version.

**Evaluation at a point of the simplicial topos (100, 102).** A point \(i_n\) and the associated fibre functor are introduced. For \(n=0\) the lemma computes a direct image and evaluates it as \(M^I\). Its corollary expresses maps into the corresponding tensor module by evaluation in degree zero. Applying this to \(k_*\hookrightarrow A_*\) produces the square on 102, with a second diagram connecting three De Rham algebras. A small independent version of the latter diagram is written at the foot of the typescript on 105.

**Adding morphisms and comparing categories (102, 104, 106).** The primed categories have the same objects and more morphisms. On 104 maps of the completed algebras respect the positive part, the divided powers and T. The four-row diagram compares the original and primed categories and their derived categories, including versions with a flasqueness condition. The text goes on to groupoids and two groups it proposes to determine. The qualifying words about these groupoids are difficult to read.

**Components of a map (106).** A triangle from a completed De Rham algebra gives component maps \(v(p,q)\). The \((1,0)\) component sends T to T, and the other components send T to zero. The sheet also displays a barred map from an overwritten B-like object and two smaller comparison diagrams.

**Conditions on the degree-one datum (108).** The faint pencil page studies maps from a completed De Rham algebra. One element in degree one appears in a pair of augmentation conditions. Subsequent boxes involve this element, its differential and its images under two maps from the 2-simplex to the 1-simplex. The minus sign in the last box is visible. The exact coefficients and several placements of the differential in the preceding box remain uncertain.

**Stages of a rational minimal model (109).** The English leaf N27 connects the subalgebra \(\Lambda_{1,n}\) with a nilpotent quotient of the fundamental group. It describes adding generators to obtain successive cohomology isomorphisms and injections. It then associates a tower of principal fibrations to the rational model and qualifies its interpretation in higher dimensions for general non-simply connected spaces. The author’s name does not occur on this leaf. Scan 110 has no mathematical writing.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Q1, 098 and 104.** The proposition on 98 explicitly says “si on suppose que” and singles out the positive \(\Gamma^i\) of a flat, universally flasque module. The condition reappears on an arrow of the diagram on 104.
- **Q2, 100.** The annotation “faux tel quel, à arranger!” is attached to the implication starting with “flasque + plat”. The nearby word “projectif” suggests a revision whose first qualification is disputed.
- **Q3, 106 and 108.** “Que nous nous proposons de déterminer” sets a research goal. “Détermination des hom.” introduces another calculation. Neither wording by itself states a conjectural theorem.
- **Q4, 109.** The English author calls the higher-dimensional significance for general non-simply connected spaces “not yet completely understood except in a philosophical way”.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1, model remark, high confidence, 097.** A change in the order of the two branches negates the functional. The tensor in (12.4.4) receives that sign in both factors. Evaluating on a pair with integer values \(a,b\) gives \((-a)(-b)=ab\). This is a check of the sign cancellation only.
- **R2, model remark, high confidence, 097 and 103.** The summand in (12.10.1) specializes to the summand in (12.4.5) at weight one. Substitution in \(wab\) gives \(ab\). This comparison concerns the summands and does not depend on the crowded source tensor to the right of the colon.
- **R3, model reading question, low confidence, 101, §13.3.** The frozen text has \(A=A_o\), with o/0 uncertain. The earlier hypothesis on 95 has a raised o-like character. Here the character is visibly low. Whether the change of level was intended is open.
- **R4, model reading question, low confidence, 106, last component condition.** The frozen first reading is \((p+q)\ne(1,0)\). The preceding line and Hua favour \((p,q)\ne(1,0)\), but the separator at this occurrence has a short traverse. No correction to the mathematics is inferred.
- **R5, model reading question, low confidence, 108, first and last sets of boxes.** The frozen alternatives include ζ and ξ. The later boxes repeat the same low-zero element, and four overwritten signs interrupt the two middle equalities. A reconstruction of all commutation identities would depend on those readings.
