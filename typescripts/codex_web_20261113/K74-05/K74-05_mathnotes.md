# Mathematical notes on cote 74, scans 89–91 and 93–105

## What the pages do

**U89 · Scan 89.** A calculation already under way describes three square structures on a four-element product by its partitions into two pairs. An orientation of a square gives bijections between its diagonals. The two composites are written with minus signs. The final wedge expression relates the orientations to the diagonal sets of the square and its dual.

**U90 · Scans 90–91.** Two charts link structures on squares, hexagons and triangles. The first has distinct lower-case *hex* and capitalized *Hex* series, maps labelled 2 or 3, and small auxiliary pictures. The second follows a smaller set of branches down to *Pav*, *trgortria* and *triaor*. The text beside the first chart groups triangular, pentagonal and hexagonal prisms. No prose on these sheets defines all the labels.

**U93 · Scan 93.** For a graph with vertex set $S$ and edge set $A$, Axiom 1 requires each edge to lie in exactly one triangle. Axiom 2 requires a vertex outside a triangle to be linked to one of its vertices. Uniqueness follows here from Axiom 1. The author chooses a base triangle $t_0$ and initially imposes the second axiom only on that triangle. Grouping the remaining vertices by their neighbour in $t_0$ defines the three fibres $\widetilde E_\alpha$.

**U94 · Scans 94–95.** The base triangle, the edges up from it and the edges among the remaining vertices give three sorts of edges. A vertical triangle pairs two vertices in the same fibre. The resulting fixed-point-free involution is denoted by $\sigma_\alpha$. A vertex in a different fibre cannot be linked to both members of the pair. Grothendieck calls the upper edges joining different fibres transverse and examines a triangle containing one.

**U96 · Scans 95–97.** Proposition 3 says that this latter triangle has a vertex in each of the three fibres. The set of upper triangles, $\widetilde\Gamma$, consequently maps to their product. Proposition 4 identifies each projection to two fibres with an injection whose image consists of linked pairs. The text names this pair before the page break and gives the projection immediately after it.

**U97 · Scans 97–98.** The *Scholie* reconstructs the vertex set and adjacency from the base triangle, three sets with involutions and a subset of their product. The main list, the interlinear addition and the sideways marginal condition need to be read together. The triangles are grouped into the base triangle, the vertical triangles indexed by the orbits $E_\alpha=\widetilde E_\alpha/\sigma_\alpha$, and the upper triangles indexed by $\widetilde\Gamma$.

**U99 · Scans 98–99.** Imposing Axiom 2 on a vertical triangle forces a vertex in a different fibre to be adjacent to exactly one of the two partners. Proposition 5 states this in terms of the projected upper triangles. The next corollary passes from a linked pair $(s,t)$ to the pair of their partners $(s',t')$. The last corollary asserts stability under the product involution and names its restriction to $\widetilde\Gamma$.

**U100 · Scan 100.** For fixed $s$, the neighbours in another fibre form a section over its orbit set. Adjacency matches the two sections associated to the other two fibres. A second corollary compares their cardinalities. The note at the foot singles out cases with all fibres empty or only one nonempty and distinguishes the two edges from the common vertex and the edge joining their other endpoints in each triangle of the latter graph.

**U101 · Scan 101.** Axiom 3 excludes those degeneracies by requiring the three fibres to be nonempty. An uncancelled argument considers first a base vertex and then a vertex in one of the fibres to establish Axiom 2 on an upper triangle. The wording of Proposition 6 above that argument is cancelled. A recapitulation with a small drawing of a star of triangles leads into the next sheet.

**U102 · Scans 102–103.** Upper triangles are now treated as a nonempty set with an involution and three compatible quotients. A condition on two-coordinate projections specifies the unique member of an involution orbit paired with a given vertex. Taking quotients downstairs produces maps from $\Gamma$ to the $E_\alpha$. The two-sheeted covers of these targets pull back to $\Gamma$, with identifications between the pullbacks.

**U104 · Scans 103–104.** The downstairs condition requires each map $\Gamma\to E_\alpha\times E_\beta$ to be bijective. The classification is divided into the description of those quotient systems and, for a given system, the classification of its order-two coverings subject to the further condition labelled $\gamma$. Scan 104 draws the three branches separately.

**U105 · Scan 105.** Two proposed identifications, a crossed triangle and a heavily cancelled diagram share a sheet with a ledger of cardinalities. The ledger gives $\operatorname{Card}S=3+6c$, $\operatorname{Card}A=3(1+c)(1+2c)$, and $\operatorname{Card}T=(1+c)(1+2c)$. A separate column gives $2c^2,c^2,2c,c$ for $\widetilde\Gamma,\Gamma,\widetilde E_a,E_a$ in that order.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 93, L2.** “Complexes triadiques ou trialitaires ?” asks what to call the structures.
- **Scans 103–104.** “se décompose donc en deux questions exactement” introduces “I) Description des systèmes” and “II) Pour un tel syst… donné, classifier les …”. The first problem concerns the quotient systems. The second asks for their order-two coverings and then for those satisfying the extra axiom. These pages pose the classification problems and do not give a list of their solutions.

## Remarks of the model

**R98.1 · Model remark, high confidence about the finite computation.** The single condition printed as α on scan 97, L19 (injectivity of each two-coordinate projection) has less force than the interlinear exclusion on L21. Take three pairs of vertices, written $a_0,a_1$, $b_0,b_1$, $c_0,c_1$, with the involution swapping partners. Use just $(a_0,b_0,c_0)$ and $(a_1,b_0,c_1)$ for the upper triples. Enumeration gives two distinct entries in every pair projection, yet the reconstructed graph puts the edge $\{a_0,a_1\}$ in two triangles. Their third vertices are its base vertex and $b_0$. The two triples violate the interlinear exclusion. This example explains why it matters which conditions the words “la condition $(\alpha)$” on scan 98 include. The intended scope of that cross-reference remains uncertain.

**R105.1 · Model remark, high confidence about the arithmetic.** With $c$ left indeterminate, two entries on scan 105 give the same triangle count. Both $\operatorname{Card}A/3$ and $\operatorname{Card}S\,(1+c)/3$ expand to $1+3c+2c^2$. The two cover counts at the foot are twice their respective quotient counts. The computation checks the coefficients and factors recorded on the page. The integer counts themselves come from the manuscript.

**Q102a · Model reading question, scan 102, L12.** The frozen wording at the start of the line is plain $\beta)$. There is a small right-angled stem with a separate wave below and slightly right of the closing parenthesis, ahead of the crossed group. This supports reading $\beta_{\widetilde\Gamma})$ as the label. The later occurrence on scan 103 also uses a subscripted beta. Confidence is high about the presence of the extra ink and medium about its attachment.

**Q102b · Model reading question, scan 102, L16.** The frozen wording after the deletion is “et si $u\in$”. A magnified view shows two small superposed curved forms where the membership clause begins. A possible reading is an earlier $s$ overwritten at the position of $u$. Confidence is low in the former letter and the extent of any cancellation. These two reading questions arose while preparing the notes and are recorded here under the freeze rule.
