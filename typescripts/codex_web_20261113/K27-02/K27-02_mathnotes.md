# Mathematical notes on cote 27, scans 15–16 and 18–26

## What the pages do

**Field extension and Picard group, scans 15–16.** Four conditions on a finite extension concern smoothness of a reduced group, rationality of component points, the radical of the reduced identity component and its split quotient. The notes then compare Picard groups before and after field extension under a vanishing condition on characters. A cancelled preliminary conclusion and a second, live version appear on scan 15. Scan 16 treats annihilation in the unipotent case and finiteness in a case of unipotent rank zero.

**Implications, scan 18.** A cancelled table and a revised list test implications between numbered conditions. The examples use characteristic two and include an additional birationality hypothesis. A separate box singles out a proposed nonimplication. Two equations at the foot combine condition labels. Scan 23 gives a list through (xi), while the other sheets also refer to higher labels.

**A common annihilated vector, scan 19.** A nilpotent Lie algebra acts on a vector space over an infinite field. Under the hypothesis that each individual operator is noninjective, the lemma asserts the existence of one nonzero vector annihilated by the entire Lie algebra. The proof invokes primary weight spaces, vanishing loci and Engel. A separate pencil computation compares a polynomial in \(t\) with a power of \(t-\lambda(x)\).

**Root brackets, scan 20.** The pencil calculation expands a bracket against a sum of root vectors. It is followed by a two-dot diagonal matrix, a six-dot upper triangular matrix and, in darker ink, a branching implication chain starting at (xiv). A framed case uses a root with vanishing barred value and a nonzero bracket. It discusses the normalizer, density and Cartan subalgebras. Its closing negative phrase is uncertain.

**Nil-spaces, scan 21.** A typewritten proof fragment compares nilpotence, containment in the nil-space of \(a\), injectivity of an induced adjoint map and equality to a connected normalizer. The missing subjects and quotients are blank on the sheet. The last converse breaks off at “alors”.

**Two characteristic-two examples, scans 22 and 24.** In the first, \(\mathrm{Sl}(2,k)\) and the Lie algebra of a Borel occur in an argument about dominance, dimension and birationality. A separate note relates smoothness of one map to injectivity on a quotient. In the second, written \(\mathrm{Pl}(2,k)\), the brackets on \(X,Y,H\) lead to the subspace \(kH+k(\lambda X+\mu Y)\). Scan 24 uses the two Borels containing \(T\) to argue that a mixed subspace need not arise from a smooth subgroup.

**Candidate conditions, scan 23.** Eleven conditions in a cancelled draft compare containing a Cartan subalgebra, containing regular elements, an adjoint operator on a quotient, properties of maps to \(W(\mathfrak g)\), existence of a smooth subgroup and smoothness of a normalizer. Arrows and braces group selected entries. The lower list experiments with formulations involving a normalizer, density, a smooth group and an injective adjoint action.

**Finite-field construction, scan 25.** Kernels of linear forms cover the two-dimensional torus Lie algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\). Characters lifting the forms define an action of \(T=G_m^2\) on \(U=G_a^I\). The argument uses failure of regularity in \(\mathfrak t\) and conjugacy of maximal tori to give an example whose Lie algebra has no regular element over the indicated field. The slanting note also identifies \(\mathfrak t\) as a Cartan subalgebra.

**A centre functor over a valuation ring, scan 26.** Starting with a group \(E\) of trivial centre and a nontrivial commutative subgroup \(F\), the construction removes \(E_k-F_k\) from the special fibre of \(E_S\). The resulting open group has fibres \(F_k\) and \(E_K\). The centre over the completion and the inverse limit of the centres over the quotients \(A_n\) have different values. The final note states a representability assertion under flatness, finite presentation and connectedness of the fibres.

## Remarks of the model

- **M24.1 · Scan 24 · high confidence.** For \(u=\lambda X+\mu Y\), the brackets at the foot of scan 22 give \([H,u]=u\). This directly accounts for closure of the particular span \(kH+ku\) used in the example.
- **M25.1 · Scan 25 · high confidence for the cases computed.** Taking the forms with coefficient vectors \((1,a)\) for \(a\in\mathbb F_p\), together with \((0,1)\), gives the advertised kernel behaviour for \(p=2,3,5\). Direct enumeration gave respectively 4, 9 and 25 vectors in the union, with only the origin in the intersection in each case.
- **M25.2 · Scan 25 · high confidence.** The assertion about a trivial torus kernel depends on how the characters are lifted. For \(p=2\), the integral weights \((3,0),(0,3),(3,3)\) reduce to the same three nonzero forms as \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\). On \(T(\mathbb F_4)\) the three lifted characters are simultaneously trivial at all nine points. Thus the infinitesimal forms alone do not settle the group-kernel assertion. The sheet permits a choice of lifts.
- **M20.1 · Scan 20 · low confidence.** The model's reading question concerns the last pencil sentence in the frame. The frozen preferred words are “pas” and “sous-”, with “que” and “non-” as alternatives. The short descender and the broken prefix alter what the sentence says about Cartan subalgebras. I would leave its use as a counterexample conditional on that reading.
