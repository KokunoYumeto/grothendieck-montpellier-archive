# Mathematical notes · cote 157-3, scans 066–081

The scan numbers identify the archive images. The manuscript pages are numbered 15–30. Line references use the working lines in the audit. Quotation marks enclose Grothendieck’s words. A doubtful word or sign matters especially in the entries on scans 069, 078 and 080.

## What the pages do

**066 · Filtered colimits.** The page opens inside an argument. Factor each $f_i$ as $X_i\to\overline X_i\to Y_i$. Functoriality and commutation with filtered colimits give the canonical factorization of $f=\varinjlim f_i$. The fibres of $\overline f$ are filtered colimits of fibres of the $\overline f_i$. Their $W$-asphericity and axiom 6 give $f\in W$. The subscript on the first class $W_\omega$ also admits the reading $\infty$.

**066–067 · A proposed characterization.** Grothendieck asks whether axioms 1–6 force $W=W_\infty$ even with the weakest fibration axiom. In the margin he considers weakening localization. On the next sheet he considers strengthening saturation by requiring closure under retracts.

**067–068 · Truncated homotopy equivalences.** The class $W_n$ is defined by bijectivity on components and on the pointed homotopy groups through degree $n$. In the displayed exact sequence the fibre groups vanish through degree $n-1$. In degree $n$ the manuscript gives a cokernel of the map in degree $n+1$, with no reason for it to vanish. This is the obstruction he finds in the proposed fibre test.

**H01 · 068 · A stronger class.** Adding surjectivity in degree $n+1$ defines $W'_n$. Grothendieck gives an equivalent description by $n$-connected homotopy fibres. This supplies the desired fibre condition. He then objects that surjectivity of $\bar g\bar f$ and $\bar g$ does not force surjectivity of $\bar f$, raising a problem for saturation.

**068–069 · Choosing the axioms.** Grothendieck credits derivator localizers $W_{\mathbb D}$ with the first three axioms, including strong saturation. He sets aside the additional axioms on fibrations, connectivity and filtered colimits when deciding what to call a fundamental localizer.

**069–070 · Comparing fibrations.** Section II revisits the earlier assumption $W_{\mathbb D}\supset W_\infty$. The class used to test a change of base affects the strength of the definition. Under the indicated inclusion the diagram runs from the ultrastong condition $a$ to the strong condition $a'$, then to $b$, then to each of $a_1,a_2$. A hypothesis on locally constant coefficients makes the last three conditions equivalent. A marginal correction qualifies the older terminology.

**071 · A change of hypotheses for $\mathrm{HOT}_W$.** Reviewing the construction of $f_!$, Grothendieck questions his use of $W\supset W_\infty$. He proposes working with $W(1),W(2),W(3),W(4\mathrm a)$. He recalls the consequence $W\subset W_\infty$ of adjoining the connectivity axiom.

**T01 · 071–072 · Failure of surjectivity on components.** For $f\in W$ the proof considers $X$ and $Y$ over the discrete category $I=\pi_0(Y)$. A missed component would give a map $\varnothing\to Y_i$ in $W$ with $Y_i$ nonempty. Products with an arbitrary $Z$ and the projection to $Y_i$ would make $Z$ aspheric, hence put every functor in $W$. The inference to the component map carries Grothendieck’s marginal objection.

**072–073 · Failure of injectivity on components.** The other case reduces to an aspheric $X$ with at least two components. Its diagonal supplies a map whose map on components misses an off-diagonal pair. The resulting corollary asserts that a nontotal $W$ satisfying the listed axioms has $W(5)$ and lies in $W_\infty$. Both the marginal objection on 072 and the written bound on 073 qualify this conclusion.

**073 · Fundamental localizer.** Section III requires a class of functors to satisfy saturation $W(1)$, the final-object axiom $W(3)$, and weak localization $W(2\,\mathrm{bis})$. Grothendieck says when he will impose more and identifies one remembered use of a stronger condition in P.S.

**D01 · 073–074 · Proposition 1 and Definition 1.** A functor is $W$-aspheric if all its slices $X_{/y}$ are $W$-aspheric. Proposition 1 gives the equivalent test that pullback along every local isomorphism belongs to $W$. The proof uses the invariance of the slices under local isomorphisms and weak localization. The opposite functor gives the definition of $W$-coasphericity.

**074–075 · Adjoints and a topos formulation.** A right adjoint $g$ supplies a final object in each slice $X_{/y}$, and a left adjoint gives the dual conclusion. Grothendieck also uses a generalized adjoint $Y\to X^\wedge$. It identifies the topos of the slice with the topos induced over $g(y)$, expressing the condition as asphericity of the sheaves $g(y)$.

**075–076 · Local isomorphisms and Proposition 2.** A local isomorphism induces isomorphisms on the slices at all source objects. Grothendieck draws the cartesian square of slices that explains stability under base change. The proof tests the composite and its factors after local isomorphisms. This gives closure under composition for aspheric functors and cancellation from $gf$ and $f$ to $g$. He states the corresponding coaspheric assertion.

**E01 · 076–077 · Two corollaries and an example.** Local isomorphisms preserve aspheric functors under pullback, and local coisomorphisms preserve coaspheric functors. Under the stated connectivity condition the inclusion of an initial object can lose asphericity on pullback to a different object. Equivalences are both aspheric and coaspheric by the adjoint criterion.

**077–078 · Proposition 3.** The four tests concern the inclusion of a fibre into a slice, the fibres of a map of coslices, the closed fibre after base change to the interval, and the category $X(x_0,u_0)$ of lifts of one arrow with specified source. The proof identifies the same lifting category in the different tests. For a point in the closed fibre the relevant category already has an initial object. A point of the other fibre gives the lifting category from d).

**D02 · 079 · Definition 2 and its examples.** The conditions of Proposition 3 define $W$-proper functors. A functor is $W$-smooth when its opposite is $W$-proper. In a weakly $\mathrm{Cat}$-cofibred category the relevant lifting categories have initial objects, giving properness. The remark expresses this through a representing object $u_{0*}(x_0)$ and gives the dual smoothness assertion.

**P04 · 079–080 · Proposition 4 and the lemma.** Proper and smooth functors are stable under composition and base change. The examples include local coisomorphisms and closed immersions on the proper side, local isomorphisms and open immersions on the smooth side. The composition argument uses the cartesian diagram on 080, the lemma about a proper functor on coslices, and Proposition 5.

**P05 · 080–081 · Proposition 5.** The proof treats a proper functor with aspheric fibres, then invokes duality for a smooth functor. It concludes universal membership in $W$. The following paragraph observes that universal membership already forces aspheric fibres. The final margin relates universal membership to universal asphericity by Proposition 1(a).

**081 · Fibrewise corollary.** With full $W(2)$, an $S$-functor between two categories proper over $S$, or between two smooth ones over $S$, belongs to $W$ if it belongs to $W$ on every fibre. The leaf ends with the assertion.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Model remark, high confidence · 073 L3–4.** Enumerating the diagonal for sets of sizes 1, 2 and 3 gives image sizes 1, 2 and 3 in targets of sizes 1, 4 and 9. The first case refutes the bound as a sufficient condition. S01 gives the bound available on the previous sheet.

**R02 · Model remark, high confidence · 068 L13–17.** The surjectivity objection already occurs with a singleton included in a two-element set, followed by the unique map to a singleton. The second map and the composite are onto. The first misses one element. This checks the cancellation mechanism at the level of functions. The pages give no specific functors realizing that example on homotopy groups.

**R03 · Model remark, high confidence · 076 L20–29.** In the category $0\to1$, each set $\mathrm{Hom}(0,y)$ has one element. The strict pullback of the inclusions of the two different objects has none. This instantiates the geometry of the example under the connectivity qualification on the sheet.

**R04 · Model remark, high confidence about the gap identified · 071 L23–31 and 072 L1–5.** The proposed theorem assumes $W(4\mathrm b'')$. The first inference on 072 needs a map between fibres to lie in $W$ because the total map does. The margin explicitly questions that use. It leaves the theorem under the weaker hypothesis unresolved on these pages.

**R05 · Model remark, high confidence about the proof’s scope · 080 L21–24 and 081 L1–9.** In the proper case the proof passes from an aspheric fibre to an aspheric slice using the coaspheric inclusion and weak localization. It then invokes weak localization for $f$ and observes that the hypotheses persist under pullback. “Le cas $f$ lisse est dual” treats smoothness separately. On 080 “et” is struck between the two adjectives. Whether any replacement conjunction was intended at that spot remains a low-confidence reading question.

**R06 · Model reading question, low confidence · 069 L23–26 and 071 L7.** The frozen words “mettre la condition a) … par des” could express the intended sense “remplacer … par”, but the latter verb is an interpretation of the sentence. The inclusion on 071 starts with a glyph read $V$ or $W$. Lines 3 and 10 of scan 071 give the inclusion with $W$. The stroke at L7 remains ambiguous.
