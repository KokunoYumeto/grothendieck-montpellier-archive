# K55-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 2 and 4 — Modules on a compact Stein space.** With $A=\Gamma(X,\underline O_X)$, the writer sends a finitely presented module $M$ to $\widetilde M$. Applying global sections to a presentation gives $M\simeq\Gamma(\widetilde M)$. The two-row Hom diagram on scan 4 is the full-faithfulness argument. For essential surjectivity, a presentation of a coherent $F$ supplies a map on global sections whose cokernel gives the required $M$.

**Scans 5 and 3 — Codimension in a fibre.** The statement of Proposition 8 starts on typed page 110 and its local argument continues on page 111. The generic point of a fibre component gives a comparison of two local dimensions. Corollary 1 reformulates it for a component above an irreducible closed subset. Corollary 2 applies the principal ideal theorem to the zero set of a section. Several marginal notes seek shorter arguments.

**Scans 7 and 5 — Dimension over a field.** On typed page 109, Corollary 2 expresses local dimension through the irreducible components containing a point. Corollary 3 bounds the dimension of the closure of an image via the inclusion of residue fields at generic points. The fraction field of a discrete valuation ring supplies a counterexample to extending the bound to arbitrary integral schemes. Corollary 4 continues on page 110 with the dimension of a product and the field-extension case. The proof uses affine covers, reduction, normalization and transcendence degree.

**Scan 6 — Lemma A.** The ringed space is quasi-compact, the structure sheaf coherent, the stalks noetherian, and $H^1(X,J)$ vanishes for coherent $J$. The lemma asserts the equivalence between finitely presented $A$-modules and coherent sheaf modules. Its three corollaries concern exactness and stalk flatness, coherence of $A$, and finite-type maximal ideals when $X$ is “pur”. A sideways variant treats modules with a global presentation.

**Scans 6 and 8 — Two separate conditions.** The remark marks exactness on taking sections of a presentation by $\alpha$, and generation by sections by $\beta$. Scan 8 draws consequences from $\alpha$ for finitely presented algebras and for modules with a finite global presentation. It then asks a contraction question for an ideal of $A[T]$.

**Scans 12 and 10 — Changing the algebra.** Scan 12 strengthens the local conditions and takes a presentation over a polynomial algebra through bounded-degree pieces $\Sigma_m$. Passing to the limit is intended to give $\alpha$. Scan 10 records full faithfulness and singles out $\beta$ as still to be proved. It isolates coherence of $J_m=J\cap B_m$ as the issue in a second passage to a limit.

**Scan 9 — Associated points and reducedness.** The sheet begins with two formulas involving $\operatorname{Ass}$. Proposition 6.7 tests injectivity of multiplication by a section. Corollary 6.8 states an analogous criterion for an ideal. Proposition 6.9 characterizes reducedness using a dense open and the absence of embedded associated cycles, or the generic length condition. A paragraph about the support of the nilpotent ideal follows. The sheet ends during a proposed definition of a reduced module.

**Scans 11 and 13 — Topological dimension.** Proposition 1 treats subspaces and finite closed covers. Its example changes the topology on a two-point set to make the dimension of a continuous image increase. A corollary gives a local finite-cover formula. Proposition 2 compares dimension with codimensions and with local dimensions. Its proof continues on typed page 102, followed by the open-cover and closed-point formulas and the two inequalities labelled (1) and (2).

**Scans 13 and 15 — Dimension zero and chain length.** Proposition 3 on page 102 characterizes dimension zero for a noetherian $T_0$ space and codimension zero for a closed subset. Its proof leaves the sheet mid-sentence. Page 104 begins at the end of an induction and names the “condition des chaînes”. Proposition 5 compares equal lengths of maximal chains with equidimensionality, equicodimensionality and the additivity formulas (4) and (5). Formula (6) is circled. The converse argument continues beyond the selected sheet.

**Scan 14 — Lemma B.** At a point with integral local ring on the support of a coherent quotient, the writer imposes a neighbourhood condition on sections vanishing near that point. The conclusion is stabilization of ascending chains of coherent subsheaves. The corollary asserts that the global ring is noetherian. The proposed compact analytic example has an interlinear replacement and a request for clarification.

**Scans 17–18 — A complex torus.** The asserted example has complex dimension two, with one-dimensional base and fibre. The writer specifies four lattice generators and considers what a complementary complex line would imply. Scan 18 writes out the coordinates of $x$ and $cx$, attempts to force $c'$ and $c''$ to be rational, and uses irrationality of $k$ to reach a contradiction. The written generators and this inference are examined separately below.

## Remarks of the model

**M03-R · Model, high confidence.** On scan 3 the unstruck claim that $A\mathfrak m$ generates the localized maximal ideal is too strong. Take $B=\mathbf F_2$ and $A=\mathbf F_2[t]/(t^2)$. Here $A\mathfrak m=0$, while the unique prime $(t)$ is nonzero and is the radical of zero. Enumerating the four ring elements also gives $\operatorname{Ann}(t)=(t)$, so the prime is associated as the text requires. This tests the generation step of the proof.

**M11-E · Model, high confidence.** The dimensions in the example on scan 11 can be checked exhaustively. In the discrete domain the nonempty irreducible closed sets are the two singletons. In the codomain they are $\{a\}$ and $\{a,b\}$, a chain of length one. Every closed set of the codomain is closed in the domain, so the identity used on the sheet is continuous.

**M17-R · Model, high confidence.** The four unmarked generators on scan 17 already exhibit a complement for this particular lattice. Subtracting the first generator from the fourth gives $ik e_2$. The integer change of basis has determinant one. Thus $\Gamma$ splits into the lattices generated by $(e_1,ie_1)$ and by $(e_2,ik e_2)$, and the quotient is a product of the corresponding one-dimensional tori. The proposed obstruction fails for the displayed example (**M17-S**). The general existence statement on the first four lines is a separate claim.

**M18-R · Model, high confidence.** Take the allowed value $k=\sqrt2$, $x=e_2$ and $c=i\sqrt2$. Both $x$ and $cx$ are lattice vectors by M17-R, and the first two real coordinates of each vanish. The conclusion that $c''$ is rational fails. If the first complex coordinate $z$ of $x$ is nonzero, $z$ and $cz$ are Gaussian integers, and $c=(cz)/z$ does have rational real and imaginary parts. This check uses the uncancelled generator list and works with either reading of the blotted sign on scan 18.
