# K57-09 — mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**152, 150, 156 — quadratic transformations of analytic surfaces.** In internal page order (39, 40, 41), the typescript states the effect of a quadratic transformation on the meromorphic function field and on projective algebraic surfaces. The Castelnuovo–Enriques theorem contracts a nonsingular rational curve with self-intersection −1. Page 40 constructs a meromorphic map from a compact analytic surface with two algebraically independent meromorphic functions to a projective algebraic surface. Quadratic transformations remove its fundamental points. Page 41 starts to show that the number of inverse images of a general point is one. It compares complete linear systems using Riemann–Roch and stops during the argument.

**151 — the Picard group of a curve with several components.** The notes use quotients of Picard groups of the whole curve to discuss the individual $C_i$ and the intersections $C_i\cap C_j$. An outlined computation counts the dimension of $G_{\mathrm{mult}}$. A pentagonal configuration accompanies the exclusion of cycles. The lower display expresses $g=\dim\operatorname{Pic}_{C/k}$ using the component genera and then $\rho+\sigma$. The marginal gloss calls $g_i$ the genus of $\widetilde C_i$. The words identifying the multiplicative and additive contributions are partly doubtful.

**153, 155 — component counts and examples.** Sketches mark genera and intersections. On 153 the three summations are organised by $g_i$ and the number $\nu_i$ associated to $C_i$. The sheet singles out genus zero with $\nu_i=2$ in the definition of $\lambda$. Two sets of conditions on 155 concern abelian $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{C/k}$ and the absence of an additive component. An outlined equality gives $\sum\nu_i=c_0-1+\rho+c_1$. The subsequent calculation uses $g-\sum g_i$ and $\lambda$. The lower examples include an elliptic curve with $k$ marked points and the two short numerical calculations.

**154 — fibres and local determination of a morphism.** Proposition (6.4.12) compares a finite algebraic fibre before and after base change, including its rank and geometric number of points. The corrected paragraph discusses families and their parameters. Proposition (6.5.1) compares morphisms by their maps on local rings. Part (i) localizes at a common denominator to obtain equality on a neighbourhood. Part (ii) starts the converse construction under a local noetherian hypothesis and reaches a composite out of a polynomial ring.

**157–158 — the Picard/divisor heading and two equivalences.** The cover introduces the subject. In A), a basis for $E=f_*\underline L$ is compared with a projective trivialisation together with line-bundle data, and with a morphism satisfying a pushforward condition. In A′), bases are taken modulo the units sheaf. The right-hand version again requires a pullback condition and singles out a “morphisme spécial”. The left margin raises compatibility with change of base.

**159 — adic algebras and completed rings of fractions.** The printed argument cycles through c), a), b), c), using finite systems of generators, a projective system of quotients and a map from a restricted power-series algebra. A pencil note questions the chosen generators. Section 7.6 defines $A\{S^{-1}\}$ by a projective limit of localizations of the quotients $A_\lambda$. Proposition (7.6.2) identifies it with the separated completion for the indicated localized ideals.

**160, 162 — projective morphisms and descent data.** A″) begins after dropping the choice of $\underline L$. A four-row inset compares trivialisations, a section and a special morphism. A‴) introduces a Brauer–Severi bundle, a principal bundle and a morphism covariant for $\mathrm{GP}(N)$. The writing then introduces a space of special morphisms. Scan 162 compares compatible equivariant maps with quotient data under two explicit conditions. Its marginal Imm condition and its last unfinished line limit how much of the intended result can be reconstructed.

**161 — the topology of an affine spectrum.** The opening formula converts a union of subsets into an intersection of ideals. The next results give the radical and closure identities, the closed-set correspondence, the noetherian implication and examples concerning its converse, the closure of a point, and the Kolmogoroff property. The page passes to the opens $D(f)$, their intersections, their basis property and quasi-compactness. It begins the proof of the basis assertion. Pencil adds a singleton formula and its application, corrects a reference and singles out $X=D(1)$.

**163 — the contents of “Le langage des schémas”.** The ten sections progress from affine schemes, morphisms and products to rational maps, Chevalley schemes, quasi-coherent sheaves and formal schemes. The accompanying paragraph explains which parts of the language will be used later and allows the reader to postpone § 10 until chapter III, § 3.
