# Mathematical notes on cote 83, scans 129–144

Locations use the line numbers in the individual page files. The identifiers link claims discussed here to CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**129. Cancelled typescript.** The writer asks how chaos dropped out of his account of order. Order is successively paired with chaos, mystery and liberty. In the «Fleur cosmique» the latter two pairings occupy opposite slopes. The sheet breaks off while describing the response to chaos.

**130. A pair of embeddings (A130).** Given maps $i:H\to A$ and $j:H'\to A$, the product $i(u)j(v)$ gives a linear map $f:P\to A$. Its restrictions recover the two embeddings. The lower margin explicitly sets aside the assumption that $f$ is an isomorphism of modules.

**131–133. Forms and their extensions (A131, A132).** Pullback of trace, determinant and the two bilinear forms from $A$ gives forms on $P$. Equation (1) specifies the quadratic form on pure tensors. The resulting extension spaces are compared on $B$, $H$ and $H'$. On scan 132, a form vanishing on the two summands of $B/k\simeq L\oplus L'$ is described using a functional on $L\otimes L'$. This gives the group acting on the extension spaces. Scan 133 adds $L_P$ and draws the restriction, polarisation, complementary-form and trace maps. The long marginal note describes the arrow from $\underline{\Phi}_B$ to $\underline L_P$.

**134–135. Difference of two extensions and existence (A134, A135).** A determinant on rank-two tensors gives a universal quadratic function vanishing on pure tensors. The lemma and its determinant construction appear at the foot of 135, while 134 opens with the target line and the universal property. For existence of an extension on $P$, scan 134 abandons a proposed tensor-product-algebra argument and invokes $M(b,c,b',c',t)$. Scan 135 writes the exact sequence with successive terms $B,P,L\otimes L'$. In particular, linear extensions of $T_B$ differ by a functional on $L\otimes L'$.

**136–137. Twists and algebra structures (A136, A137).** The diagram has six objects. Its left end parametrizes algebra structures on $P$ inducing the given ones on the factors. A point of $L_P$ is called a «twist». The recapitulatory theorem asserts bijectivity and commutativity, and identifies when the algebra is Azumaya by the invertibility of the divided discriminant of the ternary form on $B$. The note in the margin distinguishes the data needed for the source and target from the determinant data used by the map itself.

**137–138. The discriminant and its zero scheme (A138).** The divided discriminant is polynomial of degree two on the affine line of twists. Its quadratic part is squaring. The preimage of zero defines a quadratic scheme $\widetilde S$ over $S$. The proposed map from $X\times_S X'$ switches to the conjugate value when either input is conjugated. In the étale case the resulting map from the contracted product is identified as an isomorphism.

**139–140. Choosing a root (A139, N140).** Write $z=\varphi(u)$ and $z'=\varphi'(v)$. A calculation first made over a field of characteristic different from two selects the minus sign to give $t=b'z+bz'-2zz'$. The complementary value is $bb'-t$. On 140 the author gives their product and rewrites $t$ as a bilinear expression in traces and characters. Its values on the two unit slices are the traces. With adapted bases he obtains $c=c'=t=0$, placing that point on the zero scheme.

**141–142. Conjugation of a twist (A141, N142).** The preceding map is said to commute with base change. For any twist, conjugating the first input gives the same value as conjugating the second. The author checks this by comparing two traces in $A_T$. In coordinates the resulting involution is $t\mapsto bb'-t$. It preserves the discriminant and restricts to the involution of $\widetilde S$.

**142–143. Quotient and étaleness (A142).** The proposition assumes that $2$ is invertible, or more generally that at each point where it vanishes at least one cover is étale. The ideal condition written on 142 is $(2,\delta,\delta')=k$. The map from the product to $X\wedge_S X'$ is then finite locally free of rank two, with the simultaneous involution. The next sheet calls the target the universal quotient. It gives separate criteria: the projection is étale if at every point one or the other fibre is étale, and the quotient cover is étale over $S$ if both covers are étale. The manuscript underlines «ou» and inserts and underlines «et».

**143–144. A generator of the invariant algebra (A143, N144).** Here $P=H\otimes H'$ has its ordinary commutative tensor-product algebra structure. With bases chosen, the invariant subalgebra has basis $1,u*v$, where $u*v=\mathrm{Tr}(v)u+\mathrm{Tr}(u)v-2(u\otimes v)$. Scan 144 evaluates this element by the pair of characters, gives its discriminant and conjugation identities, and extends the formulas to arbitrary $u,v$ in all characteristics. The page ends by recovering the affine space $L_P$ from the quadratic cover. Its long marginal note describes restriction of a coordinate function to the cover.

## Remarks of the model

**Model, high confidence (N140, A139).** Take $b=3,c=2,b'=5,c'=6,z=1,z'=2$. The two roots selected on 139 are $7,8$, and their product is $56$. The constant written on 139 L20 is $72$. The determinant expression on 144 L7 gives $24$. Scan 140 L7 gives $56$. These evaluations distinguish the three written forms without relying on faint primes.

**Model, high confidence (D139, N142, N144).** For the refined constant $K$, the discriminant $(bb')^2-4K$ is $(b^2-4c)(b'^2-4c')$. In the quadratic root coordinates, switching either one of $z,z'$ to its conjugate interchanges $t$ and $bb'-t$ and switching both fixes $t$. The coefficient checks used integer polynomials and required no division by two. Their scope is the coordinate identities. The geometric quotient assertion in A142 is a separate claim.

**Model, medium confidence (A136, A143).** The two uses of $P=H\otimes H'$ can be tracked by the author’s own qualifications. On 136 the algebra structure varies with the twist. On 143 he specifies «avec cette fois la structure d’Alg. comm. can. sur P» just before taking invariants. This identifies the multiplication intended in the formula for $u*v$ on 143.
