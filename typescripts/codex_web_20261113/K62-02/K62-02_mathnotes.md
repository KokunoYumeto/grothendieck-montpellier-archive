# Mathematical notes · cote 62, scans 68–82

Scans are the PDF scan numbers. The short identifiers locate statements in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv. The formulas below use the first reading where a manuscript glyph has alternatives. The final page files retain the alternatives.

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 68.** The quotient of sigma functions in (6) expresses sn. Grothendieck reads off its signs under translation, its periods, its simple poles and zeros, and their residues or derivatives. He gives a parity argument and evaluates sn and its derivative at half-periods.

**U02 · Scan 69.** Two equations for squares, with initial value +1, introduce cn and dn. Their sigma quotients lead to a two-column table of parity and translation laws. The lower half specializes these laws to the poles and zeros of cn and its values and derivatives there.

**U03 · Scan 70.** The list for dn completes the preceding sheet. Three first derivative formulas (11), three pairwise algebraic identities (12) and separate differential equations for cn and dn (13) collect the relations among the three functions. The last paragraph compares the latter equations with a family for sn.

**U04 · Scan 71.** A second normalization defines $\mathrm{sn}_1^2$ from $\wp-e_1$ and chooses $\mathrm{sn}'_1(0)=1$. The sheet relates $\mathrm{sn}_1$ to sigma, records its product under one half-period shift, and lists its periods, signs, poles and zeros. Two final rows evaluate it at $\omega_2$ and $\omega_3$.

**U05 · Scans 72 and 74.** Scan 72 defines $\mathrm{cn}_1,\mathrm{dn}_1$ with the factors $e_2-e_1,e_3-e_1$, gives their sigma quotients and tabulates signs, periods, poles and zeros. Scan 74 supplies the three derivative formulas (8) and the factored equation (9) for the square of $\mathrm{sn}'_1$.

**U06 · Scan 73.** A separate recapitulation uses sn and $k$. The displayed values of the three $e_i$ give $e_2-e_1=1$ and $e_3-e_1=k^2$. The page brings the sigma quotient, half-period product, translation signs and values at the half-periods together.

**U07 · Scan 75.** Two independent periods motivate a period lattice and the field $E$ of meromorphic functions on the quotient torus. Grothendieck invokes a finite-degree assertion for $E/C(f)$. The first elementary observations concern pole-free functions, the sum of residues and the counts of zeros and poles.

**U08 · Scans 75–76.** A lattice series constructs $\wp$. Its derivative is periodic, and parity eliminates the possible additive constant in a period shift of $\wp$. Its double poles, its regular part at the origin and its even Laurent expansion characterize the chosen normalization.

**U09 · Scans 76 and 78.** For an even elliptic function with poles in the lattice, subtracting a power of $\wp$ reduces its pole order and gives a polynomial description. For an even function with other poles, a product of powers of $\wp(z)-\wp(a_i)$ removes them. This gives the rational description.

**U10 · Scan 77.** An entirely cancelled page examines the denominator of $R_2$ in an expression $\wp'R_2(\wp)$. An attempted lemma on poles, the count of preimages of a value, and the behavior of the odd derivative at the half-periods enter the abandoned argument. The lower note on the left is part of this cancelled work.

**U11 · Scans 78–79.** The invariant subfield of the involution on $E$ has index two. The decomposition $R_1(\wp)+\wp'R_2(\wp)$ gives a unique pair of rational functions. Item g) asks when they are polynomials. After crossing out an extended argument, Grothendieck points to induction on the pole multiplicity at the origin and gives the odd-function case.

**U12 · Scan 79.** The lattice sums for $\wp,\wp'$ are written again. Comparing with the Laurent series gives formulas for $c_1$ and $c_k$. The half-period values $e_i=\wp(\omega_i)$ are distinct by the count of the roots of $\wp z-e_i$.

**U13 · Scans 80–81.** An algebraic relation between an elliptic function and its derivative leads to a cubic for $\wp'^2$. Identification of coefficients gives $g_2=20c_1$ and $g_3=28c_2$. A second calculation factors the cubic through the $e_i$. Differentiating gives $\wp''$ and $\wp'''$, and two further Laurent coefficients follow.

**U14 · Scans 81–82.** An integral of the regular part of $\wp$ defines $\zeta$. The notes give its series, pole residues and additive change under a period shift. Evaluating at a half-period identifies $\eta$; a contour integral is indicated for the relation between the two quasi-periods and the periods.

**U15 · Scan 82.** The “Formule d'Hermite” writes an elliptic function from its finite list of principal parts, up to a constant. It uses translates of $\zeta$, $\wp$ and derivatives of $\wp$. The page ties ellipticity of the expression to cancellation of residues.

**U16 · Scan 82.** The product for $\sigma$ and the formula $\sigma'/\sigma=\zeta$ are followed by an integral representation. The last lines give oddness, simple zeros at the lattice and the first two correction terms after $u$ in its expansion.
