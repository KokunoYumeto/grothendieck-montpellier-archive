# K64-08 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 91–93, direct pinnings.** The first sheet continues a discussion of direct Legendre–Jacobi pinnings. It singles out the section \(i\) of the fourth roots and describes the group acting on the parameter space. Equations (51)–(54) compare the modular action with the tautological action, then restrict it to the subgroup on which \(\lambda^2=\operatorname{sg}(g)\). Scan 93 explains the interest of working over \(\widetilde S_0\).

**Scan 93, a relative Jacobi construction to be made.** The author asks for level-4 pinnings over level 2. His candidate uses sections of \({}_4E\to{}_2E\) above the nonzero 2-torsion and a three-term vanishing condition on each geometric fibre. He also asks for the associated Legendre pinning.

**Scans 94–96, the square root and its torsor.** For a cubic étale cover and a section \(t\), scan 94 describes an elliptic curve by an invertible module \(T\) and an identification of its square. After choosing \(T_0\), the other choices form the category described by (55). A choice of \(\alpha\) gives \(E_\alpha\); in (57), an isomorphism \(u:E_\alpha\to E\) produces the pinning \(u(\kappa_\alpha)\). Scans 95–96 also consider supplying a circular order and choosing a fourth root \(i\).

**Scans 96–97, linear data over a field.** The calculation starts with the three-element set \(I\), the kernel \(V_I\) of the sum on \(k^I\), and a line \(F_t\subset V_I\). The dotted points of the projective line are identified in the margin. Equations (61)–(63) express the square of the tangent datum using \(F_t\) and the two circular orders.

**Scans 97–99, the compatibility conditions.** The pinning is written first as isomorphisms indexed by \(i\), then in dual form. Equations (68)–(73) relate the two choices of order \(\omega\), the change \(\delta\mapsto\lambda\delta\), and the square of \(\psi^\omega(\delta)\). Scan 99 gathers these data into a lemma. In \(\psi_i^\omega\) at (74), the lower index now runs through \(I\); this is a different indexing role from the fourth-root index on \(\kappa_i\) in (65).

**Scans 99–100, coordinates on the line.** Writing each vector in \(k^I\) turns membership in \(V_I\) into the zero-sum equation (75). Equations (76) and (77) express the two transformation laws component by component. A further functional with coefficients \(c_i\) cuts out \(F_t\) inside \(V_I\), giving (78). The last line returns to condition (73) and stops there.

**Scans 101–102, the section algebra.** The title announces descriptions by Weierstrass \(\wp\)-functions. The next sheet starts over at (1), with functions on \(E\) allowed a pole bounded by \(n\{0_E\}\). It records their ranks, multiplication and graded algebra, then identifies the quotient in (6) with a tensor power of the tangent space at the origin.

**Scan 103, differentiation and inversion.** Equations (8)–(11) present the degree-raising differentiation map and its tensorial form in two diagrams of exact sequences. Equation (12) asserts a kernel calculation. Equations (13)–(14) introduce the checked function and the involution on the algebra. The right-hand \(f\) in (13) has a visible check.

## Remarks of the model

**R100 · High confidence.** The NB on scan 100 gives precisely the ambiguity expected for coefficients cutting out a line in the zero-sum plane. Adding a common scalar to the \(c_i\) leaves the functional unchanged on that plane. Multiplying all \(c_i\) by the same nonzero scalar preserves its kernel. The coordinate identity was checked by symbolic expansion.

**R103 · High confidence under the ordinary derivation stated in (8).** The unrestricted constant-kernel assertion in the second line of (12) fails in characteristic 3, which scan 102 allows. Take \(f\in\mathcal F_2\setminus\mathcal F_1\), using the ranks in (2)–(3). Then \(f^3\in\mathcal F_6\) is nonconstant, but every \(k\)-derivation kills \(f^3\). This objection uses the unstruck second line of (12) and does not depend on how the first line was corrected. The positive-characteristic statement supported by this check is containment of the third powers in the kernel in characteristic 3 (R103-P). A characteristic-zero, nonzero-direction version is a possible intended restriction; these pages do not state it.

**Reading question, scan 103, top middle term of (11) · Low confidence.** The frozen reading is \(\mathcal F_E\) with alternative \(t_E\). The top stroke looks like the filtration letter; the two neighbouring tensor products and (10) favour the tangent letter. The mark alone does not warrant a mathematical correction.
