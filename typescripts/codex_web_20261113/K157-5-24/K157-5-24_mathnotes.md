# Mathematical notes · cote 157-5 · scans 373–388

## What the pages do

**U1. Accessibility inside an ambient category, scan 373.** Section 4.13.5 explains what it means for a strictly full subcategory $\underline S$ to be $\pi'_0$-accessible in $M$. It asks separately for a useful cardinal for $\underline S$, accessibility of the inclusion, and the inclusion of the corresponding small objects in $M_{\pi'_0}$. The author then asks how these three conditions behave when the cardinal increases.

**U2. A bound for changing the cardinal, scans 373–374.** After several cancelled choices of a least cardinal, Corollary 4.13.6 gives a sufficient lower bound for $\pi$, together with $\pi^{\pi'_0}=\pi$. The corollary applies to $\underline S$ inside $M$. Its closing parenthesis also writes down the two Ind presentations.

**U3. Intersections of accessible subcategories, scans 375–378.** Proposition 4.13.7 states closure under a small intersection. Corollaries 4.13.8 and 4.13.9 isolate cardinal bounds and closure under certain inductive limits. The proof considers $\underline S_\alpha(\pi)=\underline S_\alpha\cap M_\pi$ and the corresponding $\underline S(\pi)$. For an object $x$, it compares colimits over the categories above $x$ and reduces the question to cofinality of an intersection $J=\bigcap J_\alpha$ in $I$.

**U4. The cofinality lemma, scans 379–381.** Lemma 4.13.10 sets out five hypotheses on $I$, the $J_r$ and the cardinality of their indexing set. The note numbered 4.13.10.1 observes that a fully faithful cofinal functor into a category large in the stated sense transfers that size property to its source. It uses the square involving $J$, $I$, $J^B$ and $I^B$. This lets the author dispense with a separate size qualifier in condition d).

**U5. Indexing the construction, scans 381–383.** For $i\in\operatorname{Ob}I$, the proof seeks a map from $i$ to an object of $J$. It introduces the least ordinal $\alpha_\pi$ of cardinality $\pi$ and a map $\varphi:I_{\alpha_\pi}\to A$ whose fibres all have cardinality $\pi$. The second statement numbered 4.13.10.1 obtains this map from a projection $A\times A'\to A$ and a bijection with the indexing ordinal.

**U6. Building a common target, scans 383–386.** The intended ordinal system puts $i_\alpha$ in $J_{\varphi(\alpha)}$. Restricting the colimit to each cofinal fibre puts its value $j$ in every $J_r$. The successor step chooses a map into the next subcategory. At a limit stage the author chooses a common upper object, equalizes the pairs of maps which need to agree, and then uses cofinality to reach the required subcategory. Scan 386 closes the proofs of 4.13.10, 4.13.9 and 4.13.8.

**U7. Weaker hypotheses and the reformulated corollary, scans 386–387.** Remark 4.13.10.2 lists a′–e′. In particular the stability conditions refer to colimits of the one ordinal shape $I_{\alpha_\pi}$. A second version of 4.13.8 introduces a non-limit cardinal $c$ and uses the initial ordinal of cardinality $c$ for the slice-category argument. The compact line defining the two suprema is discussed under Slips.

**U8. Closure for specified colimit shapes, scan 387.** Section 4.13.10.3 gives a version in the language of 4.13.23. Its first conclusion concerns intersections of cardinality at most $c$ of subcategories that are $\pi$-accessible and stable under colimits of shape $I_{\alpha_c}$. Its last paragraph treats a collection of shapes with a bound on their cardinalities and with $I_{\alpha_c}$ among them. The script letter naming that collection has a doubtful underline.

**U9. Unfilled stationery, scan 388.** Two diagonal strokes cross the printed Avignon school-inspectorate form. It carries no mathematical continuation.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · model · high confidence · scan 383.** The words « plus précis » distinguish having size $\pi$ from merely being cofinal. Every proper initial segment of the ordinal used here has size less than $\pi$. A fibre of size $\pi$ is therefore unbounded in it.
- **R2 · model · medium confidence · scans 379 and 382.** The two marginal objections concern the ability to dominate a small family of indices. An unbounded family of size less than $\pi$ in the initial ordinal would obstruct that step. The author's full convention for « grand devant » lies outside these sheets.
- **R3a · model · high confidence · scans 383–384.** Applied at zero, (*) asks that $i_0$ belong to $J_{\varphi(0)}$, while (**) sets $i_0=i$ for the initially arbitrary $i$. In the two-object order $0<1$, take one cofinal full subcategory $\{1\}$ and start at 0. The two requirements cannot both hold.
- **R3b · model · high confidence · scans 381 and 384.** For the local initial step, cofinality supplies an arrow $i\to i_0$ with $i_0\in J_{\varphi(0)}$. Its composite with the colimit map still gives the arrow $i\to j$ sought on scan 381. This observation concerns initialization only.
