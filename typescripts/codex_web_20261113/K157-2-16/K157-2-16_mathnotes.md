# Mathematical notes on scans 243–258

Scan numbers refer to the archive images. Page numbers in quotations are Grothendieck’s. The French excerpts retain his abbreviations. The numbered sections ⑥ through ⑨ are his.

## What the pages do

**243–244. A fibre inclusion over a fork.** Grothendieck takes an ordered set with an initial object and two maximal objects. He seeks a split cofibration for which the inclusion of the fibre at one of them fails to lie in $W$. The special diagram has $X_0=X_b=e$, $\beta=\mathrm{id}_e$ and $X_a=T$, with $\alpha$ selecting an object of $T$. First he considers the ordinary amalgam. Then he tries to reach $X$ directly from $T$ through the open restriction $X''$. He describes a retraction onto $X''$ and compares the cohomology of the two open pieces by Mayer–Vietoris.

**245–246. Properness after applying $\Phi$.** He first observes that a cofibration $X$ can be $W$-proper while $\Phi(X)$ fails to be $W$-proper. On the same fork, the maps $u,v$ are now taken in $W$. The new diagram has the two closed inclusions $X_a\to X\leftarrow X_b$. For $x\in X_b$ the category $x\backslash X_a$ is empty. The argument uses the exclusion of $W=\mathrm{Fl}(\mathrm{Cat})$. The marked proposition concludes that properness of $\Phi(X)$ forces $X$ to be empty. The qualification attached to the preceding sentence matters here (M2 below).

**246–248. Alternating $\Phi$ and $\Psi$.** Grothendieck forms a direct limit of the sequence starting $X,\Phi(X),\Psi\Phi(X),\ldots$ and a second one with the initial roles interchanged. He proposes $W(6\ \mathrm{bis})$ for maps between inductive systems indexed by the natural numbers. He uses the two subsequences to obtain both smoothness and properness. For $X$ in $\mathrm{Fib}_W S$, he gives $i_X:X\to\Phi_\infty(X)$ the finer class $W_S^f$. For arbitrary $X$ he gives it the class $W_S$. The slanted addition on 248 compares definitions of $C$ using one-sided infinite paths and finite paths. Several words in that addition remain doubtful.

**249–250. Comparing localizations.** The class $W_\omega''$ is the smallest class of arrows satisfying $W(1,2\ \mathrm{bis},3,6\ \mathrm{bis})$. An overwritten exponent obscures the comparison with an earlier class. The inclusion of perfect objects into fibred objects is said to induce equivalences after ordinary and fine localization. Grothendieck considers testing $W(4b)$ on split fibred objects, or testing it on perfect objects for $W_\omega''$ together with $W(6\ \mathrm{bis})$. His marginal comments relate both tests back to the full axiom.

**250–251. The adjunction calculation.** The proposed proposition identifies the two variants labelled $\mathrm{lc}$ and $\mathrm{lc}!$, gives adjunctions for the pairs written $(\rho,i)$ and $(\sigma,j)$, and deduces full faithfulness of $i,j$. The proof reduces to equality of $i_{\gamma(X)}$ and $\gamma(i_X)$ after localization. The two choices of $\gamma$ are $C$ and $\Phi_\infty$. Grothendieck singles out the second verification as unfinished.

**251–253. ⑥ Pullback.** The operation $X\mapsto X\times_S S'$ acts on smooth, proper and fibred categories over $S$ and respects the fine equivalences used to localize them. The passage to $\mathrm{lc}!$ raises a further compatibility question. One case Grothendieck knows is a universal $W$-equivalence, described as a $W$-fibration with $W$-aspherical fibres. He next discusses $W(4b)$ on fibred objects. A separate calculation on 253 assumes $f\in\mathrm{Fib}_W$ and an axiom whose character after 4 could be a dash, an *a* or a dot. The inset beginning « Oubli » gives a pullback formula for every $X$ when $p$ is $W$-proper.

**253–254. ⑦ Product.** Fibre product on smooth categories induces a product on $\mathrm{HOT}_W(S)$. Grothendieck gives its unit as the class of $\mathrm{id}_S$ and records associativity, commutativity and stability of the locally constant subcategory. The marginal formula replaces both arbitrary arguments by $\Phi_S$-images and describes when a smooth argument can be used directly.

**254–256. Multiplication by an object over $S$.** For fixed $X$, Grothendieck constructs $\mathrm{mult}_{X/S}$ using smooth representatives of the other argument. He compares $\mathrm{hot}_{S,W}(X\times_S Y)$ with the product of the two classes and gives the direction of the canonical map. He asks how much improves for fibred $X$, annotates the proposed isomorphism with additional hypotheses, and writes the distinct formulas $(**)$ and $(*)$. The section ends with compatibility of pullback and product.

**256–257. ⑧ The functors $f_!$.** Composition with $f:S'\to S$ gives a functor between slice categories. He lists three pairs of localizers and draws the induced diagram with six objects, including the two $\mathrm{lc}!$ objects. He states the left adjunction to $f^*$ under $W(1,2,3)$ and the corresponding adjunction for the version denoted by a small raised circle. A further hypothesis such as $W(4b)$ identifies the locally constant variants. He also considers the restricted question for a trivial $W$-fibration.

**257–258. Derivator conditions.** Grothendieck applies the adjunction to $\mathrm{Der}\,1,\mathrm{Der}\,4',\mathrm{Der}\,5'$. He gives a $W$-version of $\mathrm{Der}\,3b$ and relates $\mathrm{HOT}_W$-equivalence to invertibility of $\mathrm{hot}_W(f)$ and the strong saturation of $W$. A margin invokes products with arbitrary $T$. Under $W(4b)$ he states that the locally constant adjoints give quasi-inverse equivalences for $f\in W$. A second margin identifies $f_!f^*$ with $\mathrm{mult}_{S'/S}$ and refers back to the product calculations.

**258. ⑨ Change of base.** The top arrow of the square is $X'\to X$, labelled $q$. The bottom arrow is $S'\to S$, labelled $p$. After the square come two alternative hypotheses, one of smoothness and one of properness. The second map name can be read as $g$ or $q$. The extract ends there.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scan 243, model remark, high confidence.** The ordinary pushout $T\amalg_e e$ in this example is canonically isomorphic to $T$. The formal check uses the explicit choice $\beta=\mathrm{id}_e$, which determines the $e$ component of any cocone. The proposed identification of the total category $X$ in $\mathrm{Hot}$ has its own qualification, « à vérifier ! », in lines 12–16.

**M2 · Scans 245–246, model remark, low confidence about the scope of the proof.** Line 11 of 246 explicitly adds « Si on veut $u,v\in W$ » just before the deduction $X_0=\emptyset$. The separately marked statement below begins with arbitrary $X\in\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}$ and repeats only $W\ne\mathrm{Fl}(\mathrm{Cat})$. This leaves a question about which part of the preceding fibre argument is intended to cover the stated generality.

**M3 · Scans 254–256, model remark, high confidence about the two formulas.** In $(**)$ on 255 the fixed subscript is $X/S$ and the other multiplier is indexed by the class of $X$. In $(*)$ on 256 the fixed subscript is $Y/S$ and the argument of that multiplier is the class of $X$. This change explains the reference to $(*)$ beside the evaluation formula on manuscript page 200. It also locates the extra hypotheses in the discussion of the proposed product isomorphism.
