# Notes on the sheets

## Steps the pages themselves mark as unverified

- Scan 4, left of Thm 1. “S’il n’y a pas de partie infinie” adds a hypothesis beside the displayed identities. A later cancelled line has what may be “à vérifier”; its strokes are doubtful.
- Scan 5, left of the cancelled circular-play argument. “Supposons donc d’abord que …” introduces a temporary restriction. Two short struck lines and the end of the note are hard to recover.
- Scan 6, in the proof that the strategy wins. “admettons qu’elle est finie” occurs in the text. The slanted note reads “On suppose qu’il n’y a pas de partie infinie”.
- Scan 12, beside the comparison with the auxiliary game. “s’il n’y a pas de partie infinie” is written obliquely; the grammatical number of the ending is unclear.
- Scan 13. A question mark stands immediately before “Th 3” and the proposed equality. The adjacent note begins “Si C fini” and ends with the condition involving the union of the Cᵢ and “cf § 2”.
- Scan 15. The parenthesis after “ss-gpe” has a short final mark which may be a question mark or a numeral. The wording of the parenthesis remains uncertain.

Scan 9 has heading 4°), on acceptable strategies and positions. Scan 13 introduces the attempted recurrence for G(j). No explicit date, manuscript page count, or independently numbered typescript page is visible. The archive folios in the corner run from 1 through 15 on scans 2 through 16. On scan 16 the text and calculations are upside down relative to that folio. Its cancelled paragraph about Schubert cycles is in a different-looking hand; the writer is unidentified.
