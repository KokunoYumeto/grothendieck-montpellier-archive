# Mathematical notes · cote 31

The scan numbers below are those of the archive PDF. Quoted words are French. The notes describe what each sheet asserts, including claims left open or cancelled. In particular, the examples and bounds below retain the status the author gives them.

## What the pages do

**Local fields and a proposed bound · 70.** The corollary opens with a condition on the total fractions of the local ring of a closed subscheme at a geometric point. It defines a function of a point by taking a supremum over its closure. The two terms are the cohomological dimension of a residue field and twice a local dimension. The corollary compares the cohomological dimension of \(X\) with \(\varphi(X)\). The oblique note asks about a lower bound for the fraction field of a local ring.

**A spectral sequence and a working lemma · 71.** The sheet places \(H^*(G)\) alongside the terms \(H^p(X,R^qi_*G)\). The diagram at the left marks a diagonal cutoff. The boxed lemma at the bottom lists a bound on a residue field, a bound for certain \(Y\) in \(X\), and a strict inequality involving a closure and local fractions at a point different from \(x\). An arrow leads to \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell(X)\leq n\). The qualification on \(Y\) is doubtful.

**Descent and group actions · 72.** The typescript finishes a descent argument about \(Y=X/G\), affineness and finiteness of presentation or type. Corollary 2.4 states that the graph morphism for the action, and the orbit morphism belonging to a section, are closed immersions. Corollary 2.5 gives consequences for a monomorphism from a group of multiplicative type into an affine group, including existence and affineness of the quotient and a homogeneous principal bundle assertion.

**Two local assertions · 74.** Theorem \(1_n\) gives vanishing for an \(\ell\)-torsion sheaf on \(\operatorname{Spec} A[t]\) whose support satisfies a lower codimension bound. Theorem \(2_n\) concerns the cohomological dimension of the principal open \(X_f\) of a strictly local spectrum. A note obtains the second assertion by embedding the open as a closed subscheme. Lemma 1 goes in the other direction with a range of dimensions. It uses the closure of \(S\) in a relative projective line and cohomology with supports on its boundary \(T\).

**The end cases in Lemma 1 · 75.** A dimension and codimension estimate bounds the local dimension of the closure. The writer then separates \(n+1-m\geq2\), \(m=n+1\) and \(m=n\). The last case distinguishes the special fibre itself from a scheme with zero-dimensional closed fibre. The final line appeals to a dimension bound associated with a separably closed field. The exact notation for its one-variable extension is uncertain.

**Parameter algebras and direct images · 76–77.** Lemma 2 organizes the numbered assertions for rings essentially of finite type over a field. The author completes \(f\) to parameters, maps a parameter algebra to \(A\), and writes down a projection of principal opens. The target is a codimension cutoff for \(R^qf_*F\). Scan 77 tries to read the stalk calculation over strictly local rings of dimension \(i\). The qualifier in the warning at the foot of 76 is difficult to read.

**The affine dimension bound · 77–78.** In Lemma 3 the desired estimate is \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell(X)\leq\dim X\) for an affine scheme in the stated finite-type setting over a separably closed field. The proof invokes normalization, projection from an affine space and induction on dimension. The spectral-sequence calculation ends with \(p+q\leq n+1\). Scan 78 then proposes to extend the result to schemes over a noetherian base of dimension at most one. Two marginal notes call for rearranging the proof.

**Special and generic parts · 79–80.** The author writes \(V=X_f\), \(U=X_{f\pi}=V_\pi\) and \(Z=V-U\). The localization sequence separates cohomology on \(U\) from cohomology with supports on \(Z\). The former is estimated using the fraction field of the base. For the latter a spectral sequence uses the sheaves \(\underline H_Z^q(F)\). Scan 80 expresses the needed vanishing at a geometric point in terms of a punctured strictly local scheme and treats the low values of \(q\) separately.

**A bound over a punctured base · 80–81.** For \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}A\) and its punctured open \(Y'\), the proposed affine bound uses the larger of \(\dim X_0\) and \(\dim X'+1\). A second note proposes a different expression for nonaffine \(X\). On 81 the author embeds \(X\) in a polynomial ambient scheme, separates \(X=X_0\) from \(X\ne X_0\), and relates the bound to ambient codimension. The induction on the relative number of variables reaches a projection for \(r\geq2\) and ends in dashes.

**A pointwise proposal and its counterexample · 82.** The question defines \(n\) using the supremum of \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell(k(x))+\dim\underline O_{X,x}\) for \(X\) noetherian affine. The proposed inequality is \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell(X)\leq n\). The author then gives the ring of integers of a totally imaginary number field, with \(n=2\) and \(\operatorname{cd}(X)=3\) as written. The final \(cd\) has no visible subscript.

**Affine schemes over the integers · 83.** The author studies the higher direct images under the map to \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\). The two stalk bounds distinguish a generic point from a closed point. Three ranges for \(q\) give the pattern of entries in the drawn \((p,q)\) plane. The boxed conclusion is \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell(X)\leq\dim X+1\). The final formulation requires \(\ell\) to be invertible and gives alternatives involving \(\ell\ne2\) or the generic fibre over a totally imaginary field.

**Two cancelled group-scheme theorems · 84.** Theorem 3.1 concerns vanishing of Hochschild cohomology for a group of multiplicative type on an affine base. The argument uses the complex of modules under a flat change of base and reduction to a diagonalizable group. The degree condition is typed with a gap. Theorem 3.2 treats two group homomorphisms that agree after reduction by a square-zero ideal. Its conclusion writes one as an inner conjugate of the other and adds that the conjugating element reduces to the identity. Group letters have been overwritten in several places.

**Conjectures for affine schemes · 85.** For a “bon” integral affine scheme, the first conjecture compares cohomological dimension with that of the generic field when \(\ell\) is invertible. The second considers \(\ell\) neither invertible nor nilpotent. An additional term uses \(V(\ell)\) and a numerical invariant of characteristic-\(\ell\) fields defined by \(\ell^{\delta(k)}=[k:k^\ell]\). The coefficient in the written bound is \(+3\). A cancelled passage attempts \(+1\) under an additional hypothesis. Item 4 concerns an affine open of a henselian local scheme and a bound combining a ring dimension, the field invariant and a residue-field cohomological dimension. The prose specifying the residue data is uncertain.

**Fibrewise schematic density · 86.** The cancelled Corollary 4.6 gives conditions under which fibrewise schematic density implies schematic density of an open in a flat \(S\)-scheme. For the finite-presentation case it writes the base algebra as a directed limit, descends the scheme and its open to a finite stage, and describes a constructible locus using associated points. It then passes to a later index and invokes 4.4. The sentence restricting the use of 4.4 ends at the paper edge after *que dans le*.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1a · scan 78 · high confidence for the arithmetic.** The sheet bounds \(p\) by \(n-(q-1)\). Adding \(q\) to this expression gives \(n+1\). I checked the polynomial identity exactly.
- **R1b · scan 80 · high confidence for the arithmetic.** For the live expression \((n-1)-(q-1)\), adding \(q\) gives \(n\). I checked this identity separately. The two computations concern only the literal expressions and do not verify the vanishing inputs.
- **Scans 72 and 84 · medium confidence.** Both typescripts use the diagonalizable case to pass to a statement about groups of multiplicative type. One does so in an argument about quotients and descent, the other in a proof about cohomology. The leaves themselves give the references, but no common title or continuous foliation establishes their original relationship.
- **Scans 82 and 85 · medium confidence.** The first question takes a supremum over all points and explicitly adds local dimension. In the invertible case on 85 the displayed right member uses the generic field alone, and “bon” qualifies the scheme. These are visibly different formulations. The meaning of “bon” is left open here.
