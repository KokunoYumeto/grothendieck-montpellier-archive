# Mathematical notes on cote 64, scans 131–141 and 143–144

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scan 131.** A complex elliptic curve gives a complex tangent line at the origin and a discrete lattice of rank two in it. The lattice recovers both the underlying real vector space by extension of scalars and the real Lie group by taking the quotient. The complex structure on the tangent space descends to that quotient.

**U2 · Scans 131–132.** The real-part map identifies a suitable complex subspace \(L\subset V_{\mathbb C}\) with \(V\) as a real vector space. The author transports the conjugate complex structure via this map. In the reverse construction the kernel of \(x+iy\mapsto x+uy\) consists of \(x+iu(x)\). The upper margin of 132 explains the choice by the intended holomorphic dependence of the moduli parameters.

**U3 · Scans 132–134.** A compact circle subgroup acts with two eigenspaces on the complexification. The choice of an orientation of the circle distinguishes an element of order four, a character, and its eigenspace. Scan 133 specifies the ranks in which an orientation of \(V\) also determines the choice. Scan 134 distinguishes the complex-linear real-part identification from the question of which line to attach to a complex structure under the earlier convention.

**U4 · Scans 134–135.** In real dimension two, the candidate subspaces are points of a complex projective line. The condition \(L\cap V=0\) removes its real points. The two components of the complement are related to orientations. A marginal objection on 135 concerns the proposed assignment of an individual component.

**U5 · Scans 135–136.** The lattice, an orientation and a point of the chosen component give elliptic-curve data. A Torelli rigidification is an oriented integral basis. It identifies the parameter component with the upper half-plane \(\mathscr D\) using \(L_z=\mathbb C(z,1)\). A margin note considers the line with the two coordinates interchanged.

**U6 · Scan 137.** Writing \(z=x+iy\), the author computes the inverse images of \(e_1,e_2\) under the real-part map. Their ratio is \(-x+iy\). The displayed conjugation gives \(e_2/e_1=-z\), and the inserted formula (24) expresses \(z\) as the negative quotient of the periods in the chosen basis.

**U7 · Scans 137–138.** Composing the marking on the right by a matrix changes the two periods by its columns. Replacing the matrix with its inverse gives the left action. The final unstruck (25bis) is \(z'=(az+b)/(cz+d)\), followed by the underlined “victoire”. The sequence includes a cancelled calculation and a distinct cancelled marginal formula.

**U8 · Scans 138–139.** The choice of period parameter is compared with the ratio \(\tau=z_2/z_1\). Scan 139 states a universal-cover theorem for analytic moduli with Torelli rigidification. The displayed fibre is \(E_z=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z\cdot1+\mathbb Z\cdot(-\bar z))\). It also gives the fractional-linear action. The long margin note asks how the chosen basis is oriented and what remains to check about the family.

**U9 · Scan 140.** Corollary 1 identifies the fundamental group for the chosen covering with the integral special linear group. Corollary 2 gives the action of isotopy classes of automorphisms of a pointed, oriented surface of genus one on \(H\). Corollary 3 gives an analogous homomorphism for a “groupe à lacets” of type \((1,1)\) using its abelianization. The author asks for the outer action to be made explicit.

**U10 · Scan 141.** Along the zero section of the universal marked curve, the tangent lines are described as a real vector bundle with the standard action on the plane. Passing to the punctured plane gives the associated principal bundle topologically. The page chooses the complex exponential for the universal cover of the punctured fibre.

**U11 · Scans 143–144.** The numbering restarts. Over a complex analytic base, the lattice becomes a locally constant sheaf and the real vector bundle acquires the structure of a complex line bundle. A local lattice basis has holomorphic period ratio \(\tau\), with \(z_2=\tau z_1\) and \(\tau(s)\notin\mathbb R\). A Torelli structure trivializes the integral local system. The author calls the marked category rigid and stops midway through “et l’ens des”.

## Statements left without proof

- **P1 · Scan 133.** “c’est automatique si \(\mathrm{rg}_{\mathbb R}V=2\)” refers to the two eigenspaces. In the same real rank, the invariant metric “est unique à scalaire multiplicatif près”. Both assertions are stated on this leaf.
- **P2 · Scans 135–136.** He assigns the two components to orientations “(par Stokes)” and records the classification of marked curves “en corr. biunivoque avec l’ens des pts de \(\mathscr D\)”. No separate argument for the orientation correspondence is given here.
- **P3 · Scan 139.** The family “est un revêtement universel de \(M_{1,1}^{\mathrm{an}}\)”. The change-of-basis computation supplies the displayed action. The analytic and universal properties receive no full demonstration on these leaves.
- **P4 · Scan 140.** “Utilisant les grands théorèmes” precedes Corollary 2, where the natural homomorphism “est un isomorphisme”. Corollary 3 concludes “est iso.” No specific comparison theorem or separate proof of either corollary is given.
- **P5 · Scan 141.** “Pour en trouver un revêtement universel, il suffit de choisir un revêtement universel de” the punctured fibre. The exponential is written down. The passage from the fibre to the bundle is left implicit.
- **P6 · Scan 144.** The comment on rigidity is “pas d’autom. \(\ne\mathrm{id}\) d’un objet”. The author gives the marked basis immediately beforehand. No proof follows in the assigned portion.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scan 137, model, high confidence.** Substitution of the two displayed \(\lambda\)'s into \(\lambda\mapsto(\operatorname{Re}(\lambda z),\operatorname{Re}\lambda)\) gives \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Their ratio and its conjugate agree with the two consecutive lines. The calculation distinguishes the two ratios before any change of marking.
- **R2 · Scan 138, model, high confidence for the algebra.** Starting with the inserted (24) on 137 and the two unstruck period-change equations gives \((dz-b)/(a-cz)\) for right composition. Substituting the inverse matrix yields the unstruck left-action formula (25bis). This checks the algebra from those specified inputs. The bars and sign in the final fraction of (25) still have unresolved pen strokes.
- **R3 · Scans 137 and 139, model, low confidence about the author’s final convention.** Reading question at 139, TeX line 4. The frozen first candidates give \(z=-\overline{z_2/z_1}\). The alternative without the bars gives \(z=-z_2/z_1\), as in the addition (24) on 137. The raised strokes on 139 are overworked. The two expressions differ whenever the ratio is nonreal. The lattice in the theorem has its own separate, legible bar.
- **R4 · Scans 135 and 139, model, high confidence for the example.** Taking \(z=i\) in the unstruck equality \(e_2/e_1=-z\) on 137 gives \(-i\). The basis \((1,-i)\) then has negative real determinant in the usual orientation of \(\mathbb C\). This gives a concrete instance of the basis called “inverse” in the margin.
- **R5 · Scan 144, model, high confidence.** Two periods in one complex line that are independent over the reals have nonreal quotient. This explains why the note \(\tau(s)\in\mathbb C\smallsetminus\mathbb R\) belongs next to the change of local generator in (3 bis).
