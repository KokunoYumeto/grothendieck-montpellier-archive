# Mathematical notes · Cote 21, scans 002 and 004–016

## What the pages do

**U01 · Proposition 2.1, scan 004.** A family G in an abelian category tests a right-exact functor for vanishing when every object has a finite filtration whose factors are quotients of members of G. The second part tests the vanishing of a bounded-above cohomological functor in degrees greater than n. The proof descends through the degrees.

**U02 · Corollaire 2.2, scan 004.** Passing to the opposite category gives the corresponding vanishing statement below n for a contravariant cohomological functor. The author comments on whether to repeat the first part as well.

**U03 · Corollaire 2.3, scans 004–005.** For noetherian objects and a generating family, the proof chooses a maximal subobject with the required filtration. A nonzero map to the quotient would enlarge that subobject, giving the contradiction at the top of scan 005.

**U04 · Exemple 2.4, scan 005.** For finite modules annihilated by a power of I, the support of one test object, or the union of the supports of a family, is compared with Y. The proof uses a component missed by the support for necessity and a formula for associated primes of Hom for sufficiency.

**U05 · Exemple 2.5, scans 005–006.** On a noetherian scheme with an ample sheaf, negative twists of coherent sheaves whose supports cover Y provide test objects. Nonzero sheaf Hom on an affine open and sections after twisting are the two steps invoked. The affine discussion is recovered with the indicated choice of ample sheaf.

**U06 · An additive functor and a tensor object, scan 006.** T(F) acquires an A-action from endomorphisms of F. For a right A-object P of the target abelian category, the page defines a candidate tensor with a left module M by a representing property in the variable Q. The map marked (*) has source T(A) tensored with M and target T(M).

**U07 · Existence, exactness and the dual notation, scans 007–008.** The text describes exact sequences for the tensor construction, the free module on one generator, and compatibility with inductive limits and sums. It gives a cokernel construction from a matrix of relations. Passing to an opposite category introduces the Hom version and its representing identity.

**U08 · Proposition 2.6, scan 008.** The map (*) is obtained from the map sending M into Hom(T(A),T(M)). It is functorial and is an isomorphism for all M precisely when T is right exact. The proof compares right-exact functors first on A, then on finite free modules and their cokernels.

**U09 · Corollaire 2.7, scan 008.** Evaluation at A identifies the indicated category of right-exact functors with the category of A-objects in the target. Tensoring with H gives the quasi-inverse.

**U10 · Remarque 2.8 and its dual, scans 008–009.** The restriction to finite modules can be dropped under the stated hypotheses on sums, with preservation of direct sums added to right exactness. For a contravariant T the comparison map goes from T(M) to Hom over A from M to T(A). The discussion of arbitrary modules now calls for products.

**U11 · Restriction to levels, scan 009.** Write Aₙ = A/Iⁿ⁺¹. The category of finite modules killed by a power of I is presented as a filtered union of full subcategories. Restricting T to a level gives a map from T(Aₙ) tensored with M to T(M). For a fixed M the compatible maps give a projective system and, when the limit exists, a comparison from its limit.

**U12 · Proposition 2.9 and Corollaire 2.10, scan 010.** In the contravariant case the maps have source T(M). Under the exactness condition on countable filtered colimits in the target, the construction produces H from the direct limit of T(Aₙ). The proposition heading is left to summarize the argument. The corollary identifies left-exact functors with the A-objects described as locally killed by powers of I.

**U13 · Remarque 2.11, scans 010–011.** Without a general existence assumption on colimits, the data are described by an inductive system (Hₙ). Its n-th term is killed by Iⁿ⁺¹. The map to Hₘ for m ≥ n identifies Hₙ with the corresponding annihilated subobject of Hₘ.

**U14 · Corollaire 2.12, scan 011.** The page compares three conditions in degrees below n: vanishing of the functor, of its objects Hⁱ obtained at the quotient levels, and of the values on a finite-type family whose supports cover Y. The induction uses left exactness of the next functor and the Hom description of 2.9.

**U15 · Corollaire 2.13, scan 011.** At the first remaining degree the displayed formula identifies Tⁿ(M) with Hom over A from M to Hⁿ. The author proposes folding this into the statement and induction of 2.12. The live and cancelled exponent strokes are distinguished in the transcription.

**U16 · Corollaire 2.14, scan 012.** Under a componentwise support test for the nullity of T(M), the associated points of T(M) are described by intersecting the support of M with the selected set S of generic points. The proof writes T as Hom into H and tests the associated points of H with cyclic modules attached to points.

**U17 · Proposition 2.15, scans 012 and 014.** For H supported on V(I) in the stated torsion sense, exactness of Hom into H on the small category detects injectivity as an A-module. The proof starts with maps out of submodules of A. The intersection attributed to Krull permits the extension problem to be taken into a quotient killed by a power of I.

**U18 · Other Corollaire 2.14, scan 013.** The three darker lines on the reverse define the submodule killed elementwise by powers of I. The conditional sentence beginning “Si M est injectif” stops there. It supplies neither conclusion nor proof on this face.

**U19 · Corollaire 2.16, scan 014.** The submodule of an injective K consisting of elements killed by powers of I is again injective. On the test category, maps into that submodule agree with maps into K. The proof invokes 2.15.

**U20 · Remarques 2.17, scan 014.** A left-exact contravariant functor on coherent modules on a noetherian prescheme with the specified support is represented by a quasi-coherent module. The paragraph invokes ind-representability and describes the proposed representing object in terms of a limit. It ends by questioning the need for this generality in the chapter.

**U21 · Cover and Définition 1, scans 015–016.** The live cover title refers to depth at least two for sheaves of sets. The manuscript defines two properties relative to a closed part Y by injectivity or bijectivity of the restriction to its open complement followed by direct image. It gives the corresponding condition on sections over each open V and marks the surjectivity case for attention.

**U22 · Local spaces and Définition 2, scan 016.** An intervening paragraph specifies a noetherian space with unique generic points for its irreducible closed parts and with one closed point. It calls for localisation notation for both the space and the sheaf. Définition 2 applies the earlier conditions to the closed point. The sideways note refers to sections on the whole and on the punctured space.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · Scans 009–010 · High confidence.** For m ≥ n the quotient maps run from Aₘ to Aₙ. Changing the variance of T reverses their effect on the system. This accounts for the left-pointing limit arrows on scan 009 and the right-pointing ones on scan 010.

**R02 · Scan 011 · Low confidence about the cancelled material.** The live degree in the display of 2.13 reads n on both sides. The neighbouring proof has three visible additions of −1. That comparison suggests a possible source for the hatching after n, but cannot identify the hatching itself.

**R03 · Scan 014 · Medium confidence.** The last paragraph begins with coherent modules supported in Y, then identifies Ind(C) with “tous les Modules quasi-cohérents sur X”. If C still carries the stated support condition and Y is proper, its filtered colimits also have zero stalks outside Y. The later phrase would need the support condition or the earlier special case Y = X.

The surrounding restriction concerns F, but the ink has only one clear arm. The formula does not provide grounds for changing that letter.
