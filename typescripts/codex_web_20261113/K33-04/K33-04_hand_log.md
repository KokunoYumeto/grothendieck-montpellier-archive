# Hand log · cote 33, scans 98–110

Locations count manuscript lines from the top, including displays. The entries describe the cited examples. A letter in a different position was read independently.

## Scan 98

The medial strokes in “immersion” compress almost to a straight horizontal line. The short repeat before “fermée” ends early. The bare high dash with a separate lower point is the shriek in the display. The doubled uprights on R and L are visible even on the bottom line, where one initial is overwritten. These features hold at the cited places.

## Scan 99

The Latin capitals in the flowing French hand and the penned-in script capitals of this typescript belong to different contexts. On this leaf H has a lower left loop, G has a long descender, and S has a pinched closed loop at the upper right. The doubled H in the theorem and in its introductory paragraph instead has typewriter serifs. The display clearly separates two impressions of S; the line just above does not.

## Scan 100

His small degree dots often sit barely below the baseline, especially after L and M. The two Ms near the foot each enclose a straight L-shaped stroke missing in the final plain M. The two rightward strokes at the foot differ at their tips. A high stroke above the crossed “relation” introduces “famille”.

## Scan 101

The mathematical type on this reused sheet is separate from that on scan 99. Its handwritten sigma ends in a conspicuously flat rightward stroke. The tilde, when present, is above it. The bottom serif of the I under the sum nearly touches the equals sign on the following line. Both horizontal strokes of the latter remain visible.

## Scan 102

The lower-left question must be read upwards along a slight slant. The second line of the note ends in the two crossed stems of “contexte”. In the heading of the first formula the drawn T is visibly doubled, whereas the opening superscript L is single. The top edge of the formula box approaches the baseline of “naïvement”.

## Scan 103

The same typewriter form of underlined p can stand at two different heights. At the very top, p in the length is level with the tilded S. The instance in Theorem 3.8 has a lowered p balanced by a raised f. In the proof several type strikes lose their lower halves, especially equals before the sum.

## Scan 104

The loops of script H inside the derived Hom expressions contrast with the elementary capital H in the ordinary Hom expressions on the very next lines. A long curved arrow at left runs upwards from the second group to the first. Its head is above the initial R. The two notes need separate rotations to put their baselines level.

## Scan 105

This typescript contrasts a level bar made by the machine and the freer, sometimes very dark, penned wave over S. Sigma has a closed foot and straight top, unlike the open-loop phi on the epimorphism. The final inclusion is retraced but its interior stays open. No doubtful letter was required after inspection of these places.

## Scan 106

Two styles of Hom occur together. The high detached tick following an entire Hom or K is a degree dot, not a prime on the base change. The letters of the two little pullback maps require checking against the square. The marginal paragraph rises towards the right and has been read after rotation.

## Scan 107

The inclusions, the summation sign and the circle over the x are individually visible in the typescript. Underlines in p and q follow the type, and the residue-field bars differ from the free wavy stroke over S on scan 105. Read after a half-turn.

## Scan 108

Above-line words and an arrow perform different tasks. The brace over des op. takes the explanatory words back to existence. The long curved line by the functors is a transposition. Boxes enclosing strokes through the abandoned first sentence cross words, whereas the double short rule merely separates the sections.

## Scan 109

A handwritten caret can sit close to a serif. Here the completion marks vary in inclination and their two strokes are plainly separate from the type. The summation signs vary in the length of the foot. The cross in both tensor signs is a keyed x with serifs and a pen circle.

## Scan 110

In the first paragraph the returning loop of the word résulte sits above a cancellation. In the two braces, inspect each sign at the top right of D; the second brace has an unusually hooked stroke on the second row. The very last pair has an arrowhead, unlike the joins in the brace.

## Comparisons during the second reading

- **98 and 106, extraordinary operations.** The high narrow stroke of a shriek has a dot below it. Its combination with the following baseline hyphen can resemble a superscript minus and one. The dot is visible in the first N.B. on 98. Bent lines beginning below an expression are annotation leaders.
- **99 and 101, membership.** The free rounded E's on 99 change size from the inline variables to the script G. The compact type epsilon on 101 has the same two lobes after sigma and below the sum. These two sheets use different graphic forms for membership.
- **100 and 102, the ends of short arrows.** The direction of the extra two pen strokes matters more than their size. They are discernible in the first bottom resolution on 100 and the inserted map on 102. The second bottom join on 100 has no separate head.
- **103 and 105, prime ideals.** The stem of the underlined keyed p descends beneath its line even when the letter itself is at the height of the preceding S. On 103 the top length has p at normal height; compare the genuinely lowered p in the parallel formula on 105.
- **104, 106 and 110, functor initials.** The double stem on the R and L survives even in a small exponent. The H starting the sheaf Hom has an extra loop in the pen hand. The straight-stemmed H in ordinary Hom lacks this loop. The curved H of scan 99 is separately drawn in spaces left by the typist. These are local comparisons; the S in the map on 99 has less decisive second inking.
- **105, 107, 109, typescript supplements.** The circle around the keyed x does not erase the x. A wedge over R or S on 109 has two short straight sides, unlike the wavy single stroke over S on 103 and 105. Raised specks and isolated double points do not recur uniformly. They are separately marked where the image does not distinguish ink, transfer or paper marks.
- **108 and 110, category signs.** D has one main stem. The cross of a superscript plus sometimes curls back, especially in the second brace on 110. The repeated minus has one stroke. A long join has no visible point except in the far lower right entry on 110. The horizontal marks through the heading and prose on 108 are cancellations, apart from the short underline below the heading.
