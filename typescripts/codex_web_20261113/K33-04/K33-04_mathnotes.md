## What the pages do

**The immersion case and exchange by duality (98).** A preceding argument ends with the constant module and a reduction to \(F=A_Y\). Part b reduces an immersion to a closed immersion. The two lines of sheaf Hom calculations compare extraordinary inverse image of a derived Hom with derived Hom after inverse image. The author labels the intermediate complexes “flasque”, “donc \(f^!\)-acyclique” and “injectif”. After a rule, two formulas put duality on opposite sides of a direct or inverse image. The small functor initials under correction and the second operation in the heading require the apparatus.

**Operators on a tangent space (99, typed p. 72).** The translated kernel \(\widetilde F_x\) supplies operators on \(T_x\). Theorem 4.6.6 describes their algebra under composition and gives the kernel of the homomorphism from the \(J\)'s. An inner product identifies \(T_x\) with its dual; the text uses this to give the tensor square of \(A_x^p\) an algebra structure. Smooth maps on \(S_x\) with values there become matrices of smooth functions. Formula (4.6.7) gives the symbol applied to \(\mathbb H_xJ\) by adding \(\xi(x)\) to an integral over \(T_x\).

**Flat resolutions and the tensor product (100).** Section 1 of the circled sheet 26 defines a flat Module by exactness of tensoring. It gives a criterion using sheaf Hom into injectives and names the \(A_U\) as sources of flat covers. Bounded-above complexes are represented by flat ones. Passing from homotopy classes to the quotient by quasi-isomorphisms permits the derived tensor product to be calculated with the ordinary tensor product of two flat resolutions.

**Hyper-Tor and flat change of base (102).** The cohomology sheaves of the derived tensor product receive the name “hypertor” and the index \(i\). Exact triangles give long exact sequences. The boxed identity compares pullback of a tensor product with the tensor product of the two pullbacks. Preservation of quasi-isomorphisms and of flats enter separately. Passage to the fibres is the application, beside a marginal question about general topoi.

**Global hyper-Tor and the formal identities (104).** Applying derived global sections gives a global variant, qualified in the margin by cohomological dimension. The list then gives associativity, commutativity with a sign warning, and tensor–Hom identities. The three groups of comparisons distinguish sheaf Hom, derived ordinary Hom, and ordinary Hom in the derived category. A second marginal note specifies a lower bound on \(K^\bullet\) while allowing \(L_\bullet\) to be arbitrary.

**Pullback without flatness and adjunction (104, 106).** The bottom of 104 constructs derived pullback through the bounded-above flat complexes and repeats its tensor identity. The transitivity formula promised at the break is written at the top of 106. A two-object sketch and a boxed adjunction precede the computation in degree zero with \(K^\bullet\) injective and \(L_\bullet\) flat. The following sheaf Hom comparison, its corollary for ordinary derived Hom, and the calculation with \(X'\), \(Y'\) develop the localized form of the argument.

**Regularity assumptions and the Künneth heading (108).** The author explains which general formulas have been treated and sets aside variants involving regularity or finite Tor- or Ext-dimension. Section 2 brings in \(!\) and the derived tensor. The proposed theorem about a compactifiable morphism is crossed out. The live paragraph turns to constant coefficient rings \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z=A\) and the effect of derived tensor on boundedness when one starts in degree zero.

**Domains of the three functors (108, 110).** “Il faut” continues into the opening of 110. The author considers arguments beyond \(D^+\), discusses cohomological dimension for direct image, and appeals to proper base change for direct image with proper support. The three groups at the foot list source and target categories for \(\mathbb R_! f_*\), \(\mathbb R f_*\) and \(\mathbb L f^*\). The two superscripts in the second row of the ordinary-image group are graphically less secure than the bounds in the first group. The last bounded pullback pair stands separately at the right.

**The Tor expression for multiplicity (109, typed p. 3.2).** A proof already in progress treats a finite \(R\)-module \(S\) by a finite free resolution, additivity of multiplicity, and an alternating sum of Tor lengths. It then passes to completions for the general case. Corollary 3.3 gives two expressions for \(r(S/R)\) under a weak homological dimension hypothesis on \(S^f\).

**Integrality in a finite extension (109).** Lemma 3.4 concerns local domains with \(S\) finite over integrally closed \(R\) and simple residue field extension. Its proof lifts a generator of the residue field, passes through \(R[\alpha]\), and expresses \(e(S/R)\) as an integral multiple of \(e(R[\alpha]/R)\).

**Primes of full dimension and additivity (107, typed p. 3.3).** Lemma 3.5 identifies primes giving a quotient of full dimension by contraction to zero in \(R\), under its existence and integral-closure assumptions. Its proof invokes going down. Lemma 3.6 gives the multiplicity of a finite module as a sum of local lengths weighted by the multiplicities of full-dimensional components.

**Integrality for a local algebra (107, 105, 103).** Proposition 3.7 starts on 107 with the normal closure of the quotient field extension, its separable part, and a decomposition ring \(D\). On 105 the proof localizes \(D\otimes_R S\) at the kernel of the residue map and identifies the multiplicity with a sum over primes contracting to zero. It establishes finiteness of the relevant \(D\)-algebras. The first sentence of 103 invokes Lemma 3.4 to finish the integrality argument.

**The multiplicity sum and separability (103, typed p. 3.5).** Theorem 3.8 writes \(r(S/R)\) as the sum of the lengths \(L(S^f_{\underline p})\) times \(e(S^f/\underline p\mid S)\) for the indicated minimal primes. It asserts that the lengths all equal one exactly when \(K/k\) is separably algebraic. The proof singles out the kernel of \(S^f\to S\), compares its length with \([K:k]_i\), and uses the localizations of \(K\otimes_k K\) in the converse.

**Inertia and a new proposition (101, typed p. 3.6).** Corollary 3.9 identifies \(r(S/R)\) with both the number of the indicated minimal primes and the order of the inertia group in the Galois case. The proof passes to the inertial ring \(U\), identifies the primes with kernels indexed by the inertia group, and obtains multiplicity one for each quotient. Proposition 3.10 begins with a finite algebraic field extension and stops mid-sentence at “and \(S\) the”.
