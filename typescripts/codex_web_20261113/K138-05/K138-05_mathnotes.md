# Mathematical notes · cote 138, scans 060–073

The scan numbers below refer to the supplied images. Quoted words come from the transcription. Bracketed comments describe their position on the page. A crossed passage is identified where its status affects the interpretation.

## What the pages do

**060–061 · Automorphisms of a groupoid.** The cancelled opening sets $C=(C_0,C_1)$ and $G=\operatorname{Aut}(C)$ and distinguishes automorphisms fixing the objects. The sequences relate the object action, the group at a chosen object $a$, and its automorphism group. A subsequent set display imposes two identity conditions. The last construction takes $T_x=\operatorname{Isom}(a,x)$ as a right $\pi_a$-torsor and names its commutant.

**061–062 · Two corollaries and a kernel.** The first corollary gives tests involving components, the object map and the automorphism groups of representatives. The NB retains (a) and (c) for equivalence. The second asks when a prescribed $f_0$ comes from an isomorphism. The specialization $C=C'$ and the surjectivity statement lead into the product over objects other than $a$ at the top of 062. Earlier words and the product's discarded factor are retained in the source.

**062–063 · A graph from polygonal data.** A polygonal structure on $I$ is followed by a graph $K(I)$ with vertices $o,o'$ and an edge for each element of $I$. The drawing shows three labelled arcs. The small addition describes an involution on the vertices and edges and its relation to $G$. The author passes to the fundamental groupoid $C(I)$, replaces “commutant” with “normalisateur de $G$”, and identifies a canonical element $\tau$ on 063.

**064 · A diagram and its products.** The arrows lead from two objects $E^+,E^-$ to three $D$ objects and three $S$ objects. The sheet gives three four-factor products defining the positive rotations, their product at $E^+$, three quotient identifications for the $S_i$, and three negative rotations. Several small indices and a superscript are overwritten. The sheet has no explanatory prose.

**065–066 · Numerical restrictions on finite subgroups.** The heading specifies the tame case. Scan 066 starts from a genus identity and rewrites the contribution of the indices $d_i$. Setting $g'=0$ gives a positive expression for $2/N$. The case split is by the cardinality of $I$. For two indices the sheet gives $d_1=d_2=N$. With three indices it derives reciprocal inequalities and tabulates $(2,2,p),(2,3,3),(2,3,4),(2,3,5)$ with their orders. It begins the case of four or more. The pages do not assign group names to the four triples.

**068 · Four or more indices and two discarded case analyses.** Two crossed computations at the top specialize reciprocal inequalities. The following lines bound two pairs of reciprocals and conclude “Impossible !”. The large cancelled draft below treats $(N,N)$, splitting off the $p$-part of $N$ in the characteristic-$p$ discussion. It investigates a group in a Borel subgroup and then treats $(N,2,2)$ via a degree-two extension and a square of curves. The bottom line returns to the genus of $X'$. The slanted annotation and the long horizontal cancellations make the exact scope of several assertions uncertain.

**067 and 069 · University notices.** Each is a thesis-defence announcement with a candidate, subject, abstract, date, room or institution, supervisor, jury and signature. The first concerns Michèle AURELLY and Hammett correlations, the second Jean-Luc PAYEN and surface treatment. The two sheets are preserved in their physical positions in the transcription.

**070–071 · Orientations and finite punctures.** The title sheet introduces a group-theoretic treatment of compact surfaces with operators. The next sheet begins “Paradigme des courbes algébriques”. It chooses a two-element set $\omega$, forms $T$ using the displayed operation over $\mathbb Z/2$, and describes an $\omega$-orientation in terms of the orientations of $X$ and a constant sheaf. Each component is assumed to arise from a compact surface by removing a finite set.

**071–073 · Compactification and marked subsets.** The pair $(\hat X_i,S_i)$ is asserted to be uniquely determined up to unique isomorphism, and the compactified components are put together in a disjoint union. The argument reformulates the category using compact components with a discrete set of punctures, then adds a second discrete subset $J$, disjoint from $S$. The last sheet announces a passage to isotopy categories and group-theoretic descriptions. It discusses absorbing the two sets into $I$ and remembering $J\subset I$, while retaining their different rôles in the formulation. The extract ends mid-sentence on “mais”.

## Statements left without proof

Their conditions are written out, with no proof of the corollaries on these sheets.
- **060, foot.** “$\pi_a$-torseur à droite” and “et son commutant … $\operatorname{Aut}(x)$” give the torsor and the group at the other end. The commutant identification is stated here.
- **062, first display.** “On trouve” introduces the product formula for $\operatorname{Aut}^0(C,a)$ without a derivation on that page.
- **062–063.** “qui est l'identité sur $I$, et échange $o,o'$, commutant à $G$”, “en opérant transitivement” and “On a un élément canonique $\tau$”. The statements accompany the construction. The announced normalizer is not computed in this extract.
- **064.** The isolated identity $\rho_2^+\rho_1^+\rho_0^+=\mathrm{id}_{E^+}$ and the three displays identifying $S_i$ with quotients of $E^+$ and $E^-$ are given alongside the diagram.
- **066, upper half · A66.** The boxed relation beginning $2-2g'=N[(2-2g)-\cdots]$ follows the rearrangement of an initial genus identity whose geometric justification is assumed. “Si $g'=0$, on trouve $g=0$” is used in the next box.
- **068, cancelled lower draft.** “$G\simeq\mu_N(k)\simeq\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$”, “extension … résoluble”, “abéliens” and “$G\subset\mathrm{Borel}$” occur in the discussion of $(N,N)$. The second case says the new $X'/Y'$ “n'a plus que 2 pts de ramification”. These are claims in an abandoned draft, with some hypotheses hard to read.
- **071–072.** “On sait que $(\hat X_i,S_i)$ est déterminé à isom unique près par $X_i$” and “Chaque $\hat X_i$ est automatiquement $\omega$-orienté”. A parenthetic reference to Čech accompanies the first assertion. The two subsequent claims that the category “devient équivalente” or “est équivalente” are stated without a construction of inverse functors here.
- **073.** “on est ramené au cas connexe” and “on est ramené au cas où $J=\varnothing$”. The bracketed sentence indicates how the latter reduction is to remember a subset of $I$.

## Remarks of the model

- **R66 · Model, high confidence.** Substituting the three fixed triples on 066 into its boxed relation gives $2/N=1/6,1/12,1/30$, hence $N=12,24,60$ in the written order. I checked these three substitutions using exact fractions. This checks the tabulated arithmetic under A66.
- **R68 · Model, high confidence about the arithmetic, low confidence about the reading.** With $p=q=2$, each reciprocal is $1/2$ and their sum satisfies $\geq1$. **R68-strict.** The strict conclusion available one expression earlier is enough for the intended contradiction. **Reading question on scan 068, page-file line 13.** After “donc”, the frozen sign joining the fractions is $+$. The close crop also permits a short minus with a hooked endpoint. The two-fraction calculation above is conditional on the sum reading. The final transcription preserves the hashed reading.
- **R73 · Model, medium confidence.** The words “partie $J$ de $I$” account for how one of the formerly separate sets can remain distinguished in the reformulation. For the set-level reading, forming $I=S\cup J$ and recovering $S=I\setminus J$ worked for all 81 ordered pairs of disjoint subsets of a four-element set. The cut-off sentence leaves open how the author intended to use that distinction next.
