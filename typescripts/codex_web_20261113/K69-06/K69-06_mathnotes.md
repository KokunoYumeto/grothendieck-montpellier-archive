# Mathematical notes on cote 69, scans 019–033

## What the pages do

**U19 · Scans 019–020 · A graph from a marked vertex.** With a vertex u distinguished, the writer divides the vertices into u, the paired neighbours and a torsor P. Conditions (a)–(d) specify the links, including a condition on the difference y−x. The lemma tests linkage of two exterior vertices by equality of their complementary neighbourhoods on a unique triangle through u. The parity obstruction in the proof is the odd cardinality five.

**U20 · Scans 020–021 · The vertex stabilizer and forty non-linked pairs.** The corollary writes a split sequence with a four-dimensional binary kernel and a permutation group of degree five. It identifies W_u with the Weyl group of type D₅. To relate this to the graph itself, the writer counts non-linked pairs in E(u) and asks for the base giving each pair. The proof uses the group action for existence; its uniqueness argument has a long cancelled attempt.

**U21 · Scans 021 · Three positions of a vertex relative to a base.** The closing NB distinguishes membership in b, membership in the opposite base b′ and membership in neither. It records the corresponding root pairings and intersections with E(u) in three marginal braces. The clean sum at the foot is 16+16+40=72; the first numeral in the prose is overwritten.

**U22 · Scans 022–023 · A lattice built from a double cover.** Starting with a set Q, its double cover and the associated torsor, the writer gives a scalar product by three cases. He quotients by its radical, relates opposite lifts by a sign and writes the roots as differences between lifts coming from different pairs. The upper construction on 022 is crossed, while the scalar-product construction following it is live.

**U23 · Scans 022–024 · The vertex construction in lattice coordinates.** After choosing a base and a distinguished vertex, the notes list its neighbours in two rows. The resulting differences take the form ±εᵢ±εⱼ. The intrinsic account sends x to x−θ/2 and identifies forty differences with roots perpendicular to u. Sheet 18, which follows insertions 18 bis and 18 ter, still marks the D₅ statement for verification.

**U24 · Scans 024 · A base orthogonal to a vertex.** The data reduce to a six-element set with a distinguished two-element part. The writer uses permutations of that part and its complement to count objects and recover the ambient group order. Passing to an unordered pair of opposite bases adds a two-element datum and halves the object count.

**U25 · Scans 024–025 · An edge and its stabilizer.** A marked edge is drawn as the opposite side to a distinguished vertex in a marked triangle. The earlier five-element index set loses one element. A torsor under the four-coordinate binary group supplies the vertices; the six lettered clauses give the adjacency rules. The split stabilizer has permutation quotient of degree four and is identified as W of type B₄. The writer wants to recover its 32 roots inside E₆.

**U26 · Scans 026 · Orienting the marking.** Choosing an origin on the edge restricts the structural group to the augmentation kernel. The pointwise triangle stabilizer is identified with W of type D₄. The count of 24 uses pairs from eight elements with four pairs excluded. The two small shapes in the margin distinguish the hanging edge and the pair of incident triangles.

**U27 · Scans 026–027 · Reconstruction symmetric in the three vertices.** The data now consist of a three-element set t, a two-dimensional space over the binary field and a torsor under a central extension. The notes take quotients indexed by i∈t and reconstruct the graph from t and three fibres. The adjacency test across distinct fibres asks for a common lift. The oblique note identifies the torsor with triangles disjoint from t. The corollary permits permutations of t as well as automorphisms of the auxiliary space.

**U28 · Scans 028–029 · Twenty-four bases associated with a triangle.** The writer constructs sets B(i) from non-linked pairs in each fibre, then a common set B of bases meeting the relevant neighbourhoods in two elements. The count is 24. The margin relates orthogonality to all three vertices to orthogonality to two. The next sheet nevertheless asks for verification that these roots have type D₄.

**U29 · Scans 029 · An orthogonal base as three pairs.** A base splits into three unordered pairs. Their paired indices give the triangle. Counting the partitions per base or the bases per triangle gives the same number of pairs (t,b). Permuting the three classes and exchanging the two members of each class gives the stabilizer order used in the page’s second calculation of card W.

**U30 · Scans 030–031 · A nonorthogonal triangle and base.** The three pairings with r_b sum to zero. The notes distinguish the all-zero case from the case with values 1, −1 and 0. For a fixed triangle, the latter accounts for 48 bases. The extra choice is described using a triangle through the zero-pairing vertex and a distinguished neighbour. The stabilizer is described both by even sign changes in type D₃ and by permutations of the four unmarked base elements, the instance D₃≃A₃.

**U32 · Scans 032 · Two disjoint triangles and one orthogonal base.** The page associates the two triangles to two pair partitions on a six-element set, with no common pair. In the hexagon, the marked edge styles encode these two matchings. Ordering them accounts for a factor of two. A completing third triangle gives a bitriangle; the writer counts 120 ordered choices for a fixed base.

**U33 · Scans 033 · A balise and incident triangles.** An additional marked neighbour of the distinguished vertex leaves three binary torsors indexed by R_u. The writer relates the resulting automorphisms to the extended group of type D₃. The oblique note removes the extension when the balise is pinned. The page counts the markings by choosing a triangle, a vertex and one of eight neighbours.
