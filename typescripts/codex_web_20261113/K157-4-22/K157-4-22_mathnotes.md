# Mathematical notes on scans 333–347

## What the pages do

**333.** Corollary 4.4.12 closes a factorization argument. A path construction over $S$ factors $f$ through $\overline X$. The projection is perfect for the localizers under consideration. The other map belongs to the relative class indicated by the superscript on $W_S$. With $\mathrm{Loc}(8\mathrm{bis})$ for both $W_0$ and $W$, Grothendieck obtains $D_{W_0}\Rightarrow A$ and the equivalence of eight conditions.

**334–335.** Theorem 4.4.13 collects four conditions for a pair $W_0\subset W$. The first three apply $A(\cdot),B(\cdot),C(\cdot)$ to a family of maps. The fourth is $A(\mathrm{Parf}_{W_0})$. Taking $W_0=W$ gives the four unindexed names. Restricting the smaller localizer gives the horizontal comparisons in the two-column rectangle. **P1.** At its lower right, $D_{W_0}\Rightarrow F$. On 336, $F=\mathrm{Loc}(7\mathrm F)$ means that $\mathrm{Cat}$ has enough $W$-fibrations.

**336–338.** When both localizers satisfy the denumerable-limit axiom, all eight conditions agree. If only $W_0$ is assumed to satisfy it, its four indexed conditions agree and the remaining implications form a chain. Another reduction uses $\mathrm{Loc}(7\mathrm F)$ for $W_0$. For strongly saturated $W$ satisfying $\mathrm{Loc}(3\mathrm{bis})$, the converse $B\Rightarrow A$ gives $A\Leftrightarrow B$. The proof recalls the action on the categories written $\mathrm{Hot}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W$.

**339–342.** Section 4.4.14 draws ordinary and conditional implications together. The two circled copies of $A$ let the reader follow the return arrows. Grothendieck identifies three ingredients, the categories of paths, Brown's lemma and the prederivator $\mathrm{HOT}_W$. He then asks which implications survive under weaker assumptions.

**342–344.** The cofibration digression translates the question about enough $W$-cofibrations into one about open M.V. squares. Two forks, a square and an integral sign describe a canonical map to $X$. Membership of this map in $W$ is the proposed test. The text qualifies the saturation hypothesis and revisits the assumptions of an earlier theorem.

**344–346.** **E1.** The localizer written with a doubled W and subscript zero consists of maps inducing an isomorphism on $\pi_0$. Grothendieck says it satisfies $F$ and fails $A$ and even $D_{W_\infty}$. To obtain enough fibrations he forms a coproduct indexed by the components of $X$. Over $Y_j$ the projection is a product with the discrete fibre $\pi_0(f)^{-1}(j)$. For the negative assertion he recalls a covering of the circle, then adds an alternative argument in the margin.

**347.** A cancelled paragraph proposes reformulating $A$ using $D$ or $D_{W_0}$. Section 5 then states stability of $W$ under filtered direct limits and its denumerable variant. The author writes the two systems, their component maps and the induced limit map. Accessibility occurs in the heading. The supplied text ends at “Ici $I$ est”.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence.** A counterexample to $D\Rightarrow A$ would have to satisfy $F$. Taking $W_0=W$ in P1 gives $D\Rightarrow F$. Among truth assignments with $D$ true and $A$ false, that implication leaves only $F$ true. This explains the author's reservation about testing $W_\infty$ on 341.
- **R2 · Model remark, high confidence.** Separating $F$ from the weakest corner of the rectangle still leaves room for every internal arrow to be reversible. As a check of this logical point, assign false to all eight corners and true to $F$. All ten internal arrows and their reverses hold at that assignment, while the reverse arrow from $F$ to the weakest corner fails. This accounts for the remaining strictness question on 346.
