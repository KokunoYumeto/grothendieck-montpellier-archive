# Mathematical notes on cote 12, scans 51–63

## What the pages do

**Scan 51.** For smooth $X$ of dimension $n$ throughout, the sheet distributes cohomology with and without the lower exclamation mark among motivic classes and filtrations. The bounds change at $i=n$. The last two displays pass to the class of $H^i$, first with constant coefficients, then with a coefficient of weight $p$.

**Scan 52.** A cancelled fragment begins with the Albanese up to isogeny of a surface. It compares the quotient $L_i(K)/L_{i-1}(K)$ with successive motivic quotients, provisionally places its image in the primitive part, and asks about the boxed map to an indexed power of $\mathbb Z$.

**Scans 53–54.** Section 13 defines a group using twisted constant sheaves that come from motives. A Tate-conjectural separation of the realizations of simple motives is invoked for bijectivity. The sheets then give two different tests at finite points. One uses the absolute value $N(x)^{i/2}$. The other uses integrality of the characteristic polynomial of inverse Frobenius.

**Scans 54–55.** For a homogeneous object of degree $\alpha$, the first case has $i=0$ and $\alpha=2\beta$. A twist gives degree zero, and a margin note describes the resulting eigenvalues as roots of unity. The second case asks for a twist of degree $i-1$ or $i$. Scan 55 describes a finite quotient action in the first case and asks how to recover a dimensional component of a semisimple realization. A partly cancelled attempt below returns to Frobenius.

**Scans 56–57.** Section 14 compares algebraic classes with Galois invariants and an algebra of correspondences with a commutant. After passing to finite extensions, the sheet distinguishes the span of the classes from the classes themselves and the enveloping algebra from the image that generates it. It asks for the Lie image inside an endomorphism algebra.

**Scan 58.** A cancelled calculation relates a dimension bound to an $E_1^{pq}$ term and a bracket of twists. Turning the sheet reveals two rows of cohomology groups involving $Y$, $X$ and $U$. The lower calculation attempts degree conversions under $i+j=2n$. Its last line breaks off after “si $i$”.

**Scan 59.** The displayed map into $\operatorname{End}(M_l)$ leads to a proposed Lie algebra motive inside $\check M\otimes M$. Increasing $M$ is said to give a fundamental pro-motif. The question shifts to its algebraic part, with a finite-field case and a revised suggestion about other cases.

**Scans 60 and 62.** Section 15 proposes absolute cohomology from a functor $\mathbb R\Gamma_X$ into bounded complexes of abelian groups. The numbered properties concern inverse limits, transitivity, vanishing in negative degrees and a functorial description of $H^0$. The sentence about transitivity continues from the bottom of 60 to the top of 62.

**Scan 61.** A separate, cancelled typescript fragment concerns a family $(u')$ obtained by base change. “On aura expliqué au préalable” ends without an object on the surviving sheet.

**Scans 62–63.** Hyper-Ext is expressed through hypercohomology of a derived Hom. The notes compare degree zero with Hom and ask whether degree one classifies extensions. Spectral sequences lead into proposed comparison maps to the $\ell$-adic realization. The final hypothesis reads “de type fini sur $\mathbb Z$”, followed by a parenthesis about the right-hand members.

## Remarks of the model

**R01 · High confidence.** The two Frobenius tests on 53–54 have different right sides. One specifies a size, the other requires integrality. Reading the margins beside their respective braces keeps the two assertions distinct.

**R02 · Low confidence about the manuscript.** The middle sideways pair of M subscripts on 58 is compatible with the displayed $i+j=2n$. Substituting $j=2n-i$ gives a zero difference between $2n-i-2$ and $j-2$. For the last pair it gives $2(m-n)$, as recorded in L03. This computation tests only the transcribed indices. The page carries crossing strokes through both lines.

**R03 · High confidence.** The words “de” at the bottom of 60 and “type fini, on a” at the top of 62 complete the same transitivity condition. Scan 61 has a separately oriented, crossed-out typewritten passage about base change. The physical order and the sentence order can both be recovered from these boundaries.

**R04 · Reading question, low confidence.** In the last line of 51 the frozen transcription reads $p$ for the weight and for the shift in the bounds. A handwritten $\rho$ could account for the long descending stroke in the lower limit. The same small letter at the right after “poids” supplies a comparison, without giving a decisive distinction between the alphabets.
