# K154-02 · Mathematical notes

The references are archive scan numbers. L and R designate columns in reading order. The identifiers connect the few calculations and comparisons made here to CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**Transport and signs, scans 62–63.** Three rows compare transport of orientation data on a face, on its underlying space, and on a pseudo-line. A path induces identifications between pairs of two-element sets. Grothendieck expresses a dependence among these sets as a relation between characters, then introduces the orientation character of the constructed space and a combinatorial parity character. A slanted addition gives the exceptional crossing weight two.

**Two circuits, scans 63–64.** The representation into the automorphisms of the polygon leads to two local calculations. A half-circuit round an exceptional component gives a translation by \(n-1\). For a half-circuit about a point, a symmetry has sign \(-1\), and he concludes that the map is surjective. In the right column he links the weight of a path modulo two to the change in the number of vertices on the pseudo-line. A closed path has the same endpoints and hence even weight in this argument.

**Gluing choices, scan 65.** Each of two disks carries a pair of privileged or antipodal arcs. A choice of one arc from each pair still allows two identifications. He counts eight choices and compares the surfaces after composing on both sides with antipodisms. The parenthetical exception about the red object is hard to read.

**Geometry of the two arc choices, scans 66–68.** Shrinking a disk until its new boundary passes through \(s\) lets the chosen arcs meet in \(X\). One drawing continues a bank from one disk to the next. Another joins the same bank to itself. He transports a disk orientation through the join and compares the orientation it induces on \(D'\) with the one carried along the pseudo-lines. This forces him to revisit both the sign and the choice of generization arc.

**Four cases, scans 69–71.** List A treats the gluings admitting a natural map to \(X\). The first one gives an immersion. The second is immersive on the individual faces but folds along their common boundary. List B changes the isomorphism between arcs. For each B case the author again distinguishes the facing arc from the parallel-transported one. He tests the resulting circuits near a supersingular position, crosses out part of the test, and writes down a qualified new result.

**Stable positions, scan 72.** The question is how to attach disks at two stable positions related by sweeping past a vertex. The drawings sit beside the proposed modification of the disk boundaries. Of the four candidate pairs of arcs, the text retains \((a,b)\) and \((a',b')\) by symmetry. It then considers two choices of transport for each and the effect of antipodisms.

**Cyclic products, scan 73.** On a reused job listing the author writes \(N=4n\), a cyclic list of \(\sigma_i\), their squares, and successive products denoted by \(\rho_i\). He puts a condition of commutation beside the square of a product. He evaluates \(\rho_1\rho_2\). The next expression stops after its equality sign. Several pencil configurations lie below, with faint numerical labels.

**Two attachments, scan 74.** In the case \((a,b)\), parallel transport gives an immersed surface with a graph \(K\). The preimage \(L=\varphi^{-1}(X_1)\) makes the correspondence between faces and stable positions visible. The author describes enlarging each face at critical points. He compares this gluing with the one opposite to parallel transport and records different outcomes for the supersingular circuits.

**A polygon of order \(n\) and a band, scans 75–76.** The arcs on \(D\) itself suggest a second polygonal structure. To compare the resulting surfaces he draws a cut along the common part of \(D\) and \(D'\), describes a Möbius band, and takes its orientation cover. He asks whether simultaneously identifying two pairs of edges gives the cellular surface constructed from the graph or a branched cover of it.

**A graph of oriented transitions, scan 76.** Between adjacent stable positions the author keeps two directed edges, one for each sweeping direction. The final braced list describes the vertices as stable positions \(D\) and the oriented edges as pairs \((D,a)\). Here \(a\) may be a specialization arc or its antipodal arc.

## Remarks of the model

**R65.1 · Model remark, high confidence.** Enumerating two choices for the first arc, two for the second, and two choices of identification gives eight labelled triples. This checks C65.1. The question of which surfaces are isomorphic is separate.

**R72.1 · Model remark, high confidence.** Counting in the displayed ordered product, the entries whose labels both carry a prime or both lack it are exactly \((a',b')\) and \((a,b)\). This small calculation checks the list remaining on 72, conditional on the exclusion stated there. The geometric symmetry requirement was not independently established.

**R73.1 · Model remark, high confidence.** Substituting the two products written on 73 into \(\rho_1\rho_2\) gives the word with indices \(0,1,1,2\). Cancelling the middle equal pair using the displayed involution relation leaves \(0,2\), as written. The manuscript's following equality has an empty right-hand side.

**R74.1 · Model remark, medium confidence.** The first construction on 74 has an immersion and a marginal complaint about its supersingular circuits. The second is credited with the desired local circuits. This explains why the word *circuits* on 75 still needs its qualifier “plus généraux”. The correspondence between all earlier case designations and these two cases is not spelled out.

**Reading questions arising during the notes.** On 63, R, the frozen boxed formula has \(\chi_o\chi_c\), while the later displayed formula writes \(\chi_X\chi_c\). The small round subscript in the box and the later crossed X are distinct. The sheets do not decide whether \(o\) names the orientation character or whether \(X\) was intended. On 68, R17–19, the frozen adjective is “indiqués” following “les quatre possibilités”. The alternative “indiquées” would agree grammatically, but the short joined strokes at the end do not distinguish a second e. This remains a reading question.
