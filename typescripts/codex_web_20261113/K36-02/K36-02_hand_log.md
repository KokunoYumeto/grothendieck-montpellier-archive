# Hand log · cote 36, scans 38–52

Entries distinguish the front and back of each leaf by the face visible in the scan. Locations follow the upright text.

## Scan 38

The Lie-algebra typescript is upside down relative to the corner foliation. Its isolated g has two bowls and a broken, doubled-looking edge. The r and h naming subspaces have a similarly uneven impression. Compare the smooth g in *algèbre*, the summation r and the r in the last sum. This feature belongs to these particular sorts, not to every g, r or h in the prose. The header 98 is typed and the corner 37 is in pencil.

## Scan 39

The degree 1 leans against the second upright of H. It detaches before the parenthesis in the first exact sequence. Subscript n may precede or follow a group. A small low dot follows T in each boxed formula. The dark superscript on Pic can close completely. The left strokes of the arrows vary from a plain line to an unmistakable wedge. These features do not apply to every H, dot, n or horizontal stroke. In the prose, successive letters often run to a single horizontal stroke. The line carrying the addition above *limite* curls down before *sur n*.

## Scan 40

The horizontal isomorphism in the upper box has a definite arrowhead at the left. The lower box uses a straight bar under a wave. The tails of Alb and Pic are independent of the separate underline. The two downward arrows carry small vertical wave-shaped labels, the lower of them much darkened. The rightmost mark above the last Pic turns like τ. Three question marks end the first marginal question and two end the second. The margin is read horizontally.

## Scan 41

H has a loop at lower left in both upper boxes and the following unboxed lines. The H in the bottom box has a plain upright. The loop in γ goes below its two shoulders. In the middle array one can separate two superposed outlines, but the order of writing is uncertain. The X–S rule has no wedge in that array, unlike the one next to the top box. Each Hom and Ext underline is local. The thick right parenthesis in the bottom box is a closing sign.

## Scan 42

The whole draft has diagonal cancellation lines. After turning the leaf through 180°, they run upwards to the right across the diagram and paragraphs. They differ from the short horizontal local cancellations. The curved leaders place *propre* and *cycle* immediately before *de* and *divisoriel*. The usual j has a low terminal hook, clearest beneath U → Y and in fg = j. The first membership sign resembles an open E. One dense stroke in the diagonal of the square leaves the old route unclear.

## Scan 43

In the first adjunction f carries a tall stroke and a separate point, above the line on the left and below on the right. The next formula has a five-armed star on the left. A high point and a low point on the letters H and G differ from the large filled disk above the circled Pic. The two small geometric sketches each have a filled point; the left loop has a return along its upper outline and a separate short dash. The lower double shafts point left. The two upright pairs of strokes remain vertical. Neither the underlining of Ext nor the script form of H persists throughout the leaf.

## Scan 44

The arrow of f has a small pointed end, while g has a long line with a blunt dark endpoint. The long oblique stroke between the computations is a dividing rule. Underlining is confined to the functor name in the formulas.

## Scan 45

Last letters of French words are reduced to horizontal runs. The descender of subscript p is distinct from the small curved s in the boxed formulas. Irregular horizontal waves go through two entire boxed formulas, unlike the rectangular borders of the live ones.

## Scan 46

The coefficient like U has two tall uprights and no clear descent below the line. The star after A has several rays and differs from the loop on ℓ. Square-looking right brackets occur inside parenthesised exponents. The underlines at centre follow two complete lines.

## Scan 47

The long horizontal across the beginning of faisceaux is the crossbar of f. In the title the end of the word after the second i is a low continuous wave.

## Scan 48

A low n to the left belongs to the following capital. Distinguish prime strokes on P and E from raised dots. One set of cross-hatches cancels a note at the right and another crosses the lower proof, where several words or lines had already been deleted horizontally. The first strokes of Corollaire sit below the lower cancellation.

## Scan 49

The mathematical script H is entered in pen in blanks among the typescript. The degree can be a separate typed glyph (q on the upper inset). The two thin marks with a loop above (22) tie the left addition to its destination. Horizontal x-overtyping and a diagonal pen cancellation are distinguished from page folds.

## Scan 50

In the T subscript and in the set of primes, P has two verticals. Real primes to the upper right of both P and Q differ from their oval capital bodies. The square enclosure of the NB does not delete it. Internal strikes and the long line connecting the inserted projective-system qualification have different endpoints.

## Scan 51

The recurrence of the open loop and two stems separates pen-script H from typewriter H and the check above it from degree zero at its right. The isolated typescript word les at the left has matching ink keys. The crossed terminal s of couvrants reaches below the baseline.

## Scan 52

In the paired descriptions rigidifiés and rigidifiables have different endings. The initial left coefficient A_G in the decomposition has an angular capital above a small curved G. In the product of pullbacks the second pr has no visible star on the first line.
