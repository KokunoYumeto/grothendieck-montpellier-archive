## What the pages do

**U02 · Isomorphisms and the translation character, scans 236–238.** With a pregraph Γ, a subgroup Z and a family π of bijections, (49)–(53) describe the automorphisms that preserve the data and the subgroup G that acts on π by translations. The four conditions in (58) compare surjectivity, transitivity on π and isomorphisms between edge relations.

The new starting data in (62) are a graph, an automorphism group H, an oriented origin and commuting homotheties Z. Equations (63)–(64) seek the group that also preserves the choice of H. **U05 · Faithfulness and stabilizers, scan 244.** Equation (72) tests faithfulness of the action of Z on π. The paragraph identifies its kernel and takes the quotient when necessary. **U06 · Reconstruction with a chosen edge, scan 245.** In (76)–(78), the pointed graph data give a group, two stabilizers and an endpoint interchanger. Simple transitivity on oriented edges gives the trivial stabilizer in (79) and the involution in (80).

**U07 · The subgroup T_H and the orbit stabilizers, scans 246–247.** The definition at the top of 246 uses reciprocal multipliers on the two endpoints. It leads to the map from T_H to Z and the sequence (81). Under simple transitivity this becomes (81′). The action on each vertex stabilizer in (82) gives the groups in (83), their orbit descriptions (84) and their intersection (85). Exchanging endpoints exchanges the two stabilizers and inverts the character.

**U08 · Group-theoretic criteria, scans 248–249.** In (86)–(87), conjugation by σ acts by inversion modulo the kernel and interchanges the two groups B. Condition (88) describes which subgroups T_H give faithful homotheties; a marginal note attaches the faithfulness to the action on the set π of graph structures. Condition (89) specializes to a trivial oriented-edge stabilizer and states injectivity of the multiplication map from U₀ × T_H × U₁.

**U09 · Return to the larger group, scan 249.** After a cancelled point-stabilizer definition, (90) introduces T_G as the stabilizer in G of the ordered pair of Z-orbits. The two scalars attached to g yield a map to Z × Z. This is where the batch ends.

## Remarks of the model

**M01 · Scan 240 · High confidence.** The inference about orbits before (62) needs a common point. As a check, I took the permutation (0 1)(2 3). Its two orbits {0,1} and {2,3} have empty intersection and are different. The corrected inference (S10a) is that two orbits sharing a point coincide.

**M02 · Scans 244–245 · High confidence.** Relation (75) permits a nonidentity square for an endpoint interchanger. In the symmetric group on five vertices I checked σ = (0 1)(2 3 4). Its square fixes 0 and 1 and acts as a 3-cycle on the other vertices. The oriented edge has stabilizer of order six. This gives a concrete instance of the distinction used when (79) precedes (80).

**M03 · Scans 237 and 246–247 · Medium confidence.** There are two scalar maps to distinguish. The restriction in (56) sends λ to λ². The sequence (81) uses the scalar by which an element acts on the first endpoint, with reciprocal scalar at the other. The note « Attention » points to that second arrow. The arrow's short handwritten label on 246 remains uncertain.

**M04 · Scans 244–245 · High confidence for the example.** In the same five-vertex action, the vertex stabilizer has order 24 and the intersection of all its conjugates is trivial. The action is faithful even though the oriented-edge stabilizer has order six. This illustrates the two different intersections in (77) and (79).
