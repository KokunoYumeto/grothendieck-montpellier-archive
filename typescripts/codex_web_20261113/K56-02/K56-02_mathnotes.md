# Mathematical notes on K56-02

## What the pages do

**The two tables for \(E\), scan 015.** The boxes distinguish \(n+1=2\nu\) from \(n+1=2\nu+1\). In the first case \(H^{2(\nu-1)}(E)\) is split according to whether \(l=2\), with \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) in the latter case. The row for \(4\nu-1\) carries a twist. The second case has separate rows in degrees \(0,2\nu-1,2\nu+1,4\nu+1\). The overwritten exclusion in the first line and the argument of the coefficient in \(H^0\) are less clear than these row labels.

**Compact supports on the cone, scan 017.** Duality is invoked for \(E,\widehat E\) when \(X\) is smooth. Properness identifies the compact-support and ordinary theories on \(\widehat C\). The sequence for \(C\) places \(H_c^{i+1}(C)\) between \(H^i(X)\) and \(H^{i+1}(\widehat C)\). Beneath two terms the author writes groups of \(X\) with twist \((-1)\). Comparison with the calculation of \(E\) leads to a prediction and the boxed question about supports.

**The affine quadric, scan 020.** For a nonsingular \(Q^n\) over a separably closed field the sheet takes a section \(Q^{n-1}\) at infinity and writes \(U^n=AQ^n=Q^n-Q^{n-1}\). It gives coefficient groups for compact supports in degrees \(n,2n\), then an ordinary-cohomology table in degrees \(0,n\), with parity-dependent twists. The conditions for \(n\ne0\) are written beside the braces. In the lower brace the two coefficient rows and their sloping marks deserve to be read together.

**Comparison and exceptional degrees, scans 020 and 022.** Scan 020 describes a map \(\varphi\) as zero except for even \(n\) and \(i=n\), then writes a pairing equal to 2. It gives the surjectivity and injectivity ranges for a map labelled \(\alpha_*\), asks about the exceptional degrees, and treats even \(n>0\). Scan 022 opens with the odd case. Its sequence ends in zero, but the separate sentence naming an extension stops at “par”.

**The lettered list, scan 022.** Entry a) gives odd-degree vanishing for \(Q^n\). Entries b) and b′) give the groups for \(U^n\) and call for the dual statement. Entries c) and c′) arrange the ranges for the two displayed maps according to the parity of \(n\). Entry d) assigns rank one to the even degrees, apart from a rank-two middle case when \(n\) is even. The exponent on its \(H\) is overwritten. Entry e) breaks off after its parity condition.

**Generators and the middle degree, scan 024.** The sheet sets two series of \(\xi\)’s beside a middle \(\lambda\), draws maps for \(Q^{n-1}\subset Q^n\), writes a two-by-two matrix, and tabulates cases \(Q^0\) through \(Q^4\). The boxed rows distinguish even and odd dimension. A sloping note points to the middle pair in the even case and gives \(\varphi_\nu=\tfrac12(\xi^\nu+\lambda_\nu)\) in connection with an index two and a basis.

**Products and maps under an inclusion, scan 026.** A crossed polynomial expression precedes two generator lists. A separate bracket includes \(2\xi_i=\xi^i\), mixed products of the \(\xi\)’s, \(\lambda_\nu^2=2\xi_{2\nu}=\xi^{2\nu}\), and vanishing products. Below \(Q^i\subset Q^j\), the upper arrows point left. The lower arrows point right and shift \(\alpha\) by \(j-i\). The special rows send \(\lambda_\nu\) to zero, with different source and target parities in the two groups of arrows.

**Ranks on a field and ranks in a family, scan 018.** Definition 1.5 defines the reductive, nilpotent and unipotent ranks of \(G\) using \(G_{\bar k}\). The next paragraph invokes base change for the centralizer. Remark 1.6 considers a smooth affine group over a discrete valuation ring with multiplicative generic fibre and additive special fibre. The trivial torus is maximal in the special fibre and ceases to be maximal in the generic fibre. The opening of 1.7 introduces the two functions \(\rho_r(s)\) and \(\rho_n(s)\). The sheet ends before the conclusions.

**Semicontinuity for finite groups, scans 021 and 016.** Scan 021 compares \(w,w'\) and states 2.2 for an étale, separated, quasi-finite group over \(S\). The geometric fibre defines a function to ordered isomorphism classes of finite groups. The lemma asserts lower semicontinuity and relates local constancy to the fibre cardinality and to finiteness over a neighbourhood. Its proof sketch reduces to a complete valuation ring. The proof on scan 016 uses an open-and-closed finite subgroup, written \(G'\), and a monomorphism \(A\to B\) into the geometric generic fibre to obtain \(f(s)\le f(t)\). It explains why the cardinality condition suffices.

**Subtori and quotients, scan 016.** Lemma 2.3 compares \(W(R)\) and \(W(T)\) for \(R\subset T\). It claims that \(W(R)\) is isomorphic to a quotient of a subgroup of \(W(T)\). The paragraph that starts to interpret the groups as groups of points has been cancelled.

**A maximal torus and a commuting subtorus, scan 025.** In 1.14 the image of \(R\times_S T\to G\) is a torus \(T'\) containing the maximal torus \(T\). The text concludes \(T'=T\), hence \(R\subset T\), and refers back to 1.13. Corollary 1.15 applies this to a smooth affine commutative group of locally constant reductive rank, obtaining a unique maximal torus containing every subtorus.

**The nilpotent-fibre corollary, scan 025.** Corollary 1.16 uses connected nilpotent geometric fibres and the inserted hypothesis of locally constant reductive rank. It asserts that the unique maximal torus is central and contains every subtorus. The proof invokes local existence for fpqc, reduces to centrality, then to an algebraically closed field. The sheet ends at that reduction.

**The reductive centre and a quotient, scan 023.** A radiciality and bounded-rank argument leads to a largest subgroup \(Z\) of multiplicative type in the situation under discussion. An inverse-image argument excludes a nontrivial such subgroup in \(G/Z\), and the text carries the assertion to \((G/Z)_K\). For the universal property of \(Z\), it reduces factorisation through \(Z_S\) to vanishing of maps into \(U_S\), with \(U=G/Z\). The face ends before the last reason is given.

**The fibre criterion, scan 027.** For the criterion referred to as 4.3, the image \(K\) of \(H\times_S Z\to G\) is compared with \(Z\) on fibres. The inserted sentence closes the proof. The next paragraph says that the fibre condition can be checked over the algebraic closure of \(k(s)\).

**Existence of the centre, scan 027.** Theorem 4.4 asserts existence of a reductive centre for an affine algebraic group over a field. Its proof reduces to the commutative case and then to an algebraically closed field. It uses a promised decomposition \(Z\times U\), where \(Z\) is of multiplicative type and \(U\) is called “unipotent”. The last sentence starts “Nous allons”.
