# K157-2-11 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**163 · Factorization over a base.** Théorème-Scholie 2 starts with a category \(M\), classes \(W\) and \(\mathrm{Fib}\), and a factorization of \(X\to S\) through an object fibrant over \(S\). In A the replacement \(C(X)\) and the map out of \(X\) are functorial. In B the first map belongs to \(W\) and to a class specified by a lifting property. The definition of the full subcategory \(\underline{\mathrm{Fib}}\,S\) is an insertion.

**164–165 · The first localization.** The inclusion of the fibrant objects over \(S\) induces an equivalence after inverting \(W_S\). The statement includes intermediate full subcategories. A quasi-inverse is described on objects by \(\gamma(X)=C(X)\). Its action on arrows is obtained differently under A and B. The author names the localized category \(\mathrm{Ho}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W(S)\).

**165–166 · A second localization and an adjunction.** A smaller localizer, printed \(W_S^0\), agrees with \(W_S\) on fibrant objects. Its localization has a functor \(\rho\) to the preceding category. Passage through the fibrant subcategory gives \(i\) in the other direction. Under B, \(i\) is right adjoint to \(\rho\) and fully faithful. The boxed Hom formula uses \(C(Y)\) in the target argument on the \(W_S^0\) side.

**167 · The path construction A′.** A stronger version of A uses an endofunctor \(\mathrm{Ch}\) on \(M\), source and target maps, trivial paths and a composition of paths. The author asks for a two-sided unit property and explicitly dispenses with associativity here. The braced conditions at the foot concern \(\alpha_X\), \(p_X\), and \(X\to e\).

**168 · Corollary for an already fibrant target.** For \(Y\) fibrant over \(S\), the corollary identifies the two localized Hom sets from \(X\) to \(Y\). The proof uses the adjunction, then the agreement of the localizers on the arrow from \(Y\) to \(C(Y)\). This is statement C168 in the check record.

**168–170 · Application to categories over \(S\).** The manuscript returns to the localizations of \(\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}\), \(\underline{\mathrm{Parf}}\,S\), and the intervening full subcategories in an earlier diagram. It relates the comparison of seven categories to the two scholia. For the categories of fibrations and cofibrations it also singles out the split variants. An explicit path functor and a factorization using Q-fibrant arrows are described as two approaches.

**170–172 · Varying the base.** For \(S'\to S\), the author describes inverse image on relative homotopy types using presheaves of categories. On such presheaves the operation is precomposition by the opposite base map. For a split fibration, it is represented by \(X\times_S S'\). The text then forgets the splitting and works with functors over \(S\) between fibrations, using equivalences on the fibres.

**173 · Smooth objects.** Localization of all of \(\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}\) at \(W_S^d\) presents a difficulty under change of base. Restriction to a point illustrates why fibrewise equivalence matters. On the smooth subcategory the two indicated classes agree. Proposition 1 identifies \(f^*\mathrm{hot}_{W,S}(X)\) with the class of \(X\times_S S'\) when \(X\) is smooth over \(S\).

**174 · Locally constant types.** The same calculation on the category read as \(\underline{\mathrm{Fibhot}}\,S\) describes \(f^*\) on \(\mathrm{HOT}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W\). Corollary 1 applies the formula to \(X\) Hot-fibrant over \(S\). The paragraph notes the inclusion of \(\underline{\mathrm{Liss}}_0\) into this category.

**175 · Arbitrary \(X\).** Corollary 2 gives two representatives of the inverse image. It takes the base change of \(\Phi(X)\), described as smooth and split fibrant, or of \(C(X)\), described as Hot-fibrant and even perfect. The glyph printed \(\Phi\) also permits \(Q\). The discussion that follows asks when one can take the base change of \(X\) itself.

**175–176 · Colocal equivalence and relative homology.** For a derivator \(\mathbb D\), a test on the categories written at the left of \(X\) and \(Y\) is expressed by an isomorphism in relative homology for every coefficient \(a\). The first diagram identifies the homology terms with direct images carrying a low exclamation mark. Commutation of those direct images with proper base change yields the lemma on stability of \(\mathbb D\)-colocal equivalence. The author then lists types of base changes that can serve as tests.

**177–178 · The proper case and Corollary 3.** The main text formulates preservation of the indicated localizers under proper base change as a conjecture. The marginal NB points to a later proof. For a base change possessing the preservation property, the comparison of localizations identifies its induced functor with inverse image. Corollary 3 consequently applies the formula to arbitrary \(X\) under that hypothesis.

**178 · Compatibility with the locally constant localization.** The author distinguishes the localizers \(W_S^d,W_{S'}^d\) used above from the coarser \(W_S,W_{S'}\). He writes a square comparing inverse image and the two localization functors \(\rho_S,\rho_{S'}\). Condition a) of Proposition 2 asserts commutation up to isomorphism. The margin records one implication and identifies strong saturation as the missing ingredient for the converse. Only a) is written out in this package.

## Remarks of the model

- **R168 · Model, high confidence about the stated dependency.** The corollary on 168 uses agreement of the two localizers only after both ends of the replacement arrow are in \(\underline{\mathrm{Fib}}\,S\). This is exactly where condition 3°) on 165 enters its short argument. The subscript on that arrow remains a separate low-confidence reading.
- **R175 · Model, high confidence.** Corollary 2 takes two products over \(S\). Their left factors are the two replacements of \(X\). This explains why the author can state it for every \(X\) before asking about a product formed directly with \(X\). Both displayed factors were checked against the prose on 174–175.
- **R178 · Model, medium confidence.** Two qualifications have different scopes. The later-proof note on 177 attaches to preservation for a proper base change. “Pas prouvé” on 178 attaches to the converse implication involving the square and saturation. The placement and direction of the two marginal notes support this distinction. The b conditions and the cited later proof lie beyond the sixteen scans.
- **Reading question · Model, low confidence.** On scan 173, TeX line 11, the frozen parenthesis reads “si S n’est … isomorphe à la catégorie ponctuelle”. One might expect “n’est pas isomorphe”. The earlier adjective is cancelled and a new one is inserted. The clearly underlined pas belongs to the outer clause, and a second pas cannot be distinguished in the parenthesis.
