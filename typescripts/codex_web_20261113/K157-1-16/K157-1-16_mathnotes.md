**248–249.** Starting from a derivator $\mathbb D$ and its fundamental category $\mathcal A=\mathbb D(e)$, the author proposes two operations with a homotopy type. One acts by product or tensor, the other by an internal hom. He writes their expected exchange under duality and the comparisons with restriction along $f:I'\to I$.

**249–251.** For a type arising from a diagram $X$, formula (4) expresses the two operations using pullback along $p:X\to e$, followed respectively by $p_!$ and $p_*$. Since the allowed diagram shapes may cover only some types, scan 250 passes to $\mathrm{Hot}_0$. Scan 251 relates descent of the two expressions to the question whether quasi-isomorphisms give $\mathbb D$-equivalences.

**251–252.** Formula (7) pairs the two actions by adjunction. For a type represented by $X$, both sides are identified with a Hom set in $\mathbb D(X)$. For the circle the author draws a four-object ordered set, chooses an origin and an orientation, and tentatively identifies the resulting operations with suspension and loops.

**252–253.** A product on small homotopy types is described using $X\times Y$. Formula (9) compares nested actions with the action of a product. The first square on 253 explains the calculation for represented types, using a comparison annotated “car $q$ est $\mathbb D$-lisse”.

**253–254.** The proposition gives right exactness for the product action and left exactness for the internal hom. For the latter the author draws the square over $f:I\to J$, moves the direct image past pullback by change of base, and ends the calculation with “qed.” He obtains the product assertion by duality.

**254–255.** For fixed $M,N$ the author writes the common Hom set of the adjunction as a contravariant functor of the type $\xi$. He asks for a representing object $H$ in (10). He then introduces a Hot-valued external hom, writes its two-variable functor in (11), and takes the functorial comparison (12) as additional data.

**256.** Formula (13) proposes comparisons between external hom objects for a second parameter $\eta$. The internal hom in HOT is characterized using the cartesian product. The author evaluates the proposed comparisons by taking ordinary Hom out of a fixed $\xi$ and starts calculations for the first two members. The last long line meets the image boundary.
