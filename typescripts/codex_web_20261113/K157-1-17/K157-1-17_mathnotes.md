# Mathematical notes on scans 257–271

## What the pages do

**U1 · Three Hom expressions, scan 257.** The page begins with the third comparison in a calculation already under way. The Hom in Hot out of a product is identified with a Hom indexed by that product, and then with a Hom into N. Grothendieck says that an additional external Hom equipped with the trifunctorial isomorphism (12) also requires isomorphisms (13) compatible with (12). The first operator has compressed, possibly parenthesised final letters.

**U2 · The circle, scan 257.** Taking the second variable to be the homotopy type of the circle specialises (13) to (14). The three terms put suspension on M, loops on N, and loops on the external Hom, respectively. Grothendieck questions the formula in Hot and locates the suspension–loop formalism in the pointed homotopy category.

**U3 · Abelianisation and the module example, scans 257–258.** Starting with the derivator derived from an abelian category, he takes the coefficients to be modules over a ring k. A homotopy type has an « abélianisé » in the derived category of abelian groups, in nonpositive cohomological degrees. Formula (15) uses its derived tensor product and its derived Hom. Formula (16), associated with the Künneth formula, leads to the two iterated expressions below it. Truncation in positive degrees suggests a candidate for the external Hom of two objects in the derived category of k-modules.

**U4 · Testing the candidate, scan 259.** He tests (10) by relating a Hom in Hot to one between abelian objects, then calculates with the truncation in the derived category. He subsequently considers keeping the external Hom in Hotab or in the negative derived category obtained by making translation invertible. In the circle example he sees unwanted degree-zero cohomology alongside degree −1.

**U5 · Substantial diagrams, scans 260–261.** The cover introduces chapter IV. The next sheet gives the underlying-diagram functor from a prederivator evaluated on I to a category of diagrams in its coefficient category. Its action on F records the family indexed by the objects of I. Grothendieck invokes Dér 2 for conservativity and asks how much of F an underlying diagram can recover. He calls the objects of the derivator « diagrammes substantiels » and uses their ordinary diagrams as pictures.

**U6 · Giving a skeleton substance, scan 262.** The interval and the four-vertex square illustrate the reversal of arrows when the underlying diagram takes values in the coefficient category. The same drawing can designate an ordinary diagram or a substantial one. He proposes starting from a skeleton and, when possible, making the construction canonical by working through an increasing sequence of subdiagrams.

**U7 · The loop square, scans 263–265.** For a pointed derivator the proposed square has M at lower left and ΩM at upper right. Its other two live vertices are e. A further square to the right is cancelled. Grothendieck describes the cartesian condition using direct image from the three-vertex subcategory. He first extends from the initial vertex by the functor with a lower exclamation mark, then by the functor with a lower star. He identifies the image of their composite with the cartesian squares whose fibres at a and b are zero and asserts uniqueness up to unique isomorphism under that constraint.

**U8 · Adjoint arrows, scans 265–266.** Returning to the interval, he asks for substantial realisations of fundamental arrows. For a functor f from I to J the unit and counit for the inverse and direct image functors are the first examples. He then starts with an arrow from G to F over f. Its two ordinary descriptions are an arrow from the inverse image of G to F and its adjoint from G to the direct image of F. He seeks corresponding objects for the two interval-shaped diagrams, guided by the case of a « catégorie de modèles “nue” ».

**U9 · The category K(f), scans 267–268.** Presheaves on an auxiliary category are to encode G on J, F on I, and the arrow between them. I and J occur as full subcategories. There are no arrows from J to I. The arrows from an object i of I to an object j of J are the arrows from f(i) to j in J. The retraction to J acts as the identity on J and as f on I. The page gives its action on arrows case by case, then writes a transformation α from the identity of K to the composite of the retraction with the inclusion of J.

**U10 · Recovering the arrow, scans 268–269.** A presheaf Φ restricts to F and G along the two inclusions. The calculation at the bottom of 268 identifies the restriction to I of the pullback along the composite through J with the inverse image of G. On 269, applying Φ to α and restricting gives the arrow labelled u, drawn pointing left from the inverse image of G to F.

**U11 · A universal property and two candidates, scans 269–270.** Grothendieck proposes an isomorphism between a functor category on K and the category of triples. The word « isomorphe » is inserted over « équivalente ». The structural data include the two inclusions, the retraction and α. He tentatively describes the triple with identity on the inverse image of G by pulling G back along the retraction. For the triple of the counit at F, his candidate is the direct image of F along the inclusion of I. He explicitly admits the comparison between its restriction to J and the direct image along f.

**U12 · From K to substantial arrows, scans 270–271.** He seeks canonical functors from the derivator evaluated on K to its values on the products of I and J with the interval. Applying the skeleton functors should give the two arrows labelled u and v. He then suggests treating open and closed immersions. For K(f) he writes a unique map to the interval for which I is the inverse image of the singleton containing 0. The continuation ends with « Donc ».

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · The two directions of arrows, scans 267–269. Model remark, high confidence.** For x in I the mixed arrow in K points from x to the object over f(x). A presheaf on K carries it to a map with source G(f(x)) and target F(x). This accounts for the direction of the arrow labelled u in 269. This objectwise comparison leaves the author's naturality check open.

**R2 · The zero vertices, scans 263–265. Model remark, high confidence.** The phrase « fibres en a et b sont nulles » records information one can actually locate in the drawing. With the order of the vertices fixed by 262, those are the two corners occupied by e in the live square on 263. This ties the essential-image description to the particular skeleton used for ΩM.

**R3 · The two uses of K(f), scans 269–271. Model remark, medium confidence.** On 269 the presheaf Φ already gives the ordinary arrow. On 270 Grothendieck still asks for functors to the derivator evaluated on a product with the interval. This distinction places the open/closed-immersion proposal at the step where a *substantial* arrow is wanted. The word « Donc » gives no construction beyond that point.
