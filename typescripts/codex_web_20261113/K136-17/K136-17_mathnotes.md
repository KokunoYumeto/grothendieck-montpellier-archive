# Mathematical notes · K136-17

## What the pages do

**252 · Tests for localization.** The page starts with modules over \(k\), a graded category and the operations \(i(N)=N\otimes_k S\) and passage to degree zero. It gives a right adjoint for \(\tau_{N,0}i\). Two lists relate full faithfulness, a comparison on degree-zero components, the essential image, and the Hom maps in degrees \(0\leq\alpha\leq N-1\). The last line is a Hom vanishing for \(S/\tau_{N-\alpha}S\).

**253 · Divided powers.** Starting with \(J\subset A\), this upside-down sheet puts a presentation in the \(X_i,Y_i\) beside computations with \(\Gamma\). Lower down are maps between cohomology groups with coefficients in \(M\), divided powers and symmetric powers. The last sketch places \(x^{(i)}\), \(x^i\) and \(i!x^{(i)}\) beside two oppositely directed maps. A long diagonal crosses the work.

**254 and 256 · Negligible modules.** Scan 254 writes a quotient category as a limit of \(\mathcal M_N\) and says that its filtered limits are not exact. Scan 256 defines \(N\)-negligibility by the vanishing of \(M_i\) for \(i\geq N\). Infinity-negligibility allows the bound to depend on the module. The broader condition of negligibility is expressed using inductive limits, finite-type submodules and annihilation of individual elements. A diagram compares two finite truncation levels.

**257 and 259 · Passing to the limit.** Scan 257 asks whether a direct limit of \(\varphi_N\tau_N P\) maps bijectively to the object at infinity. Its two columns calculate Hom and reduce the comparison to cofinality of subobjects of \(M\); the text singles out finite type. Scan 259 computes a degree-\(i\) component using shifted copies of \(S\). It treats injectivity by excluding nonzero negligible elements of \(P\), and surjectivity by extending maps to \(S\). A separate triangle records degrees of maps under multiplication by a divided power.

**261 · Derived Hom.** The page first writes the adjunction on Hom, then computes a graded component. Several \(\tau_N\)'s before \(P\) are cancelled. Writing \(h_M=\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)\), it composes with \(\tau_N\) and derives the comparison. Projectivity of \(M\) supplies the exactness of \(h_M\) used in the boxed Hom and Ext formulas.

**263 · A shifted example and an exact functor.** The example \(M=S[-i]\) relates a component of \(R^n\varphi_N\) to an Ext group involving \((\tau_{N-i}S)[-i]\). The first NB restricts a change of truncation levels by \(i\geq N'\). A second NB is crossed out. The fresh argument starts with an exact functor \(i\) from an abelian category, an essential-image hypothesis, and a comparison \(i\simeq\varphi\tau i\). It deduces a positive-degree vanishing using exactness.

**265 · Induced modules.** The argument specializes to \(C=\operatorname{Mod}(k)\) and \(i(M)=M\otimes_k S\). Its box gives Ext vanishing for \(n>0,\ i\geq0\). Below the rule, finite presentation qualifies a limit comparison and projectivity appears in a derived version. The example near the bottom writes \(M=S[i]\) and ends with componentwise Hom and Ext expressions.

**260 · IX, page 99.** Open immersions indexed by field extensions give an inductive system of smooth groups. Equations (8.3.2)–(8.3.4) introduce finite étale quotients and a limit in fppf sheaves. The text says the resulting group scheme can be non-quasi-compact and identifies its component quotient by reference to a later calculation.

**262 · IX, page 94.** A perfect duality on \(U\) and an orthogonality relation precede Proposition 7.5. Over a complete trait, the proposition attaches an abelian part \(B=A^{\mathrm{ab}}\) to an abelian variety with semi-stable reduction. It transfers a divisorial correspondence to the abelian parts, specifying its inverse image on the reductions indexed by \(n\).

**264 · IX, page 76.** The text asks for a Barsotti–Tate subgroup that could play the role of the fixed part. It gives the affirmative semi-stable case and uniqueness when a solution exists. In §6.2.1 it reduces the failure of a general affirmative answer to an example with specified special fibre and simple generic fibre.

**266 · IX, page 84.** Proposition 7.1.5 asserts algebraizability of \(\hat B\) and projectivity of \(B\) when \(S\) is normal. The argument forms a birigidified module from a relatively ample invertible module and uses its symmetric biextension. Remarque 7.1.6 discusses dropping normality for algebraizability. The sheet ends among the hypotheses of Lemme 7.2.1, after the isotrivial torus.

**255 and 258 · University notices.** The first announces A. Bensoussan’s seminar on reflected diffusions. The second announces Gilbert Dalverny’s chemistry thesis defence and identifies his director and jury. Each has its own date and subject.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Scans 256–257 · High confidence.** For a graded submodule \(M'\subset M\), infinity-negligibility of \(M/M'\) means that \(M'\) and \(M\) agree in every sufficiently large degree. This is the criterion from scan 256 applied to the quotient components. It explains the appearance of the tails \(\tau_N M\) in the cofinality argument on scan 257.

**M2 · Scans 259 and 265 · Medium confidence.** The limit expression on 259 has \(\operatorname{Hom}^i(\tau_N S,P)\); the last example on 265 has \(\operatorname{Hom}^i(\tau_{N-i}S,P)\). For fixed \(i\) these subscripts differ by a constant shift. Reading the two as reindexings of one system would require the ranges and transition maps. Neither displayed line specifies both.
