# Mathematical notes · cote 138, scans 32–45

## What the pages do

**U1 · A pair from three roots of unity (32–34).** In (24) the three coordinates take the three distinct values in μ₃. The author divides the six orderings by simultaneous scalar multiplication and maps the resulting pair into the curve. He pulls the construction back by coordinatewise powers, passes to an inverse limit, and describes the resulting subset of Π∞ as the inverse image of ω in Π₃. The NB on scan 34 relates ω to a line over the field of three elements. The letter naming one point of the pair is uncertain.

**U2 · Fibres over three other fixed points (35).** In (1.8) the transpositions supply the fixed-point labels 2, −1 and ½. The six positions are drawn on a circle. In (34) the fibre at the point indexed by i is expressed in terms of roots of −½, with one coordinate for each remaining label. Formula (35) introduces the finite and limiting sets K. The next lines describe the torsor for T and relate K(−½), K(−1) and K(2).

**U3 · The acting groups and the choice of field (36–37).** Formulas (38)–(41) introduce the group acting on T and K(½), exhibit translations and linear parts, and compare it with the action of Γ. The author asks about the image of Γ. In (1.9) he observes that the preceding constructions did not use the chosen complex embedding. He discusses a prime field, indices prime to the characteristic, and a universal abelian tame cover. He then asks for a choice of point to identify the profinite cover.

**U4 · Representatives of the lifts (38–39).** The three triples in the μ₃-class on scan 38 differ by cyclic permutation. The author chooses exponential representatives, gives finite and limiting lifts, and writes an identification of T with the profinite integers. He represents Π∞ as the quotient of three copies of the profinite integers by the diagonal. The two marked triples have coordinates (−1,+1,0) and (1,−1,0). The three coordinate classes ρ₀, ρ₁ and ρ∞ are followed by their product relation (49).

**U5 · Affine coordinates and transport (40–41).** Choosing compatible roots of 2 as an origin identifies K(2) with the profinite integers. The written formulas give the actions x ↦ ax+b on K(2) and x ↦ ax−b on K(½). The two three-branched diagrams and the pairs beneath them specify source and target fibres. Scan 41 reduces eighteen maps to two and follows a marked point through the composite via the fibre over ½. The finite and limiting triples at the bottom are the last explicit calculation there.

**U6 · Varying the three-element set (42).** The labels are replaced by an object I of Ens₃. The page collects the quotients defining Π(I) and Πₙ(I), the permutation and scalar actions, the fibres E(I,i), their disjoint union, and the orientation set Or(I). It locates ω in the quotient at level 3 and draws a map from its inverse image towards E(I,i).

**U7 · The degree-two Fermat example (44).** The author evaluates the genus formula at six degrees and turns to the conic x²+y²+z²=0. He counts eight triangles, proposes a spherical octahedron, gives its oriented automorphism group, and takes three degree-two quotients. The large drawing labels the vertices. The smaller drawing and the ramification arrays accompany the marginal discussion of subdivision of the bi-èdre.

**U8 · Administrative material (43, 45).** Scan 43 is a notice for Jean Goussopoulos’s thesis defence and includes its abstract and jury. Scan 45 is a circular from the director of U.E.R. III asking the juries about renewal or modification of their specialized degrees. A diagonal pen stroke traverses the circular. The dates belong to these documents.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Mine, high confidence (32, 34).** I enumerated the six orderings of the three residues modulo 3 and the three simultaneous translations. They fall into two orbits of size three. Separately, in the quotient of three coordinates by the diagonal over $\mathbb F_3$, cyclic permutation fixes exactly one of the four lines. With coordinates $(a-c,b-c)$ its nonzero vectors are $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$. These are finite checks of the counts appearing in A1 and A2.
- **R2 · Mine, low confidence as a proposed correction (38, 41).** Scan 38 writes $\zeta_n=\exp(2\pi i/(3n))$. Scan 41 places $\exp(\pi i/n)$ in one row and $\zeta_n$ in the corresponding slot of the next. At n=1 the two displayed exponential factors differ. This isolates the numerical mismatch. The intended normalization, and whether a different root was meant in the overwritten coordinate, remain open.
- **R3 · Mine, high confidence (44).** Substituting 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12 into the displayed genus formula gives 0, 0, 1, 3, 10 and 55, in the manuscript’s order. The group of two binary coordinates also has exactly three order-two subgroups, matching the count used in the quotient paragraph.
- **R4 · Mine, low confidence, reading question (39, last two lines).** The frozen group name has the readings Gr or G under a hat with subscript 2 and a superscript plus. The upper return could belong to Γ and the lower strokes could form 12. The left margin rule comes close to the first letter. The identification with Π∞ gives too little graphical evidence to settle the name.

- **R5 · Mine, medium confidence, reading question (32, (24)).** The frozen sign after Isom and before the set of triples is an equals sign. Its upper bar has a slight wave, so a two-line isomorphism sign is another reading. The earlier equality after the curve symbol is straighter.
