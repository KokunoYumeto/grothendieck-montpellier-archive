# Mathematical notes · cote 161-3, scans 41–55

## What the pages do

**Generators and points (41).** A generating subcategory \(C\) gives a functor from \(E\) to \(\widehat C\) by \(X\mapsto(A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(A,X))\). The text relates generation, conservativity and full faithfulness. It places examples from spaces, group actions and presheaf categories alongside notes about fibre functors.

**A ring criterion (42, cancelled).** The draft asks which rings \(C\) turn specified arrows \(A\to B\) into bijections on \(\operatorname{Hom}(-,C)\). After trying affine spaces, it writes a map from \(\mathbb Z[t]\) to \(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]\times\mathbb Z\). The resulting elements are described using a decomposition of \(C\) and a unit on one component. The connectedness argument stops within a bracket.

**Morphisms and examples (43, 45, 47).** Composition of morphisms of topoi is treated as a functor between categories of morphisms. Equivalence is expressed by composites equivalent to identities. The examples include spaces, the two-point “doublet”, the classifying topos \(B_G\), a slice \(E_{/X}\), and the final and empty topoi. The list on 47 ends with the undeveloped heading about sums.

**Sketch of categories of a prescribed type (44).** A triangle \(S\to S'\to R\) precedes a comparison of functor categories. Changing \(\lambda\) to \(\lambda'\) gives two columns joined by solid and dotted arrows. A five-item list asks for existence of limits, an adjoint and a description of \(R_T\).

**Presheaf functoriality (45, 50).** A functor of indexing categories acts on presheaves by precomposition. The pages distinguish its two adjoints. The cancelled calculation on 50 specializes their Hom formulas to representables and compares the resulting functors with possible adjoints to the original functor.

**Reconstruction from the category over sets (46, 48).** Scan 46 specifies a cartesian-square condition and an equivalence involving \(T(\widehat C)\). It proposes recovering \(T(C)\) as a full subcategory of functors \(C^\circ\to\mathscr S\), tested for representability by components of \(\beta\). Scan 48 explores a category generated by \(\Sigma\) under direct limits and a restriction equivalence for functors preserving limits of given types. A diagonal cancels most of this latter construction.

**Presheaves on an ordered category (49).** Basic opens \(O_x=\{y\mid y\leq x\}\) give the proposed space. The author examines a covering of \(O_x\) and uses the member containing \(x\) to analyze the sheaf condition. In the converse direction he singles out points with a smallest open neighbourhood.

**Localization and a group action (51–52).** For \(H\subset G\) and \(X=G/H\), the fibre at a chosen point gives an equivalence between \((B_G)_{/X}\) and \(B_H\). The page identifies the two adjoints with a contracted product and an equivariant Hom. It then interprets the example through coverings of a space with fundamental group \(G\). Scan 52 passes to a space \(Z\) with a group action and exhibits restriction to \(H\) by a slice over \(Z'=Z\times X\).

**Sites (53).** The outline passes from covering families to covering sieves, then to the condition that a presheaf be a sheaf or separated. It considers the order on topologies, generators for a topology and the finest topology adapted to a given family of presheaves.

**Presentations of topoi (54).** Theorems numbered 3 and 5 concern the correspondence between topologies and full subcategories of presheaves, and presentations of a category as a sheaf category. Theorem 4, “Comparison lemma”, compares two sites when one embeds fully faithfully and covers the objects of the other.

**Associated sheaf (55).** After the base-change test for a family of sieves, section 4 defines \(LF(X)\) as a direct limit of \(\operatorname{Hom}(R,F)\) over covering sieves. The properties of \(F\to LF\) lead to the associated-sheaf functor. Two corollaries treat limits and generation by sheafified representables. The page then gives criteria for a covering family and a covering sieve.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01 · Model remark, high confidence (49).** The use of an ordered category makes the covering argument particularly short. If a basic open \(O_{x_i}\subseteq O_x\) contains \(x\), the defining inequalities give \(x\leq x_i\leq x\). Consequently these two basic opens coincide, even if two equivalent elements of the preorder are written with different names.
- **R02 · Model remark, high confidence (51–52).** The role of the chosen point on 51 is taken by the chosen section on 52. This comparison explains why the base of that section matters in L04. Both pages give the data explicitly.
- **R03 · Model remark, low confidence as an objection to the manuscript (42).** For the cancelled ring map as transcribed, \(C=\mathbf F_2\times\mathbf F_2\) gives a test of S03. Each of its four elements is the unique unit on its supporting direct factor, extended by zero on the complement. The resulting map has four singleton fibres, yet \((1,0)(0,1)=0\). Thus this map alone would not detect fields. A connectedness restriction or other tests on \(C\) may have been intended in the abandoned argument.
- **R04 · Model reading question, low confidence (53, formula below the order relation).** The frozen denominator reads \(C/F\) in the formula beginning \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat C}(R,F)\). A possible reading is \(C/R\), since \(R\) is the presheaf in the source and the limit uses values \(F(X)\). The visible letter has an F-like upper stroke. The collation was restricted to marked spans, and this note leaves that reading open.
- **R05 · Model remark, high confidence (54–55).** The order of theorem numbers on the sheets reverses the order of exposition. Th. 2 on 55 recovers covering sieves from their images under the functor into sheaves. Th. 3 on 54 states the corresponding order-reversing relationship with full subcategories. Their shared test is visible without supplying the unfinished last sentence of 55.
