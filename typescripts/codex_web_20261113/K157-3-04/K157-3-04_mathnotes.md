# Mathematical notes · cote 157-3, scans 50–65

Scan numbers refer to the supplied images. A page number in a quotation is the author’s reference.

## What the pages do

**50–51 · Chapter XII.** The cover gives three subjects. The contents situate the characterization of $W_\infty$ before a discussion of fibrations, homotopy pushouts and pullbacks, and homotopy fibres. Several entries have been cancelled or moved. The second appendix is marked “conjectural”.

**52 · Saturation and localisation.** Grothendieck seeks conditions on a class $W$ of arrows in $\mathrm{Cat}$ that imply $W\subset W_\infty$. The first axiom concerns identities, composition and the two composites $fg,gf$. The second detects membership locally over each object of a base. He singles out the case $Y=S$ and calls the weaker condition $W(2\,\mathrm{bis})$ in the margin.

**52–53 · Proposition 1.** The three conditions concern cylinder projections, categories with a final object, and homotopisms. He uses slices to deduce membership of the cylinder projections in $W$ from the asphericity of categories with a final object. A marginal note records where localisation enters.

**53 · Homotopic maps.** A lemma on $f,g$ gives equality of their images under $\mathrm{hot}_W$ and equivalence of their membership in $W$. The proof compares the two sections of a cylinder projection. He applies it to $gf$ and $fg$ and returns to the other implications of Proposition 1.

**54 · Final and initial objects.** He adopts the final-object formulation as axiom 3. Three corollaries concern the slices $X_{/y}$, categories with an initial object, and homotopisms and homotopic maps.

**54–55 · Proposition 2 and duality.** He recalls a category whose objects are the arrows of $X$ and uses it to discuss $f$ and $f^\circ$. A corollary compares axioms 2 and 3 with their duals. The statement, argument and subsequent references do not all carry the same signs or numbers.

**55–56 · Conditions on a fibration.** Four conditions on a map $X\to Y$ test changes of base. The diagram places $a$ above $a'$, with $a_1$ and $a_2$ below. In axiom 4 the left condition is to give the strongest change-of-base property. He discusses duality and asks how much is already forced by $a_1$.

**57 · Proposition 3 and its corollary.** For a map between $W$-fibrations over $S$, the fibrewise hypothesis gives membership of the total map in $W$. The proof compares fibres with slices. The corollary uses a set of objects meeting each component of $S$.

**57–58 · Converse and auxiliary lemma.** For the converse he wants to change base to an aspherical category over a component. This leads to the lemma about a fibration $S'\to S$ from a $W$-aspherical source when $S$ is $0$-connected.

**58–59 · Paths out of a fixed object.** He proposes two choices for $S'$, using $\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}$ or $\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}_\infty$, with the map taking a path to its target. He pursues the first to avoid imposing a hypothesis about inductive limits. He separates what is known for $W_\infty$ from what has to be shown for $W$.

**59–61 · The path argument.** A functor to the category of path types has fibres denoted $Z_\tau$. He reduces to smoothness and properness of $p_S:\underline{\mathrm{Ch}}(S)\to S\times S$. For smoothness he compares a category of liftings with a category having a final object. He obtains properness by passing to the opposite category and declares the auxiliary lemma proved under a fibration hypothesis.

**61 · Proposition 4 and connectedness.** He states the converse fibre criterion using axioms 1–4. Axiom 5 requires a map in $W$ to induce a bijection on $\pi_0$. The componentwise argument begun at the foot of this page is struck through into scan 62.

**62 · Factorisation.** He replaces $f$ by a map $\bar f$ which is a fibration for both classes. Propositions 4 and 3 enter a reduction to $W$-aspherical categories. Applying the path construction at $x_0$ brings in its fibres.

**63 · Iterated loops.** The lemma says that the loop category of a pointed $W$-aspherical category is again $W$-aspherical, and he iterates it. The corollary adds axiom 5. He pauses over the identification of these iterates with classical loop spaces.

**63–64 · Theorem 1.** He states $W\subset W_\infty$ under five axioms and characterizes $W_\infty$ as the largest class satisfying them. He then considers intersections, a smallest such class and a possible additional descent condition.

**64–65 · Weaker forms of axiom 4.** He lists variants involving left and right fibrations, proper and smooth maps, and conditions on fibres. The revised implication diagram runs through $a,a',a'',b,b',b''$. He returns to the weakest version needed for the lemma on manuscript page 12.

**65 · Filtered limits and Proposition 5.** Axiom 6 concerns a filtered inductive limit of $W$-aspherical categories. A possible equivalent formulation would concern arrows. The page ends with the opening hypotheses of Proposition 5, followed by a comma.

## Questions and conjectures

- **51.** “Recollement d’un ouvert et d’un fermé … (conjectural)” is the second appendix title. The mathematical prefix is doubtful.
- **52.** “Je voudrais caractériser $W_\infty$” sets the objective. “Je présume” and the immediate “non, j’ai dû” record a change of mind about weak saturation.
- **55–56.** “Ce que j’aimerais éviter (si je peux)” concerns using $W_\infty$ to define the fibration axiom. “Si je me rappelle bien” qualifies the recalled criterion. “Je ne vois pas, sans axiomes supplémentaires sur $W$, comment passer de $a_1$ à $a'$ seulement” states the unresolved implication.
- **56–58.** “Alors a-t-on $f_s\in W_s$ ?” returns to the fibrewise converse. The proposed auxiliary result begins “Lemme (?)”. The margin later says “Est prouvé moyennant l’axiome” and mentions remaining small verifications.
- **59–61.** “Il y aurait cercle vicieux !” explains the search for a direct argument. “Je prévois” qualifies the anticipated weak fibration properties. After Proposition 4 he asks “si j’en sais assez pour pouvoir prouver” the inclusion.
- **63.** “‘On sait’ est un tout petit peu léger”; he asks about “les $\Omega^n$ classiques (??)” and the definition of asphericity, “définie comment ?”.
- **64.** “Ou est-ce que ces propriétés assurent déjà que $W=W_\infty$ ?” leads to a question about the structure of $W_\infty$-aspherical objects and to an “axiome de ‘descente homotopique’”.
- **65.** “Je ne pense pas que même la version la plus forte” expresses doubt about equality with $W_\infty$. “Ceci devrait être équivalent” introduces the prospective formulation of the filtered-limit condition.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model observation — high confidence, scans 56–57 and 61.** “Montrons déjà au moins l’inverse” explains the order of Propositions 3 and 4. Their hypotheses and conclusions put membership of the total map and membership of its fibre maps on opposite sides.
- **Model observation — high confidence, scans 58, 61 and 63.** The marginal “Est prouvé” on 58 refers to the connected-base fibration lemma. Scan 61 explicitly calls that lemma proved. The separate concern on 63 is the comparison of iterated loop categories with classical ones.
- **Model reading question — low confidence, scans 59, 64–65.** At the right edge of the intersection paragraph on 64 the frozen subscript is omega or infinity. Pages 59 and 65 speak of a smallest class for a shorter list of axioms, whereas the paragraph on 64 follows a list numbered 1 through 5. The later clearer omega does not settle the damaged mark or establish that both smallest classes are the same.
