# K137-02 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**017 · Multiplicities.** The first four conditions recover the multiplicities in an earlier decomposition from elementary divisors, ranks of homotopy or homology groups, and ranks of boundaries. Two further rank formulas concern cycles and the term in a given degree. Several coefficients in these formulas have been overwritten.

**017–018 · Splitting the chain complex.** The argument invokes Dold–Puppe to pass to chains. The diagram at the bottom of 017 identifies a two-term inclusion with a boundary inside a cycle group. On 018 the author takes the inverse image of the torsion subgroup in a quotient. The resulting saturated submodule and the torsion-free quotient give the two rows of the next diagram.

**018–019 · Elementary divisors.** Under the inserted qualification « si k principal », the inclusion splits into elementary maps from k to k. The unit factors are counted separately. Ordering the remaining scalars by divisibility gives divisor multiplicities. The complex with scalar n is then identified with the object Φ(i,n). Its extension has two successive exterior degrees of Ψ as its ends, and the page computes its homotopy in degree i−1.

**019 · Hilbert function and recognition.** The Hilbert function of the elementary object is compared with that of an exterior power of Φ. The rank of the level indexed by n is written as two binomial sums. The next statement describes the isomorphism class using the ranks and torsion data together with the function for large n. The isomorphism itself need not be unique.

**020 · Flasqueness and the list of generators.** The author specifies which factors may occur before and after passage to the field of fractions. He then reduces the description of the additive category to morphisms among the displayed indecomposables. Allowing the unit parameter reduces the list to two kinds of object.

**021 · Maps out of an elementary object.** A map into a complex is represented by an element whose differential lies in a prescribed multiple of the preceding term. Applying this to two elementary objects produces the cases j=i and j=i+1, with vanishing away from those degrees. The page describes a generator in the adjacent-degree case and writes an endomorphism group as multiples of the identity.

**021–022 · The Ψ terms and the extension.** The tables also compute maps to and from exterior powers of Ψ. They name the identity, the canonical projection or inclusion, and the appropriate degree shifts. The two-row diagram under « NB » expresses the class of Φ(i,n) by changing the left end of an exact sequence through multiplication by n.

**023 · Abstract setup.** Conditions (a) and (b) on the cochain complex Φ specify acyclicity and the Hom pattern between its terms. Cycles give the objects Ψ. Formula (*) presents Φⁱ between Ψⁱ⁻¹ and Ψⁱ. Three tables describe the resulting groups of maps between terms and cycles, including the special case i=j=0.

**023–024 · Maps of extensions.** The author makes Φ(i,n) by changing the extension class and draws the maps on its ends. Away from equal degrees, a surviving map can factor through a quotient and a subobject. At equal degree the end maps act by p and q. The obstruction is written as a difference of two extension classes. With the basis hypothesis (c), it yields qm=pn. The vanishing of the remaining Hom accounts for uniqueness once the two end maps are given.

**025 · The remaining degree for maps between extensions.** In the case j=i−1, the three-row diagram singles out a composite between Ψ terms. Its scalar multiple vanishes. The argument concludes α=0. The following brace summarises all degrees and describes the generators in the two possible nonzero cases. The last paragraph refers to the choice of a greatest common divisor.

**026 · From Φ(i,n) to Ψʲ.** Restriction to the subobject and comparison with a neighbouring Φ term narrow the possible degrees. The diagram tests j=i−1. The author shows that the candidate factors through a zero Hom. The final two-case display leaves the canonical map when j=i.

**027 · From Ψⁱ to Φ(j,n).** Projection to the quotient first detects the unequal-degree case. In equal degree the proposed map would induce a scalar d on the quotient. The resulting pulled-back extension has zero class, forcing the scalar to vanish in the argument on the sheet. Only degree j=i+1 survives in the final table. The paragraph below the divider adds the finite-sum hypothesis (d).

**028 · Additive conclusion.** Knowing the full subcategory on the named objects, with its « ½-additive » structure, is said to determine the category up to equivalence. An identification with strictly ∂-perfect ½-simplicial abelian groups follows. The text explicitly leaves composition unwritten and the tensor structure and operations undetermined.

**029 · The restricted representable functor.** For a full subcategory A of C, the canonical map to presheaves on C is followed by restriction to A. Presenting each object of C as the specified colimit of a diagram in A yields a full-faithfulness criterion. The second diagram compares the group-valued and set-valued versions via forgetful functors.

**029–030 · Essential image.** A further limit condition describes how the image is built from representables. Stability under the specified limits is used to state an equivalence. The last paragraph specialises to an additive C in which every object is a finite direct sum of objects of A. The image then consists of finite direct sums of representables among additive contravariant functors.

## Remarks of the model

**R24 · Arithmetic check, high confidence for the numerical example.** With m=6, n=4, p=3, q=2, both qm and pn equal 12. Here q/p=2/3, n′/m′=2/3 and m′/n′=3/2. This distinguishes the two readings algebraically. The attribution of a slip to the manuscript remains low confidence because of the handwriting. See S24b.

**R28 · Composition and the scope of the description, high confidence.** The author has named many one-generator Hom groups by 027. On 028 he says that their composition was omitted. A reader reconstructing the additive category still has to identify the composites of those named generators. His subsequent sentence separately asks for the tensor structure and the operations.

**R29 · Changing the type of limit, medium confidence.** The first criterion on 029 uses objects of A over X and has rightward limit arrows. The additional condition near the bottom of 029 and the limits on 030 have leftward arrows. This difference matters to a reading of the essential-image claim. The later occurrence of « d'objets de C » after the leftward limit may also deserve comparison with A, but the direction of the argument there is less explicit than in the square-bracketed aside S29.

**R30 · Finite sums, high confidence.** The final live example puts « finie » above the direct-sum clause about objects of C. At the end of the same sentence it writes « une somme directe finie de foncteurs repr. ». The two finiteness conditions are present at different places in the ink.
