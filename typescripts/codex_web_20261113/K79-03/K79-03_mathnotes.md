# Mathematical notes · cote 79, scans 33–48

## What the pages do

**Scans 33–36 · Changing a repère.** Two triangles share the edge $\{s,t\}$. Replacing the third vertex by $u'$ or permuting the three entries gives the three moves in the second (81). With respect to $(x,y)$ the manuscript writes three matrices in (82) and takes their products in (83). Lifting from the primed groups leaves sign choices. Fixing the unipotent image of $\rho_0$ leaves (87) and (88). Their images of $\rho_\infty$ have orders six and three respectively. Scan 36 weighs this distinction against the simple geometric description by transpositions and a reflection.

**Scans 37–38 · Universality.** The relations for $r_i$ and $\lambda_i$ are followed by two proposed group presentations. Grothendieck distinguishes satisfying the relations from proving that the resulting homomorphisms are isomorphisms. He expresses the claimed universality geometrically. A choice of direct repère on his integral object and a repère on an oriented triangulated 2-polyhedron $P$ should determine a unique homomorphism carrying the first repère to the second.

**Scans 38–39 · Forgetting the symplectic structure.** Section III starts with a free module $M$ of rank two over $\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, with $n\ge3$ in the finite case. It forms $P_M$ as a direct sum of unoriented polyhedra indexed by $(\det M)^*/\{\pm1\}$. The two elements of $\mu$ give the orientations in (91). Over the finite ring the text counts $\varphi(n)/2$ components and lists $3,4,6$ as the connected cases in (92).

**Scans 40–41 · Three extra structures.** The scalar group acts on the polyhedron in (95). It also gives a torsor structure on the set of components in (97). Equation (98) describes the action induced by $\dot g$ on those components using its determinant, and (99) gives the square for a scalar. The third structure (101) identifies the vertex sets of different components. A strict automorphism respects all three.

**Scans 41, 43, 42, 44 · The strict automorphism group.** Scan 43 supplies the missing end of the definition on 41, compares two exact sequences, and states the isomorphism theorem (104). Injectivity invokes a previous identification for one component. In the surjectivity argument on 42 the author chooses $i$, matches the restriction of an automorphism on $P_{M,i}$, and uses (101) to propagate the identity through the vertex sets. The remark at the foot of 42 continues at the head of 44.

**Scans 44–45 · Triangulations on a common set.** The data in (105) consist of a vertex set $S$, a torsor $\pi$ and the family $\Sigma(i)$ of triangles. Conditions a–c describe connected triangulations, a common automorphism group $G$, and disjoint sets of edges and faces for different indices. Conditions d and e introduce the torsor action, surjectivity onto $Z'_\Lambda$ and a permutation $T_{\dot\lambda}$ specified by its effect on adjacency.

**Scans 46–47 · Recovering a vertex.** The sets $S_{u,i}$ collect the neighbours of $u$ in each triangulation. Their union and the map $p_u$ retain which index supplied each neighbour. Equation (113) claims that these data determine $u$. The author relates the claim to agreement up to sign of the forms $z\mapsto u\wedge z$ and $z\mapsto u'\wedge z$, then leaves a reduction about two lines in a plane to be explained.

**Scans 47–48 · The pairing.** Equations (114)–(117) put the determinant torsor $\pi$ inside the line modulo sign $L'$ and set $S=M^*/\{\pm\mathrm{id}_M\}$. The wedge gives a symmetric, $L'$-valued pairing on $S$. Its value $i$ selects the edges. The three edges of a triple select its face. Equations (122)–(123) express the requirements at an edge and at a vertex, respectively.

## Remarks of the model

- **M33 and M35 · High.** Multiplying the three pairs of integral matrices from (82) in the written order reproduces all three matrices in (83). Direct powering gives orders 3 and 6 for $-\lambda_\infty$ and $\lambda_\infty$. These checks use the displayed matrices.
- **M39 · High for the finite check.** Counting the units for $3\le n\le20$ gives $\varphi(n)=2$ precisely at $3,4,6$ in that interval. The computation does not address larger $n$.
- **M41 · High.** The determinant of a rank-two scalar matrix is its scalar squared. This accounts for the square when (98) is specialized to (99).
- **M47 · High for the two examples.** Over $\mathbb F_3$ and $\mathbb F_5$, the complement of the coordinate axes contains the independent pair $(1,1),(1,2)$. These examples test the last reduction. The general argument requested in the margin remains open on these pages.
- **M47a–b · Orbit-size reading.** For $n=10$, multiplication by units on $(\mathbb Z/10\mathbb Z)/\{\pm1\}$ has four orbits, represented by $\{0\},\{1,3\},\{2,4\},\{5\}$. The determinant generators give $\{1,3\}$, but $\{2,4\}$ also has size $\varphi(10)/2=2$. **High** for this finite enumeration (M47b). **Low** for attributing an error to the manuscript (M47a), since the orbit sentence contains a doubtful word and the construction may presuppose a qualification. On the literal size-only reading, the example refutes uniqueness.
- **R33 · Reading question, scan 33, L54.** The frozen wording is “qui du reste”. On enlargement the last word has a tall central $t$, and the next sheet begins “point de vue actuel”. “qui de notre” fits both the strokes and the sentence across the break. High confidence in this alternative.
- **R41 · Reading question, scan 41, L14.** (100) reads $S_{M,i}\simeq M^*$. The alternative suggested by (117) is $M^*/\{\pm\mathrm{id}_M\}$. No denominator is visible at (100). Low confidence that the author meant to identify the two notations here.
