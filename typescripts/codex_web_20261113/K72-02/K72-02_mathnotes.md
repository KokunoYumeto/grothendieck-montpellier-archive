# Mathematical notes on cote 72, scans 18–21 and 23–34

## What the pages do

**Scan 18.** The diagonal values $c_i=f(a_i)$ and two ordered pairings of neighbouring basis vectors give $c_{i+1}=\beta_i c_i$. The manuscript writes $f_V$ up to an overall factor, first as two sums, then term by term and in grouped form. It concludes that the projective bundle $C$ has a unique invariant hyperquadric $Q_C$. A lower part of the resulting automorphism diagram is cancelled.

**Scan 19.** The category denoted by a boxed $C_0'''$ has objects $(C,Q_C,\varphi_G^C,h_0)$. The three conditions concern the generating pseudoreflections, independence of their centres in each fibre, and incidence of one centre with the next hyperplane. The sheet attempts to express the last two conditions using the group action alone.

**Scans 20–21.** For even $n$ the manuscript passes to an action through $\operatorname{SO}(f_V)$. In the example $n=2$, the smooth quadric $Y=Q_C$ is a projective-line bundle. The conditions on the original projective action become conditions on $Y$. These include absence of an invariant divisor of degree two in each geometric fibre and noncommutation of each consecutive pair of generators.

**Scans 23–24.** Automorphisms inducing the identity on $C$ are sought in the form $u=\mathrm{id}+\pi\otimes\lambda\widetilde o$. Orthogonality gives equation (3), $\lambda(\lambda\sigma'\rho'+\sigma)=0$. The manuscript separates even and odd $n$ in (4) and imposes $\lambda\rho=-2$ for a reflection in (5). Cases a)–c) distinguish an invertibility hypothesis from characteristic two. Formulas (6) and (7) give the automorphism called the *antipodisme*.

**Scans 25–26.** The opening NB asks when the antipodisme is fibrewise nonidentity. After a double horizontal rule the direction of construction changes. From the action on $X$ and its subbundle $C$, the manuscript recovers the sequence with terms $V,E,\underline{\mathcal O}_S$, lines $L_i,L'_i$ and consecutive pairings. A section $s_0$ fixes the scale of $a'_0$. In the reverse construction a choice of $a'_0$ gives an affine line $\Delta'$ inside the plane $F=\bigcap_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathcal H_i$.

**Scans 27–28.** The four-page heading introduces an invariant Hermitian form. Scan 28 sets $c_i=f(a_i)$, $\mu_i=\lambda_i-1$ and $d_i=c_i/\mu_i$. For adjacent vectors it writes $d_{i+1}=\overline{\beta_i}\,\overline{d_i}$. The expansions distinguish even and odd indices, then impose conditions for quantities to belong to $k_0$. Several bars, exponents and trial factors have been crossed out.

**Scan 29.** Two ordered products of rank-two matrices give a common trace and determinant. Substitution into $A(\tau_0\tau_1)$ yields an expression in $\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\beta$. The page then introduces $x=c/(2-k_1)$ and reduces the search for real solutions to a quadratic. The case in which one of $p,q,r$ is 2 is set aside. **M29**

**Scan 30.** A crossed calculation compares the preceding coefficients with trigonometric expressions. Below it, a boxed parameter replaces $\beta_i$ by a scalar times $\mu_i\mu_{i+1}$. The recurrence for $d_i$ is written again, then converted to one for $c_i$ involving norms. The left-hand list starts at $c_0=1$. Two adjoining formulas give values of the pairing in the opposite orders of its arguments.

**Scan 31.** A diagonal cancellation traverses the sheet. The draft starts with $f(a_0)=1$, calls $f(a_1)$ the scalar $c$, and develops the rank-two positivity condition $0<c<2-k_1$. It displays a three-by-three matrix, a boxed normalized trace expression, and the actions and matrices of $\tau_0,\tau_1$. These are cancelled working lines throughout.

**Scans 32–33.** The new title asks how to recover the generators $\tau$ from the products $\rho$. Scan 33 begins with $V$, a quadratic form, rank-one subbundles $L_i$ and a section $a_0$. It requires orthogonality at nonconsecutive positions and nonorthogonality for neighbours in each fibre. A two-row drawing connects primed and unprimed lines. The relation $\varphi(a_i,a_{i+1})=-f(a_{i+1})$ successively fixes bases. The calculations at right use an auxiliary $b_{i+1}$ paired to 1 with $a_i$.

**Scan 34, above the divider.** The squared pairing of two neighbours, divided by their diagonal quadratic values, is identified with $\beta_i$. The page writes $\beta_i=4\cos^2\theta_i$ and $\alpha_i=\beta_i-2=2\cos 2\theta_i$. It calls $\alpha_i$ the invariant of the product of the two reflections. **M34**

**Scan 34, below the divider.** Two ray sketches and the products $\rho_0=\tau_0\tau_1$, $\rho_1=\tau_1\tau_2$ lead to presentations of $G_2$ and $G_2^+$. The author defines two restricted sets of homomorphisms into $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$. On the subgroup side he imposes fibrewise conditions and adds a local projective incidence condition involving $Z$ and $X_0,X_1,X_2$. He ends by asking about bijectivity of restriction. **P34**

## Remarks of the model

- **R29. Model, high confidence.** In the rank-two calculation $A(M)=(\operatorname{Tr}M)^2/\det M-2$ is unchanged by scalar multiplication of an invertible matrix. This accounts for its usefulness when the underlying action is projective. The identity and the two product matrices were checked symbolically.
- **R34-Q. Model, high confidence.** Wherever the denominator is invertible, $\varphi(a_i,a_{i+1})^2/[f(a_i)f(a_{i+1})]$ is independent of the individual scalings of the two vectors. Bilinearity supplies one copy of each scale in the pairing and homogeneity supplies its square in each diagonal value.
- **R34-G. Model, high confidence about the scope of the calculation.** The ray sketches and group identities specify the products that any extending triple of reflections must have. Existence and uniqueness of such a triple with the stated projective incidence conditions would require more. The question mark on the last line leaves that part open.

**Reading questions noticed while preparing these notes.** On scan 33, L41, the frozen word beside the boxed equality is “inv.”. The small initial stroke, low join and final point can also be read “i.e.”. The former would label an invariance condition, while the latter would introduce the explanation below. On scan 34, L65, the frozen live word after the cancelled word now read *sans* is “doit”. Its middle letter can equally resemble an open *u*, giving *dout[e]*. The enlarged ink has a short terminal stroke, so the spelling and the scope of the cancellation remain uncertain. Both questions have low confidence.
