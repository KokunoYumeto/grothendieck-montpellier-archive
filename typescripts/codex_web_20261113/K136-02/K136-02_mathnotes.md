# Cote 136 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**343, algebra maps.** A frame sets maps \(u_i:A\to B\) beside polynomial evaluations \(T\mapsto b_i\). It records a linear relation among the maps, relations among powers of their values, and the prime ideals \(\mathfrak p_i=\ker u_i\). The calculation stops after a difference of two evaluations.

**343, recovery of a simplex.** A second construction assigns to \(X_*\) maps out of \(F_{X_*}\). A ladder compares Hom out of an inverse limit with a direct limit of Hom terms over simplices. At the foot, a separate triangle relates the projections of a product of \(F_i\) to the maps \(u_i\).

**345, the cochain functor.** The sheet uses the multiplicative and divided-power structure of \(C^\bullet(X_*)\) in a formula for \(F_*(X_*)\). On a simplex, \(P^m_*\) appears as a fibre and a torsor. A short sequence involving \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) accompanies it.

**345, a calculation with two faces.** The sheet writes \(X_*\) as a “Coker” of two arrows between simplices and \(C^\bullet(X_*)\) as a “Ker” of the induced arrows. A diagram follows the projections of \(\Phi_1=k^2\) to \(k\), and compares them with the exterior square. The last Hom expression uses a two-term complex with zero differential.

**346, operator relations.** Two lists compare \(d,\delta,\lambda,\mu_i\) and the degree operator \(\Theta\). The product-rule calculations lead to boxed identities involving \(\mu_f,\mu_{f'}\) and \(\mu_{\Theta f}\). The lower left arranges words in the operators into sums with prescribed exponent positions.

**346, divided powers.** The right column opens with \(q=1\). It expands a polynomial expression in the \(\lambda_i\varphi_i\), collects terms by the \(p_i\), then writes a map on a product of divided powers indexed by the \(q_i\). Its target lowers one exponent \(q_\alpha\) at a time. Several arrows and a final divided-power term carry questions.

**348, a half differential algebra.** The definition begins with a resolution \(C^*\) of \(k\). There are two products, \(Z^i\times C^j\to C^{i+j}\) and \(C^i\times Z^j\to C^{i+j}\). The numbered conditions address agreement on pairs of cycles, associativity when two factors are cycles, a unit, graded commutativity, and the square of an odd cycle. A square and an adjacent formula record compatibility with the differential.

**349, a nilmanifold.** The English typescript gives forms \(dx,dy,dz+x\,dy\) for a group \(H\), takes a quotient by \(\Gamma\), and draws a map from \(M\) to \(H/\Gamma\) by integration along paths. The next arrow takes the abelian quotient to a torus. The text identifies the composite with the Jacobian determined by two forms.

**350, passage to sections.** For an object \(U\) the notes form \(C^*(U)\), discuss its cohomology product, and write \(H^*(C^*(U))\to H^*(U,k_U)\) when \(C^*\) resolves \(k\). The exact wording about the multiplicative properties of this comparison is faint.

**350 and 352, an exterior-algebra example.** The section \(T\) acts by exterior multiplication in the complex beginning with \(\Phi\to\bigwedge^2\Phi\). The quotient \(\Psi=\Phi/k.T\) enters the identification \(Z^i\simeq\bigwedge^i\Psi\). The next handwritten leaf puts the known cycle products above and below an intermediate map \(\sigma_{ij}\), and writes a Hom group for the indeterminacy of choosing it.

**351, periods of loops.** In the model with \(dy=x_1\wedge x_2\), the typescript gives two ordinary periods and a third integral with an iterated correction. It associates homology invariance to the first two and homotopy invariance to the third. A triangular matrix describes the multiplication on traversing two loops in succession.

**353, a comparison of models.** Theorem D compares the smooth de Rham model with the rational homotopy model after extension to the reals. A smooth triangulation supplies smooth and polynomial forms mapping to piecewise smooth forms. The paragraph uses cohomology and uniqueness to conclude the comparison.

**353, consequences for homotopy.** The typescript distinguishes the simply connected case, including Whitehead products, from the tower \(N_k\) in the presence of a fundamental group. It describes \(\Gamma_k\) inside \(N_k\) as discrete with compact quotient and states that this property determines \(N_k\) up to isomorphism.

**355, functors and representability.** The upper block compares Hom categories using \(A_0,B_0,F_0,G_0\) and opposite categories. A box writes \(F(X_*)\) using two chain conventions. In the turned block, the two expressions \(\mathrm{Hom}(X,\varphi(M))\) and \(\mathrm{Hom}(\varphi(X),M)\) lead to different representability conditions. An intervening triangle compares Hom categories by restriction.

**357, the Eilenberg–Zilber–Cartier comparison.** For a bisimplicial abelian object the notes compare totalization after normalization with normalization of the diagonal. They give maps in both directions, homotopies \(h,k\), and equations for comparing two choices of these homotopies.

**357, shuffles and the cup product.** The permutation conditions preserve the order in each of two blocks. The sheet depicts a shuffle by line segments, writes a sum of degeneracies, then gives an Alexander–Whitney expression using face operators. The cochain diagram leads to the cup product and to the statement about commutativity on cohomology.

**358, a product on Eilenberg–Mac Lane objects.** The product \(K(\mathbb Z,i)\times K(\mathbb Z,j)\to K(\mathbb Z,i+j)\) factors through a tensor product in the first triangle. The second diagram gives a construction after normalization and displays \(\mathbb Z[i]\otimes\mathbb Z[j]\). A separate calculation involving invariants and \(H^*(BG)\) is struck through.

**359 and 360, equivariance and commutativity.** Scan 359 introduces \(W_n\), gives an adjoint chain map \(\lambda\), and treats \(n=2\) using successive sphere actions. A second diagram asks about iterated products. Scan 360 compares the associativity and symmetry properties of shuffles and Alexander–Whitney, sketches a trisimplicial diagram, and states a resolution theorem attributed to Dold.

**359 and 361, a cohomology operation.** A cocycle, its tensor power, \(\lambda\), and coefficient multiplication appear in the construction of \(Pf\). Pullback along the diagonal and a decomposition using the group factor lead to the classes \(D_i x\). The last sheet relates their reindexing to \(P^i x\) and ends with the cyclic subgroup of \(\Sigma_p\).

## Remarks of the model

**R349, model remark, high confidence.** Scan 351 supplies an internal comparison for S349b. For the real family literally printed on 349, take \(B\) by setting \(x=y=z=0\), and \(A\) by changing only \(y\) to 1. Then \(A\ne B\) but \(AB=BB\). This calculation localizes the problem even if no ambient group of invertible matrices is assumed. The intended replacement of the two printed zeros remains an editorial inference.

**R348, model remark, medium confidence.** The two input types on 348 explain the shape of the square on 352. The top and bottom products concern cycles and the middle arrow is to be chosen. The pencil is too pale to derive the exact indeterminacy assertion from the prose alone.

**R357, model remark, low confidence.** On the left half of 357, the small indices on the identities are much less legible than the domains in the later equations for \(h\) and \(k\). A claim that the writer reverses a composition would depend on those indices and on which pair of arrows one follows.

**R359, reading question, low confidence.** Scan 359, page-file line 83. The frozen marginal reference reads “p 39”. On magnification the first digit appears to have a nearly closed upper loop, allowing “p 99”. The double underline is clear. The transcription keeps 39.

**R353, reading question, low confidence.** Scan 353, page-file line 13. The frozen forms diagram uses an ordinary \(\otimes\) between “polynomial forms” and \(R\). Its uneven outline permits a hand-completed tensor sign as an alternative to a single typed impression. The mathematical sign itself is clear.

**R361, model remark, low confidence.** The exponent in \((-1)^{(\ \ )}\) on 361 has a visible space between two parentheses. Neither this line nor the name of Cartan's formula supplies the sign or determines the doubtful subscript on \(D\).
