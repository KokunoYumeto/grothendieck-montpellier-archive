# Mathematical notes on cote 157-3, scans 82–97

Scan numbers refer to the archive images. Letters such as $a_0$ and $b$ refer to the author’s tests. The quotations below come from the sixteen supplied sheets.

## What the pages do

**Scans 82–83, end of an argument and Proposition 6.** The opening square compares $f_s$ on the fibres with $f_{/s}$ on the slices. Membership of the vertical maps in $W$ gives the local condition on $f$, and $W(2)$ gives $f\in W$. Proposition 6 says that pulling back a $W$-aspherical map along a $W$-smooth map gives a $W$-aspherical map. Its proof passes to a slice and invokes Proposition 5 for the comparison of the two bases.

**Scans 83–84, corollary to Proposition 6.** Pullback along a $W$-smooth $S'\to S$ preserves maps that belong to $W$ locally over the base. The diagram on 83 compares the two sliced maps. The corollary recovers Proposition 5 on taking $Y=S$. Scan 84 adds the dual statement for a $W$-proper map and the localizers with superscript $d$.

**Scans 84–85, transport of fibres.** For $W$-proper $X\to S$, the assignment $s\mapsto\mathrm{hot}_W(X_s)$ becomes a covariant functor to $\mathrm{Hot}_W$. For $u:s\to s'$, the map of slices gives the lower edge of a square. After localization the two vertical inclusions are invertible, determining the dotted upper edge. Scan 85 retains the weaker hypothesis that the fibre inclusions belong to $W$ after every base change. The margin calls the map « $W$-prépropre ». The triangle records base-change compatibility. Using the other slices gives contravariant transport.

**Scans 86–87, Proposition 7 [P7].** Suppose both sets of inclusions belong to $W$ universally, as when $f$ is both $W$-proper and $W$-smooth. For each $u$, the transports $\lambda(u)$ and $\lambda^\circ(u)$ are inverse. Pullback to $\Delta^1$ gives the two endpoint maps $i_0,i_1$. The manuscript writes the two transports in the orders $i_1^{-1}i_0$ and $i_0^{-1}i_1$.

**Scans 87–89, tests for transport.** The remarks isolate an interval test for each direction. For covariant transport the endpoint is $1$, for contravariant transport it is $0$. The more general tests use the inclusion of a final or initial object. For a final $s'$ the author uses $S'_{/s'}\simeq S'$. He singles out properness and smoothness for practical verification and for stability under composition.

**Scans 89–90, proposed hierarchy [B90].** The common test asks which maps of categories over the base become members of $W$ on pulling back by $X$. Cases $a_1,a_2$ use a final or initial object. The interval case has an overwritten label on 90 and is called $a_0$ on 91–92. Condition $b$ uses contractile categories and has a formulation by inclusion of fibres. The variant $b'$ uses $W$-asphericity. Condition $c$ allows any comparison $Y''\to Y'$ in $W$.

**Scan 91, Definition 3 and implication diagram [G91].** A $W$-perfect map is both $W$-proper and $W$-smooth. The author asserts an equivalence of the preceding variants, apart from $a_0$, for $W=W_\infty$. In the general diagram the chain $c\Rightarrow b'\Rightarrow b$ meets an arrow from « $W$-parfait » at $b$. From $b$ the two branches pass through $a_1$ and $a_2$ to $a_0$.

**Scans 92–93, Proposition 8.** The left-fibration test $a_1$ is equivalent to preproperness together with the condition that the maps of right slices belong to $W$. It is also equivalent to preproperness together with the pseudofibration condition $a_0$. Preproperness is inserted in both clauses. The proof invokes Proposition 7 for the interval comparison and uses a final object for the converse. The marginal corollary formulates a transition-map test in the Cat-cofibrant case. The dual on 93 gives a right fibration under the pre-smoothness hypothesis.

**Scans 93–94, the strict-fibre question [Q93].** If $f:X\to S$ is $W$-perfect and $S$ is contractile, must $X_s\to X$ belong to $W$? Scan 94 takes up the problem for a $W$-smooth left fibration. The square compares $X$ with a fibrant replacement $\Phi_S(X)$ and their fibres. The author defers the two verifications needed for the reduction.

**Scans 95–96, lemma on pullback.** For $X$ in $\underline{\mathrm{Fib}}_0 S$, pullback defines a functor on $\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(S',S)^\circ$. The lemma places the induced arrows in $W^d_{S'}$. The argument evaluates the transformation at $s'$. In the displayed component the arrow runs from the value at $g(s')$ to the value at $f(s')$. The conclusion on 96 is « dans $W$ fibre par fibre ».

**Scans 96–97, Corollary 1 [C96].** After localization, pullback factors through the fundamental groupoid of the functor category. In one connected component the two pullbacks of a fixed $X$ are isomorphic, « non canoniquement, en général ». The author then varies $X$ as well. At the top of 97 he concludes that homotopic $f,g:S'\to S$ induce isomorphic pullback functors.

**Scan 97, Corollary 2 and application [C97].** A homotopy equivalence of bases induces an equivalence of the localized categories. The argument uses a homotopy inverse and the two composite endofunctors. For a contractible $S$ the functor induced by $p_S:S\to e$ gives the equivalence $\mathrm{Hot}_W\to\mathrm{HOT}^{\mathrm{lc}}_W(S)$. The last sentence stops at « $X$ est isomorphe ». The object to which it is compared falls outside these scans.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · High confidence · Scans 90–93.** The arrow from « $W$-parfait » to $b$ in the diagram is the step to watch in the later question. Scan 90 formulates $b$ by inclusions of fibres over contractile bases. Scan 93 explicitly names $b$ before asking whether those inclusions belong to $W$ for a $W$-perfect map. The diagram and the question preserve different stages of the author’s assessment.

**R2 · High confidence · Scan 96.** A choice of path in the fundamental groupoid gives an isomorphism between the two pullbacks. The manuscript specifies only their connected component. This explains why the same sentence can assert existence and add « non canoniquement, en général ».

**R3 · High confidence · Scans 93–97.** The question on 93 concerns a specified inclusion of categories, $X_s\to X$. The last completed assertion on 97 gives an equivalence of localized categories. The final, unfinished sentence has only begun to describe $X$ inside the localized category. The supplied text therefore leaves the application to the specified inclusion incomplete.
