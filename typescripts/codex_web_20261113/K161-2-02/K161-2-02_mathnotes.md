# Mathematical notes · 161-2, scans 20–35

## What the pages do

**Scans 20–21 · Presenting a structure.** The starting data are objects $(X_i)_{i\in I}$. The author adds arrows, equalities between composites, and axioms on cones. The chain ending in $R_3\simeq R$ and the diagram in the turned margin keep track of categories of generators and successive topoi. The note at the foot asks how many steps can be combined. The following sheet describes a shorter approach to the assertion that a topos is “algébrique”, by writing it in terms of a small category and imposing continuity.

**Scans 21–22 · Adjoining arrows and forming a product.** The three operations labelled a), b), c) distinguish the empty structure, the addition of axioms, and the addition of a small family of arrows with given sources and targets. For c) the author writes the resulting theory as a 2-fibre product. To construct the corresponding product of topoi, he chooses small generating categories $T_0,R_0,S_0$, forms an amalgamated category $U_0$, then imposes the conditions on functors out of it. These concern finite cones and covering families. The final paragraph discusses when the image of a category of generators remains generating.

**Scan 23 · A transfinite limit.** The paragraph d) passes from two factors to a diagram of topoi, written inductively on the category side and projectively on the topos side. Compatible small generating categories $C_\alpha$ give a category $C$ and a subtopos of $\widehat C$. A separate strip of diagrams compares the $J$ and $R$ stages, with localisation arrows and a note on the free analyseur.

**Scans 24–25 · Points and subtoposes of the topos freely generated by an object.** The page begins with $B_G=\widehat C$, relativisation, and a question about restrictions of theories. It then turns to one object $X$. Tables headed by open and complementary closed subtoposes are followed by a further subtopos $V$. The lattices compare the six entries by their theories and by instances in sets. The labels distinguish objects covering $e_E$, subobjects of $e_E$, final objects and empty objects. The closing questions replace one object by an arrow or an operation.

**Scan 27 · Exactness data.** The theorem starts with pairs $(S,\lambda)$ and systems $(R_1,\delta_1,R_2,\delta_2,\varphi)$ with $\varphi$ going from $R_2$ to $R_1$. It describes categories in $\Delta$ by the existence of limits of specified diagrams and by an equivalence on categories of exact functors. The admissible functors between two such categories must preserve the indicated exactness. Conditions A) and B) give smallness assumptions and relate the two sets of test data.

**Scan 28 · The universal pair.** Given $C$ and cones $d$, the author states the existence of a map $i:C\to C'$ into an essentially small object of $\Delta$. Restriction along $i$ induces an equivalence of functor categories. The construction starts at $(C,d)$, applies an operation $K$ at successors, and takes a colimit of the $C_j$ with the images of their cones at a limit ordinal.

**Scan 29 · Requirements at a successor stage.** Clause a) prescribes the effect of restriction on functors out of the successor. Clause b) bounds its new distinguished cones. The next clauses ask for pre-exactness and for lifts to the category with duplicated cone vertices. The group $e_0,e_1,e_2$ separates equality, lifting of a morphism, and lifting of an object for the test indexed by $\varphi$. An earlier version of a lifting condition is crossed out above this group.

**Scans 30–31 · Passing to a sufficiently large ordinal.** The author collects two consequences of the successor and limit clauses, then lists what $(C_\omega,d_\omega)$ must satisfy. For exactness he compares the indexing categories of cones with a bounding cardinal. For (4) he factors a functor from $S$ through an earlier stage, passes to the duplicated cones, and arranges for the comparison arrows to become isomorphisms at a common later stage. The argument ends with “cqfd.”.

**Scan 32 · Duplicating a cone vertex.** For each specified cone the author adjoins a new vertex, a map from the old vertex to it, and a family of legs factoring the original legs. He also gives the dual instruction. The diagram with three targets illustrates the factorisation. The new distinguished cones are denoted $\bar\lambda$. The text relates preservation of the original cones to invertibility of the arrows $\bar u(i_c)$ and starts a definition of pre-exactness.

**Scan 34 · Lawvere theories and comparison with algebras.** The sheet juxtaposes a cohomology expression, a category of models, an adjunction with sets, and conditions on an object $P$. Effectiveness, a Hom test for epimorphisms and fibre products appear in the numbered list. Farther down are a comparison with a category of algebras, free/forgetful functor formulas, parallel pairs and a split diagram with face and section identities. The sheet gives no continuous argument joining all these clusters.

**Scan 35 · The dual comparison and an example.** A split diagram is followed by $\pi=f^*f_*$, its two structure maps and a comparison involving co-algebras. The sheet associates faithfulness and conservativity with the comparison and lists preservation of certain equalizers. In the group example $f_*(Y)$ is written as $\mathrm{Hom}(G,Y)$ and iteration gives a Hom expression involving $G^2$. A separate note writes down the existence of a topos with enough points alongside $B$.
