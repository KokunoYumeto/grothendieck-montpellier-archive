# Mathematical notes on scans 192–206

## What the pages do

**192–194 · Four comparison maps.** The end of the proposition compares the square $Q$ with its opposite and compares smooth with proper integration. Grothendieck identifies pairs of opposite maps, states the equivalence of four membership tests in $W$, and compares four larger diagrams of shape $\Gamma^\circ$. The stronger assertion that the canonical isomorphisms form a transitive system is left open.

**194–195 · A general indexing category.** Starting from $X_*:S^\circ\to\mathrm{Cat}$, he describes the smooth and proper integrals and the effect of taking the opposite value at each object. He adjoins an object with chosen arrows to the others, explicitly without commutation relations. An inductive system of maps $X_s\to Y$ supplies comparison maps to $Y$. He calls $Y$ a $W$-homotopy inductive limit when the comparison belongs to $W$.

**196–197 · The universal square for a span.** For a fixed span with source $X_0$, the pushout in $\mathrm{Cat}$ supplies $Q_0$. Any completion $Q$ gives a triangle through that pushout. Grothendieck then asks whether the comparison for $Q$ can belong to $W$ even when the comparison for $Q_0$ does not.

**197–198 · Theorem 4.** Section 3.4 relates the $W$-smoothness of $\varphi_Q$ to that of the three maps $f_1,f_2,f_1\alpha_1=f_2\alpha_2$. It also describes its fibre at $y$ as the smooth integral of the span of the three fibres over $y$. Successive marginal annotations distinguish the hypotheses used for the forward and converse implications. One of them refers the reader to manuscript page 156.

**198–199 · Corollary 1.** Smoothness and the $W$-asphericity of the displayed fibre integrals give $\varphi_Q\in W$. The argument invokes the assertion about smooth maps with aspherical fibres. Taking opposites gives a version with proper maps. The adjective added in the conclusion is discussed under S2 below.

**199–200 · Corollary 2.** The author treats a pushout with an open immersion and a smooth map, and gives the closed/proper case by duality. Outside $X_2$ his fibre diagram has just one nonempty branch. Inside $X_2$ one branch map is an isomorphism, and he invokes Proposition 13. A marginal note sets out the weaker data the fibre argument uses.

**200 · Corollary 3.** For a cover $X=U_1\cup U_2$ by two opens, their intersection supplies the remaining vertex of a $W$-cocartesian square. The text points to Corollary 2 and mentions the dual assertion for two closed subcategories.

**200–201 · Corollary 4.** For an open immersion with one-point complement $t$, the criterion reduces to the $W$-asphericity of $U_{/t}$. The proof passes to the square of slices over $t$. Grothendieck uses the final object of $X_{/t}$ and characterizes the relevant pushout by a presheaf calculation.

**201–202 · Corollary 5.** The complement may now contain more than one object. Grothendieck singles out a closed point $t_0$ with trivial endomorphism monoid and assumes the slice condition at the other objects. He factors $U\to X$ through $X\setminus\{t_0\}$ and applies Corollary 4 to the second inclusion.

**202–203 · Beginning the smoothness proof.** The open vertices of $\Phi$ give open subcategories with images $i_1(X_1)$ and $i_2(X_2)$. The restrictions test smoothness there. At a point of $i_0(X_0)$ the argument passes to a localized square and the fibres of the localized comparison map.

**203–205 · Lemma 1.** The slice of the total category at $x_0$ is identified with the integral of the span of slices at its three images. The proof views the slice as a category fibred over $\Phi$ and computes its three fibres. It admits the two identifications needed for the transition functors and the comparison map.

**205–206 · Lemma 2.** The fibre over $y$ is expressed as the integral of $X_{1y}\leftarrow X_{0y}\to X_{2y}$. The marginal diagram gives the three pullback squares. The argument checks how the transition functors act on the fibre subcategories and starts to check that the indicated cartesian arrows lie over the identity of $y$. The final sentence continues beyond scan 206.

## Questions and conjectures

- **194–195.** The interlinear « [a/à] l'air de » introduces the question of how far the four-diagram argument generalizes. « Sauf erreur » qualifies the proposed answer on 195. The accent in the addition and a few words in the later qualification remain doubtful.
- **196–197.** « Mais il doit y avoir des cas intéressants où » introduces a possible failure for $Q_0$ even though a different $Q$ works. « C'est d'autant plus plausible » leads into a comparison of pushouts in $\mathrm{Cat}$ with pushouts in categories of presheaves. No example is constructed on these leaves.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model observation, high confidence.** In the triangle on 196, the top map goes from the integral to the pushout and the right-hand map goes from the pushout to $Y$. Following them in that order gives the source and target of the diagonal. This checks the order of the factors in $\varphi_Q=\alpha_Q\varphi_{Q_0}$ on 197.
- **R2 · Model computation, high confidence.** In the first fibre diagram on 199, the source and lower vertex are empty. Enumerating objects over the three vertices leaves the one object of the upper $e$. The only arrow with both endpoints present is its identity. This gives the displayed $e$ for that case alone.
- **RQ1 · Reading question, 202, l. 1.** The frozen subscript on the identity reads $t$ with $t_0$ as an alternative. Both arguments of the adjoining Hom are $t_0$, which favours the second reading contextually. A hook at the edge of the closing brace could also be the small zero. Confidence in distinguishing the strokes is low.
