## Legibility

The widely spaced formulas usually leave enough room to distinguish a prime from a subscript and to follow an arrow. The intervening French is often harder: letters within a word share a single horizontal, while a descender may run into the next line. I trust the crossed and overlaid words least, particularly the compound on 66, the argument in the margin on 67, the bracketed reference on 69, and the words under the diagonal intervention on 71. The final words of the example heading on 73 are the least secure uninterrupted prose. The counts of uncertainty marks describe the transcription. They give no measured error rate.
