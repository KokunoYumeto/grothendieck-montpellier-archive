# Mathematical notes on cote 64, scans 40–52

## What the pages do

**40. Descriptions of the cover.** A diagram relates a pair involving $X$ and $\underline I$ to data involving $\underline J$, $t$, a covering of $\Sigma_J$, and a torsor. The annotations distinguish data that can be chosen independently. At the foot, a section of $\underline I$ gives $\underline I\simeq\underline J\amalg S$ and a canonical isomorphism $X\simeq\Sigma_J$. Several superscripts and qualifications on the cover are difficult to read.

**41. University circular.** The president of the Université des Sciences et Techniques du Languedoc sends voting materials to the teaching staff. The circular identifies the lists, explains how to use the three envelopes, and gives the dates for receipt and public counting.

**42–43. Endomorphisms of a plane over $\mathbb F_2$.** The three nonzero vectors give a three-element permutation set. The proposition distinguishes the odd and even automorphisms. Adjoining zero to each class gives the two planes $V'$ and $W$ in the endomorphism algebra. It describes their trace pairings and the conjugation action on them, including a nonsplit extension with middle term $W$. The final line identifies the trace kernel as $V'+\mathbb F_2\,1_V$.

**44–45. Three axes on the projective line.** The title leaf opens part II. The first numbered page takes a rank-two elementary abelian group acting faithfully on each fibre of a projective-line bundle. For a chosen basis it studies the fixed divisors of the two involutions, their disjointness and the quotient section defining the second divisor. Their product gives the third involution.

**46–47. Local coordinates (R46).** After trivializing the first axis and choosing a square root étale-locally, the first two involutions act by $z\mapsto-z$ and $z\mapsto1/z$. Their product acts by $z\mapsto-1/z$. The disjoint union of the three fixed divisors is a degree-six étale divisor. In the split model its points are $0,\infty,1,-1,i,-i$, with $i^2=-1$.

**48–49. Vertices, opposite pairs and rotations.** For an octahedral divisor $\Delta$, the manuscript introduces the full automorphism group of the pair $(X,\Delta)$ and proposes to identify it with the direct octahedral group. It recovers the three diagonals from equality of vertex stabilizers. The edges are the pairs outside these diagonals. The faces are the sections of the projection onto the three opposite pairs.

**49–50. The stabilizer computation.** In the split model the pointwise stabilizer of $0,\infty$ is identified with $\mu_4$. For the pair $\infty,-1$, the author writes the affine transformation $z\mapsto az+(a-1)$ and tests the remaining four points. He concludes that their two stabilizers have trivial intersection. The characteristic-five example below bears directly on that conclusion.

**51. Regularity.** An antipodism acts freely on the six vertices. The octahedral structure is called regular in $X$ when its direct automorphism group acts compatibly on $X$. The upper diagonal note discusses directness of induced automorphisms. The lower note relates the two orientations to a cover of $S$ involving a square root of $-1$.

**51–52. The three sets in the theorem.** From a regular structure the text singles out an invariant abelian subgroup acting on $X$. The theorem lists subgroups locally isomorphic to the group in part a, subgroups locally isomorphic to $\mathfrak S_4$, and configurations equipped with an antipodism and a compatible action. It announces canonical bijections. This packet ends at the announcement.

## Slips

- **50, intersection of stabilizers (S50).** The text concludes that the intersection for $\infty,-1$ is $\{1\}$. In characteristic five, the correct pointwise stabilizer for this pair in the full projective automorphism group of the displayed six-point divisor has order four. For $i=2$ over $\mathbb F_5$, the divisor is the entire projective line of rational points. The nonidentity map $z\mapsto2z+1$ fixes $\infty$ and $-1$ and permutes the divisor. This also prevents the identification of the full group in (7) with the direct group in (8) in this example. Confidence high for the counterexample (F5). The hypothesis on scan 47 permits characteristic five.
- **50, two diagonal numerical notes (P50).** The upper note gives $a^4=\pm4$ and the lower one gives $a^4=1/4$ or $-1$. Read as fourth powers of the candidates on their adjoining rows, the values in characteristic zero are respectively $16,-4,-4$ and $1/16,-1/4,-1/4$. Confidence high for the arithmetic and medium for the attachment of the marginal computations to those rows.
- **52, item c (W52).** The live text reads « configurations icosaédrales ». The six vertices, antipodism and direct octahedral group in the same item support « configurations octaédrales ». Confidence high for this local mismatch. The two words can be distinguished on the uncrossed line itself.

## Remarks of the model

- **42–43 (F2).** My finite computation checked the claims about the two planes under conjugation, their trace pairing and the trace kernel on all sixteen binary $2\times2$ matrices. The three nonzero elements of $V'$ form one orbit. The two trace-one elements in $W$ are exchanged, so neither supplies an invariant complement to the identity line. Confidence high for this finite check.
- **49 (O49).** My enumeration from the three opposite pairs gives twelve edges and eight three-point sections. This confirms the two interlinear counts independently of the assertion about the full automorphism group. Confidence high.
- **45, 47, 51–52 (D45).** My reading finds both $\mathbb D_2$ and $\mathbb D_4$ for the groups under discussion. The displayed identification on scan 47 specifies $\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2$. Confidence low in any inference that the changes of index represent a deliberate new convention.
- **45, basis sentence (Q45), and 48, (7) (Q48).** The marked field index on 45 has a short foot which can read 1, although the preceding field is $\mathbb F_2$. In (7) the subscript of Aut resembles $\Delta$, where the surrounding relative notation would suggest $S$. I would keep the first as a reading question and the latter as a possible subscript slip. Confidence low concerning what the author intended.
