# Mathematical notes on cote 105, scans 19–36

## What the pages do

**Intervals and weak equivalences (20).** The open inclusion is \(W\subset\widetilde W\). Grothendieck asks how large a family of intervals is needed to obtain it. Factoring a map puts the problem in terms of \(F\cap W\subset TF\) or \(C\cap W\subset TC\). He asks whether lifting against the indicated generating maps gives lifting against all monomorphisms in \(W\), and singles out two possible test-category settings.

**A proposed closed model structure (22).** He starts again with monomorphisms for \(C\) and a given class \(W\). He defines the right classes by lifting, then lists what still has to hold for the proposed trivial classes, the factorizations and the base-change condition. At the foot a section in a factorization makes the original map a retract of the first factor.

**Lifting classes and Quillen pairs (25).** The upper and lower stars denote classes defined by the left and right lifting properties. The page defines saturation on each side and factorization for an orthogonal pair. A proposition relates equality with the full lifting class to closure under direct factors. The two equivalent descriptions immediately below it are called a Quillen pair.

**Quillen triples (25).** The pair \((C,F\cap W)\) and the pair \((C\cap W,F)\) must both be Quillen pairs. The page adds conditions on \(W\), asks whether one is redundant, and compares membership in \(W\) with becoming invertible under localization.

**A cancelled investigation (27).** An opening question about a Kan fibration leads into tests involving \(fg\), \(gf\), identities, homotopy and membership in \(W\). Several drawings distinguish retract data from the stronger-looking condition that both composites belong to \(W\). The page ends with three numbered tests and arrows of implication. The larger cancellation passes through this investigation.

**Equivalent conditions and the replacement diagram (28).** Grothendieck compares the given classes \(TF,TC\) with classes defined by lifting, using two boxed conditions. He relates closedness, the two Quillen pairs and stability under direct factors. In the last diagram a map \(f\) is compared with a map \(g\) between replacements. The displayed tests at the right restrict the localization question to arrows between objects in \(M_{cf}\) and impose a cofibration or a fibration condition.

**Constructing the triple (30–32).** Starting from \(C\) and \(W\), he sets \(TF=C_*\), \(TC=C\cap W\) and \(F=(TC)_*\). Two factorizations are required. The two small squares exhibit the retraction used to reach \(F\cap W\subset TF\). The proposition lists the assumptions on \(C,W\). On 32 a fourth condition demands stability of \(W\) under specified base and cobase changes. The ensuing note compares a class \(W\) given by a homological or cohomological criterion with one approached through generators and lifting.

**The first factorization step (34).** For a fixed arrow \(f\), the author takes squares from members of \(\Phi_0\) to \(f\) and forms pushouts. Their amalgamated sum under \(X\) gives \(X_1(f)\) and a first factorization. He lists the size of the indexing family and the closure conditions that would put the new left map in \(\Phi\). Several bounds are revised in the margin.

**Successor and limit stages (34–36).** The right map at stage \(\alpha+1\) is obtained by applying the first step to the right map at stage \(\alpha\). At a limit ordinal he writes a direct limit. He chooses the stopping ordinal in relation to how the Hom functors of the sources and targets in \(\Phi_0\) preserve filtering direct limits. Finite presentation is his example in which the first infinite ordinal suffices. The last paragraph claims a functorial factorization under the stated assumptions.

## Slips

- **25, parenthesis below the class table, medium confidence.** The line ends \(f\in TF\). The stated factorization is \(f=pi\), with \(i\in TC\); its pairing elsewhere on the sheet suggests \(p\in TF\). The last letter has the same tall crossed stroke and descending part as the initial live \(f\), independently of the scratched character earlier on the line. The transcription retains \(f\).
- **22, condition c.** The crop shows the apostrophe between d and n in “should'nt”. The usual spelling is “shouldn't”.
- **26, printer notice.** “ELLES NE DOIVENT DONC UTILISEES” apparently lacks a form of ÊTRE before UTILISEES. The reversed printing was turned and read across the same uninterrupted line. This is a slip in the reused printer notice.
- **Reading questions.** On 34, c), the reduced sign after “rather” could be \(d\), \(\delta\) or \(c\). On 36, the frozen final object is \(X(f)\) with a doubtful, struck \(\Sigma\) or \(Z\) beneath \(X\); the lower bar could instead underline \(X\). Neither place establishes a mathematical error or a change of convention.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model remark, high confidence, scan 30.** The orientation of the retraction agrees with the equations. The pair \((i,\mathrm{id}_Y)\) gives a map from the arrow \(f\) to the arrow \(p\); \((s,\mathrm{id}_Y)\) gives its retraction. The displayed \(si=\mathrm{id}_X\) makes their composite the identity on \(f\). This checks the direction needed when closure under retracts is applied to \(p\in TF\).
- **R2 · Model remark, medium confidence, scans 34–36.** Two different size questions are visible. The bound involving the product with \(c\) controls how many squares enter one amalgamated sum. The ordinal chosen using \(\pi\) controls how far the iteration runs. This reading uses the positions of b), d) and the formula for \(I_{\alpha_0}\). It does not require deciding the overwritten small letter in c).
