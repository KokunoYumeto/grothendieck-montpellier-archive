# Mathematical notes on cote 16, scans 31–43

## What the pages do

**Scans 31–33, the two covers and the formulary.** The first cover names Hodge algebras. The second names the operators $L,\Lambda$. The typescript begins with a polarized smooth connected projective $X$, a smooth hyperplane section $Y$, its immersion, the operators of degrees $2$ and $-2$, and the projectors onto the primitive pieces.

**Scan 33, A and B [33AB].** Part A describes images and kernels by ranges of primitive projectors. The formulas numbered 1′), 2′) and 3) record products which vanish, including all four indicated products with $p_n^X$. Part B gives $\Lambda_XL_X$ and $L_X\Lambda_X$ as the identity less a sum of projectors. Both ranges include $n$.

**Scan 33, C and Corollaire [33C].** The map $\phi_*\Lambda_Y\phi^*$ is compared with $\mathrm{id}_X-\sum p_i^X$. The additions define $T_X$ and rewrite the three-term expression containing $L_X^2\Lambda_X$, $2L_X\Lambda_XL_X$ and $\Lambda_XL_X^2$ in terms of $L_XT_X+T_XL_X$. The corollary gives $C(X)$ from $A(X\times X)$ and $C(Y)$. A note at the foot proposes a condition in critical dimensions. Its two exponents are overwritten.

**Scan 34, primitive parts and two diagrams.** The proposition distinguishes the isomorphism in degrees $i\leq n-2$, the injection in degree $n-1$ and the kernel description for $i\geq n$. In the square, the two horizontal maps are powers of $L$ on $X$ and $Y$. The two vertical arrows go in opposite directions. The triangle singles out $j\geq n$.

**Scan 34, decompositions and corollaries.** The two decompositions group terms $L^jP^i$ by ranges of $i+j$. The first corollary follows restriction on the bihomogeneous pieces and isolates the map between the two primitive pieces in degree $n-1$. The second gives a kernel and describes the cokernel $E^{n-1}$ first using $H^{n-1}$, then using $P^{n-1}$.

**Scan 35, Karoubi's introduction.** Karoubi defines the Grothendieck group of an additive category by generators and the displayed additive relation. He proposes groups $K^n$ with cohomological properties related to Bott periodicity. The numbered references belong to his text.

**Scan 35, definition 1.1.** A Banach category is described using Banach spaces of morphisms over the indicated real or complex field. Condition a) writes the composition map and says that it is bilinear. A footnote specifies a universe convention.

**Scan 36, the map out of $H^*(Y)$.** The opening decomposition separates $E^{n-1}$. The following lines identify the action of $\varphi_*$ with $L_X$ on the indicated summand and say that it vanishes on $E^{n-1}$. The corollary sets out its kernel and image. The parenthesis explaining the image uses $\varphi_*\varphi^*=L_X$.

**Scan 36, compatibility identities.** Two rows compare the projectors through $\varphi_*$. The lower group of formulas gives the commutations with $L_X,L_Y$ and the identities for the two orders of pushforward and pullback. A canceled verification ends with $L_X^2$.

**Scan 37, English Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.4.** The lemma passes from an injective hull at a prime of a noetherian ring to an injective sheaf. The proof describes the sheaf on a closed set, then extends a map on a stalk. Proposition 3.4 starts the equivalence between injectivity and a direct-sum description of quasi-coherent modules. Its statement continues beyond this sheet.

**Scan 38, action on the decomposition.** The text identifies the action of $L_Y$ on the indicated summands and describes its kernel. It then treats $\Lambda_Y$, including its action on $E^{n-1}$ and its injectivity on the remaining part. Some index ranges were revised in place.

**Scan 38, comparison of operators.** A canceled pair of tentative formulas precedes two surviving pairs. The latter insert sums of projectors into the comparisons with $\varphi^*$ and $\varphi_*$. A note at the left writes a version with a power $m$.

**Scan 39, English Theorem 3.1.** After the end of a description of an injective hull, the typescript cites existence and uniqueness up to non-unique isomorphism. The theorem attributed to Matlis expresses an injective module as a direct sum of hulls of residue fields.

**Scan 39, English Proposition 3.2.** A hull at a prime is expressed as a direct limit of the submodules $E^1,E^2,\ldots$. A dimension formula compares successive quotients with successive powers of the prime tensored with its residue field.

**Scan 40, composition and algebraicity.** The first calculation names the complement of a sum of projectors $q$; the next writes $q\Lambda_X^{m-1}q$. The formula involving the projector onto the vanishing part leads to the boxed $\Lambda_Y=\varphi^*\Lambda_X^2\varphi_*$. The accompanying conclusion reads “Donc $C(X)$ … implique … $C(Y)$.”

**Scan 40, projectors with two indices.** The last paragraph considers the projections onto the separate $L_X^jP^i(X)$ components. The calculation increments the second index. A marginal question sign accompanies a symbol in this product. The algebraicity assertion that follows ends mid-thought at “Mais”.

**Scan 41, English exact sequences.** A presentation of $L$ gives a Hom–Ext exact sequence. The comparison between $\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}^i(L,M)$ and $\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i-1}(L',M)$ then supplies the induction for coherence or quasi-coherence.

**Scan 41, English Corollary 2.2.** The corollary supplies two sets of hypotheses for coherent or quasi-coherent cohomology of the derived sheaf Hom. The second set includes an Ext-vanishing bound for all coherent $F$.

**Scan 42, criterion and descent along sections [42C].** Assuming $C(Y)$, the first condition asks for an inverse on the indicated cohomology to come from an algebraic class. The second says that $\Lambda_X\varphi_*$ is algebraic. The note places this correspondence on $X\times Y$ and computes the effect of the product polarization. The resulting equivalence is $C(X)\Longleftrightarrow[C(Y)\text{ et }A^\circ(X\times Y)]$. The last line iterates it over a descending sequence of smooth hyperplane sections.

**Scan 43, separability and the case (ii).** The French typescript first extracts a residue-field separability condition from nonramification of the finite morphism. In case (ii), it passes to $A'=A/J$, records a local isomorphism and a surjection of differential modules, then chooses a minimal prime of the required dimension. It applies (18.11.5) to $A'$ and factors the resulting homomorphism. The sentence giving the conclusion continues past the bottom edge.

## Slips

- **Scan 34, final corollary [S34].** The coproduct is over $j\geq n$ but the summand reads $P^i(X)$. The decomposition higher on the sheet has $P^j(X)$ in the corresponding range. That suggests $j$ as a possible correction. Confidence medium.
- **Scan 36, second projector identity [S36].** The projectors carry $j$ and $j-2$, while the written range is in $i$. Using $j$ for that bound would bind the index in the equation. Confidence low for a correction to the whole range because its first endpoint is overtraced.
- **Scan 37, proof of Lemma 3.3 [S37].** The text has $\overline{\{X\}}$ twice after choosing a point $x\in X$. The taller X is distinct from the small x in Proposition 3.4(ii) on the same sheet. The point closure $\overline{\{x\}}$ appears intended. Confidence high.
- **Scan 38, first kernel decomposition [S38a].** The range uses $i\geq n+1$ and the summand uses $P^j(X)$. Changing the bound or changing the exponent would bind the free index. Confidence low as to the intended repair because the adjoining exponent is itself doubtful.
- **Scan 38, first surviving brace [S38b].** The factor beginning $\mathrm{id}_Y$ has a projector marked $p_X^j$. The corresponding projector on $Y$ would have lower index $Y$. Confidence low because the adjacent summation is overtraced.

The coefficient of each projector is $-1$ after the repeated occurrence at $n$ is counted back once. This checks the endpoint bookkeeping of the live formulas.

The stars in the first two places appear high on the crop. Confidence low in any suggestion of a mathematical correction.
