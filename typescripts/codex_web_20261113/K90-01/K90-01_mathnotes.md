# K90-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**H1 · Saturation over a Dedekind ring (002).** For a submodule N of a flat A-module M, the lemma defines Ñ as the inverse image of the torsion submodule of M/N. It lists four equivalent conditions. These concern the length of Ñ/N for finite-type N, the exclusion of a module A(p), a condition at each maximal ideal, and writing M as a direct limit of finite saturated submodules. Separation by linear forms is sufficient and is also claimed necessary when M is countably generated. A crossed formula involving f(gh) sits above the lemma.

**T1 · End of a connectedness proof (003, statement on 005).** After changing base to a geometrically unibranch Y, the text reduces the problem to whether a point disconnects a local spectrum. A formula for depth separates the general case from the case of a zero-dimensional fibre. The last paragraph reduces further to discrete valuation rings and a punctured spectrum consisting of one point. Several relations after *prof* and *dim* are blank in the typescript, so the exact inequalities cannot be recovered here.

**H2 · A finite invariant submodule (004).** Lemma 2 asks for a saturated finite-type N containing a given finite-type M in the algebra B of an affine group. The extra condition is ΔN ⊂ N ⊗ N. Left and right translation are expressed as a single action of G × G. Their two separate tensor-product conditions are then intersected. A parenthesis identifies the passage to saturation as a step still to be written out.

**T2 · Theorem 18.9.7 (005).** A flat local map with separable fibres is considered under either of two hypotheses on A and the residue-field extension. The theorem states that the generic fibre is connected. It also gives connectedness of X − T when the closed subset T lies over a proper closed subset of Y.

**T3 · Corollary 18.9.8 (005).** Under the strict-local hypothesis, or the henselian and primary-extension hypothesis, all fibres are asserted to be geometrically connected. The proof uses a finite local base change and reduces to the generic fibre in the integral case.

**T4 · Corollary 18.9.9 (005).** The formal fibres of the specified henselian, universally catenary local ring are said to be geometrically connected. The geometric qualifier is added by hand. The reason given is the preceding corollary and the trivial residue-field extension.

**H3 · A faithful linear representation (006).** In the finite-type case the first corollary chooses N generating B as an algebra and stable in the required sense. Its pointwise argument passes from a point acting trivially on N′ to one acting trivially on B′, then identifies the point as the identity. The last letter of the tensor condition is overwritten.

**H4 · Affine groups over a field (006).** A second corollary states that an affine group over a field is a strict projective limit of finite-type affine groups. A parenthesis qualifies the transition morphisms by the words read as “fid plats”.

**T5 · Full faithfulness for étale preschemes (007, earlier draft on 009).** Corollary 18.9.5 says that the base change $Z\mapsto Z\otimes_A A'$ is fully faithful. For separated target Z₂ the argument identifies morphisms with suitable open-and-closed subsets of a product. For a general target it descends the open graph, brings in the two projections from the double fibre product, and breaks off at “Appli-”.

**H5 · Factoring the coaction (008).** A filtration of B by Bᵢ is assumed to have flat quotients, comultiplication taking Bᵢ into Bᵢ ⊗ Bᵢ, and finite projective terms. An E is called i-admissible when its coaction factors through Bᵢ ⊗ E. The text expresses the category of finite-type modules with action as an inductive limit of these subcategories. Some letters inside the category notation are overwritten.

**H6 · Dual algebras and transition functors (008, 010).** The dual Uᵢ of Bᵢ is called an associative unital algebra. The Uᵢ form a strict projective system. The category belonging to i is identified with a category of finite-type Uᵢ-modules, and the transition functors with restriction of scalars. Several lines of an attempted qualification at the foot of 008 are cancelled.

**T6 · Open-and-closed subsets after completion (009).** Corollary 18.9.4 gives a bijection on open-and-closed subsets under the indicated base change to the completion. Its proof uses the quotient topology and connected fibres. The next paragraph obtains a bijection of connected components for locally connected X and cites Bourbaki.

**T7 · Earlier full-faithfulness argument and a cancelled remark (009).** An earlier version of 18.9.5 describes the functor using successive corrections to the classes of étale objects. Two long strokes cancel the statement and proof. The marginal “inutile !” refers to the separatedness qualifier. The following remark starts a possible characteristic-zero statement and has no surviving conclusion here.

**H7 · A projective module surjecting onto a representation (010).** Under the preceding hypotheses, a finite-type module carrying a G-representation is said to be a quotient of a finite-type projective module on which G acts. The word *projectif* is inserted and underlined.

**H8 · A locally free equivariant quotient (010, 012).** The final handwritten proposition concerns a flat affine group over a Dedekind ring and invokes lemma 1 for its algebra. On the scheme in the hypothesis, every finite-type quasi-coherent module is assumed to be a quotient of a locally free module of finite type. The conclusion applies the same kind of presentation to modules with group action. The source of the quotient is described on 012 as “un idem, qui est de plus loc. libre”, with *idem* uncertain. A possible prime on the scheme symbol in 010 is also uncertain.

**T8 · Unibranchness and universal catenarity (011).** The sheet starts with the end of an irreducibility criterion for unibranchness. The next proposition passes universal catenarity from a noetherian local ring A to the ring denoted by A with a left superscript hs. Its proof refers to (18.7.5). The last digit of the proposition number has been changed by hand.

**T9 · Formal fibres (011).** Theorem 18.9.1 states that geometrically normal formal fibres of a noetherian henselian local ring are geometrically integral, and thus geometrically connected. The argument reduces to an integral ring and the fibre over its generic point. Its last words lead directly into the next corollary.

**T10 · Completion and fraction fields (011).** Corollary 18.9.2 deduces that the completion of the indicated integral ring is integral, citing unibranchness and an earlier result. Corollary 18.9.3 concerns the fraction-field extension. It asserts separability and algebraic closedness of K inside L. Its corrected proof cites geometric integrality of the generic fibre.

**T11 · Local properties under strict henselization (013).** A continuation about separable extensions precedes the four-part corollary. It compares the Cohen–Macaulay, (Sₙ), regularity and (Rₙ) properties of the local ring with the corresponding properties of the ring denoted by left superscript hs.

**T12 · Associated primes (013).** The next corollary relates membership in the sets Ass on the two sides for a semilocal noetherian ring. The second condition literally contains the membership sign in *p ∈ A*. The demonstrations are referred back to three numbered statements.

**T13 · Geometric unibranchness (013).** Proposition 18.8.14 compares geometric unibranchness, with its reduced version, to irreducibility or integrality of the spectra of essentially étale local algebras and of the strict henselization. The final direction is argued by identifying a ring with a subring of a field by flatness. A newly labelled corollary begins with a henselian local ring and runs beyond the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · High confidence (006, H3).** The move from “N engendre l'algèbre B” to trivial action on B′ has a direct algebraic explanation. An algebra endomorphism fixing the base and each of a set of algebra generators fixes every element built from them by sums and products. This checks only that step of the faithfulness argument.

**R2 · High confidence (008, H5).** Once Bᵢ ⊗ E embeds into B ⊗ E as asserted in the preceding sentence, a factorization of the coaction through it is unique. Two possible values have the same image under an injective map. This explains the passage from “se factorise” to the displayed δᵢ even though the handwritten connector has no clear arrowhead.

**R3 · High confidence (009, T6).** A connected fibre cannot meet both an open-and-closed set U′ and its complement. Their intersections would disconnect the fibre. This accounts for the specific use of “les fibres de g sont connexes” in the saturation argument.
