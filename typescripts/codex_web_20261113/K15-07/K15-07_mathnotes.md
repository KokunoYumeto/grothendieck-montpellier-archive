# Mathematical notes on cote 15, scans 184–187 and 189–194

The English restriction argument and the French Lie-algebra typescript are two separate texts among the handwritten notes. Their authors are not identified on these faces. Quotations retain the language of the relevant sheet. The words “sans doute”, “possible” and similar qualifications matter to the mathematical status of the claims.

## What the pages do

**Restriction of sections, 186 and 184.** Read in its own pagination, the English argument starts here on p. 72. An isomorphism with \(O_V[(n-1)E]\) gives the dimension identity (15) from an earlier (14). The author locates the common zeros on the indicated subvariety and starts to exclude common zeros there as well. Sequence (16) defines the restriction map stalkwise. On p. 73 the argument passes to the sequence with \([nE]\) in (17) and the cohomology terms in (18), then chooses a maximum of two bounds on \(n\). The last sentence runs off the supplied extract.

**Real polarisations, 185.** A homomorphism out of \(\mu_{2,\mathbb C}\) is compared with a map obtained by restricting \(i(s')\) and composing with \(u(s')_{\mathbb C}\). The glyph naming the composite is uncertain. The transported polarisation and the one defined by this construction are required to agree. Holding the twist \(\xi(s')\) fixed gives a condition on the collection of \(u(s')\).

**Effectivity and Frobenius, 185 and 187.** The sentence about Tate’s conjectures crosses from “la catégorie” on 185 to finite-dimensional \(G_{\mathbb R}\)-modules on 187. The latter sheet describes a cone generated by rays associated with the \(v(s')\). It relates this condition to the eigenvalues of the Frobenius elements \(f(s')\) in representations.

**Headings, 189 and 190.** The first cover names the Galois theory of motives and its generalities. The next names motivic fibre functors and the motivic fundamental group. Neither cover introduces a theorem.

**Fibre functors in positive characteristic, 191.** For a connected prescheme the page considers \(X_{\rm réd}\) in characteristic \(p>0\). Its three items distinguish an \(\ell\)-adic object for \(\ell\ne p\), a \(p\)-adic object over \(W_p(k)\), and its reduction over \(k\). The last item carries a question mark. “DeRham-Hodge” labels the arrow toward \(k\). The margins introduce \(F,V\), a derived category and filtered structure. The text then passes to obstructions over a finite field and to possible uniqueness up to isomorphism, both at finite level and at the inverse limit.

**Dependence on points, 193.** The first paragraph discusses the geometric point and the action of \(\pi_1(X)\) on \(\xi_\ell\). For \(\xi_p^\alpha\), the author discusses points valued in a perfect field, the perfect closure of \(k(x)\), and the availability of comparisons as the point varies. The text distinguishes conjugacy under field automorphisms from the existence of chosen isomorphisms. A vertical note adds \(F,V\) with \(FV=p\). The notation table distinguishes the barred geometric point, a point denoted \(x\) by abuse of language, and the two \(p\)-adic symbols \(\xi_{x,p},\xi_{x,p}^{0}\).

**Editorial plan for existence theorems, 192.** Sheet (9) gives directions for the naming of generators, a normalization of \(X_\alpha\), and an exercise concerning Weyl’s \(h,x,y\). Its proposed §4 builds an algebra from three pieces, introduces \(E(\lambda)\), presents \(\underline g\) as a quotient, then defines \(W(\lambda)\) and states a finite-dimensionality condition. The last paragraph sketches an argument for semisimplicity using opposite root spaces and commutative ideals.

**Subalgebras and generators, 194.** Sheet (8) revises propositions about \(\underline h\)-stable subspaces, closure of a root subset and the radical, and gives criteria for reductivity and semisimplicity. It pairs the positive and negative nilpotent subalgebras with the orthogonal subalgebras named in the text. Further instructions give the repeated-adjoint relations, identify the triple \((H_i,X_i,Y_i)\) with an épinglage, and ask that a root condition be made explicit.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · High confidence.** The order p. 72 → p. 73 is directly testable across scans 186 → 184. The unfinished “then r over P is” joins “a homomorphism of restriction of a function”, and “We obtain from (16)” points to the sequence on 186. This establishes the local reading order of these two pages.
- **R2 · High confidence.** The sentence broken after “dans la catégorie” on 185 continues grammatically with “des …-modules de dim finie” on 187. The cone on 187 thus elaborates the specific effectivity just named.
- **R3 · Low confidence in the proposed identification.** The notation table on 193 supplies a later, separately written instance of a high 0 on a \(p\)-adic symbol. That makes zero a plausible reading of the small oval on 191. A superscript circle has the same outline there.
- **R4 · Low confidence.** Infinity and α are possible readings of the overwritten lower mark on \(T\) in the last paragraph of 191. The inverse-limit expression makes infinity meaningful, but the dense ink does not decide whether an index was intended to survive.
