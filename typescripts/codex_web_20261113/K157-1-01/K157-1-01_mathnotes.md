# Mathematical notes on 157-1, scans 002–017

Scan numbers refer to the supplied images. Manuscript page 1 is scan 004. French excerpts preserve the author’s wording, including provisional language. IDs identify particular statements in the check-event register.

## What the pages do

**002. Plan of the work.** Nineteen proposed chapters run from (pre)derivators and cofinality through localization, homotopy types, model categories and accessible categories. Curved arrows connect some entries. Pencil question marks accompany several of the model chapters.

**003–004. Successive adjoints.** A separate question asks when the exceptional operation for a map to $e$ exists. The numbered text starts with presheaves on an index category with values in $A$. For inverse image along $f$, it writes a provisional five-term string, then separates ordinary adjoint existence from that of the two more distant adjoints. Small ordered sets illustrate the restrictions under discussion.

**005. Two complementary immersions.** A closed $i$ and an open $j$ give descriptions of full subcategories by restrictions of coefficients. One pointed square has $F$ at the top and $e$ at the bottom. Another presents the term associated with $i^!$ through a cartesian square. The sheet writes the corresponding exact sequences in the abelian case. The second sequence as written ends at the $j$-term.

**006. Strings on simplexes.** The author first brackets two little-used operations on $i$ and $j$. He then asks whether different semisimplicial maps between two fixed simplexes give the same strings of iterated adjoints. He singles out adjacent dimensions, distinguishes four end terms from the middle terms and reports two small cases.

**007–008. Heuristic definition.** A domain of diagrams is treated as a 2-category. A derivator assigns a category of coefficients to a diagram shape, inverse image to a functor, and a transformation to a morphism between functors. Two pairs of pictures specify the directions and the order of composition. The text adopts strict composition and writes identities for both levels.

**009–010. Existence axioms.** Der₁ requires two adjoints to inverse image. The NB separates the left and right variants. Der₂ distinguishes closed and open immersions and their further adjoints. A marginal qualification introduces pointedness, while another marginal formula compares the value on a coproduct with a product of values. The author next asks how to formulate the square for a complementary pair inside a derivator.

**011–013. From an underlying square to a coefficient.** The square shape is first used to construct a functor from coefficients over a product to ordinary diagrams of coefficients. He then asks whether a coefficient over that product can give rise canonically to the stated square. For the cartesian square a three-vertex open subcategory $W$ and the direct image along its inclusion supply the proposed construction. For a bicartesian square, the two partial-diagram constructions still need comparing.

**014. Fundamental coefficients and conservativity.** The base category is identified with the value at $e$. A coefficient over $I$ yields an ordinary diagram indexed by $I^{\circ}$ in this category. **D14** is the conservativity requirement labelled Der₃. **C14** is the corollary asserting conservativity of the comparison for $I\times J$. The author supplies the point-evaluation formula **E14**, $F_{i,j}\simeq\Theta(F)(j)_i$. A slanted NB distinguishes conservativity from faithfulness.

**015–016. A construction from models.** The values $F_i$ are called “fibres” of a coefficient with additional “globalisation” data. Starting with a category and distinguished arrows, the text forms diagram categories, distinguishes arrows pointwise and passes to their categories of fractions. The example with simplicial data is named HOT. Its fundamental coefficient category is named Hot.

**017. Morphisms of derivators.** A family of functors indexed by $I$ is required to commute with inverse images. A second square spells out the condition on functors and their transformations. The author distinguishes commutation on objects from the stronger requirement on arrows, then asks for compatibility with the direct images up to canonical isomorphism.
