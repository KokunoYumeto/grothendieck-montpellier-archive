## Legibility and limits

The typed English prose is generally clear; the many symbols filled into its blanks must be assessed separately. Their double strokes, primes, indices and hand-drawn brackets do not always follow the typography of adjacent letters. On the handwritten sheets, compressed words, retracing and cancellations are frequent. The least secure places are scan 196's lower sentence about descent and uniqueness, the small representation labels and corrections on 197–198, the slanted note and crowded formulas on 203, the opening revision and dimension/index labels in the bottom diagram on 205, and the last recurrence sentence and cancelled passage on 209. The pencil questions on 204 are particularly faint.

All three physically illegible spans are cancelled prose: approximately two words on 197, three on 205 and one on 207. No formula or diagram symbol has been replaced by an illegibility count. A marked reading is an explicit limit, including where mathematical context suggests a familiar alternative. The least secure passages should be checked directly against the supplied scans before being quoted as exact wording.
