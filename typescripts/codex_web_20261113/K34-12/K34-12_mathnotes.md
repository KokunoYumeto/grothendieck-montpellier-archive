# What the pages do

**Arithmetic fundamental groups (196).** The page compares fundamental groups with groups coming from the base field or arithmetic base and asserts that the associated algebraic groups are infinite-dimensional. It then returns to §6.13 and the arithmetic origin of the twisted constant adic sheaves encountered in practice. The lower sentence about descent and uniqueness is heavily compressed and remains marked as doubtful.

**A category of representations (196–197).** The continuation describes a direct-limit category of twisted constant sheaves on the X_lambda and relates it to linear adic representations of Pi. The two small alg/an labels on 197 are not securely distinguished; the transcription retains their alternatives.

**Proper smooth cohomology and Tate modules (197).** Example 1 takes the higher direct images for a proper smooth morphism and mentions maps supplied by morphisms and algebraic correspondences. Example 2 takes the Tate module of an abelian scheme and relates its dual to the first higher direct image. The final instruction to tensor is legible only with an uncertain word ending.

**Endomorphisms and Tate's conjectures (198).** Functoriality gives an injective endomorphism-ring map into endomorphisms of the representation denoted E. The following remark attributes its bijectivity, under the stated hypothesis on X, to Tate's conjectures and mentions a case proved by Tate. The sheet's formulation about algebraically closed fields is retained without reconstructing its intended hypotheses.

**Smaller image groups and Lie algebras (198–199).** The notes describe the large fundamental-group objects as difficult to control and propose working with fixed quotient groups relevant to a particular geometric problem. The endomorphism ring is then expressed using the analytic image group, its algebraic envelope, and, under an additional condition, their Lie algebras.

**Title (200).** The cover announces an appendix on applications of the standard conjectures on algebraic cycles. The Roman numeral is doubtful, and the visible spelling “standart” remains unchanged.

**Normal-crossing divisors (201).** Section 1 forms a spectral sequence from the cohomology of intersections of components, assigns weight q to E_2^(p,q), and states degeneration and a resulting filtration. A marginal note makes independence of dimensions from ell conditional on the standard conjectures.

**Nonproper smooth schemes and filtrations (201, 203).** Section 2 writes X=X'−Y and uses an exact sequence with compactly supported cohomology. It identifies a lower-weight piece and claims independence from the chosen resolution. The continuation describes a quotient filtration, then introduces a dual presentation using cohomology with support, intersection sheaves and twists. It ends with vanishing of d_r for r≥3.

**Duality of complexes (205, opening).** A revised paragraph tentatively compares the complexes from the preceding constructions by Poincaré duality, up to a twist. Cancellations and uncertain words make the exact comparison less secure than the general subject of the paragraph.

**The Leray presentation and remaining differential (205).** A third presentation uses the immersion X into X' and writes higher direct images in terms of intersections of the boundary components. The weight calculation leaves E_3 to compute. The bottom diagram displays the d_2-shaped map between the terms and their duals; several dimensions and indices remain doubtful.

**Special-fibre calculation (207).** A new diagram introduces X over S and U, with a simplification Y=X_0 and U=X_eta. The notes give a spectral sequence on X_0, compare weights in two cases for a differential, and conclude that higher differentials vanish in the marked range. A final list gives proposed filtration terms and weights, with a further marginal qualification.

**Independence and inertia (209).** Subject to identifying d_2 by canonical “motivic” morphisms, the page claims independence of dimensions and Frobenius characteristic polynomials. An exact sequence relates cohomology over eta to inertia invariants and coinvariants over barred eta. The page lists consequences and sketches an induction, after cancelling a proposed duality shortcut. Compatibility is explicitly left to verify.

**Numerical stability and orbit maps (204, 206).** The typescript records properties of the numerical function mu and announces a criterion for semistability and stability. It discusses reductions to projective space and the affine cone. A later typescript sheet uses an invariant homogeneous function and relates proper stability to properness of orbit maps; its final projection argument continues beyond that sheet.

**Valuative decomposition and coordinate inequalities (210, 208, 202).** In typescript page order 2.10, 2.11, 2.12, the proof uses the valuative criterion and Iwahori's theorem to factor a point of G. It then passes to coordinates, obtains inequalities for the exponents and mu, and treats specialization to zero. The supplied scan order is different and is preserved in the transcription.

**Properness detected by one-parameter subgroups (202, Proposition 2.4).** The final typescript proposition characterizes properness of the group action through its restrictions to nontrivial one-parameter subgroups. The proof starts with the valuative criterion and announces another application of Iwahori's theorem. The physical sheet ends at “we can write”; the missing continuation is not supplied.
