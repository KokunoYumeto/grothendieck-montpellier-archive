# Mathematical notes on scans 95–105

## What the pages do

**95–96 · The proposed operations.** Grothendieck wants a notion of higher groupoid in which associativity can itself be witnessed by an arrow. He writes sets of objects in each grade, source and target maps, identity maps, inversion, and compositions indexed by the distance between grades. Two equations describe how source and target behave under composition. A separate pair covers distance one. The Godement relation uses two different composition indices.

**96–98 · Coherence and the letter to the recipient.** An equality between composites can become a higher arrow supplied as part of the data. Applying this idea to the pentagon raises the question of the next coherence. Grothendieck asks about the recipient’s description of higher categories using semisimplicial sets and about a construction in a model category using a cylinder for the final object. The letter ends with the signature “Alexander”.

**99–100 · Which data come first.** On resuming the letter, he singles out source and target as the primitive data. Equivalences of ordinary groupoids illustrate the problem of choosing strict inverses. Path composition illustrates the effect of a choice of identification of two joined intervals with one interval. These examples motivate successive levels of structure, including identity and composition operations.

**100–102 · A category for each stage.** He proposes categories $C_n$ carrying universal partial structures. The limits and colimits needed to express a structure determine which categories and functors can carry it. In this example he drops the requirement of finite products and retains specified iterated fiber products. The stages have the same objects and acquire new arrows. The proposed limit category has morphisms given by the direct limit of the morphisms at finite stages. A functor from it that commutes with the specified products is to describe a realization.

**103–104 · Representing spaces.** The functors $X\mapsto F_i(X)$ are represented by spaces $D_i$. Source and target give a pair of maps from $D_{i-1}$ to $D_i$. Composition of paths is related to a map of an interval into two joined intervals. Grothendieck asks whether the discs and their successive gluings give a universal example in the opposite category. A later marginal note revisits his belief in that expectation.

**104–105 · Faces, composition and gluing.** The two maps into the next disc have hemispherical images. Iterating them gives pairs of faces and a cellular decomposition. A composition operation is associated with a map from a disc into a sum of two discs glued along a face, with a prescribed boundary restriction. The triangle and the two arcs forming a circle illustrate why some gluings would be excluded by a contractibility requirement.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Model, high confidence.** In the first source equation on 95, both composites are well typed from $F_i$ through $F_{i-1}$ to $F_{i-2}$. This is a check of the domains and codomains; the equality is one of the author’s stated relations. The same check applies to the target equation.
- **M2 · Model, high confidence.** The labels on 105 yield the stated count: one cell of dimension $n$, with a positive and a negative cell for each $j$ from zero to $n-1$. For example, a two-dimensional disc has two vertices, two one-dimensional cells, and one two-dimensional cell. This count uses only the displayed range and labels.
- **M3 · Model, medium confidence.** The marginal note of 104 attaches by a star to “I believe it is”. Its position makes it evidence about the author's changing view of universality. The earlier description of the formal sequence of categories also has its own explicit justification on this sheet.
