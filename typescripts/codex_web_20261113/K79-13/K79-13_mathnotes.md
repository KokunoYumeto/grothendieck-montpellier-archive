# Mathematical notes on cote 79, scans 190–204

References in parentheses are the manuscript’s equation numbers. Section 3 restarts the numbering. These notes use ordinary mathematical names for objects whose handwritten letterforms have alternatives in the transcription.

## What the pages do

**U1 · A local condition (190).** The fragment begins in mid-argument. For a star \(E=\mathrm{Et}_{A_0}(s)\), condition b) concerns stability under \(\widetilde U_s\) and the preservation of \(A_{E,\lambda}\) by its action. The marginal note sets the argument aside.

**U2 · A triangular graph and its contours (191).** In \((S,A_0)\) every vertex lies on an edge, and each edge \(\{s,t\}\) has exactly two common neighbors. On the star of \(s\) the induced edge set is \(P_2(E)\cap A_0\). Its contour components have order at least three. A bracketed passage introduces an automorphism group and its action on oriented edges and ordered triangular frames.

**U3 · Keeping the stars while changing the edges (191–192).** The image of the star map is a family of subsets of \(S\). The union of the local contour edge sets recovers \(A_0\). Vertices, edges, oriented edges, frames and triples spanning triangles enter the discussion as homogeneous spaces. Grothendieck then seeks the family \(\Pi\) of triangular graph structures with the specified symmetry and the same family of stars.

**U4 · Evaluation at one star (193).** A candidate \(A_i\) determines its own map from vertices to stars. Transitivity on the family of stars makes the local structure on a chosen \(E_0\) determine the entire invariant family. Equation (15) embeds the parameter set into the contour structures on \(E_0\). The next question is which invariant structures belong to the image.

**U5 · The polygonal stabilizer (194–195).** Triangular-frame transitivity is restated as transitivity on vertices together with a condition on frames in a star. Once the contour is connected, its automorphism group is described as dihedral. The image of the point stabilizer and that of the setwise stabilizer agree in this action. A polygon structure selects an unordered pair of inverse cyclic generators. Scan 195 starts, then crosses out, a lemma about operations on the corresponding torsor.

**U6 · Oriented contours on a cyclic torsor (196).** The proposition identifies an oriented contour with its generating rotation. Invariance requires this permutation to commute with the given cyclic action. The short proof identifies the commutant with the acting cyclic group. Corollary 1 describes the resulting rank-one module over \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and its generators. The margin discusses the effect of allowing or excluding contours made of one- or two-cycles.

**U7 · Forgetting orientation (197–198).** Corollary 2 pairs the opposing orientations. The displayed parameter set removes zero and indicates a further exceptional element when \(n\) is even, then takes the quotient by sign. Polygonal structures correspond to generators modulo sign. The operators \(T_\nu\) are expressed using powers of a chosen generator; the underlying contour uses the pair \(\{\omega_0^\nu,\omega_0^{-\nu}\}\). The long NB on scan 197 places the dihedral action inside the affine normalizer.

**U8 · Reconstructing a candidate graph (198).** To an invariant family \(C_E=(E,A_E)\) the manuscript assigns \(A=\bigcup_E A_E\). It records stability under \(G\) and transitivity on oriented edges. Whether the original parameter is realized is the question that introduces this construction.

**U9 · A second set for the stars (199–200).** Section 3 presents a strict graph by its symmetric, diagonal-free relation \(R_0\). The distinct vertex stars form a new set \(S^*\). Incidence gives a relation \(R_*\subset S\times S^*\). The vertex-to-star map is surjective; the injectivity condition says distinct vertices have different stars. Equations (15)–(16) recover the adjacency relation and the edge set from incidence and the chosen map.

**U10 · Separation and duality (201).** For the four data \((S,S^*,R_*,\varphi_0)\), Grothendieck imposes separation by incidence on both sides, bijectivity of \(\varphi_0\), symmetry, and exclusion of self-incidence. A groupoid diagram relates these data to strict graphs whose stars determine their vertices. Interchanging \(S\) and \(S^*\), transposing incidence and inverting \(\varphi_0\) gives the dual description. The two maps at the foot exhibit the asserted canonical isomorphism.

**U11 · Removing the chosen identification (202).** The incidence data and the bijection are now two separate pieces of structure. The former alone are called a “pré-graphe”. A gauge identifies its two sets so that the resulting relation is symmetric and antireflexive. If gauges exist, one may be chosen as an origin.

**U12 · Comparing gauges (203–204).** Relative to \(\varphi_0\), another bijection is written \(\varphi_0\lambda\), or \(\lambda^*\varphi_0\). The two permutations are conjugate by \(\varphi_0\). The manuscript expresses symmetry and antireflexivity in terms of adjacency and rewrites the symmetry as a condition on how \(\lambda\) acts on stars. Equation (31) returns to the two sides of the incidence relation.

**U13 · A rank-two example (204).** Two free rank-two modules in duality give two sets of vectors extendible to a basis. The display forms sign classes and prescribes incidence by the existence of representatives pairing to 1. A cancelled continuation discusses gauges induced by linear isomorphisms, alternating nondegenerate forms and the dual determinant line.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark, high confidence (196).** For the cyclic torsor on residues modulo 3, 4 or 5, enumerating all permutations gave exactly 3, 4 and 5 permutations commuting with the unit shift, respectively. Each was a translation. This checks the commutant in the proof for those three sizes.

**R2 · Model remark, high confidence (199–200).** The open stars in the four-cycle illustrate the separation condition: opposite vertices have the same star, giving only two distinct stars. The five-cycle has five distinct stars. These counts follow directly from listing the two neighbors of every vertex.

**R3 · Model remark, high confidence for the finite computation (203).** For the 64 labelled simple graphs on four vertices and all 24 permutations of each, symmetry of the relation \(s\sim\lambda(t)\) agreed in every case with the transformed-edge equivalence of (29′), using the exponents written clearly in its implication line. Of the 1,536 graph–permutation pairs, 248 gave symmetry and 146 satisfied antireflexivity as well. The counts concern these finite examples. The check does not resolve the cancelled exponent on the preceding manuscript line.
