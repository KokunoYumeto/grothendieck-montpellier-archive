## What the pages do

**603–604 · An open subcategory and its complement.** The inverse image of nonempty sets gives $\underline S=\underline S_a$. Its object condition is $a(x)\ne\emptyset$, also written $x\leq a$ for the canonical preorder. The complementary $\underline S'$ is presented as the inverse image of the subcategory of sets reduced to the empty set. The author describes stability under several kinds of limits before asking what can be said about accessibility.

**604–605 · Proposition 4.** The setting is a universe with an infinite cardinal $\pi_0$ for which the specified ultrafilters are trivial, and a field $k$ with cardinal at least $\pi_0$. The category $M$ consists of commutative $k$-algebras. Part (a) asserts that no $\pi$ gives the unprimed class the stated $L_\pi$-stability. Part (b) asserts inaccessibility of the primed class despite its $L_0$-stability. A question in the margin and the discussion on 610 qualify the status of (b).

**605–606 · Corollary 1 and the filter calculation.** For an infinite $E$, the author takes the filter $\Phi_0$ of complements of finite subsets and states that $\mathrm{Hom}_M(k_{\Phi_0},k)$ is empty, also considering finer filters. He writes $k_\Phi$ as a direct limit of $k^J$. The calculation relates maps to $k$ first to maximal ideals with the specified residue field, then to ultrafilters finer than $\Phi$. Formula (*) describes the remaining possibilities in terms of points of $E$.

**606–607 · Application to (a).** Each nonempty $J$ supplies an object called $k_J$ in $\underline S$, while the inductive limit constructed here lies outside $\underline S$. For a given $\pi$ the author takes $\operatorname{card} E=\pi'>\pi$ and the filter of complements of subsets of cardinal at most $\pi$. He ends with “Cela prouve … le a) de prop. 4”.

**607–609 · A lemma on a closed subcategory.** With a suitable cardinal and presentations $x\simeq\varinjlim x_i$, the lemma characterizes $\pi$-accessibility of $\underline S'$ by the existence of a stage $x_i$ in $\underline S'$ for each $x$ in it. The argument invokes a prior theorem, relates existence to cofinality of the stages in the closed subcategory, and identifies the essential image of the relevant $\mathrm{Ind}_\pi$ construction.

**609–610 · Two corollaries.** The first passes accessibility to the complementary open class under the explicit condition that it be stable under the indicated limits. The proof compares the open class with an essential image. The author then notes that the required stability fails in his example. The next corollary restates the question for $\underline S'$ using subalgebras: an algebra with no map to $k$ must contain one of cardinal at most $\pi$ with the same property.

**611–612 · New chapter.** The cover reads “XIX” and “Modèles (4)”. The contents move from couples de Quillen to triples de Quillen, then to catégories à cofibrations. Three theorem references accompany the second entry.

**613 · Orthogonality and closure.** For classes of arrows $\Phi,\Psi$, (1.1) relates their lifting notation to $\Phi\subset\Psi^*$ and $\Psi\subset\Phi_*$. The writer allows the reversed ordering of the letters with the ends of the relation correspondingly reversed. In (1.3.) he distinguishes reinforcement on the left, on the right and on both sides, and gives the terminology *Q-clos*. The first right-hand condition is heavily overwritten.

**614 · Factorization.** One vertical stroke across the relation adds the factorization property. The author calls a Q-orthogonal, Q-factorizing pair a *précouple de Quillen*. Requiring bilateral closure gives a *couple de Quillen*. The end-strokes in the four variants of (1.5) are recorded individually in the transcription.

**614–615 · Forms of saturation.** The left-hand definition mentions isomorphisms, change by cobase, transfinite compositions and direct factors. There is a dual on the right. The author describes stability under the indicated sums, including amalgamated sums in the indicated category, then distinguishes simple saturation, transfinite saturation and total saturation. The implications run downward in (1.6).

**615–616 · Constructing the intermediate classes.** The chain (1.7) runs from $\Phi$ through the simple, transfinite and total left saturations to its Q-closure. A class $\Phi'_0$ is inserted between $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$. The square in item 1 gives the elementary change of cobase. Items 2 and 3 describe finite multiple compositions and transfinite compositions. The bottom of 616 asks whether taking direct factors of $\Phi''$ already gives $\Phi'''$.

**617 · Theorem 1.1.** Assume $M$ has the indicated small inductive limits and its elements are accessible. The size hypothesis on $\Phi$ can be replaced by a small part $\Phi_0\subset\Phi$ whose Q-closure contains $\Phi$. For $\Psi=\Phi_*=(\Phi_0)_*$ the theorem gives $f=pi$ with $i\in\Phi''_0$ and $p\in\Psi$, and then the corresponding assertion for $\Phi''$. The margin adds functoriality in $f$.

**617 · Corollary 1.2.** Under the theorem’s hypotheses, (1.2.1) identifies total left saturation, the indicated class of direct factors of $\Phi''$, and the Q-closure of $\Phi$. A further statement begins with a transfinitely left-saturated $\Phi_1$ between $\Phi_0$ and the common closure. Its conclusion lies beyond the end of scan 617.
