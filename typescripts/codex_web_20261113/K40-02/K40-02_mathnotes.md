# Mathematical notes on cote 40, scans 16–28

The identifiers locate statements in `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv`. The register records the mathematical checks and dependencies in this run. A summary of what a page says is not an independent check.

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 16.** The page leaves a verification open. A map from \(B\) to \(C\otimes C\) is defined by composition and must satisfy relations (i) and (ii). The sign for the first map in the composite remains doubtful. “Pour ceci,” leads into a blank.

**U02 · Scan 17.** The author equips \(A[X]/X^pA[X]\) with a coproduct in which \(X\) is primitive, an augmentation taking \(X\) to zero and the operation taking \(X\) to \(-X\). He describes the hyperalgebra as isomorphic to its dual.

**U03 · Scans 17–18.** An action on a commutative \(A\)-algebra \(C\) is expressed by an \(A\)-linear operator \(D\). The relations are \(D^p=0\) and the Leibniz rule. For the principal homogeneous case the powers \(D^i\) are to form a basis of the endomorphisms. The first letter before “-module” on 18 is doubtful. The scalar action written alongside it and “sur \(C\)” on 19 specify the role of \(C\).

**U04 · Scans 18–19.** Given a one-element \(p\)-basis \(u\), the choice \(Du=1\) gives \(D(u^n)=nu^{n-1}\). Evaluation on powers of \(u\) gives the triangular matrix on 18. The product of factorials on 19 supplies the invertible determinant in the proposition.

**U05 · Scans 19–20.** The two numbered questions ask which sub-hyperalgebras can produce the twisted endomorphism hyperalgebra and which principal homogeneous spaces occur up to isomorphism. The end of the first question singles out a restricted Lie algebra of rank one and the case of zero restricted power.

**U06 · Scans 20–21.** The argument about a free direct summand \(M\) identifies it by the operators that vanish on it. Applying the double-annihilator calculation to \(M=A\) gives the description of the kernel of \(D\). The slanted margin suggests extending scalars from \(A\) to \(C\).

**U07 · Scan 21.** The author seeks \(u\) with \(Du=1\), unique up to addition of an element of \(A\). A long, cancelled local argument and a sideways argument with an endomorphism \(\Theta\) occupy much of the page. Below them he records that \(x^p\in A\) for \(x\in C\), and that the powers of the chosen \(u\) form a basis.

**U08 · Scan 22.** After crossing out an attempted proof of independence, the author sets \(u^p=a\) and compares \(A[U]/(U^p-a)\) with \(C\) by sending \(U\) to \(u\). He invokes the isomorphism property for an equivariant map of principal spaces. The square of products and the query about finite algebras accompany that invocation.

**U09 · Scan 23.** The theorem identifies the classes with \(A/A^p\). The representative for \(a\) has algebra \(C_a=A[U]/(U^p-a)\) and derivation \(D_aU=1\). The displayed criterion identifies two classes when their parameters agree modulo \(A^p\). The author singles out compatibility with addition as still to be proved.

**U10 · Scans 23–24.** The corollary first describes a principal structure through a generator modulo addition by \(A\). It then describes the change \(v=au+b\), with \(a\in A^*\) and \(b\in A\), for the corresponding classes of one-element bases. The closing formula calls \((C/A)/A^*\) the associated projective space.

**U11 · Scan 25.** For a prescheme over \(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\), the author introduces the Frobenius sequence of additive groups. On the polynomial algebra Frobenius sends \(T\) to \(T^p\). The inverse image of the unit section gives the kernel with coordinate algebra \(\underline O[T]/(T^p)\).

**U12 · Scans 25–26.** The stated sense of surjectivity is a factorization property for an equivariant map to a target with trivial action. The map factors through the projection to \(X\). The author questions the reduction to affine targets, then writes a cohomology sequence in which two copies of \(A\) are joined by Frobenius.

**U13 · Scans 26–27.** The sequence describes the degree-one group of the kernel as an extension by \(A/A^p\) of a subgroup of the structure-sheaf cohomology. For a complete variety over an algebraically closed field, the continuation identifies a Frobenius kernel in a finite-dimensional space.

**U14 · Scan 27.** Over \(k[[X]]\) the author studies covers obtained by adjoining a \(p\)-th root. He gives a normal form with radicand \(X^n\), discusses changing the uniformizer and distinguishes the regular case \(n=1\). In the characteristic-three example, adjoining \(X^{2/3}\) gives the subring of series in \(Y=X^{1/3}\) whose linear coefficient vanishes.

**U15 · Scans 27–28.** The author calls the preceding cover a sum of two regular covers with radicands \(X\) and \(-X+X^2\). Their fibre product already has nilpotents. He identifies its ring over the first cover with \(C_1[T]/(T^p)\).

## Remarks of the model

- **M01 · 18–19. High confidence.** For prime \(p\), every factor in the stated determinant has nonzero residue modulo \(p\). Its image is therefore a unit in any nonzero unital \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra. The calculation for \(p=2,3,5,7\) gives residues \(1,2,3,6\).
- **M02 · 23. High confidence.** Translating a generator by \(b\in A\) changes its \(p\)-th power by \(b^p\). This directly checks the parameter change in U09. It leaves the separate identification of addition of principal-space classes in P05 to be made.
- **M03 · 27, L16–19. High confidence.** With \(Y=X^{1/3}\), the example has generators \(Y^3\) and \(Y^2\). Even exponents occur as multiples of 2 and every odd exponent at least 3 is 3 plus an even exponent. This accounts for the single missing power \(Y\), independently of the doubtful digit after “si on prend”.
- **M04 · 28, L3–8. High confidence.** Write \(u^p=X\) and \(v^p=-X+X^2\). The translated generator \(w=v+u-u^2\) has \(w^p=0\). Translation in a monic polynomial ring over \(C_1\) gives exactly the displayed truncated polynomial algebra. This calculation uses the two clear radicands.
- **M05 · 27, L5–11. Low confidence about the scope of the claim.** If the upper bound \(p-1\) is meant to cover *all* these rings up to isomorphism, characteristic two already gives a test case. The ring \(k[[X]][T]/(T^2-X^3)\) is the reduced, singular ring \(k[[Y^2,Y^3]]\). The model with \(n=1\) is regular. The manuscript's qualifier before “isomorphes” and the retouched lower bound leave the intended scope uncertain.
- **M06 · 24, L5. Reading question, low confidence.** The frozen words are “la structure correspondante”. A taller initial between the article and the rest of the noun could give “la B-structure correspondante”. This observation arose while tracing the distinction between a chosen action and a chosen generator. The final transcription retains the frozen wording at this unmarked place.
