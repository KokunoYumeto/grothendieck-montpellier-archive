# Mathematical notes on cote 137, scans 31–44

## What the pages do

**Scans 31–32. Representing objects by functors.** The opening parenthesis identifies the one-object additive example with finite free right modules through \(X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(A,X)\). The author then passes to the dual statement. Objects of \(C\) give covariant functors on \(\mathcal A\), with values in sets or abelian groups. Generation by projective limits gives a fully faithful functor from \(C^\circ\). Stability under the indicated limits enters the description of its essential image.

**Scans 32–33. Two presentations of the same category.** The proposition fixes two types of indexing categories, \(\mathcal T\) and \(\mathcal T'\). It assumes that the corresponding projective and inductive limits exist in \(C\) and that each object has presentations of both kinds by objects of \(\mathcal A\). The diagram on scan 33 puts the two functor categories on opposite sides of \(C\), with copies of \(\mathcal A\) included in each. The author distinguishes limits formed in the subcategory \(D\) from those computed in the ambient functor category.

**Scans 33–34. A ring example.** When \(\mathcal A\) has one object \(A\) and both types are finite discrete categories, the anti-equivalence becomes the duality between finite free left and right modules over \(k=\operatorname{End}(A)\).

**Scan 34. Restriction to generators.** The author considers functors from \(C\) to \(C'\) that commute with the inductive limits of type \(\mathcal T'\), and restricts them to \(\mathcal A\). He marks the full-faithfulness assertion for checking. In the additive case with finite discrete indexing categories he writes an equivalence between additive functors on \(C\) and half-additive functors on \(\mathcal A\).

**Scans 34–35. The tensor pairing.** Let \(D\) be the essential image of the indicated embedding into a category of functors on \(\mathcal A^\circ\). Applying the inverse of the restriction equivalence and evaluating gives a pairing denoted \(F\otimes_C G\). In the ring example, \(F\) is finite free on the right, \(G\) has a left action of \(k\), and the author calls the result the “produit tensoriel” “ordinaire”.

**Scans 35–36. The Hom pairing.** The second embedding uses the opposite of an essential image \(D'\). The reverse equivalence again gives a pairing with values in \(C'\). This one is written \(\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_C(F,G)\). A line separates this construction from the application below it.

**Scans 36–37. What restriction retains.** Inside a category \(\mathscr S\), the author chooses an additive subcategory \(C\) of abelian objects and a full subcategory \(\mathcal A\) of generators for its direct sums. An object \(X\) defines the functor \(M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr S}(X,M)\). The reduction is to study its restriction to \(\mathcal A\).

**Scan 37. Two choices of test objects.** For \(\mathscr S=\widehat\Delta\) the first case uses half-simplicial abelian groups with the indicated \(\partial\)-perfectness condition. The page lists generators \(\Phi(i,n)\) and a second family indexed by \(j\). The second case singles out objects with torsion-free \(\pi_i\) and calls the subcategory \(C_0\). The margin calls \(C\) abelian and \(C_0\) additive but nonabelian. The superscripts in the last two generator formulas remain uncertain.

**Scan 38. Operations on the Hom functor.** For a Dedekind base ring and then a general \(k\), the author relates knowledge of the functor of maps from \(X_*\) to knowledge of the complex of cochains called “non dég”. Two displayed maps give the operations to recover. One starts from \(F(M_*)\otimes_k F(N_*)\) and lands in \(F(M_*\otimes N_*)\). The other compares applying \(\Gamma^i\) before or after \(F\).

**Scans 38–39. A reconstruction problem.** The question across the page break compares cochains equipped with these operations with the De Rham–Sullivan complex in characteristic zero and a de Rham complex with divided powers for general \(k\). It leads to the question of how much of the homotopy type of \(X_*\) the cochains determine.

**Scan 40. An additive category generated by a complex.** The generators \(C^i\) are the terms of a cochain complex. The prescribed \(\operatorname{Hom}(C^i,C^j)\) vanishes except for \(j=i\) and \(j=i+1\), when its displayed generators are the identity and \(d^i\), respectively. Every object is to be a finite sum of these generators. The full subcategory on the generators inherits a \(k\)-linear structure.

**Scan 40. A chain-complex model.** The author takes bounded acyclic chain complexes whose terms and cycles are finite free over \(k\). A generating object has \(k\) in degrees \(i+1\) and \(i\) with identity differential. The map to the next generator is specified by its component in the common degree. In the sideways continuation he draws the corresponding two-term cochain object and the reversed map and refers to the opposite category.

**Scan 41, cancelled upper part. A half-simplicial model.** The draft relates decomposition into generators built from exterior powers to finite freeness, eventual polynomial rank and vanishing of the indicated homotopy groups. It writes a differential involving \(T_*\wedge\) and calls the resulting complex “universel”. The oblique margin introduces a freeness assumption on finite projective modules. Long diagonals cross this entire development.

**Scan 41, Prop 1. Restriction and evaluation.** The live diagram takes functors with target a \(k\)-additive category \(\mathcal B\), restricts to \(\mathcal A\), then sends \(F\) to \(F(C^\bullet_*)\). It identifies the last category with cochain complexes of \(\mathcal B\). The subscripts on the first Hom and the source capital have been overwritten or are ambiguous.

**Scans 41–42, Exemple 1. A covariant representable functor.** With \(\mathcal B=\mathrm{Ab}_k\), the object \(L_*\) gives \(M_*\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(L_*,M_*)\). The resulting fully faithful functor, labelled \(\alpha^\bullet\), assigns to \(L_*\) the cochains \(\operatorname{Hom}(L_*,C^\bullet_*)\). Scan 42 draws a generator’s two terms, the map between consecutive models and its identity component. The inset gives a finite-freeness and vanishing-cohomology description of the essential image under a stated assumption.

**Scan 42, Ex 2, continuing on scan 43. A contravariant representable functor.** With \(\mathcal B=\mathrm{Ab}_k^\circ\), \(M_*\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(M_*,L_*)\) gives the other construction. The grid tracks restriction and passage to opposite categories. The outcome is labelled \(\beta_\bullet\). Scan 43 computes its effect on the generators and on \(d^i\), adding a separate formula for \(C^0\). Several degree labels have two superposed versions.

**Scan 43. Passage between the examples.** Dualizing \(M\) is said to exchange the chain and cochain descriptions on finite free modules. A triangular diagram places the two functors on its sides. In the long sideways note the author treats \(\alpha_n(x)\) for \(n\geq1\), the transitions for \(n\geq2\), and then the maps involving degree zero. The lower-degree formula contains a doubtful dual sign.

**Scan 43, Prop 2. A perfect pairing.** The author states a functorial perfect duality. The clearest row pairs \(\operatorname{Hom}(C^n,x)\) with \(\operatorname{Hom}(x,C^{n+1})\) by composition into \(\operatorname{Hom}(C^n,C^{n+1})\simeq k\). The nearby expression in the two alpha functors has more overwritten ink.

**Scan 44, Prop. 3. Recovering a functor from a cochain complex.** Returning to Prop 1, the question is how to evaluate the functor corresponding to \(C^\bullet\) at \(x\), given the description of \(x\) by \(\alpha^\bullet(x)\). The proposed bifunctorial isomorphism expresses \(F(x)\) by the Hom object of \(\alpha^\bullet(x)\) and \(F(C^\bullet)\).

**Scan 44. Defining the Hom object.** The definition asks for an object representing \(b\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal B^\bullet}(b\otimes_k K^\bullet,C^\bullet)\). Finite freeness of the \(K^i\) is invoked to define the tensor. A more general construction begins with the kernel of the map on products whose component is \(d u^i-u^{i+1}d\). Conditions on kernels and representability of the individual terms follow. The page ends at “et les deux”.
