# Mathematical notes · cote 157-1, scans 98–113

## What the pages do

**U98 · A complete cohomological fibration (098).** The sheet opens in mid-argument. Proposition 4 assumes a form of Der 3 a′. It relates the earlier definition to two other tests, local constancy and fibrewise calculation of the direct image after base change, and preservation of a cohomological equivalence in the induced diagram. A fourth formulation restricts the test to a point and a category with a final object.

**U99 · Completeness and a proposed strengthening (099–100).** Définition 3 requires both complete cohomological and complete homological fibration. A test with Hot-aspherical bases is followed by a proposal to require a 1-complete equivalence upstairs. Grothendieck then considers also requiring this whenever the map downstairs is a 1-complete equivalence. He asks about composition and about finding examples.

**U100 · The internal-Hom calculation (100).** For locally constant coefficients with suitable products and internal Homs preserved by inverse images, the displayed comparison expresses an ordinary Hom using the unit and the direct image to the point. He uses it to identify 1-complete and cohomological equivalences in this case, and to pass from a complete cohomological fibration to the homological and strengthened conditions. The unit and the small capital below the last Hom carry reading marks.

**U101 · Proper and smooth test cases (101–102).** He seeks examples and asks how much can be checked over the original base. For a proper map he tests the slice maps along arrows of the base category. The smooth case suggests an argument using the shriek image of the unit and its behaviour under base change. He stops over properties of HOT that he has yet to establish, then returns to the cohomological setting.

**U103 · A lemma about HOT (103).** In a pair of base changes, he wants the upper comparison to be a Hot-equivalence. A locally constant sheaf on the new source may fail to come from the old source. The lemma turns to a comparison first with constant and then with locally constant coefficients. The angled note explicitly warns that he is losing the distinction between HOT-équivalence and Hot-équivalence.

**U104 · The attempted justification (104–105).** The formulas with internal Hom and the object written \(\mathrm{hot}(Z)\) address constant coefficients. Applying \(\pi_0\) gives an ordinary Hom comparison. He singles out the passage to a varying locally constant coefficient as delicate. The next sheet records what the lemma would imply for HOT-fibrations, including the proper and dual smooth cases.

**U105 · Variants of Der 3 (105–106).** The dated entry restarts the terminology. The a and a′ conditions concern a Hot-aspherical diagram and the essential image of coefficients from the point. The b conditions concern a Hot-equivalence and the effect of pullback on constant or locally constant coefficients. Separate cohomological and homological variants each have a comparison display. Seven named variants occur in the implication diagram.

**U107 · Four senses of equivalence (107, 109).** Définition 1 recalls complete cohomological and homological equivalence, then 1- and 2-complete equivalence by full faithfulness or equivalence on locally constant objects. One diagram gives general implications. The special case with products and internal Homs continues in the opening diagram on 109. He then sets apart what he expects for HOT.

**U108 · The intervening document (108).** A two-column statement addressed to a court occupies the reused sheet. It discusses vaccination and the case of Monsieur COULARDEAU. Much of the printed text has been cancelled, including the two concluding paragraphs. The signature stands above the professional imprint of Dr Simone Fayeton. The mathematical discussion resumes on scan 109.

**U109 · A fibre and slice criterion (109).** Proposition 1 pairs the equivalences for the fibre-to-slice inclusion and the maps between slices with a statement for a *constant* coefficient on the base. Its pullback followed by direct image is locally constant and calculated fibre by fibre. Proposition 1′ is entered simply as the dual.

**U110a · Weak fibrations and base change (110).** Définition 2 calls the conditions of Proposition 1 and its dual weak cohomological and weak homological fibration. He says which older names this changes. Proposition 2 specifies smooth base change for the cohomological case and proper base change for the homological case.

**U110b · A test on fibres (110).** The inserted Proposition 3 fits a map between two objects over \(S\) into a triangle. Both structure maps are weak fibrations of the indicated kind. Testing the maps on fibres over \(S_0\) suffices when \(\pi_0(S_0)\to\pi_0(S)\) is surjective.

**U110c · The proper or smooth simplification (110).** Proposition 4 characterizes the weak condition under a properness or smoothness hypothesis by local constancy of the displayed direct image for every constant coefficient. It also asserts persistence after arbitrary base change. The justification is a reference to criterion b) of Proposition 1.

**U111 · The tests used for a D-fibration (111).** Proposition 5 compares changes between Hot-aspherical bases, changes along a Hot-equivalence, two restricted tests, and persistence of the two weak fibration conditions. One further proposed test is struck out. The implication chart is followed by two statements under different Der 3 hypotheses. For the sequel he assumes a′ of Der 3. Définition 3 uses the five conditions written into its line. The definition itself refers to “prop. 4”.

**U112a · Properness, smoothness and fibre transport (112).** Proposition 6 places the D-fibration condition beside the appropriate weak condition, the condition on arrows between slices or coslices, and the two direct-image tests for constant and locally constant coefficients. The added corollary tests a Cat-fibrant or Cat-cofibrant map on its fibre transports, with the opposite directions written out.

**U112b · Two limitations (112).** He regards stability under base change as immediate, doubts composition, and considers an example using \(\mathrm{Der}(k\text{-}\mathrm{Mod})\). A long marginal NB describes cases in which composition would hold. He separately asks whether both displayed composites with a general coefficient are calculated fibre by fibre.

**U113 · Universal equivalence and trivial fibrations (113).** Proposition 7 equates universal D-equivalence with a D-fibration whose fibres are D-aspherical. Définition 4 calls the resulting fibration trivial. A warning distinguishes this from being both a D-fibration and a D-equivalence. The final Cartesian square asks what follows upstairs when the bottom horizontal arrow is a D-equivalence. He singles out HOT in the closing parenthesis.
