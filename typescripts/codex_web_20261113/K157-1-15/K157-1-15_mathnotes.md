# Mathematical notes · cote 157-1, scans 227–241

Scan numbers below refer to the supplied sheets. L numbers refer to the frozen page files. The author's terms are retained where his terminology changes.

## What the pages do

**227, relations of the first kind.** The opening square asks for the existence of a dotted arrow for each commutative square with sides \(i\) and \(p\). The discussion then passes to ordered sets. A decreasing map from the parts of \(P\) to the parts of \(Q\) that takes suprema to infima is determined by its values on singletons. Those values amount to a relation on \(P\times Q\). The author asks what changes when one works with selected families of subsets.

**228, transporters relative to \(W\).** Section 6 fixes a category \(M\) with fibre products and a class of arrows \(W\). It introduces two ordered families \(P,Q\). In (6.3), \(A\) and \(L\) are related when pulling back any arrow of \(A\) along an arrow in \(L\) gives an arrow in \(W\). A marginal note points forward to the revision of the families.

**229–230, largest elements on the two sides.** For fixed \(A\), the allowed \(L\)'s form a lower set. Its largest member is the class \(\mathbb L_W(A)\) of base changes carrying arrows of \(A\) into \(W\). Conversely \(\mathbb A_W(L)\) consists of the arrows in \(W\) that remain in \(W\) under the indicated base changes. The stacked squares explain how a further base change acts.

**231, revision of (6.2).** The author now assumes that \(W\) contains the isomorphisms and is stable under composition. In (6.7), \(A\) contains the isomorphisms, lies in \(W\), and is closed under composition. \(L\) contains the isomorphisms and is closed under base change. In the written revision of \(Q\), “composition et” is crossed out. The two equalities (6.8) select the mutually closed pairs.

**232–233, the two extremes and Proposition 6.1.** Starting from the isomorphisms on the \(A\) side gives the pair of universally \(W\)-arrows and all arrows. Starting from the isomorphisms on the \(L\) side gives \(W\) and its fibrations. For a closed pair, Proposition 6.1 relates closure of \(L\) under composition to stability of \(A\) under base change along \(L\). A further assertion about arbitrary composition-stable \(L\in Q\) runs onto 233.

**233–234, proposed names and examples.** The transporter of \(A\) in \(W\) is \(\mathbb L_W(A)\). The other class is called the \(L\)-kernel of \(W\), with several trial names for its arrows. Taking \(A\) as its own ambient class gives its stabiliser. In Cat the text identifies smooth and proper maps with stabilisers of the aspherical and coaspherical classes, and asks for descriptions of further stabilisers.

**235–236, four relative filtering conditions.** Definition 7.1 (D235-coslice) tests \(f:X\to Y\) on the categories \(y\backslash X\). Four variants and their duals are listed. Proposition 7.3 passes the appropriate property from \(Y\) and these categories to \(X\). The following corollary, also numbered 7.3, gives stability under composition.

**236–237, base change and cofinality.** Sending \(e\) to \(1\) in \(\Delta^1\) furnishes a counterexample to unrestricted base change for the two global variants. The local variants are left open. The absolute condition on \(X\) is read off from \(X\to e\). The next paragraph relates cofiltered functors, cofinality and the axiom \(\mathrm{Loc}\,8\).

**237–238, Proposition 7.4 and limits.** Part (a) treats étale precomposition and étale factorisation. Part (b) concerns the induced functor on slices at \(x\) and \(f(x)\). Part (c) restricts to an open \(U\), with a qualification about the target \(V\) discussed below. The two comparisons on 238 go in opposite directions: the direct-limit comparison for a cofaisceau points right and the inverse-limit comparison for a préfaisceau points left.

**238–239, Lemma 7.5 and the proof of (a).** For an étale \(X'\to X\), each component of \(x\backslash X'\) has one object in the discrete fibre. That object is initial in its component. The proof uses the resulting description by components in two different diagrams. The first uses \(y\) throughout. The second introduces \(y'\) over \(y\).

**239–240, proof of (b) and (c).** The argument uses the final object of \(X_{/x}\) over the final object of \(Y_{/y}\), then passes from local cofilteredness to cofilteredness using connectedness. For (c), \(z\backslash U\) is the inverse image of \(U\) in \(z\backslash(X_{/x})\). The text calls that open nonempty by construction and applies the property of nonempty opens.

**240–241, two closing observations.** The first repeats the restriction-to-an-open argument for a cofiltered \(f\). The second assumes each \(y\backslash X\) is a disjoint sum of categories with initial objects. It draws a coasphericity conclusion for a fundamental localiser without invoking the limits axiom. The two examples at the foot are étale \(f\) and discrete \(X\).

## Remarks of the model

- **C236-example · Model, high confidence.** In the two-object ordered category \(0\to1\), the functor selecting \(1\) has one object and one arrow in each of its two coslices. Its strict fibre over \(0\) is empty. A finite enumeration checks the example on 236. The question about the two local variants remains open on these sheets.
- **C237-literal and C240-equality · Model, high confidence in the finite calculation; low in attributing a slip.** Take \(f\) to be the identity of the one-object category and take \(U\) empty. The open generated by the image of \(U\) is empty. The test using \(y\backslash X\) includes the sole object. With this larger target, the induced map from \(U\) has an empty coslice. This distinguishes the two readings of the target in both passages.
- **C240-relative · Model, high confidence for the stated nonemptiness test.** Testing \(y\backslash U\) asks whether there is an arrow \(y\to f(u)\) for some \(u\in U\). This is the precise membership condition needed in the sentence « non vide par construction de V ». The full restriction theorem has not been independently established here.
- **C239-terminal · Model, high confidence in the conditional assertion.** A functor carrying a terminal object to a terminal object supplies a terminal object in each of these coslices: its structure arrow is the unique arrow to the target terminal object. This identifies the particular terminal object used at the foot of 239. The page separately invokes local cofilteredness.
- **C241-discrete · Model, high confidence.** For discrete \(X\), a morphism between two objects of \(y\backslash X\) can only be an identity of \(X\). Each nonempty connected component is therefore a single object with its identity, as needed in the final example.
