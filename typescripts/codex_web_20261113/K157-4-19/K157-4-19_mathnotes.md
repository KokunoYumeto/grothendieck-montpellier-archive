# Mathematical notes on scans 288–302

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 288, 291–292.** The author considers defining $\pi_0(X)$ by maps from a final object and writes down the resulting adjunction with $I\mapsto I_{\mathcal H}$. When the canonical map $I\to\pi_0(I_{\mathcal H})$ is bijective, he asks what would make the map out of $\mathrm{Hom}(X,I_{\mathcal H})$ bijective as well. Scan 291 resumes this sentence, states full faithfulness in the case of $\mathrm{Hot}_W$, and constructs maps $\alpha$ and $\beta$ between $\mathrm{Hom}_W(e_W,X)$ and $\pi_0(X)$. Scan 292 reduces one identity to surjectivity and begins the argument about maps to the empty object under Loc 5.

**U2 · Scan 289.** A preceding proof ends with a homotopy and an equality in $\mathrm{Hot}_W$. The “Commentaire final” singles out the connectedness axiom. The author compares it with the other axioms by taking $W$ to consist of all arrows of $M$. He then lists how the direct-sum clauses of Loc 4 depend on the square, fibration and filtered-limit axioms.

**U3 · Scans 290, 293.** A cube is a map between two squares, each coming from a cover by two opens and their intersection. Loc 6a, b and c impose three different implications on the four component maps. Scan 293 reserves the name Loc 6 for a and b together. Its primed variant uses a single Mayer–Vietoris square and compares membership of $i_1$ and $j_1$ in $W$, in either direction.

**U4 · Scan 294.** Proposition 3.1 passes from cube conditions to the primed conditions by specializing the four vertical maps in cubes $Q$ and $Q'$. The stated conclusions are a ⇒ a′, b ⇒ b′ and c ⇒ both primed conditions, where the letters here stand for the corresponding clauses of Loc 6. An abandoned third drawing and the marginal continuation complicate the attribution of one intermediate implication.

**U5 · Scans 294–295.** The “minimum vital” under discussion is a usable criterion for a map of categories to be a $W$-cofibration. Open Dwyer immersions provide the proposed class. The author identifies an assumption about these immersions in his earlier passage from the square axiom to the cube axiom.

**U6 · Scans 296–297.** Lemma 3.2.1 gives conditions on a class $C$ in a localiseur $(M,W)$ ensuring $C\subset\mathrm{Cof}_W$. The manuscript moves factorization into third place and makes base-change stability of $C\cap W$ condition 4. Corollary 3.2.2 compares that stability, the universal class, membership in $\mathrm{Cof}_W$, and preservation of a weak equivalence across a pushout along $C$. Brown’s lemma supplies the arrow from a to b′ in the written argument.

**U7 · Scans 297–299.** The intended application takes $M=\mathrm{Cat}$ and $C$ to be the open Dwyer immersions. Proposition 3.3 identifies the paired primed conditions with the paired cube conditions, and discusses the additional direct-factor hypothesis for c. The author then singles out the direct primed clause alone. The missing base-change assertion for $W$-trivial Dwyer immersions becomes the central question.

**U8 · Scans 299–300.** Two tests are proposed under weaker hypotheses. The first compares a $W$-cocartesian square with the canonical map from the integral of its span to its fourth vertex. The second concerns monomorphisms of presheaves and those which also belong to $W_A$. Scan 300 reports the author’s checks against XII, distinguishing the corollary he needs from others that use both clauses of the stronger axiom.

**U9 · Scan 301.** The live version of 3.5 records $\mathrm{Loc}\,6c\Rightarrow\mathrm{Loc}\,6\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{Loc}\,6'$ and gives a chain of equivalences under the displayed condition on $W$. The raised sign in that condition is read as a natural, with sharp also possible. A separate line gives $6a\Rightarrow 6'a$ and puts a question mark over the converse.

**U10 · Scans 301–302.** Proposition 3.6 proposes to characterize Loc 6a by A, factorization into a $W$-cofibration followed by a member of $W$, and B, the $W$-cocartesian open-cover square. A′ names the Dwyer-map condition. Corollary 3.7 adds C, the implication $j\in W\Rightarrow i\in W$ in a $W$-cocartesian square, for the combined axiom.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · Model, high confidence for the substitution; low for the proposed textual slip. Scans 290, 294.** I checked all four truth assignments for $i_1\in W$ and $j_1\in W$, assigning true to identity maps. On the written tuple for $Q$, clauses a, b and c respectively give the forward implication, a tautology and the reverse implication. For $Q'$ they give a tautology, the reverse implication and the forward implication. This accounts for Proposition 3.1 and isolates the intermediate attribution flagged in S3. The check is conditional on the displayed tuples.
- **M2 · Model, high confidence. Scans 293, 298, 301.** The paired equivalence in 3.3 is logically compatible with leaving the direct converse open. A truth-table check allows both unprimed clauses to be false, only the primed direct clause to be true, and the conjunctions still to agree. This resolves a possible confusion in reading the summary. The existence of a localiseur exhibiting those values remains a separate mathematical question.
- **M3 · Model, high confidence. Scans 288–294.** The sentence ending “cette flèche est […] bijective,” on 288 continues with “ou encore que la flèche” on 291. The source and target in the neighbouring formulas confirm the connection. In between, the cube heading on 290 connects with the numbered conditions and primed square on 293. The repeats of folios 16, 17 and 18 thus agree with two strands of exposition in the preserved scan order.
