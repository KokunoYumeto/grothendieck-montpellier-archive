# Mathematical notes · Cote 123, scans 37–39 and 41–48

## What the pages do

**Catalogue of spaces (37–38).** The cover introduces homogeneous spaces related to principal simple rank-one subgroups. The first three braces of the next sheet vary the additional choice: a pinning, a Killing pair, a maximal torus, a Borel, or a pointed form of a torus or additive subgroup. Some rows present the space inside a Hom scheme. The lower catalogue of cyclic lines, elements and pairs carries a block cancellation; the local overwrites still matter to the history of these names.

**Two diagrams (39).** One graph relates spaces of choices. It starts at Isom(G₀,G), passes through the named spaces of scan 38 and reaches less decorated objects. The second graph has group or stabilizer labels, including T, N₀, N₁, Z(bᵤ) and N(bᵤ). Numbers or quotients stand beside its edges. Most connecting strokes do not specify a direction.

**Principal classes and stabilizers (41 a–b).** The draft aligns conjugacy classes of rank-one subgroups, unipotent elements and certain cocharacters. It describes cocharacters using coordinates 0, 1 and 2. The all-ones case gives the principal objects. A list distinguishes centralizers of elements, normalizers of subgroups and stabilizers of successively decorated subgroups.

**Principal elements and Coxeter representatives (41 c).** A principal homomorphism u and a primitive h-th root ζ give u(ζ). The author compares their conjugacy class with representatives of a Coxeter element. He writes a normalizer description for a centralizer, then queries whether it is T.

**Cyclic lines and elements (41 d–42).** The draft moves from regular eigenlines for a Coxeter operator on Lie(T) to lines in the Lie algebra of G. A sum of nonzero multiples of simple-root vectors and a vector of weight −ψ supplies cyclic elements. The discussion passes from the centralizer of an element to the normalizer of its line and to a quotient D/μ. A separate note asks what choices are needed for the negative highest-root vector.

**Coxeter pairs (42 e).** The pairs consist of a maximal torus and an element of its normalizer mapping to a Coxeter element of W. After asserting conjugacy, the author describes a discrete stabilizer, a fixed-point extension and a quotient of the lattice P with a Tate twist.

**Killing pairs and principal rank-one tori (43–45, (i) and (i bis)).** The second cover introduces a résumé. Over a base S it assigns to a Killing pair a homomorphism from Gₘ by descent from the split case. Reversing the parameter gives the opposite pair. The normalizer and the associated order-two action lead to principal rank-one tori and unordered opposite Borels.

**Kostant homomorphisms and their grading (45–46, (ii)).** Restriction to μₕ gives a Kostant homomorphism. Its action on Lie(G) gives a cyclic grading. The draft identifies degree zero with a Cartan subalgebra and records two root-height conditions in degree j. It then relates the Weyl stabilizer to the centre of a covering group and describes a set Σ′ of degree-one roots.

**Normalizer question and sections in degree one (46–47, (iii)).** The discussion of the exceptional simple root continues across the page break. The normalizer question asks how much of Aut(μₕ) is obtained. In characteristic zero the author attributes to Kostant criteria for a degree-one section to be regular semisimple or nilpotent. He then discusses conjugacy of lines under T, the stabilizer in T and a distinguished μₕ-torsor of isomorphisms.

**Normalizer of a cyclic line (48, (iv)).** A local eigencharacter condition defines a cyclic line. The sheet links the connected centralizer to a maximal torus T′ and its normalizer to the inverse image of a subgroup in W′. The qualification referring back to a) is part of the statement.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1 · High confidence.** Scans 41–42 and 44–48 cover several of the same constructions. The all-ones cocharacter, its restriction at h, and the normalizer of a cyclic line recur. This follows from the formulas on both sets of sheets.
- **M2 · Medium confidence.** The change from the element x to its line D accounts for two different groups in the middle of 42: the centralizer of x and the normalizer of D. The sentence explicitly switches from « élément » to « droite »; the precise notation on the latter group is less certain.
- **M3 · Low confidence.** The endpoint h in the range on 46 could label the zero residue. Scan 45 treats degree zero apart from 1≤j≤h−1. This observation does not establish that the displayed sum works at the endpoint.
- **M4 · Low confidence.** In 47 a, the parenthetic exponent that looks like 2 may be a very short α. The next root-space exponent favours α. This remains a reading doubt.
- **Reading question raised after the witness check, 38, lower pairs, row 2.** The final text has « en opposition ». The opening vowel could instead be a small *a*, giving « en apposition ». It is compressed immediately after *en*. The text has been left as hashed.
- **Reading question raised after the witness check, 47, last bracketed paragraph.** Where the text has ∃1 before « (unique) », the last black stroke could be !. It has little visible foot; the final section numeral 1 at the bottom has a distinct base. The repetition « (unique) » permits either reading.
