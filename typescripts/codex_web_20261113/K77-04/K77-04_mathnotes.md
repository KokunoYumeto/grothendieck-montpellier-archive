## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 50.** A corollary describes the action of $\mu_2$ and identifies $x\mapsto-x$ with the canonical involution in the case under discussion. The presentation of $B$ as a quotient of the affine-function algebra leads to the more general formula $\sigma x=b-x$ in (94).

**U02 · Scans 51–52.** In characteristic two the trace of the unit vanishes. The trace then gives a section $\underline b$ of $L$, independent of the splitting, with $D=\underline b^2$. The class of the torsor $F$ lies in $H^1(S,L)$. The involution becomes translation by $\underline b$.

**U03 · Scans 52–53.** When $\underline b$ is nonzero on every fibre, the notes describe the étale case by the quotient $F/\underline b$. Formula (101) uses the arrow $x\mapsto x^2+\underline b x$. Pushing the torsor along this arrow gives the torsor $C$ in (102). A section of $C$ recovers the quadratic cover by inverse image. The comparison with the torsor of § 6 is explicitly deferred.

**U04 · Scans 53–54.** The first “Cas mixte” starts with an invertible module $L$ and a section $D$ of its tensor square. The construction is separated over the open sets where $2$ and $D$ are invertible. Over $S_2$ the equation is $x^2=D$. Over $S_D$ the question is how to extend the étale cover from their intersection. A crossed first plan survives beside the heading.

**U05 · Scans 54–55.** With $f$ regular, the pair consisting of an $L$-torsor $F$ and a section of $fF$ is described in terms of a section $b_0$ of $L_0$. The local description uses fractions $x$ for which $fx|S_0=b_0$. A slanted marginal addition considers a quasi-coherent $L$ on which $f$ acts regularly. In the quadratic problem formula (106) requires $b_0^2=D_0$.

**U06 · Scans 56–57.** The candidate quadratic structures form a torsor $Q(\underline b)$. The function $\Theta$ on it changes by $-4\varpi$ on translation by $\varpi$. Solving $\Theta(q)=D$ leads to a section $a_1(D)$ on $S_1=V(4\underline O_S)$. Its vanishing is (114). The theorem collects the data $(L,D,b_0)$ and the two conditions on them.

**U07 · Scan 58.** The first corollary asks when data realised on a retrocompact open $U$ give a cover on $S$. The condition is schematic density of $U\cap S_0$ in $S_0$. The second corollary, under reducedness and normality of $S_0$ at each point, phrases the existence test at the local schemes of its maximal points.

**U08 · Scan 59.** A second statement headed “Cas mixte” sends a quadratic cover to $(L,D,b_0)$. Part a) asserts full faithfulness. Part b) discusses a representation $D=b^2-4c$ and the role of injectivity on $H^1$. Part c) states the criterion $D_1=\gamma(b_0)$. Here $\gamma$ sends a residue modulo 2 to the square of a lift modulo 4.

**U09 · Scans 59–61.** After a cancelled start, the proof of a) works on an affine base with models $\Delta(b,c)$. It expresses an isomorphism between two presentations by a translation $\tau$ satisfying $b'=b+2\tau$ and $c'=c+\tau b+\tau^2$. Regularity of 2 gives uniqueness. The proof of b) uses the torsor of lifts of $b_1$ and the injectivity hypothesis to obtain a global section $b$.

**U10 · Scans 61–62.** The proof of c) passes from the affine case to the general case by locality. The following NB separates the mod-4 square condition from the existence of a global lift $b_1$ of the prescribed $b_0$. It places the obstruction in $H^1(S_0,L_0)$. Corollaire 1 mentions its vanishing, including the affine case.

**U11 · Scans 62–64.** For reduced $S_0$, the notes forget $b_0$ and describe the image in terms of $(L,D)$. The local square condition on $D_1$ is separated into a square condition on $D_0$ and the equation $D_1=\gamma(b_0)$. A second formulation is $\alpha(D_1)=0$, with $\beta(\alpha(D_1))=\gamma(b_0)-D_1$. Multiplication by two identifies the kernel used to define $\beta$. Corollaire 3 returns to the maximal-point test when $S_0$ is reduced and normal.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01 · Model, high confidence. Scans 60–61.** Direct expansion of $(b+2\tau)^2-4(c+\tau b+\tau^2)$ gives $b^2-4c$. This checks the displayed live change-of-coefficients rule. The blackened superscript in the later equality on scan 60 remains a separate reading problem.
- **R02 · Model, high confidence. Scan 59.** For two lifts $x$ and $x+2k$, the difference of the squares factors as $4k(x+k)$. This checks the residue calculation used to define $\gamma$.
- **R03 · Model, high confidence for the example; medium for the intended scope. Scan 62.** Corollaire 1 states $b_1^2=D_1$ without repeating $b_1|S_0=b_0$ from the question above. The distinction matters for a nonreduced fibre. In $\mathbb Z[e]/(e^2)$ take $D=1$ and prescribe $b_0=1+e$ modulo 2. Modulo 4 the section 1 squares to 1, while every lift of $1+e$ squares to $1+2e$. An exhaustive check of the sixteen residue classes confirms the distinction. The corollary can be read with the preceding lift requirement understood.
- **R04 · Model reading question, medium confidence. Scans 50, lines 1–4; 53, lines 15–21; 59, lines 3–5; 60, line 9.** The transcription uses $\Delta$ for the cover. Its manuscript shape is a low round arch with two outward feet, closer in places to $\Omega$. This affects a recurring letter throughout the excerpt. The same referent is used consistently in the formulas.
- **R05 · Model reading question, low confidence about the alphabet. Scans 51, (98); 56, lines 8–11; 59, lines 3–5.** The discriminant is transcribed $D$. Its loop with a long crossing stroke and low returning tail can also look like $\partial$ or $\vartheta$. The author’s clearly crossed oval for $\Theta$ on scan 56 gives a point of comparison without settling the other letter.
- **R06 · Model reading question, low confidence. Scan 59, lines 25–27.** The frozen interlinear reading before “Zariski” begins “le prétors…” and marks the ending. On further inspection the small uprights and their grouping also suggest “le petit site”; the next word may end “-kien”. The cancellation crosses both lines.
