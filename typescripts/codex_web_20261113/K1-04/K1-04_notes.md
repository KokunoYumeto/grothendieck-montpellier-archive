## Steps the pages themselves mark as unverified

- **Scan 94, lines 2–4 and 29–34.** A pencilled question mark stands beside the introduction to Weyl's method. A second, less distinct mark stands beside the construction beginning « Si $N_0$ est une norme de Schatten … ». The sentence « Il est trivial » continues onto scan 95, where the triangle inequality is argued.
- **Scan 95, lines 27–31.** A faint possible question mark sits beside « forme indéterminée $0\times\infty$ ». The words specify that the second member is to take the value zero. There is no written question beside the mark.
- **Scan 97, left margins.** « Inutile, il suffit de procéder par dualité » proposes an alternative to the series argument for completeness. Beside the polar norm he writes « comme $(N^\circ)^\circ=N$, on peut le vérifier .... ». The dots are on the sheet. The subsequent crossed words cannot all be read.
- **Scan 98, lines 19–25.** A question mark stands beside the step describing a subspace as « total » and, in an insertion, « et fortement fermé ». It has no attached sentence.
- **Scan 100, upper left and foot.** The slanting note begins « Cas $N'\simeq N_\infty$ ou $N''\simeq N_\infty$ » and makes the reduction conditional on a compact case, « si l'on [adm.] ceci ». The abbreviated word is doubtful. A separate question mark stands beside the final compactness and duality argument, near the inference of completeness. It does not specify a single equation.
- **Scan 102, lines 2–5.** The isolated sign after « pour $x$ positif » could be a brace, a section sign or a question mark. Its connection to the proposition is uncertain.
- **Scan 104, lines 14–15.** A question mark in the left margin stands beside part 2, at « On a » and identity (20) for the adjoint. It has no verbal qualification.
- **Scan 105, lines 14–30.** « Tout est démontré, sauf 4. […] et 5. » refers to the numbered parts just stated. He then argues part 4 and refers part 5 to the first convexity theorem. Within the argument for 4, « (voir --) » leaves a reference blank. The ellipsis in this quotation represents the cancelled short word between « 4. » and « et 5. ».
