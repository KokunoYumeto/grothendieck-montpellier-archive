# K1-04 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**B93 · Scan 93, third convexity theorem.** Formula (41) compares a symmetric increasing convex function of the first $n$ singular values of $u+v$ with the same function of their coordinatewise sums. The following corollary and (42) are crossed through.

**Scan 93, norm inequalities.** A Schatten norm on $R^n$ gives a quantity on compact operators by evaluation on the first $n$ singular values. The boxed conclusions (44) and (46) are triangle inequalities. Between them, (45) records the finite partial-sum estimate for the $p$-norm.

**Scans 94–95, Schatten norms on sequences.** The definition requires symmetry, dependence on the absolute coordinates, monotonicity on positive coordinates and normalization at the first unit vector. The examples are $N_p$ and $N_\infty$. A given norm in $n$ variables is extended by evaluating it on the $n$ largest absolute coordinates. Diagonal multiplication on $\ell^2$ supplies the triangle inequality for the extension.

**B95 · Scans 95–96, truncations.** For a complex sequence $x$, the limit of the norms of its positive finite truncations defines $N(x)$, possibly infinite. Formula (5) treats homogeneity and subadditivity. The text gives the value zero to the product $0\times\infty$ here. Finiteness defines $\ell^N$, and (6) places it between $\ell^1$ and $\ell^\infty$.

**Scans 96–97, completeness and the smaller sequence space.** A long cancelled calculation on scan 96 bounds the norm of a series by the sum of its norms. Scan 97 uses its label (7) in a live proof of completeness. The closure of the finitely supported sequences is denoted by $\ell_N$. Formula (8) places that closure between $\ell^1$ and $c_0$.

**B97 · Scans 97–98, polarity.** The coordinate pairing defines a polar norm. The prose uses a prime and the display uses a small circle. Formula (10) bounds the sum of the absolute coordinate products. Restriction to the finite sequences and passage through truncations identify the continuous dual of $\ell_N$ with the sequence space for the polar norm.

**B98 · Scan 98, reflexivity of sequence spaces.** The proposition relates reflexivity of four spaces, using the original and polar norms and the upper and lower positions of the indices. An interlinear addition gives the equivalent equalities of each smaller space with the corresponding larger one. The argument goes around the four implications, then returns to the bidual.

**Scans 98–99, operator spaces.** The sequence of singular values of a compact operator supplies the defining norm of $L^N(E,F)$. The proof for $L_N(E,F)$ uses finite-rank truncations of the singular-value expansion and the vanishing of tails in $\ell_N$. In the sideways note on scan 99, a supremum involving a finite-rank trace pairing treats the case of the supremum norm.

**B99 · Scans 99–100, composition.** A coordinatewise estimate involving three norms becomes a bound on a product of operators. Scan 100 invokes the second convexity theorem and a product inequality for the first $n$ singular values. Taking the two polar norms gives the trace-norm bound (17).

**Scan 100, trace duality.** The writer starts with the isometry from $L^{N^\circ}(F,E)$ into the dual of $L_N(E,F)$ and argues that it is onto. He reduces to a positive operator and argues against an infinite-rank orthogonal projection. The argument ends with the completeness of the two operator spaces.

**Scan 101, corollaries and characterization.** The first corollary concerns the modulus of an operator. A cancelled corollary on order is followed by a corollary on its hermitian real and imaginary parts. A separate argument starts with a unitarily invariant norm on finite-rank tensors and a normalization on rank-one tensors. Restricting it to diagonal tensors recovers a Schatten norm.

**Scan 102, outline.** The boxed heading introduces a short sequence-space outline followed by an operator-space outline. There are two versions of the operator definition. The later version uses a supremum of traces to define the norm of a bounded operator. The ensuing statements cover closure, inclusions, adjoints and composition with bounded operators.

**Scan 103, consequences and products.** A projection argument makes operators in $L^N$ compact outside the case $N\simeq N_\infty$. The sheet specializes to $N_p$, records duality and reflexivity assertions, then revisits the three-norm product estimate using finite-rank compressions. It ends with a comparison for the eigenvalue sequence and a characterization heading.

**Scans 104–105, five-part theorem.** Part 1 compares the singular-value and trace-supremum definitions. Part 2 treats adjoints and multiplication by bounded operators. Part 3 collects product and trace estimates. Part 4 gives the trace dual and the conditions for reflexivity. The argument uses two orthonormal systems to put a copy of the sequence space into the operator space. The heavily revised list retains a second d). Part 5 gives (27) for the eigenvalues and refers back to convexity.

**Scan 107, factorization of entire functions.** The Weierstrass theorem starts a different text. With $f(0)\ne0$, formula (1) gives an entire exponential times a product over the zeros, using nonnegative integers $p_n$. A summability condition permits a specified sequence of these integers. Formula (2) gives a fixed-degree version. The sheet ends with the second product.
