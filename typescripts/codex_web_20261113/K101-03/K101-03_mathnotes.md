# Mathematical notes on cote 101, scans 30–43

## What the pages do

**Change of rings and duality (30).** Lemme 6 gives a cohomological spectral sequence for $A'\to A$. The adjacent diagram factors the Hom functor, and the sideways corollary records a one-degree change on passing to a quotient by $x$. Prop. 12 applies the lemma with a fundamental module in the second argument, relating $H^{-i}(M)$ and the duals of Ext groups with coefficients $\Omega_A^{(q)}$.

**Proposition 13 and its corollaries (31–32).** Below a cancelled draft, Grothendieck assumes $\operatorname{Ext}^{n-1}_A(k,M)=0$. For finite-length $N$, he writes $\operatorname{Ext}^{n}(N,M)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(N,H^n(M))$ and relates exactness, the injectivity of $H^n(M)$ and the next Ext vanishing. The proof and corollaries on 32 bring in a multiplicity $m$, lengths at a prime and a dualizing condition.

**The fundamental-module criterion (32–33).** Proposition 14 adds a Cohen–Macaulay hypothesis on $M$. Its conclusion is that $A$ is Cohen–Macaulay and $M\simeq\Omega_A^m$. The next sheet revises the corollaries, including a version in terms of the quotient of $M$ by a system of parameters. Both sheets ask which hypotheses the conclusion actually needs.

**Criteria for the ring itself (33–35).** Proposition 15 compares four conditions beginning with “$A$ est un module fondamental pour $A$”. Its proof uses $D(A)$, a multiplicity and lengths. A cancelled definition and theorem precede Prop 16 bis. That formulation adds that $A$ is Cohen–Macaulay and gives criteria involving Ext, a quotient by parameters and a presentation as a quotient of a regular ring.

**Gorenstein rings (35).** Déf 2 calls a Cohen–Macaulay ring fundamental over itself a Gorenstein ring. “Ex : les réguliers” precedes Proposition 17 on localization and parameter quotients. The final corollary is written as a noun phrase.

**A chart of implications (36).** Grothendieck fixes $M$, $A$, an ideal of definition, parameters, a prime of corank $n$, and a finite-length variable $N$. The chart groups Ext conditions with statements on $H^n(M)$, faithfulness and local lengths. The dotted return arrow carries a separate equidimensionality qualification. Condition (v) uses the dimension of an annihilator in the parameter quotient.

**Following the arrows (36–37).** The discussion of the converse singles out annihilators in $M$ and in $A$. On 37 the lettered implication chains compare exactness with duality and use a length bound to obtain $m=1$. Partway through the attempted implication from f) to a), the assumption that $M$ is Cohen–Macaulay is added above the line.

**Problems A, B and C (37–38).** The first two problems ask when a one-dimensional annihilator in a parameter quotient or an Ext module forces vanishing back in $M$, or in both $M$ and $A$. Problem C starts with two adjacent Ext vanishings and an inserted nonvanishing in the middle degree. It asks for a Cohen–Macaulay ring and a direct power of its fundamental module.

**The theorem and induction (39–40).** The theorem changes hypotheses in (i), (i bis) and (v). Under the Cohen–Macaulay condition on $M$, the proof takes a common non-zero-divisor, compares Ext over $A$ and over its quotient, and uses induction. On 40, the comparison of annihilators produces maps $H^n(A)\longrightarrow D(M)$ and $M\longrightarrow\Omega_A$. The argument uses equidimensionality to remove the kernel of the second map.

**A revised list (41).** The new list assumes $A$ is a quotient of a regular local ring. It brings the adjacent Ext vanishings, faithfulness, the length bound and the conditions on $H^n(M)$ into a single display with several cancellations. The oblique notes try examples and modifications. The boxed problem and the two cancelled “Le seul cas” paragraphs carry different qualifications.

**Exactness in degree $i$ (42).** The proposition identifies Ext on finite-length modules with Hom into $H^i(M)$ under a left-exactness hypothesis. The next paragraphs distinguish the identically zero and nonzero cases of an exact functor and ask what follows for $A$. A further formulation starts with right exactness, overwrites several exponents, and introduces $0\to M\to J\to M'\to0$.

**The ring case and a quotient calculation (43).** A numbered list for $M=A$ ends with the barred isomorphism $\overline A\simeq\overline\Omega_A$. A diagram arranges implications between groups of these conditions. The lower half starts a proof from (vii), compares the fundamental module of $A/xA$ with a quotient of $\Omega_A$, and ends with Ext, $\Omega_{A/x}$ and Hom in a short exact sequence.
