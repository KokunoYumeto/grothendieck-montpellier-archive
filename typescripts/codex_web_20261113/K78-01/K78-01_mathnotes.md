# K78-01 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**U1 · Scans 3–4.** A polygon in an affine plane $E$ over $k$ has a finite vertex set $S$ and a pair $\omega=\{u,u^{-1}\}$ of inverse circular permutations. The pair has two elements, hence at least three vertices. Regularity asks that the combinatorial automorphisms extend uniquely to affine automorphisms. The author adds unimodularity for the rotations and compares it with the action of a side reflection.

**U2 · Scans 4–6.** An edge of the combinatorial polygon gives a line of $E$. The drawings accompany the distinction between incidence with an edge and incidence with its geometric line. Properness identifies these incidences. Affine spanning, tested using three consecutive vertices, enters the uniqueness of an extension. A pinning by a fixed combinatorial polygon first uses a map on vertices. The next sheet considers separate maps on vertices and sides that respect incidence.

**U3 · Scan 7.** Taking $E=\mathbb C$ as a real plane, the author sets $s_i=\zeta^i$ for a primitive $n$-th root. Complex conjugation and multiplication by $\zeta$ give the displayed actions on the indices. The closing classification statements identify $\zeta$ with $\zeta^{-1}$ and introduce the parameter $\alpha(\zeta)=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$.

**U4 · Scan 8.** The first paragraph organizes the real models by the torsion group of $\mathbb C^\times$ and the action of inversion. Below it, the relation between four consecutive vertices compares two directed differences. The calculation gives $r=1+(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$, and the drawing marks the angle $\arg\zeta$.

**U5 · Scan 9.** After extension from the reals to the complex numbers, the rotation is displayed with diagonal entries $\zeta,\zeta^{-1}$. The vertex $1$ acquires coordinates $(1,1)$, and the reflection interchanges the axes. The author looks ahead to algebraically closed fields with characteristic prime to $n$.

**U6 · Scan 10.** The cancelled sheet records trace and determinant calculations for two plane endomorphisms. A brace calls the mixed term $\varphi(u,v)$. Lower down are triangular matrices, conditions involving $uD\subset D$ and $\operatorname{Tr}u=0$, and a quotient written next to a projective-space arrow. The ink gives no explicit transition from these calculations to the polygon argument.

**U7 · Scans 11–12.** A frame can be specified by an incident vertex and side, by consecutive vertices, or by a vertex and an orientation. Proposition 1 makes the frames a torsor for $\operatorname{Aut}(S,\omega)$. The corollaries express isomorphisms through the image of a frame. For a pinned object the scholium states uniqueness up to unique isomorphism. The NB asks how to write its affine plane and its vertices explicitly.

**U8 · Scans 13–14.** A regular polygon supplies a dihedral subgroup of the affine group and an orbit. One vertex specifies that orbit. To remember the cyclic structure one also chooses a pair of inverse generators. The author describes its cyclic subgroup, the quotient of order two acting by inversion, and a lift of the nontrivial quotient element with square one.

**U9 · Scans 15–16.** The proposed conditions include finiteness of the rotation subgroup, a torsor of vertices under it, and the requirement that the vertices span the plane. The next sheet starts instead with an orbit of the rotation subgroup and asks for stability under the full group. Condition (10) asks for a nonidentity symmetry fixing a chosen vertex. The last lines express a full pinning by $s_0$ and a neighbour $s_1=us_0$.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scans 7–8 · High confidence.** In these coordinates the four-vertex coefficient is $1+\alpha(\zeta)$. The final fraction on scan 8 and the definition on scan 7 give the same expression. Exact cancellation of the rational functions gives zero difference.
- **R2 · Scans 13–14 · High confidence for the example.** On five labelled vertices, the steps $1$ and $2$ give different inverse pairs of generators. Enumerating all permutations of the five labels gives the same ten-element normalizer for both pairs. This provides a small instance of the author's “Non”.
- **R3 · Scans 11 and 16 · High confidence for the example.** In that five-vertex action, the ten group elements send one ordered adjacent pair to ten different frames. Exactly one nonidentity element fixes a chosen vertex. These counts distinguish the stabilizer of a vertex from that of a frame in the two manuscript statements.
- **R4 · Scan 10 · Low confidence as an interpretation of this cancelled sheet.** The brace under the trace expression appears to record the mixed term of the determinant. Expanding the determinant of the sum of two symbolic $2\times2$ matrices gives precisely that term. The calculation does not settle the adjoining pale words or the reason this sheet lies here.
