# K157-1-11 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**PS1, PS2 and the equalizer (166–167).** The argument begins in the middle of the proof of 2.6. Grothendieck asks what PS2 says for two maps from a to b. He draws their equalizer as the pullback of the diagonal of b along (u,v). The bottom of 166 contains successive versions of the connectedness and nonemptiness argument. On 167 he passes to a site over W and a generating family in the induced topos.

**Lemma 2.9 (167).** For a full generating subcategory S of 0-connected objects, the lemma compares 0-connectedness of the topos with 0-connectedness of S. Its longer formulation compares subobjects of S that are both open and closed with the corresponding decompositions in the topos. He points out where he had already used the comparison.

**Lemma 2.10 (168–169).** If S is cofiltered, the product of two of its objects, considered in the presheaf category, is 0-connected. The proof takes two maps into the product, constructs a common predecessor and applies PS2 twice, once for each component of the pair. The implication diagram on 169 records what this has proved about the previously named conditions.

**Proposition 2.11 and corollary 2.12 (169–171).** Grothendieck restates the condition on products as the possibility of completing diagrams by adding an object e. The large diagram on 170 identifies two squares that must commute. The margins investigate the converse, including a groupoid example. The corollary on 170–171 gives equivalent conditions expressed with induced topoi and slices. It explicitly sets c and c′ aside.

**Definition 2.13 (171).** Local total 0-connectedness of a topos is expressed in terms of connected induced topoi, or of a fibre product of two 0-connected objects over a third. The text also describes a locally cofiltered full generating category of 0-connected objects. The final paragraph returns to the outstanding claim about ∞-connectedness.

**Corollary 2.14 and the argument about coverings (172).** For the topos of a topological space T the corollary identifies the local condition with local irreducibility. The connected nonempty open subsets furnish the proposed generators. A reference to Serre distinguishes general sheaf cohomology from cohomology with constant coefficients. Grothendieck then uses the diagonal of U and the connectedness of U×U in the argument that a covering of the final object is trivial.

**Vanishing for constant coefficients (173–174).** For a constant abelian sheaf F the target is the vanishing of Hⁱ for i>0. The argument proceeds by induction, choosing an epimorphism U→e on which a class ξ vanishes. Grothendieck draws the remaining rows in a spectral sequence and places ξ under the middle group in an exact sequence. He identifies the complex appearing at the left with the complex of powers of I=π₀(U). Its positive-degree cohomology vanishes, giving ξ=0 at the top of 174. The page indices and several exponents in the first sequence are overwritten.

**Proposition 2.15 (174–176).** The category X is filtered precisely when the final presheaf is ind-representable. Formula (2.15.1) expresses it as the colimit of the representables indexed by X. For the converse, write it as a filtered colimit indexed by I. The Hom-sets become nonempty, and two images of an arrow eventually agree. These are used separately to produce a common target and to equalize a parallel pair.

**Corollaries 2.16 and 2.17 (176–177).** For a fundamental localizer satisfying the stated limits axiom, Grothendieck asserts W-asphericity for a filtered or cofiltered category. In the filtered case he gives the stronger assertion of total W-asphericity. The argument follows the final presheaf through f! and represents the resulting category over Y as a filtered colimit of categories over the objects f(xᵢ).

**Corollary 2.18 (177).** A presheaf F is ind-representable precisely when the category X/F is filtered. Grothendieck applies 2.15 to X/F and passes from its final object to the presheaf F on X.

**Question and proposed proposition 2.19 (178).** The opening question asks whether total W∞-asphericity forces filteredness. The proposed argument uses cofinality of the diagonal and a square involving f×f to obtain total W∞-asphericity. The author writes « Faux » beside the proposition and « C’est faux » beside the proof. The corollary involving the category denoted by a doubled triangle belongs to this rejected argument.

**Counterexample 2.20 (179–180).** Grothendieck adjoins a closed point e to X as the complement of an open subcategory, specifying its incoming arrows by a presheaf H. He identifies the relative slice over e with the category over H. Applying 2.19 would make every 0-connected presheaf on a totally 0-aspherical X ∞-connected. He rejects this consequence using a test category and gives the doubled triangle as his example. He then returns to the implication from total W∞-asphericity to filteredness and calls it a conjecture.

**Return to cofinality, formulas (3.1)–(3.6) (181).** The new section considers the localizer W₀ detected by π₀. It gives a canonical equivalence for HOT indexed by I and a formulation involving the derivator of sets. It describes the functor from a diagram of categories by applying π₀ and, in the description using Cat/I, assigns to i the set π₀(i\\X).

## Questions and conjectures

- **169–171.** « Mais pas l’implication inverse », « si et seulement si ? » and « est-elle vraie » qualify the proposed converse from the conditions on the slices. The groupoid paragraph in the left margin examines it. Several words in the conclusion of that paragraph remain unresolved.
- **171.** The corollary excludes c and c′ as « plus faibles apparemment ». The qualification is part of the statement. A separate oblique note ends in a question about the reference to proposition 2.3.
- **175.** « c’est dû à P. Deligne ? » asks who should receive credit for the reindexing result.
- **178.** « Cela implique-t-il que X soit ... filtrante ? » is followed by « Ça ne m’étonnerait pas » and a proposed weaker hypothesis involving 1-connectedness. A marginal note appears to substitute « 2-connexe ». At the bottom he also asks whether total W∞-asphericity implies total 0-connectedness of Xᵒ.
- **179–180.** « Contr’exemple à la proposition fausse 2.19. » withdraws the proposition. After « Mais c’est manifestement faux », the separate implication “X totally W∞-aspherical ⇒ X filtered” is left under « la conjecture ... pourrait être valable ».

## Remarks of the model

**R174 · High confidence.** Evaluating (2.15.1) at a gives a singleton. In the colimit of the sets Hom(a,x), every element has the same class as the identity of a. This checks the displayed identity at each object, independently of the hypothesis of filteredness.

**R179 · High confidence.** The description H(x)=Hom in X′ from x to e explains the identification X/e≅X/H. On each side an object over x is specified by the same arrow x→e, with the same compatibility condition on morphisms. This checks the particular identification used on 179.

**R181 · High confidence.** In (3.6), precomposing by a morphism i′→i takes an object under i to an object under i′. Applying π₀ gives the variance of the presheaf in (3.6). This local check supports the suggested opposite in the target of (3.2); it does not check the equivalence of derivators.
