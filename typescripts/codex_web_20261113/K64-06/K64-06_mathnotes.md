# Mathematical notes on cote 64, scans 66–77

## What the pages do

**Four-torsion and its lifts, scans 66–67.** With the residual characteristics different from 2, \(M={}_4E\) is presented as an extension of \(M_0\simeq {}_2E\) by \(M_0\). The nonzero elements of \(M_0\) form the degree-three cover \(\underline J\). Formula (44) defines \(V_{\underline J}(A)\) as the kernel of the sum from \(A^{\underline J}\) to \(A\). For \(A\) killed by 2, (45) identifies it with \(\operatorname{Hom}(M_0,A)\). In (43) the inverse image of the identity endomorphism is the torsor whose section enters the pinning of \(M\).

**Relative pinning and the octahedron, scans 68–69.** A section of \(P_M\) amounts to a lift \(\underline J\to M\) of the inclusion \(\underline J\hookrightarrow M_0\) with trace zero. Projecting into the quotient of \(E\) supplies a map \(\underline J\to\Delta_t\). The condition that its composite with \(f_t\) be the identity selects a face of the combinatorial octahedron. The last sentence mentions a section of \(\mu_4^\ast\) and a biquadratic cover. It ends with suspension points.

**Tame ramification and tangent lines, scan 71.** For a smooth relative curve \(X/S\) with a section \(t\), the author considers finite maps \(X'\to X\) with a marked lift \(t'\). He gives an étale local model \(z^n=\varphi\), where \(\varphi\) cuts out \(t(S)\), and specifies in the margin that \(n\) is prime to the residue characteristics. The local norm yields (2), an isomorphism from the \(n\)-th tensor power of the tangent line at \(t'\) to the tangent line at \(t\).

**The classifying data, scans 72–74.** An action of a finite étale group \(\underline G\) gives a torsor over \(X^\ast\). The action of the stabilizer of \(t'\) on the tangent line yields a monomorphism from \(\mu_{n\,S}\) into \(\underline G\). The restriction over \(X^\ast\) gives the section \(\xi\) of (4). The proposed category records \(\underline G,n,i,\xi,T,k\), including the invertible module \(T\) and its specified \(n\)-th power. The morphisms permit different ramification orders, with \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{n/n_1}\) on roots of unity. The author asserts that the resulting functor is an equivalence.

**Quadratic specialization, scan 74.** Taking \(\underline G=\mu_2\) and \(n=2\), the text fixes a class satisfying total ramification and describes the remaining choice as a line \(T\) with \(T^{\otimes 2}\simeq T_{X,t}\). It then turns to a projective line associated to a cubic étale cover and to the image of the zero section of an elliptic curve. The sentence breaks off at the foot of the leaf.

**Elliptic curves and cyclic orders, scan 75.** The argument restarts over an algebraically closed field. The three nonzero points of order 2 give \(J\). The projective line \(\Sigma_J\) carries its marked divisor \(D_J\) of degree 3. The image \(t\) of the origin lies off \(D_J\). A tangent line \(T=T_{E,0}\) with its square identified with \(T_{\Sigma,t}\) completes the data. In (6) the tangent bundle is expressed using \(\underline O_\Sigma(2)\) twisted by the two cyclic orders on \(J\).

**The square and the twist, scan 76.** The naive choice \(O_t(1)\) gives two natural identifications of its square, indexed by the two orientations. Grothendieck introduces a torsor \(Q_0\) under \(\mu_4\), pictured in the prose as the vertices of a square. He twists \(\underline O_t(1)\) by \(Q_0\). The tensor square then involves \(D_{Q_0}=Q_0/\{\pm1\}\), the set of diagonals. Formula (11) gives identifications of the orientations of the square with \(\{i,-i\}\) and of its diagonals with the orientation set of \(J\).

**Legendre pinning, scan 77.** The chosen square and (11) supply (12). A pinning is an isomorphism \(k:T_0\to T_{E,0}\) whose tensor square fits into (14). The two choices form a \(\mu_2\)-torsor. For varying \(E\) the definition also requires an isomorphism \(\alpha:D_{Q_0}\to\underline\omega_J\). The leaf closes with a comma after the compatibility condition.

## Statements left without proof

- Scans 66–67 give the torsor interpretation with « La donnée d'un \(M\) équivaut à celle d'un \(M_0\) » and « dont l'image inverse dans (43) est le torseur cherché ». The displayed maps explain the construction. A separate proof of the full equivalence is absent from these leaves.
- Scan 71 states « on définit un isom. canonique » at (2) after giving the local model. The gluing of the local norm identifications is implicit.
- Scans 72–74 say « est défini à isom unique près » and « je dis que ce foncteur est une équivalence de catégories ». They describe the objects and maps, without giving a quasi-inverse or proving full faithfulness here. Scan 73 also says in the margin that the target triple « est déterminé à isom can. près, quand on se donne » the group map.
- Scan 75 states « La donnée de \(E\) est équivalente à celle de \(J\) » and goes on to specify \(t,T\) and the isomorphism (5). It says the equivalence is « valable en fait sur base \(S\) quelconque à car. rés. \(\ne2\) ». In the same scan, « Notons qu'on a un isomorphisme canonique » introduces (6) without a derivation.
- Scan 77 says of the compatible pinnings, « Il y en exactement deux, différant par le signe ». The triangle gives the required compatibility. A separate existence argument is absent from this leaf.

## Questions and conjectures

None explicitly stated. The phrase « qu'on précisera » on scan 76 concerns the choice of \(Q_0\).

## Slips

- Scan 69 has « standart ». The final t has a cross-stroke, unlike the d in « défini » beside it. The usual spelling is « standard ». The separate possible extra accent on « octàèdre » remains a reading question.
- Scan 74 has « étales au dessus de \(X\) » immediately before total ramification along \(t\). The character after « de » is a bare \(X\) in the first strip. \(X^\ast=X\setminus t(S)\), defined on scan 71, is the likely intended locus.
- On scan 74 the written operator in the cohomology class has the circle low and the star high, \(f_\circ^\ast\). Formula (4) on scan 72 has \(f^\circ_\ast\) for the direct image. That is a possible intended reading on scan 74. Confidence in diagnosing a mathematical slip is low. The differing heights of the two marks can be seen on the scan.
- Scan 77 has « Il y en exactement deux ». After « en » the next strokes already form the beginning of « exactement ». Ordinary syntax calls for « Il y en a exactement deux ».
- Scan 77 has « la donnée d'un épinglage de Legendre sera défini ». The ending of « défini » has a short dotted i and no further e. Agreement with « la donnée » would give « définie ».

## Pointers

On scans 67–69, « la structure d'extension (39) », « dont l'image inverse dans (43) » and « la condition de relèvement pour (47) » point back within the four-torsion construction. Scan 72 recalls « (3) (4) et de (2) ». Scan 77 connects the pinning to the earlier formulae through « définissant \(T_0\) par (8) », « muni des données (11) » and « satisfaisant la compatibilité (14) ». « Legendre » occurs in the title on scan 70 and in the name of the pinning on scan 77. The leaves give no citation to a specific work by Legendre, to another cote or to a named paper.

## Remarks of the model

- **R76.1 · High confidence.** Numbering the vertices of the square cyclically \(0,1,2,3\) makes the quotient in (10) concrete. A half-turn identifies opposite vertices. A quarter-turn exchanges the two resulting diagonals. Enumerating the four rotations on the two diagonal sets gives, in order, identity, transposition, identity, transposition. This checks the finite square model used to illustrate (10).
- **R77.1 · High confidence.** The two views of \(k\) on scan 77 have compatible directions. In (13) the source \(T_0\) is on the right of a leftward arrow. In (14) its tensor square is on the left of a rightward arrow. Both point from the chosen line towards the tangent line of \(E\). The arrowheads and (5), (12) identify the corners without relying on the ambiguous mark beside the left diagonal.
