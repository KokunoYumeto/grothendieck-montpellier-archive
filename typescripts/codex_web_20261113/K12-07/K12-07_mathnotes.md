## What the pages do

**99 · Outline.** The author distinguishes the weight and level of a Galois module, then lists the geometric cases he wants to treat. He distinguishes smooth, separated, proper and arbitrary $X$, asks about the largest quotient of weight $i$, and reserves entries for virtual Betti numbers, counterexamples and twisted coefficients.

**101 · Geometric example.** Two branches of $T$ cross in one drawing. The corresponding branches of $\widetilde T$ are drawn apart, with one marked point on each. The sheet defines $T'=\{a,b\}$, $X=\mathbb P^2-T'$, $Y=T-T'$ and $U=\mathbb P^2-T$. Several cohomology diagrams compare the groups and their images. The diagonal arrow in the last diagram still carries a question.

**104 · The first two degrees.** The $H^1$ table places levels $0,1,0$ under weights $0,1,2$. The $H^2$ table places $0,1,2,1,0$ under $0,1,2,3,4$. Braces pick out the proper and smooth ranges. The prose singles out weight two and considers possible extensions for $H^2_!$.

**105 · Boundary calculation.** For smooth quasi-projective $X$ the author writes $X=X'-Y$, with $X'$ smooth projective and $Y$ a union of smooth divisors meeting with normal crossings. The exact sequence puts $H^i_!(X)$ between boundary cohomology and $H^i(X')$. The second calculation takes cohomology of intersections of boundary components. Its term of bidegree $p,q$ receives weight $q$.

**105–106 · Proposition 1 and its dual.** The proposition gives weights from 0 to $i$ for $H^i_!(X)$, and identifies its last weight piece by the image in $H^i(X')$. For $i\ge n=\dim X$, the lower weight bound becomes $2(i-n)$. On 106 the terms of the filtration are tabulated, including an image quotient and a kernel. The corollary gives the dual ordinary-cohomology statement. Its marginal note stresses that the levels there decrease.

**107 · Smooth schemes beyond the quasi-projective case.** For separated $X$ the author invokes a compactification of Nagata and resolution. For nonseparated $X$ he works with ordinary cohomology and an affine covering, and records effectivity and upper bounds for weights and levels. He isolates one possible improvement as a question.

**107–108 · Singular schemes.** For compact supports the argument cuts off a dense smooth open $U$ and applies induction on the dimension of its complement. For ordinary cohomology it uses reduction to affine $X$, induction on degree and a descent spectral sequence. The term with $p=0$ is dealt with as a subobject of $H^i(X')$; the page also cites vanishing above the dimension in the affine case.

**108 · Smooth twisted coefficients.** The coefficient $F$ is first taken as a direct factor of $R^jf_*$ for a smooth projective morphism. Degeneration attributed to Deligne makes $H^i(X,F)$ a piece of $H^{i+j}(Y)$. The page transfers bounds from this group and gives a dual compact-support statement. It treats proper $X$ separately at the foot.

**109 · Local descriptions of $F$.** The author cuts $X$ into pieces for cohomology with proper supports. Under the smooth twisted-constant hypothesis he passes to ordinary cohomology by duality. For a general effective coefficient of weight and level $j$, C.2 asserts weight at most $2\inf(i,n)$ and level at most $\inf(i,n)$ in ordinary cohomology. The argument breaks off with a marginal question mark.

**111 · Special sheaves and twists.** The prime is fixed and $Q_l(1)$ is defined using the Tate module of $G_m$. In 2.1, a special sheaf of weight $i$ is described using a twisted quotient of a twisted-constant subsheaf in a smooth projective higher direct image. The lemma lists closure properties, tensor products, inverse images, a twisted dual, and a Tate example. Short justifications refer to a disjoint union, a fibre product, base change, Lefschetz and projective space.

**111–112 · Effective admissibility.** The definition uses filtrations on affine opens with successive quotients $i_!G$; sets $P$ constrain the weights of the pieces. A marginal warning concerns intersecting such sets. Criterion 3.2 uses a partition by immersed subschemes of finite presentation and filtrations on those pieces. A long return arrow places it in relation to Proposition 3.3. The proposition treats subquotients, finite sums, extensions, tensor products, base change and twisted duals.

**112–113 · Weight together with level.** Section 4.1 permits pieces $G'(-k)$ and links the underlying weight $j$ to $j+2k\in P$. The same pieces must realise the level bound. Proposition 4.2 gives the corresponding closure properties and writes the weight of a twisted dual in terms of $\nu,k,h,n$.

**113 · Theorem 5.1.** For a separated morphism of finite presentation and $F$ strictly special of weight $j$, the conclusion concerns $R^if_!(F)$. Its weight bound is $i+j$ and its level bound is $\inf(i+j,2n-i+j)$, where $n$ is the supremum of fibre dimensions. When $i\ge n$, a table pairs successive weights beginning at $2(i-n)$ with level bounds beginning at zero. The proof starts with a smooth projective direct image, invokes proper base change and dévissage, and arranges a smooth compactification with a relative normal-crossings divisor after a change of base.

**114 · Continuation and corollaries.** The draft considers reducing the relative dimension to $j$ and then uses degeneration of a composite-direct-image spectral sequence. Corollary 5.2 makes a semisimplicity hypothesis in order to extend the conclusion to an effectively admissible coefficient. The final corollary states preservation of effective admissibility. Its short parenthesis is significant for interpreting the hypothesis and is discussed below.

The set expression corresponding to the left side is $\{2n-i\mid i\in P\}$. The leading $2$ on the left and the bare $n$ on the right are clear. In the enlargement the article has a separate final curl, but the verb and especially the end of the participle are reduced to short strokes. ## Remarks of the model

**R105.1 · My arithmetic observation, high confidence.** Under the sheet's $i=p+q$, the expression $2(n-p-1)-q$ in the last display on 105 equals $(2n-i-2)-p$. The two uncancelled expressions can be compared independently of the uncertain preceding infimum.

**R113.1 · My arithmetic observation, high confidence.** In 4.2(d), substituting the written $n=\nu+k+h$ in $2n-\nu-2k$ gives $\nu+2h$. This checks the identity in the paragraph alone.

**R114.1 · My arithmetic observation, high confidence.** In the uncancelled table on 113 the difference between the displayed endpoint weights $i+j$ and $2(i-n)$ equals $2n-i+j$. That is also the handwritten upper bound in the table's last level cell. The observation gives a reason to consider the longer candidate on 114, without raising confidence in that reading.

**My reading question, low confidence · Scan 111, lemma (d), L36.** The frozen margin reads « d) utiliser Lefschetz ». On the enlargement the first word has several low connected strokes and a tall final group, compatible with « modulo ». The main line explicitly appeals to the Lefschetz theorem under either reading.

**My reading question, low confidence · Scan 114, Cor. 5.3, L33.** The frozen reading is « sous conj. type Tate ». The middle of the four-letter word after the parenthesis could also read « sans ». The curves merge, and the nearby reference to semisimplicity does not choose between them. The two readings have opposite consequences for the scope of the hypothesis.
