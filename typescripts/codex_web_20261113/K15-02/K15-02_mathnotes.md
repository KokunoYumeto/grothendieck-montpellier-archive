# Mathematical notes on cote 15, K15-02

Scan numbers refer to the archive PDF. The internal page numbers in the analysis typescript establish a second reading order.

## What the pages do

**Equivalences and orders (68, 70).** The manuscript lets the category of self-equivalences of a Deuring category \(C\) act on \(C\). It distinguishes the orbit described by orders up to isomorphism or conjugacy from classes of bimodules. Tensoring with a line module gives the functor from \(\mathrm{Pic}(A)\). The last display on 68 identifies a quotient with a divisor quotient and writes a cyclic term at each ramified \(p\). Scan 70 specializes to a local Dedekind ring, then discusses the obstruction to lifting an automorphism in the global case. Its last sequence has separate annotations for units, Frobenius and two different sets of classes.

**Lattice models (72).** For orders \(o,o'\), the page relates locally free bimodules of rank one to lattices in \(E\). It places the condition on inner automorphisms in an insertion. A cancelled expression for composition is followed by \(L\otimes_{o'}M\). A second description has special lattices as objects of a Hom category and elements \(\lambda\) satisfying \(\lambda L=M\) as arrows. A note points from the uncertain exponent on \(E\) to “centre de \(E\)”.

**Commutativity (74, 76).** Condition (C) says \(LM=ML\) for the special two-sided lattices in question. The manuscript uses representatives \(L',M'\) in \(E\) and their products to describe a commutativity constraint. The proposition on 76 says it is independent of the choice of \(X\). Changing the representative brings in a lattice \(P\). The lower edge of the triangle sends \(L\) to \(PLP^{-1}\); the diagram below compares the two orders of multiplication.

**The common Picard category (78).** The conclusion identifies the Picard categories attached to different \(C\) and names the common category after Brandt. With one object fixed, the assignment \(C'\mapsto\underline{\mathrm{Equ}}(C,C')\) is displayed as an equivalence with a 2-category of tors-categories. The paragraph on base change states the hypotheses on \(C_{A'}\) and writes maps on both the Picard categories and the objects they act on.

**Square roots and majorants (79, 77).** Typewritten page −16− ends a square-root argument with the factorization \((g-g_1)(g+g_1)=0\) and the local invertibility of \(g+g_1\). It then defines a majorant by a bound for every coefficient norm. Page −17− starts with a bound on those norms, gives a product majorant and a second one obtained by substitution into a geometric series. The isolated handwritten calculations on 77 run the other way across the theorem; they include \(Q(i)\), a congruence modulo 4 and a bracket with \(-1\) above \(p\).

**Implicit holomorphic functions (77, 73, 75).** The theorem on typewritten page −17− assumes the functional determinant is nonzero. It states local existence with assigned values and begins the clause on a continuous competing solution. Page −18− completes that clause, translates the given point to the origin and uses the inverse of the matrix of linear coefficients to reach the system (5). Page −19− compares coefficients in (5). Formula (7), the lower bounds on the summed indices and the last strict inequality explain the induction in total degree. The excerpt stops in the middle of its last sentence.

**A differential system of majorants (71).** Typewritten page −25− compares (17) with (15), deduces the coefficient bound by induction, and chooses a rational majorant with three denominator factors. It reduces summability to finding \(p\) series solving (18). The sentence identifying their common value continues beyond the page.

**Frobenius (69).** Typewritten page −30− differentiates a composite, defines \(w_{kj}\), and exhibits its homogeneous linear differential system (27). The zero initial value and uniqueness give \(w_{kj}=0\). The page returns to \(u\), \(z\) and \(v\) to finish the differential equations and initial condition, then raises an editorial question about exterior algebra and local primitives.

**Polarization and a compact torus (84, 86).** A commutative link of a gerbe leads on 84 to an algebraic group \(G\) of multiplicative type. A faithful motive \(E\) and a polarization give an endomorphism algebra with involution. The identity on \(\ker\varepsilon\) leads to the claim that \(U^0\) is a compact torus. On 86 the algebra generated by \(G(\mathbb Q)\) is written as \(\prod L_i\). The page describes the involution on each quadratic extension \(L_i/K_i\), and writes \(H\) and \(H^u\) as products indexed by these fields. The final note asks for a further description of \(\varepsilon\) and \(j\).

**Sheaves and cohomology (85).** After a remark about the universe used for sheaves of sets, the typescript states full faithfulness and displays two comparison isomorphisms between cohomology on \(X_{\mathrm{ét}}\) and on \((\mathrm{Sch})_{/X}\). It identifies the maps as edge homomorphisms and invokes the comorphism criterion at the end. Several places reserved for symbols in the opening paragraph are blank on the sheet.
