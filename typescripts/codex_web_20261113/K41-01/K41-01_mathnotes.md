# Mathematical notes on cote 41, scans 002–014

## What the pages do

**The functor and its proposed representative (004).** Grothendieck singles out maps $\varphi$ that choose the generic point of an open part of each fibre. He considers such sections after a change of base $Y'\to Y$, names the resulting functor $F$, and asks if it is representable. His proposed test for a pair $(M,\varphi_M)$ compares $M(\Omega)$ with the irreducible components after extension to an algebraically closed field $\Omega$. The precise name he gives the chosen sections is doubtful.

**Reduction to a local base (005).** A set map $\psi:Y\to M$ is constrained by a residue-field condition and by the induced choice of components. The argument passes from the local ring at $y$ to an open neighbourhood, then to a complete local ring for existence. A marginal question concerns the descent step in the non-separated case.

**Choosing a component above the closed fibre (006).** Over a complete noetherian local ring the sheet starts with an irreducible closed fibre, with a qualified sense of irreducibility. The first argument follows two possible generizations to a discrete valuation ring and excludes a crossing. The next uses a finite extension of the fraction field and normalization. The resulting map $\varphi$ chooses a generic point over each point of the base. The abbreviations qualifying irreducibility remain particularly difficult here.

**Continuity and gluing (007).** The author returns to continuity of $\varphi$. With several closed-fibre components he takes one $\varphi_i$ for each. Open pieces of the representative $M'$ over the punctured base are identified with copies $Y'_i$. Gluing gives a quasi-finite étale prescheme $M$ and a map $M\to X\times_Y M$. The small primes in the gluing instruction affect which pieces one glues.

**The local lemma and separation (008).** The lemma collects the complete local base, the closed-fibre hypothesis, universal openness, an étale representative over the punctured base, and an exclusion of component merging after finite base extension. It concludes with a representative over $Y$. A corollary asks for separation on the punctured base and rules out a generic component acquiring two distinct specializations over a discrete valuation ring.

**The valuative criterion (009).** The theorem assumes a neighbourhood condition near the generic point of each fibre component. The neighbourhood maps universally openly and quasi-finitely to a polynomial space over $Y$. The proposed criterion for a separated étale $M$ tests how generic and special components correspond over a discrete valuation ring. The condition written at the foot is moved before the other by an arrow. The order of the two fibre arguments in that condition remains marked.

**The extra existence condition (010).** The NB describes an injection from the components over $y'$ to those over $\eta'$. The corollary adds (iii): each generic point $\xi'$ of a relative component of the generic fibre has a specialization $x'$ which is generic in a component of the special fibre. The concluding bracket explains how a fibre of $\overline{\xi'}$ could have smaller dimension than a component containing it.

**Two remarks (011).** The first asks how far condition (i) alone goes when separation is dropped. The second relates separability and properness of $f$, the number of relative irreducible components, the behaviour of a component under specialization, and existence and properness of $M$. The list (a)–(c) follows a cancelled attempt to phrase the equivalence.

**The proposition on irreducible fibres (012).** Put $\varphi(y)$ equal to the generic point of $X_y$. The sheet compares continuity of $\varphi$, openness of $f$, preservation of specialization by $\varphi$, and the lifting of a specialization of base points to a pair $x,x'$. The equality $\varphi^{-1}(U)=f(U)$ explains the connection between the first two conditions. The locally noetherian and finite-type hypotheses enter in the converse implication. The condition numbers have been revised.

**Definitions and restriction to opens (012–013).** Definition 2 names a morphism satisfying the proposition. Definition 3 describes a section obtained from a universally qualified open $U$. On 013 a smaller open surjecting onto the base retains the property. The slanted paragraph considers unions and an open associated with $\varphi$. The text then expresses the condition point by point and calls it local on $Y$.

**The second functor (013).** The author writes $I(X/S)$ and $I(X\times_S S'/S')$, describes contravariance in $S'$, and asks about representability. The diagram places the prospective $M$ and $M'$ beside $S$ and $S'=\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega)$ and shows the corresponding four schemes above them.

**Specialization under change of base (014).** A cancelled lemma seeks an open $U$ meeting a fibre densely and a specialization of a generic point after generizing the base point. The diagrams below distinguish original, primed and double-primed bases. The six hypotheses set up a pair of points in specialization. Conclusion (a) begins with $x'$ and seeks $\xi'$. Conclusion (b) starts with $\xi'$ and seeks $x'$, with an added hypothesis on $Y'$ and the point over $y$. The two crossed maps and their proposed surjectivity remain a separate discarded formulation.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1, high confidence, scan 012.** For an open $U$, the equality $\varphi^{-1}(U)=f(U)$ uses exactly the fact that every nonempty open in each irreducible fibre contains its generic point. This accounts for both directions of the continuity/openness comparison on that sheet.
- **R2, low confidence, scans 006 and 014.** The two uses of « lemme a) » and « lemme b) » fit the opposite starting points $x'$ and $\xi'$ in the two conclusions on 014. The shared lettering and hypotheses suggest that connection. No sheet number is cited.
- **R3, medium confidence, scans 009–010.** In 009 (ii) the phrase is « au plus un », whereas 010 (iii) says « il existe un ». For the same fixed generic component these impose uniqueness and existence respectively. This explains the position of the extra hypothesis after the NB about surjectivity.
