## What the pages do

**Gluing the family, scan 82.** Two products with fibre $\overline S$ are glued by $u_a$ and $u_b$. The first is the identity. The second fixes the two barred zeros and exchanges $\bar a$ and $\bar b$. The diamond identifies the four objects involved. A lemma tests properness and proper $\mathbb D$-fibration over a closed cover. The marginal note uses two open product charts for smoothness and records both $\mathit{Cat}$-fibrancy conditions.

**The cohomology calculation, scan 83.** To compare $S$ and $X$, Grothendieck decomposes the direct image of $k_X$ into a constant summand and a twisted degree-one summand. Around $S=S^1$ the monodromy is $\lambda\mapsto-\lambda$. He writes the coinvariant quotient $k/2k$ with zero underneath and concludes that $f$ is a $\mathbb D$-equivalence. The margin mentions a two-sheeted covering.

**The self-base-change, scans 83–84.** For $X'=X\times_S X$, relative Künneth produces a tensor square of the twisted summand. It appears as an untwisted degree-two term. The final comparison is underlined “pas iso”. The square on scan 84 identifies the lower arrow with $f$ and the upper arrow with the first projection. Grothendieck takes this as a counterexample to implication from the two conditions b), b′) to condition a) of his earlier Proposition 2.

**The finite-group construction, scans 84–85.** A finite group $G$ acts on a category $Z$. Grothendieck sets $S=[G]=B_G$, describes $E_G$ by its objects and its arrows, corrects the side of the free translation action to the left, and writes $B_G=E_G/G$. The total category is the quotient of $E_G\times Z$ by the diagonal action. One diagram compares the family over $B_G$, over $E_G$ where it is a product, and over $e$.

**The fibre inclusion, scans 85–86.** Here $|G|=n>1$ is invertible in $k$, and $\mathbb D=\mathrm{Der}(k\text{-}\mathrm{Mod})$. The manuscript calls $E_G\to B_G$ a $\mathbb D$-equivalence. Its calculation identifies the cohomology on the total space with $G$-invariants of the cohomology of $Z$. The example takes the order-two reflection of a circle. The inclusion of the fibre need not be a $\mathbb D$-equivalence even over the stated $\mathbb D$-aspherical base.

**Passing to ordered sets, scans 86–88.** The desired data are a free $G$-object $E$ with $H^\cdot(E,k)^G\simeq k$ and a $G$-object $Z$ with a nonisomorphic inclusion of invariants. Item b) writes a $G$ on both sides of that inclusion (S7 below). Grothendieck asks for ordered models of $E$, $Z$ and their quotients. If the invariants of $Z$ are also $k$, he presents $X$ as aspherical while the fibres of $X\to S$ fail to be $\mathbb D$-aspherical.

**Sphere and projective plane, scan 88.** The choice is $E=S^2$ with antipodal action, $B$ the real projective plane, and $Z=S^1$ with reflection. Grothendieck contrasts this base with the circle base of his first example. The three pairs of globular cells in dimensions $0,1,2$ give a six-object ordered set. Antipodism exchanges the two cells of each dimension. The quotient has parallel arrows, so he seeks another presentation.

**Subdividing the model, scan 89.** He gives the two relations for the parallel arrows, crosses out an alternative involving a two-object groupoid, and takes the barycentric subdivision. The resulting octahedron has six vertices, twelve edges and eight faces. Its quotient has three vertices, six edges and four faces. The picture of three projective lines labels the quotient cells. Two diagrams give the four-element model of the circle fibre, and the text counts $4\times13=52$ vertices for $X$.

**A revised base-change test, scan 90.** For a pair of arrows $Y''\to Y'\to Y$, the new a) asks for a $\mathbb D$-equivalence on the pullbacks when both testing bases are Hot-aspherical. The text singles out bases with final or initial objects for the universal left or right condition. The diagram draws implications from a) to b) and b′). Grothendieck asks for converses and suggests a way to reduce the tests in the margin.

**Der 3 and local constancy, scans 91–92.** Part a) asks for full faithfulness from $\mathbb D(e)$ when $Z$ is Hot-aspherical. Part b) concerns a Hot-equivalence and constant objects. Part a′) identifies the essential image with locally constant objects, described by sending every arrow to an isomorphism. The discussion of $i$-types invokes the $(i+1)$-type of the indexing space. A proper or smooth map then suggests a test of local constancy of $f_*f^*a_Y$ or $f_!f^*a_Y$. The stronger b′) concerns all locally constant coefficients.

**Levels of completeness, scan 93.** Grothendieck assigns the name 0-complete on the left to the cohomological comparison and on the right to the homological comparison. He calls an inverse-image functor on locally constant coefficients 1-complete for full faithfulness, 2-complete for equivalence. The main proposition assumes Der 3 b′ and tests a fibration by pullback along Hot-equivalences. A separate marginal argument proposes internal Hom objects for a converse about full faithfulness.

**Complete asphericity and fibrations, scan 94.** A category is completely $\mathbb D$-aspherical when its map to $e$ is 2-complete. A weaker axiom is proposed for shapes with an initial or a final object. The definition of fibration now tests base changes $Y''\to Y'$ that are 1-complete $\mathbb D$-equivalences. Under the indicated axiom the text identifies this definition with each earlier universal one-sided condition. The letters and primes on two axiom labels are doubtful.

**Cohomological and homological comparisons, scan 95.** Definition 1 tests all locally constant coefficients on the target by the induced isomorphism on cohomology, and gives the dual comparison on homology. Proposition 1 gives composition and two cancellation rules. For the cancellation that recovers the first map, the second map is required to be 2-complete so that the coefficient on the intermediate object comes from the last one.

**Complete cohomological fibrations, scans 95–96.** Definition 2 tests the fibres and the categories over objects, and requires the tests to survive every base change. The dual notion is named. A Hot-proper map supplies the example, using the admitted Der 3 b′₁. Proposition 2 (P2) says that $f_*F$ is locally constant, commutes with all base changes and is calculated on fibres.

**Corollary 1, scans 96–97.** In the cartesian square, a cohomological equivalence of bases gives a cohomological equivalence of the total categories under the fibration hypothesis. Scan 97 displays the proof. It passes through cohomology on $Y$ with $f_*F$, uses the lower arrow $p$, identifies the base-changed direct image by Proposition 2, and returns to cohomology on $X'$.

**Stability and Corollary 2, scan 97.** Proposition 3 asserts stability under base change and composition. For composition Grothendieck invokes Corollary 2, which characterizes the fibrations by preservation of cohomological equivalences after arbitrary pullback over $Y$. He derives necessity from Corollary 1 and starts the sufficiency argument with maps involving slices. The supplied text ends after “en”.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model remark · high confidence.** The zero under $k/2k$ on scan 83 needs a condition on $k$. Invertibility of 2 suffices. With $k=\mathbb Z/2$, multiplication by 2 has image zero and the quotient has two elements. The supplied excerpt starts partway through this example, so its earlier coefficient assumptions are undetermined here.

**R2 · Model remark · high confidence.** Counting the chains in the six-cell diagram on scan 88 gives $6,12,8$ by chain length. Pairing each chain with its simultaneous sign reversal gives $3,6,4$ orbits, as on scan 89. Their sum is 13, which accounts for the factor next to 4 in the manuscript’s count of 52.

**R3 · Model remark · high confidence about the wording.** On scan 93 the change from « équivaut sans doute » to « implique » limits the claim to one direction. The next parenthesis explicitly questions the converse. The margin conditions a possible converse on the availability and compatibility of internal Hom objects.
