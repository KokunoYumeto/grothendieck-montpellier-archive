# Mathematical notes on scans 018–033

## What the pages do

**Direct image with proper support (018, 020).** The opening fragment discusses an extension to morphisms of finite type and a reduction to an open immersion. Item 14 then factors a compactifiable $f$ into an open immersion $i$ and a proper $\bar f$. It forms $R^q\bar f_*(i_!F)$ for torsion $F$, asserts independence of the factorisation up to isomorphism, and identifies degree zero with direct image with proper support. The list on 020 gives comparison over $\mathbb C$, base change, cohomological dimension, finiteness, the Leray sequence, a vanishing assertion, étale trace and covariance, and relative cohomology.

**Local class for one equation (022–024, even scans).** With $n$ prime to the residual characteristics, a regular section $\varphi$ cuts out $Y$ in $X$. On the complement it is a unit. The quotient by units on $X$ removes the dependence on multiplication by a unit. The exact sequence with supports yields $\beta_{Y/X}$ in degree one. The Kummer boundary sends it to $\gamma^{(n)}_{Y/X}$ in degree two. The author describes Zariski localisation and base changes under which the pulled-back equation remains regular.

**Several equations and purity (024, 026, 028).** Locally writing $Y$ as the intersection of $d$ hypersurfaces, the author takes the cup product of their classes. He states independence of the presentation, admissible base change, the product rule without excess dimension, and compatibility as $n$ varies. He then assumes vanishing of the local cohomology sheaves below $2d$, lists situations in which he invokes purity, and uses a spectral sequence to pass from sections of the first potentially nonzero sheaf to cohomology with support. A margin note on 028 identifies a family of supports. Another relates the divisor class to the Kummer boundary and records the vanishing of its image with multiplicative-group coefficients.

**The two examples (028, 030).** For $X$ smooth over a separably closed field and a rational point $x$, the first example puts $\gamma_{x/X}$ in top cohomology with proper support. For geometrically connected $X$, the author calls it a basis independent of $x$. He recalls the complete-curve calculation with a map $t$ satisfying $t(\gamma_{x/X})=1$. In Example 2 both schemes are smooth over $S$. A diagram maps supported cohomology to sections on $S$ of a higher direct image with proper support. Its upper target was abandoned while being written.

**Global classes (032).** Item 16 fixes $A=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ with $n$ invertible on the schemes, specifies pure relative dimension for a smooth morphism, and writes $T_{X/Y}^{(n)}$ for the $d$th tensor power of the roots-of-unity sheaf. The construction is made piecewise in variable dimension. The page ends as a canonical homomorphism for a smooth, compactifiable morphism of pure relative dimension is being introduced.

**Graphs and root systems (033, 021, 019, in typescript order).** Page 188 describes the vertex weights of a Dynkin diagram. Proposition 2 extends a diagram isomorphism from simple roots to a linear map and compares Weyl group orbits. The corollary, continued on p. 189, identifies the quotient $A(R)/W(R)$ with diagram automorphisms. The typescript describes the edge orders of the Coxeter diagram and begins the classification with five drawn diagrams. Page 191 constructs roots from one or two length shells of a lattice, checks reflection stability using integrality, and lists the vectors of squared length one and two in the first lattice.

**Type $E_7$ (031, 029, 027, in typescript order).** Intersecting the ambient root system with the hyperplane perpendicular to $\varepsilon_7+\varepsilon_8$ gives the rank-seven system. The pages count its roots, describe its simple roots and highest root, list seven fundamental weights and give the exponents and Weyl group order. The last paragraph of this part records that the diagram automorphism group is trivial.

**Type $E_6$ (027, 025, 023, in typescript order).** Two orthogonality conditions give the rank-six subsystem. The typescript determines its simple roots and highest root, lists six fundamental weights and adds them to obtain $\rho$. It projects a lattice in the dual space, finds a cyclic quotient of order three, and uses the connection index and highest-root coefficients to compute the Weyl group order. It ends this extract with two equations for the remaining exponents.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model, high confidence.** On 031 and 027, the printed root-count expressions give 126 and 72. Dividing by the respective ranks gives the stated 18 and 12. The damaged typewriter letter in the sign-choice function plays no part in these numerical checks (M31-root-count).
- **Model, high confidence.** On 027 the seven $E_7$ exponents give $\prod_i(m_i+1)=2{,}903{,}040$, equal to the printed prime factorisation (M27-order). On 025, adding the six fundamental weights in simple-root coordinates gives $(8,11,15,21,15,8)$, as printed for $\rho$ on 023 (M23-rho).
- **Model, medium confidence.** The wording “intersection complète de dim relative 1” on 022 precedes construction from one regular equation. On 024 the author expresses the number of local defining equations as a codimension. This makes codimension one a plausible interpretation of the earlier example. The expression on 022 is preserved (M22-dimension).
- **Model, medium confidence.** Example 2 on 030 says “de codim 1” and then writes $2d$ and a $d$th twist. Specialising the notation to $d=1$ makes the prose and diagram consistent. Whether the author intended to state the example for general $d$ remains open (M30-dimension).

- **Reading question, model, low confidence.** Scan 023, typescript p. 218, (VIII), source file line 13. The frozen reading is “mais comme”. The final s of *mais* and the c of *comme* have unusually heavy impressions. A second strike of the same types is possible, as is uneven inking of single strikes. Neither impression reveals a different underlying letter. Compare the whole-page rectangle (641, 1021) to (856, 1104), measured from the top left in original pixels.
