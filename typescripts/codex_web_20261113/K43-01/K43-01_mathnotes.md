## What the pages do

**Modulus and class groups (scan 4).** The page introduces idèles, absolute and ordinary divisors, divisors prime to a modulus, and principal divisors satisfying congruence and real-place sign conditions. It gives a ray-class quotient, sequences relating it to the ideal class group, and a description through a quotient of the idèle class group.

**Norms and a first reciprocity statement (scan 5).** Norm quotients connect the modulus-dependent groups with the idèle class group. Frobenius substitutions lead to a proposed reciprocity map, followed by existence of a sufficiently large modulus, surjectivity, a distribution statement, and a norm-kernel description. The sheet writes the target as G(L/K); its precise scope is discussed below without changing that notation.

**Norm-one groups and units (scans 6–7).** Local multiplicative groups, local units and value groups are assembled into idèles and absolute divisors. Exact sequences and a diagram organize principal idèles, roots of unity and class groups. The norm-one compactness assertion is related to finiteness of the ordinary class group, the unit rank and a torus extension description.

**Open subgroups and ray-class calculations (scans 8–10, opening).** The notes describe open subgroups through congruence subgroups, use an approximation argument to compare idèle and divisor quotients, and identify principal divisors with a congruence condition. An exact sequence records units and local quotients. A local element is then represented by a divisor, with a separate construction when its place divides the modulus. The converse sentence on scan 8 is left unfinished.

**Global class-field assertions (scans 10–11).** The numbered Artin–Takagi statements concern a reciprocity map into G/G′, a modulus on which principal divisors vanish, surjectivity and distribution of Frobenius classes, the norm kernel, and an existence theorem for open subgroups. The final paragraph reduces the assertions to the maximal abelian subextension and a norm-image equality.

**Local reciprocity and the product formula (scans 12–13).** Local symbols take values in the common global quotient. The notes compare them, under an abelian hypothesis, with maps from local maximal-abelian Galois groups to decomposition groups. Statement 5 identifies the two local constructions; their compatibility is expressed by a product equal to 1 on global elements.

**Geometric diagrams and local-symbol pairings (scan 15).** Two ladders organize groups of points, the handwritten U/W/J groups, divisor groups and Hom terms, first for G and then in the multiplicative case. Pairings through local symbols are accompanied by local and global vanishing statements. A separate cohomology block and an Ext-origin note occur at upper right. The final pairing at the foot has no written target.

**Descended pairings and extensions (scan 16).** Further pairings are assigned to a generalized Albanese description, augmentations and local-symbol theory. A Hom map defines a kernel denoted J⁰, and an intersection of kernels defines J′. The final box places ξ in a two-input Ext¹ expression, with two underbrace identifications and the note “accouplement de Lang.” The sheet gives a construction outline, not a completed proof.
