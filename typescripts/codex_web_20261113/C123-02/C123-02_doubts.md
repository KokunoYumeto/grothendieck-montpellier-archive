## Legibility

The typed text is generally clear, but fading and overtyping sometimes leave the original letter uncertain. The French accents were checked directly, including the grave accent in bigèbre. The two right-edge truncations on scan 28 are retained without reconstruction.

The least reliable readings are the journal abbreviation and original letters under the corrections on scans 22–23, the cancelled references and last footnote sentence on scan 28, the rewritten ending in the top note on scan 40, and the tiny cancelled word before module libre in its lower note. One heavily inked word after fonctorielles on scan 40 remains physically unreadable and is represented by one illegible-word mark. All other uncertain readings carry explicit alternatives or a doubtful-reading mark. The handwritten signature is identified from its visible strokes with a doubt mark, without consulting an external signature specimen.

The inventory's attribution to Grothendieck is a working palaeographic attribution within the supplied sheets, not an independent authentication. Scan 40 is classified reused_typed because it is a typescript of another subject with handwritten additions; no conclusion about an unseen reverse side is implied. The long vertical line on scan 28 is treated as cancellation, and the smaller local corrections are preserved inside that state. The uncertain boundary/punctuation curve on scan 40 is retained as handwritten.

All four pages have completed first and second readings; the audit records changes and unresolved doubts. No text from omitted archive pages or external editions has been supplied.
