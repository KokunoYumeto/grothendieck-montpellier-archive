## What the pages do

**33 B01–B02.** The cover groups the sheets as « Calculs divers ». A pencil count includes a name and a question mark. It does not assign the individual arguments to a writer.

**35 B04–B06.** Theorem 1 describes the quotient of the fundamental group of \(B=E/G\) by the image of \(\pi_1(E)\). The proposed quotient is \(G/S\), with \(S\) generated by vertex stabilizers. Signed 1-simplices, their two ends, paths, loops and endpoint-preserving homotopy supply the vocabulary for the proof.

**36 B03–B06.** Lemma 1 lifts an edge path with a prescribed initial vertex. Lemma 2 uses a lifted loop and a translated loop to show that the image of \(\pi_1(E)\) is normal in \(\pi_1(B)\).

**36 B07–37 B06.** A path from \(x_0\) to \(gx_0\) defines \(\chi(g)\) after projection to \(B\). Lifting gives surjectivity. Concatenating a path with a translate gives the multiplication rule. A path through a vertex fixed by \(s\) shows that \(s\) maps to the identity. Conjugating stabilizers accounts for normality of \(S\).

**37 B07–38 B04.** Lemma 3 identifies \(E/S\) as a normal covering of \(E/G\) with group \(G/S\). The proof studies an element with \(gx=s_0x\) and concludes Theorem 1. The short cancelled line beneath the conclusion survives on scan 38.

**39 B03–B06.** Theorem 2 claims that the image on homology contains the multiples of \(n=|G|\). A sum over all translates of chosen lifts of the simplices in a cycle is asserted to be a cycle projecting to \(n\) times the original one.

**39 B07–B08.** When \(E\) is contractible and \(S\) finite of order \(n\), Theorem 3 claims that each higher homotopy element has order dividing a power of \(n\). The proof passes to the universal cover \(E/S\) and breaks off during the appeal to Theorem 2.

**42 B01–B05.** For a finite group acting on a ring \(B\), the notes compare surjectivity of the trace onto \(A=B^G\), existence of an element of trace one, and vanishing of Tate cohomology for compatible modules. Conjugation of the endomorphism given by multiplication by \(b\) relates the trace on the ring to the trace on endomorphisms. The first corollary concerns annihilation by trace elements.

**42 B06–B10.** A short exact sequence with middle term \(M\otimes_Z Z(G)\) gives a shift of cohomological degree. The action calculation is written out on a pure tensor. A second corollary gives invertibility of the ramification index as a sufficient condition in the stated local situation.

**43 B01–B07.** Passing to \(G'\subset G\) replaces the trace by a relative transfer. Two transfer conditions, two degree-specific labels, a square of implications and the reduction to degree zero organize the argument. The long oblique note considers inertia and Sylow subgroups.

**43 B08–B13.** A separate lemma asks what follows if the identity of \(M\) is a relative trace of a \(G'\)-endomorphism. The two arrows have opposite directions. The text describes stable elements in the image of the reverse arrow. A marginal enclosure describes a quotient for the first arrow, and a final line sets down a relation between transfers.

**44 B01–B08.** For a finite group acting transitively on the maximal ideals of a ring called « \(1/2\) local », the sheet chooses a decomposition subgroup \(G_d\) and an inertia subgroup \(G_i\). It gives a trace condition, then turns to first cohomology of units. The proposed restriction bijection and its matrix-group analogue are posed as questions.

**44 B09–B15.** A chain of identifications compares cohomology before and after passing to local factors. The writer attaches a Zariski justification to the first equality and draws a comparison diagram. The margin describes the horizontal maps \(\alpha,\beta\) as injective with dense images. The word for the passage made at the right side of the diagram is uncertain.

**45 B01–B06.** Two rows concern the Galois group of a maximal abelian extension, its unramified quotient, finite idèle units modulo principal units, and a contribution written \(Z_2^{r_1}\). A cancelled draft proposes analogous rows for a set \(S\) of places and asks about one of their quotients.

**45 B07.** A separate local lemma characterizes an unramified extension by norms of units. The sheet gives a second formulation using the norm of \(L^*\).

**47 B01–B11.** A proposition compares cohomological bounds over \(k\) for commutative algebraic groups, finite groups, irreducible finite modules, groups split as powers of \(G_m\), and unit groups of separable commutative algebras. Some clauses have unfinished right-hand sides. The first reduction filters a finite module by successive multiples of \(\ell\) and then reduces to irreducible factors.

**47 B12–48 B03.** The argument for \(b\Rightarrow a\) starts over a perfect field with the largest linear subgroup. The quotient reduces the argument to the complete and linear cases. The linear case is split again into unipotent and semisimple parts. The author explicitly queries descent of this decomposition.

**48 B03–49 B01.** In the semisimple and complete cases, the text extracts the torsion subgroup \(U\), describes \(G/U\) as torsion-free and divisible, and compares cohomology with that of \(U\). The groups \(U(m)\) of elements killed by \(m\) enter a direct limit. The sheet emphasizes this passage to the limit and inserts the finiteness of \(U(m)\).

**49 B02–B07.** For a connected unipotent group the sheet distinguishes characteristic zero and characteristic \(p\). In the second case it filters by \(p^iG\) and reduces to \(pG=0\). The argument invokes vanishing for a general linear group in both cases. The final line introduces an arbitrary field and stops.

**50 B01–B04.** Starting with \(\ell\ne p\), the sheet introduces the group algebra \(\bar k(\Gamma)\), its unit group \(G\), and the norm-one subgroup \(G_1\). It considers how \(\Gamma\) meets \(G_1\) and compares \(\Gamma\) with the kernel of multiplication by \(\ell\) on \(G_1\). The page ends at a dimension and order computation.

**51 B01–B04.** A lemma asserts quasi-algebraic closure for a finitely generated field of transcendence degree one over an algebraically closed field. Two corollaries concern vanishing for \(G_m\) and for a finite abelian group with trivial Galois action. The superscript on \(K\) in the first corollary is doubtful.

**51 B05–B10.** The subgroup of roots of unity in a Galois closure gives rise to a cohomology comparison qualified « mod \(p\) ». A power map is used for integers prime to the characteristic. For the remaining \(\mathbb Z/p\) case the text gives an additive exact sequence.

## Remarks of the model

- **R38.1 · Model, conditional, high confidence.** At 38 B02 the inference from moved vertices to a « simplexe disjoint » depends on “disjoint.” In the oriented three-edge cycle the rotation sends the closed edge \(01\) to \(12\). It moves every vertex, yet these closed edges share vertex \(1\). Their interiors are disjoint. The example bears on that sentence under the closed-simplex interpretation. The covering in this example still exists.
- **R39.1 · Model, high confidence.** The coefficient \(n\) in 39 B05 is accounted for by the projection: all the translates \(g\tau_i\) project to \(\sigma_i\). This checks the equality after projection. Whether the displayed chain is a cycle is a separate assertion.
- **R50.1 · Model, high confidence under the stated hypotheses.** At 50 B03 the exceptional intersection can occur. Take \(k\) algebraically closed of characteristic \(3\), \(\Gamma=C_2=\{e,s\}\), and \(\ell=2\). The Galois action on the one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-module is trivial. Left multiplication by \(s\) interchanges the two basis vectors of \(k[\Gamma]\), so its determinant is \(-1\). Thus \(\Gamma\cap G_1=\{e\}\). This checks the unqualified exclusion against the page’s own determinant definition.
- **R51.1 · Model, low confidence about the reading.** The exponent on \(K\) in 51 B02 was read at the freeze as a star, 2 or a prime. The mention of \(K'\) just below supplies a reason to consider the prime. Its shape in the corollary does not settle the matter.
