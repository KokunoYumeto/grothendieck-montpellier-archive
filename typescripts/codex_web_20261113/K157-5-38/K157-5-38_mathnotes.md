# Mathematical notes on scans 588–602

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 588.** On reused stationery, Grothendieck puts a boxed product of categories beside an underlined Hom. He writes a comparison for maps out of that product, tries it on diagrams and pointwise direct limits, and asks about special Quillen structures. The lower schemes give possible classes of arrows. A diagonal crosses the whole manuscript field.

**U02 · Scan 589.** Item 1 writes the universal property in both orders and ends with a Hom of two variables, with a semicolon before \(P\). It identifies \(\mathrm{Ens}\) as a two-sided unit. Item 2 compares passage to the cardinal \(\pi\) with the boxed product and with \(\mathrm{Ind}_\pi\). Item 3 asks how to interpret these operations for species of algebraic structures.

**U03 · Scan 590.** Item 4 specializes to categories of the form \(\mathrm{Ind}(C)\). The subscript \(\mathrm{PF}\) selects finitely presented objects in the displayed description of the product. The two labels over the boxed products on this sheet differ. A separate enclosed example concerns two gerbes with band an abelian sheaf \(H\).

**U04 · Scans 591–593.** The divider introduces an appendix to XVIII. The text forms \(k_J=\prod_{\xi\in J}k_\xi\), restricts along inclusions of subsets, and takes the filtered direct limit \(k_\Phi\) over a proper filter. A finer filter \(\Psi\) induces a map \(k_\Phi\to k_\Psi\). Grothendieck observes that the construction uses small products and small filtered direct limits.

**U05 · Scans 593–595.** Proposition 1 characterizes the filters for which \(k_\Phi\) is a division ring. For a decomposition \(E=A\amalg B\), the characteristic functions have sum one and product zero. For the converse, he represents a nonzero class by \(g\) and constructs its inverse on a member of the ultrafilter. He then considers equal factors and the extension of the common field. The discussion of general products resumes with an express restriction to commutative factors.

**U06 · Scans 595–598.** Proposition 2 identifies the ultrafilters on \(E\) with the maximal ideals of \(k_E\), by \(\Phi\mapsto\mathfrak m_\Phi=\ker(k_E\to k_\Phi)\). Corollary 1 recovers membership from characteristic functions. For surjectivity he starts with a maximal ideal, applies its quotient map \(\chi\), and uses the subsets for which \(\chi(f_A)=1\). The cancellations on 597 show the change from a nonvanishing test to equality with one.

**U07 · Scan 598.** The lemma says that the \(f_A\) for \(A\notin\Phi\) generate \(\mathfrak m_\Phi\). The inserted refinement gives \(f=ff_A\) for an individual \(f\) in the kernel. This completes the comparison of ideals. Corollary 2 says that every integral-domain quotient of the product of fields is a field.

**U08 · Scans 598–599.** For an arbitrary filter \(\Phi\), Corollary 3 identifies \(\operatorname{Max}(k_\Phi)\) with the ultrafilters finer than \(\Phi\). The diagram on 599 gives the factorization through \(k_\Phi\). The second passage headed *Corollaire 3* on 599 is cancelled. It states a membership test using invertibility of the image of \(f_A\).

**U09 · Scans 600–601.** With a common nonzero ring \(k\), Proposition 3 asks when the diagonal map \(k\to k_\Phi\) is surjective. The decomposition \(f=c+g\) says that \(f\) is constant on a member of the filter. The fibres of \(f:E\to k\) turn this into a partition criterion. The *Scholie* formulates the criterion for partitions with at most \(\pi=\operatorname{card}k\) parts and calls such a filter a \(\pi\)-ultrafilter.

**U10 · Scans 601–602.** Grothendieck considers finite \(\pi\), principal ultrafilters, and the possible existence of nonprincipal examples for transfinite \(\pi\). He names the smallest universe containing an infinite set, considers an infinite field such as \(\mathbb Q\), and formulates a presumption involving a larger universe and a distinguished cardinal in it.

**U11 · Scan 602.** For an accessible category \(\mathcal M\) and an object \(a\), the last construction sends \(x\) to \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal M}(x,a)\). The text passes from \(f^o:\mathcal M^o\to\mathrm{Ens}\) to \(f:\mathcal M\to(\mathrm{Ens})^o\) and asserts accessibility. “Considérons la sous-catégorie str. pleine” is the last clause on the sheet. The object condition for this subcategory falls outside the supplied scans.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 588.** “Si … des str. de Quillen spéciales, aussi … ? et … ??” asks the question first for the boxed product and then for the underlined Hom.
- **Scan 589, item 2.** “si \(\pi\) est utile pour \(\mathcal M\) et pour \(\mathcal N\), l'est-il pour \(\mathcal M\boxtimes\mathcal N\) ? Oui !” A circled question remains over the \(\mathrm{Ind}_\pi\) comparison. A further question belongs to the cancelled Hom formula.
- **Scan 589, item 3.** “Y-a-t-il une pour … ? J'en doute…” concerns an interpretation for the underlined Hom. The pronoun is uncertain.
- **Scans 600–601.** “On se pose la question quand … c'est bijectif” is answered by Proposition 3 and the fibre description on the next page.
- **Scan 601.** “Si \(\pi\) est transfini, il n'est pas clair qu'il existe des \(\pi\)-ultrafiltres non triviaux.”
- **Scan 602.** “je présume que … est trivial” concerns \(\pi\)-ultrafilters on sets in \(\mathcal U\), with \(\pi\) the first cardinal of that universe belonging to no strictly smaller universe. This is the live occurrence of *je présume*.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01 · Scans 596–599 · High.** For nonzero factors, the image of \(f_A\) is one precisely when \(A\) contains a filter member. This explains why the marginal observation can recover an arbitrary filter, while the live zero-versus-nonzero test of Corollary 1 uses an ultrafilter.
- **R02 · Scans 600–601 · High.** For the principal filter at a point \(x\), the selected fibre of a function \(f\) is the fibre over \(f(x)\). This directly connects the partition criterion with the author's trivial examples.
- **R03 · Scan 602 · High.** The two limit arrows agree with the written variances. Maps from a colimit to \(a\) correspond to compatible families of maps to \(a\). Taking the opposite category explains the switch from inverse limits in sets to direct limits in \((\mathrm{Ens})^o\).
