# Mathematical notes on cote 23, scans 83–99

## What the pages do

**Scans 83–84 · Codimension after cutting by one equation.** A closed part $Y$ lies in the zero locus $V(x)$ of a section of an invertible module. The lemma compares its codimension there with its codimension in $X$. It singles out the components where the infimum is attained. The proof uses the local dimensions at their generic points. Two corollaries and a marginal version turn to height and partial systems of parameters in local rings.

**Scan 85 · A cancelled axiom for a graded category.** The sheet introduces graded Hom groups, a degree-zero category, objects denoted $X(1)$ and $X(-1)$, and arrows of degrees $1$ and $-1$. Two braces record how the grading on Hom is shifted. An older formulation tries to specify a representing object by a universal property.

**Scan 87 · Depth under restriction of scalars.** For a finite algebra $B$ over $A$, the lemma compares $(S_k)$ for a finite $B$-module considered over the two rings. The converse is stated with an equality of local dimensions over the same point. The proof sets a depth infimum beside a dimension supremum. Several abandoned versions are still visible.

**Scans 88–89 · Finiteness of normalisation.** The statement starts on scan 89. Its criterion for an integral quotient of a regular ring uses the nonregular points of dimension one and the normalisations of their local rings. The construction passes from a finite intermediate ring to a sheaf on an open set and extends it by $i_*i^*$. Scan 88 carries the continuation about coherence, $(S_2)$ and $(R_1)$. Its foot contains a crossed-out version of the restriction-of-scalars argument.

**Scan 90 · Two layers of working notes.** The pen layer tests a depth implication, draws the rings and modules involved, and compares a quotient $B/fB$ with a quotient of a free module. The transverse English pencil discusses complete intersections, divisor and Picard questions, and restriction maps on fundamental and Picard groups. It places different depth bounds next to injectivity and bijectivity. Some hypotheses in the pencil are obscured.

**Scan 91 · Title.** “Fibres des morphismes plats de présentation finie” stands by itself on a cover.

**Scans 92–94 · A jumping codimension.** Two local schemes $X'$ and $X''$, of different dimensions, are joined along a one-dimensional $D$. Compatible local functions define a section on their union. The notes compare the non-Cohen–Macaulay locus away from the section with its intersection with the section. They construct a candidate $X'$ at the vertex of a cone and use local affine space for $X''$.

**Scans 96–97 · A criterion for graded algebras.** For $A\to B\to C$, the degreewise lifts $u^j$ enter maps $v^n$ obtained by multiplication in $B$. The conditions concern their surjectivity and bijectivity, the kernel of $B\to C$, and flatness or projectivity of the graded pieces. Fibrewise ranks give a coefficient-by-coefficient comparison of three power series.

**Scan 98 · Equivalent conditions and a question.** The corollary combines bijectivity, finite projectivity and equality of the rank series. The page names the conditions “bonnes” and “très bonnes”. A separate question asks about flatness of the rings over one another.

**Scan 99 · Polynomial case.** When $C$ is a polynomial ring on homogeneous generators, the corollary identifies $B$ with a polynomial ring over $A$ and describes the two maps. Its closing bracket relates an identification to an algebra section $C\to B$ and to a linear lift involving $C^+/C^{+2}$.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Model, high confidence for the example under the stated interpretation.** Question Q3(i) fails if condition (a) is read using the section identity on scan 99. Take $K=\mathbf F_2$, $A=B=K[e]/(e^2)$ with $e$ of degree one, $C=K$, $\psi$ the identity, and $\varphi$ the augmentation. The maps $v^n$ are bijective and $A$ is free over $K$. After tensoring the inclusion $(e)\hookrightarrow B$ with $C$, the resulting map has rank zero between one-dimensional spaces. This checks the nonflatness of $C$ over $B$. The literal left-inverse wording on scan 96 would impose a stronger hypothesis.

**M2 · Model, medium confidence.** The series comparison on scan 97 and the strict comparison of codimensions on scan 93 use visibly different signs. On 97 the author glosses the pair of leftward strokes as “majoration coefficient par coefficient”. That gloss gives the comparison its meaning on this sheet.

**M3 · Model, low confidence.** In scan 90 the two sets of notes share the physical page. Their writing directions, ink densities and subject matter differ. I cannot identify the pencil hand or establish a mathematical dependence of the French argument on the English one.
