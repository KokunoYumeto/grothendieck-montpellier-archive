# Mathematical notes · Cote 82, scans 17–31

## What the pages do

**Checking compatibility, scan 17.** Grothendieck rewrites (56) in the more precise form (56 bis), pairs with $x'$, and reduces the calculation to the interchange of two wedge factors. His « ce qui est bien vrai, hurrah ! » closes that calculation. He then returns to (52) and writes it as the vanishing of a sum of two pairings.

**The action on the dual, scans 18–19.** The underlined « Construction » starts with the adjoint action of $E'$ on itself. Formula (58) defines the action on $E$ by negative transpose. Formulas (59) and (59 bis) express the condition using the pairing. The opening of scan 19 recognizes a preceding computation as (58). Two sideways notes record a trace-zero condition (60) and a diagonal specialization (52 bis) involving $\varphi$.

**A basis calculation, scans 19–20.** Two ordered bases give opposite displayed signs for the volume elements (61) and (64). Formulas (65) and (66) give $f$ and $f'$ on the basis vectors. The matrix entries in (67) lead to the coordinate pairing (68) and its diagonal $q'(\eta)=h^2+4xy$ in (69). The observation about $h^2$ in characteristic two follows immediately below.

**The bracket and its action, scan 21.** Formula (70) identifies the bracket on $E$ in the tilded basis with that of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$. The text relates $f$ to the isogeny (71) with kernel $\mu_2$. The three rows of (72) give the images of the basis vectors under the action of $X,Y,H$. The author writes « sic » beside the factor $1/2$ in the last row and refers back to his « formule oubliée » (56 bis).

**Two cases for the base, scans 22–23.** When 2 is invertible the text identifies $E$ with $E'$ and $f'$ with $2\operatorname{id}_E$. When 2 vanishes, $f$ kills the line generated by $\widetilde H$ and $f'$ kills the span of $X,Y$. The page identifies each kernel with the other map's image. The slanted note distinguishes the alternating $\varphi$ from $\varphi'$. The remark about forms in characteristic two continues onto the first three lines of scan 23.

**The Casimir forms, scans 23–25.** The first attempt at (73)–(75) is cancelled. The live (73) defines two bilinear forms by traces of products. The live (74) identifies them as $2\varphi'$ and $-2\Delta\varphi$. Scan 24 compares scaling weights, invokes proportionality, and evaluates both sides on $H,H$ and $\widetilde H,\widetilde H$. Scan 25 specializes to the diagonal. With $\Delta=-2$, (76) gives $8q(x)$ on $E$; (77) gives $2q'(x')$ on $E'$.

**Rescaling the data, scan 26.** Formula (78) assigns the weights $\lambda\mu$ and $\lambda\mu^2$ to the two brackets. Equations (79) and (81) ask when two data sets give the same bracket. For invertible $f$ and fixed $\omega$, (80) identifies a fibre of $f\mapsto f'$ up to a scalar $\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon^2=1$. There is a short determinant argument in square brackets. One of its primes remains uncertain.

**Affine planes in characteristic two, scans 27–28.** The notes describe the two kinds of Lie algebra by extensions of modules of ranks one and two. The paired extension diagram defines what it means for the algebras to be « accouplées ». A duality and a symplectic structure on $V$ give the description by « plans affines symplectiques ». The page assigns dimensions 5 and 6 to the two automorphism groups being discussed. Two composites then recover $f$ and $f'$. An older explanation of the action below them is crossed out.

**The group covering and the induced Lie maps, scans 29–30.** Starting with a smooth quadratic form, Grothendieck chooses $\omega$ satisfying (82), writes the oriented automorphism group in (83), and introduces its simply connected covering. The displayed kernel is $\mu_2$. The quotient action and the adjoint actions appear in (87)–(89). Passing to Lie algebras gives a homomorphism and an action by derivations in (90)–(92).

**The canonical identifications, scans 30–31.** Formula (93) names the two module isomorphisms $i$ and $j$. The next page lists the transported brackets, the map $f$ and the action. It constructs $j:E'\to\mathfrak g$ from endomorphisms preserving $q$ and $\omega$ infinitesimally. The sheet ends after reducing the isomorphism claim to injectivity on fibres.

## Remarks of the model

- **C20 · High.** Substituting the same coordinate triple twice in (68) gives $h^2+4xy$. Reducing that polynomial modulo 2 gives $h^2$, the value in the next paragraph. This checks the diagonal computation without relying on the disputed prime in the name of the form.
- **C22 · High.** In the order $X,H,Y$, the diagonal entries of (65) and (66) are $(1,2,1)$ and $(2,1,2)$. Their product has all three entries equal to 2. Modulo 2 they leave complementary coordinate summands, as described on scan 22.
- **C24W · High.** Expanding the two weight squares on scan 24 gives $\mu^2\lambda^2$ and $\mu^4\lambda^2$. This checks the written powers on that line.
- **C24 · High.** The diagonal actions read from (70) and (72) have squared traces 8 and 2. With the values of $\varphi,\varphi'$ and $\Delta$ on scan 24, the two right sides of (74) give the same numbers.
- **C25 · High.** Substitution of $\Delta=-2$ into $-4\Delta q(x)$ gives the coefficient 8 of (76).
- **R31 · Low, reading question.** At scan 31, lines 6–8, the frozen reading is $\mathfrak s=\operatorname{Lie}(G)$ with $G'$ as an alternative at the clipped edge. The diagram just above starts with $\mathfrak g$. The shape before the equals resembles the writer's $\mathfrak s$. The local context favours the alternative $\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}(G)$, while (85) also allows a lost tilde to be considered. This is the reading difficulty behind S31a.
