# Mathematical notes on cote 161-6, scans 018–034

## What the pages do

**018 — An angular condition for equal edges.** A pentagonal family in a plane supplies directions about an axis \(u\). The sheet compares \(x(\alpha)\) and \(y(\alpha)\) to \(u\) and to one another. Equating the three distances gives the boxed \(\cos(\alpha/2)=1/(2\sin(\pi/5))\). The final NB identifies \(2\sin(\pi/5)\) with a side of the regular unit pentagon.

**019 — An order-60 group acting on five objects.** An order-three automorphism commutes with the action on a 15-element set \(I\). The quotient \(J\) has five elements. The prose obtains a stabilizer of order 12 and, assuming faithful action on \(J\), identifies \(G\) with \(A_5\). The decagon, its primed vertices and the separate list \(P,Q,R\) give two different ways the sheet records permutations.

**020 — Cosets and exploratory trigonometry.** The calculation with \(b'=bh\) leads to expressions involving \(a^{-1}Ha\). Beside it are a chain of normalizers, permutation rows written at a right angle, and independent trigonometric trials including \(\alpha=60\,?\). Their placement does not supply a single continuous argument.

**022 — Projective icosahedra and a form.** Three assertions concern local existence, conjugacy under \(SO(Q)\), and isotropy. The computation uses a parameter satisfying \(r^2+r-1=0\). It obtains \(\cos2\alpha=-3/5\), then boxes \(B(x,y)^2=\tfrac45 Q(x)Q(y)\) and, with \(B'=\tfrac12B\), \(B'(x,y)^2=\tfrac15 Q(x)Q(y)\). The letter read as \(r\) also resembles \(\gamma\).

**023 — A cancelled topos construction.** For a topos \(E\) and a small category \(C\), the sheet describes \(\pi(C,E)\) using functors out of \(C^\circ\). It writes formulas for the induced morphism, builds comparison maps to \(\widehat C\) and \(E\), and asks whether the map to their product in the category of topoi is an equivalence. It considers sums, a connected groupoid and \(E=\widehat D\). A single cross runs through this whole unit. The subscript on the product in the rectangular diagram is written “Top”.

**025 — The invariant at a vertex.** An embedding in an oriented surface induces a cyclic order on the oriented edges issuing from each nonisolated vertex. These orders give \(\omega(\varphi)\). For fixed data \(\alpha\), Th 1 passes from embeddings of \(X\) to embeddings of a closed tubular neighbourhood \(U_\alpha\).

**026 — Reconstructing the ambient surface.** The proposition describes the components of \(U_\alpha-X\) by products with a circle. A surface containing that neighbourhood is obtained by attaching an oriented surface \(U'\) along the oppositely oriented boundary. The page allows disconnected complements and specifies which components may be assumed to meet the boundary.

**027 — Classifying the pieces to be attached.** A partition \(\pi\) groups the labelled boundary circles into the connected components of \(U'\). For each part, the sheet specifies the genus and the number of ends, removes disks and a finite set from a compact surface, and obtains a canonical embedding into \(S_{\alpha,\pi,g,\beta}\). Th 2 states the resulting classification up to isomorphism. Its margin asks for the genus after gluing.

**028–029 — Restriction and its stabilizer.** For a fixed surface and fixed data \(\rho\), restriction maps isotopy classes of surface isomorphisms onto the chosen fibre of graph embeddings. The action on the source is described as a torsor. The stabilizer \(H\) identifies the target with a quotient. The next sheet translates the question about \(H\) into one about induced maps on fundamental groups and singles out the case of a surjection from a connected graph.

**029–031 — A proposed criterion and a sequence.** The text considers closing boundary circles by disks and asks for a criterion on \(\alpha\) when the resulting genus is zero. For disconnected \(X\), it asks whether the necessary condition on every \(\pi_1(X_i)\) suffices. The short sheet 031 sets a quotient beside a sequence involving \(\pi_1\) and \(\pi_0\). Its brace is marked « ?? ».

**033–034 — A transitive symmetry group of a graph.** The first sheet tries several descriptions using vertices, oriented edges, an involution and endpoint data. It separates isolated vertices and circular edges, then uses cosets and stabilizers. The following sheet treats noncircular edges, asks for the action to be faithful, and expresses injectivity of the two-endpoint map in terms of an intersection of subgroups.

## Slips

**S18, scan 018, L36.** The margin has \(4\pi/5<2\pi/3\). The corrected comparison is \(4\pi/5>2\pi/3\), since the left side exceeds the right by \(2\pi/15\). **High confidence.**

**S23, scan 023, L46.** In the explanation following \(\gamma_2^*\), the subscript of the next \(\gamma\) was read first as 1, with 2 an alternative. A 2 would agree with the component being described. **Low confidence**, from a doubtful numeral on a cancelled sheet.

**S29, scan 029, L38, continuing on 030.** The exponent on \(S\) is 1 twice. \(S^2\) is a possible intended ambient space for the first occurrence, since the associated closed surface on the continuation has genus zero. The following parenthesis is also meaningful as an assertion about circle homeomorphisms. **Medium confidence** for this interpretation of the first occurrence.

**S34, scan 034, L16–17.** The box identifies \(H'\) with \(G_a\) and \(H\) with \(G_s\). Interchanging these identifications would agree with 033 and with the source \(G/H\) in the later endpoint map. **Low confidence** for a convention slip, since the comparison on 033 lies in a cancelled block.

## Remarks of the model

**M22 — High confidence.** In the algebra on 022, \((1+2r)^2\) reduces to \(5\) using \(r^2=1-r\). Independently, \(2/5-1=-3/5\), so the next doubling gives \(-6/5\). These computations check two local steps and do not require choosing between the glyphs \(r\) and \(\gamma\).

**M26 — High confidence under the connected, untwisted oriented-neighbourhood interpretation on 025–026.** A bouquet of two loops can have one or three boundary components depending on its cyclic order. Label the four edge ends \(0,1,2,3\) in cyclic order. For opposite pairings the boundary walk has one cycle \((0,3,2,1)\). For adjacent pairings it has three, \((0,2),(1),(3)\). Both graphs have \(b_1=2\). This gives an explicit counterexample to the unrestricted \(b_1+1\) suggestion in Q26 and explains why the vertex orders matter to this count.

**M31 — Medium confidence.** The appearance of the group fixing \(X\), followed by groups and component sets of the quotient, suggests that the bracketed sequence is intended as a homotopy sequence. The missing heads on several strokes and the two question marks leave the status of that interpretation open.
