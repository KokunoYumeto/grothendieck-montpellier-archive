# Mathematical notes on cote 31, scans 87–88 and 90–102

## What the pages do

**M01 · A qualification about local rings, scan 87.** A three-line N.B. attributes a verification for “bons anneaux locaux henséliens de car. résiduelle nulle” to Artin. The claim being verified is on an earlier leaf.

**M02 · Detection by torsion subgroups, scan 88.** The cancelled typescript starts in the middle of a reduction to the diagonalisable case. It compares homomorphisms on the subgroups indexed by n, then compares two subgroups of multiplicative type. Their graphs enter the reduction from the first assertion to the second. A further corollary uses the closedness of a subgroup over a field. A remark concerning the residue characteristic and the prime divisors of the torsion of M breaks off.

**M03 · A hyperplane section, scan 90.** For a regular projective X, a regular hyperplane section Y, and U = X − Y, the two-row diagram relates cohomology with support on Y to the cohomology of Y with a twist. Vanishing on U gives the stated surjectivity and bijectivity ranges for the map to Hⁱ(X,F). The corollary identifies the top group with ℤ/Nℤ for connected X and N prime to the characteristic.

**M04 · Induction and duality, scan 91.** The proof of the corollary passes from dimension n to a hyperplane section of dimension n−1. Beneath the coefficient on Y the writer identifies the twisted tensor power of μ with the power indexed by n−1. Corollaire 2 turns to the restriction Hʲ(X,F) → Hʲ(Y,F). The bounds now read j ≤ n−1 for injectivity and j ≤ n−2 for bijectivity.

**M05 · Extending an étale cover, scans 92–94.** The title leaf introduces the specialization notes. Lemme 1 uses a local ring of dimension at least three, a regular element t, a normality hypothesis on a completion, and a finite normal extension B. A condition on B/tB off the origin is used to extend an étale cover across the exceptional set. The proof passes to the complete case, compares a formal cover with the trivial one, and invokes depth at closed points to make the comparison regular.

**M06 · Relative curves with a section, scans 95–96.** Lemme 2 concerns a smooth relative curve, a section and a normal Galois covering of order prime to the residue characteristics. The statement links the generic-fibre condition and a rational point over the section to étaleness along the section. Several alternatives for the hypotheses are struck out. The argument reduces to a codimension-one point of the base, notes that the section is locally a Cartier divisor, and passes to a discrete valuation ring.

**M07 · The two-parameter local calculation, scans 96–97.** Lemme 3 uses a regular local ring of dimension two with parameters x,y. Ramification is confined to V(x), and the cover is required to split at the indicated point of V(y). The conclusion is étaleness over A. The calculation invokes Abhyankar to write a cyclic group and a presentation A[T]/(Tⁿ−x), then reduces the presentation modulo y. The two labelled axes of the sketch correspond to the two divisors in the hypotheses.

**M08 · Local 1-asphericity, scans 97–98.** The theorem asserts local 1-asphericity for a smooth morphism and finite groups of order prime to the residue characteristics of Y. Its proof reduces to a strictly local noetherian base and relative dimension one, then stops at a line of dots.

**M09 · Three applications, scan 98.** The first places higher direct images beside smooth extension of the base. The verb after the direct image remains in doubt. The second displays an isomorphism on cohomology after an extension of separably closed fields for prime-to-characteristic torsion coefficients. The third proposes the calculation of the local cohomology sheaves for a smooth subscheme of the smooth X and calls it relative purity.

**M10 · Three local-constancy conditions, scan 99.** For $V=X-Y$ and the dual object $F'=\operatorname{Hom}(F,I_{X/S})$, the physical rows are labelled (ii), (i), (iii). They concern $\mathbb R^i h_!$ on the restriction of F′ to V, $\mathbb R^i h_*$ on F restricted to V, and $\mathbb R^i g_*$ on F′ restricted to Y. The author attributes one equivalence to the cohomology sequence of the displayed short exact sequence and the other to duality. A smoothness condition and a tentative extension to normal crossings follow the list.

**M11 · Divisors and essential valuations, scan 100.** Typed page VII–9 decomposes div(x) in the elements P of P(A), relates nonnegativity of all v_P(x) to membership in A, and states the noetherian corollary. Section 4 defines divisors, their classes and the essential valuations. A comparison of the divisorial ideals for P and 2P produces an element with v_P(x)=1. Proposition 6 is represented only by its opening line.

**M12 · A sheaf defined on the open, scan 101.** The N.B. asks for the higher direct images of a locally constant sheaf on V which need not extend to X. It writes a spectral sequence involving the direct images for the immersion and the map out of X. Local constancy of its terms is the stated sufficient condition. Wild ramification is identified as a difficulty, even in the smooth case mentioned in the interline.

**M13 · Intersections and a criterion for Krull rings, scan 102.** Typed page VII–7 restricts families of valuations to prove that a finite intersection of Krull subrings of a field is Krull. It gives the subfield-intersection corollary. Théorème 1 then characterizes Krull rings by complete integral closure and a maximal-element condition for nonempty families of integral divisorial ideals. The proof begins by assigning a supremum of values to an ideal and ends here in the opening clause of a numbered assertion.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01 · Model remark, high confidence, scans 90–91.** In the induction the substitution i=2n meets the bijectivity bound i≥n+2 for every n≥2. The difference between the two sides is n−2. This checks the numerical transition on scan 91.
- **R02 · Model remark, low confidence as a reading, scan 90.** The left-column shift would take the upper-right degree i+1 to i−1. This gives a reason for the second reading of the smudged lower exponent. It cannot distinguish the surviving ink strokes. See S02.
- **R03 · Model remark, high confidence, scan 102.** The restriction changes the domain of the valuation. Following the domains in the two consecutive sentences singles out w as the symbol the author introduced on the fraction field of S. See S05.
- **R04 · Model remark, high confidence about the document order, scans 99–102.** The question on scan 101 uses the open V, the sheaf and the direct-image problem from scan 99. Readers following that argument can join these two handwritten leaves. The intervening typewritten leaf and the last one carry the same document number and their own VII pagination.
