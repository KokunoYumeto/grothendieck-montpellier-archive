## Comparison across sheets

- Scans 86–89. The enlarged Γ has a single downstroke and top arm. I has two crossbars. J turns at its foot. The dotted index in the pointed case on 89 is a lower-case i. At the group to the right of the hooked arrow on 87, the curled S matches the terminal S on the first display of 86. Its exact alphabet remains open at the first occurrence on both pages.
- Scans 88–89 and 97–99. The small loop in the twisting parameter on 88 can be read as tau, delta or a cursive T. The plain t on 89 has its own crossbar. The horizontal cap and turning stem of the boxed period on 99 identify a separate tau on the elliptic pages. The different uses do not settle the hand on 88.
- Scans 91, 92 and 100. The quotients of P and Q have a baseline 2 followed by an upper-case letter. The first denominator on 100 rises close to the height of k, and the alternative was kept there. The formula for M′ lower down on that sheet provides an independent specimen of 2k. The displayed module M′ and its prime on 91 and 92 were checked against that letter.
- Scans 93–101. The lattice pi has a broad top with two descending strokes. The raised sign under the generators on 97 partly joins the neighbouring mark and could be capital Gamma there. It is distinct from the lower-case Galois gamma on 101. The names E and E′ share the same flat upper bar; the extra tick of E′ was compared with the lower a and its prime in the reciprocal parameters. The final torsor sign on 101 is an exceptional raised mark.
- Scans 97–101. The horizontal s-shaped hook of the involution sigma differs from the tail on j in the modular invariant, and from the rounded low u and v that label the lattice drawings. The 0, 1, plus and minus subscripts in these drawings were read against the adjoining coordinate identities. On 99 the i on the baseline of both boxed periods is dotted; the second underbrace has a less certain endpoint.
