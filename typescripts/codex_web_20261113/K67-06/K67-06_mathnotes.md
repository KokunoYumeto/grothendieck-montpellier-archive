# Mathematical notes on K67-06

## What the pages do

**Octahedra and subgroups, scans 86–87 [B86].** A four-group $V$ acting on a projective line has a six-point ramification set. The proposition on 86 puts $V$, its normalizer of type $\mathfrak S_4$, and an octahedral six-point configuration in correspondence. Each nonidentity element of $V$ fixes a pair. On 87 the three pairs give the antipodal involution. The author distinguishes an ordinary four-point orbit $I$ from the torsor $I(V,X)$ constructed from the octahedron.

**Reconstruction, scans 88–89.** The difference between the two torsors specifies a twist of $X(I)$. A point $\zeta$ on the quotient determines the fibre to be identified with $I$. The theorem packages this as an equivalence of groupoids. The end of 89 treats a distinguished point $i_0$ and relates an admissible isomorphism of projective lines to an isomorphism of $V$-torsors.

**Modules with involution, scans 91–92 [B91].** With multiplication by 2 injective, the positive and negative eigenspaces $P,Q$ intersect trivially. The position of $2M$ between $2P\oplus2Q$ and $P\oplus Q$ records how $P$ and $Q$ fit together integrally. Modulo 2 this gives a submodule meeting each coordinate summand trivially. Scan 91 formulates an equivalence and a rank-two corollary. Scan 92 works through the inclusions and the operators $1+\sigma$ and $1-\sigma$ on printer paper.

**Two real families, scan 93 [B93].** The data are a rank-two lattice, its oriented conformal structure, and an orientation-reversing involution preserving that structure. For the two integral types of involution the author normalizes the positive quadratic form to $x^2+ay^2$ in eigenvector coordinates, with $a>0$. He calls the families $E_a,E'_a$. He then restricts the orientation-preserving automorphisms commuting with the involution and identifies the universal nonidentity one as central inversion.

**Real structures on one complex curve, scans 94–95 [B94].** A real structure is described by a reflection preserving the lattice. The paper distinguishes two actions on the reflections. Multiplying on the left makes them a torsor for the rotations. Conjugating by the rotations produces the orbits relevant to isomorphism. The group is described as dihedral, with rotation subgroup of order 2, 4 or 6. Scan 96 contains ten uncaptioned drawings on a printer sheet. There is no written identification of them with particular cases.

**The generic, square and hexagonal cases, scans 97–98 [B97].** Replacing $\sigma$ by $-\sigma$ interchanges the two eigenlines and takes $a$ to $1/a$. In the generic case the paired structures belong to the same family. For the square the two classes are $E_1$ and $E'_1$. For the hexagon the text puts both classes in the primed family and gives the parameters as $\sqrt3$ and its reciprocal. The quadratic-form issue is recorded below as H98.

**Periods and real points, scans 99 and 101.** In the split case a period is $\tau=i\sqrt a$. In the other case $v=(e_0+e_1)/2$ gives $\tau=(1+i\sqrt a)/2$. Fixed points modulo the lattice occupy two circles in the first case and one in the second. Scan 100 intervenes between the two real-point calculations.

**Integral matrices, scan 100 [B100].** For a principal ring with the stated hypotheses at 2, the gluing subspace is zero or a line different from either coordinate line. Reduction of units is part of the discussion of the line case. A basis $(2,0),(1,1)$ gives the nonsplit matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The margin describes both integral lattices in common real eigenvector coordinates.

**Torsors, scan 101 [B101].** The period-lattice exact sequence identifies the cohomology group classifying real torsors with $\pi_+/(1+\sigma)\pi$. The author obtains one nonzero class of order two for $E_a$ and none for $E'_a$. He writes $E_a^!$ for the nontrivial torsors.

## Statements left without proof

- **86.** « Ceci résulte de la théorie combinatoire » … « ou du cube c'est kif kif. » The identification giving the $V$-torsor structure on $I(V)$ appeals to octahedral or cubical combinatorics.
- **86.** « il suffit qu'il … soit stable par extraction des racines carrées » weakens the algebraically closed field hypothesis in the proposition. The page gives no separate argument for that weakening.
- **87.** « On sait que la donnée d'un octaèdre » … « revient à celle d'un système $(I,\omega)$ ». The equivalence using codiagonals and orientations is recalled here.
- **88.** Beside $X(T\wedge T')=X(T)\wedge_V T'$ the instruction is « à expliciter ».
- **89.** « Ce groupoïde est équivalent à celui des couples » is the theorem. The preceding twist construction supplies the objects. The text describes the pointed case without giving a general check on every morphism.
- **91.** « Le foncteur précédent … est une équivalence ». The text describes reconstruction on objects. It gives no separate verification on morphisms here.
- **93.** « elles sont d'ailleurs isogènes... » follows the two possibilities for $(\pi,\sigma)$. No isogeny is displayed at that point.
- **94.** « celles dont l'invariant modulaire est réel ». The passage from being isomorphic to the conjugate curve to the condition on the invariant is stated directly.
- **94–95.** « $G^+$ est cyclique d'ordre » … « $n=2,4$ ou $6$ ». These possible orders are used in the case analysis. Their exhaustion is stated here.
- **101.** « Les torseurs non triviaux fournissent les surfaces conformes … bord non orientables de genre $1$ ». The word « sans » before « bord » is struck. The sheet computes torsor classes and gives no geometric construction of the surfaces.
- **101.** « La suite exacte de $\sigma$-modules … fournit » introduces the isomorphisms with $H^2(\gamma,\pi)$ and with invariants modulo the norm. Their derivation is omitted. R101 below checks the resulting two lattice quotients.

## Questions and conjectures

- **94.** « Peut-il y en avoir plusieurs ? Si oui, les structures réelles correspondantes sont elles isom » asks both about multiplicity and about conjugacy.
- **95 [P95].** « Je présume » introduces the proposal that one orbit has type $E_a$ and the other $E'_a$ with the same $a$. The author's classification on 97–98 retains this pairing in the square case and gives reciprocal parameters in the same family outside it.
- **95 and 97.** « De plus il faudrait. » continues with « expliciter le lien du paramètre $a$ avec le paramètre $\tau$, ou mieux, avec l'invariant modulaire $j$ ». The coefficient before « $\log a$ ? » remains open. The boxed periods on 99 answer the part concerning $\tau$.
- **101.** « Pour bien faire, il faudrait aussi classifier les courbes elliptiques réelles inhomogènes ». The calculation below the question gives the answer for the two families.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark R95, high confidence.** The rectangular lattice with $Q=x^2+4y^2$ gives a concrete test of P95. Its only two lattice reflections are the diagonal sign changes. Both integral involutions split, and their parameters are $4$ and $1/4$, as in the later classification.
- **Model remark R101, high confidence.** The last quotient can be read from the two integral matrices alone. The norm $1+\sigma$ has image of index 2 in the invariant line for the split matrix. The nonsplit matrix has norm with first row $(2,1)$, so its image is the whole invariant line. This checks the numerical groups on 101.
- **Model remark R99–101, high confidence.** In the basis $u,v$ the two displacements are $(0,-2y)$ and $(y,-2y)$. Modulo the lattice the allowed $y$ are respectively $0,1/2$ and $0$. This recovers the two component counts directly.
- **Model reading question, 88, definition of the twisting torsor.** The frozen first occurrence was $\tau$, with $\delta$ and a cursive $T$ as alternatives. The final text has $\delta$ there and $\tau$ in the following two expressions. The first lower loop closes. The later stems are less distinct. A repeated $\delta$ is possible. Confidence low.
- **Model reading question, 92, lower addition for $2x$.** The frozen and final reading is $2x=(x+\sigma x)+(m-\sigma x)$. An $x$ in place of $m$ would agree with the addition on 91. The first sign in the last parenthesis is rounded, unlike the adjacent terminal $x$, and lies against the printer texture. Confidence low.
