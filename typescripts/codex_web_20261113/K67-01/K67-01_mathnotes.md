# Mathematical notes on cote 67, scans 2–14 and 16–18

## What the pages do

**Cutting and gluing, scan 3.** Grothendieck cuts a conformal surface along a compact rectifiable 1-complex $K$. A compact part of the boundary of the cut surface is distinguished. In the other direction an “admissible” equivalence relation on the boundary supplies the gluing data.

**Changing the collars, scans 3–4.** Shrinking removes annular collars at the distinguished boundary. A rectifiable identification with the old boundary transports the gluing relation. Enlarging adds a collar. In the mixed operation one chooses a circle $\Gamma_i$ in each tubular component $T_i$. For an isolated real-analytic loop the description is particularly concrete. Remove an annulus and join the two edges with a cylinder. The slanting marginal note records both orders of composing shrinking and enlarging.

**A finite moduli problem, scans 5 and 7.** A reference triple $S_0\subset K_0\subset X_0$ and an isotopy class of topological identifications rigidify $S\subset K\subset X$. He tries restrictions on the edges and on the boundary identifications. Geodesic parametrizations, equidistance of the vertices and the exceptional disc pieces all enter. Scan 7 resumes the sentence left at “Il” on scan 5 with a proposal for piecewise homographic maps.

**The intervening leaf, scan 6.** A diagram relates $(\widetilde K,\widetilde L)$ to $(K,L)$. The complements are identified, $\widetilde L$ is a preimage, and a colimit over $x\in K\setminus L$ describes $\widetilde K$. Two groups of drawings accompany the formulas. A slanted calculation compares the two coordinate derivatives with a square root and then compares the corresponding integrals.

**Counting parameters, scan 7.** The sum for the real parameter count $N$ runs over complementary faces that are not discs. An Euler-characteristic calculation uses the genera $g(f)$, the numbers $\nu(f)$ and the connectivity $\lambda$ of $K$. The sheet compares $N$ with $3g-3$ for $g\ge 2$ and with $1$ for $g=1$. In the later live formula $\mu$ counts disc faces. An earlier definition of $\mu$ is crossed out.

**Elliptic data, scans 9, 11 and 13.** The three lists successively compare a closed geodesic on an elliptic curve, a compact real torus, primitive fundamental-group data and a discrete cyclic subgroup of a complex multiplicative group. Scan 9 leaves items 5 and 6 empty. Scan 11 brings in a line $E$, its punctured line $E^*$ and a multiplier $b$ with $0<|b|<1$. Scan 13 uses a projective line with an inverse pair of automorphisms, then a form of a group whose handwritten name is uncertain. Its last chain relates orientation, a generator of $Z$, fixed points and Borels. The chain continues up the right edge.

**The reciprocal pair, scan 14.** On an envelope he writes $\alpha=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}$, compares $\lambda\ne\pm1$ with $\alpha\ne\pm2$, and writes the interval $[-2,2]$. A separate calculation uses $\bar\lambda=\lambda^{-1}$ to reach a real sum. The sign beside $\alpha^2-4$ is difficult to distinguish.

**Letter to Lipman Bers, scans 16–18.** The letter of 15 April 1984 compares a marked point on a compact conformal surface with a boundary circle whose hyperbolic length tends to zero. Allowing both gives a proposed larger space with length coordinates $\ell_i\ge0$. Grothendieck asks for a retraction to the pointed-curve space that respects the modular group. His surgery fills a boundary circle with its cone. The filled surface carries marked points *and* disjoint discs. He formulates admissibility by comparing two circle actions on each non-degenerate boundary, then looks for a radius in the tangent line at the marked point. The supplied letter ends mid-discussion of the torsor of possible discs.

Scan 2 is a cover. Scan 8 is a property list. Scan 10 has drawings on an addressed envelope and scan 12 has an address.
