# Hand log

## Scan 2

The pen makes a single shallow wave for several consecutive letters. The break at the paper tear affects “surfaces” specifically. The two final 7s have crossbars and rounded tops.

## Scan 3

In “conforme” the f has a deep loop. The beginning of “complexe” may be reduced to a horizontal line. This happens several times in the numbered heads and legends. The curls over X are separate from both the prime on the boundary sign and the star. The long rising strokes at the foot pass behind the collars and some words. They belong to the drawing.

## Scan 4

The angular capital in the circular-section label recurs in the prose with the same right angle. A final long t-stroke is particularly evident in the adjective after “tubulaire”. The diagonal marginal lines change direction relative to the main text. The three horizontal rows there must be read along their own baselines.

## Scan 5

An upright rising from the crowded baseline in “standart” has a distinct cross stroke. The small adjacent circle after X, K and S in the fixed situation is consistently a subscript zero. “cell.” in the second paragraph has two looped l strokes and a small detached stop.

## Scan 6

The turned sheet uses broad black strokes for the K–L formulas and finer slanted strokes for the inequalities. K is often thick along the leg without a free parallel stroke. The tilde on L is sometimes almost angular. These particular forms are confined to this sheet in the opening sequence.

## Scan 7

The v-shaped ν recurs inside the sums. The lower-case g has a loop followed by a long descender. The thin words of the cancelled definition can still be distinguished between the broad waves of cancellation.

## Scan 8

The typewriter gives the digit 1 serifs at both ends. The headings and reference numbers use no mathematical alphabet. Two long pen strokes cross the list in the same direction. The little upright archive number is at the bottom when the scan is held as supplied.

## Scan 9

Six small outlines enclose the list numbers, with 3 and 6 heavily traced. The oval and descender of γ differ from the two perpendicular strokes of Γ. The vertical stroke inside C is quite distinct from the left boundary.

## Scan 10

The addressee's name is written in a rounded upright hand with a large G and deep loop. The address hand is distinguishable from the compressed running notes. The small marks on the ink outlines are local retracings and short ticks.

## Scan 11

The list numbers sit inside irregular freehand loops. A long horizontal line begins items 2 and 3 as a repetition mark. Thickening is frequent at the vertex of Δ and across the diagonal of Z. Independent parallel strokes distinguish the double-struck letters. The compressed phrase after the last equality is squeezed against the lower edge.

## Scan 12

The address hand forms a long bottom loop in J and a detached upper curve in P. Its last long downstroke has a small leftward foot and no distinct dot, unlike the i of Pierre.

## Scan 13

The carets here are mostly long bent leaders rising from the left of the word they qualify. The ink of the Japanese postal address is thick and regularly spaced. Where the notes enter a postal letter, a short stroke can disappear. The underlining below the cancelled discret is broken.

## Scan 14

The fine calculations run upside down relative to the postal address. The macron-like accent in the destination is comparable to the long horizontal marks in the sender's address. The pencil numeral is in a third, lighter stroke.

## Scan 16

This typescript mixes typewritten placeholders with fine pen tildes, corrections and Greek letters. In particular the operator X, the Roman type for the field names, and the uppercase initials of names do not have a second independent downstroke. A pen loop converts the typewritten 1 in the length to ℓ. The rightmost one or two letters occasionally occupy the cut edge.

## Scan 17

In this part of the letter F floats above the line, with a descending leader that points to the interval immediately after fiber. It is not an index of the line above. Thin tildes float over the typewritten capitals, and the lower pen ν is sometimes scarcely larger than the comma preceding it. The inserted ond was typed above an incomplete word.

## Scan 18

The letter's pen Δ shows an overshoot at the apex. The index on the union bends left below the line, unlike the many straight lower i characters in the two indexed families. The line across systems in both occurrences comes from the insertion leader. It leaves the printed word in the reading.


## Cross-page check

The lower-case i in scan 3 and in the letter has a short stem and a dot. The second width on scan 3 and the union index on scan 18 have descending j forms. On the elliptic leaves the straight angle Γ in group and torsor notation differs from a curve with a returning stroke, γ. The alternation is visible on the same sheet in scan 9. The blackboard letters on scans 9–13 have pen strokes alongside the main stem. The C and R on scans 16–17 have a horizontal line below the baseline. The M and MB in the letter bear a separate pen wave. Their typewriter capitals have no separate inner pen stroke. These habits do not determine the isolated group initial in item 3 of scan 9, the heavily corrected capital in item 6 of scans 11 and 13, or the pencil surname on scan 12. Each stays individually marked.

The digits under f, the strokes of ν and the fork of λ on scan 7 were compared with the later elliptic leaves. The exponent −1 is raised high after λ on scans 13–14. The longer oblique that returns from the correction below the baseline on scan 13 belongs to its insertion. The two quotient signs and the rotated equality in scan 9 were checked apart from the long crossed passages in scans 11 and 13.
