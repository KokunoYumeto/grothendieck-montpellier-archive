# Mathematical notes on scans 80–94

## What the pages do

**80, hypotheses on an immersion.** In a topos \(X\) a sheaf \(\Phi\) receives an immersion from \(\mathbb Z\). The notes impose a flatness condition, then require flasqueness of positive exterior and divided powers to persist under tensoring with any abelian sheaf. They pass to mixed powers, first of \(\Phi\), then with the cokernel \(\Psi\) in the exterior factor.

**80, the complex and extension of scalars.** The graded terms of \(K_u\) are formed from \(\Gamma^*\Phi\) and \(\Lambda^*\Psi\). The differential on generators uses the canonical map \(\Phi\to\Psi\). The marked generator \(T\) maps to \(1\) in the kernel. The page calls the resulting complex a resolution of \(\mathbb Z\{T\}\), then gives the corresponding statement and flasqueness condition over a sheaf of rings \(k\).

**81–82, gradings and comparison.** The exterior degree of \(\Gamma^i\otimes\Lambda^j\) is \(j\), and its total degree is \(i+j\). The theorem takes global sections and compares their cohomology with that of \(k\{T\}\). It gives an isomorphism in the bidegrees \(j\leq i\) and states vanishing of the source for \(j>i\). The corollary uses a direct image under a morphism of topoi. Its continuation on 82 describes the degree restriction and suggests expressing it by a truncation.

**82, functoriality.** A triangle over \(\mathbb Z\), with a map \(\lambda\colon\Phi\to\Phi'\) satisfying \(\lambda u=u'\), gives maps of the complexes with and without scalar extension. For another such map \(\mu\), the note puts \(\mu=\lambda+\Theta\) and obtains \(\Theta u=0\).

**83, elementary divided-power De Rham algebras.** The page lists properties (a)–(g) of a bigraded differential algebra. It identifies the generators \(\Phi,\Psi\), requires an epimorphism \(d\colon\Phi\to\Psi\), specifies a free algebra with divided and exterior powers, and imposes flatness. For \(L=\ker d\) it displays \(\Gamma^*L\) in exterior degree zero and zero in positive exterior degree. A locally free rank-one condition on \(L\) is then considered, possibly with a trivialization.

**84, conference programme.** The first announcement for a November 1975 meeting on quadratic forms lists seven speaker entries, one without a title. The announced topics include sesquilinear forms, Hermitian class groups, additive categories, étale cohomology, Witt rings and algebraic Stiefel varieties.

**85 and 87, data and morphisms.** The structure is given by a ring \(k\), a flat \(k\)-module \(L\) and an immersion into \(\Phi\). A morphism includes a ring homomorphism and a semilinear map of modules inducing a map on \(L\). After choosing identifications of \(L,L'\) with the scalar rings, the condition is phrased in terms of marked sections. Scan 87 asks about two such morphisms with the same scalar map and the same effect on the marking.

**86 and 88, thesis notices.** The first announces Michel Follet’s thesis on the reactivity of the triazine cycle and the spectral analysis of bicyclic derivatives. The second announces Mouhiddine Dassouki’s study of a direct-current machine with rotating thyristors. Each form gives the defence arrangements and names a jury.

**89, universal monomorphism lemma.** The lemma starts with properties of the cokernel of \(u\) and a flasqueness condition after tensor products. The proposed construction tensors \(u\) with the identity of the input module. It is used to produce a monomorphism stable under tensoring, a flat cokernel under a flatness hypothesis, and the indicated flasque terms. The initial module letter is overwritten.

**89 and 91, comparison and transitivity.** The notes define a sheaf-level DR object and its global sections, then seek a transitive system of isomorphisms as \(u\) varies over a fixed ring. The diagrams use a coproduct and a common ambient module. A second comparison addresses independence from that module. Three copies of \(\Phi\), with distinct primes, enter the final transitivity diagram on 91. The next sentence mentions the analogous direct-image construction.

**91, a De Rham condition on the coefficient ring.** The condition is the existence of an immersion \(u\) with the earlier properties. The text passes from \(k\) to a \(k\)-algebra and asks especially for the integral sheaf on the topos. It names the topos with a hatted \(\Delta\) and the topos of a paracompact space as examples. It then asks separately about cosimplicial sets.

**91, adjoining a resolution to an algebra.** For a positively graded algebra \(A\) with \(A^{00}=k\), a tensor product over \(k\{T\}\) defines \(\overline A\). The augmentation induces \(A\to\overline A\). The claim that the map is a quasi-isomorphism is followed by the beginning of an explanation.

**92 and 90, the analytic-group typescript, chapter IX.** Typescript page 174 describes quotient maps and their fibrewise injectivity, shrinking the base to obtain a closed immersion. It introduces \(B=A^\natural/T\), an extension by \(T\), and the associated Lie algebra. Page 175 continues with the exponential exact sequence and a criterion for the abeloid property near a fibre. The underlined passage asserts uniqueness of an extension inducing a given one on the fibre.

**90, §13.4 of the typescript.** A new section recasts a preceding dictionary in terms of Deligne’s mixed Hodge structures. It forms a sheaf \(\mathscr E\) by tensoring with the structure sheaf. Local constancy of \(R\) yields a locally free sheaf of finite type, and the parenthesis warns that coherence can fail without this hypothesis. The last sentence is incomplete in this selection.

**94, special De Rham structures.** The sheet specifies the allowed bidegrees, a differential of bidegree \((0,1)\), divided powers, a marking \(T\), and conditions on cohomology and flatness. An oblique addition describes flasqueness for terms strictly below the diagonal. The three category names distinguish special, flasque and universally flasque structures. The last lines describe the derived categories obtained by inverting all the arrows.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1, scans 80–81, high confidence.** My arithmetic check concerns the grading alone. In the factor indices a differential step from \((i,j)\) to \((i-1,j+1)\) changes total, exterior and complementary degree by \(0,+1,-1\), respectively. This agrees with the three verbal statements on 81. The check event is recorded separately.
- **R2, scan 94, medium confidence.** My reading question concerns the range of the line \(H^{*i}(A^{**})=0\). No condition follows it on the sheet. The same brace identifies \(H^{*0}\) with \(k\{T\}\), and the analogous display on 83 explicitly separates \(i=0\) and \(i>0\). These lines favour understanding \(i>0\) on 94. The transcription preserves the unqualified line.
- **R3, scan 89, low confidence.** My reading question concerns the horizontal link from the heavily retraced object labelled \(T''\) or \(T\) to \(N''\). The fragment uses a hooked right arrow. The left hook is visible, but the right endpoint could be a plain stop. The earlier link from \(S\) to \(N\) has a clearer rightward head.
- **R4, scans 85 and 87, high confidence.** My editorial observation concerns a sentence split by a typed notice. The phrase « et la condition sur \(\lambda_\Phi\) » at the foot of 85 continues with « devient alors de transformer section marquée en section marquée » at the top of 87. The two lines locate the discussion of a marked section. Scan order is preserved in the fragment.
