# Mathematical notes on cote 60, scans 2–13

## What the pages do

**Scans 2–3.** The cover names the functional equation. The first leaf starts with an endomorphism $f$ of an $n$-dimensional vector space and writes $P_f(t)=\det(1-ft)$ both as a product over eigenvalues and as a sum of exterior-power traces. Its roots are the reciprocals of the nonzero eigenvalues.

**Scan 3.** The reciprocal $Z_f=1/P_f$ has symmetric-power traces as coefficients. Exact sequences give ordinary products of series. For tensor products the leaf introduces a second multiplication by the rule $(1-at)\ast(1-bt)=1-abt$. A small cancelled array of numbers stands in the margin. The text gives it no explicit role.

**Scan 5.** The braced identities apply the tensor rule to $P$ and $L$. The $\lambda$-operation selects products of eigenvalues with distinct indices. A generating series then relates exterior powers of $f$ to the $\sigma$-operations on $L_f$, and symmetric powers of $f$ to the $\lambda$-operations on $L_f$. In the middle of this computation an entire product of sums is crossed out.

**Scan 7.** Inverting the eigenvalues of an automorphism gives the reciprocal-argument identities for its contragredient. The two boxes have opposite exponents of $-t$ and reciprocal determinant factors. The last paragraph restricts the substitution to finite alternating products of polynomials with constant term 1 and invertible leading coefficient. It singles out the leading-coefficient condition when $k$ is a general ring.

**Scan 9.** The argument passes to a sheaf over the finite field. The page identifies $n$ with the rank, then introduces a motive of weight $\rho$, the sign $\varepsilon(E)$, and the determinant factor $p^{n\rho/2}$. It invokes Lefschetz for $\check E\simeq E(\rho)$ and writes out the resulting twist of the argument of $L_E$.

**Scan 11.** The first box finishes the finite-field calculation. Below the dividing rules a constructible sheaf on $X$ is introduced, with « propre » inserted. A cancelled cohomology calculation gives way to $\mathbb Rf_*(DE)=D(\mathbb Rf_*E)$. Equation (1) relates $L_{DE}(t)$ to $L_E(t^{-1})$ by $(-t)^{-\chi(E)}\delta(E)$. Two marginal labels distinguish the rank or Euler characteristic, « invariant géométrique », from the alternating determinant, « invariant arithmétique ».

**Scan 13.** The computation pairs $i$ with $2n-i$ and singles out $i=n$. Under the symmetry of the $b_i$, the exponent becomes $(n+\rho)\chi(E)/2$. Equation (2) uses this exponent to write the functional equation with the sign $\varepsilon(E)$.

**Scan 13, lower half.** Assuming the displayed self-duality globally on $X$, the author writes $D(E)\simeq\check E(n)[2n]\simeq E(\rho+n)[2n]$. Each $[2n]$ has a leader to the English word « shift ». The last live formula replaces $t$ by $p^{-(\rho+n)}t$ in the $L$-function of the direct image.

**Scan 10.** A separate typescript states existence and uniqueness up to scale of a Haar measure on a locally compact group. It recalls the transformation formulas using the character $\Delta_G$, refers back for uniqueness, and begins the existence construction. A finite combination of point masses acts on a function by the sum of its translates, $\gamma(\mu)f(x)=\sum_i a_i f(s_i^{-1}x)$.

**Scan 8.** A bracketed editorial note explains the choice of measures with finite support. The construction defines $A(f,g)$ by the inequality $f\leq\gamma(\mu)g$ for nonzero positive $\mu$ with finite support. Its infimum of masses is the quantity $(f:g)$. Items (i)–(vi) concern translation, scaling, monotonicity and bounds between these quantities.

**Scans 6 and 4.** Item (vii) gives subadditivity. Item (viii) gives a reverse estimate with error $\varepsilon(h:g)$ when the support of $g$ is sufficiently close to the identity. The two leaves run through the uniform-continuity estimate and the choices of $\delta$, $\eta$ and the neighbourhood $V$.

**Scan 4, lower half.** The ratios $(f:g)/(f_o:g)$ have an ultrafilter limit. The preceding inequalities yield additivity, followed by an extension to a positive linear functional and a representing measure. The last line starts applying translation invariance and breaks off at « quels que ».

**Scan 12.** A second typescript gives Proposition 2 on exactness of the dual of a sequence of finite-length modules. The proof uses left exactness of Hom and a length count for surjectivity, then reduces the general three-term sequence to the short exact case.

**Scan 12, lower half.** Proposition 3 identifies submodules with their orthogonals in the dual and interchanges sum and intersection. The proof identifies the dual of the quotient with the orthogonal. The example takes $A=Z$ and $K/A=Q/Z$, with double underlines beneath the ring letters, and mentions duality for finite abelian groups.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scan 9.** « satisfaisant aux conj. de Weil » is a hypothesis on the motive in the determinant assertion.
- **Scan 11.** The slanting note begins « avec les conjectures standart » with *standard* as another reading. The condition involves $X$, the insertion « lisse », and a weight $\rho$ for $F$. The small words specifying the hypotheses are marked in the transcription.
- **Scan 13.** « Ou encore, si on admet que » introduces global self-duality. The parenthesis begins « il suffit que ce soit vrai sur le corps des fonctions de $X$ » and ends « donc c'est sans doute tjrs vrai ». *Fractions* is another possible reading for *fonctions*. The author gives no argument for the passage to all of $X$.

## Slips

- **Scan 4, before the bound.** The typescript has $[F:g]$ in the prose and $(F:g)$ in the adjacent estimate. The preceding definition uses round parentheses. The likely intended reference is $(F:g)$. Confidence is high for the difference in the fences.
- **Scan 6, proof of (v).** The norm formula has two equals signs, one at either side of the physical line break. One equality suffices. Confidence is high for both impressions.
- **Scan 8, nonemptiness of $A(f,g)$ (S8-A0).** The definition allows $f=0$ and requires $\mu\ne0$. The proposed covering construction takes $M=\sup_K f$ for $K=\operatorname{supp}f$. When $f=0$ this support is empty and the proposed cover supplies no positive mass. Handle this case by taking $\mu=\varepsilon_e$. Confidence is high because the two conditions are explicit.
- **Scan 10, last sentence (S10-ZERO, S10-OR).** The conclusion reads « que si $\mu=0$ et $f=0$ ». With nonnegative inputs the correct conjunction here is « ou ». A nonzero point mass acting on the zero function already contradicts the typed statement. Confidence is high because the conjunction and the finite sum are clear.
- **Scans 9 and 11, linked arguments (S9-TWIST).** The denominator in each of these formulas reads as $p\rho$ with $p^\rho$ also possible. The preceding factor $1-p^{-\rho}t$ favours the raised exponent. Confidence is low about a slip because the vertical position is uncertain.
- **Scan 11, lines defining $\chi$, $\delta$ and $b_i$.** The rank is written with $\bar E$, the determinant and $b_i$ with $\bar F$. Using $\bar E$ throughout would keep the first coefficient object, but the marginal hypothesis separately names $F$. Confidence is low about an unintended switch.
- **Scan 12, proof of Proposition 2 (S12-END).** The first two dual sequences in the proof both end at $L^\ast$. The first may have been meant to end in $L^\ast\to0$, since the next sentence treats surjectivity as the point still to be shown. Confidence is high in the typed endpoints and medium that the first line omits a terminal zero.
- **Scan 13, sign before the power of $p$.** The overwritten live coefficient is read first as $\delta(E)$, with $\varepsilon(E)$ possible. Equation (2) and the annotation $\varepsilon(E)=\pm1$ favour $\varepsilon(E)$. Confidence is low about a slip because two strokes occupy the same place.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark, scan 5 (S3-DET, S5-PROD).** The many-factor formula has an undecorated product sign where the two-factor formula has $\ast$. If it meant ordinary multiplication, two identity maps on lines at $t=1/2$ would give $1/4$ on the right and $2$ on the left. Confidence is high in this scalar check and low about what the writer intended by the product sign.
- **Model remark, scan 10 (S10-OR).** If a weight is positive and $f$ is positive at some point, its corresponding translate makes the sum positive somewhere. This gives the converse needed for the corrected « ou » statement. Confidence is high by positivity of each summand.
- **Model remark, scans 11 and 13 (S11-CHI, S13-SYM, S13-PAIR, S13-WEIGHT).** As a check on the paired index and the factor $1/2$, all 1,364 tested symmetric lists satisfied $\sum(-1)^i i b_i=n\chi$. With seven choices of $\rho$, all 9,548 weighted sums gave $(n+\rho)\chi/2$. Here $0\le n\le4$, each independent $b_i$ is in $\{0,1,2,3\}$, and $-3\le\rho\le3$. Confidence is high in these exact finite computations. The geometric symmetry of the $b_i$ is an input to the check.
- **Model reading question, scan 3, crossed array, TeX line 32.** The frozen fragment has a comma between $p$ and $p+1$ in the third row. The small stroke meets an oblique cancellation. Juxtaposition without the comma is also possible. The crop is 003_N01_arraycomma.jpg. Confidence is low in this punctuation.
