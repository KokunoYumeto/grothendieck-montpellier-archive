# K14-08 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**101 · The transition systems.** A square relates the primed and unprimed E’s at three indices. The vertical arrows are called transition homomorphisms. The following N.B. describes the unprimed group by a quotient by a power of an ideal, and the primed group using a symmetric algebra and a power of its augmentation ideal. It refers the proof of Lemme B to an earlier special case.

**101 · Lemme C.** Given the system of indices and the map in Lemme B, the revised part a) asks for a further index and a homomorphism of A-algebras. The two conditions concern an epimorphism on associated gradeds and a composite equal to the transition homomorphism. A former part b), with another stage of maps, is cancelled.

**102–103 · A surface and its hyperplane section.** The cover brings together local conjectures and an extension to intermediate Jacobians. For a nonsingular projective surface X and hyperplane section Y, the next sheet puts three restriction maps beside their kernels or images. It calls B¹(X) the “partie primitive”, describes it by divisor classes of degree zero, and maps it to J¹(Y). A further diagram relates K to the quotient by the image of J¹(X). The last paragraph discusses the effect of limiting curves in a pencil.

**104 · Dimension formula and local proof.** For a dominant finite-type morphism, corollary 1 compares the generic-fibre dimension plus the codimension of the closure of y with a residue-field transcendence degree plus the codimension of the closure of x. The displayed sign in (13) is ≥. The proof passes to affine schemes and local rings and invokes (12). Two handwritten versions use local-ring dimensions. The long marginal comment asks for a commutative-algebra formulation and a geometric formulation.

**104 · Consequences.** Corollary 2 imposes closedness of x in the fibre and a regular-ring quotient hypothesis on the local ring at y. Formula (14) gives the codimension of the closure of x. Corollary 3 has only its hypotheses and the words “on a” on this leaf.

**106 · The local ranges.** The setup takes a strictly local ring A, punctures Spec A at the closed point to form X, cuts out Y with f and puts U=X−Y. X and Y are assumed regular. Part 1 lists vanishings or finiteness statements for groups labelled N, J and A. Part 2 lists the ranges where the J and N maps from X to Y should be bijective, injective or zero. Small marginal qualifications single out p and finite groups.

**107 · The complement and the problem about forms.** After the corresponding range for A, part 3 puts the restriction from X to U beside the degree-shifting map from Y to X. Both have stronger conclusions in the indicated lower ranges. Part 4 asks for integer-valued intersection forms and a theory of vanishing cycles for the groups and maps written there.

**107–109 · Local examples.** At N=2 the box singles out finiteness of N¹(X) and names Mumford–Hodge. At N=3 the working groups are J¹(X) and N¹(X). At N=4 the next leaf singles out J²(X) and N¹(X). Each of the latter two cases leads to a request for a natural complement and an interpretation of a comparison with superscript π. The last block separates N=2m+1 from N=2m and gives proposed vanishings modulo finite groups.

**111 · Global restriction and pushforward.** The hypothesis introduces smooth projective X of dimension n and its smooth hyperplane section Y. The opening list distinguishes r, a and τ under the groups of cycles. Part 1 runs from X to Y with codimension i raised. Below the first boundary the map is called bijective. The sheet then distinguishes the boundary cases 2i=n−1 and 2i=n, including induced maps with an outer τ. Part 2 runs from Y to X with dimension i lowered and has its own thresholds and surjectivity assertions.

**M112 · Scan 112, powers of E.** The continuation finishes the pushforward list with the upper τ groups. Part 3 writes an E raised to n−2i from degree i to n−i on the a groups. A second E is raised to n−2i+1 on the r groups with an outer τ, with target degree n−i+1. Both are labelled bijective modulo finite groups. The following E raised to p has target degree i+p, with separate bounds for injectivity and surjectivity. The degrees in the first two formulas are the data used in R115 and R116 below.

**113 · The open complement.** Part (2′) describes the group on U=X−Y as a quotient of a group on X by an image from Y. Two statements about lower-indexed groups on U then give zero in the indicated ranges. Both carry a question about p.

**113 · The punctured cone and primitive groups.** Part (3′) takes the punctured affine cone E over X. It assigns finiteness or vanishing to groups in two high ranges. The N.B. pairs groups on E with primitive groups on X in the lower ranges and with zero in the upper ones, modulo finite groups. The last two lines describe kernels of powers of L. The names of the kernels have been overwritten. The right margin refers to “coh. algébrique” and “V.A. intermédiaire”.

**114 · Cancelled cohomological-dimension argument.** Th. 4 proposes a bound on the cohomological dimension of U=S_f for a complete local ring. It reduces to vanishing of Hⁱ(U, ℤ/ℓℤ) for i>n, introduces a finite extension from a formal power series ring and discusses separability and an étale construction. A diagram and the last product formula relate S, S₀, S′₀ and S′. The sheet is crossed from top to bottom. Some of the hypotheses and transitions cannot be recovered securely.

**115–116 · The global examples.** For n=2,3,4 the tables specialize restriction, pushforward, powers of E and open-complement statements. The vertical double strokes identify individual groups with ℤ or zero. Some O.K. marks have an explicit hypothesis about the equivalence relation on cycles. In the n=3 example a double arrow links the pushforward question to the E¹ question. For n=4 the questions labelled “continue” and “numérique” have separate arrows and targets.

## Slips

**101, below the square.** The word is typed “flêches”. The first e carries a visible circumflex. Ordinary French spelling is “flèches”. In Lemme C the type reads “en vertu de lemme A”, where the usual phrase is “en vertu du lemme A”.

**101, Lemme C, a) · Possible index slip.** The map is introduced as w with a low j, and the first property begins with a handwritten w with a low k. If it names the same map, j is the expected index. The transcription preserves k. The final letter in “idéah” and the expression “form simple” are reading questions, with no correction asserted.

**S113 · Scan 113, cone dimension.** The line reads “de dim 2n+1”. If X is the n-dimensional variety of 111 and E is its usual punctured affine cone, the expected dimension is n+1. In a projective chart a lift to the punctured cone has one free nonzero scalar. Confidence in this conditional dimension count is high. The sheet does not restate the dimension convention for X here.

## Remarks of the model

**R115 · Model remark, low confidence about a slip.** On 115, n=2, part (3), the second brace has a target of degree 2 on its first line, and the next line has an identity in degree 1. Direct substitution of i=0 and 1 into the τ formula on 112 would give target degrees 3 and 2, with powers E³ and E¹. The first target subscript is doubtful and the table identifies zero groups. It is unclear whether both lines were meant as literal substitutions.

**R116 · Model remark, high confidence about the numerical comparison.** The E⁴, E² and id rows at the top of 116 continue n=3. Using n−2i+1 on 112 gives powers 4, 2, 0 for i=0,1,2 and target degrees 4, 3, 2. For n=4, the a formula gives powers 4, 2, 0, and the τ formula gives 5, 3, 1. These are the two series on the lower table, with id at zero power and E written without a superscript at power one. This check concerns the indices only.

**Reading question · 107, part (4).** The frozen text has the N map with source power (N−2)/2 and target power (N−1)/2. The last numeral above the target denominator could be 2. That would give equal powers on the two groups. On enlargement the target stroke is narrower than the source-side 2 and resembles the 1 over the preceding J. The two N powers remain different in the text. Confidence in a change is low.

**Reading question · 106, opening and part (1), and the cycle tables on 111–116.** The page files generally use an italic A for the cycle groups. On 106 the pen makes the ring A with an angular crossbar and the group A with a round left loop. A calligraphic A is a possible transcription of the second shape. This distinction is clearest when both appear on one sheet. Confidence that they represent two distinct alphabets is medium.
