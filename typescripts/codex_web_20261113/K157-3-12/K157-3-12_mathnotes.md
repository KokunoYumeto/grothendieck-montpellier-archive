# Mathematical notes on cote 157-3, scans 177–191

## What the pages do

**Opposite edges of a square (177).** The page begins during an argument. Under $W(7\,\mathrm{bis})$, membership of $\alpha_1$ in $W$ is equivalent to membership of $i_2$, and membership of $\alpha_2$ to that of $i_1$. The proof passes to four primed arrows using the preceding lemma. The oblique NB singles out the forward implication.

**A corollary for maps of forks (177–179).** A morphism $(f_0,f_1,f_2)$ of two forks induces a map of their integral categories. The three conditions concern $f_0$, the pair $f_1,f_2$ together, and the induced map. Assuming $W(7)$, any two of the conditions imply the third. The argument compares cubes and ends on 179.

**The universal property in §3.2 (179–180).** To give a map $\mathscr X\to Y$ is to give three functors and two transformations $\lambda_i:f_i\alpha_i\to f_0$. The notes place this description in the more general setting of maps out of a fibered category. They write a transformation $f_i\circ u^*\to f_j$ for an arrow $u:i\to j$ of the base.

**The comparison map and Definition 3 (181–182).** A commutative square $Q$ gives $f_0=f_1\alpha_1=f_2\alpha_2$. The two identity transformations give a functor $\varphi_Q:\mathscr X\to Y$. The cube on 182 records what it does to the structural maps and transformations. Definition 3 calls the square $W$-homotopically cocartesian when this functor belongs to $W$.

**Two corollaries of the definition (183–184).** The first transfers the opposite-edge test to the original square, pairing $\alpha_1$ with $f_2$ and $\alpha_2$ with $f_1$. The second compares two such squares in a cube. Its three conditions concern the vertical over vertex 0, the two verticals over 1 and 2 together, and the vertical at $Y$. The proof invokes the corollary on manuscript p. 126.

**The construction with open immersions in §3.3 (184–186).** The underlying fork defines a cofibered category over $\Psi=\Phi^0$. The two opens of the base have intersection $\{0\}$. Their inverse images meet in $X_0$ and give a square ending in $\widetilde{\mathscr X}$. The two maps from $X_1,X_2$ to the indicated pieces belong to $W$. The last diagram on 186 exhibits the reversed direction of the two transformations in the new universal problem.

**Passage to opposites (187–188).** There is now a second comparison $\widetilde\varphi:\widetilde{\mathscr X}\to Y$. The author asks whether its membership in $W$ gives the same definition. Taking opposites presents $\widetilde{\mathscr X}^0$ as an integral of the fork of opposite categories. He sets it beside the original integral.

**The functor $\Sigma$ (188–189).** The span from $\Sigma(X)$ to $X$ and $X^0$ is functorial in $X$. Taking the opposite interchanges $p$ and $q$. Applying this construction at each vertex produces a span of forks. Integrating it gives two maps from a common category to $\mathscr X$ and $\widetilde{\mathscr X}^0$.

**A diagram retaining the map to $Y$ (189–190).** The author appends the comparison map to the integral fork and calls the resulting diagram $\widehat Q$. Diagram (***) compares three such diagrams. He describes their values at vertices $0,1,2,4$, then separately at the integral vertex 3. The two oblique notes correct the proposed description of the indexing category $\Gamma$.

**Restriction and Proposition 14 (191).** Restriction to $\{3,4\}$ and the added dotted portion give four adjoining squares. The line below them relates five membership tests in $W$, from $\varphi_Q$ to $\widetilde\varphi_Q$. The text also identifies the five vertical arrows up to isomorphism in $\mathrm{Hot}_W$. Proposition 14 begins by displaying $Q$ and $Q^0$. The last supplied sheet ends after the second diagram, before the proposition’s conclusion.
