## What the pages do

**N297 · Truncations and adjunctions (297).** The page begins inside a graded Hom computation. It puts a shifted truncation next to the grading index, states a condition for a left adjoint to the inclusion, and passes to the opposite category. A square compares two truncated module categories. The closing line lists four adjoint functors. The first two Hom lines are crossed out; the object letters in this calculation deserve particular caution.

**N298 · Reduction to a trait (298).** The reused typescript takes the finite complement of the good-reduction locus on a connected regular base of dimension one, chooses extension indices pointwise, and takes a compositum. The Galois action then reduces the question to one point over the closed point of a trait. Corollary 3.7 restates the condition in terms of an open subgroup and an action on $T_\ell(A(\overline K))$ which is “unipotente d'échelon 2”.

**N299a · Comparison of graded Hom groups (299).** A diagram compares two truncations, then runs down through expressions involving $\tau_\gamma$, $\varphi_\beta$ and $\varphi_0$. There are separate conditions on the cutoffs and on the sign of $d$. Several indices and a line under the horizontal arrow have been overwritten.

**N299b · Calculation with divided powers (299).** The sheet takes a $k$-module $M$ and writes $M_{S_k}=M\otimes_k k\{T\}$. A map $u$ is specified on $T^{(i)}$ by coefficients $\xi_i$. The two product lines lead to the boxed relation $c_{i,\ell}\xi_{i+\ell}=c_{i-d,\ell}\xi_i$. The line below it singles out the index $d$.

**N300 · Base change and the Picard scheme (300).** The other reused typescript places two families in the square (12.8.2), with base map $g$, and introduces the degree-zero Picard object in (12.8.3). It relates formal smoothness to the displayed $H^2$ vanishing on a fibre of dimension one. It assumes representability by a group scheme and mentions a version using an algebraic space.

**N302 · Simplicial notation and the aim of the notes (301–302).** The cover announces the subject. Folio 1 distinguishes the category of simplexes, its presheaves and the notation $\mathrm{Ss}$. A lower star and an upper star distinguish simplicial and cosimplicial objects. The author proposes studying operations and structures on invariants of spaces from the standpoint of universal algebra. His sideways note explains the choice of two names for the same category.

**N303a · Degrees and duality (303, left).** A bracketed degree $K_{[n]}$ belongs to the simplicial object. A duality between $A$ and $B$ allows a single letter to denote corresponding objects, with the star changing level. Left and right finite-type projective modules over $k$ furnish the example.

**N303b · The universal cosimplicial object (303, right).** The fully faithful functor to presheaves sends a simplex to a representable. Its evaluation formula identifies maps from that representable with a degree of $X_*$. As the simplex varies, the result is the “objet cosimplicial universel”. The bottom of the leaf begins a convention for objects with two indices.

**N304 · Interchanging two variables (303–305, opening).** The text orders and positions the two coordinates of $K$. It gives a separate left superscript $\sigma$ to the result of permuting arguments. This preserves the distinction between permuting coordinates and raising or lowering indices by duality. The paragraph then extends the convention to more than two indices.

**N305 · Proposition 1 (305).** Evaluation at the universal cosimplicial object identifies the indicated colimit-preserving functors into $A$ with objects of $A^*$. The second part presents the opposite, limit-preserving version with $B_*$. The page describes inverse assignments using limits over simplexes of $X_*$. Its paragraph headed “Yoga” applies the correspondence to covariant and contravariant invariants.

**N306a · Represented functors and homotopy (306).** For abelian targets the text writes the functor represented by $K_*$ as $X_*\mapsto\mathrm{Hom}(X_*,K_*)$. It discusses which algebraic structures the functor inherits. The author calls the resulting invariants too “grosse” and “fruste” for the homotopy question and proposes inverting arrows coming from homotopy equivalences in categories carrying the extra structure.

**N306b · Dold–Puppe notation (306–308).** For an additive Karoubian category the page draws the opposing ND and DP arrows. The dot signifies a complex; the star signifies a simplicial or cosimplicial object. The location of the mark distinguishes chains from cochains. The equivalence and duality yield four related objects with the same letter. With several indices, each slot can be converted separately; scan 308 lists the four component patterns for two selected kinds of object.

**N309 · Bicomplexes and their total degree (309–310).** The pages interpret dot indices in the same or opposite positions as bicomplexes of chains, cochains, or one of each. The mixed case admits positive and negative degrees. The choice between a total differential of degree $+1$ and one of degree $-1$ is left open. The author independently fixes *commuting* component differentials, explicitly requiring a sign in the total differential.

**N311 · Proposition 2 and the universal additive invariant (310–311).** The Dold–Puppe equivalence recasts Proposition 1 in terms of complexes. In the contravariant abelian case, the author forms the degreewise free abelian object on $X_*$ and its normalized chain complex $\mathbb{C}_{.}(X_*)$. The boxed formula expresses $F(X_*)$ by maps of complexes from $\mathbb{C}_{.}(X_*)$ to $K_{.}$. This gives the first announced conclusion about an additive invariant. The closing NB starts a statement about reconstructing a multiplication and breaks off at the foot of the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

**R299 · Model, high confidence.** Substitution of $i=d$ in the *boxed* equation yields $c_{d,\ell}\xi_{d+\ell}=c_{0,\ell}\xi_d$. The separate final line has $\xi_d$ alone on the right. Passing from one line to the other therefore uses $c_{0,\ell}\xi_d=\xi_d$. The coefficient normalization itself is not stated on this sheet. The comparison does not depend on either of the doubtful exponents above the box.

**R310 · Model, high confidence.** The sign mentioned on scan 310 has a concrete effect even on a single commuting square. With identity maps on its four edges over the integers, the unsigned total differential squared sends the upper-left generator to twice the opposite generator. Giving the vertical map the sign $(-1)^p$ in horizontal degree $p$ gives zero on the same generator. This example illustrates the final sentence of the Remarque and does not assign a sign convention to the author.

**R311 · Model, medium confidence.** The representation formula on the last sheet identifies what data determine the abelian *group* $F(X_*)$. The NB singles out a multiplication on that group as additional structure. The sentence breaks at “par”. Its precise claim about reconstruction cannot be recovered within this package.
