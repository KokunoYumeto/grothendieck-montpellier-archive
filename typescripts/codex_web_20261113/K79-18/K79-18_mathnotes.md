# Cote 79 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Scans 265–266, the action on \(H\).** Formula (144) identifies the image of \(T_0\) in \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) by conditions involving \(U_0,T_H\) and the coset of \(\sigma\). The value of an automorphism on \(\sigma\) defines \(c_0\). Conjugation by \(t\in T_H\) supplies a map in the other direction. Their composite sends \(t\) to \(t^{-2}\). The argument for diagram (148) uses a congruence modulo the centralizer of \(H\). Two displays numbered (149) describe the images of three groups and the subgroup on which \(\delta_{T_0}\) vanishes.

**Scans 267–268, the coset and the degenerate case.** Adjoining \(T_H\sigma=\sigma T_H\) to \(T_H\) defines \(N_H\). In the nondegenerate case its quotient by \(T_H\) is recorded by a sign. The author also defines \(N_G\). A first list of equivalent conditions for degeneracy is struck through. The next sheet treats \(\sigma\in T_H\) separately, writes \(U_0=U_1=\{1\}\), and relates the case \(\sigma=1\) to \(A_0\) consisting of one-point parts of \(S\).

**Scans 268–269, a marked multigraph.** The digression fixes an origin \(s_0\) and an incident ray. It identifies \(S\) with \(H/U_0\) and arranges the groups in the inclusion diagram (159). The stabilizer of the unordered pair of rays gives a sign homomorphism. The fibre over \(-1\), the ray \(D_1\), and the coset written \(\Pi\) are related as \(Z\)-torsors. Formula (162) writes the right action as \(g\mapsto gu(\lambda)\).

**Scan 270, the subgroup data.** In (163) the author passes from a marked nondegenerate multigraph with its action to the data \(H,U_0,T_H,N_H\). He imposes four conditions, concerning normalization and intersection, index two, squares in the other coset, and \(U_0\cap U_1\). He asks whether \(B_H\cap N_H=T_H\), reduces this to excluding \(\sigma\) from \(B_H\), and gives a short contradiction. The longer, cancelled argument considers an alternative of exponent two.

**Scan 271, a graph condition.** A graph is called “triangulant” when both ends of each edge have a common neighbour. Formula (2) defines \(F_0\) using the two-element subsets of a three-element set. For a multigraph the condition is applied to each graph structure. The author discusses when one structure suffices.

**Scans 273–274, the table.** For \(n=2,3,4,5\), the columns record group orders, proposed group identifications, counts for \(M^*\) and for its quotient by sign, polyhedron types, and counts for \(P(M)\) and the two families denoted by \(F\). The group identifications in the last two columns and the overwritten penultimate numerical row have different kinds of uncertainty. The former carry the author's own question marks. Scan 274 carries only the expression with \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), a raised 2 and a star.

**Scans 276–277, recovering a vertex from its star.** If distinct vertices \(s,s'\) have equal stars, the text describes the triangulated polyhedron as the double cone on their common polygon. The two apices have valence \(n\) and the other vertices have valence four. Equal valences lead to the octahedron. For \(n\ne4\) the text singles out the apical pair and describes an involution interchanging its members and fixing the common star. Its orbits express equality of stars.

**Scan 277, the exterior-product criterion.** Point 2 asks when conditions on \(u\wedge x\) and \(u'\wedge x\), for vectors in a free rank-two module over a semi-local ring, force \(u'\in\{\pm u\}\). A lemma tests the unit-determinant implication by collinearity after reduction at each maximal ideal. A choice of basis and two substitutions for \(x\) reduce the sign question to \(u=e+f\) and \(u'=e\pm f\).

**Scans 277–278, the exceptional ring.** For the minus choice the coordinate calculation compares \(\alpha+\beta\) and \(\alpha-\beta\), normalizes \(\alpha+\beta\) to 1, and reaches the boxed condition \(2\Lambda=\{0,2\}\ne\{0\}\). The annotation on 277 describes a square-zero ideal generated by 2. Scan 278 considers the pairs that fail the expected conclusion and identifies \(2M\) in an exact sequence with a two-dimensional space over the residue field \(\mathbb F_2\).

**Scans 279–280, the canonical antipodism.** Of the nonzero elements of \(2M\), two remain after excluding \(2u\). Their translates of \(u\) give the same class modulo sign. The text uses that class to define the fixed-point-free involution \(\underline a\) of \(S_M\). It also describes the involution through the kernel of reduction of the automorphism group. For \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) it identifies the octahedron, discusses dropping semi-locality and asserts \(\det\underline a=-1\).

**Scan 281, the path and the coordinate calculation.** Two incidence conditions are used to choose intermediate vertices in an \(A_0\)-chain. A second diagram accompanies the condition \(A(s)=A(t)\). A faint pencil drawing accompanies the return to the octahedron example. The bottom calculation compares the two wedge products for \(x=e+f\) and \(x'=e-f\). It ends with the alternatives \(2a=0\) or \(2b=0\) and the two unit requirements on \(b-a,b+a\).
