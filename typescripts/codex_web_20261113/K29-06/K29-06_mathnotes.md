# Mathematical notes on cote 29, scans 77–94

## What the pages do

**Scan 77. A bound from a generic curve.** The note starts mid-argument. Choose a projective, proper, normal model $X$ over $k$, take a “courbe générique $C$” in $X$, and take its genus for the proposed bound. The name of the bound reads $n(K)$ or $u(K)$. The writer asks for a direct demonstration.

**Scan 78. Title sheet.** “Revêtements formels étales et th. de Mumford” groups the material under formal étale covers and Mumford’s theorem. A cancelled heading concerns a henselian ring and its completion.

**Scan 82. Comments on chapters I–III.** The writer proposes restricting certain morphisms to adic morphisms. For tame ramification he asks for a Lefschetz variant, an application after Mumford’s theorem, and examples where the components of the divisor have equations. He singles out both descent for covers and the use made of chapter III.

**Scan 84. Comments on chapters IV and VI–IX.** The suggested rewrite of VIII, 3/8.1.6 starts with geometric points above $x,y$ with $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$. A path from $\bar y$ to $\bar x$ is described by a lifting to the strict localization at $\bar x$, then by a $k(y)$-homomorphism from the displayed product of fields to $\overline{k(y)}$. Other entries ask to combine examples, question an equivalence of categories, and compare two implications in chapter IX.

**Scan 85. The Raynaud extension.** This cancelled typescript applies 7.2.1 when the abelian formal scheme $\widehat B$ is algebraizable. A handwritten insertion assumes $T_0$ is isotrivial. The torus $T$ lifting $T_0$ occurs in an extension of $B$. The page characterizes the extension through its reductions and its formal completion, then states its functoriality and compatibility with the specified base changes.

**Scan 86. Comments on chapter X.** Item 1 asks for a statement detecting finite type and finite presentation of $\mathcal E$ from its two pieces. Local freeness appears in an insertion. Item 12 formulates a test for tameness of a finite flat cover by restricting it to $S_1$ and $S_2$.

**Scan 87. Inverse image of stratified modules.** This cancelled draft asks whether the operation on quasi-coherent modules gives the intended inverse image on stratified modules. A diagram relates the base changes of $T_0,T,X$ and $S$. The text introduces infinitesimal neighbourhoods of all orders for an immersion, with a parenthetical extension to the unramified case. The page ends during the explanation following the direct-limit formula.

**Scan 88. An extension and its class.** Start with a regular connected formal scheme and a normal-crossings divisor with distinguished component $D_0=X_0$. The diagram exhibits the tame fundamental group as an extension of $\pi_1^t(U)$ by an inertia group $I_0$. The author introduces $\alpha\in H^2(\pi_1^t(U),I_0)$ and asks for it explicitly. Lemma 1 and the low-degree cohomology diagram (A88) lead him to determine $\alpha$ from $\rho(\alpha)$.

**Scan 90. Self-intersection and the inertia quotient.** Lemma 2 (A90) identifies $\rho(\alpha)$ with the image of the self-intersection class $\beta$ of $X_0$. The next diagram gives a map from $\pi_2^t(U)$ to $\mu_0$. The author first states that its image maps to zero in $I_0$, then claims that this image is the entire kernel. He begins constructing an extension with the resulting quotient as its kernel.

**Scan 92. Realizing a quotient as inertia.** The extension just proposed has an epimorphism $\Pi'\longrightarrow\pi_1^t(\mathfrak X/D)$. To identify the two groups, the author asks to realize each eligible quotient $J_0$ of $\mu_0$ as inertia in a tame cover. He passes from vanishing of a cohomology class on the tame cover to vanishing at finite level, with a qualification about how to define cohomology in the locally noetherian case.

**Scan 94. Lifting divisor classes.** After a reduction with $\mu_n$ split on $X_0$, an insertion expresses $\beta_0$ as $n\gamma_0+\sum\lambda_i\beta_0^i$, with terms coming from the divisors $D_i$. The author then considers the kernels of $\operatorname{Pic}(X_n)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X_0)$ and claims unique divisibility and a resulting surjectivity. He boxes a request for verification. The denominator of the displayed quotient and the reworked conclusion are uncertain.

**Scan 89. Diagonalizable groups.** A separate typescript discusses base change for duals and evaluates $D(M_S)$ on a variable $S'$. For a commutative group $M$ it identifies the representing scheme as the spectrum of its group algebra over $S$. Definition 1.1 gives the diagonalizable and locally diagonalizable cases. A cancelled definition of a constant or locally constant lattice begins at the foot.

**Scan 91. Reflexivity and representable Hom.** This typescript starts inside an argument. Disjoint opens indexed by $M$ describe a locally constant map to $M$. Corollary 1.4 asserts reflexivity and a bijection reversing the order of the arguments under duality. Corollary 1.5 gives representability for the indicated Hom functor with $M$ finitely generated. Its final sentence treats locally diagonalizable $G,H$ with $H$ of finite type.

**Scan 93. A point count and an exactness theorem.** The opening computation (A93) gives the number of $k$-points of $D(T)$ using the roots of unity in the cyclic factors of $T$. Theorem 3.1 then dualizes an exact sequence of commutative groups over a nonempty base. The text addresses the kernel and the quasi-compactness of $u'$. The argument for the last assertion continues beyond the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

- **Model remark M90, high confidence.** The manuscript distinguishes determining $\alpha$ for a given inertia quotient from determining the quotient itself. On scan 90 the words “sous réserve de connaître le quotient $I$ de $\mu$” follow the class formula. The stronger kernel equality comes later, and scan 92 sets a further task to obtain it.
- **Model remark M93, high confidence for the example.** The point count on scan 93 depends on which roots of unity lie in $k$. For $k=\mathbf F_2$ and $n=3$, direct enumeration gives exactly one cube root of unity, although $3$ is prime to $2$. The sheet opens mid-sentence. The hypotheses imposed on $k$ on the preceding page are unavailable here.
- **Model reading question, scan 82, beside item 25 (source line 43), low confidence.** The final text starts the item with “25”. Immediately below the number is a small dark pointed mark with a faint downward tail. It could be an upward pointer or a stray stroke. Its target is unclear.
- **Model reading question, scan 84, upper-left edge (before source line 3), low confidence.** The final text begins at the chapter IV heading. Near the edge, an isolated, faint vertical stroke might be a numeral 1. It has a darker head and a broken lower end, and stands well apart from the chapter and item numbers.
- **Model reading question, scan 90, opening of Lemma 2 (source line 5), low confidence.** The final text has a cancelled letter before $T_{X_0/\mathfrak X}$ and a closing parenthesis after it. A curved stroke to the left of the cancelled letter could also be an opening parenthesis. It turns back towards the inserted Chern-class phrase, so it could be part of the insertion mark.
