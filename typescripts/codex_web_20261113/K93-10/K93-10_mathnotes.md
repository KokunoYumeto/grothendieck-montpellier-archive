## What the pages do

**Pointed genus-one curves, 003.** The typescript starts with a proper flat curve of finite presentation and a section in the smooth locus. It specifies the geometric fibres, names their elliptic, multiplicative and additive types, and defines the line bundle $\omega=e^*\Omega^1_{E/S}$. [U1]

**The handwritten formulary, 004.** A single sheet collects the relations between $c_4,c_6,\Delta,j$, special values with extra automorphisms, a degree for $\omega$, and two series in $q$. It puts the Legendre expression for $j$ beside six transforms of $\lambda$, then gives a Hasse polynomial and a table by residues of $p$ modulo 12. The two signs at 504 cannot be separated securely. [U2]

**Filtration by pole order, 005.** Fibrewise vanishing and Riemann–Roch give bundles of sections of $\mathscr O(ne)$ and an exact sequence between successive twists. Formula (1.1) records the associated graded, with $i=0$ included and $i=1$ excluded. [U3]

**Invariant differentials, 005–006.** Relative duality introduces the sheaf of regular differentials. Two isomorphisms identify its sections with sections of $\omega$, and pull the latter back to the curve. [U4]

**Coordinates and the cubic, 006–007.** An invertible section $\pi$ and a filtered basis $(y,x,1)$ supply coordinates. Their changes have parameters $u,r,s,t$. Comparing sections with poles of order at most six gives the Weierstrass equation. The plane equation also gives the displayed expressions for $\pi$ in terms of the derivatives of $F$. [U5]

**Polynomial invariants, 007–009.** The lists define the $b$’s and $d$’s, then $c_4,c_6,\Delta$. The change-of-coordinate formulas show that $c_4,c_6,\Delta$ have weights 4, 6 and 12. The corresponding sections are independent of the chosen coordinates. [U6]

**The case with 2 and 3 invertible, 009–010.** Killing $a_1,a_2,a_3$ gives a short cubic. The substitution $Y=2y$, $X=x$ yields the model with coefficients $g_2,g_3$. Proposition 2.5 gives a moduli scheme with a chosen invertible differential. Formula (2.6) identifies $c_4^3-c_6^2$ with $1728\Delta$. [U7, A1]

**The case with 2 and $c_4$ invertible, 010–012.** After killing $a_1,a_3$, a translation arranges $d_6=0$. Equations (3.3)–(3.4) relate the remaining coefficients to the invariants. Proposition 3.5 describes the coefficient ring by inverting $a_2^2-3a_4$ and imposing $a_2a_4-9a_6=0$. [U8, A3]

**The nilpotent-2 case, 012.** With $c_4$ invertible, (4.1) gives a cubic containing $xy$. Formulas (4.2)–(4.3) describe the remaining coordinate changes and (4.4) gives the invariants modulo 2. [U9, A4]

**Vanishing $c_4$ in small characteristics, 012–013.** In characteristic 3 the text distinguishes $a_2^2=0$ from the additional assumption $a_2=0$. In characteristic 2 it distinguishes $a_1^4=0$ from $a_1=0$. Each stronger hypothesis yields a displayed normal form and its coordinate changes. [U10]

**Fibre types and $j$, 014–015.** Proposition 6.1 tests elliptic, multiplicative and additive type using $\Delta$ and $c_4$. The argument examines multiple roots and tangent directions. Without an additive fibre, the two invariants do not vanish together and give the projective-line-valued $j$ of (6.2). [U11]

**The isomorphism scheme, 015.** Proposition 6.3 states finiteness and unramifiedness, surjectivity for equal invariants, and an étale cover of rank two away from 0 and 1728. The proof uses integral dependence of the transformation parameters and the absence of infinitesimal automorphisms fixing the origin. [U12]

**The quadratic equation, 015–016.** Under the stronger invertibility hypotheses of 6.4, the text writes the isomorphism scheme as $\lambda^2=\mu$, with $\mu=c_4c'_6/(c_6c'_4)$. It compares the weights of the three normalized coefficients. The last paragraph begins a reduction for the general étale-cover assertion and breaks off at “soit”. [U13]
