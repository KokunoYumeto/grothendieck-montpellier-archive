# Mathematical notes on scans 48–62

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 48.** Part c) identifies the two residue fields attached to the valuations. Part d) localizes a product of prime-ideal powers. Only the factor indexed by i contributes at the chosen prime, and the final line gives $e(v_i/v)=v_i(a)$.

**U02 · Scan 49.** The sheet opens in the middle of a dimension bound, $(n-1)d$ with $d=\dim G$. For a list of places, it records the effect of changing each place by a Galois element on the corresponding $g_i$.

**U03 · Scans 49, 51.** The bracketed assertion asks for an element of an open compact coset in an adelic group whose components are all generic over $\mathbb Q$. The manuscript uses this assertion and the strengthened Tate conjecture in its proposed change of places.

**U04 · Scan 51.** In the reverse direction an approximation assumption puts a rational point in the specified coset. The stated consequence is a choice of conjugate places giving commensurable lattices. The final bracket calls attention to the dependence of these choices on $M$.

**U05 · Scan 52.** The typescript defines the diagonal map from $K$ to restricted adèles. Proposition 3 identifies the adèlic ring and its distinguished subring as completions. The proof obtains density by clearing the finitely many negative valuations and using the cited density for $A$.

**U06 · Scans 52, 50.** The end of 52 introduces the topology of $\mathrm{SL}(n,\underline A)$. For determinant one, the text expresses the entries of the inverse by minors to obtain continuity. Scan 50 continues the sentence identifying $\mathrm{SL}(n,K)$ as a subgroup.

**U07 · Scan 50.** Proposition 4 proves this subgroup is dense. The closure contains elementary matrices supported at one prime, then matrices with finitely many nonidentity components. The stated topology supplies the last density step.

**U08 · Scan 50.** Lemma 1 proves finite length for a finite-type torsion module under the given noetherian domain and maximality hypotheses, by considering its associated primes and citing a preceding result.

**U09 · Scan 50.** Lemma 2 compares the length of a filtered union with the supremum of the lengths. It treats the unbounded case, then chooses a member attaining the largest finite length. This leaf stops at “puisque la famille”.

**U10 · Scan 58.** A chain of implications runs from c) through g) back to a). The argument passes between ideals and divisors, compares a prime with a maximal ideal after localization, and discusses the local discrete-valuation case. The full list of conditions does not occur on these sheets.

**U11 · Scans 58, 56.** Proposition 1 on 58 states that a semi-local Dedekind ring is principal. The proof describes an integral ideal by finitely many valuation inequalities. The opening of 56 continues the final word “pour” on 58, chooses $x_0$ with the prescribed valuations, and concludes that the ideal is $Ax_0$.

**U12 · Scan 56.** Section 3 identifies nonzero fractional ideals with divisors, then expresses each fractional ideal uniquely as a product of prime powers. The integral case is characterized by the signs of the exponents; for a principal ideal the exponents are the corresponding valuations.

**U13 · Scan 54.** Formulas (2)–(5) give the exponents of a product, quotient, sum and intersection of two ideals by addition, subtraction, infimum and supremum. They use the two factorizations displayed at the end of 56.

**U14 · Scan 54.** Proposition 2 prescribes lower bounds on approximation at finitely many distinct primes and asks for integrality elsewhere. In the case of integral targets, the proof reduces the problem to simultaneous congruences.

**U15 · Scan 53.** For complete places of a field isomorphic to $\mathbb C$, the manuscript places the lattices in a common product. It describes a Galois action component by component. At infinity the text says “½ linéaire” and writes the scalar-extension isomorphism that defines the action.

**U16 · Scan 55.** An element $g$ comparing the lattices at $v$ and $w$ is determined up to right multiplication. The manuscript then tries to associate an element to $\sigma$; a direction-of-revision arrow joins two lattice formulas. The eventual boxed quotient map on this sheet is crossed out.

**U17 · Scan 57.** After fixing the finite components, the author singles out an element at infinity and writes the boxed map from $\operatorname{Gal}(k/k_0)$ to $G(k)$. A brace records the action on the lattice and the finite and infinite entries of $\sigma_*$.

**U18 · Scans 57, 59.** Scan 57 writes two product laws, the second after setting $\psi_v=\varphi_v^{-1}$. Scan 59 calls the resulting construction a 1-cocycle for the conjugation action. The single Greek letter at the start of 59 is doubtful.

**U19 · Scan 59.** The cocycle gives a principal homogeneous space with Galois action and a descended space over $k_0$. The manuscript describes the space using isomorphisms of fibre functors. It then connects genericity of the indicated point with Zariski density of the displayed image under transcendence conjecture No 1.

**U20 · Scans 59, 61.** The unfinished N.B. continues on 61 with a possible finite extension of $k_0$. The formulas use a generic $g$, its conjugates and an inverse expression. The page then considers the kernel of the finite-component map and ends with a claim about the closure of the total image.

**U21 · Scan 60.** Document 383 draws the type $G_2$ root system. It labels the two simple roots, the intervening positive combinations and $2\alpha_2+3\alpha_1$ as “plus grande racine”. The six opposite endpoints have no root labels.

**U22 · Scan 62.** Starting with $M_w^{\mathbb Z}=gM_v^{\mathbb Z}$, the computation changes the representative of the Galois action. The finite component reduces to $\sigma_l$. The bottom box writes the infinite-component change with a superscript $\sigma$ on its first $g_\infty$ and an inverse on its last $g_\infty$.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Scan 57 · Model, high confidence for the live formulas.** Taking pointwise inverses reverses the order of the two factors in the displayed law for $\varphi_v$. The result has the order printed in the second law for $\psi_v$. The test uses only the surviving factors.
- **R2 · Scan 54 · Model, high confidence.** A small integer example tests S54a. With residues 0 modulo 2 and 1 modulo 3, the two conditions have the solution 4 modulo 6. If both congruences are imposed at one and the same prime with exponent one, the two residues conflict. This checks the role of the missing index.
- **R3 · Scan 61 · Model, high confidence for the conditional identity.** If the left and right superscripts on g denote the same Galois transform, the inverse of $g^{-1}g^\sigma$ is $(g^\sigma)^{-1}g$. This matches the ordering in the two expressions placed together in the upper paragraph. The calculation does not identify the cancelled word between them.
