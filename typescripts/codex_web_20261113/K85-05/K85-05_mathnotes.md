# Mathematical notes · Cote 85, scans 58–71

## What the pages do

**U1 · Two invariant lines (59–60).** For the case $n=1$ in characteristic zero with finite $G_0$, the writer takes two strict pseudo-reflections and records the nondegeneracy conditions. A projectively invariant divisor of degree two leads to lines $D,D'$. Assuming one generator preserves a line, the pairings first give $\beta=-\mu_0$ after exchange of the indices. The second calculation ends at $\lambda_1=0$, « absurde ». In the remaining exchange case, he writes two off-diagonal matrices and concludes that the generators have order two. This is his ordinary regular-polygon case.

**U2 · The remaining finite images (60–61).** In the absence of an invariant divisor of degree two, the writer lists the projective groups $A_4,S_4,A_5$, their inverse images in $\mathrm{SL}(V)$ and the scalar factor. The cyclic subgroup generated by a strict pseudo-reflection meets the scalars trivially. Its image gives the list of possible orders $2,3,4,5$.

**U3 · Traces and the quadratic (62–63).** For $u=\tau_0\tau_1$, the invariant $K=(\operatorname{Tr}u)^2/\det u-2$ is restricted to five values. Expressing $\beta$ in terms of $\zeta$ and the two eigenvalues leads to a quadratic. A trigonometric calculation gives a circled discriminant proportional to $K+2$ and a single solution when $K=-2$. The writer counts 15 pairs of orders after excluding $(2,2)$, and 120 preliminary choices for the four other values of $K$. He proposes testing positivity of the invariant Hermitian form and the effect of replacing the angles by admissible multiples.

**U4 · From the projective data to the lift (63–66).** The text relates three conjugacy classes to the projective situation. Choosing one fixed point for each projective generator gives four choices before normalization. The scheme on 65 links those choices to the eigenvalue data, strict pseudo-reflections in the plane and the projective pair with two marked fixed points. The summary on 66 distinguishes one associated hyperpolygon when both generator orders differ from two and two possibilities when one generator has order two.

**U5 · Inverted draft (65).** Below the horizontal rule, in the opposite orientation, the writer evaluates maps at coordinate vectors, sets down three involution identities and leaves products to be determined. Three pencils of intersecting lines and an expansion of $(s_0+ic_0)^2$ share this area. The large diagonal strokes cross several entries.

**U6 · Counting cases (67–69).** The choice of primitive roots of orders three, four and five normalizes the first two eigenvalues. The notation $5,5'$ distinguishes the possible values $\alpha,\alpha'$ of $K$. Underlines identify triples admitting two hyperpolygons. The writer reports $6+13+25=44$ cases, 14 ambiguities of numerical type, and 180 possibilities before normalization. His right-hand note relates the last calculation to the product of two totients. On 69 he uses invariance of $\zeta$ under taking powers prime to the respective orders to reduce the explicit work to the normalized cases. The written counts and a recount of the individual entries are distinguished in R1.

**U7 · Locating the roots (70–71).** The writer evaluates $f_K$ at 1, bounds the difference of the two angles, sketches a parabola meeting a segment and asks about the squared difference of its roots. The next sheet tests the expression with the positive square root against 1. Its boxed trigonometric inequality is worked out for five labelled triples in four groups.
