# Mathematical notes on cote 52

## What the pages do

**Scans 002–003 · The automorphism theorem (U1).** Under the title “Th. de Torelli”, the first theorem sends an automorphism of a family of curves of genus at least two to the induced automorphism of its degree-zero Jacobian. It asserts injectivity. The proof takes an element of the kernel and describes its action on a related Jacobian torsor as translation by a section. The superscript on that torsor in 003 is doubtful. Corollaire 1 restates the claim for the two group functors and adds the closed-immersion assertion using properness.

**Scan 004 · The geometric lemma and Corollaire 2 (U2).** Over an algebraically closed field, the induced action on the degree-one Jacobian has translation parameter \(a\). On the embedded curve the calculation is \(\sigma(x)=x+a\). The trace formula is invoked to obtain a fixed point. At such a point \(a=\sigma x-x=0\). Corollaire 2 gives the corresponding assertion about a translation preserving the curve.

**Scan 005 · Corollaire 3 (U3).** The manuscript passes from automorphisms to isomorphisms between two curves. The displayed morphism from the isomorphism functor of the curves to that of the two Jacobians is asserted to be a closed immersion. Above it is a cancelled approach using a square-zero extension.

**Scans 005–006 · The functors (U4).** Fix a polarized abelian scheme \(A\) over \(S\). The functor \(F\) classifies curves together with a polarization-compatible identification of their Jacobian with the prescribed abelian scheme. An early, crossed construction of \(F'\) adds a trivialization involving a tensor power of differentials. It includes the dimension calculation \((2h-1)(g-1)\). The later construction chooses a section of the degree-one Jacobian.

**Scans 006–008 · Representability and the quotient (U5).** A heavily revised theorem discusses a representing scheme \(M\) and its map to \(S\). The argument uses a scheme \(M'\) for the auxiliary problem, the action of \(A\), and \(M=M'/A\). Embedded curves occur on a Hilbert scheme. Requiring the natural map from the Jacobian to \(A\) to be a polarized isomorphism cuts down the locus. Scan 007 invokes classical Torelli for the geometric points and uses points valued in the dual numbers to discuss ramification. The end of 008 returns to the Hilbert argument and ends in mid-sentence.

**Scans 009–010 · First-order deformations (U6).** A deformation of \(C\) in \(A\) is compared with an infinitesimal translation. The sequence with terms \(T,V,N\) relates the tangent sheaf of \(C\), the ambient tangent sheaf on \(C\), and the normal sheaf. The text asks for surjectivity on global sections and then injectivity on first cohomology. By duality it arrives at the multiplication map from two copies of \(H^0(C,\Omega)\) to \(H^0(C,\Omega^{\otimes2})\). The author distinguishes the hyperelliptic case and gives \(\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/t^2\) as the fibre in the case under discussion.

**Scans 010–011 · Extension over a valuation ring (U7).** The desired factorization of \(M\to S\) leads to a valuative question. Given a curve over the generic point and a specified isomorphism of its Jacobian with the generic fibre of \(A\), the corollary asks for a curve over the base with that generic fibre. Choosing a point on the auxiliary moduli scheme permits a generic embedding; the manuscript takes its closure.

**Scans 011–013 · The special fibre (U8).** A result of Hoyt about the components and their Jacobians is introduced in brackets. A margin note says Matsusaka suffices. For a positive irreducible divisor \(\Theta\), the conditions involve its top degree and a numerical equivalence \(\Theta^{n-1}\equiv(n-1)!\,Z\). The construction takes the closure of the generic divisor and specializes the numerical conditions to \(C_a\). Irreducibility of the polarization divisor is used to conclude that the specialized curve is irreducible and nonsingular.

**Scan 013 · Corollaire 1 on the image (U9).** Put \(T=\overline{f(M)}\), where \(f\) is the structural morphism. For \(t\in T\) the criterion for membership in \(f(M)\) is expressed through irreducibility of the polarization divisor on \(A_t\). The word added immediately before “irréductible” remains doubtful.

**Scan 014 · Corollaire 2 on families (U10).** Part (i) proposes a fibrewise criterion for a polarized abelian scheme to be a Jacobian. Part (ii) asserts a unique compatible \(S\)-isomorphism between two curves equipped with the given Jacobian identifications under the stated local hypothesis. A crossed earlier statement, a marginal revision of the base hypothesis and a circled objection remain on the sheet. The precise hypothesis in (i) is one of the uncertain readings.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scan 004 · High confidence about the arithmetic.** The live expression \(-1+2g-1\) equals \(2(g-1)\). The crossed expression \(1-2g+1\) is its negative. The page does not explain the switch of sign in its use of the trace formula. Any diagnosis of a sign error would have low confidence because the calculation was revised.

**R2 · Scan 006 · High confidence about the identity.** Expanding \(2h(g-1)-(g-1)\) gives \((2h-1)(g-1)\), independently of the doubtful name on the left. The calculation belongs to the abandoned construction of \(F'\).

**R3 · Scan 010 · High confidence about the logical distinction.** A one-dimensional tangent space alone does not determine the displayed fibre. For example, \(k[t]/(t^2)\) and \(k[t]/(t^3)\) have the same one-dimensional \((t)/(t)^2\) and different lengths. To deduce the asserted scheme structure, the argument needs information beyond that dimension. This observation does not decide which fibre the moduli problem actually has.

**R4 · Scans 012–013 · High confidence about the substitution.** Taking \(Z=C_a\) in the stated numerical condition gives the special-fibre equivalence written on 012. The subsequent paragraph separately invokes irreducibility of the polarization divisor. These are two distinct steps in the application of the criterion.

**R5 · Scans 009–010 · High confidence.** In the cohomology sequence, surjectivity from the sections of V to the sections of N is equivalent to injectivity from the first cohomology of T to that of V. Exactness at the two groups on either side of the connecting map suffices for this step.
