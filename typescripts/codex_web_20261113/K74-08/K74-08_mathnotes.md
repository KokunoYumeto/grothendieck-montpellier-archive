## What the pages do

**Two constructions on three indices (scans 2 and 4).** In I, a three-element index set labels sets $E_i$ and a relation $\Gamma$ on their product. Two entries in distinct slots determine a unique entry in the remaining slot. In II, groups $G_i$ act on the sets. The sheet describes each $E_i$ as a bitorseur, reversing one action. It then asks which orbits of the diagonal action have the required properties. The triangle on scan 4 follows the isomorphisms around the three groups. The last three coordinate equations successively determine $g_k$, $g_i$ and $x_k$. The two sheets are separated in scan order by a letter.

**Research-credit letter (scan 3).** The director of U.E.R. N° 5 notifies Grothendieck of a first allocation of 10.450 F. for 1977. The handwritten entry M 022 fills the two spaces for the research code. The letter asks for the order number to follow that code.

**Personnel notice (scan 5).** A circular asks teaching staff to sign the forms for the individual research allowance for 1977. Its printed date is 9 Juin 1976. The deadline in the body is 8 Juillet 1977.

**A functor and its morphisms (scans 7 and 6).** Scan 7 states full faithfulness of the functor from the primed category to the unprimed one. After choosing a point of $\Gamma$, the argument uses triples in $G^3$ satisfying $xyz=1$. A morphism gives three maps $G\to G'$. Scan 6 begins with the conditions on those maps. It sets a variable to 1, identifies the maps with one another and obtains $u(xy)=u(x)u(y)$. The syntax joins the bottom of 7 to the top of 6 even though the transcription follows scan order.

**A cycle of isomorphisms (scan 9).** With a triple of points chosen, three maps $\alpha_i(a_i)$ give the directed triangle between $E_1,E_2,E_3$. The composites of consecutive edges are called $u_3,u_1,u_2$, in that written order. The boxed condition on their product is introduced by “Admettons”. These $u_i$ are maps between the three sets on this sheet.

**Allocation by points (scan 10).** Four researchers have assigned values 5, 3, 5 and 7. The writer applies 25% to the total, boxes “5 points”, and lists a value per point and a fixed component. The hand differs visibly from that of the mathematical notes.

**A multiplication from a relation (scans 11 and 13).** Scan 11 puts a ternary relation on a pointed set $(G,e)$. Its three uniqueness clauses place the unknown in different slots. The three ways of using $e$ define maps $\sigma_i$. The text identifies them with one involution and uses $(x,y,\sigma(xy))\in\Gamma$ to specify multiplication. Scan 13 rewrites the conditions as conditions on a binary operation. It describes bijective translations, a two-sided unit and a two-sided inverse $\sigma x$. The line asking about associativity follows these deductions.

**Meeting minutes (scan 12).** Staff discuss reclassifying service agents doing secretarial work as office technicians. A motion dated by reference to the meeting of 11 Mai 1977 is adopted unanimously. A three-column list names the collective charged with follow-up. The diagonal extends through the list and ends above the time of adjournment.

**The example on sheet A (scan 14).** Here $E_1=E_2=E_3=G$, $\Gamma$ consists of the triples with $xyz=1$, and $abc=1$ supplies a base triple. The sheet writes the three $f_i$ using inverses and the three $g_i$ as composites of pairs of $f$'s. It computes $g_1g_2g_3(x)$ and $f_3f_2f_1(x)$, and ends by transforming the coordinates of a triple. The third defining triple has a visible $e$ where the later calculation uses $c$.

**Changing the parentheses (scans 15 and 16).** Sheet B writes out both parenthesisations of a triple product. Its repeated expansions of $g_1g_2g_3$ keep the parentheses visible. A box singles out $c((ax)b^{-1})=(b^{-1}(xc))a$. Scan 16 substitutes a word for $c$, marks an implication from two product constraints with a question mark, and ends with a framed formula $((bz)(xb))c=b((zx)(bc))$. The substitutions $z=1$ and $b=1$ appear at the foot. The layout records trials as well as claimed equalities.

**Small sets and opposite-facing pencil work (scan 17).** The dark layer tries products on two, three and four named elements. It asks whether $ab=ba$ in one case. The two four-element tables each fill only the $a$ row, with entries $e,c,b$ and $b,c,e$ respectively. Nearby are cyclic rewritings of equations with product 1 and two formulas for $f_i$. The opposite-facing pencil entries compare reassociations and inverses. Their indexed $a$'s are particularly faint.

## Slips

- **Scan 11, condition c).** The third triple reads $(x,\sigma_3x\,e)$. A comma before $e$ would give the same three-slot notation as the two previous lines. Confidence medium.
- **Scan 13, last triple on the left.** The visible expression for $(x,yz,\sigma(x(yz)))$ lacks one of those closing parentheses. Confidence medium.
- **Scan 14, third definition of $f_i$.** The ink reads $(x,f_3(x),e)$. The chosen triple $(a,b,c)$ and the next expression $f_3(x)=x^{-1}c^{-1}$ suggest $c$ was intended. Confidence low concerning that intention.
- **Scans 14–15, the product under $\sigma$.** The box on A reads $\sigma y\,\sigma x$; the side calculation on B gives $\sigma x\cdot\sigma y$. If B is repeating A under the same hypotheses, reversing the two factors would restore agreement. The working does not establish that identification. Confidence low.
- **Scan 12, administrative typescript.** “une société privé” and “Les Professeur présents” would ordinarily read “une société privée” and “Les Professeurs présents”. The body is crossed and the wording is retained. Confidence low as claims about the intended revision.
