## Legibility and editorial decisions

The dates, amounts and text of the university documents are generally distinct. A terminal stroke of the accounting signature and very small typewriter marks remain open. On the mathematical sheets the greatest losses of detail are inside the cancellations on scans 2, 11 and 14–17. The difference between a plain connector and an arrow is often just one pen turn. The lower pencil work on scan 17 is weak enough that even the position of some indices is uncertain.

Scan 3 is inventoried as a letter. The notice and minutes on scans 5 and 12 are inventoried as reused typescripts. Scan 10 is a handwritten list in a visibly different hand. On scan 12 one long diagonal extends through the typed names, and a shorter stroke cancels the t of choisit. On scan 17 two diagonals cross the dark-ink working. That layer is transcribed as cancelled; the entries facing the other way retain their local cancellations. The four scans with administrative content are part of the transcription.
