## What the pages do

**U01 — Admissibility and the theorem-scholie (66–68).** Starting from a uniform left quillénisation, the author calls u admissible when its ordinary right direct image, restricted to fibrant objects, respects the indicated localizers. A shape is admissible when its map to e is. The theorem treats slice tests, finite ordered sets, and a proposed noetherian/sober extension. It then asserts a right adjoint on the localized categories and the slice formula for its values. The corollary announces Der 4 and Der 5 on the domain of finite ordered sets. This continues **K157-4-04 U12**, which had introduced uniform structures and ended by announcing this summary; it addresses the finite-shape part of **K157-4-04 Q04** under the present hypotheses.

**U02 — Finite induction and the fork (68–71).** The proof separates the case of a greatest element, where the written inverse limit is evaluation at that element, from the case of several maximal elements. Two open pieces and their intersection reduce the latter to the outgoing fork. The lemma on manuscript page 63 characterizes fibrant diagrams of that shape by fibrant objects and two arrows in F. The large lifting diagram and its auxiliary square establish the indicated lifting construction. A map of cartesian squares is then invoked to obtain the required W-preservation. The note “C'est la dém. de la prop. 3 p. 50” connects this argument to the earlier arrow criterion recorded in **K157-4-04 U07**.

**U03 — Noetherian extension (72–74).** The author first isolates the smaller portion of the fork-fibrancy hypothesis used by the homotopy-cartesian argument. He then formulates the induction step (α), uses descending opens to explain its sufficiency, and separates irreducible and reducible cases. The latter uses a union of two proper opens and the same fork. The proof of c) applies the topological hypotheses to the indicated slices. This is the continuation of the “sauf erreur” extension on scan 67; the notes do not upgrade it to an independently checked theorem.

**U04 — Descent of adjunction (74–78).** The diagrams compare cofibrant and fibrant subcategories with their localizations. For a cofibrant F and fibrant G′, the author studies how the ordinary adjunction bijection interacts with the quotient relations defining localized Hom sets. Without admissibility he writes a surjective reverse map and adds a doubt about functorial choice in G′. With admissibility restored, he chooses i″ and its adjoint i′, uses the four-object Hom square, and concludes representability and functoriality. This develops the distinction between ordinary and homotopy adjoints highlighted in **K157-4-04 U06/R02**. The wording “adjoint à gauche” at the end is retained and discussed below.

**U05 — Fibre calculation and limits of the theorem (79–81).** The slice restriction calculation closes the theorem: “Cela achève la démonstration du th.-scholie sur les images directes.” The commentary then calls it a “pis-aller”. It asks about infinite discrete categories, the natural-number chain, and two non-poset examples. The proposed extra assumptions include accessible M, exact filtered colimits and preservation of W under them. These are questions in the manuscript, not consequences established by the present transcription.

**U06 — Beginning the dual discussion (81).** The author turns to the desired u_!^W and says the corresponding quillénisation should be exact on the right. The packet ends at the written equation TF=F∩C. The dual construction is not completed within these sixteen scans.
