# Legibility

Scan 102 has faint type but mostly strong handwritten mathematical additions. The coefficient Q alphabet is less secure than the cohomology indices and arrow directions. The diagrams and superscripts were checked again on the second reading; the Q alphabet remains explicitly unresolved.

Scans 121 and 123 are darkest and most heavily corrected. Their overtyped fragments, the cancelled word after base on scan 121, the E-shaped mark before the handwritten theorem qualification on scan 123, and several compressed handwritten words are the least secure readings. The signature on scan 117 is also uncertain; no writer identity is inferred from it. Scans 126–138 are mostly legible, with variable duplication darkness and some faint lines. The raised oval labels on scans 134/136 and the angle letter on scan 134 remain ambiguous. R and Z were examined separately and read as plain typewriter capitals. Classification of small filled-in mathematical strokes as hand or type is less secure than their mathematical shape where the duplicated ink merges them; this is a material limitation of these supplied images. No illegible-word placeholder was needed: difficult text has tentative readings and, where supported, alternatives. All eleven whole images and their three supplied thirds were viewed personally, and all pages underwent an independent crop-first rereading. No OCR, external text, mathematical correction, or compilation was used.
