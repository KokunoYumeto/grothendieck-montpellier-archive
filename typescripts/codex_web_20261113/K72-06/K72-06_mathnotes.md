# Mathematical notes on cote 72, scans 82–97

The scans begin in the middle of an argument and end with a theorem statement. References below use scan numbers and the approximate manuscript lines in the page files. The identifiers also locate the entries in CHECK_EVENTS.tsv.

## What the pages do

**U1 · Invariant subspaces (82–83).** A space $Z$ lies between the span of $h_{i+1},\ldots,h_n$ and the intersection $H_0\cap\cdots\cap H_i$. When they have neighbouring dimensions, $Z$ must be one endpoint. The margin on 82 compares this incidence with invariance and $\beta_i=0$. The proof on 83 uses the alternative $Z\ni h_i$ or $Z\subset H_i$ for each operator, then propagates incidences along the indices. This gives the two endpoint cases and a case where the behaviour changes at $i$. A preceding remark relates irreducibility to nonvanishing parameters and a nondegenerate form.

**U2 · A realization and a flag (84–85).** After the projective title sheet, a regular combinatorial $n$-carte $C$ has automorphism group $G$ and a left torsor $R$ of frames. Choosing a frame gives generators by (1). A projective realization includes an action and a maximal flag; the $i$th generator fixes the $j$th member when $i\ne j$. The proposition lists ways to express nondegeneracy, in terms of the face maps, generation by points, intersection of hyperplanes and movement of the $i$th flag member.

**U3 · Generating points and induction (86).** Starting at the point $s_0$ of $X_0$, the author successively applies the generators to form $s_1,\ldots,s_{n+1}$. The right column states incidence conditions on their centres and hyperplanes. The left margin runs through the dual construction. In the implication diagram the author singles out the passage to e); below it he tries to establish projective independence together with $X_i=\operatorname{Lin}(s_0,\ldots,s_i)$.

**U4 · The incidence calculation (87).** The sheet sets the total intersection of the $H_i$ beside the span of the $h_i$. Partial intersections $Z_{i+1}$ and spans $Y_{i-1}$ form the central ladder. Two boxed maps recover an intermediate flag member either by meeting $X_n$ with $Z_{i+1}$ or by joining $X_0$ with $Y_{i-1}$. The primed and double-primed spaces are written in two columns.

**U5 · Parameterizing flags (88).** Part A restates the equivalent conditions, now for sections of a projective bundle and with conditions on each fibre. Part B gives a local existence condition for an adapted nondegenerate flag. In C, choosing $s_0$ identifies such flags with sections of a line with two specified sections removed. The general index version selects an intermediate $X_{i_0}$ and uses the pencil between $Y_{i-1}$ and $Z_{i+1}$. The successive versions of the two exceptional sections are intertwined in the manuscript.

**U6 · Automorphisms and rigidification (89).** The indicated automorphism group acts on the line $D_0$ with its marked divisor $\Delta_0$. The author identifies the open part $D_0^{**}$ as a torsor. The margin distinguishes the fibres when $c$ and $d$ coincide from those when they differ. The scholium calls a system with a flag a *rigidification*; it then uses the three sections $c,d,s_0$ to normalize coordinates and describes the corresponding torsor in the finite-centre case.

**U7 · From frames to the category of realizations (89–91).** A chosen frame of a regular quasi-polyhedron produces a generator system; changing the frame conjugates it. This explains why the conditions and the marked line are intrinsic to the realization. The pair consisting of a geometric realization and a torsor appears in the categorical description at the foot of 90. The continuation on 91 describes the actions by homotheties and translations according as the two sections are distinct or coincide.

**U8 · Universal coordinates (92).** The span $C$ is chosen as hyperplane at infinity. The affine complement has coordinate points $s_i$ and the vector envelope carries the map sending $\sum\lambda_i s_i$ to $\sum\lambda_i$. The operators are written as identity plus a tensor. The numbered pairing conditions and recursion lead to a normalization with $a_i=s_{i+1}-s_i$ and a triangular expansion of $a'_i$.

**U9 · Cancelled recurrence (93).** The page compares the new vector with earlier ones modulo $F_n$ and $F_{n-1}$ and relates its coefficient to $\lambda_n$. It refers to conditions (5), (8) and (9). The lower corollary proposes a fibrewise vector-bundle version; a second formulation returns to a locally free $E$ and the data $(s_0,\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_n)$. Diagonal cancellation and the shorter local strikes make the status of individual equalities uncertain.

**U10 · Generator calculations (94).** A box collects the generators in the normalized coordinates. The page asks when a generator squares to one, begins an expression for a higher power, expands the product of two adjacent generators and places a coefficient matrix beside a matrix of pairings. Some relevant coefficients were revised more than once.

**U11 · Projective lifting lemma and agenda (95).** The lemma gives a vector pseudo-reflection inducing a projective one in rank at least three. It discusses the quotient action when the centre lies in a projective subbundle, then restricts to an affine complement of a hyperplane. The final four lines of work concern stability, commutation and orders.

**U12 · Vector pseudo-reflections (96–97).** After the second title sheet, Théorème 1 starts with $k$, a $k$-module and $\tau_i=\mathrm{id}+a'_i\otimes a_i$. It defines the iterates $s_i$, the submodules $\mathcal E_i$ and the product $\lambda_i$ of adjacent pairings. Part 1 gives containment and a congruence, the latter under a restriction on $a'_i$. Part 2 relates commutation, fixation of $s_0$ and freeness of the iterates to vanishing pairings, freeness of the generators and invertibility of $\lambda_n$. Part 3 records the generated submodules and which operators preserve them.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Model, low confidence for the exact dimension reading (82–83).** The two endpoint equalities for $Z$ use a dimension difference of one between a span and an intersection. This accounts for the otherwise abrupt « Et par une raison de dim » on 82. The precise final digit of the span dimension remains marked in the transcription; the general dimension observation is conditional on the bounds differing by one.

**R2 · Model, low confidence as a reading of the revision (94).** Using the three entries $1,\lambda_i,1+\alpha_i$ of the boxed row, the coordinate differences give $\lambda_i-1$ and $1+\alpha_i-\lambda_i$. These are the diagonal and upper-right entries of the lower matrix. This multiplication does not determine which term was under the cancellation beneath the product.

**R3 · Model, high confidence for the calculation (97).** The meaning of « libre » matters over a general ring. For $k=\mathbb Z$, $n=0$, take $s_0=e_1$, $a_0=e_2$ in $\mathbb Z^3$, and $a'_0=(2,0,1)$. Formula (1) gives an automorphism of determinant one and $s_1=(1,2,0)$. The vectors $s_0,s_1$ are independent over $\mathbb Z$, yet $\lambda_0=2$ is a nonunit. Their reductions coincide modulo 2. Consequently ordinary linear independence over the ring is insufficient for the stated implication to (13).

**R4 · Model, low confidence as interpretation (93, 97).** The phrase « fibre par fibre » on 93 suggests testing freeness in every fibre as one possible interpretation of the family in (9). It excludes the example in R3. Scan 97 does not explicitly define « libre », and this observation establishes no general equivalence.

**Reading question · Scan 86, L10–17.** The frozen margin ends the incidence condition with $\{c'\}=D\cap C$; the preceding, overwritten letter was read as $d$. The mark on the small letter inside braces might also belong to a corrected $d$. On 87 the corresponding intersection is labelled $d$. The letter in braces was outside the frozen doubt mark and its transcription remains as recorded.
