# K161-2-03 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**37 · Charts of ring conditions.** Several versions of an implication diagram place local, strictly local, reduced, integral, connected and compact-spectrum rings alongside kinds of fields. Smaller sketches test the conditions with polynomials, idempotents and the loci \(V(f^n)\). The lower graph has a tally beside it. Some of its links are unheaded.

**38 · Open subtoposes and sieves.** Beside an example involving \(X_\infty\), the page puts a subtopos \(B'\), a subobject \(S\) of \(e_B\), sieves on \(R\) and \(B\), and a distinguished substructure \(T'\) into correspondence. It describes \(S\) using \(i_!\) of a final object, then asks what an \(S\)-restricted model looks like over a varying topos. The inverted lines introduce a separate layer of calculations.

**39 · Locality, strict locality and Henselian locality.** Starting from the presheaf presentation of rings, items 2, 4 and 3 take up the Zariski, étale and Henselian covering tests in that written order. For strict locality the page gives a universal monic polynomial and localizes by its derivative. For Henselian locality it tests covers on local Henselian rings.

**39 · Ultralocal rings.** Item 5 proposes an fppf presentation and examines a converse via local homomorphisms into ultralocal rings. The proposed lemma specifies an inductive limit of faithfully flat algebras of finite type.

**39 · Reduced and integral rings.** Item 6 compares reduction with kernels of power maps and a topology tested on reduced objects. Item 7 draws the zero-product square, states a separate condition excluding \(0=1\), and describes covering on reduced irreducible components. A final equalizer-like diagram compares sections on components and their pairwise products over the base.

**40 · Irreducibility.** Item 8 asks for the reduction by the nilradical to be integral. The zero-product square is modified by replacing the zero locus with the nilradical. The covering test uses irreducible components. The writer distinguishes coherence of a subtopos from the finite-subcover behaviour of the chosen topology.

**40 · Connectedness.** Item 9 tests idempotents against 0 and 1, first locally and then in a universal diagram labelled « projecteurs ». It relates covers split on connected components to presheaves taking certain sums to products and treats the nonempty case separately.

**40–42 · Dimension zero and the zero ring.** Item 10 presents a covering by the invertible and nilpotent loci, including a universal formula. Scan 41 is an almost empty sheet with a cancelled, inverted fragment identifying a punctual subtopos with the zero ring. Scan 42 resumes with a covering test on Artinian local rings and a dimension-zero qualification.

**42 · Pseudofields and points.** Item 11 gives an epimorphism involving \(A^*\) and \(0_A\), a fibrewise-section criterion and a presentation involving \(R_{\rm cons}\). A cancelled continuation tries several descriptions of fields and asks how to test intersections of classifying subtoposes on points.

**43 · Compact spectra and compact reduced rings.** Item 12 states an idempotent criterion for each \(f\). A universal family \(D_n\) records three elements \(g,f,e\) and covers \(A\). Item 13 specializes the discussion to a product involving \(A_f\) and \(A/fA\), then describes a topology through sections fibre by fibre. The order of the factors in one vanishing condition matters below.

**43–44 · Separable and algebraic closures.** Items 14 and 15 distinguish separably closed fields and algebraically closed fields. One description uses separable points over the fibres and an étale topology. The next uses surjectivity and finite surjective families on the indicated category.

**44–45 · A specified base ring.** Item 16 first imposes \(n1_A=0\) and then inverts \(n\). An open-subtopos description accompanies each construction. The generalization to \(\Lambda\)-algebras uses a monomorphism to a final object and descriptions by representable functors. The diagram on scan 45 compares the functor categories and base change; the examples include characteristic zero as an intersection indexed by \(n\geq2\).

**45–46 · Subobjects of the final object.** A more general category \(R\) with finite limits leads to a sieve or subterminal \(S\), left-exact functors and the slice presentation \(\widehat R_{/S}\). In the central display of scan 46, inverse image uses product with \(S\); direct image is described by a Hom functor.

**46 · A filtered family.** For a decreasing family \(S_\alpha\), the writer intersects the associated conditions on models, first tests set-valued models and then asks for the small classifying category. A long rotated note proposes a category built from the slices \(R_{/S_\alpha}\) and discusses the functors embedding the resulting subtoposes.

**47 · Perfection.** Item 17 requires \(p1_A=0\) and that \(x\mapsto x^p\) be an isomorphism. It describes the site by perfect closures and splitting after perfect closure of the base. Items 18 and 19 add compactness and then the field condition; radicial points appear in the fibrewise descriptions.

**47–48 · Varying characteristic.** A first version of item 20, cancelled on scan 47, decomposes the base into pieces of specified characteristic. Scan 48 starts the item again. It asks that a ring be locally of positive prime characteristic or of characteristic zero, writes the corresponding covering family, and points out the infinite limit involved on the characteristic-zero side.

**48–49 · Gluing two subtoposes.** For \(V,W\subset B\), the issue is whether local factorization can be classified by one subtopos of \(B\). Overlapping ovals depict two opens and their common part. Scan 49 formulates a criterion in terms of opens in \(V\) and \(W\); its sufficiency argument glues them and singles out an embedding still to verify. The example applies this construction to positive characteristic and characteristic zero.

**49–50 · Adding properties characteristic by characteristic.** The discussion passes from the amalgamated description of the characteristic pieces to prescribed subtoposes \(Z(p)\). Item 21 separates the pieces according to whether the zero-ring point occurs and states a criterion for having enough points.

**50 · A bound on connected components.** Item 22 seeks a local decomposition into at most \(n\) connected factors using \(n\) orthogonal idempotents of sum 1. It writes their equations, draws universal diagrams over \(\Pi_n\), and proposes a largest subobject defined by an epimorphism condition. The last words concern change of base.

## Slips

**S43 · Scan 43, item 13, L30.** The sheet has \(e(1-f)=0\). The preceding item puts the nilpotence condition on \((1-e)f\). In the reduced case the apparent intended vanishing is \((1-e)f=0\). The difference is visible even in \(\mathbf F_3\): with \(f=2\) and \(e=1\), \(ef\) is a unit, \((1-e)f=0\), and \(e(1-f)=2\). Confidence is high for this slip. This calculation checks the disputed factor order only.

**S43-label · Scan 43, headings 12 and 13, L4, L26.** Both headings display \(X_{\rm cpct}\), although the second heading adds « réduit ». An extra qualifier distinguishing the second case may have been intended. Confidence is low in any particular replacement subscript.

**S48 · Scan 48, lower paragraph, L44.** The local factorization reads « par \(B\) ou \(B'\) ». The two introduced subtoposes are \(V,W\), and the subsequent overlap is \(V\cap W\). The apparent intended pair is \(V,W\). Confidence is low about the intended correction, since part of the intervening factorization is doubtful.

**S48-prime · Scan 48, bottom display, L61.** The line gives \(Z'=Z'\cap V'=Z'\times_Z V\). The last factor suggests \(Z'\cap V\) in the middle. The small prime is close to the letter and to other raised strokes in this crowded line. Confidence is low.

**S50-agreement · Scan 50, item 21, L8.** « le topos ponctuelle » follows replacement of « la catégorie » by « le topos ». The earlier feminine noun explains the visible ending. The agreement with the new noun would be « le topos ponctuel ». Confidence is low about the course of the revision because it rests on a cancellation and insertion.

**S50-index · Scan 50, item 22, L12 and lower diagram L28.** In \(A_j=A/\sum_{j\ne i}e_jA\), \(j\) is free on the left and bound on the right, where \(i\) is free. Keeping the displayed name \(A_j\) gives the apparent intended quotient \(A/\sum_{i\ne j}e_iA\). The same crossing of indices appears in the quotient over \(A_{\Pi_n}\). For the two coordinate idempotents in \(\mathbf F_2\times\mathbf F_3\), removing the ideal of the other coordinate gives quotients of sizes 2 and 3, respectively. Confidence is high for the first occurrence. The smaller, retraced index in the diagram carries less weight on its own.
