# Mathematical notes · Cote 77, scans 2–17

All references are to the supplied sheets. Identifiers connect individual checks with CHECK_EVENTS.tsv. The symbols below follow the first readings in the transcription.

## What the pages do

**Scans 2–3. Rank-two forms.** A title sheet opens a list of categories over $S$. The first objects are regular rank-two quadratic modules $(V,q)$. The next description uses a rank-two étale algebra $A$, an invertible $A$-module $V$, and a section $\xi$ attached to its norm.

**Scans 3–4. Other descriptions of the same data [A1].** The list moves through a form of $\mathrm G_m$ and a torsor, a projective-line bundle with an étale divisor of degree two, a plane conic and a transverse line, an affine conic, an orthogonal triple with a rank-two subbundle, and an étale subalgebra of a rank-four Azumaya algebra. The sideways entries use a maximal torus in a form of $\mathrm{GP}(1)$ or $\mathrm{SL}(2)$. The tenth entry uses torsors under a group written with $\mathbb Z/2$ and $\mathrm G_m$.

**Scan 4. Trivialising the square of a torsor [A2].** The Kummer sequence leads from a trivialisation of $T^{\wedge2}$ to a reduction to $\mu_2$, an orbit, and a covering $X\to X'$ with a section off the divisor. The last paragraph describes triples $(A,L,\varepsilon)$ with a trivialisation of $L^{\otimes2}$. In the margin the author objects to the proposed Zariski-local triviality over a real field.

**Scans 5–6. Three reflections and a quaternion algebra [A3].** Fixed loci of $\tau_0,\tau_1,\tau_2$ are related to three points $h_i$ and their orthogonal lines $L_i$. Conditions A–D concern commutation, independence, and the intersection $H_1\cap H_2$ with respect to the determinant quadric. A second diagram connects reflections, points off the conic, non-isotropic lines, degree-two divisors, planes, and rank-two étale subalgebras.

**Scan 6. From conjugation to a reflection [A4].** Conjugation by $\sigma$ has eigenvalues $1,-1$, both with multiplicity two in the calculation. The author composes it with $u\mapsto u^*=\operatorname{Tr}(u)1-u$ to obtain the boxed formula $\widetilde\sigma(u)=u-\operatorname{Tr}(u\sigma)\sigma$.

**Scans 7–9. Dihedral quasi-invariants [A5].** After a second cover the author writes $\sigma,\sigma',u$ in coordinates, decomposes $E=D_1\oplus D_2\oplus D_0$, and lists six summands of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(\check E)$. For distinct weights, $\sigma$ exchanges four eigenlines and acts trivially on a two-dimensional summand. The case $\zeta^3=1$ introduces two spaces exchanged by $\sigma$ and yields the same conclusion on the invariant lines.

**Scans 9–10. The square [B1].** Under the stated invertibility assumption, $\zeta^4=1$ leads to $\zeta^2=-1$ and $\alpha=0$. The author lists the remaining weights and the signs by which $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ act. In square coordinates he exhibits $x^2-y^2$ and $xy$, draws the labelled vertices, and compares the values of the two forms there.

**Scans 10–12. The invariant conic [B2].** The degenerate conic $x^2-y^2=0$ is a union of diagonals. The next calculation uses the form $Q=az^2+bxy$ after quadratic base change. Passing through the polygon vertices gives $a+b=0$ and the conic $z^2-xy=0$. In affine coordinates the sheet writes $xy-1$ and, with a different normalisation, $xy$ equal to 1 on the vertices.

**Scans 12–13. Five points and one minor [B3].** Evaluating the general polynomial $ax^2+bxy+cy^2+ux+vy+\lambda$ at $(\zeta^i,\zeta^{-i})$ for $-2\le i\le2$ yields a matrix with two identical columns. The author asks for rank five, writes the resulting determinant $\Delta$, and identifies factors supported on third and fourth roots of unity. The margin calls for further study of its vanishing.

**Scan 14. A second coordinate calculation [B4].** A damaged opening links five consecutive vertices to all vertices. The translation-space form is $x^2+y^2-\alpha xy$ with discriminant $\alpha^2-4$. For the ternary form the author gives a symmetric matrix, its discriminant $-2(\alpha+2)$, and its divided discriminant $-(\alpha+2)$.

**Scans 15–16. Choosing an isotropic point [B5].** A slope satisfies $\zeta^2-\alpha\zeta+1=0$. Choosing it factors the binary form, and the ternary form acquires the additional term $-(x+y)z$. A point $d_\zeta$ on the conic determines a pencil of lines and the identification with $\mathbb P(E/D_\zeta)$. The sheets sketch the tangent, introduce basis vectors $f_i$, and calculate a slope $1/(\zeta-1)$ in the quotient.

**Scans 16–17. Cancelled construction [A6].** A crossed draft proposes giving a polygon with an isotropic line by a unit $\zeta$, bundle data involving $L$ and $F$, and a section of $\mathbb P(F)$. The next sheet describes automorphisms fixing a relative degree-two divisor pointwise. It calls the resulting group smooth of relative dimension one and its neutral component commutative. A bare third item ends the sheet.

## Remarks of the model

- **M1, scan 6. Model remark, high confidence in this example.** For $\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ in $2\times2$ matrices, both expressions for $\widetilde\sigma$ exchange the diagonal entries of $u$ and preserve its off-diagonal entries. This checks the boxed formula in a split example.
- **M2, scan 10. Model remark, high confidence in the evaluation.** At the four vertices in the written order, $x^2-y^2$ gives $(0,0,0,0)$ and $xy$ gives $(1,-1,1,-1)$. The heavy mark immediately before the manuscript's 0 stays uncertain.
- **M3, scans 12–13. Model remark, high confidence in the calculation for a formal unit $t$.** Taking $t$ for the first reading of the root parameter, the five-column minor in the written order is $(t-1)^{10}(t+1)^4(t^2+1)(t^2+t+1)^2/t^{10}$. This gives the multiplicities behind the question at the foot of scan 13.
- **M4, scans 14–15. Model remark, high confidence in the algebra.** The determinant of the nine entries in (5) is $-2(\alpha+2)$. Expanding the two factors on scan 15 gives the stated translation-space form when $\alpha=t+t^{-1}$. Subtracting $(x+y)z$ gives the ternary expression used there. The overwritten signs are still uncertain as readings.
- **M5, scan 16. Model remark, low confidence as a reading question.** In the frozen NB row the last subscript in $e_3=f_2$ reads 2. A 3 would agree with the coordinate vector two lines above. The small horizontal foot looks like the author's 2, and the surrounding indices show overwriting. The notes do not amend the row.
