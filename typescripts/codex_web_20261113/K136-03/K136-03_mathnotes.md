# Mathematical notes · Cote 136, scans 362–376

## What the pages do

**362 · Shifting symmetric powers.** Three rows beginning at zero use the symmetric, exterior and divided powers of the terms of a simplicial sequence. The boxed outcome compares $L_r\operatorname{Sym}^iK(M,n+1)$ with $L_{r-i}\Lambda^iK(M,n)$. Beneath it the author writes the shifts for $X[1]$ and $X[2]$. The heavily cancelled formulas above the sequences belong to an earlier attempt.

**363 · Grading by weight.** The Dold–Puppe comparison puts derived symmetric powers beside the free abelian group construction. It gives the author a grading on the homology of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. He qualifies the comparison for a complex concentrated in a single degree.

**363–364 · From a space to an operation.** The infinite symmetric product computes homology through its homotopy groups. Applying this to a Moore space leads to a suspension comparison in the range $r<n$. The next sheet uses $K(\mathbb F_p,n)$ to represent cohomology. A square records how the members of a family of operations commute with suspension.

**364–365 · Operations and admissible words.** The odd-prime list gives the degrees of $P^i$ and $\beta$. The next page gives the corresponding list of squares at 2. The Cartan formula describes the effect on a product. The author then writes the exponents in a word $P^I$, its excess, and the inequality for admissibility. He states Serre’s description in cohomology and its divided-power counterpart in homology.

**366 · Two methods of calculation.** The first starts with the classifying-space spectral sequence. Its $E_2$ term is a Tor group over the homology algebra at level $n$. The bar diagrams compare products of groups with tensor products of group rings. The second method displays two cohomology factors and convergence to the cohomology of a point.

**367 · The dual algebra and its coproduct.** The Milnor formulas describe the generators $\xi_j$ and $\tau_j$ and give their coproducts. A separate group of formulas singles out the weights $p^s$ for derived symmetric powers. At the foot, a pairing $A\otimes B\to C$ induces a map on the smash product of two Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. The author identifies the corresponding cohomology map with comultiplication.

**368–369 · The bar construction.** For an augmented differential graded algebra, scan 368 lays out consecutive tensor powers, then draws the group-ring example. The labels $\mu$ and $1\otimes\varepsilon$ distinguish two face maps. The last diagram passes through $\bar B(A\otimes A)$ to give a product on $\bar B(A)$. Scan 369 is an unwritten ruled face.

**370 · Algebraic K-theory.** A diagram places the Steinberg and elementary groups between $K_2(R)$ and $K_1(R)$. The second part moves to a scheme $X$ and the spectral sequence from Zariski cohomology of K-sheaves to $K_{j-i}(X)$. The diagonal term is expressed using cycles modulo rational equivalence. The three resolutions end in sums over points. A final quotient leaves the argument of its denominator blank.

**371 · Changing coefficients.** Page I lists the four homology groups obtained by choosing $\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb F_p$ as space parameter and as coefficient. In the stable range the examples at $n+2$ and $n+3$ give $G/2G$ and $\,{}_2G$. The examples at $n+4$ and $n+5$ add the corresponding terms at 3. Two Bockstein maps act on the algebra generated by the $\xi$ and $\tau$. The final diagram relates the two mixed-coefficient cases by conjugation.

**372 · Cartan’s interchange.** Page II names a symmetry between the group parameter in $K(G,n)$ and the coefficient group. The author compares $K(\mathbb Z,n)\wedge X$ with a reduced free abelian group on the suspension of $X$. An Eilenberg–Zilber map and a partial homology theory enter the argument. A boxed example refers to the diagram marked $(*)$ on page I. The final line invokes a permutation of the two smash factors.

**373 · Canonical resolutions.** Iterating the free abelian group construction gives a simplicial resolution. The central comparison maps $K(\mathbb Z,n)\wedge K(G,p)$ to $\mathbb ZK(G,n+p)$, with the written bound $r=\min(n+2p-1,2n+p-1)$. The long diagram at the foot applies the comparison when there is a further $K(\mathbb Z,m)$ factor.

**374 · Right derived symmetric powers.** The operation of degree zero retains a coefficient endomorphism $F$. The author interprets cohomology operations using maps into $K(F,n)$ and identifies $H^n(K(G_a,m),G_a)$ with $R^n\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb F_p[m])$. A theorem describes the algebra generated by admissible words. Its proof sketch reduces to $K(G_a,1)$, then climbs the classifying-space tower using Borel and Leray. The Witt extension supplies a degree-two class. The last lines compare finite-field evaluations with polynomials of degree $<p^h$ in each variable.

**375–376 · Self-extensions of the additive group.** The $F$-torsion proposition and its corollary for the perfect site precede a universal-coefficient spectral sequence in sheaves of $\mathbb F_p$-modules. The author sets $X=K(G_a,n)$ with $n$ large and singles out its homology in degree $n$. On the next sheet, two lemmas describe the edge map and the image of a generator. The proposed output gives zero in odd degrees $2i+1$ for $i>0$ and $k[F]/(F^d)$ in degree $2i$, with $d=v_p(i)$. A second calculation uses a resolution, its dual tensor diagram, and the expression $\operatorname{Tor}^{A_*}(\mathscr A_*,\mathbb F_p)$.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scan 365 · High confidence.** A degree check leaves the author’s question open. On this sheet $\deg\mathrm{Sq}^i=i$, so the left side and both summands in the questioned relation have degree 3. This calculation tests only the grading.

**R2 · Scan 376 · High confidence, conditional on the displayed formula.** At $p=3$, the indices $i=1,3,9$ have valuations $0,1,2$. The displayed quotients in degrees $2,6,18$ would therefore be $0$, $k[F]/(F)$ and $k[F]/(F^2)$ respectively. These are numerical instances of the proposed formula.

**R3 · Scans 374 and 376 · High confidence.** There are two distinct pieces of information about degree zero. On 374 the coefficient operation is written $P_R^0=P^0\otimes F$, and the presentation of $\mathscr A$ expressly omits the relation $P^0=1$. On 376 the map from $\mathscr A$ to ordinary $A$ sends $P^0$ to 1. Reading the two displayed maps together explains how the coefficient factor can persist in the calculation.

**R4 · Scans 371–372 · High confidence.** The square labelled $(*)$ on page I links two mixed choices of coefficients. Page II explicitly calls its boxed diagram an interpretation of $(*)$ and places a factor swap across its upper row. This identifies the example to which the author applies the interchange.
