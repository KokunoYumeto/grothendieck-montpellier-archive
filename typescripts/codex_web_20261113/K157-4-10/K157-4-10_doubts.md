In the cardinal estimates, a line over a whole category of objects or arrows is usually wavy. In the Quillen material, a wave over the left class and a bar over the right class are distinguishable, especially on scans 157–160. Short, almost horizontal instances are recorded separately below. Φ has a loop intersected by an upright with a foot. Ψ has two raised arms and a descending stem. The initial T of TC and TF shares a long horizontal with the following capital. All these samples are manuscript letters. The manuscript’s two little quotation strokes are encoded as a pair of backticks and a pair of apostrophes.

## Legibility

The shafts of the implication diagram on 159, the order of most class letters, and the difference between the long waves and straight bars are especially clear. The hatching over the bottom of 157, the interleaved old and new text of the sideways corollary on 162, and the overwritten powers and numerals on 148–153 are less secure. On the last line of 163, the first compound and the adjoining relation need comparison with the original leaf. Short pen lifts can look like a prime, an arrowhead or an abbreviated ending.
