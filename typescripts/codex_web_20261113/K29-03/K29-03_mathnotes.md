# Mathematical notes on cote 29, scans 203–207 and 209–217

## What the pages do

**Reconstruction from a category and a functor, scans 203–204.** The first sheet asks how to recover $(R_S,r_S)$ from $(C,s)$. For an object $X$ the author considers a subcategory $C_X$ and assigns to $M$ a relative étale covering over $s(M)$. These coverings give a cartesian section. For a sieve $C_0$ he constructs a comparison functor $\alpha$ from $R_S^{C_0}$ to a limit of categories over $C_0$. Varying the admissible sieve gives the proposed formula for $R_S$. The margin on 204 adds an epimorphism assumption.

**Lifting decompositions, scans 204–207.** After the test $\alpha(X)=\emptyset\Rightarrow X=\emptyset$, the author starts with $Z=\alpha(X)=Z_1\amalg Z_2$. He cuts up $X\times\varphi(M)$ and asks whether its first summand descends. The two inverse images along $T\rightrightarrows\varphi(M)$ are compared after adjoining an object and using sections of a locally constant object. The surviving ending uses local filteredness to compare choices of $M$. Scan 206 also preserves a cancelled comparison by intersections and complements.

**Conditions A and B, scan 209.** A describes $C$ by a topos and its locally constant finite objects, by a topology on $C$, and by local trivialisation through families of effective universal descent. Another formulation is referred to sheet 1 bis. B adds a condition on the final object, expressed as a sum of connected objects. The earlier formulation inside the struck box explicitly uses the word « finie ».

**Axioms and the proof of A, scans 210–211.** The list G0–G5 concerns size, finite limits and sums, factorisation, connected summands and a numerical bound after base change. Several versions occupy the same lines. A product of Galois categories and a fibre functor on each component also occur. For A the author writes the implications among (iii), (ii bis), (ii) and (i), then decomposes the final object by the rank of $X$. The object of isomorphisms from the standard $i$-element object is denoted $P_i$. Over $P_i$ he writes $X_{P_i}\simeq[1,i]_{P_i}$.

**Proof attempts for B, scans 211–212.** A cancelled passage compares decompositions of the final object, gives a bound on the number of connected components of a cover in terms of its rank and considers a sum of classifying topoi. The live argument returns to producing an effective descent cover on which $X$ becomes constant finite. A second cancelled paragraph tries a stationary family of subobjects. The surviving reduction uses a connected $Y$, a connected $X$ above it and induction on the integer in G5. A remark asks whether G5 is needed.

**Degree axioms, scan 213.** On sheet 1 bis the author asks for classes of maps of each degree $n$. The clauses concern base change, sums, isomorphisms, splitting the target by degree, positive-degree descent and the empty degree-zero source. Separate circled clauses treat monomorphisms as universal summands, finite limits and size. A proof of A(iii) reduces to fixed degree. It handles degree zero, then begins induction.

**Fibred powers and partitions, scan 214.** This unnumbered sheet writes $X^E$ as a union indexed by partitions $P$ and describes the inverse image of a diagonal under $f^E$. For three indices it writes out the intermediate partitions, the three kinds of terms in the union and their dimension labels. It then asks how closures of the loci meet diagonals and other closures. The small drawings show two intersecting regions, two sources over a common target and a quadrilateral cut by parallel lines. The writing and subject differ from the adjoining inset leaves.

**The induction step and its corollary, scan 215.** On sheet 1 ter the square with $Y'=X$ exhibits a diagonal summand of $X'$. Its complement $X'_1$ has degree $n-1$. The author applies the inductive hypothesis to that complement and writes the resulting product with $[1,n]$. The paragraph « Explicitation » sets out a criterion involving effective epimorphisms and effective equivalence relations. The corollary characterises degree $n$ by trivialisation after an epimorphic base change.

**The connected case, scan 216.** Corollary C adds connectedness of the final object to the earlier descriptions. One clause asks for a conservative functor to finite sets, left exact and commuting with finite sums. The last gives a profinite group $\pi$ and an equivalence $C\simeq C(\pi)$.

**Exact functors, scan 217.** The proposition compares faithfulness, conservativity and the detection of the empty object. Next it tests full faithfulness with the map on subobjects. The marginal addition concerns stability of the image. The last clause characterises equivalence using full faithfulness and subordination of objects in the target to objects in the image.

## Remarks of the model

**M1 · Model remark, high confidence in the count.** A three-element set has exactly three partitions into two nonempty blocks. The three pairs of blocks on scan 214 exhaust them. This count was checked by enumerating set partitions. The author’s intended label for $P_1$ is a separate question, with low confidence because of the cancellation.

**M2 · Model remark, high confidence in the textual connection.** The last line of 213 ends « supposant que », and 215 starts « c'est prouvé pour les entiers < n ». Together the two numbered inserts give one continuous induction. Their line endings and folios identify the join across the unnumbered sheet.

**M3 · Model remark, low confidence about the sign.** In scan 212, last line, the frozen reading before the empty set was $\ne$ or $=$. The proposal concerns detection of a connected component, which favours $\ne$. The short crossing between the bars is too faint to establish that choice. The comparison did not settle it.
