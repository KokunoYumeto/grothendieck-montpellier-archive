# Mathematical notes · scans 115–130

## What the pages do

**115 · A fibrewise comparison.** The opening finishes a base-change argument. In c) the author asks whether the displayed map from \(\Phi\) also gives a \(W\)-equivalence on fibres. He reduces this to the comparison of \(X_s\) with \({}_{s\backslash}X\) for smooth \(X\) over \(S\), then invokes \(S\mapsto S^\circ\) for the other side. The outstanding issue is the full faithfulness of \(i,j\).

**115–117 · Two localization functors.** The identity of a category can induce a new functor when the same category carries two classes of equivalences. The author displays a four-category chain for each of the two fine classes \(W_S^d,W_S^g\). Both end at \(\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}\), which also carries \(W_S\). The resulting functors from the two fine localizations to the coarse one are denoted \(\rho,\sigma\).

**117–119 · Functors in the reverse direction.** The map from \(\mathrm{Parf}\,S\) to \(\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}\) yields \(i\) or \(j\), according to the localization. The diagram on 118 compares them with a quasi-inverse called \(C\). The component \(X\to C(X)\) accounts for the displayed isomorphisms \(\rho i\simeq\mathrm{id}\) and \(\sigma j\simeq\mathrm{id}\). On 119 the author also gives his definition of a localization functor using the arrows it takes to isomorphisms.

**120–122 · Finding the adjunction maps.** Write \(A=\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}\), \(\Sigma_0=W_S^d\), \(\Sigma=W_S\). The map \(i_X:X\to C(X)\) is available, but the projection tried on 121 does not give an \(S\)-morphism. The lower part of 121 is a cancelled alternative. On 122 the author reverses the proposed comparison arrows and sets \(p_X=i_X^{-1}\) in \(A\Sigma^{-1}\). This leaves two triangle identities.

**123–124 · The remaining identity and the path construction (C2).** The first identity is immediate in the coarse localization. The second becomes \(i_{C(X)}=C(i_X)\) in the finer localization. The two path diagrams put an empty path on opposite sides of \(c\). The author chooses a path construction with units on both sides and defines \(r(x,c,c')=(x,c'\circ c)\). He observes that both composites with \(r\) are identities, then uses \(C(i_X)\in W_S^d\) to finish the lemma.

**125–127 · Théorème-Scholie, I–III (C1).** Part I treats the coarse localization of the 13 categories in the earlier diagram. Part II treats the two chains and tests their fine equivalences by slices. Part III compares the three classes with fibrewise equivalence on the internal categories, then distinguishes what happens on the two boundaries and at \(\mathrm{Cat}_{/S}\). Its fork displays the three localized categories together.

**127–128 · Part IV.** The two Hom comparisons give the adjunctions \(\rho\dashv i\) and \(\sigma\dashv j\), in that order. Scan 128 opens with the underlined conclusion that \(i,j\) are fully faithful. An initial attempt to describe the essential image is crossed out.

**128–130 · Part V and the essential image.** Taking the homotopy type of a fibre or slice gives functors with sources \(S^\circ\) and \(S\), respectively. Scan 129 states the local-system criterion for the common essential image and writes a factorization through the fundamental groupoid \(\Pi S\). The following note compares the two conditions for smooth, proper and \(\mathrm{Fibhot}\) objects. For two objects coming from a common object, the functors agree on objects up to the indicated comparison and act on arrows by inverse isomorphisms.

**130 · The enlarged diagram and VI (C3).** The drawing adds \(\mathrm{Liss}_0 S\) and \(\mathrm{Prop}_0 S\). The older terms “bordantes” and “internes” give way to “rez de chaussée” and “premier étage”. The last paragraph identifies the squares as cartesian and describes the exterior functors from split structures to the corresponding categories with unsplit structures.

## Remarks of the model

- **R1 · Model observation, high confidence for this example.** For a two-edge path \(c=(u,v)\), the ordered pairs \((c,())\) and \(((),c)\) differ, and both concatenate to \((u,v)\). This is the distinction visible in the two diagrams of 124. The example checks the placement of the empty paths.
- **R2 · Model observation, high confidence for the transcribed graph.** The solid-arrow graph on 130 has 15 vertices and 22 edges. Counting pairs of distinct two-edge paths with common endpoints gives seven quadrilaterals. This agrees with one reading of the inserted numeral. The count addresses the shape of the picture alone. Cartesianity is the author's further claim.
