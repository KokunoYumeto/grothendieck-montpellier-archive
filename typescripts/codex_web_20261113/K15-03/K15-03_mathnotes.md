## What the pages do

**Scan 87, VI.5.** The text passes from a local condition on a map of finitely generated modules over an integrally closed noetherian domain to its double dual. Theorem VI.5 realizes a locally free sheaf on $Y$ as the restriction of a finitely generated reflexive module under the stated hypotheses. The proof takes sections on $U$ and on $Y$, defines $N^{**}$, and reaches the map $\delta:N\to N^{**}$ at the page break.

**Scan 88, quotient gerbe.** The note forms $L'=\operatorname{corad}(L)$, describes a quotient gerbe with commutative link, and identifies the connected part of a kernel attached to $H$ as a compact torus. Below the double rule it takes up a category of motives and a multiplicative group $G$ with an element $f$, called “frobenius”, that generates it in the sense of Zariski. The target of the tensorial construction is difficult to read.

**Scan 89, Remarque 3.13.** The remark takes a subset $E$ that remains “très dense” after étale base change. The fibre functors at the indicated points form a conservative family. The text gives three choices of points, using closed points of a Jacobson prescheme, points closed in their fibres, and points with finite closure in a locally noetherian prescheme. Several relation signs in the typewritten formulas have blank spaces in their positions.

**Scans 90 and 92, three descriptions and a weight condition.** The opening list passes between a group $T$ with a generator, a Galois-stable subgroup $M$ and a category of vector spaces with an operator. A map $j$ provides degrees, and a character $\varepsilon$ enters the absolute-value formula. The second list juxtaposes compactness modulo the image of $j$, the sizes of the elements of $M$, and the sizes of eigenvalues on a degree-$i$ object. Scan 92 argues from (a) to (c) by writing a positive power of $f$ as $u\,j(x)$, using compactness for $u$, and comparing powers after applying $\varepsilon$. A question mark sits beside a cancelled discussion of the converse.

**Scans 93 and 91, descent.** In the order of the typescript’s own foliation, 42 then 43, the diagram starting with $G$ characterizes the descended sheaf. The author compares the map to $F'$ after a change of base admitting a section, treats proper maps by a later base-change result, and localizes in the quasi-finite case. For case (d) the base is reduced to a point, then to the spectrum of a field. A pen addition on scan 91 closes the proof of 9.4.

**Scan 94, conjugate absolute values.** The sheet introduces $Q'$ as a totally real closure, relates a condition on powers of $\lambda$ in $Q'(i)$ to the common absolute value of its conjugates, and states a corollary about $\lambda_\alpha\bar\lambda_\alpha$. In the proposition the author sets these conditions beside realization as an eigenvalue in a finite-dimensional group representation and as the image of an element of a commutative Riemann algebra. The left margin treats the real case and contains a separately cancelled example using $p^{1/4}$.

**Scans 99, 97 and 95, regular elements and rational conjugacy.** In their own order, pages −4−, −5− and −7−, the sheets first describe a normal form $N$, fundamental characters and a differential criterion for regularity. They then discuss when the construction descends to a perfect field and when semisimple conjugacy classes over that field have rational representatives. Theorem 1.10 widens the rational-representative statement under a dimension assumption. Section 2 begins by fixing notation for the identity component, centralizer, semisimple and unipotent parts, rank and root subgroups. The sentence in 2.1 continues outside this batch.

**Scan 96, norm-one element.** In a quadratic extension $L/K$, two primes $p,p'$ over a split prime give rise to a principal multiple of their difference. The proof chooses $y$ with the specified divisor, puts $u=y\,\sigma y$, and constructs $x=y^2/u$. Its displayed divisor is $2h(p-p')$. The NB below changes to the totally real/totally imaginary setting and examines an algebraic integer satisfying $\lambda\bar\lambda=p^i$. Its conclusion uses a root of unity.

**Scan 98, valuations of units.** The sheet associates weight $i$ to an absolute value $q^{i/2}$, separates an element $u$ satisfying $u\bar u=1$ in a square case, and collects valuations over $p$ into a map on $U$. It describes a finite kernel and a finite-index image in terms of trace-zero divisors. Choosing one prime above each split prime gives the last lattice description, up to a finite group. The definition of the indexing set continues beyond the last line.

**Scan 100, a proposed existence criterion.** The first three conditions on a Frobenius eigenvalue concern algebraic integrality, membership in a field, and an expression involving an absolute unit away from $p$. The boxed reformulation uses all conjugates. Four diagonal lines cancel a proposed fourth valuation condition, and an arrow from a circled annotation calls it a consequence of 2 and 3. The author names the weight, compares it for $q$ and $q'=q^m$, and conjectures that the necessary conditions suffice.

**Scan 101, a projectivity criterion.** Chapter III opens with an assertion deduced from theorem 2. Positivity of $D^kW_k$ on irreducible subvarieties leads, for sufficiently large $n$, to a biregular embedding associated to the “fibre” $[nD]$. For a complete normal variety with a quasi-projective open subset, the text introduces ratios of homogeneous coordinates, boundary divisors, and a minimum of orders along each such divisor.

**Scan 102, passage to finite extensions.** The page checks $G/G^0$ against the group dual to roots of unity and $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. An extension from $\mathbb F_q$ to $\mathbb F_{q^m}$ gives a leftward map on groups and a rightward power map on their character groups $M(q)$. The author discusses its kernel and cokernel, then takes a direct limit. The margin describes a decomposition in an even-exponent case and how transition maps act on its factors. The final description of the dual group breaks off.
