# K132-02 · Mathematical notes

The six manuscript sheets are followed by two Notes of Yves Ladegaillerie. Section numbers below are those printed in the two Notes. M identifies a manuscript passage. Quotations retain the reading of the transcription. In the manuscript summaries, “component group” refers to the recurring curled letter whose alphabet is uncertain.

## What the pages do

### Manuscript

**M19a · Scan 19, upper diagram.** PLO branches towards SO, a tubular-neighbourhood category and SOB. Boundary arrows lead from the two occurrences of SOB to CO. Beside one branch the writer puts a complex K, a choice involving its circuits, and a map to the natural numbers. Several category labels remain uncertain.

**M19b · Scan 19, right and centre.** Finite families of pairs (gᵢ,nᵢ) record genus and number of holes. The writer discusses extensions of symmetry groups and displays a sequence with kernel T′ and a permutation group as quotient. A framed, hatched passage also tries to express a kernel and a quotient.

**M19c · Scan 19, lower left and foot.** The writer reduces a determination of symmetries to connected circularized complexes. He credits the calculation of g and n to Yves and leaves the map from circular automorphisms to the automorphisms of the thickening to be determined.

**M20a · Scan 20, B, hypotheses and boxes.** The three hypotheses concern connectedness and simple connectedness of H̃₀, containment of π in H̃₀, and discreteness. The boxes identify the identity component, the component group, and π with both fundamental groups π₁(H⁰) and π₁(H).

**M20b · Scan 20, numbered data and first NB.** One chooses H and an extension H̃ of H by π which prolongs the universal covering extension over H⁰. The writer discusses ambiguity in H² and, with an express reservation, an existence obstruction in H³. For a semidirect product he writes down a lift using the same component group and H̃⁰.

**M20c · Scan 20, second NB and last paragraph.** The slanted note gives a sequence from π through π₀G̃ to π₀G, identifying π₀G̃ with G₀. With N discrete, the proposed classification describes extensions of H by N by pairs consisting of an extension G₀ of the component group by N and a map φ from π into the centre of N. The last line writes a superscript on the target. The next page calls φ a homomorphism for the component-group action.

**M21a · Scan 21, first two paragraphs.** The writer pulls G₀ back along H → π₀H and uses φ to change the kernel of H̃ from π to the centre of N. The resulting extensions have compatible actions. Their contracted product supplies the desired G.

**M21b · Scan 21, middle diagrams.** A covering induces the displayed isomorphisms on πᵢ for i ≥ 2. The diagrams connect π₁G to a kernel and π₀G to an extension. The exact sequence π → G₀ → π₀G → 1 uses the composite through the centre of N and N.

**M21c · Scans 21–22, B′ and upper two diagrams.** The writer drops condition α. Under the retained discreteness assumptions he again displays the higher homotopy isomorphisms and expresses π₁G as a kernel. A boundary map ∂ for the extension by π now appears before φ. The second diagram gives an extension of π₀H by the cokernel of π₁H → N.

**M22a · Scan 22, bottom diagram.** The writer pulls G₀ back along π₀H → the discrete group to obtain G₁. He then displays π₀G as the cokernel of π₁H → G₁. A bracket labelled “faux” singles out this last assertion.

**M23a · Scan 23, upper ladders.** From a continuous homomorphism u from H′ to H, the writer pulls back the extension by π, takes the inverse image H̃′₀, and maps the resulting quotient into the old quotient. The first two ladders compare both short exact sequences with their originals.

**M23b · Scans 23–24, third ladder and items 1°–2°.** The pullback G′ over H′ inherits a strict trivialization after pullback to H̃′₀. It determines an extension G̃′₀ by N and a homomorphism φ′ from π centralizing N. If π maps trivially to the quotient, the target of this homomorphism reduces to the centre of N.

**M24a · Scan 24, “Ceci posé”.** G̃′₀ is described as the inverse image of G̃₀. The map φ induces φ′. With π contained in H̃₀, the two homomorphisms into the centre agree.

**M24b · Scan 24, final paragraphs.** Conditions β and γ pass to the primed data. Connectedness of H̃′₀ need not follow from condition α on H̃₀. The final proposed description of G′₀ as a quotient of π₀G̃′ is crossed through.

### Ladegaillerie, 22 July 1974

**J7.1 · Scan 26.** For an embedding of a connected compact 1-complex in an oriented compact surface with boundary, five conditions are declared equivalent. These involve homotopy equivalence, a standard embedding, components and fundamental groups, first homology, and first cohomology. The last three include an exclusion involving the sphere. The pair satisfying the criterion is called an oriented tubular neighbourhood.

**J7.2 · Scan 26.** A tubular neighbourhood consists of a compact surface with boundary and an embedding which is a homotopy equivalence. In TUB a morphism is an isotopy class of homeomorphisms carrying one embedded complex onto the other.

**J7.3 · Scan 26.** TUBO adds an orientation to the object and requires the morphism to respect it.

**J8.1 · Scan 26.** A circularized complex carries a circulation. Its morphisms are isotopy classes preserving that circulation.

**J8.2 · Scan 26.** The functor T assigns the standard tubular neighbourhood to a circularized complex. On each arc rectangle its map sends (x,t) to (h(x),t).

**J8.3 · Scan 26.** T is asserted to be an equivalence between circularized complexes up to isotopy and TUBO.

**J9.1 · Scans 26–27.** An oriented tubular neighbourhood is equipped with a fixed-point-free involution reversing orientation. Morphisms commute with the involutions as well as satisfying the tubular-neighbourhood condition.

**J9.2 · Scan 27.** Q sends such a neighbourhood to its quotient and sends equivariant morphisms to quotient morphisms.

**J9.3 · Scan 27.** The quotient functor Q is declared an equivalence with TUB.

**J10.1 · Scan 27.** On the graph side the involution is fixed-point-free and reverses the circularization. Morphisms preserve the circularizations and commute with the involutions.

**J10.2 · Scan 27.** T induces a functor on the objects with involution. A theorem asserts it is an equivalence, and the corollary identifies the resulting category of graphs with TUB up to equivalence.

**J11.1 · Scan 27.** A cochain assigns an element of ℤ/2 to each oriented arc, unchanged on reversal. Two circularizations differing at vertices by equality or inversion give local signs. A “cobord” on arcs without degree-one endpoints compares endpoint signs, with zero assigned to an isolated loop. The printed ratio at the second endpoint repeats the same primed term.

**J11.2 · Scan 27.** A cocircularization η is an equivalence class of pairs consisting of a cochain and a circularization. Equality up to reversal of the vertex orders is accompanied by the requirement that the cobord equal the difference of the cochains on its domain.

**J11.3 · Scan 27.** Morphisms preserve the cocircularization and include signs for isolated points, isolated arcs, and the specified isolated loops. The signs record the possible local orientation behaviour of a neighbourhood map.

**J11.4 · Scan 27.** The category defined with η is declared equivalent to TUB. A paragraph describes a standard-neighbourhood functor and a quasi-inverse which takes the induced cocircularization.

**J11.5 · Scans 27–28.** A circuit is a cyclic sequence of compatible arcs, and a loop pairs a circuit with its inverse. A representative (γ,ω) gives a successor rule on an arc together with a bit, using j = i + γ(a). Isolated loops with γ(b) = 0 are included separately. The resulting loops are said to be independent of the representative and to correspond to boundary components.

**J12.1 · Scan 28.** Two embeddings of K in compact surfaces are equivalent if a homeomorphism of the ambient surfaces intertwines them.

**J12.2 · Scan 28.** An embedding meeting every component of the surface induces a cocircularization of K from a tubular neighbourhood. Its equivalence class supplies the first invariant.

**J12.3 · Scan 28.** Boundary circles on the same component of the complementary surface form a block of a partition. Each block gets an orientability sign and a genus. The classification must take account of neighbourhood symmetries and restrictions on extending boundary maps across orientable pieces.

**J12.4 · Scan 28.** For a compact nonorientable surface with boundary, the proposition asserts extension of a boundary homeomorphism. The target in its last phrase is printed W′.

**J13.1 · Scan 28.** The Euler characteristic of the ambient surface is the sum of those of K and the complementary surface.

**J13.2 · Scan 28.** A minimal embedding realizes the least genus for an embedding in a connected compact orientable surface. This defines the genus of the complex.

**J13.3 · Scan 28.** Ω(K) is the supremum of the numbers of circuits over the circularizations. For connected K the stated genus is 1 − (χ(K) + Ω(K))/2. The author points to Edmonds’s algorithm.

**J13.4 · Scan 28.** The genus of a complex with several components is asserted to be the sum of their genera.

**J13.5 · Scan 28.** Minimal embeddings of the components are assembled by joining their ambient surfaces by cylinders along the edges of a tree. The theorem states that this describes the minimal embeddings of the whole complex.

**Jend · Scan 29.** References (1)–(3) accompany the claims in §§7, 13.3 and 13.4. A pencil drawing with an oval rim and long sides lies below the references and address. No label on the page identifies it with a particular construction.

### Ladegaillerie, 27 May 1974

**A1.1 · Scan 30.** A compact 1-complex is defined by local models with finitely many radial branches, including a point. A point with branch number other than two is a vertex, and a zero-branch vertex is isolated.

**A1.2 · Scan 30.** One category uses compact 1-complexes and isotopy classes of homeomorphisms. The other uses five pieces of finite data recording vertices, arcs, arcs with a source, the fixed-point-free reversing involution, and the source map. A morphism is a compatible pair of bijections.

**A1.3 · Scan 30.** The functor from topology to the finite data takes the components of the complement of the vertices, orients them to obtain arcs, and records their reversal and any source.

**A1.4 · Scan 30.** The functor is asserted to be an equivalence of categories.

**A2.1 · Scan 30.** After defining “surface” and “surface à bord”, the author defines an embedding as a continuous injection into the interior of an oriented surface with boundary.

**A2.2 · Scan 30.** An injection of a closed interval into the plane extends to a homeomorphism of the plane. Three references accompany the proposition.

**A2.3 · Scan 30.** An embedded star in the oriented plane induces a cyclic order on its branches. It is ambiently isotopic to a standard star with that order.

**A2.4 · Scan 30.** Equivalence of embeddings in the oriented setting requires an orientation-preserving homeomorphism intertwining the embeddings.

**A2.5 · Scan 31.** For two oriented surfaces of the same genus, an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of their boundaries extends to an orientation-preserving homeomorphism from the first surface onto the second.

**A3.1 · Scan 31.** A circularization gives a cyclic order on the outgoing arcs at each vertex. The author credits an earlier combinatorial treatment.

**A3.2 · Scan 31.** Isolated vertices get oriented disks. An oriented arc gets a rectangle, identified with its reverse using opposite transverse coordinates. At a vertex, the cyclic successor specifies the gluing of adjacent rectangles.

**A3.3 · Scan 31.** The canonical embedding uses the middle of each rectangle and the centres of isolated-vertex disks. A homotopy-equivalence property characterizes it up to equivalence.

**A3.4 · Scan 31.** The arc successor reverses an arriving arc and applies the cyclic order at its target. It fixes the other elements, which include isolated vertices. Its orbits are the circuits that correspond to the boundary components of the thickening.

**A4.1 · Scan 31.** An embedding in an oriented surface determines a unique circularization and admits an embedded tubular neighbourhood equivalent to the standard model. This transports the boundary-circuit bijection to the given embedding.

**A4.2 · Scan 31.** Equality of induced circularizations is equivalent to equivalence of the embeddings on suitably chosen neighbourhoods.

**A5.1 · Scans 31–32.** When the embedded complex meets every component, the boundary of its complementary surface carries the opposite boundary orientation. Its complementary components partition the boundary circuits. The genus of each component labels its block. Both the circularization and the labelled partition are invariant under equivalence.

**A5.2 · Scan 32.** Equality of the invariants characterizes equivalence. The theorem further states that every such system of invariants is realized by a class of embeddings.

**Aend · Scan 32.** The four references give the earlier combinatorial account and the three sources cited for the plane extension proposition. The same Montpellier institution and street address close both Notes.

## Questions and conjectures

- **M19c, scan 19.** “Il reste, pour un tel K … de déterminer” points to the boxed automorphism map. The opening question concerns computing that map for connected circularized complexes.
- **M20b, scan 20.** “sauf erreur l’obstruction à l’existence d’une telle ext. est dans” H³. This is the writer’s qualification of the obstruction claim.
- **M22a, scan 22.** “faux” is the writer’s verdict on the bracketed cokernel description. The sheet supplies no replacement formula. The crossed ending on scan 24 also has no proposed replacement in this selection.
- The printed Notes use definitions, propositions and theorems. No passage in either is explicitly presented as a conjecture.

## Remarks of the model

**R1 · Scans 28 and 31. Model remark, high confidence.** The two different boundary descriptions can be compared without identifying their circuits. In May §3.4 the permutation acts on arcs and isolated vertices. In July §11.5 its successor rule also transports a bit, and opposite circuits are paired. Each construction explicitly identifies its resulting set with components of the boundary of its own tubular neighbourhood.

**R2 · Scan 27. Model remark, high confidence.** I evaluated the printed §11.1 expression for all four choices of signs at the two endpoints. The printed value depends only on the sign at s₁. For example, if the sign at s₀ is −1 and the sign at s₁ is +1, the printed result is 0 and the endpoint difference is 1 in ℤ/2. In exactly the two cases with sign −1 at s₀, removing the prime from the second denominator changes the value. This checks the diagnosis in S3 for the four possible signs.

**R3 · Scans 28 and 31. Model remark, high confidence.** An isolated point is a small consistency check on §13.3. Its standard neighbourhood in §3.2 is a disk. The permutation of §3.4 fixes the single isolated vertex, giving one circuit. With χ = 1 the formula gives 1 − (1 + 1)/2 = 0. This calculation tests this one complex.

**R4 · Scans 22 and 24. Model remark, high confidence about the annotations.** The bracket with “faux” begins beside the last claim on scan 22. On scan 24 long diagonal strokes pass through the final paragraph. These are two distinct kinds of intervention. The scans give an explicit judgement only in the first case.

**R5 · Reading question, scan 19, page-file line 21. Model remark, low confidence.** The frozen text read “une partie” immediately before the marked “liée|finie”. The witness’s reading of the overlapping phrase suggests “une partition Π′ de”. Rereading settled the marked part as “Π′ de”. The preceding word remains “partie” in the final transcription. Its ending runs very close to the following symbol. The printed partition of circuits on scan 32 makes “partition” plausible but cannot decide that manuscript stroke.
