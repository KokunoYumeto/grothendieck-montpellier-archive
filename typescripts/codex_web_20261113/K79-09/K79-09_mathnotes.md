# Mathematical notes on cote 79, scans 129–144

## What the pages do

**Scan 129, (62)–(63).** The diagonal map in the diagram sends a central element to its square. The image of δ therefore contains the squares in Z′. The orbit of a base structure consists of equivalent structures isomorphic to it. An axiom from the preceding discussion makes this orbit all of Π and gives the short exact sequence (63).

**Scan 130, (1)–(4).** Section VIII starts afresh with a family F₀ of three-element faces on a set S. From their two-element subsets it obtains the edges A₀. To 1 asks for a connected combinatorial polyhedron, including the local connectivity of faces at a vertex. To 2 recovers the faces from triangles in the edge graph.

**Scans 131–132, (5)–(15).** The automorphism group G₀ acts on S. In its permutation centralizer, Grothendieck singles out elements λ that change the edge set while preserving the graph up to isomorphism. To 3 requires the resulting permutations to commute. The edge sets Aλ form a family Π. The permutations preserving that family form G. The marginal notes distinguish the action on Π from ordinary transport and qualify the quotient in the bipyramidal case.

**Scans 133–134, (16)–(19).** To 4 puts Z in the centre of G. The resulting sequence has kernel G₀ and, with the bipyramidal qualification, translation group Z as target. Formula (18) compares structures by an isomorphism and by a permutation commuting with G₀ that transforms their incidence data. A precongruential structure is then the whole equivalence class. Its members share an automorphism group, and the class carries a torsor structure.

**Scans 135–136, (20)–(25 bis).** Starting with the union F of the face families, he takes all pairs contained in a face as edges and joins faces to edges by inclusion. Connected components of this incidence graph recover the constituent families. Each component must cover S and have connected local contours. An oblique addition considers oriented flags, faces, edges and their stabilizers. It is here that the stabilizer notation is hardest to read.

**Scans 137 and 139, (26)–(32).** In the new numbering, To 5 fixes a common finite vertex order n. Transport changes the centre assigned to a star while keeping its underlying n-element set. Giving each such set its polygon of neighbours recovers the global edge set by (31). The octahedron is singled out because distinct vertices can have the same underlying star.

**Scans 138–140, oriented and unoriented polygons.** An oriented polygon on E is described by a cyclic permutation ω of E, or a torsor for the additive group of integers modulo n. A unit ν acts by taking ω to its νth power. Reversing the orientation replaces the cycle by its inverse. The unit action thus gives an operation of units modulo sign on unoriented polygons. The scan order places (33)–(35) on 138 before (30)–(32) on 139.

**Scans 140–141, To 6 and (34)–(36).** For a given λ, one exponent ν(λ) must work at every vertex. Outside the octahedral exception the star then determines λs, giving an injection into the units modulo sign. The image condition asks for the local transformed polygons to give a global triangulation equivalent to the starting one.

**Scans 142–143, (37)–(38).** At n = 5 the nontrivial class exchanges associated icosahedral structures. The «icosaèdre gauche» raises a separate question because its commuting group is asserted to be trivial. An example modulo 7 and a list of quotient orders lead into To 7, requiring the injection to be surjective.

**Scan 144, (39)–(44).** To 8 asks for a regular, orientable polyhedron. The author proposes describing the orientation-preserving group by rotations and the larger group by involutions. He collects the two orientations of every Pᵢ in a single set over Π, introduces an action by the units modulo n, and records the compatibility of the two polygon structures on a common star.

## Questions and conjectures

- **Scans 132–134.** «Il faudrait réexpliciter l'opération “astucieuse” de Z sur Π». The author proposes writing the action as T with subscript λ. He also asks whether the definition can be enlarged to structures «ne satisfaisant pas (To 4)» and observes that the axiomatics is «pas encore réduite vraiment à l'os».
- **Scans 135–137.** «On se propose d'expliciter les conditions sur F» and «Je voudrais en arriver à définir un isomorphisme». The first goal is a reconstruction from faces. The later goal is the isomorphism (26) with units modulo sign, with an exceptional quotient on the source.
- **Scans 141–142.** The condition «pour tout» ν «mérite d'être examinée pour son propre mérite». In «Mais l'icosaèdre gauche ?» he points to an example in which the operation on polygons still has meaning although he does not expect surjectivity.
- **Scan 143.** «Est-il vrai que pour tout 2-polyèdre […] triangulé […]» the transformation with exponent 2 at order 7 «correspond encore à un tel 2-polyèdre ?» He adds «Même question» for vertex orders 8 and 10. The order of the group of exponents alone does not answer this reconstruction question.
- **Scan 144.** «Il vaut mieux séparer en deux axiomes distincts (39)». He suspects that in many arguments a transitivity assumption on G₀⁺ suffices. The terminal S or F in the margin is crossed and doubtful. The paragraph «on doit pouvoir traduire notre axiomatique» proposes a group-theoretic formulation.

## Remarks of the model

- **R139F · scans 138–140 · high confidence for the finite check.** I represented the polygon on residue classes by edges joining x to x + u for each unit u. For every modulus at least 3 in the manuscript's numerical list, two such edge sets agreed precisely when their parameters were equal up to sign. This gives concrete instances of the margin's faithfulness assertion. The test compared 2,124 pairs of units.
- **R141 · scans 139 and 141 · high confidence.** For the tetrahedron I counted four vertex stars, each contributing three local edges. Their union contains six edges and their coproduct contains twelve tagged edges. For the octahedron there are three distinct star sets and each of the twelve edges occurs once among their polygons. These two counts explain why the disjoint-union symbol in (36) matters.
- **R143 · scans 142–143 · high confidence for the arithmetic.** Direct enumeration gives orders 1 for n = 2, 3, 4, 6 and 2 for n = 5, 8, 10, 12. The other pairs in the list give 3 for 7 and 14, 4 for 16 and 20, 5 for 11 and 22, 6 for 13 and 26, 8 for 17 and 34, and 9 for 19 and 38. Modulo 7, the stated inverse of 2 is indeed 4. The two discrepant order entries are recorded under Slips.
- **R144 · scan 144, (41) · high confidence.** With the order of the factors exactly as written, ρ₀ρ∞ρ₁ is the identity. I substituted the three words in τ and cancelled adjacent equal involutions. The order of the subscripts in this observation is essential.
