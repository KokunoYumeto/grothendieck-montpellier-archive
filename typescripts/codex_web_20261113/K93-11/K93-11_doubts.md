## Legibility and hands

The typewritten equations are generally distinct. Broken impressions in the assumption and part (IV) of Proposition 6.9 make two words uncertain. The handwritten notes on 18, 21 and 23 are small. The thickened initials and the detached mark at the foot of 18 are less secure than the case table. The labels on the left of the diamond on 29 are considerably fainter than its arrows. On 31 the slanted notes can be turned to a horizontal baseline; the words underneath the two broad cancellations remain the least secure readings. A doubt inside a deletion describes its undertext.

The inventory uses “typed” for printed matter as well as for the typescript, in accordance with its four permitted hand labels. The journal note names Marcus Wanderley as its author and Henri Cartan as presenter. The typescript excerpt does not carry a title or signature. The compact notes on 18, 21 and 23 and the small annotations on the typescript are unsigned. Their hand is entered as “unknown”. The larger, more cursive writing on 31 is assigned provisionally to Grothendieck’s working hand from the folder context. The pen used for a map arrow or a brace cannot identify its writer. The cover has no signature. No external source was consulted.
