# K93-11 · Mathematical notes

## What the pages do

**Normal forms and the invariant j (17, 19).** The typescript opens partway through an argument with 2 nilpotent. When the two values of j agree, the first equation in the change of variables defines an étale cover of degree two. After reducing to equal second coefficients, the text uses the formula for 1/j in terms of a₂ and a₆. For an algebraically closed field it then treats the exceptional values 0 and 1728, distinguishing characteristics other than 2 and 3, characteristic 3, and characteristic 2. The resulting curve equations are labelled (6.5)–(6.8).

**A formal local question (18).** A handwritten sheet asks about an elliptic curve E over a perfect field k. It puts a completed expression involving W(k)[j] beside the quotient of a formal M by Aut(E). A table compares j with t³ at 0 and t² at 12³ away from characteristics 2 and 3, with t a relative regular parameter. The two remaining rows give the group sizes 6 and 12, and an adjacent note gives divisibilities involving v(j). The group question at the foot is separate from the framed comparison.

**Automorphisms (19).** Proposition 6.9 starts with a genus-one curve without additive fibre. Part (I) names the identity and the symmetry when c₄ and c₆ are invertible. A handwritten qualification adds “vrai seulement en faisceautisant !”. Parts (II) and (III) use the action on invariant differentials. When 2 and 3 are invertible the map to Gₘ is a closed immersion, with image μ₆ or μ₄ in the specified cases. For a field, the characteristic-3 case gives a noncommutative semidirect product and image μ₄. The characteristic-2 case gives geometric order 24 and image μ₃.

**Calculation of the automorphisms (20, 22).** The proof uses the action on π, the conditions on λ⁴ and λ⁶, and then explicit change-of-variable parameters. For (6.7) it gives u ∈ μ₄ and r ∈ F₃. For (6.8) the conditions become u ∈ μ₃, s ∈ 𝔽₄ and t²+t=s³. The hypotheses for the composition formula are on 20 and the typed product is on 22. Its middle coordinate has a problem recorded under Slips.

**A prospective corollary (21).** The handwritten “Cor” starts with flatness over ℤ and offers a bracketed weaker hypothesis involving 2 and 3. The ensuing expression $\Gamma(S,\mathcal O_S)[\ldots]$ has three ink dots. A right-hand side or a list of generators cannot be recovered from this sheet.

**Integral modular forms (22).** Section 7 defines a weight-n form by its assignment of a section of the nth tensor power of ω, compatibly with change of base. Proposition 7.1 gives generators c₄, c₆ and Δ over ℤ and the relation c₄³−c₆²=1728Δ. The proof first considers the case with 2 and 3 invertible and reduces the remaining issue to divisibility in characteristics 2 and 3.

**The two reductions (23–25).** A short handwritten note identifies a₁ and ±b₂ as “les invariants de Hasse Witt”. Proposition 7.2 of the typescript gives the rings with generators a₁, Δ in characteristic 2 and b₂, Δ in characteristic 3. It expresses c₄ and c₆ as powers of the respective first generator. The argument first inverts a coefficient, writes a form as a homogeneous polynomial times a negative power, and asks when it extends. The characteristic-3 argument finishes on 25 at j=0.

**Data for an orientation theory (27–28).** The “Orientation” cover precedes Marcus Wanderley’s journal note, presented by Henri Cartan. The note defines categories of manifolds of bounded dimension with open embeddings. Restriction to the interior extends a contravariant functor to manifolds with boundary. The data of an orientation theory are the functor Ω, the Stokes map δ to orientations of the boundary, and a normalisation ν identifying the two signs with the orientations of a point. Exactness requires a sheaf on the opens of each manifold.

**Local axioms, signs and morphisms (29).** Separation makes the open coincidence set of two orientations closed as well. The two local conditions on Stokes say when orientations with the same boundary value agree nearby and when a boundary orientation extends locally. A proposition describes the associated space as a two-sheeted covering. Opposite orientations are related by the action of μ₂. The diamond in axiom (d) imposes opposite signs on the common boundary of two pieces. Morphisms commute with Stokes and normalisation.

**Existence and uniqueness (29).** Theorem 1 asserts existence, at most one morphism between two theories, and invertibility of any such morphism. Part (c) asserts that the unique isomorphism exists between any two theories when N ≠ 4. The succeeding page supplies the stated ingredients.

**Monoids, the degree, and stability (30).** Theorem 2 relates local constancy, separation and the action by automorphisms of the monoid of continuous injective maps of ℝᵐ into itself. Theorem 3 describes locally constant sheaves by sets with an action of its associated group Π(m). Brouwer degree gives a map to μ₂. Theorem 4 relates this map being an isomorphism to stability of degree-+1 homeomorphisms; the corollary invokes Kirby for m ≠ 4.

**Stokes triples (30).** In dimension m a triple consists of separated sheaves in dimensions m and m−1 and a natural boundary map satisfying the local-isomorphism axiom. Theorem 5 identifies the category of triples with Π(m)-sets by evaluation in dimension m.

**The handwritten orientation theory (31).** This sheet uses a bar for the category with boundary and specifies a functor, a distinguished orientation ω⁺ of a point e, and a map from orientations of the interior to orientations of the boundary. The first two conditions concern the sheaf property and the two orientations of e. The final paragraph uses the two “rives” of a codimension-one submanifold Y. Its last two maps express how a local orientation of X together with a choice of side gives an orientation on Y.

In characteristic 2 both powers on the left become a₁¹²; in characteristic 3 they become b₂⁶, since the negative sign on c₆ disappears on squaring.
