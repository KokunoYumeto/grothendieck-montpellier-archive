# K157-5-36 · Mathematical notes

These notes refer to the frozen transcription of scans 558–572. The numbered statements in the cancelled draft on 566–567 are identified separately from the later statements bearing the same numbers.

## What the pages do

**U01 · Scan 558.** The end of an earlier argument uses \(N=\{\varnothing\}\) to explain the extra stability condition. The parenthetical NB says that it ensures \(L_\pi\)-stability of \(\underline S\) in \(M\) for a given \(\pi\). The letter naming the subcategory in the stability hypothesis has a cap and a foot and is uncertain.

**U02 · Scans 558–559, § 4.16.3.** Grothendieck asks whether closing the essential image of an accessible functor under direct factors gives an accessible subcategory. He gives the example \(M_z\), defined by \(\operatorname{Hom}_M(x,z)\ne\varnothing\), and identifies it with the essential image of \(M_{/z}\to M\). He then proposes taking the smallest strictly full \(L_\pi\)-stable subcategory containing the image. He conjectures accessibility of this subcategory and eventual stationarity as \(\pi\) increases, and asks a corresponding question involving opposite categories.

**U03 · Scan 560.** Starting with a small strictly generating full subcategory \(C\) of accessible objects, he considers \(M\hookrightarrow C^\wedge\) and its essential image \(\overline M\). He asks how \(L_\pi\)-stability, accessibility of the image and accessibility of \(M\) itself are related. The two oblique notes propose a construction and then question the existence of a left adjoint needed for it.

**U04 · Scan 561, § 4.16.4.** He conjectures that \(M\) is essentially small if both \(M\) and \(M^\circ\) are accessible. He asks how far one can replace accessibility by the existence of small strictly generating or strictly cogenerating subcategories.

**U05 · Scans 561–562, § 4.16.5.** For the right cone on a small \(I\), he singles out the functors whose value at the added final object is the inductive limit of the other values. He asks about accessibility of that full subcategory of the functor category. A fork and its completed square give a consequence for the category written \(\underline{\mathrm{Ep}}(M)\) inside \(\underline{\mathrm{Fl}}(M)\). The marginal NB relates the question, under a cogeneration hypothesis, to conjecture 4.16.2.

**U06 · Scans 562–563, Proposition 4.16.6.1.** For \(f:\mathcal E\to C\) with \(C\) small and apparently karoubienne, Grothendieck imposes a condition \(H'\) on nonidentity arrows and accessibility conditions on certain base changes. The stated conclusion is accessibility of the total category and of \(f\). He prefaces the proposition with an expectation that an earlier argument will yield it.

**U07 · Scan 563.** Two finite categories \(Q,Q'\) are described by objects, arrows and equations. They share a retraction with arrows \(\sigma,\rho\) and a projector \(p\). The arrows adjoining \(y\) go into \(y\) in the first presentation and out of \(y\) in the second. For the intended test, the functor to \(C\) must send \(p\) to a nonidentity arrow. A note in the margin questions this restriction.

**U08 · Scans 564–565, Corollaries 4.16.6.2 and the following corollary, Question 4.16.6.4.** The first corollary compares the general small karoubienne base-change test with tests by \(\Delta^1,Q,Q'\). Clause b′) retains \(\Delta^1\) when the base has no projectors. For a fibrant or cofibrant functor, the next corollary states consequences for the fibres and for transport along an arrow. The question on 565 asks whether the fibre and transport conditions suffice, in particular over \(Q\) or \(Q'\).

**U09 · Scans 565–566, § 4.16.6.5.** He proposes the expression “catégorie accessible sur \(C\)”. To support that terminology he wants a base-change result for every accessible \(M\to C\). On the next sheet he considers dropping smallness of \(C\) and adding an \(L_\pi\) hypothesis.

**U10 · Scan 566, cancelled heading.** A proposed § 4.17 announces a return to paratopos and the operations \(M\boxtimes N\) and \(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_!(M,N)\). The heading is crossed out before any development of those operations here.

**U11 · Scans 566–567, cancelled § 4.16.7.** The draft theorem asserts accessibility of every closed subcategory of an accessible \(M\) and smallness of their collection. A corollary reduces a question on open subcategories to the examples \(M_z\). Its argument describes an open subcategory as a union. A fully faithful functor and a proposed surjectivity assertion for inverse images of closed subcategories enter the beginning of the proof of b). The draft ends in a multiply cancelled line.

**U12 · Scans 568–569, Proposition 4.16.7.1.** The new text considers a strictly full subcategory whose objects are a possibly large union of closed accessible subcategories. It states that accessibility in \(M\) is equivalent to the existence of a small subfamily with the same union of objects. An insertion gives an eventual-stationarity form for an ordered increasing family. The proof selects one \(i_x\) for each object of a small reduced representative \(\underline S(\pi)_0\), forms \(J\) from those indices, and uses the factorization of the displayed ind-object functor through \(\underline S_J\).

**U13 · Scan 570, Proposition 4.16.7.2.** The closed subcategory generated by \(x\) consists of the \(y\) for which \(\operatorname{Hom}(x,y)\ne\varnothing\). Grothendieck identifies it as an essential image, then as the inverse image of the subcategory written \(\mathrm{Ens}^*\) under the representable functor \(h_x\). This gives the stated accessibility in \(M\) by reference to 4.13.

**U14 · Scans 570–571, Corollary 4.16.7.3.** The small-subfamily criterion is applied to the union of the principal closed subcategories generated by the objects of \(\underline S\). An alternative uses the ordered set of small parts \(J\) of its objects and the closed subcategories they generate. The criterion becomes the equality \(\underline S_J=\underline S\) for one \(J\).

**U15 · Scans 571–572, Proposition 4.16.7.4 and beginning of proof.** The existence of a closed subcategory inaccessible in \(M\) is related to a transfinite sequence with \(\operatorname{Hom}(x_\beta,x_\alpha)=\varnothing\) for \(\beta<\alpha\). The text specifies the closed subcategory generated by the sequence and states that all the closed inaccessible subcategories arise this way. The proof begins by considering a small set of indices \(J\) and choosing an ordinal above its members. The supplied page ends after the resulting Hom-vanishing formula, with a comma.

## Remarks of the model

- **R01 · Scan 563 · High confidence.** In the clearer \(Q'\) presentation, the written relations \(\rho\sigma=\mathrm{id}\), \(\sigma\rho=p\) and \(u=\sigma u_0\) give \(\rho u=u_0\) and \(pu=u\), as written at the right. This checks those two displayed consequences. The accessibility criterion is a separate claim.
- **R02 · Scans 558 and 570 · High confidence.** The two principal subcategories fix different positions in Hom. On 558 the fixed object \(z\) is the target. On 570 the fixed object \(x\) is the source. This distinction is visible directly in the two nonemptiness conditions.
- **R03 · Scans 565–566 · Low confidence as an interpretation.** The desired base-change map on 565 ends at \(M\). The next sheet calls the projection to \(\mathcal E\) canonique. Both are projections of the displayed product. The pages leave open how the change of target affects the proposed terminology.
- **R04 · Scan 572 · Low confidence as a criticism of the proof.** Applying the displayed vanishing to all \(\beta\in J\) requires \(\beta<\alpha\). The wording “qui majore les \(\beta\in J\)” leaves strictness implicit. Equality is the case that the displayed condition does not cover. The statement has overwritten indices and the proof continues beyond this sheet.

**Reading question arising after the freeze.** On 562, body line 20, the target in the NB reads \(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(I^\circ\times C,\mathrm{Ens})^\circ\). The source immediately before it has \(I_{\cdot}\), which suggests checking \(I_{\cdot}^{\circ}\) as an alternative in the target. The small writing does not show a separate lower point there as clearly as it does in the source. The frozen reading remains \(I^\circ\).
