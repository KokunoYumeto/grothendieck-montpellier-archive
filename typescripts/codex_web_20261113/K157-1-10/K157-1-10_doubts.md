# Legibility

The large upright formulas and the labelled objects of the uncancelled diagrams are generally the clearest writing. A prime, bar or slash can remain visible even when a neighbouring noun is compressed into a few strokes. The final lines on one sheet sometimes begin a sentence completed only on the next.

I trust the long corrections in the left margins of 154, 158 and 161 less than their central text. On 155 the final hatch covers most of the correction. The cancelled corollary on 163 and the lowest example on 158 also have superposed writing that cannot be separated throughout. On 150–152 the distinction between a quickly written C and G can change the object of a functor. On 159 and 165 the most consequential doubtful readings concern a projection target and the last proof obligations. The question marks and word-count estimates remain part of this transcription.
