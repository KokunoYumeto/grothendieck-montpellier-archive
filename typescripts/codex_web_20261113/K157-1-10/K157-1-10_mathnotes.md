# What the pages do

**Scans 150–151, the augmentation.** Applying derived Hom to the augmentation from $L\alpha_!(k_{C'})$ to $k_C$ recovers the comparison of cohomology groups under pullback. Grothendieck gives a corresponding formula for a relative homotopy type. He identifies the degree-zero comparison with adjunction and invokes shifting for the other cohomological degrees.

**Scans 151–152, internal adjunction.** A unit object for internal Hom would let him interpret $\alpha_*\alpha^*F$. He writes a proposed internal adjunction and passes to its tensor counterpart. The last line singles out the canonical comparison whose invertibility he wants to establish. Its right-hand arguments have the C/G ambiguity recorded in the doubts file.

**Scans 153–154, irreducibility.** Section 2 starts with the opens of a topos. The terminal open must differ from the empty one, and two nonempty opens must intersect. The local definition tests the induced topos over each 0-connected sheaf. The discussion passes to a space and then to the topos associated to a category. A marginal “Faux” changes which of the proposed conditions imply one another.

**Scans 154–155, categories and orders.** Grothendieck considers a common source of arrows to two objects. A category with a least object supplies an example. The discussion of a finite or Artinian associated order runs in the converse direction. The two diagrams at the foot of 155 distinguish completing a square (PS1) from equalizing parallel arrows (PS2).

**Scans 156–157, Proposition 2.1.** The proposition relates local irreducibility of the associated topos, connectedness of opens, and a common-source condition in the slices. A long insertion arrow moves the clause about the spaces of the slices into the list. The next sheet uses two parallel arrows from 0 to 1 to contest the converse to the assertion about connected components.

**Scans 157–158, Definition 2.2.** The four clauses distinguish weak filtering on the left, its local form in the slices, filtering on the left, and its componentwise form. The author revises the names as he writes. Clause c introduces the equalizer test. The marginal example on 158 challenges the second NB about components. The sheet ends with dual terminology and a four-object example with relations among composites.

**Scans 159–160, Proposition 2.3 and Definition 2.4.** After comparing what the order, the space and the topos determine, the author states tests involving products of 0-connected objects and the functor $\pi_0$. He calls a nonempty, locally connected topos satisfying the tests “tot. 0-connexe”. The margin interprets the categorical version by the diagonal and finite powers.

**Scans 160–161, Proposition 2.5.** Total 0-connectedness is asserted to imply irreducibility. Grothendieck distinguishes the local question by discussing the simplex category and its slices, then returns to the special case of a topological space. The marginal “Faux” on 160 points specifically to the added asphericity assertion above Definition 2.4.

**Scans 161–162, Theorem 2.6 and its corollary.** A locally connected topos is equipped with a generating family of 0-connected objects. The conditions involve its induced topoi, fibre products, connected components and filtering of the generators. The bracket and the long marginal note qualify the status of c′. The next sheet specializes the comparison to $\mathrm{Top}(X)$, draws the implications, and begins a subsequently cancelled argument toward PS1.

**Scans 163–165, Corollary 2.8.** Here the fibre-product test asks for nonemptiness. The right-hand chart on 163 records the initial implications. To handle the remaining direction, the argument takes objects of the generating family over $W$ and considers the ones whose images meet the image of $a$. This gives a sieve. Its complement is also a sieve. The two nonempty parts would disconnect $W$. The last paragraph returns to the outstanding PS2 implications of 2.6.
