# Mathematical notes on K136-21

## What the pages do

**312–313. Coefficients and internal Hom.** The opening distinguishes knowing the chain complex $C_{\cdot}$ from remembering the additional structures that come from its origin in $X_\ast$. For a $k$-module-valued $F$, it puts the scalar action on $K_{\cdot}$ and writes $F(X_\ast)$ as a Hom out of $C_{\cdot}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}k$. For an additive target $B$, it defines the Hom object by its maps from $N$, forms a bicomplex with entries indexed by $i,j$, and takes cycles in total degree zero. The top of 313 gives the $k$-linear version.

**313. The universal choice of target.** Prop. 3 takes $B_0=(\mathit{Ab}_{\cdot})^\circ$ and sends $X_\ast$ to its chains. Precomposition with this functor is claimed to give an equivalence of categories of limit-preserving functors. The author then replaces chains by the free simplicial abelian object and writes a second equivalence with a question mark over its arrow.

**314. The restriction argument.** Passing to opposite categories gives $G_0(X)=\mathbb Z(X)$. The author identifies one functor category by restriction to $\Delta$. To handle the other, he writes $\operatorname{Hom}(\varphi X,M)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(X,\psi M)$ and evaluates at $\mathbb Z(x)$. This is the argument immediately preceding the description of abelianization as a universal functor.

**314–315. The corollary and its dual.** For $\Delta$ the category of simplices, $n\mapsto\mathbb C_\ast(\Delta_\ast^n)$ is presented as a universal cosimplicial object for additive categories with colimits. The corollary extends to the corresponding cochain complex. Part b) passes to an opposite category and states the corresponding result for a simplicial object and its chains.

**315–316. Invariants and operations on them.** The author associates $K$ to an invariant $F$ and $L$ to an operation $\Omega$. In the contravariant case the two displayed evaluations are Hom constructions, first out of the chains of $X_\ast$ and then out of $L$. The covariant displays use decorated tensor products. The note beside the latter asks for their meaning to be made precise. The variance of the answer in $L$ reverses between the two cases.

**316–317. Restricting the operations.** The proposed category $\mathcal U_L$ is generated from the universal values on simplices using (co)limits of allowed index shapes. Its description is given both in $B_0$ and in the opposite category. For $L=\varnothing$ the author names the full subcategory generated by the $G_0[n]$ using finite sums.

**317–318. Matching $F$ to $\Omega$.** When $B=\mathit{Ab}$ and $K=L$, the boxed expression evaluates $\Omega$ on the universal chain or simplicial object. The circle on a value of $\mathbb C$ indicates passage to the opposite category. The author follows the possible values of $\Omega$ through $B'=\mathit{Ab}^{\circ}$ and uses the two descriptions to specify a class of functors $F$.

**318. Tensor operations and the role of DP.** The author contrasts the sizes of $L_{\cdot}$ and $L_\ast$, calls attention to a direct summand, and distinguishes the two multiplicative structures. Exterior, divided and symmetric powers appear together. Transporting structure by DP is mentioned as one way of placing such operations on the chain side.

**320. Iteration and multiplication.** The upper diagrams follow $f:A\to X$ through $\Gamma^{pq}$ and through $\Gamma^p\Gamma^q$. The circled square evaluates the comparison at $X=A$ and $f=\mathrm{id}_A$. The lower square starts with the diagonal of $A$ and has the boxed output $u_pu_q=c_{pq}u_{p+q}$, accompanied by $u_0=1$ and $u_1=\mathrm{id}$.

**321. A cancelled attempt at functoriality.** The sheet asks how a map of degree $n$ induces a map on values of $F$. A bilinear map involving $F_A(X)$ and $F_B(Y)$ is evaluated on two identities to obtain $A\to A\otimes A$. Associativity, commutativity and compatibility with the unit are listed. Much of this work is crossed through.

**322. The product calculation.** For two maps $f,g:A\to X$, the diagrams follow their divided powers, tensor the resulting maps and precompose with $\Delta$. The author specializes both maps to the identity. The resulting identity for $1^{(p)}1^{(q)}$ explains the role of the coefficient $c_{pq}$ in the product square.

**323. The addition calculation.** A map $F_A\to F_A\circ\Gamma^p$ is described by an element $u_p$ evaluated at $A$. Taking the two injections into $M=A\times A$ makes their sum the diagonal. The concluding formula is $\Delta(u_p)=\sum_{i+j=p}u_i\otimes u_j$.

**324. Divided powers on a Hom ideal.** The first question concerns $\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,J)$ for a coalgebra $A$ and a divided-power ideal $J$ of $B$. Finite free $A$ gives the tensor description with $\check A$. The next question sets $B=\Gamma^\ast A$, fixes $u_1$, and asks about iteration and uniqueness. A further paragraph reverses the roles of algebra and coalgebra and places a divided-power structure on the kernel associated to $B'\to B$.

**325. Four formulations.** Items 3)–6) successively record a family $f\mapsto f^{(p)}$ natural in $M$, a divided-power law involving $\Gamma^+M$, its version evaluated at $A$, and a law on $\operatorname{Hom}_k(A,J)$ natural in $(B,J)$. Some of the connecting conditions end mid-sentence.

**326. Groups, a torus and two coordinates.** Two rows with $\mu_2$, special linear groups and projective groups link the real and complex cases. The neighbouring torus calculation writes a matrix with rows $(a,b)$ and $(-b,a)$, imposes $a^2+b^2=1$, and sets $\lambda=a+ib$, $\mu=a-ib$. The expressions for $a,b$ in terms of these factors are written out separately.

**326. Differentials and an operator.** Another cluster places powers of $\Omega$ beside the $P^n_{X/k}$, then draws maps from $E$ and a map marked $D$. A scalar equation involves successive derivatives of $f$. The short expression $Df+\alpha f=0$ is written separately. The sheet gives no prose connecting all of these sketches.

**327. A canonical torsor.** For a complex curve of genus at least two, the author takes its pointed universal cover and considers isomorphisms from the pointed Poincaré domain. Extension of the group gives the fibres $P_x$. The author identifies them with fibres of a flat principal bundle and sketches the disc, a marked point and a curved path.

**327. A normalized disc and a connection problem.** The statement labelled « Th » specifies a disc image in $T_x(X)$ and identity tangent map at $\tilde x$. Below it lie maps of torsors and groups, two copies of $E\to E\otimes\Omega$, and a projective bundle built from $\Omega^{-1}+\Omega$. The framed request asks for a canonical integrable algebraic connection on that bundle.

## Remarks of the model

**R1. Model observation, high confidence.** Scans 320 and 322 both specialize to identity maps, but the specializations serve different calculations. On 320 the circled square compares two ways to iterate divided powers. On 322 the lower diagram compares their product with the power of total degree $p+q$. The explicit labels $X=A$, $f=\mathrm{id}_A$ and $X=Y=A$, $f=g=\mathrm{id}$ identify the two steps.

**R2. Model observation, high confidence.** On 327 the normalized map into $T_x(X)$ and the framed connection problem have different status. The first is stated as a theorem with a uniqueness condition. The second begins with the infinitive « Trouver ». Neither the disc drawing nor the bundle formulas supply a written solution to the latter.

**R3. Model computation for S7, high confidence in the arithmetic.** Put $\lambda=2$ and $\mu=1/2$, which satisfies $\lambda\mu=1$. The left expression for $b$ is $-3i/4$. The last expression gives $0$ with $\mu^{-1}$, or $3/4$ with $\lambda^{-1}$, when the written coefficient is read as $1/2$. This is an exact check of those readings under the stated substitution. Confidence in attributing a slip to the manuscript remains low because the inverse base is doubtful.

**R4. Model reading question, low confidence.** On 325, item 3, TeX line 16, the frozen summation condition is <code>i+j=\unsure{0&#124;p}</code>. A possible reading of the small sign immediately before the last oval is $\geq$. The two short strokes are so close to the ruling that their relative slopes are unclear. The last symbol and the preceding sign are separate reading questions.
