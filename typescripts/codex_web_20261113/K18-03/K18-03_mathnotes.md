The identifiers locate statements in the check-event register. Quotations retain the language of the sheets. The English typescript on 44 and the French typescript on 46 have their own arguments.

## What the pages do

**U33 · Two conjugations, scans 33–34.** A comparison with the de Rham realization gives two real structures after extension of scalars. The writer distinguishes the conjugation that preserves the filtration from the one that exchanges it with the opposite filtration. He writes down their product, the real Frobenius, and describes its action on the Hodge structure. On the real subgroup denoted by U, the displayed conjugation formula ends in the inverse of λ.

**U35 · A rational subspace, scan 35.** Writing z = x + iy with fx = x and fy = −y picks out a rational subspace W. A square compares V and W and their intersection. The question about tensor products occurs before the particular comparison V → W, whose scalar on the negative summand is i. At the foot of the sheet the writer considers a twist of Betti by a cocycle.

**U36 · The real-point criterion, scan 36.** The opening relation between the conjugate of α and f∞α leads to the displayed equivalence between f∞ = 1 and α being real. The writer separates the case in which M contains the Tate motive. A lower diagram connects trivial real Frobenius, the real field generated by α and generation by an even Tate twist. A slanted qualification about the Hodge conjecture stands beside the diagram.

**U37 · The descent datum, scan 37.** Under « Motifs sur ℝ », complex conjugation on complex points produces an order-two automorphism of the Betti functor. The sheet relates the two conjugations on a realization using f∞, writes the isomorphism torsor between the functors, describes a cocycle and ends with the conjugation action on j(λ).

**U38 · Fields of definition and a cancelled calculation, scan 38.** Starting with a real β, the writer equates the fields obtained by adjoining γ and α. The remark restricts when f∞ can act as the identity on M. The lower, cancelled calculation conjugates C to its inverse, passes to a quotient group and compares two normalizers. Its internal cross-outs distinguish several discarded formulations.

**U39 · Independence of the comparison, scan 39.** For a topological field K isomorphic to ℂ, the writer compares the cohomology of the underlying space with Hodge cohomology. The triangle records what happens for complex-conjugate choices. Its downward arrow is labelled antilinear. The sideways addition discusses a real subspace and multiplication by √−1. For projective nonsingular X the bottom formula gives a decomposition compatible with the filtration.

**U40 · Descent and an archimedean place, scans 40–41.** The writer first takes K isomorphic to ℝ and passes to a closure K′ with group π. Commutation with π gives a descended comparison. Taking the π-invariants is worked out in the left margin. A choice of archimedean topology T then gives a decomposition after completion. The intersections on 41 define subspaces of the original cohomology. The fields R_T are in turn intersected over T to identify a totally real subfield.

**U42 · A research programme, scans 42–43.** A title sheet connects variation of the discrete lattice at an archimedean place with a criterion of transcendence. The following page asks about comparisons among Betti, de Rham, l-adic and p-adic cohomology. Its questions specify what is to be compared, including lattices for different topologies, real structures and splittings. Two small additions qualify the valued-field question.

**U44 · A direct-image argument, scan 44.** The English typescript concludes an induction on polynomial degree, then writes a homomorphism induced by an imbedding and its direct images. Removing the singular locus W gives an open set and its inverse image under f. The final paragraph restricts the homomorphisms to these opens. The tensor-product map and the definitions of the opens are handwritten.

**U45 · Two elliptic curves, scan 45.** For C × C′, with neither curve having complex multiplication as described in the opening, two group expressions carry dimensions 7 and 5. The sheet compares a transcendence-degree bound of 5 with dim G − 1. The characters at the end of the opening line are uncertain. An upside-down, cancelled passage uses Čech-type cohomology and a term labelled « préfaisceau » on the other edge of the sheet.

**U46 · Local determination of Haar measure, scan 46.** The French typescript finishes a normalization on ℚₚ. Proposition 7 gives a condition on a measure on an open part of a locally compact group under which it extends uniquely to a left Haar measure. The proof compares translated measures on overlaps and cites a gluing result. The corollary starts to transport such a measure through a local isomorphism; its proof continues after this sheet.

**U47 · Several places, scan 47.** The writer compares integral lattices in a product of realizations. A reference functor gives spaces of isomorphisms Pᵢ at the chosen places. A point of their product gives a point of a quotient; the slanted note specifies the diagonal action. After several cancelled expressions he writes a double quotient and sets m equal to the rank of M. The assertion about the transcendence degree of the point breaks off at « en question ».

## Remarks of the model

- **R35 · Model remark, high confidence.** The two tensor-product sentences on 35 concern different constructions. The sheet answers « Oui » about forming W. Its chosen linear comparison multiplies the negative summand by i. On a product of two negative factors this contributes i² = −1, whereas the comparison on a positive summand contributes 1. This explains the stated failure of this particular map to respect tensor products when the product in question is nonzero.
- **R36 · Model reading question, low confidence.** Scan 36, L45. The frozen reading is « central » in « ne peut être central que si ». A possible reading on returning to the boxed note is « autre ». The letters between the initial stroke and the last tall stroke are tightly joined just above the underline. The two readings give different assertions, so I leave the conclusion of that note open.
- **R45 · Model remark, high confidence for the arithmetic.** With the values printed on 45, 7 − 1 = 6 and the stated upper bound of 5 is strictly smaller. This computation does not establish either the group dimensions or the transcendence-degree bound.
- **R47 · Model remark, high confidence about the extent of the text.** Scan 47 stops after « en question » before any numerical bound or comparison is given. The quotient construction can be described from these sheets; a value for the transcendence degree cannot be attributed to this passage.
