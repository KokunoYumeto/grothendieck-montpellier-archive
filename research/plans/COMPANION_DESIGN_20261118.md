# The Montpellier companion: the design (18 Nov 2026)

Written by the main session (Claude Opus 5.5 at effort max) from its own reading of the model editions. It builds on
`COMPANION_MODEL_20261118.md`, which holds the user's words and the first sketch. The models read:
- the user's copies: the Ramanujan Chapter 16 memoir, Netz's Archimedes vol. 1, Neumann's Galois, Heath's Euclid
  vol. 3, Whiteside's Newton vol. 5 and the English Grothendieck-Serre correspondence;
- the Andrews-Berndt front matter;
- three further models found by search (section 1.8).

## 0. What the companion is

From the user's words in COMPANION_MODEL:
- **Layer 1** is his text, transcribed and translated, made professional and readable.
- **Layer 2** is the modern contextualization in the same register as layer 1. The two are seamless.
- A unit is "a nice little introduction, contextualization, a little bit of history, ... and then writing what
  Grothendieck writes".
- Status lines are dated and linked: "open as he left it; developed further in ...".
- The companions are offered as AI drafts that people who know the field may take up.

The companion is not a book on the mathematics written in place of his text. The text is given whole, and everything
else serves the reading of it.

## 1. What the models do, in detail

### 1.1 Ramanujan, Chapter 16 (Adiga, Berndt, Bhargava, Watson, Memoirs AMS 315, 1985): the entry format

- **The chapter introduction** (pp. 1-3) surveys the whole chapter before any entry:
  - its structure (135 results in 39 sections, "one of the best organized chapters");
  - which results are rediscoveries (Heine, Jacobi, Cauchy) and which appear new;
  - the famous ones, and who first proved them in print and when ("established in print by Hirschhorn in 1972 while
    being unaware that the result is found in Ramanujan's notebooks");
  - the notation decisions, with their reasons: his f(a, b) is kept "for those reading this paper in conjunction with
    the notebooks", and his x becomes q, the modern letter;
  - the sources used (Watson's unpublished notes).
- **Each entry** (pp. 4-6):
  1. his number, "ENTRY n.";
  2. the statement with all hypotheses, in modern notation;
  3. one to three sentences of attribution, with exact locators ("Entry 3 is normally attributed to Cauchy. However,
     Entry 3 can be found in Jacobi's Fundamenta Nova [p. 232] published 14 years earlier.");
  4. a proof, or "For a proof, see [6, p. 20]";
  5. how the entries follow from one another ("Letting b and c tend to 0 ... we deduce Entry 3").
- **After an entry:**
  - the later history ("A generalization of Entry 13 appears in the lost notebook; see Andrews [eq. (1.4)]");
  - the editors' own contribution, marked as theirs ("The proof that we have given is different, and perhaps simpler").
- **Errors** are stated plainly, with the corrected form and the probable intention (p. 49): "the 'corollaries' are
  incorrect. We shall give two corrected versions ... Most likely, the first versions are what Ramanujan had in mind."
  On p. 64: "Ramanujan [vol. 2, p. 202] has an erroneous factor of (-1)^(n-k)".
- **His text is not reproduced.** The memoir is read beside the facsimile, and the entry numbers are the link.

### 1.2 Netz, The Works of Archimedes, vol. 1 (CUP 2004): the faithful translation and two kinds of comment

- **The translation's goal** (pp. 1-4) is "to remove all barriers having to do with the foreign language itself, leaving
  all other barriers intact". It keeps his order, his formulaic language and his diagrams: "Style and mode of
  presentation are not incidental to a mathematical proof: they constitute its soul".
- **Interventions in the text are marked** (p. 3):
  - glosses in ⟨ ⟩;
  - the steps of a proof numbered (1), (2), ...;
  - constructions lettered (a), (b), ....
- **Footnotes give the grounds of each step:** the known result it uses (Euclid, cited by book and proposition),
  "to help the readers in checking the validity of the argument" (pp. 42-44).
- **After each proposition come two kinds of comment** (pp. 42-45):
  - textual comments: the readings, interpolations and the editor's decisions;
  - general comments: his practices and his character as an author ("He is an architect here, not a mason").
- **The introduction says what the comments do not try to do,** and where the reader finds it instead.

### 1.3 Neumann, The Mathematical Writings of Évariste Galois (EMS 2011): the warts-and-all French with the English

- **The earlier editors are judged by kind** (I.7, pp. 12-16):
  - Liouville made the mathematics available to mathematicians, correcting slips and displaying theorems ("He did
    his job well");
  - Tannery and Bourgne & Azra gave "an un-retouched photograph".
- **This edition does both** (I.8-I.10, pp. 17-32):
  - the French is warts and all: every deletion, insertion and spelling of his;
  - the facing English follows modern conventions: statements in italics, modern punctuation;
  - most of the struck material is translated, so that the two pages run in step.
- **Translation is interpretation** (I.8.1-3): the words of the period (géomètre, permutation and substitution,
  équation algébrique) get their own notes.
- **Each dossier opens with its physical description** (VI.1, pp. 201-203):
  - the size of the sheet, the cover annotation, the margins, the torn corner;
  - the earlier editions;
  - the dating evidence, and the editor's own doubt ("one small but puzzling detail ... the page is 0.5 cm shorter").
- **The margins of the French carry small notes** on discrepancies with earlier editions ("BA1962 has 'soit'") and on
  his work ("Three attempts to start this paragraph"; "Words supplied where page is torn off are conjectural").
- **The signs** (I.10, pp. 31-32):
  - ^above the line^, _below_, *margin*;
  - a replacement is written with no space before the insertion, an addition with one;
  - [?] stands for each illegible syllable.

### 1.4 Heath, The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements, vol. 3 (1908/1926): history first, then the proposition

- **Each book opens with a historical note** (Book XIII): who did what before the author, and how reliable each
  source is (the Pythagoreans, Theaetetus, Aristaeus, Hypsicles).
- **Each proposition is given in full,** with the results it uses in the margin: [VI. Def. 3, VI. 17], [VI. 1].
- **Heath's note after the proposition** (XIII.1):
  - its role ("in the nature of lemmas ... not in themselves of much importance");
  - its probable origin ("probable that the five theorems are due to Eudoxus");
  - the argument restated in modern terms, with the quicker route ("seen more readily by means of the figure of
    II. 11");
  - the textual questions, with each manuscript's evidence and each scholar's conjecture.

### 1.5 Whiteside, The Mathematical Papers of Isaac Newton, vol. 5 (CUP 1972): the running commentary in footnotes

- **The general introduction** (pp. xi-xii) states what the volume holds and how. The footnotes and the facing
  line-by-line English are "two species of running commentary". The introductions are "padded out with certain
  conjectures we have dared tentatively to formulate in lieu of any pertinent documentary evidence ... The reader will
  make his own mind up." Evidence and conjecture are kept openly apart.
- **The footnotes** are keyed to his lines, numbered per piece, short, exact and dry. They fall into a few kinds
  (pp. 33-45):
  - his drafts: "Newton first wrote '...'" with a translation; "Newton has cancelled '...'"; "'aequationem' is justly
    cancelled"; "The manuscript has the uncorrected original choice of equation ..., replaced in the sequel";
  - his slips: "Read '3'!", followed by the check (y = −2 gives 34, which has no factor −5); "A trivial slip for the
    correct '+14'"; "by a slip of its author's pen ... copied blindly by Whiston in his 1707 editio princeps";
  - the mathematics: "In modern terms, if each of the p-th derivatives ...", followed by where the idea first appears
    later in his own papers, with the shelfmark (ULC. Add. 3960.10: 173-7) and the date;
  - facts checked: "In fact, the three real roots of the quintic lie in ...";
  - cross-references: "compare 2, §1: note (555)".
- **The locators** sit in the running heads, e.g. [1, 1, §2] "Preliminary drafts", with the archive's shelfmarks.

### 1.6 Colmez and Serre (eds.), Correspondance Grothendieck-Serre (SMF 2001; English GS.pdf): Grothendieck himself

- **The foreword lists the only changes** (p. vii):
  - abbreviations expanded ("com." for "commutative");
  - spelling and typing slips corrected;
  - personal passages cut to [...].

  "Wrong statements have been preserved as they were. They are corrected (insofar as it was possible) in the Notes",
  and these notes "also give some more recent results". They are signalled by numbers in the margin.
- **Each letter opens with a headnote by someone who was there** (p. 1): "Grothendieck was in Lawrence (Kansas), where
  he had been invited (by N. Aronszajn, I believe) because of his work on Topological Vector Spaces. He had decided
  to change subjects ...".
- **The notes are short.** They identify references ("[Se53b]"), define terms ("(HLC) means 'homologically locally
  connected' ..."), and place a statement in the field ("In the Cartan seminar of 1948/1949, Cartan showed that ...").

### 1.7 Andrews and Berndt, Ramanujan's Lost Notebook, Part I (Springer 2005): order by topic, and the guides

- **Loose pages are organized by topic,** not by page: 442 entries in 18 chapters (preface).
- **A Location Guide** (p. 409) maps every page of the notebook to where it is discussed. **A Provenance section**
  (p. 415) gives the manuscript's history.

### 1.8 Three further models, found by search

- **Einstein's Zurich Notebook.** The Genesis of General Relativity, ed. J. Renn, Springer 2007: vol. 1 has the
  facsimile and the transcription, vol. 2 a line-by-line commentary by Janssen, Renn, Sauer, Norton and Stachel. It
  is a working notebook commented by specialists, line by line, in the order he wrote it, saying what he tried, where
  he went wrong and what came later.
- **Jacob Bernoulli's Meditationes,** in Bernoulli-Euler Online (University of Basel, https://bernoulli-euler.dhlab.unibas.ch/).
  It is a region-based, multilayer digital edition of the 367-page notebook. Each region of a page links to its
  diplomatic transcription, a normalized text following the logic of the entry, the translation, the commentary and
  the indexes. The mathematics is LaTeX inside XML.
- **Gödel's philosophical notebooks** (Gabelsberger shorthand). A German-English edition (De Gruyter, ed. E.-M. Engelen,
  tr. M. Carl), and an electronic edition whose transcriptions record their history: a first transcription, revisions,
  and the final one, each credited.
- Others not studied here:
  - the Riemann Nachlass (Weber and Dedekind 1876, Narasimhan's 1990 edition with commentary);
  - Bolzano's mathematical diaries (in preparation);
  - the methodology volume *Mathematical Correspondences and Critical Editions* (Springer 2019).

## 2. The design

### 2.1 The whole

- **The archive's topics are the volumes** (Andrews-Berndt order by topic, and the archive is catalogued by topic). The
  folders of a topic are its chapters, in the order of his work where it can be dated (Whiteside), else in
  catalogue order.
- **A unit** is one piece of his writing: a numbered section, list or question of his, a dated session, a
  construction with its proof attempt, a letter, a typed draft with his annotations. He makes most of the divisions
  himself ("Questions ouvertes", 1), 2), 3)), and we keep his numbering (Berndt's "ENTRY n."). Where he makes none, the
  unit is the stretch of argument that one headnote can introduce.
- **Locators** take the form cote:scan.line, for example 22:20.3, together with his own page number (\mspage) and his
  own numbering. They are printed in the running heads and in every note (Whiteside's [1, 1, §2]).

### 2.2 The chapter's introduction (one per folder)

Five parts, each in plain prose, a few paragraphs in all:
1. **The folder as an object** (Neumann's dossier description):
   - the catalogue's title and what the folder physically holds (offprints, typescripts, his notes, letters), with
     their numbers of pages;
   - his own page numbers and dates;
   - the hands and reused sheets (the back of a typed page, a varitypist's stamp);
   - the dating evidence, with any doubt stated as a doubt.
2. **What he does in it, in today's terms** (Berndt's survey):
   - the lines of work;
   - which results were standard at the time, which he reproves, which appear new, and which became famous later;
   - what he leaves open.
3. **A little history** (Heath's historical note):
   - before: the sources he uses or answers, cited by exact locators;
   - after: where it went in his published work (EGA, SGA, papers) and in the work of others, with dates.
4. **His notation** (Berndt): his notation is kept in the text, with a table to today's notation and terms (his
   "formellement simple" for today's "formellement lisse", for example).
5. **This edition of the folder:**
   - who transcribed it (models with efforts, dates), who checked what, the measured reliability;
   - earlier work on it, credited (Hua's transcription, by commit);
   - what the commentary does not try to do, and where that is found (Netz).

### 2.3 The unit

Four elements, always in this order:

1. **The headnote.** Two to six sentences, before his text, in the voice of a colleague who knows the period (the
   Colmez-Serre headnote; Whiteside's introductions to each piece). It says where we are in the folder and in his work,
   the date, what he is doing in today's terms, and what comes before and after. It may say "we think" where it
   conjectures, and says so (Whiteside).

2. **His text, French with English facing** (Neumann; Whiteside).
   - **The French is the reading text.** It is his words in his order, with his numbering, his notation and his
     spelling:
     - his abbreviations expanded in square brackets: f[ormellemen]t, hom[omorphisme];
     - our glosses in ⟨ ⟩, never mixed with his words (Netz);
     - in a proof, his steps numbered in the margin (1), (2), ... where the commentary needs to refer to them (Netz);
     - his insertions placed where he put them;
     - his margins set as marginal notes beside the line they belong to;
     - his struck passages left out of the reading text. Each one that matters, a false start or a changed statement
       or word, gets a note: "He first wrote ...; he struck ..." (Whiteside). The rest are kept in the diplomatic
       layer;
     - an uncertain reading printed as read, with a note giving the alternative.
   - **The English** is a close, faithful translation in modern conventions: statements in italics, punctuation
     modernized, his abbreviations written out (Neumann). Our glosses carry over in ⟨ ⟩. The struck passages that the
     French notes discuss are translated in the notes.
   - **The diplomatic transcription** (our layer 1 as it is: every deletion, insertion, margin, doubt mark, each line
     linked to its region of the scan) stays the source of record. In the digital reader it is one click from every
     line (Bernoulli-Euler Online). In print it is in the volume's appendix or its online supplement. Neumann's signs
     are the model for its printed key.

3. **The notes,** numbered per unit and set at the foot of the page, where his line is (Whiteside). Each is one to
   four sentences, opens with the locator, and is of one of these kinds, in fixed phrasings:
   - *the grounds:* the result he uses, by exact locator, the reader's check (Netz, Heath): "By EGA 0_IV 19.3.5."
   - *in today's terms:* "In today's terms: ..." (Whiteside);
   - *a step he leaves out:* "The step left implicit: ...";
   - *his draft:* "He first wrote '...'"; "He struck '...', which ...";
   - *his slip:* "Read '...'", with the check that shows it (Whiteside); a slip is called a slip, plainly;
   - *the reading:* "Reading uncertain: '...' or '...'; the ink ... (scan 20, line 3)";
   - *a link:* to his other folders ("the same question in folder 23, scan 12") and to his published work ("this is
     EGA IV 11.1.1, published 1966"), with dates;
   - *a fact checked:* "In fact, ..." (Whiteside).

   No note interprets at length. Interpretation belongs in the headnote, and the developments in the status block.

4. **The status of his claims and questions,** after the unit (Berndt's entries; the Colmez-Serre notes on wrong
   statements and later results). One block per claim or question of his, in his numbering. Each block holds:
   - the statement as he makes it (a pointer to his line), and today's precise statement with all hypotheses, marking
     what is our reconstruction (Berndt);
   - **as he left it,** with his date: proved there; sketched; stated without proof; corrected by himself later (both
     places); false as stated (with the counterexample and the corrected statement, and the version he most likely
     had in mind, as Berndt does); or open;
   - **later,** dated and with exact references: in his own published work ("EGA IV 7.8, 1965"); proved by others
     ("M. André 1974"); still open, with the best known result;
   - **developed further in** our work: a line and a link to C2 (our proofs) or C3 (open problems), dated, with who
     did it (model and effort) and its check status. It never reads "solved two days later".

### 2.4 How AI work and our results are marked

- **The volume's front matter names everything once.** It gives the AI draft notice (COMPANION_MODEL section 4) beside
  the rights notice, and it states which models produced which layers, with their efforts and the checks.
- **A glossary of model names comes first, and it only names.** The user, 18 Nov 2026: "It should just be on model
  naming glossary, and just basically saying this is the company, this is their model, this is what it means." The
  short name is the model, its version and its effort ("Astra 6 Pro, 6.2 Max, or Astra 6.2 Max ... Opus 5.5 Max ...
  Fable 5.2 Max. That's how you say that"). Work and web are the same model, so the short name does not distinguish
  them. For example:

  | Short name | Company | Model | What the name means |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Astra 6 Pro | OpenAI | GPT-6 Astra | run in ChatGPT's Pro mode, its longest-reasoning setting |
  | Astra 6.2 Max | OpenAI | GPT-6.2 Astra | at its maximum reasoning effort |
  | Astra 6 Max | OpenAI | GPT-6 Astra | at its maximum reasoning effort |
  | Opus 5.5 Max, Opus 5.5 High | Anthropic | Claude Opus 5.5 | at its maximum, or its high, effort setting |
  | Fable 5.2 Max | Anthropic | Claude Fable 5.2 | at its maximum effort setting |

  The volume's own table lists only the models that worked on it, from the run records (an API run, for instance,
  with the model and effort recorded for it). Hua's transcription is not ours and keeps its own credit line.
- **What each model did, why, and how it was tested go in an appendix at the back,** not in the front matter (see
  2.5).
- **On the page, a tag says whose work a reading, a note or a development is, by short name:**
  - a reading decided against the ink: "reading: Astra 6.2 Max, 18 Nov 2026";
  - a reconstruction of ours: "our reconstruction";
  - a development of ours: "our result, C2 §x, checked by (who, date)".

  His words are never in our paragraphs, and ours are never in his.
- **Earlier work is credited where it is used,** with its version: Hua's transcription, by commit; the published
  editions.

### 2.5 The volume's apparatus

- **The introduction:**
  - the archive and the history of its papers;
  - each earlier transcription or edition, described fairly by kind (Neumann I.7);
  - our method, with the measured error rates;
  - the signs;
  - what the companion does not do (Netz);
  - the AI draft notice.
- **The guides and indexes:**
  - a **location guide**: every scan to the unit that discusses it (Andrews-Berndt);
  - a **claims index**: every claim of his with its status line;
  - a **problem index**: his open questions across the folders, each with its occurrences;
  - a **notation index**: his notation against today's;
  - the references, every one checked.
- **An appendix: the models, a short primer** (one or two pages, at the back). The user, 18 Nov 2026: "imagine you're a
  random person reading this and you're not deep in AI. What would you want to know about these models and why they
  were used and why we give a shit ... not at the beginning". It answers, plainly:
  - **what these models are:** large language models from OpenAI and Anthropic, which read images and write text,
    LaTeX and code; what a "reasoning effort" setting changes (time spent on each step) and what Pro mode is;
  - **what each one did here, and why that one:** Astra 6.2 Max read the ink because it read best on the controls; Astra
    6 Pro did the mathematical working-through, which needs long reasoning; the Claude models designed, checked and
    assembled;
  - **how they were tested:** control folders whose readings were checked line by line against the scans, scored in
    errors per 100 words, marked and unmarked (CTRL-148: 1.69 for Astra 6.2 Max against 3.26 for Astra 6 Pro's first
    pass, with the caveats), and the second control;
  - **how they go wrong:** a plausible word in place of the written one, a plural read as a singular, an index or a sign
    misread; a note that rests on its own misreading. These are why every doubtful reading is marked and every
    reading carries its tag;
  - **why this matters to a reader:** the tags and status lines let the reader weigh each reading and each claim; nothing
    is called checked unless a check is recorded with who, how and when.

### 2.6 The digital reader

- **Region links** (Bernoulli-Euler Online): every line of the reading text links to its region of the scan and to the
  diplomatic line; the scan shows the regions.
- **The layers are toggles:** reading text, diplomatic, English, notes, status.
- **Links out:** each status line links to C2 or C3 and to the literature by DOI.
- **The guides are indexes,** searchable by object, by notation and by his words.

### 2.7 What the runs must output for a script to set this

The working-through runs (`second_pass/PLAN_WORKING_THROUGH_PRO_20261118.md`) deliver, per folder:
- `units.json`: id, locators, his title or ours, the date, the headnote (LaTeX);
- the corrected diplomatic pages (TeX), plus `expansions.tsv` (each abbreviation with its expansion, per occurrence);
  the reading text is built from these by script;
- `english.tex`: the facing translation, unit by unit, line-aligned to the reading text;
- `notes.json`: unit, locator, kind (one of the eight above), text (LaTeX), sources (earlier note ids consumed);
- `claims.json`: id (his numbering), unit, locator, his statement, today's statement, reconstruction, as he left it
  (status and date), later (fate, date, references), developed further (C2 or C3 link, who, check), confidence;
- `chapter_intro.tex`: the five parts of 2.2;
- `references.bib`, every entry checked (link or DOI opened), and `CHECK_EVENTS.tsv`.


**How the runs are cut** (18 Nov 02:21 UTC, for runs of 1-2 hours; SP04, 120 scans in one run, crashed after 5 hours):
- run A per slice of about 12-17 pages: the reading on the ink, the units and headnotes, the keyed notes, the
  consumed earlier notes, the status of each claim from the literature, the open questions listed;
- run B per slice, text only: those open questions attacked under the push rule and the dated ledger;
- the English facing text is a separate translation pass over the corrected reading text, unit by unit.

### 4.1 The units

A unit is one piece of mathematics he worked on as a whole: a group of statements with their proofs, a computation, a
list of questions, a letter, a plan. A unit is usually 1-6 scans long, and a folder has between 3 and 20 of them. Run A
draws the boundaries, in `units.json`, from the pages and their notes. Cote 22, from the Pro first pass and its 93
notes, would come out roughly as:

| Unit | Scans | What it is |

### 4.2 Which notes go in

Every note on record about a folder gets a verdict in that folder's run A (PLAN section 2): kept, merged, corrected,
or dropped. That covers Pro's heavy notes (13.3 MB over 105 finals), Work's light notes (about 1,900 words a folder)
and the notes of the controls.

- **A note goes into layer 1 when it tells the reader something about his page.** That means: what the page does, where

- **Dropped:** notes that rest on a misreading, duplicates, and notes with no content. Every drop is recorded with its
