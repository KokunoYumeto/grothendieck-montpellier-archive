## 2. Verdicts on the main question: Th. 4

### JAC-T4.1: the primitive incidence summand. Targeted proof check, confirmed.

- The primitive projector e_h on H^{2m−1}(A) is algebraic: Lefschetz standard conjecture and Künneth projectors for abelian
  varieties, Kleiman 1968, 2A11.
- The incidence-detection lemma JAC-P01 makes e_h an idempotent on Saito's higher Picard variety P^m(A). I did not check
  JAC-P01 itself.
- e_h is self-transpose, by the orthogonality of the Lefschetz decomposition.
- So the duality is block-diagonal, and the block on B = P^m(A)_prim is nondegenerate.
- The normalized family z has cohomology in H¹(B) ⊗ H^n(A)_prim.

### JAC-T4.2: the curve test on J(C) × A. Targeted proof check, confirmed; I recomputed it.

- (a × 1)_*[C × A] = θ^{b−1}/(b − 1)! × [A] (Poincaré's formula), so ∫_{J×A} w²θ^{b−1} = (b − 1)! ∫_{C×A} ξ².
- w is primitive for H = θ + h on J × A:
  - hw = 0 by middle primitivity on A;
  - θ^b w = 0, since its degree on J is 2b + 1.
- The Hodge-index sign in codimension m on dimension b + 2m − 1 is (−1)^m, with exponent b − 1.
- The algebraicity of w uses the Lefschetz inverse on J, which is algebraic (Lieberman, Kleiman).
- The input is 032, Corollary 8.4: the Hodge standard conjecture for every abelian variety in every characteristic.

### JAC-T4.3: Th. 4 for abelian varieties. Targeted proof check, confirmed.

**The statement.** For every abelian A of dimension 2m − 1 over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic,
ℓ_B = ((−1)^m/2)Δ^*d_B is ample on P^m(A)_prim.

**The proof.**
- deg_C ℓ_B = ((−1)^m/2) deg(z_C · z_C) ≥ 0 by T4.2, so ℓ_B is nef.
- d_B is nondegenerate, so χ ≠ 0.
- Nef and nondegenerate on an abelian variety gives ample, by Mumford's index theorem. This holds in every characteristic.

**Sign calibration.** On an elliptic curve, P = Δ − E × 0 − 0 × E induces −φ_{O(0)}, and deg Δ^*P = −2, so m = 1 gives
the positive polarization. The sign (−1)^m against (−1)^{m−1} is correctly explained as divisor incidence against the cup
product.

**Over an arbitrary field (JAC-T4.5).** Galois descent and purely inseparable descent of the idempotent. Ampleness descends
(Stacks 0D2P). Confirmed.

**Bearing, and the step from the archive.**
- Grothendieck's margin on our scan 008 ("il faut tenir compte de la décomposition primitive") is exactly the qualification
  needed.
- JAC-P03 shows it is essential: on E³ the uncorrected correspondence is indefinite.
- What is new is the passage from the Hodge standard conjecture to the polarization, by curve tests. It is not the
  unproved claim that a nondegenerate correspondence is a polarization.

**Novelty.**

- In characteristic p it is new, conditional on 032 Corollary 8.4 (through Milne's report).
- Milne's report does not state it: a text search for "intermediate", "Picard variet" and "polariz" finds only the Hodge
  standard conjecture itself (§3.6–3.10).

## 3. Verdicts on Th. 5 (H9) and the comparison with H04 and HODGE_SIGN

### H9-C1: Th. 5 fails as displayed, for every m ≥ 2 and every characteristic. Targeted proof check, confirmed; I recomputed it.

**The example.**
- X is the smooth 2m-dimensional quadric uv + Σw_iz_i = 0, with the pencil au + bv = 0.
- The base B is Q^{2m−2}. The two singular members (u = 0, v = 0) are cones, so every fibre is geometrically integral.
- Z = P_{∞,+} − P_{∞,−} is a difference of m-planes from the two families. Its square is
  (α − β)² = α² + β² − 2αβ = 2(−1)^m, using [P]² = [h^m](1 + h)^{m+1}/(1 + 2h) = (1 + (−1)^m)/2.
- On the generic odd quadric Y over K, the two (m − 1)-planes are rationally equivalent over K, through the K-conic C_t. So
  Z|_Y = 0 in CH^m(Y).
- The displayed formula would give (−1)^m Z·Z = 2, while the pairing is 0.

**The local corrections.** Let e = j_*p^*δ_B. Then:
- e² = −δ_B² = 2(−1)^m, and t_X = q^*Z has t_X² = 2(−1)^m, with t_X·e = 0;
- u_∞ = t_X − e and u₀ = −t_X − e, so the matrix is diag(4(−1)^m, 4(−1)^m) and t_X·u = (±2(−1)^m);
- each fibre's Schur term is (2(−1)^m)²/(4(−1)^m) = (−1)^m. After the sign (−1)^m, each node contributes 1, and the two
  cancel the 2.
