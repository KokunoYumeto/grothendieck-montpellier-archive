## 2. The mechanism

## 3. The obstacle, exactly

## 4. The next tool: the hexagonal self-similarity

**The geometry (verified by hand).** For y² = x³ + ax + b, D = 4a³ + 27b², the double cover of the x-line branched
at the four roots of the 3-division polynomial 3x⁴ + 6ax² + 12bx − a² is Y² = Z³ + D/108, via
t² = x⁴ + 2ax² + 4bx − a²/3, X = (t + x² + a)/2, Z = X − a/3, Y = Xx + b/2 (E.190.8). Indeed
X² − aX + a²/3 = x²X + bx, so Y² − Z³ = b²/4 + a³/27. The two points over x = ∞ are O and P = (−a/3, −b/2). So the
3-division quartic of every elliptic curve is equianharmonic, and E ↦ P identifies the modular orbifold with
(E₀ ∖ O)/μ₆, E₀: Y² = Z³ + 1, with j/1728 = −Z(P)³. This is the fibre of M₁,₂ → M₁,₁ over the hexagonal point.

**The groups.** Γ₁,₂ = B₄/centre (a, b, c = σ₁, σ₂, σ₃), q: Γ₁,₂ → Γ₁,₁, (a,b,c) ↦ (u,v,u), kernel K = π₁(E ∖ O).
The map **φ: u ↦ ab, v ↦ cb** is a homomorphism B → Γ₁,₂ with φ(s) = Δ₄ (order 2), φ(r) = abcb (order 3),
φ(τ) = (ab)⁻¹, and image in G₃ = q⁻¹⟨R₊⟩, R₊ = uv of order 6. Proof of the relations: (ab)(cb)(ab) = abc·bab =
abc·aba = Δ₄ = (cb)(ab)(cb), and abcb is conjugate to abca, whose cube is Δ₄². φ is an isomorphism onto G₃: it sends
u⁻¹v to b⁻¹(ca⁻¹)b ∈ K, is onto on K^ab (a unit multiple of a generator of the Z[R₊]-module Z²), and sends τ⁶ to
the puncture loop (ab)⁻⁶; a homomorphism of free groups of rank two with these properties is an isomorphism
(Nielsen). On completions one also needs the full topology of K in Γ̂₁,₂ (E.182). This is my explicit form of the
map; E.190.3 defines φ geometrically and I did not compare the two markings. All of GT̂ acts on B̂₄/centre by the standard braid action Φ⁽²⁾ (LNS 2026,
§1 and Theorem 2.1). It is compatible with q: ba²b maps to −u⁻², which commutes with u², so Φ⁽²⁾(c) maps to u.
