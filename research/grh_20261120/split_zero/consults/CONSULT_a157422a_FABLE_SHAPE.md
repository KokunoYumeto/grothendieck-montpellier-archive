## Q1. The statement and its framework

### 1.1 The typing layer: G(R) is the value semiring of a norm

**Proposition 1 [proved here].** For a positive semidefinite Hermitian space (V, Q) put N(v) = Q(v, v) for v ≠ 0 and
N(0) = τ, with values in G(R≥0). Then N(v ⊕ v′) = N(v) ⊕ N(v′) on orthogonal sums and N(v ⊗ v′) = N(v) ⊙ N(v′) on
tensor products. The radical of Q is N⁻¹(A), A = {e, τ} the Boolean pair.
Proof. v ⊕ v′ = 0 iff both vanish, and τ is the additive identity; v ⊗ v′ = 0 iff one vanishes, and τ absorbs; a
nonzero null vector has N = e, and e + e = e, e·x = e for x ≠ τ. ∎

So e is "a direction is present and the form vanishes on it", τ is "no vector". Instance 1 of section 11 is this
bookkeeping; instance 2 is its smallest case (below).

**Operator form of e [standard].** In the Toeplitz extension 0 → K → T → C(S¹) → 0, T is generated by an isometry S
with S*S = 1 and SS* = 1 − e₀; the index map sends [z] to −[e₀]. The idempotent e₀ is the defect of the isometry;
the isometry is unitary exactly when e₀ = 0. This is the "−1 per generator" of section 2.3, and Theorem 2 below is
the statement that a degenerate positive form kills the defect.

**Sheaf form.** p_R is extension by zero, χ_R the domain of definition; a supported zero is a zero inside the
domain. The "projective inflow" of section 6.1 is, for U = P¹ ∖ {0, 1, ∞}, the vanishing of the composite
H¹(U) → H¹(Z; B_j) → H²_c(U) in the long exact sequence of v35's boundary defect B_j = Cone(Rj_! → Rj_*) (v35, TeX
line 7548): the residue vector of a logarithmic form has sum zero. v35 already carries the three states (absent,
vanishing vector, nonzero vector) for anomaly lines (Proposition "Absent, vanishing, and nonzero anomalous

### 1.2 The confinement layer, circle form

**Theorem 2 (kernel line; Carathéodory–Fejér 1911, Grenander–Szegő §4.1; this proof written here).** Let Q be a
Toeplitz Hermitian form on V_n = polynomials of degree ≤ n, Q(z^j, z^k) = c_{j−k}. Suppose Q ≥ 0, Q is positive
definite on V_{n−1}, and Q is degenerate. Then:
(a) the radical is a line, spanned by a polynomial P of exact degree n;
(b) f ↦ zf mod P is a unitary operator U on (V_{n−1}, Q), and P is its characteristic polynomial;
(c) all zeros of P lie on |z| = 1 and are simple;
(d) adj(T) = c·ξξ*, c > 0, ξ the coefficient vector of P: the adjugate of the degenerate form is the projector on
    the kernel line. If the corank is ≥ 2, or T = 0, then adj(T) = 0 and no line is defined.
Proof. (a) rad ∩ V_{n−1} = 0, so dim rad ≤ 1 and deg P = n. (b) For f, g ∈ V_{n−1}, Q(zf, zg) = Q(f, g), and P is in
the radical, so Q(Uf, Ug) = Q(f, g). U is multiplication by z on C[z]/(P), with cyclic vector 1. (c) A unitary
operator is diagonalizable with spectrum on the circle; with a cyclic vector its eigenvalues are distinct. Direct
form: if P = (z − a)R then Q(P, R) = 0 gives Q(zR, R) = a·Q(R, R), and Q(P, P) = 0 gives (1 − |a|²)Q(R, R) = 0.
(d) Linear algebra. ∎

**Theorem 3 (the same statement in algebraic geometry) [standard: Weil; Hartshorne V.1.9 and Ex. V.1.10; assembled
here].** Let C be a smooth projective curve of genus g over F_q, N_k = #C(F_{q^k}), c_k = (q^k + 1 − N_k)/q^{k/2}.
In Num(C × C)_R let h₁, h₂ be the two rulings and v_j = Γ_j − (Γ_j·h₂)h₁ − (Γ_j·h₁)h₂ for the graph Γ_j of the j-th
power of Frobenius. Then v_j·v_k = −q^{(j+k)/2} c_{j−k} [proved here: Γ_j·Γ_k = q^k N_{j−k} for j > k, Γ_j² =
q^j(2 − 2g)]. So the Toeplitz matrix (c_{j−k}) is the Gram matrix of the classes q^{−j/2}v_j for the negative of the
intersection form, on the orthogonal complement of the hyperbolic plane ⟨h₁, h₂⟩. The Hodge index theorem makes it
positive semidefinite. By Theorem 2, its first singular section has a kernel line, and the kernel polynomial is the
radical of the normalized L-polynomial of C: the Frobenius eigenvalues satisfy |α| = √q.

The rulings h₁, h₂ are null vectors of the intersection form (h_i² = 0, h_i ≠ 0): supported zeros of the canonical
form. They are the poles of the zeta function (H⁰ and H²). So instance 6 (the pole) and instance 2 (the Lorentz null
cone) are the hyperbolic plane of a Hodge-index form, and instance 3 is its definite complement.

**Corank one in algebraic geometry is Zariski's lemma [standard: Barth–Hulek–Peters–Van de Ven III.8.2].** On the
components of a fibre of a fibred surface the intersection form is negative semidefinite with radical spanned by the
fibre. For a fibre of type I_r this is the circulant (cyclic Toeplitz) matrix with kernel (1, …, 1), whose polynomial
1 + z + … + z^{r−1} has its zeros at the nontrivial r-th roots of unity. The augmentation −Σ with kernel A_{r−1} of
section 2 is pairing with that kernel line, and Shioda–Tate (section 2, item 8) is the decomposition into the
hyperbolic plane, the fibre parts and a definite Mordell–Weil part.

**The small instances [proved here].** A 2 × 2 Toeplitz matrix [[a, b], [b̄, a]] is positive semidefinite and
singular iff |b| = a; its kernel polynomial is z − u with |u| = 1. Inside the null cone of det on Herm₂ (instance 2)
the shift-invariant forms are those with direction on one great circle, which is the "great circle" of section 10.5.
For c_k ≡ 1 the kernel polynomial is z − 1; in the coordinate z = e^w its zeros are the periods 2πiZ, on the fixed
line of w ↦ −w̄. Instance 4 is Theorem 2 at n = 1 in the logarithmic coordinate. The poles of a curve's zeta function,
the zeros of 1 − q^{−s} and 1 − q^{1−s}, are of this type.

### 1.3 The confinement layer, half-line form: the main session's candidate

**Theorem 4 [standard: Aissen–Schoenberg–Whitney and Edrei, J. Analyse Math. 2 (1952); Thoma, Math. Z. 85 (1964);
checked and placed here].** Write ξ(½ + z) = Σ a_{2n} z^{2n} (all a_{2n} > 0, unconditionally), e_n = a_{2n}/a₀,
E(w) = Σ e_n w^n, an entire function of order ½. Define Z(2ℓ) by Newton's identities from the e_n (under RH,
Z(2ℓ) = Σ_{γ>0} γ^{−2ℓ}). The following are equivalent:
(i) RH;
(ii) (e_n) is a Pólya frequency sequence: the Toeplitz matrix (e_{i−j}) is totally nonnegative;
(iii) the specialization ρ_ζ of the ring Λ of symmetric functions, e_n ↦ e_n, is nonnegative on every Schur function;
(iv) the central function on S_∞ that is multiplicative over disjoint cycles with value Z(2ℓ)/Z(2)^ℓ on an ℓ-cycle
     is positive definite.
Proof. (ii) ⟺ (iii): the minors of the Toeplitz matrix are the skew Schur functions (dual Jacobi–Trudi), and skew
Schur functions are nonnegative combinations of Schur functions. (ii) ⟹ (i): by Edrei's theorem
E(w) = e^{cw}Π(1 + β_i w)/Π(1 − α_i w); E is entire of order ½, so there are no α_i and c = 0, and the zeros of
ξ(½ + z) are z = ±i/√β_i. (i) ⟹ (ii): E(w) = Π(1 + w/γ²) with Σγ^{−2} < ∞. (iii) ⟺ (iv): Thoma's theorem, after
normalizing p₁ = 1; the generating function of the character is G(w) = 1/E(−w/Z(2)), which determines the Thoma
parameters uniquely. ∎

So the candidate is correct. Under A the character is extremal with Thoma parameters α_k = γ_k^{−2}/Z(2), β = 0,
Σα_k = 1 [computed: Z(2) = 0.0231049931, α₁ = 0.21663, α₂ = 0.09794, α₃ = 0.06919; the 96 Jacobi–Trudi determinants
with |λ| ≤ 9 are all positive]. I have not seen (iv) stated in the literature and did not search; (ii) is the
classical Pólya–Schur form (Katkova, Comput. Methods Funct. Theory 7 (2007), studies its minors).

**Is it the precise form of the shape?** It is one of three coordinate forms of one positive point:
- circle, additive: positive semidefinite Hermitian Toeplitz forms (Herglotz, Bochner; Weil and Li for ζ);
- half-line, additive: positive semidefinite Hankel forms of the power sums Z(2ℓ) (Stieltjes, Hermite);
- half-line, multiplicative: total nonnegativity of the Toeplitz matrix of the e_n (Edrei–Thoma).
For ζ each is equivalent to RH. The third adds the canonical-basis language and the semigroup structure (products of
ξ-functions are unions of parameters; positivity is preserved because the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients are
nonnegative). It does not contain the typing layer except through Theorem 5(c), and it concerns a positive point of
Λ. GCT's PH1 asks for something of another level: a positive formula for structure constants (Kronecker, plethysm).
Lusztig's canonical-basis positivity gives positive structure constants, and that is what makes "totally positive
point" definable (Lusztig, "Total positivity in reductive groups", 1994). A supplies a point; it supplies no
structure constants.

### 1.4 The statement that covers both forms

**Theorem 5 (support of a positive point) [proved here].** Let (R, B) be a commutative ring with a basis B ∋ 1 whose
structure constants are nonnegative (Λ with the Schur basis; a representation ring; a ring with a canonical basis),
C = N⟨B⟩ its cone, and x: R → ℝ a ring homomorphism with x(B) ⊂ R≥0.
(a) c ↦ [x(c) ≠ 0] is a semiring homomorphism C → B (the Boolean semiring).
(b) For b, b′ ∈ B: x(b) = 0 or x(b′) = 0 iff x(b″) = 0 for every b″ occurring in b·b′. The zero set of x on B is
    decided by the occurrence calculus of (R, B).
(c) For R = Λ and E_x a polynomial of degree r: x is Schur-nonnegative iff E_x(w) = Π(1 + α_k w) with all α_k > 0
    [ASW 1952]; then s_λ(x) > 0 iff λ has at most r rows; and the Hankel form (p_{i+j+1}(x))_{0≤i,j≤r} is positive
    semidefinite, its kernel is the set of polynomials of degree ≤ r vanishing on {α_k}, and for distinct α_k this is
    the line spanned by the coefficients of Π(z − α_k).
Proof. (a) is Theorem 6 below applied to x: C → R≥0. (b) x(bb′) = x(b)x(b′) = Σ c^{b″} x(b″), a sum of nonnegative
terms. (c) Tableaux give s_λ(α) > 0 iff ℓ(λ) ≤ r for positive α; the Hankel form is Σ_k α_k v(α_k)v(α_k)ᵀ with
v(α) = (1, α, …, α^r), and the kernel of a positive sum is the intersection of the kernels (Theorem 7). ∎

## Q2. Two regimes: precise or analogy

**Theorem 6 (criterion) [proved here; the equivalence (i) ⟺ (ii) is in Golan's Semirings and their Applications].**
For a commutative semiring P with 1 ≠ 0 the following are equivalent:
(i) x ↦ [x ≠ 0] is a semiring homomorphism P → B;
(ii) P is zero-sum-free and has no zero divisors;
(iii) p_P: G(P) → P has a semiring section (then unique: s(0) = τ, s(x) = x otherwise; its image contains no e);
(iv) for every arithmetic circuit over P with nonzero constants and every input, the output is nonzero iff the
     monotone Boolean shadow of the circuit is 1.
Proof. (i) ⟺ (ii) is the definition read twice. (ii) ⟹ (iii): s is additive since x + y ≠ 0 for x, y ≠ 0, and
multiplicative since xy ≠ 0. (iii) ⟹ (ii): a section sends 0 to τ and fixes the rest; x + y = 0 with x, y ≠ 0 would
give τ = x ⊕ y = e. (i) ⟹ (iv) gate by gate; (iv) ⟹ (ii) with the circuits x + y and xy. ∎
For a nonzero ring none of these holds (the note, section 3, item 4, has (iii) for rings).

**Theorem 7 (the same for forms) [standard linear algebra; proved here].** For positive semidefinite A, B:
ker(A + B) = ker A ∩ ker B, so the support map A ↦ im A is additive into the lattice of subspaces. A Hermitian form
has a linear null set iff it is semidefinite; the null cone of an indefinite form spans the space.
Proof. ⟨(A + B)v, v⟩ = 0 forces both terms to vanish. For indefinite Q take orthogonal e, f with Q(e) = 1,
Q(f) = −1; e ± f are null and span e, f; add the radical. ∎

So the two regimes are the two sides of one criterion. In the positive regime support is functorial: no supported
zero occurs (Theorem 6), and kernels are computed by a monotone rule (Theorem 7). Carathéodory–Fejér, Theorem 5(c)
and Razborov's method all use exactly this. In the ring regime the failure set is the set of supported zeros.

**Corollary (section 9, sharpened) [proved here].** Every circuit C over (XOR, AND, 1) computing f carries, at no
cost, a monotone circuit computing a monotone majorant m_C ≥ f, and its supported zeros are m_C⁻¹(1) ∖ f⁻¹(1). For
monotone f: mon(f) ≤ min{|C| : m_C = f} ≤ 3·mon(f) (OR = x ⊕ y ⊕ xy never produces e on {τ, 1}). Supported zeros at
inner gates cost nothing; only the output matters. In G(F₂) negation x ↦ 1 ⊕ x satisfies N³ = N and N²(x) = x ⊕ e:
double negation sends τ to e. (Non-cancellative circuits: Sengupta–Venkateswaran, TCS 237 (2000).)

**The exact bridge inside representation theory [standard].** s_λ = det(e_{λ′_i − i + j}) (Jacobi–Trudi, with
cancellation) = Σ over semistandard tableaux (no cancellation). A specialization lies in the positive regime iff
every one of these determinants is nonnegative (Theorem 4). In GCT's terms this is the difference between a GapP
formula and a #P formula, and PH1 (item 10(b) of prompt 54) asks for the tableau side for Kronecker and plethysm
coefficients.

**The same dichotomy for strata [standard].** Support patterns of Plücker coordinates of a totally nonnegative point
of a Grassmannian are positroids, their strata are balls, and realizability is combinatorial (Postnikov,
arXiv:math/0609764; Galashin–Karp–Lam 2022). For arbitrary real points the strata realize every semialgebraic
homotopy type and realizability is as hard as the existential theory of the reals (Mnëv 1988; Shor 1991).

**Where it is a duality: the explicit formula.**

**Theorem 8 (finite model) [proved here; the content is classical].** Let c_k = Σ_i m_i u_i^k (k ∈ Z), u_i ∈ C×
distinct, m_i > 0, with c_{−k} = c̄_k. Equivalent: (a) all |u_i| = 1; (b) every Toeplitz section (c_{j−k}) is positive
semidefinite; (c) |c_k| ≤ c₀ for all k; (d) (c_k) is bounded.
Proof. (a) ⟹ (b): Q(f, f) = Σ m_i |f(u_i)|². (b) ⟹ (c): 2 × 2 minors. (d) ⟹ (a): if ρ = max|u_i| > 1, then
c_k/ρ^k is, up to o(1), a trigonometric sum with mean square Σ m_i² > 0 over the u_i of modulus ρ; so |c_k| ≥ cρ^k
infinitely often. For min|u_i| < 1 use k → −∞. ∎
For a curve: RH ⟺ Hodge-index positivity of the Frobenius Gram matrix ⟺ |N_k − q^k − 1| ≤ 2g·q^{k/2} for all k.
Confinement of the zeros, positivity of the form, and square-root cancellation in the counts are one statement.

Lee–Yang in this form [standard: Lee–Yang 1952; the reading through Theorem 8 made here]: for a ferromagnetic Ising
model the zeros u_i of the partition function in the fugacity lie on |u| = 1. By Theorem 8 this is the positive
semidefiniteness of the Toeplitz matrix of the power sums c_k = Σ u_i^k, which are k times the coefficients of the
expansion of the logarithm of the partition function. The source of positivity is the ferromagnetic coupling, where
for curves it is the Hodge index. Section 13's reading of RH as the Lee–Yang property of the Bost–Connes system
is the case of Weil's form.

For ζ the same three are: RH; Weil positivity (Weil 1952; Bombieri, Rend. Lincei 2000); and
Σ_{n≤x} μ(n) = O(x^{½+ε}) (Littlewood 1912; Titchmarsh, Theorem 14.25(C)) [standard]. So the two regimes of section
11 are the two sides of the explicit formula: the zero side is a positive sum, the prime side an alternating one,
and RH says that confinement on one side is square-root cancellation on the other. Under A all three hold for ζ.

**Where it breaks [open].**
1. There is no involution exchanging the regimes; they are properties of a presentation, not of the function. The
   same Weil form is alternating on the prime side and positive on the zero side.
2. For circuits there is no transform playing the part of the explicit formula. GCT proposes one (multiplicities
   in coordinate rings). What exists without it: separations inside the positive regime (Razborov; Jerrum–Snir),
   savings from cancellation in particular cases (Valiant, "Negation can be exponentially powerful", 1980; Tardos),
   and in proof complexity the failure of positivity certificates on parity (Grigoriev 2001; Schoenebeck 2008).
3. The direction of "power" depends on the task. Confined zeros make evaluation easy (zero-free regions give
   approximation algorithms: Barvinok's interpolation method); free zeros make expression easy.

## Q3. What A changes that sections 12–14 have not drawn out

1. **No finite corank-one stage [proved here, given A].** Under RH, W(f * f*) = Σ_ρ |f̂(ρ)|² for f ∈ C_c^∞(R₊*), and
   this is positive for f ≠ 0: f̂(½ + it) is entire of exponential type in t, so it has O(R) zeros in |t| ≤ R, while
   ζ has about (R/π)·log R. So the Weil form is positive definite on every window [λ⁻¹, λ]; its least eigenvalue
   ε(λ) is positive, decreasing, and tends to 0 faster than any power of log λ (test functions φ(log x / L)). The
   configuration of section 11.3 holds for ζ only as λ → ∞. The finite substitute is the least eigenvector ξ_λ
   (Connes–Consani–Moscovici; Connes–van Suijlekom, "Quadratic forms, real zeros and echoes of the spectral
   action", prove real zeros of its transform under conditions; cited from memory). **Open, and now a statement about
   a definite form:** the zeros of the transform of ξ_λ converge to the zeros of ζ(½ + it).
2. **Semilocal positivity [consequence of 1].** The form on the window [λ⁻¹, λ] involves only the primes p ≤ λ²
   and ∞. So for every finite S = {p ≤ λ²} ∪ {∞}, the semilocal Weil form of S on that window is positive definite.
   This is a statement about the semilocal models of the defect strand (section 2.3) that did not assume RH in its
   formulation; Connes–Consani (Selecta 2021) proved the case with no finite prime directly.
3. **The spectral space exists [standard GNS, given A].** The completion of C_c^∞(R₊*) for the Weil form carries
   the scaling action unitarily, with pure point spectrum the ordinates γ. Section 13's "packet space all critical"
   is this space.
4. **The Li–Toeplitz kernel [proved here; computed].** With λ_n Li's coefficients and u_ρ = 1 − 1/ρ, RH is
   equivalent to positive semidefiniteness of K(m, n) = λ_m + λ_n − λ_{|m−n|} (m, n ≥ 1): under RH,
   K(m, n) = Σ_ρ (1 − u_ρ^m)·conj(1 − u_ρ^n) is a Gram matrix, and K(n, n) = 2λ_n gives the converse by Li's
   criterion (Li 1997; Bombieri–Lagarias 1999). Under A every finite section is positive definite (a polynomial
   Σ c_m(1 − z^m) cannot vanish at infinitely many points) [computed for sizes 2 to 10: least eigenvalues 1.5·10⁻⁵
   down to 9.2·10⁻³⁵]. λ_n is then the Lévy–Khinchin exponent of the zero measure on the circle. This is section
   11.3's Toeplitz instance for ζ in exact form.
5. **The arithmetic Hodge index [proved here, given A].** With a = f̂(0), b = f̂(1), the sum over places of the
   explicit formula is G(f) = 2·Re(a·b̄) − Σ_ρ |f̂(ρ)|². It has positive index exactly one. Its hyperbolic plane is
   spanned by the two poles, which are null directions. So the Lorentz form of instances 2 and 6 has an exact
   counterpart: the explicit-formula form, of signature (1, ∞) under A.
6. **Scope: where M is needed.** ζ_L is the Hecke L-function of the trivial character over L. Effective Chebotarev
   (Lagarias–Odlyzko 1977; Bach–Sorenson 1996) needs GRH for ζ_L only, L the Galois closure. If A's "abelian Hecke
   L-functions" means Hecke characters over every number field, then Koiran's HN ∈ AM, Bürgisser's transfer and
   CX-K02 rest on A alone, and M is needed only beyond GL₁ (holomorphy of Artin L-functions, automorphic L-functions
   of higher rank). If it means abelian extensions of Q, section 14's marks stand. This is to be read off A's
   statement; I have not opened it.
7. **Strict total positivity [given A; Theorem 4].** Every Schur function of the parameters γ_k⁻² is strictly
   positive. On the Schur basis the ζ-point has neither τ nor e: both conditions of section 8 hold at once. For a
   curve the Schur functions with more than 2g rows vanish, and that support pattern is the genus (Theorem 5(c)).
   For item 10(c) of prompt 54: GRH forces the Boolean structure attached to ζ on the Young graph to be constant
   equal to 1, and forces the parity label (−1)^{Ω(n)} on integers to cancel to square-root order.
8. **An extremal character of S_∞ [given A; Theorem 4].** Parameters γ_k⁻²/Z(2). By Vershik–Kerov (1981) it is the
   limit of normalized characters of S_n along diagrams whose k-th row has frequency γ_k⁻²/Z(2), and it defines a
   finite factor representation of S_∞.
