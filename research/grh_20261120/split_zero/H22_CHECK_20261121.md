G(R) = R ⊔ {τ}, τ the additive identity and multiplicative absorber, e = 0_R, for a commutative ring R ≠ 0.

1. **Normal form.** (p, χ): G(R) → R × B is an injective semiring map with image {(x, b) : b = 0 ⇒ x = 0}.
   Re-proved: the operations are coordinatewise on the image; injectivity is immediate. [independent re-proof]
2. **Spectrum.** An ideal I ≠ {τ} contains some r ∈ R, hence e = 0·r, and I ∩ R is an ideal of R; so the ideals
   are {τ} and J ∪ {τ}, and the primes are {τ} and P ∪ {τ}, P ∈ Spec R. Every prime other than {τ} contains e, so
   {τ} = Spec G(R) ∖ V(e) is open, it lies in every prime, and V(e) ≅ Spec R through p; dim G(R) = dim R + 1 when
   finite. Localizing at {τ} inverts the idempotent e, which forces e = 1 there and collapses R to one point: the
   residue semifield at the generic point is B. [independent re-proof; computed for Z/n, n = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12]
3. **Gluing.** For a domain R with fraction field K, G(R) = R ×_K G(K); and G(A × B) ≅ G(A) ×_B G(B) over the
   support characters (CRT on the supported part, τ ↦ (τ, τ)). [independent re-proof; G(Z/6) computed]
4. **Modules.** For a G(R)-semimodule M, ε(m) = e·m satisfies m + ε(m) = (1 + e)m = m and m + (−1)m = ε(m), and
   ε(m) is additively idempotent. So (M, +) is a commutative monoid that is a union of groups indexed by its
   idempotents: a commutative Clifford monoid, i.e. a strong semilattice of abelian groups (Clifford 1941; Howie,
   Fundamentals of Semigroup Theory, Ch. 4). The semilattice is eM; the groups are the fibres M_l = ε⁻¹(l) with
   zero l; the structure maps are T_{l,l'}(m) = m + l'. Since r·ε(m) = ε(r·m), R acts on each fibre, linearly, and
   the T_{l,l'} are R-linear. With the converse construction of paper 01 (x ⊞ y = ρ(x) + ρ(y) in the fibre over the
   join), this gives G(R)-SMod ≃ support diagrams. [independent re-proof of the decomposition; computed on 80
   subsemimodules of G(F_p)^n, p = 2, 3, n = 2-4, generated by random vectors, so not built from diagrams: fibres,
   linearity, functoriality and the recovery of + through the join all hold]
5. **Projectives (proved here, 21 Nov ~17:3x UTC).** Theorem: a finitely generated G(R)-semimodule is projective iff
   it is isomorphic to P(J, Q): skeleton Idl(J) for a finite poset J, fibre ⊕_{j∈U} Q_j over U, transports the summand
   inclusions, each Q_j a f.g. projective R-module (paper 02's statement).
   - *Lemma (retracts of Boolean lattices).* A finite join-semilattice with 0 is a retract of some P([n]) (maps
     preserving joins and 0) iff it is a distributive lattice. Proof. If L is a retract via i, r, then e = i∘r is an
     idempotent join-endomorphism of P([n]) and L ≅ e(P([n])) as posets. The image is closed under unions, and its meet
     is U ∧ V = e(U ∩ V) (e(U ∩ V) lies below U and V and above every image point below both). As e preserves unions,
     U ∧ (V ∪ W) = e((U ∩ V) ∪ (U ∩ W)) = (U ∧ V) ∪ (U ∧ W): distributive. Conversely Idl(J) ⊂ P(J), with down-closure
     S ↦ ↓S as retraction (it preserves unions and fixes ideals); by Birkhoff every finite distributive lattice is an
     Idl(J). [computed: all 11, 123, 2360 idempotent join-endomorphisms of P([n]), n = 2, 3, 4, have distributive
     images; h22_checks.py]
   - *Only if.* Let M be a retract of F = G(R)^n via semimodule maps i, r. They commute with ε, so they are maps of
     support diagrams: eM is a retract of eF = P([n]), hence eM ≅ Idl(J) by the lemma. Each fibre M_l is an R-module
     retract of R^{i₀(l)}, hence f.g. projective. Each transport T_{l,l'} is split injective (left inverse
     r ∘ π ∘ i with π the coordinate projection). Pulling back through i and pushing through r gives
     M_{l∨l'} = T(M_l) + T(M_{l'}) and T(M_l) ∩ T(M_{l'}) = T(M_{l∧l'}), using l ∧ l' = r₀(i₀(l) ∩ i₀(l')). The second
     step lifts i(x) to the fibre over i₀(l) ∩ i₀(l') by replacing its supported zeros outside that set with τ. So
     l ↦ M_l is a lattice map from Idl(J) to direct summands of M_top. With Q_j = M_{↓j}/M_{↓j∖{j}} (projective) and
     sections s_j, the map ⊕_{j∈U} Q_j → M_U, (q_j) ↦ Σ s_j(q_j), is an isomorphism compatible with inclusions. Proof
     by induction along a linear extension of J, using the modular isomorphism and U_{k−1} ∩ ↓j_k = ↓j_k ∖ {j_k}.
     So M ≅ P(J, Q).
   - *If.* P(J, R) = {x ∈ G(R)^J : supp x ∈ Idl(J)} is a subsemimodule, and r(x) = (x on supp x, the supported zero
     0_R on ↓supp x ∖ supp x, τ elsewhere) is a semimodule retraction G(R)^J → P(J, R). P(J, Q) is a retract of
     P(J, Q ⊕ Q') ≅ P(J, R^{n_j}) fibrewise, so it is projective.
   - Remark: the retraction that makes P(J, R) projective fills the down-closure with supported zeros e = 0_R, so the
     projectives are classified by the τ/e distinction itself; over the ring R alone the skeleton disappears.
   [independent proof by the main session; the lemma's statement is presumably classical (finite projective
   join-semilattices are the finite distributive lattices; Horn-Kimura 1971), still to be checked against the source]
